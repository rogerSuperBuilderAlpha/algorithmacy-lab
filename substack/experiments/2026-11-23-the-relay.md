# Put a messenger between the coordinator and the people, and the group binds a third as tightly

*When the coordinator's decision reaches people through a relay, "any yes" and "all yes" still hold together as a whole, but at a third of the strength, and the whole group is no longer the tightest unit. A noisy majority doesn't notice the relay at all.*

## The question

So far the coordinator's decision reached everyone at the next step. In practice decisions pass through
someone first: a manager relays a ruling, an app queues a notification. What happens to integration when
there is a messenger in the middle, or two?

We measured integrated information Φ (IIT 4.0, computed exactly with PyPhi), which says how far a setup
fails to split into independent parts. It is not a claim that anything is alive or aware.

## What we ran

A coordinator reads three people and applies "any yes", majority, or "all yes", either reading them
correctly or misreading each one 10% of the time. Its decision passes through zero, one or two
messengers, who copy it exactly, before the people act on it. 18 setups of up to six members; the
largest took several minutes each. For every state the system can reach, we computed Φ for every
sub-group. Predictions committed first; all five held.

## What we found

**One messenger cuts integration to a third.** "Any yes" and "all yes" have Φ = 3 with no messenger. With
one, Φ = 1. A second messenger changes nothing (still 1). With the coordinator misreading people, the drop
is from 1.85 to 0.73.

| whole-system Φ | no messenger | one | two |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| "any yes" (or "all yes"), clean | 3.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| "any yes" (or "all yes"), 10% misreads | 1.85 | 0.73 | 0.73 |
| majority, clean | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| majority, 10% misreads | 0.027 | 0.027 | 0.027 |

**The whole group is no longer the tightest unit.** With a messenger, the most tightly bound piece is the
coordinator, the messenger and one person (Φ = 2), not everyone. The setup still doesn't split cleanly in
two, but it binds most strongly along single lines from the coordinator to one person at a time.

**A noisy majority doesn't notice the messenger.** Its Φ is 0.027 with zero, one or two relays, to six
decimals, and its tightest piece is the same (0.108).

## What it means

Delay doesn't break a three-way arrangement, but it weakens and spreads it. The more steps between the
third party's decision and the people's action, the more the arrangement looks like separate channels
running from the coordinator to each person, rather than one bound whole. For algorithmacy, a platform
whose rulings arrive through intermediaries binds its users less tightly than one that acts on them
directly.

## Limits

Three-person yes/no models. One shared chain of messengers, who never err. At most two messengers, one
error rate. The fact that the noisy value at one or two messengers is exactly 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.729 is
something we noticed, not explained.
