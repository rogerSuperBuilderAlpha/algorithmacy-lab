# Errors in, errors out

*Algorithmacy Lab, foundations stream. Study: `org_frontier/studies/noise_swap_symmetry/`.*

A coordinator S collects votes from three parties, applies a rule, and the parties then follow what S
decided. Errors can happen on the way in (S misreads a vote) or on the way out (a party misreads S). In the
last study we noticed something we hadn't predicted: for the "any one yes is enough" rule (OR3), 5% in and
10% out gave the same integrated information (Φ, computed exactly with IIT 4.0 in PyPhi) as 10% in and 5%
out. Here we tested that properly.

## What we ran

Both error rates over 0, 2%, 5%, 10% and 20%, all 25 combinations, for OR3 and for the majority rule (MAJ3).
We wrote down three predictions first.

## What we found

- **OR3 can't tell the two kinds of error apart.** Swap the in and out rates and whole Φ is the same at all
  ten swaps, to fifteen decimal places. For example, 2% in and 20% out, or 20% in and 2% out: both 0.980269.
- **But two errors aren't one bigger error.** We predicted Φ would only depend on the overall chance a vote
  ends up flipped. Wrong: 5% in and 5% out gives 2.042084, while the same overall flip chance from input
  errors alone gives 1.903424.
- **Majority is lopsided.** We predicted MAJ3 would also be symmetric. It isn't. Errors on the way out alone
  leave its whole Φ at zero. Only errors on the way in create it, and errors on the way out then barely move
  it (at 20% in: 0.036624 with no output errors, 0.036060 with 20%).

## Takeaway

For OR3, where the error happens doesn't matter, only how much there is on each side. For majority, where
it happens is everything. We don't yet have a derivation of the OR3 symmetry; that's next.
