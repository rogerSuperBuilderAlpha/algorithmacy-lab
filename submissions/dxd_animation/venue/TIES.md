# Theoretical Issues in Ergonomics Science (TIES), Taylor & Francis: submission fact sheet

## Read in the author's browser, 2026-11-11 (supersedes the [NOT FOUND] items below where they overlap)

[Added by Claude (Opus 5.5, the lead), 2026-11-11.] The TIES Instructions for Authors page (T2,
`https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=ttie20`) opened in
the author's Chrome session. Quoted verbatim from the page text:

- **Article type:** "Theoretical Issues in Ergonomics Science accepts the following types of article:
  Original Article."
- **Required statement:** "Articles must include a “Relevance to human factors / ergonomics theory”
  statement between the abstract and the beginning of the text."
- **Length:** "A typical paper for this journal should be no more than 10000 words, inclusive of:
  Tables / References / Figure or table captions". So references count toward the 10,000.
- **Review:** "it will then be double anonymous peer reviewed by two independent, anonymous expert."
  [sic] "If you have shared an earlier version of your Author’s Original Manuscript on a preprint
  server, please be aware that anonymity cannot be guaranteed."
- **Structure:** "title page; abstract; keywords; main text introduction, materials and methods,
  results, discussion; acknowledgments; declaration of interest statement; references; appendices (as
  appropriate); table(s) with caption(s) (on individual pages); figures; figure captions (as a list)."
- **Spelling and quotation marks:** "Please use British (-ise) spelling style consistently throughout
  your manuscript." "Please use single quotation marks, except where ‘a quotation is “within” a
  quotation’."
- **Generative AI:** "Declaration of generative AI use. Please include a statement disclosing whether
  and how generative AI has been used in the research and manuscript preparation process." The page
  points to the T&F-wide guidance (§8 below); I found no TIES-specific ban on AI-assisted drafting on
  this page.
- **Reference style:** the page says "Please use this reference style when preparing your paper". On
  2026-11-12 I followed the link in the author's browser: it reopens the same Instructions page and
  names no style. The style is **[NOT FOUND]**; the draft uses APA 7. Ask the editorial office or
  check a recent TIES article's reference list before submission.

**Consequences for the draft.** Body text of about 8,000–8,500 words leaves room for about 1,500–2,000
words of references. The "Relevance to human factors / ergonomics theory" statement goes between the
abstract and the introduction. British spelling and single quotation marks are a copyedit step at
submission; the working draft keeps the author's American spelling and double quotes so that his
sentences stay verbatim until he converts them.

## 2. Aims and scope [snippet, T1; wording unverified]

Search snippets of the About page give this scope:

- TIES aims to advance "the science and philosophy of human factors and ergonomics (HFE)".
- The scope covers theoretical issues, methodology, and philosophical dialogue about human interaction within simple and complex systems. The journal sets itself apart from application-focused HFE journals by concentrating on theoretical aspects of the discipline, "such as causality and behaviour in a complex human-technology-environment systems".
- Applied or experimental work is in scope if it "must have implications to, or address in some manner, theoretical issues in ergonomics science".
- The journal publishes "original, high-quality, peer-reviewed papers as well as commissioned reviews and peer-reviewed commentaries".

**Theory papers without new data:** I found no line that explicitly welcomes data-free papers **[NOT FOUND, T1/T2]**. The scope as snippeted makes theory the journal's focus, and it requires empirical work to bear on theory. That implies, but does not state, that a pure theory paper is in scope.

## 6. Review model and anonymised file

- **Review model [snippet, T1]:** Search results for the official page say that all peer review is **double anonymized**. Per the same snippets, submissions first get "initial appraisal" by the Co-Editors and then go to "independent, anonymous, expert referees". The exact wording is unverified.
- **How to prepare the anonymised file (T&F-wide) [verbatim, P5]:**
  - "You will need to prepare two copies of the manuscript for submission. One file will need to have all your author details included, and one will need to be anonymized. Both versions should include the title, abstract, body, and references."
  - "Don't mention any grants or acknowledgments in the anonymous version of your manuscript."
  - "Don't add any page headers or footers that would identify you."
  - On self-citation: refer to your own work "in the third person. For example, “Smith and Black (2007) have demonstrated” – not “We have previously demonstrated (Smith & Black, 2007)”." If that cannot be avoided, "delete the author names, or other identifying information (such as your institution) and replace it with [author(s)] in the text. In the reference list, delete the citation, and use a placeholder [author(s)]."
  - Remove hidden metadata with Word's Document Inspector ("Click ‘Check for Issues’, and then click ‘Inspect Document’." … "Click ‘Remove All’").
  - The non-anonymous version must contain "author details (names, institutions, and email addresses), acknowledgements, funding information, details of any clinical trial registrations, and complete references."

## 8. Generative AI policy (T&F-wide; a TIES-specific rule may be stricter)

- **Journal override [verbatim, P1]:** "Note that some journals may not allow use of Generative AI tools beyond language improvement, therefore authors are advised to review the “Instructions for authors” on the journal homepage prior to submission." The TIES instructions page could not be opened (T2), so whether TIES restricts further is **[NOT FOUND]**.
- **Supported uses [verbatim, P1]:** T&F "supports the responsible use of Generative AI tools … in cases such as: Idea generation and idea exploration; Copyediting, language improvement and translations; Literature classification; Coding assistance".
- **Not acceptable [verbatim, P1]:** "Authors should not submit manuscripts where Generative AI tools have been used in ways that replace core researcher and author responsibilities, for example: Text or code generation without rigorous revision; Synthetic data generation to substitute missing data without robust methodology; Generation of any types of content which is inaccurate including abstracts or supplemental materials".
- **Authorship [verbatim, P1]:** "Authors are responsible for ensuring that the content of their submissions … is created by the author." Also: "Generative AI tools must not be listed as an author".
- **Disclosure [verbatim, P1]:** "Authors must clearly acknowledge within the article or book any use of Generative AI tools through a statement that includes: the full name of the tool used (with version number), how it was used and the reason for use. For article submissions, a specific statement of AI usage disclosure must be included."
- **Disclosure [verbatim, P2]:** "Please add a Declaration of generative AI use statement disclosing whether and how generative AI has been used in the research and manuscript preparation process." Further: "When you submit your work to a Taylor & Francis journal you may be asked to confirm that you have disclosed within your manuscript any use of AI tools."
- **Copyediting use [verbatim, P2]:** "Use of AI tools to assist copyediting, reference management and language refinement is permitted. Authors are responsible for ensuring references are valid, accurate (i.e. not hallucinations) and support the stated claims. Authors must retain the original and revised versions of the article, which must be shared with the Editor upon request." The required declaration covers originality and accuracy, the tool's name and version, a check of the tool's terms of use, and "full responsibility for the integrity of the whole content, including accuracy of references."
- **Acknowledgements policy [verbatim, P2]:** "Any assistance from AI tools for content generation (e.g. large language models) … must be clearly acknowledged within the article."
- **Where the statement goes:** "within the article" [P1, P2]. Neither page names a section for journal articles.
- **Policy date:** neither page shows one. P1's footer reads "© 2026 Informa plc".

## Fit for this paper

1. Length: no verified TIES limit. Recent conceptual papers run 11–22 typeset pages, so a 9–12k-word theory article is plausible, but someone must open T2 in a browser before the author cuts or submits.
2. Theory fit: by its snippeted scope, TIES centres on theory and the philosophy of HFE, and it publishes human-AI conceptual pieces (Hoffman & Brézillon 2026, Hammarbäck et al. 2024). I found no line that explicitly welcomes data-free papers.
3. Preprint: T&F allows the AOM on "your own personal website", "social media" and non-commercial servers without counting it as duplicate publication. GitHub and Substack are not named, and a public, named repo can unmask the author under double-anonymous review.
4. AI: use is allowed with a "Declaration of generative AI use" statement (tool and version, how, why). However, T&F bars "Text or code generation without rigorous revision" and warns that some journals allow only language improvement, so the TIES instructions must be checked.
5. Blockers: none confirmed. Open items before submission are the TIES instructions page (limits, reference style, AI rule) and the APC, which applies only if the author chooses OA.
