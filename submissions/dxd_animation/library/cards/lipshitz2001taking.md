## What it argues

Abstract-only card; the review was not read. Per the abstract, Lipshitz, Klein, Orasanu and Salas review the progress of naturalistic decision making (NDM) over the decade since its first conference in 1989. After a short history they identify its essential characteristics and take up five contributions: recognition-primed decisions, coping with uncertainty, team decision making, decision errors and methodology. They say NDM drew attention to neglected areas, such as how experts size up situations and generate options, introduced new models, concepts and methods, and brought applied investigators into the field. Above all, they say, it gave a new view of how decisions, "broadly defined" as committing oneself to a course of action, are made. They also list challenges: more and more rigorous empirical research, and confirming the validity of its prescriptive models.
