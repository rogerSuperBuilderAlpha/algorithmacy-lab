## What it argues

Anderl, Klein, Sarigül, Schneider, Han, Fiedler and Utz report two preregistered online experiments with 1,222 participants in total (final samples of 556 and 666). Participants rated the credibility of short answers to general-knowledge questions, some accurate and some with two to four planted errors. The answers appeared in one of three forms: a "dynamic text-based agent" (ChatGPT), a "voice-based agent" (Alexa) or a "static text-based online encyclopedia" (Wikipedia). The content was identical across forms. In Experiment 2 participants were randomly assigned to branded or unbranded versions. People were better at telling accurate from inaccurate information in the static text than with either conversational agent (Abstract). The authors call this "discernment". The dynamic text agent fell between the other two: in Experiment 1 its discernment did not differ significantly from static text (p = 0.212), in Experiment 2 it was significantly lower (p = 0.029) (Results). The authors read the pattern as a reading-versus-listening modality effect working alongside a conversational-nature effect (Discussion). Social presence was higher for both agents than for static text, and the mediation models support the authors' reading, though they warn that mediators were not manipulated. They advise technology companies to consider reducing how human-like interactions feel during information search (Discussion).

The dynamic condition was recorded video, not a live stream. For the dynamic text agent "we asked participants to follow six screencasts, each showing a user asking ChatGPT a question and ChatGPT typing the answer to that question" (Methods, Experiment 1). Participants did not interact with the applications; they watched passively.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c5** (animation is opaque: missing frames; the interstices are the craft): Anderl et al. connect the visible "typing" of an LLM reply to a measured judgement, the ability to spot inaccuracies, which is the decision-level outcome DXD cares about. It stretches the source: the stimulus is a screencast of typing, and the contrast with static text differs in much more than frame rate (agent framing, a user's question shown, branding, social presence). The paper cannot assign the effect to the missing frames or the typing pace. In Experiment 1 the dynamic-versus-static discernment difference was not significant.
- **c6** (animation is co-optive: the viewer locks in; the user also animates): the authors' result that conversational presentation lowers discernment, and that social presence mediates part of the credibility effect, fits a viewer who is drawn into the character. It stretches the source: participants only watched, so the "user also animates" half of c6 is not tested, and the authors say real interaction might change the results (Discussion).

## Caution

- The abstract, and the triage note quoting it, say people were better at detecting inaccuracies in static text "compared to both types of conversational agents". The Results show that the dynamic-text-versus-static-text discernment gap is significant in Experiment 2 only (p = 0.029) and not in Experiment 1 (p = 0.212). The strong, repeated difference is voice versus static text. A use of this card for dynamic text alone should cite Experiment 2.
- Stimulus. As the triage decision required, the card states that the dynamic condition was recorded screencasts of ChatGPT typing, not a live stream, and that participants watched another person's ostensible interactions.
- Pairing with Bhat (2025), another card in this batch: Bhat measured perceptions, not discernment, and used a live simulated-typing interface in a within-subjects design. "The same display that builds trust weakens discernment" (the triage pairing) joins two different studies and measures; Anderl's own global trustworthiness results ran the other way from the credibility results (Results, "Global trustworthiness").
- Imprint. The text layer gives volume 14 and article number 17127 (from the PMC header). It does not give an issue number, so `issue` is empty. Triage reports the same article number from the same header.
- Credibility scale reliability was alpha = 0.63 in Experiment 1 (Methods, Measures); the processing-fluency scale had alpha = 0.66.
- The article cites Abercrombie et al. (2023) as an arXiv preprint (reference 47). That is the paper whose proceedings version is carded as abercrombie2023mirages.
