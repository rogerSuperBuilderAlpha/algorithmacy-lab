## What it argues
Abstract card. The full text was not read; this rests on the registry abstract.

Simonson takes up the "growing consensus that preferences are inherently constructive and largely determined by the task characteristics, the choice context, and the description of options". He does not dispute that construction often matters. He argues that much of the evidence for construction reflects people's difficulty in evaluating absolute attribute values and tradeoffs, and their tendency to gravitate to available relative evaluations. He adds that some key demonstrations used unusual tasks and may have "benefited" from the effects they showed, so the conclusions drawn are "rather sweeping, unqualified". He then introduces "more stable inherent preference" components not determined by context, and says inherent preferences matter more when reference points and forces of construction are less salient, most of all when objects are experienced. The last section draws implications for decision and marketing research.
