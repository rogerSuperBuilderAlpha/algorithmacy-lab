## What it argues
Abstract card. The full text was not read.

Siles and colleagues examine "the mutual domestication of users and recommendation algorithms on Netflix". They base it on 25 interviews with users and an inductive analysis of users' practices and profiles. They discuss five dynamics: personalization (individualized user–platform relationships); the integration of recommendations into "a matrix of cultural codes"; the rituals that place recommendations in the spatial and temporal processes of daily life; "the resistance to various aspects of Netflix as a form to enact agency"; and the conversion of private consumption of the platform into a public issue. The conclusion draws theoretical and analytical implications "to rethink the relationship between algorithms and culture".

## What the paper uses it for
- c3 (contrast). The paper treats the platform as co-optive. Siles et al. describe users acting on the platform as it acts on them, so a one-way reading of co-optation is too strong. The paper can concede this in §3.2.
- c6 (contrast, partly support). The paper says the user also animates. Users enacting agency through resistance fits that, but it is the paper's link: the abstract does not mention animation, characters or LLM interfaces, and its object is a recommender, not a conversational agent.
