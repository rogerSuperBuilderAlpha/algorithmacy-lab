## What it argues

This card rests on the abstract only (the Crossref record's JATS abstract). In it, Ananny and Crawford say that "models for understanding and holding systems accountable have long rested upon ideals and logics of transparency", and that being able to see a system "is sometimes equated with being able to know how it works and govern it". They ask whether black boxes can ever be opened and whether opening one would ever be enough. The authors then "critically interrogate the ideal of transparency", trace its roots "in scientific and sociotechnical epistemological cultures", and "present 10 limitations to its application". Their focus is "the inadequacy of transparency for understanding and governing algorithmic systems". They close by sketching "an alternative typology of algorithmic accountability grounded in constructive engagements with the limitations of transparency ideals".

The abstract does not list the ten limitations, does not name the typology's parts, and does not contain the "across, not inside" reading that CHECK F10 reports.
