## What it argues

Abstract-only card; the article was not read. Per the Crossref abstract, Chen, Huang and Xie start from firms deploying high-personalization chatbots that capture consumers' behaviour in real time and personalise service across the decision stages. Earlier research counted personalization as a firm's competitive advantage, but consumers do not seem to favour such chatbots at every decision stage. Across five studies, the authors show that high-personalization chatbots may backfire in the goal formation stage, but not in the goal fulfillment stage. They name the mechanism as reduced decision autonomy, and show a moderating effect of whether consumers have interdependent (as against independent) selves. They conclude that firms should adopt such chatbots with care and consider the decision stage, especially goal formation.

## What the paper uses it for

- c1 (a moving goal breaks conversion-era design): the nearest empirical case the triage found of a chatbot measured separately at goal formation and at goal fulfillment, with the formation stage the one where help hurts. It fits the paper's starting point that the goal is still forming when the user meets the interface. Caution on fit: the setting is consumer chatbots in marketing and the measure is the user's reaction to personalization, not goal movement during use; the abstract also does not say the goal changes mid-interaction.
