## What it argues

Abstract card: I read the OpenAlex abstract, not the paper. Luger and Sellen report interviews with 14 users of conversational agents (CAs). They say CAs from Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Facebook are now embedded in everyday software and that HCI has studied multimodality more than dialogue alone. They "find user expectations dramatically out of step with the operation of the systems, particularly in terms of known machine intelligence, system capability and goals", and use Norman's "gulfs of execution and evaluation" to draw implications for future design. Crossref gives the subtitle "The Gulf between User Expectation and Experience of Conversational Agents" and the venue CHI'16, San Jose, 7 May 2016.

## What the paper uses it for

- c4 (an LLM interface is an animation): F3 lists it among the evidence that users read an agent as a someone with capabilities and goals. The abstract speaks of expectations about "machine intelligence, system capability and goals", which fits but does not say users take the agent for a character. That step is the paper's.
- c6 (co-optive): FACETS F3 lists it as "the expectation gap conversational agents open (c6)". The abstract supports the gap between expectation and system operation; it says nothing about users locking in, so the co-optive reading is the paper's step.
- The abstract describes 2016 assistants, not LLM interfaces; the paper's "as of October 2026" frame needs that gap stated.
