## What it argues

Hollanek, writing from Cambridge's Centre for Film and Screen, argues that opening the algorithmic black box is not mainly an engineering job. Expert committees have asked for AI that is transparent, explainable and auditable, and he says this treats transparency as a feature to design in. His claim is that design, at root, works by concealment and obfuscation. He draws on Vilém Flusser, who reads design as trickery (the weaver hides the warp behind the pattern), and on Benjamin Bratton's "Stack", in which each new layer of design covers the layers beneath. From this he concludes that "design has always been a form of blackboxing," so transparency by design is not natural to making.

He proposes critique as the counter-move. Critique here means Critical Theory-style methods that dismantle objects to reveal the social and economic structures built into them. His examples are the plates of artisans at work in Diderot and d'Alembert's Encyclopedia (which he calls "undesigning"), Crawford and Joler's *Anatomy of an AI System*, Crawford and Paglen's work on datasets, and Chaslot's Algotransparency experiment on YouTube recommendations. He insists that critique is not reverse engineering: taking a phone apart shows its parts, not "in whose interest" it works. Industry ethics teams (Microsoft's Aether Committee, DeepMind Ethics and Society, auditing firms) are for him an open question: he asks whether such in-house checks can lead to "anything more than design that anticipates critique" and whether critique must stay external to designing to remain genuine (p. 2077). He ends by arguing that design and critique look incompatible but must be reconciled, and that responsibility lies mainly with designers, who should learn from critical design and critical making (Dunne and Raby; Ratto). The article is a conceptual essay in a student-forum section. It has no empirical study.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c8 (support, with a limit).** The paper says DXD must apply the animator's methods rather than hide frames or expose the backend. Hollanek gives that dilemma a source in design theory. He says design conceals by nature, and he says showing the insides is not enough, because it does not show whose interest the system serves. That backs the paper's rejection of "expose the backend." Where the paper goes further is its own: Hollanek's remedy is critique, meaning critical design and critical making and a reflective designer, not the animator's craft of choosing which frames to show. Nothing in the article mentions animation, frame sequences or interface timing. The match with the paper's third path is by analogy.
- **c3 (support, partial).** For the claim that the platform environment is opaque, Hollanek states that "corporate strategies of secrecy and user interfaces that hide traces of AI-driven personalization" combine with the opacity of deep learning, and he asks "in whose interest" systems work. That supports opacity and a party with interests. He does not model a triad, and he does not use the word co-optation. His Zuboff-based account of platforms that prompt users to act and "automate us" (p. 2074) is the nearest he comes to the paper's co-optive claim, and it is Zuboff's account, not his own finding.

## Caution

- Year: Crossref gives online 2020-11-17 and print 2023-10, volume 38(5). The card takes the volume year 2023 and sets `registered_year: 2020` (README rule 4; DECISIONS "Smaller corrections"). The article's own copyright line reads "© The Author(s) 2020".
- The article sits under a "Student Forum" heading on p. 2071. It is an essay by a single author, so weigh it as a position, not as evidence.
- Hollanek does not discuss animation or LLM interfaces. He treats "the interfaces of Facebook and YouTube" as examples of a label that could flag algorithmic curation (p. 2072), and he says this is not enough, but that is as close as he gets to interface design.
- His Flusser and Bratton passages carry the "design as trickery" claim. The paper should cite Hollanek for the argument and not attribute Flusser's or Bratton's words to him. Several quotations in the article are quotations of others (Zuboff, Bogost, Kittler); I took none of them as loci.
- The triage note on this work (F7-F8.md row 4) describes the abstract accurately. Nothing in the abstract or body names a user–platform–third-party triad.
