## What it argues

This card rests on the abstract only (Crossref's structured abstract). Chiou and Lee write a narrative literature review of "human trust in automation" meant "to guide research and design for increasingly capable automation in complex work environments" (Objective; Method). Two trends drive it: "the development of increasingly capable automation and the flattening of organizational hierarchies" (Background).

The authors say that much research has aimed to calibrate trust "to promote appropriate reliance on automation", and that this approach "neglects relational aspects of increasingly capable automation and system-level outcomes, such as cooperation and resilience" (Results). In its place they "adopt a relational framing of trust based on the decision situation, semiotics, interaction sequence, and strategy". The goal, they say, "is not to maximize trust, or to even calibrate trust, but to support a process of trusting through automation responsivity" (Results).

Future work, on their account, should consider "not just individual characteristics and how automation influences people, but also how people can influence automation and how interdependent interactions affect trusting automation" (Conclusion). The new contexts "shift human operators to co-operators of automation", and there "automation responsivity and the ability to resolve conflicting goals may be more relevant than reliability and reliance" (Conclusion). They offer "a conceptual model comprising four concepts—situation, semiotics, strategy, and sequence" (Application).

**What the source counts as a third party (Checkpoint B ruling 4).** On the abstract:
- *Is there a third party?* The abstract names none. The parties it names are "people" or "human operators" and "automation". It speaks of "interdependent interactions" and of "flattening of organizational hierarchies", which may imply more people, but it names no deployer, platform, organisation or other party standing behind the automation.
- *Does it have interests of its own?* The abstract mentions "conflicting goals" without saying whose goals conflict: operator against automation, operator against operator, or anyone against an organisation. It cannot be read as a third party with its own interests.
- *Does it act through the artefact on the user?* The abstract speaks of "how automation influences people" and "how people can influence automation". Influence runs both ways between operator and automation. Nothing says a party acts through the automation on the operator.
- *Modelled or only named?* The four-concept model (situation, semiotics, strategy, sequence) is the only model the abstract describes. No third party appears in it as described.

The full text may answer each question differently; this card cannot.
