## What it argues

Caroline Ruddell reads the way anime depicts movement through Thomas Lamarre's pair of terms, the 'cinematic' and the 'anime-ic'. She takes anime to be often judged 'flat', and she argues that it still offers spectacle, character development and 'depth through the very form of movement' (abstract). Her main case is the television series InuYasha. In its fight scenes the foreground figure is frozen or slowed while the background races past, and she reads those frozen moments as comments on the character's vulnerability, power and inner state (pp. 8–14).

On limited animation she reports Lamarre rather than arguing the point herself. Limited animation uses fewer frames than full animation, and it asks the viewer for a way of 'reading' that involves scanning and re-reading (pp. 6–7). Ruddell's own addition is stillness: she turns from Lamarre's flatness to 'the temporal matter of stillness' (p. 7).

In the last third she asks how viewers identify with such images. She uses the film-theory idea of suture (viewers 'sewn' into a narrative) and Philip Brophy's 'animatic apparatus', and she concludes that viewers probably shift between a cinematic and an animatic mode of viewing (pp. 18–22; the 'sewn' phrase is p. 5). She leaves the question open ('begins to explore', abstract) and does not test it with viewers.

## What the paper uses it for

- c5 (animation is opaque: missing frames; the interstices are the craft): Ruddell gives an open-access, peer-reviewed statement that limited animation uses fewer frames and demands a particular reading from the viewer (pp. 6–7), while Lamarre's own book stays closed. This is a stretch of the source in one respect. Ruddell never says the unseen frames are the animator's craft; she reports a viewing technique, and she reports it at second hand from Lamarre.
- c8 (DXD must apply the animator's methods rather than hide frames or expose the backend): the paper could cite her for the point that anime builds meaning out of interruption and stillness instead of a seamless chain of events (pp. 12–13, 18, 24). She says nothing about interface or decision design, so any link to c8 is the paper's own step.
