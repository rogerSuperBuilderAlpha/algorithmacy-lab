## F3 Anthropomorphism, the ELIZA effect, the media equation (c4, c6)

F3 has three upgrades (`epley2007seeing`, `mori2012uncanny`, `luger2016like`; Crossref records confirmed today:
10.1037/0033-295x.114.4.864, 10.1109/mra.2012.2192811, 10.1145/2858036.2858288) and four pointers (lima
`natale2021` and `reeves1996`, both `extended_preview`; lima `nass2000`, full_text; dial
`waytz-heafner-epley-2014-mind-in-the-machine`). The searches show three gaps. No library holds Weizenbaum's own
report, although OUTLINE §4.2 and Stark (2024) both lean on "the ELIZA effect". Nothing carries the LLM-era
version of the character claim (c4) except Stark himself. And F3 has no contrast: every pointer argues that users
respond socially to machines, while the CASA literature has itself revised that claim, and an NLP literature
argues that designers should damp anthropomorphism, not build on it. The shortlist answers those three gaps.
Holtgraves et al. (2007), a FACETS anchor, goes to reserve: Crossref, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar all carry
no abstract, and OpenAlex marks it closed, so it could only be a metadata card.

## F4 Stream timing: typing delays, token pacing, latency (c5)

F4 has no upgrades and six spaces pointers (`andes2024`, `xiaoyang2025`, `hzhu2026`, `pu2026`, `parklee2024`,
`liu2023captions`); none of the held chatbot-timing PDFs has a card or note. OUTLINE §4.1 lists Gnewuch et al.
(2022) as "no card yet" and "To fetch" item 7. The searches show two gaps beyond that. F4 has no HF baseline in
any library, although the facet's own "Establish" paragraph needs one. And F4 has no work in which delay or
dynamic display *helps*, while §4.1 reads the missing frames as a cost the platform hides. Three held works fill
the second gap: Tan et al. (2026) find that 2-second waits made outputs look less thoughtful, Gnewuch et al.
(2018) that dynamic delays raised perceived humanness and satisfaction, and Bhat (2025) that typed-out display
raised warmth and trust. Anderl et al. (2024) give the decision-level cost of the same display, which makes F4
the place where the frames meet a decision.
