## F7 Platform opacity, co-optation, transparency critique

The facet's three anchors are not equally held. D. Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024) is read in full in four
libraries (`starkvandenbroeck2024`, lima; `stark2024principles`, monograph). Burrell (2016) is read in full too:
`burrell2016how` (monograph) quotes the three forms of opacity verbatim at p. 1, "(2) opacity as technical
illiteracy" among them, so CHECK F7 is settled by pointer, as the lead corrected in FACETS. **Ananny & Crawford
(2018) is not held at any depth that can carry a claim.** The registry holds seven cards for it and none is
full-text: monograph, coordsov and hospitality `abstract`, lima and designeth `abstract_only`, research
`metadata_only`, and dial `verified` with no depth stated. OUTLINE §3.2 and §5 lean on it twice, once for the
transparency critique and once for "exposing the backend" as a failed option, so it needs a full-read card.
FACETS lists it only as a pointer, so I take one of F7's three slots for it. The searches add two gaps. No card
yet says that transparency is itself a mediated arrangement of what is shown, which is the bridge from Ananny &
Crawford to the animator's control of frames (c3 to c5). And F7 has no contrast: every held card reads the
platform as acting on a user it opaquely co-opts, while one strand of the folk-theory literature finds users
shaping the platform back. Seamful design, a FACETS query, is already held in full (pointers below), so it takes
no slot.

## F8 The HF automation dyad versus a triad

**The §3.2 claim is in trouble, and the cards the lab already holds show it.** HF has multi-party models.
`oneill2022human` (dxdfound, full, verified) counts 22 of 76 human-autonomy-teaming studies with two or more
humans and 7 that are multi-agent. Its card concludes that "the lineage models multi-party teams but never an
interested third party". `parasuraman2000types` (dxdfound, full) records that "two-node loop" is the earlier
draft's phrase, not the source's, and that the paper's own ATC example joins controller, pilot and uplinked
clearances. `klein2004ten`, `klein2005common` and `christoffersenwoods2002` (full) model joint activity among an
"ensemble of people" and agents. The searches add two harder cases. Stanton et al. (2006) propose a distributed
situation awareness in which human and non-human agents "each hold their own situation awareness" and have
"different purposes" (abstract). Cameron et al. (2024) say outright that "the dyadic model of HRI misses a key
complexity" and propose a Social Triad of User, Robot and Deployer, in which a robot's trustworthiness depends
on the user's view of whoever deploys it (abstract). That deployer is very close to the paper's platform-animator.
Chiou & Lee (2023), in *Human Factors* itself, argue that calibrated trust "neglects relational aspects" and
that people become "co-operators of automation" (abstract).

**My suggestion, for the author to decide (Claude's wording, not the author's):** soften §3.2 from "HF's
automation models are dyads" to something like this. HF's canonical automation models take one operator and one
aid as the unit of analysis (Parasuraman et al., 2000; Endsley, 2017). Where HF admits more parties (teams,
distributed SA, joint activity), the parties share a goal. A third party with interests of its own, the deployer
or platform, has entered HRI and trust research only recently (Cameron et al., 2024; Chiou & Lee, 2023), and the
paper builds on that move rather than discovering it. The same works bear on c9 (gap): the triad is new to DXD
and to animation-as-interface, not to HF. Each sentence needs its card read first.

So the F8 slots go less to the dyad, which `parasuraman2000types` already carries, than to the works that test
it. FACETS names two book anchors, Sheridan (2002) and Hollnagel & Woods (2005). The author holds neither
(Checkpoint A, ruling 4), so either would be a metadata card. I keep Sheridan, because FACETS uses him for the
dyad "stated by its author" and `sheridan1978human` is only a partial pointer. I move Hollnagel & Woods to
reserve, because a metadata card cannot show whether the joint system is "still two parties" (FACETS's
assertion, unread), and the full-text joint-activity pointers carry the position. AI-mediated communication
needs no new card: `hancock2020ai`, `hohenstein2020ai`, `jakesch2019ai`, `mieczkowski2021` and
`guzman2020artificial` are all held in full (pointers below). The Consensus "beyond dyads" review is real but
weak (reserve R2).
