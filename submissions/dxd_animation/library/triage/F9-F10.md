## F9 Interface animation in HCI (c8)

No card yet holds HCI's own line on interface animation. The library has one abstract-only card in
`dxd_animator` (chang1993animation, to be upgraded) and Lasseter 1987 under F1, and OUTLINE §5 cites Chevalier
et al. (2016) on record only (CHECK F11). The searches show what the line measures. Chang and Ungar borrow
cartoon timing, slow-in/slow-out and anticipation so that objects "move solidly" (abstract); Heer and Robertson
find that animated transitions "can significantly improve graphical perception" (abstract); Dragicevic et al.
test slow-in/slow-out on object tracking. Every one measures perception or tracking. Only González (1996; and
González & Kasper, 1997) measures **decision quality**, which makes it the one HCI precedent for c8's claim that
animation choices change what a user decides. No hit treats the gap between two interface states as the place
to surface a decision. Two hits do the opposite: Huhtala et al. (2010) design transitions that "can hide
processing delays" (abstract), and Ash et al. (2018) show credit-site designers tuning transitions and
thresholds to carry users past friction into an application. That is the "hide the frames" option §5 argues
against, so Ash serves as the contrast. May, Dean and Barnard (2003) remain the precedent for carrying a
moving-image craft (film cutting) into interface design.

## F10 Agent-mediated shopping and agentic commerce (c1)

The lab already holds the 2025–26 agentic-commerce record in depth: allouah2025 and allouah2026what, alavi2026when,
busch2025consumer, tang2026dark and the rest of FACETS' pointer list, and the held PDFs of Allouah, Alavi and
Busch are all carded. What the facet lacks is the earlier agent literature that c1 needs as a contrast, and a
current study that treats the user's goal as a variable. Guttman, Moukas and Maes (1998) supply the first: they
place agents inside a Consumer Buying Behavior model. The card must test FACETS' claim that this work "fixes the
goal before the agent acts". He, Jennings and Leung (2003) list "need identification" as one of the activities
agents perform (abstract), so the model has a goal-forming stage. The contrast therefore holds only if that
stage comes first and stays closed while brokering runs, and the card must check this in Guttman's text.
Wadi and Ma (2026) supply the second. Across eight deployed LLMs, a "vague goal prompt" under acquisition costs
leads agents "to omit diagnostic attributes" and to choose "suboptimal choices resembling human heuristics",
while a "specific goal prompt" preserves search (abstract). Goal specificity is their manipulated variable, so
the raincoat → umbrella case has an empirical neighbour. No hit studies a goal that **changes** during an
agent-mediated purchase. That absence is c1's opening, and S9 should log it.
