## F5 Deciding with a moving goal: goal formation, constructed preference, NDM (c1, c2)

F5's claim is that the commuter forms the goal while deciding, and that conversion design assumes the goal is
already formed. Today no source in any lab library carries this claim at more than abstract depth. The three upgrades
(slovic1995construction, klein2006making, simon1955behavioral) are abstract-and-record cards, and OpenAlex marks
all three closed. Of the pointers, kahneman2009conditions and hutchins1985direct are full text, but
norman1988psychology's body says no text of the book was read, and haublmurray2003 is abstract only. The
searches expose three gaps. First, no card shows goal formation inside a chatbot or agent conversation, which is the raincoat →
umbrella case itself. Second, Norman's goal-first stage model, the thing c1 says conversion design inherited,
reaches the outline only through secondaries. Third, nothing argues the other side: that much of what looks
constructed is a stable preference meeting an unfamiliar task. The shortlist answers all three. It keeps four of FACETS's seven
named targets and swaps the three book-bound ones (Lichtenstein & Slovic 2006, Klein 1993, Klein 1998) for two
agent-era works and a contrast. The author holds no books (Checkpoint A, ruling 4), so those three would be
metadata cards that say only that the work exists. Lipshitz et al. (row 3) carries recognition-primed decision
in a readable review, as FACETS intended. The three go to reserve with their records confirmed.

pooled nudge effect, but Maier et al.'s correction for publication bias, read in full in the F2 facet file (B6),
has no card anywhere.
