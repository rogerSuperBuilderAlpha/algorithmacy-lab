**Event:** EURAM 2027, "Rising Above: Embracing Resilience, Reimagining History, and Rethinking Management for a Sustainable Future". Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, 22–25 June 2027.

## Dates (verbatim)
- "Paper submission opens: Late November 2026"
- "Deadline for Paper Submission: 12 January 2027, 2.00 pm Brussels time"
- "Notification of Acceptance: 11 March 2027"
- "Authors and Panelists Registration Deadline: 22 April 2027"

## Format (verbatim)
- "The maximum length of the paper is 40 pages (including ALL tables, appendices and references). The minimum length of the paper is 10 pages."
- "Use Times New Roman 12-point font, double-spaced, and 1-inch (2.5 cm) margins all around."
- "The abstract should not exceed 400 words and should be included both in the PDF and the submission form."
- 3 to 6 keywords; one anonymised PDF.
- "If you submit a paper, you agree to review at least two papers for EURAM 2027 in the track you have submitted your paper to."

## Originality and limits (verbatim)
- "Submitted papers must NOT have been published or accepted for publication before the EURAM annual Conference (neither in print nor online, in any form – online preprint, working paper, etc.)"
- "Each paper can only be submitted to ONE track."
- "ONE PRESENTING AUTHOR = ONE PAPER"
- The guidelines say nothing about papers under review at a journal.

## Tracks
- **ST09_03, "AI, Big Data, Algorithmic Management and Emerging Tech in Managing People"** (Yumei Yang): "Key concerns include impacts on health, job quality, autonomy, and fairness in algorithmic decision-making. Ethical issues surrounding transparency and accountability further complicate adoption."
- **T09_04, "Distance, Voice and Good Work in Digital Collaboration"** (Sylvia Rohlfer): "We welcome conceptual, empirical, and methodological contributions on cognitive, socio-relational, operational, and engagement-related distances in remote working and collaboration; competence development at different levels".
- **T09_07, "Human, Artificial, and Organizational Cognition"** (Davide Secchi): "environments where a mix of human and artificial agents can interact but maintain separate individualities".
