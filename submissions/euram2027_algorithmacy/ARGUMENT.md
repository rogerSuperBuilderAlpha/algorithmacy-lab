# Argument: the OT paper in short form, for ST09_03

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. This restates the OT paper's argument for a conference audience. The argument is the author's; the P/P/C framing is Claude's.]

**P1.** Communicative competences are defined by what stands between the people coordinating. Oracy covers direct and chained dyads, and literacy covers dyads moderated by a text that a human still interprets and judges.
- *Sources:* the OT manuscript, §"What Stands Between as a Defining Criterion"; `lima_pdw/literature/cards/simmel1902.md` (group size and social form).

**P2.** Algorithmic management places an opaque, adaptive intermediary between two people. It evaluates both, binds both to its verdict, and was chosen by neither. This turns the dyad into a triad and dissolves the integrating conditions of coordination: accountability, predictability and common understanding.
- *Sources:*
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/starkvandenbroeck2024.md` (p. 1, p. 14)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/curchod2020.md`
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/burrell2016.md` (abstract depth)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/okhuysen2009.md` (p. 463 for the definition of coordination; the three conditions at pp. 483–490)

**C.** Working in that triad requires a distinct competence, algorithmacy. It has three practices, each of which restores one condition: interpreting ambiguous results restores accountability, specifying intent across the filter restores common understanding, and tracking unannounced rule changes restores predictability. This gives HRM and OB a competence to develop, alongside AI literacy rather than inside it.
