# T: Does the author's triad-versus-dyad distinction hold?

*Facet T of the Decision Experience Design (DXD) Part 3 literature base. Agent-drafted on 2026-10-03 (seat T of a four-seat run). The author has not reviewed it, and none of the text below is the author's. Every claim about Peirce, Simmel or Quine is tied to a card in `submissions/triadic_reduction/library/cards/`; every claim about Hanson, Mansour, Kalshi or the law is tied to a numbered claim or entry in facets H, M, L, D or X. Sentences marked **[inference]** are mine. Nothing here uses Φ or IIT in wording meant for public prose; where I lean on the lab's results I say so and keep them as background.*

**The claim under test (the author, lightly cleaned, from the brief).** "If the goal of DXD is to create conditions for people to make the best decision, it can't be through a zero-sum model. While in prediction markets people are in a sense betting within a triad, they are really betting the plain win or lose. Bridging the hedge to the betting pool does not solve for the real triadic coordination a decision, as opposed to a choice, has to consider. Prediction markets pretend to be triads when they are really dyads, unlike the Forex market, where those who participate normally are always getting what they need while coordinating through an algorithm; only those speculating win or lose. Speculation is not a triadic activity. Presenting a dyad as a triad is a dark pattern and poor DXD."

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The author's instinct is right and the vocabulary is wrong. The thing that separates a currency hedge from a sports bet is not the number of parties or the shape of the mediation; it is whether each side leaves with what it came for at the moment the intermediary matches them, or must wait for an event neither controls to decide which of them gets it. Stout calls the first an indemnity and the second a difference contract; Lockhart calls them extant and created risk; Kalshi's own 2024 brief and its concessions in the Nevada litigation put its sports contracts on the second side. By contrast the triad/dyad framing fails on its own terms: by Part 2's definition a matching engine is a third that reads both traders and binds both, and by the lab's own criterion a posted price "has the control's shape" (ARGUMENT.md P39), so the exchange is triadic in exactly the sense the lab has defended for a year. What is dyadic is the bet, not the market. The essay should keep the diagnosis, drop "really dyads", and adopt the criterion in section 5.

## 1. The candidate criteria

### (i) Part 2's criterion: a third reads both and binds both

**Statement.** "Coordination is triadic when a third party reads both human parties and commits an outcome binding on both" (Part 2 `DRAFT.md` l. 11, restating Part 1). Part 1 gives the fuller form: "an adaptive, opaque algorithmic intermediary that reads multiple parties simultaneously and commits binding determinations that neither party fully controls" (`published/2026-09-30_decision-experience-design.md` l. 34), with three structural properties, "opacity ... mutual bindingness ... adaptivity" (l. 118).

**What it separates.** It separates an intermediary from a medium: a text does not read its reader, a matching engine reads both orders. It does not separate a hedge from a bet. A matching engine reads two orders and commits a fill binding on both, so every exchange trade passes, and so does every Kalshi contract. Kalshi's own filings describe the structure in these words: "Traders do not bet against the house, but rather enter into contracts with other traders on an exchange" (Sottile declaration ¶27, facet M B1). Under (i), then, the sportsbook is the dyad (bettor against the house, Levitt 2004 in facet X line 1) and the exchange is the triad. That is the difficulty the brief names, and it is real.

**One thing (i) does carry.** Part 1 adds a sentence the author can use: "offering a costless exit path makes the parties substitutable, collapsing an irreducible triadic mediator back into a separable, manageable dyad" (l. 146). An anonymous order book is the limiting case of substitutable counterparties, and Kalshi's own declaration says users trade in and out before resolution (Sottile ¶33). **[inference]** By Part 1's own sentence, a liquid exchange is already the "separable, manageable dyad" Part 1 contrasts with the irreducible triad. The essay can cite itself here. It cannot also say the exchange is a triad by (i); it has to choose, and section 3 says which.

### (ii) The lab's criterion: joint determination in a whole

**Statement, in the lab's words.** "An irreducible triad here is a party jointly determined by two others inside a system that does not factor" (`triadic_reduction/README.md` l. 15–16; ARGUMENT.md D3). The operational form: there is a party X whose next state is set by two other parties Y and Z together, X acts back on both, and no part of the three can be cut away without loss (ARGUMENT.md D3 operational form; P27 for the exemplar, A′ = M, M′ = A ∧ B, B′ = M). Three riders from the same document matter here. An active third is necessary and not sufficient: "the verdict is per form, and a platform counts only once its rule is modelled" (P42; CLAIM.md "Do not claim ... That an active third constitutes an irreducible triad"). Direct ties between the two humans do not break the triad (P34). And the lab has modelled no market, worker or platform (P46; CLAIM.md "That the lab shows any worker, platform or real rule is triadic. No such case is modelled").

**In plain words.** A coordination is triadic when the thing in the middle is set by both people at once, not by one and then the other, and both people then act on what it tells them. A relay is not a triad: the writer fixes the text, then the reader reads it (P40). A tally is: both votes set it, and both voters read it.

**What it separates.** It separates joint determination from a relay, and a whole from a pile of parts. It does not separate a hedge from a bet, and it classifies the price against the author. ARGUMENT.md P39 states the point for the talk: "Take a scoreboard, a vote tally or a posted price. Each is set jointly by both parties, read by both, and pursues no objective of its own. Each has the control's shape." A market price is the lab's own example of the algorithmacy form. **[inference]** Under (ii), then, the price-forming part of an exchange is a triad among the traders who set it, whatever they are trading. What (ii) does separate is the price from the settlement, and that split does real work in section 3.

### (iii) A needs-based criterion: each side leaves with what it came for

**Statement.** A trade is coordination when each party has a need that exists before the trade and the trade's terms meet it at the fill, whatever the underlying event later does. It is a bet when what each party came for is decided after the fill by an event neither controls, and only one of them can have it.

**Sources that support it.** Stout documents the common-law line between a difference contract and an indemnity. Where "the goods are not to be delivered, but one party is to pay to the other the difference between the contract price and the market price ... then the whole transaction constitutes nothing more than a wager, and is null and void" (`stout1999` pp. 714–15, quoting *Irwin v. Williar*); the "indemnity exception" shows that "judges recognized that difference agreements could be used for insurance as well as for gambling" (p. 719); and the economic reason to care is that "speculation on disagreement is a zero-sum game in which one player's gain is necessarily the other's loss ... when HE trading involves transaction costs, speculators' average returns become negative" (p. 745). Lockhart tests five heuristics for separating derivatives from gambling and keeps one: of "information-gathering potential, risk profile, time horizon, the existence of an underlying asset, and extant versus created risk—only the last principle sufficiently separates the two activities" (`lockhart2025` pp. 2175–76, 2221). The Ninth Circuit applies the same test to Kalshi: its sports contracts "create risk, largely for ordinary consumers, where none previously existed" (`ca9_2026assad` p. 29). The CFTC's own 2012 order read the public-interest test as "an event contract's utility for hedging and price basing purposes" (`cftc2012nadex` p. 3), and the 2026 proposal demotes that to one "significant factor" (`cftc2026nprm` p. 35829), which is the regulatory version of the author's "bridging the hedge to the betting pool".

**What it separates.** It separates the hedge from the bet cleanly, by the party and not by the system. It does not separate dyads from triads: a bilateral forward between an importer and a bank is a dyad that meets both needs, and a grain futures market is a triad that meets them too. Section 3 says what this costs the author.

### (iv) Simmel's three thirds: mediator, *tertius gaudens*, party

**Statement.** Simmel distinguishes the non-partisan who mediates from the third who profits by the pair's opposition. The mediator "seeks to eliminate himself, and only to bring to pass that the two disunited or ununited parties may unite directly" (`simmel1902number2` pp. 167–68), "must constantly hold himself this side of actual decision" (p. 169), and acts as "a sort of central station which, in whatever form the controverted material may come in from one side, may give it out to the other side only in objective form" (p. 169). The *tertius gaudens* "can set his own conditions for the decision" (p. 176), and "the quantity of his power is determined exclusively by the strength which each of them has relative to the other" (`simmel1950sociology` p. 157): "the forces of two antagonistic elements paralyze one another and thus actually give unlimited power to the intrinsically extremely weak position of a third element not yet engaged in the issue" (p. 157). In *divide et impera* "the third element intentionally produces the conflict in order to gain a dominating position" (p. 162). Simmel also grants that three parties need not make a triad: when the third "is at such a distance from the other two that there exist no properly sociological interactions which concern all three elements alike. Rather, there are configurations of two" (`simmel1950sociology` p. 145; 1902 "configurations of twos", p. 166).

**What it separates.** It separates a venue that passes orders through in objective form from a venue whose gain is set by how evenly the two sides are opposed. Kalshi's filed fee is 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P), which peaks at P = 0.5 (`kalshi2022cftc_fee`, facet D; current coefficient not reconfirmed from a 2026 document); Polymarket's fee is "symmetric around 50% probability" (`polymarket2026docs_fees`); Kagan describes "lower fees at the tails, higher fees in the middle" (`mansour2026freakonomics`, facet M B2). **[inference]** The venue earns most where the two sides are most evenly matched. That is Simmel's *tertius gaudens* arithmetic applied to a fee schedule. Simmel wrote about power, not fees, and the cards do not mention markets, so this is my mapping and the essay must present it as the author's analogy.

### (v) Hanson's criterion: a consumer who does not trade and a funder who pays

**Statement.** A decision market has a consumer of the price who is not a trader (a legislature that "might accept this estimate when considering whether or not to pass this hidden-gun bill", facet H claim 5) and a funder, either a patron who subsidises a market maker or "someone committed to make a policy choice based on the market estimate" (claims 9–11). Without a subsidy, "Once rational agents have hedged their risks regarding the events covered by an information market, they should not want to trade with each other" (claim 12), so an unsubsidised market runs on irrationality, signalling or fun. In 2026 Hanson describes the retail model as "The customer is the trader who just pays a tax ... I would rather people who get the information pay for it" (claim 17).

**What it separates.** It separates a decision market from a prediction market by what sits downstream of the price, and it separates a funded market, in which an informed trader can expect a positive return, from a fee market, in which traders pay each other and the venue. **[inference]** It also converges with (ii): a decision-maker bound to the price is a party determined by the price, whose decision feeds back into which conditional contracts pay, so Hanson's missing funder is the same thing as the lab's missing joint determination. Section 3(c) develops this.

### (vi) Peirce: welded or appended

**Statement.** Peirce's test for a genuine triad is whether three relations are "welded into one fact" (`peirce1890guess`, CP 1.363, vol. 1 p. 188), against an imitation in which "the thirdness is externally appended" (`peirce1904letter`, CP 8.331) or the whole is "merely one dyadic relation followed by another" (`peirce1931papers`, CP 1.345, vol. 1 p. 176). Genuine giving "consists in A's making C the possessor according to Law" and needs no material transfer (CP 8.331); "Giving is a transfer of the right of property" (CP 1.345). Headcount does not decide (ARGUMENT.md C1b).

**What it separates.** It separates the author's "pretend to be triads" from the headcount reading. Peirce's test lies in the binding, not in the count of parties, so the essay should not rest anything on "two parties versus three". It does not separate a hedge from a bet: both are transfers "according to Law", and nothing in the Peirce cards speaks of wagers, hedges or markets. **[inference]** The one Peircean phrase that fits the author's thesis is "externally appended": on a sports contract the decision frame is appended to a bet whose settlement does not involve it. Use the phrase as an analogy, with the card locus, and do not write "A is determined by B and C together" in quotation marks; the lab's card records that no such sentence exists in Peirce (`peirce1931papers`, Notes).

## 3. Where the distinction is clean and where it strains

### Clean

The author's diagnosis survives on three of the four criteria that have sources behind them. A sports contract between two retail users is a difference contract with no indemnity on either side (Stout), a created risk (Lockhart; Ninth Circuit), a market whose only funder is the trader paying a tax (Hanson claim 17), and a venue whose fee is set by how evenly the two sides disagree (Simmel's *tertius gaudens*, by my mapping). Kalshi's own record supplies the premise that no one hedges a game: the 2024 appellate brief, the *Hendrick* concessions, the confidential sports appendices (M A5), the one sports-hedging example being a sportsbook hedging its own book (M A2), and Macey and Enriques' "A prediction market contract is a side bet" (L `macey2026` p. 30). The hedge the author sets against it meets a need at the fill. The contrast is real and well sourced. It is a contrast between a transfer and a wager, and the law has drawn it since *Irwin v. Williar* (1884).

### Strain 1. "The bet factors" is true of the settlement and false of the price

The price both traders face is jointly produced by every order in the book. That is joint determination in the lab's sense, and ARGUMENT.md P39 names a posted price as an instance of the algorithmacy form. So the sentence "prediction markets pretend to be triads when they are really dyads" is false under the lab's own criterion: the market is a triad, and the lab's talk says so.

**[inference]** The split that rescues the author is between two stages of one trade. At the fill, the engine commits terms for both sides; the price those terms carry is set by everyone. At settlement, the payoff is a function of the game alone, A's payoff is one minus B's, and neither reads the other. The first stage has the control's shape; the second is a relay from an outside event, which is literacy's shape in the lab's vocabulary (P40) and "one dyadic relation followed by another" in Peirce's (CP 1.345). For a hedger the two stages coincide: the fill is the outcome, because the extant exposure makes the net position insensitive to the event. For a bettor they come apart: the fill is a ticket and the outcome arrives later. This gives Part 2's definition something to hold onto. The engine "commits an outcome binding on both" in the hedge; in the bet it commits only the terms, and the outcome is committed by the game.

The Part 3 thesis already contains this split. "The market computes a probability, and the interface asks the user to trade" is the same sentence as "the price stage is coordination and the settlement stage is a bet". The essay should write it that way and retire "really dyads".

### Strain 2. Liquidity is a need, wherever a hedger exists

Mansour's A4 is sound as far as it goes: "a robust market depends in part on the liquidity provided by speculators" (SJ memo p. 5, facet M); Kalshi's brief: "speculators provide necessary liquidity that hedgers cannot" (p. 56). In a grain book the speculator meets the farmer's need for a counterparty at planting, and Brown and Yang show participation raising forecast accuracy in a quasi-experiment (X line 3). So the author cannot say "speculation is not a triadic activity" without qualification. A speculator facing a hedger is the hedger's coordination partner, and is paid for it.

What the author can say is narrower and holds. In a book with no hedgers, the need that liquidity serves is the other speculator's wish to bet, and the maker's wish to be hit by a less informed taker: Bürgi, Deng and Whelan describe makers as "relatively well-informed traders who post offers" and takers as those who "accept these offers", with makers at −9.64% and takers at −31.46% (X `burgi2026makers`; ARGUMENT_MAP marks the figures checked). Lockhart's test puts this liquidity on the created-risk side: the need did not exist until the market did. Facet M's own conclusion is the one the essay should carry: A4 "cannot distinguish a hedging market with speculators from a speculation market with no hedgers; that requires A2/A3 evidence", and for sports that evidence is Kalshi's confidential appendix.

### Strain 3. The non-trading reader makes the system triadic only when the reader is bound

Hanson's legislature reads the price and votes (claim 5). Fed staff read Kalshi's rate distributions and find them as accurate as the New York Fed survey at 150 days (X `diercks2026kalshi`). Does a reader who does not trade make the system triadic at a higher level?

**[inference]** Two cases, and the lab's own distinction between a branch and a whole decides them (ARGUMENT.md C6, P40 "Broadcast"). A reader whose use does not feed back is a branch: the price is read by traders and by the reader, and nothing the reader does changes the price or the settlement. The lab classes this as a fan-out, a three-term fact on the effect side in a system that factors, and under D3 "no party is jointly determined". Fed staff reading Kalshi are this case as documented; Diercks, Katz and Wright write staff research under a disclaimer, and no source shows the FOMC acting on it (facet M B5 entry on the FEDS paper). A reader who is bound closes the loop: the sponsor's decision is set by the price, the settlement is set by the decision, and the traders bid knowing it. That is Hanson's second funder (claim 11) and it is the control's shape. So Hanson's "missing funder" and the lab's "missing joint determination" are one gap seen from two sides. The essay can say this without Φ: a prediction market becomes a decision market when someone has tied their hands to the number, and neither platform has such a person.

This also answers the public-good concession (X line 4, ARGUMENT_MAP X4). The price has a public life; the system is still not a decision market, because the public reads and does not bind.

### Strain 4. "Zero-sum" is the wrong word twice over

Among the bettors the game is not zero-sum but negative-sum: Stout's "when HE trading involves transaction costs, speculators' average returns become negative" (p. 745), and on Kalshi the average per-contract return is about −20% (X `burgi2026makers`). The venue is the residual claimant on the fee. And at the level of the whole system the word fails the other way: the price is a positive externality that non-traders consume for free, which is Hanson's complaint (claim 17: "all the information that it's produced is a side effect that everybody can get for free") and the counter-case's strongest line (X4).

**[inference]** The hedge is also zero-sum in cash. The importer's gain on the forward is the bank's loss on it. What makes the hedge positive-sum is the importer's extant exposure, which is counted in welfare and not in the contract's cash flows. So the author's "it can't be through a zero-sum model" needs two repairs. Write "negative-sum for the people trading, with the venue taking the difference" for the bettors, and write "positive-sum once each side's prior exposure is counted" for the hedgers. Then meet the externality head-on with X5: stakes are not what makes the number good. Play money matched real money on NFL games (X `servanschreiber2004prediction`, mean absolute error 0.439 against 0.436), and the same traders' stated beliefs scored at least as well as their own market's prices (X `dana2019are`, Brier 0.210 against 0.227). The public good does not need the negative-sum bet to produce it.

### Strain 5. Recreation is a need, and the criterion cannot exclude it

Hanson classes sports betting under entertainment (claim 15: "sports betting markets enhance entertainment from watching sporting events"), lists "the fun of action and proving yourself in competition" as one of three market functions (claim 31), and does not mind it (claim 29). Two friends who each want action on the game have a pre-existing need that the other's stake meets, and the bet is positive-sum in enjoyment the way a poker night is. Criterion (iii) does not exclude this, and if the author applies it as if it did, that is the special pleading the brief warns against.

What rescues the dark-pattern charge is the frame, not the structure. **[inference]** The charge is not "a dyad presented as a triad" but "a wager presented as a decision instrument". The evidence for the presentation is in the record: the word "bet" does not appear in Kalshi's four introductory help articles (facet D l. 74) while the Ninth Circuit records Kalshi advertising "the first app for legal sports betting in all 50 states" (L `ca9_2026assad` p. 10); Kalshi's help centre says "The contract price for a particular event is directly tied to this probability" (D `kalshi2026help_prices`); Mansour says "The charts are really the key component of our experience" (M C6). A platform that presented itself as betting would escape this charge and fall under gambling-design ethics (facet B), which is a different essay. The author should say so.

### Strain 6. Part 2's obligation does not reach a voluntary venue

Part 2 grounds the designer's debt in mandatory participation: "Where participation is mandatory, a design that offers disclosure without predictability, a channel for intent, and standing fails the person it mediates" (`part2_ethics/DRAFT.md` l. 101; "mandatory participation" defined at l. 27). Nobody must use Kalshi. Exit is free and in-play (Sottile ¶33). **[inference]** Part 3 therefore cannot inherit Part 2's obligation as stated; its DXD criteria must come from Part 1 (the declared envelope, scaffolding, counter-delegation, structural refusal, bounded outputs; OUTLINE.md section 10) and from Part 1's dark-pattern line (l. 40, Gray et al. 2018; Mathur et al. 2019), not from the mandatory-participation argument. The author should check the outline's "by the criteria in Parts 1 and 2" against this.

### Strain 7. "Really dyads" is a reduction claim, and the talk argues that reductions mint a third

The ALGOCON argument's own move against Löwenheim and Quine is that every reduction of a triad to pairs mints an object that stands for the triple: Quine's universe of Θ′ "is to comprise all objects of the universe of Θ and, in addition, {x, y} for every x and y" (`quine1954reduction` p. 180; ARGUMENT.md C3b), and "In coordination, the minted object is a third party. The reduction therefore moves the third; it does not remove it" (C3b). **[inference]** The author's "really dyads" is a reduction of the same kind. To describe two Kalshi users as a pair, one has to posit the price they both face, and the price is the third. The essay should not make a claim in Part 3 that the author's talk, three weeks later, says cannot be made. Quine must not be quoted as against Peirce (CLAIM.md "Do not claim").

### Strain 8. The lab's results are not about markets

Every (ii) verdict above is my reading of Boolean forms onto institutions. The lab's own scope statement forbids more: "The forms are Boolean models of three and four binary elements. They are not people. No worker, platform or rule from the field is measured" (ARGUMENT.md P46), and CLAIM.md lists "That the lab shows any worker, platform or real rule is triadic" under "Do not claim". In public prose the essay may use "set by both at once" and "comes apart" as the author's analogy, with no Φ and no claim of a lab result.

## 4. Decision versus choice in the series

**What Parts 1 and 2 say.** Part 1 does not define "decision" against "choice" as terms; it contrasts disciplines. Choice architecture has "an identifiable human designer deploying a fixed interface or default menu to steer an isolated chooser" (l. 42) and "universally assumes an isolated actor facing an isolated display" (l. 48); DXD "must address situations where choices are brokered through an adaptive, opaque algorithmic intermediary that reads multiple parties simultaneously" (l. 34). The unit shifts "from the task to the decision itself" (l. 22), and the cognitive-systems principle is "the unit of analysis is the decision, not the screen" (l. 58). The outcome measure is "joint welfare outcomes across both parties in a coordination" (l. 168). Part 1 uses "choice" freely for the thing brokered ("human choice", l. 18; "choices are brokered", l. 34), so the published text does not reserve "decision" for the triadic case.

Part 2 sharpens it. Informed choice is to "let an informed chooser decide" (l. 15); the chooser is the person whose options are arranged (l. 33, l. 35). The jargon table rules: "Choice architecture arranges the options a chooser faces; decision experience design designs the conditions — predictability, channels for intent, scaffolding — under which a person decides through an intermediary and learns to do so better (Part 1)" (`part2_ethics/OUTLINE.md` l. 64). The pitch put it as "being given inert information so you can make a choice" against "being guided through an active decision experience" (`PITCH.md` l. 127).

**What the author adds in Part 3.** "A decision, as opposed to a choice, has to consider real triadic coordination." That is consistent with Parts 1 and 2 and new as a definition. Neither published Part 1 nor drafted Part 2 says that a decision differs from a choice in what it must take account of; they say DXD differs from choice architecture in what it designs.

**Why Part 3 needs it.** A sports bet is a choice made through an intermediary that reads both sides and binds both on terms, so by Part 1 l. 34 it is squarely DXD's object. The author needs the decision/choice line to say the bet is a choice and not a decision. **[inference]** The line the sources support: a *decision*, in the series' sense, is an act whose quality is judged by the joint outcome of the parties the intermediary coordinates and whose outcome the intermediary commits; a *choice* is a selection among arranged options whose joint outcome is fixed before the selection is made. A bettor's pick is a choice: the joint outcome of the two bettors is fixed at zero less fees, and the intermediary commits only the terms. A hedger's fill is a decision in this sense: the joint outcome is positive and committed at the fill. Define this once in Part 3, flag it as sharpening Part 1's usage, and add it to the jargon table.

## 5. Recommendation

### The criterion

Adopt the needs-at-the-fill criterion, stated for a general reader in three sentences:

> A trade is coordination when each side leaves with what it came for the moment the intermediary matches them, whatever happens next. The importer has its rate, the farmer has its price, and the bank or the speculator who took the other side has its spread. A trade is a bet when what each side came for is decided later, by an event neither of them controls, and only one of them can have it.

Then the thesis sentence: *a prediction market's screen is built for the second kind of trade while its name promises the first.*

This criterion has the sources (Stout, Lockhart, the Ninth and Sixth Circuits, Kalshi's own concessions, Macey and Enriques, the CFTC's 2012 order and its 2026 reversal), it separates every case in the table the way the author wants, and it needs no triad/dyad count. Keep Simmel's mediator and *tertius gaudens* as the picture of what the venue is doing (one slide's worth), and keep Hanson's bound decision-maker as the picture of what a decision market would be.

### What adopting it commits the essay to conceding

1. **Speculator-to-speculator currency trading is also a bet.** The Forex contrast must be about hedgers in the book. "Those who participate normally are always getting what they need" becomes "those who hedge get what they need at the fill; those who speculate against each other are betting, and the market is positive-sum because hedgers are in it." Mansour's own words agree that FX and grain are "very tilted towards speculation" (M A4).
2. **The matching engine is a third that reads both and binds both.** Part 2's definition stands and the exchange passes it. "Pretend to be triads when they are really dyads" goes. What the engine commits in a bet is the terms; the outcome is committed by the game.
3. **A market price is triadic in the lab's own sense** (ARGUMENT.md P39). The dyad is the bet, not the market. The author's talk and the author's essay must not contradict each other on this three weeks apart.
4. **The hedge in its plainest form is a dyad.** An importer and a bank in a forward are two parties. The criterion is about extant risk and not about headcount, so the essay drops "triad versus dyad" as the organising contrast or confines it to Simmel's gradient of thirds.
5. **Grain futures pass the criterion, and that is Mansour's analogy.** The essay grants grain and locates the difference in the absence of a hedger on sports, citing Kalshi's 2024 brief (M A2, L `dmd2025martin` p. 16), the *Hendrick* concessions (L `ca6_2026schuler` p. 27), the confidential appendices (M A5), and Macey and Enriques (L p. 30), with the caveat that the sports risk-mitigation analysis is unpublished rather than refuted.
6. **Liquidity is a real need wherever a hedger exists.** The essay denies it only for hedger-free books, and says that on Kalshi the liquidity relation runs from takers to makers at −31.46% against −9.64% (X `burgi2026makers`).
7. **"Zero-sum" becomes "negative-sum among the bettors, with the venue taking the fee", and the price is conceded as a public good** (X4; Diercks et al.). The reply is X5: play money matched real money on NFL games and stated beliefs matched prices, so the public good does not require the bet.
8. **Recreation is a legitimate need and the criterion does not exclude it.** The dark pattern is a wager presented as a decision instrument, not a dyad presented as a triad. A venue that presented itself as betting would fall outside Part 3 and inside facet B's gambling-design ethics.
9. **Hanson's decision market passes the criterion.** The essay says the platforms fail a test Hanson's own design passes, and does not put "sports betting in disguise" in his mouth (H claim 34; ARGUMENT_MAP H10).
10. **Part 2's mandatory-participation obligation does not reach Kalshi.** Part 3's DXD criteria come from Part 1's design principles and its dark-pattern line.
11. **The decision/choice distinction is new in Part 3.** Define it once and mark it as sharpening Part 1.
12. **Nothing from the lab's Boolean results is evidence about a market.** "Set by both at once" and "comes apart" appear as the author's analogy, without Φ and without a claim of a lab verdict (ARGUMENT.md P46; CLAIM.md "Do not claim").

### What the essay may then hold without qualification

The gap it names is unoccupied. Facet L finds that "no legal source addresses the interface" and that the courts "treat [the screen] as evidence of gambling; none asks whether a different interface would change the category" (L (iv) "Refutes"). Hanson left "user interfaces" as "the main category of issues not dealt with" (H claim 38). Mansour says the chart is the product (M C6), and Sah et al. find r/Kalshi users struggle to read it (X line 7). The criterion above tells the essay where to stand: the screen asks for a bet; the number it displays was produced by coordination; and the person who wanted the number is sent through the bet to get it.
