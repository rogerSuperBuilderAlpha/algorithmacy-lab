# D. Platform facts and primary sources

*Facet D of the Decision Experience Design (DXD) Part 3 literature review. Agent-drafted on 2026-10-03 and rewritten the same day in a local session with network access. The author has not reviewed it, and none of the text below is the author's.*

**What this facet covers.** How Kalshi and Polymarket present markets (D1), how contracts resolve and what documented disputes show (D2), and the volume mix, regulatory status and partnerships that shape the interface (D3). The first pass on 2026-10-03 ran in a container whose egress proxy blocked every platform, regulator and court host; it left D1 empty and every platform claim as a `[verify]` lead from search-result summaries. This rewrite opened the primary sources: both platforms' help centres and developer docs, Kalshi's public Trade API and contract-terms PDFs, Polymarket's Gamma API, Polymarket US's rulebook and CFTC fee filings, CFTC press releases, orders and self-certification filings, and the three circuit-court opinions. Every entry below carries the source URL, the date on the source where one is shown, the access date (2026-10-03 unless stated), and a Wayback URL where I found a snapshot. Bib keys are in `D_platform_facts.bib`, which also records a tier for each source: **P1** platform's own page, API or filing; **P2** regulator or court record; **S1** named data provider or reputable press that names its data source; **S2** trade press; **A** academic.

**Still blocked or unreachable this session.** kalshi.com itself (HTTP 429 to every request, including its fee-schedule PDF; the Wayback copy of that PDF from 2026-09-21 is a Vercel bot-check page, not the schedule), The Block (403), SSRN (403), CourtListener (403), federalregister.gov (redirects to an "unblock" page), the WSJ article on UMA disputes (paywall), gamingamerica.com (403), theblock.co articles reached once then 403. Where a fact rests on one of these I say so.

**Legend.** *Read* = page or document opened and quoted. *API* = JSON retrieved from the platform's public endpoint. *Secondary* = a press or dashboard page opened and quoted, labelled S1 or S2. *Not opened* = seen only in a search-result summary. Bearing on the thesis (sports betting dressed as a decision market; the dress is the design failure) is marked **supports**, **complicates** or **refutes**.

**kalshi2026help_fees**; **kalshi2022cftc_fee**; **kalshi2026apidocs** — Fees article (updated 2026-04-19); CFTC filing of 2022-09-09 (effective 2022-09-22) with the schedule exhibit; docs.kalshi.com "Fee Rounding". *Read*. P1/P2.
- The current schedule PDF (`https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf`) could not be opened (HTTP 429; Wayback copy is a bot-check page). The help article gives no formula; it says maker fees apply on "some markets ... often due to special events (such as the election, awards ceremony, or a large sporting championship)".
- The CFTC-filed schedule of September 2022 states: "fees = round up(0.07 x C x P x (1-P)) / P = the price of a contract in dollars (50 cents is 0.5) / C = the number of contracts being traded / round up = rounds to the next cent", with a lower coefficient of 0.035 for indices markets, and "There is no settlement fee. There is no processing fee. There is no membership fee."
- The API marks every series I inspected `"fee_type": "quadratic", "fee_multiplier": 1` (e.g. KXKHAMENEIOUT and the sports series KXSAUDIPLADVANCE), and the docs give rounding of the trade fee "up to the nearest $0.000001" for direct members and to the cent for non-direct members, with a per-order rounding accumulator.
- *Bearing:* **supports** the fee-as-design point. Peak fee at P = 0.5 is 0.0175 per contract under the 2022 formula. Whether 0.07 is still the general coefficient in October 2026 is not confirmed from a 2026 document; the API's "quadratic" marker and the help centre's wording are consistent with it.

Caveat: the 30-day measure attributes a long-lived market's whole lifetime volume to its settlement month, which overweights long-horizon (non-sports) categories, so it is a conservative lower bound on the sports share.

Gamma covers the main venue only; Polymarket US is a separate book.

- Revisions made while running (recorded so the method can be judged): (i) a 30-day pass over Kalshi's settled markets was infeasible, because combo markets settle by the thousand per minute and almost all carry zero volume; the final script `kalshi_share2.py` uses `mve_filter=exclude` for singles and a capped `mve_filter=only` pass for combos.
