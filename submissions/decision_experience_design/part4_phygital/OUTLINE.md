## (a) Thesis

The author's statement (2026-10-04): decision experience design is not only a software or digital concept.
The essay reviews physical decision experience design, starting from the issues raised by researchers who
study physical/digital hybrid experience — the phygital.

The run tested four forms of this thesis against fifteen arrangements (`README.md`; `T_adjudication.md`).
The result, before the author rules:

- **Form 1** (the algorithmic intermediary reaches into physical space) holds at full strength for
  ride-hail and, with a human at the point of commitment, for the airport gate.
- **Form 2** (a physical or human arrangement is itself an intermediary) has no case with all four marks.
  Akrich's electricity meter has two. The human cases depend on a ruling the author has not made.
- **Form 3** (physical design is only choice architecture) is the default verdict and covers most of the
  physical and phygital design the facets document.
- **Form 4** (the physical layer is where the ruling becomes a fact and exit is priced) has the most
  evidence, and holds equally for arrangements with no algorithm in them.

### 1. Opening: one decision point, two bodies

  `cameron2024making` p. 486 [checked]. `mohlmann2021algorithmic` pp. 2007–2010 [checked].
- Contrast: a cafeteria line arranged to put fruit first. No one is read; one rule serves everyone.
  `thaler2008nudge` pp. 1–3 [checked].
- The essay's question: which physical arrangements belong to decision experience design, and which
  belong to its predecessors? [none — the essay's own framing]

### 2. What phygital researchers say

- "Phygital" entered scholarship from marketing practice and its theory is thin by its own reviewers'
  account: "the term phygital lacks a clear definition"; "Vagueness in using the term characterises most
  of the studies". `mele2023phygital` §§1, 3.1 [checked].
- Batat's framework treats phygital as the customer's movement between physical and digital settings,
  to be made fluid. `batat2024phcx` [abstract]. **Cannot enter a draft until the paper is read.**
- The field's ideal is the seamless journey. 118 works join "phygital" to "seamless" and one joins it to
  "seamful". Facet D gap search [full; a database count, not a reading].
- The problems phygital researchers themselves name:
  - Captivity: "coerced participation in systems that have become unavoidable".
    `wilsonnash2026captivity` §2.1 [checked].
  - "algorithmic mediation of agency" and "digitally enforced rules". `shabnam2026tpsr` [full;
    Emerald version of record, read by facet A in the browser, not re-opened by me].
  - Friction as defect, and its defenders. Facet A synthesis A3 [full].
  - The employee's role; customer and employee "rarely examined together". Facet A, Mulcahy et al. 2026
    [abstract].
- What the field does not do: no phygital paper located models an outcome committed for two parties at
  once. Facet A, gap searches logged [full for the papers read; 27 of 45 entries are abstract only].

### 3. The predecessor: designed physical environments

- Kotler names atmospherics as the conscious design of buying environments. `kotler1973atmospherics`
  pp. 48–64 [checked for the words; the record has no Crossref entry].
- Bitner's servicescape has two users, customers and employees, whose needs the designer settles in
  advance. `bitner1992servicescapes` pp. 58, 61 [checked for the words; pages are the facet's].
- Physical nudges keep a fixed arrangement for everyone. The field's own typology defines them as "not
  designed to be interactive or tailored to specific individuals" and excludes the system that reads
  purchases. `hollands2017tippme`, "Focus" [checked].
- So the predecessor draws the boundary at tailoring and interaction. That is where the series' fourth
  mark sits. [none — inference from the row above; T section 3, form 3]
- **Collision with Part 1.** Part 1 l. 48 says choice architecture has "a visible human designer".
  Thaler and Sunstein say most architects act "without realizing it" and "some nudges are unintentional".
  `thaler2008nudge` pp. 3, 10 [checked]. The author decides: see (f) 7.

### 4. Things that bind: scripts, and one two-party device

- Latour's door-closer, seat belt and speed bump bind their user on behalf of an absent designer. They
  read no one and hold still. `latour1992missing`, 1992 pages 225–258 [checked for the words].
- Akrich's electricity meter binds producer and consumer: "il faut « l'accord » des deux pour le faire
  tourner". `akrich1987comment` §26 [checked]. This is a pre-digital device with two of the four marks.
- Do not repeat the Moses bridges story. `joerges1999politics` [full]. Akrich 1987 n. 1, Latour 1988
  p. 302 and Lessig 2006 p. 128 each repeat it (facet C [full]; Latour 1988 not confirmed by me).
- Joerges's objection stands against a strong form 4: built form rarely binds; legal control does.
  `joerges1999politics` p. 20 [full].
- This section repairs a debt from Part 2, which cited Latour and Akrich unread. Verbeek's books remain
  unread (facet C, not obtained).

### 5. Computing in the room: what HCI already knew

- Weiser's ubiquitous computing; his own slides ask for "seamful systems, with beautiful seams".
  `weiser1994invisible` slide 19 [checked].
- Chalmers: seams are technical limits users exploit, as when players used GPS shadows in a city game.
  `chalmers2003wearable`; `chalmersgalani2004seamful` [full].
- Bell and Dourish call the seamless ideal "a chimera". `belldourish2007yesterdays` [checked].
- Phygital seamlessness repeats the position this literature argued against, without citing it. Facet D
  synthesis [full, with the database count above].
- Suchman on what a machine can read of a person's situation, and her preference for engineering repair
  over disclosure. `suchman1985plans` p. 124 [full]. A precedent for "algorithmacy is not legibility".
- **Collision with Part 1.** Part 1 l. 152 glosses seamful design as "allowing users to understand its
  limits". That fits Ehsan 2024, not Chalmers. The author decides: see (f) 7.

### 7. What physical decision experience design is, and is not

Depends on the rulings in (f). On the route this outline takes:

- Most physical design is the predecessor's subject and stays there. [T section 3, form 3]
- Physical DXD exists where an intermediary reads and commits for two parties who then meet the outcome
  with their bodies, during the task. Wood et al. distinguish control during the work from control at the
  end of it. `wood2019good` p. 64 [checked for the copy; locator is the facet's].
- The physical layer is where Part 1's principles are kept or defeated: whether exit has a price
  (`dhs2025biometricrule` [T1, partial read]), whether the envelope is posted and kept (`gao2022facialtestimony`
  [T3, partial read]), whether failure is conspicuous (the Amazon cart, G [checked]), and whether a person
  is present to answer (T section 3, form 4 [none — inference]).
- The essay should say that form 4 holds for arrangements with no algorithm, and why it is still a DXD
  claim. [none — T section 4, strain 6]

### 8. Design implications

- Carry Part 1's three principles into the physical layer with one case each: a priced versus an unpriced
  opt-out (airport); a posted rule that is kept (SFpark); a commitment the person can see accruing
  (the cart receipt).
- Two gaps stated as gaps: no study measures a competence moderating outcomes in a physical setting; no
  tool acts for the person at the point of delivery. Facet E, E5 [full, searches logged].
- Do not offer signage or explanation as the remedy (author ruling).

### 9. Propositions and limitations

Candidate propositions, each refutable:

1. In physical services, arrangements that read the person and adapt produce more reports of being unable
   to leave than fixed arrangements with the same exit cost. [none]
2. A posted, kept operating rule (SFpark's form) produces more accurate predictions by users than an
   unposted adaptive one, at the same price level. [none]
3. Where a human is present to answer for an algorithmic ruling at the physical point of commitment,
   refusals are resolved without loss of the service more often. [none]
4. Phygital studies that examine customer and employee together will find outcomes committed for both
   at once in a minority of settings. [none]

Limitations to state: the Batat frameworks unread; the shelf-label and Singapore cases provisional; the
service-research papers that model technology as a third party (Larivière et al. 2017; van Doorn et al.
2017) not obtained; Schüll's book chapters and Dourish 2001 not obtained; the central static/adaptive
contrast already appears in Barlow and Johnson 2026, read at abstract level.

## (f) Open questions for the author

In the order in which each constrains the next. Options and what each lets Part 4 claim are in
`T_adjudication.md` section 6.

1. **Does a human reader count as the intermediary?** If yes, form 2 gains the maître d' and the immigration
   officer, and Part 1's boundary needs rewriting. If no, form 2 has one two-mark case. A middle rule: a
   human counts when reading from a screen the party cannot see.
2. **Must the intermediary be independent of the provider?** A strict reading leaves two physical cases
   and also disqualifies Part 1's own feed and e-commerce examples.
3. **What counts as "does not hold still"?** Per person, at run time, unannounced, or tailored and
   interactive (the predecessor's own line). A threshold that excludes SFpark excludes the case that best
   fits Part 1's declared-envelope principle.
4. **Is form 4 a claim about DXD or about physical design?** As a DXD claim it needs the clause about who
   is present to answer.
5. **Does reading a party's record (a manifest, an order) count as reading the party?**
6. **Is the test at the match part of what makes a case DXD, or a separate test on exchanges?**
7. **Which sentences in the live Part 1 will Part 4 amend, restate or leave?** The run found four that the
   sources do not support as worded: "visible human designer" (l. 48); the gloss of seamful design
   (l. 152); "Traditional media hold still" without a threshold (l. 118); "costless exit… collapsing"
   read at the level of an option (l. 146). With the dark-pattern sentence (l. 40) and the Eslami claim
   already open from Parts 2 and 3, that makes six.
8. **Can the author supply the two Batat papers** (*Journal of Strategic Marketing* 32(8); *Journal of
   Services Marketing* 40(4))? Section 2 cannot be written from abstracts. Library copies of Schüll 2012,
   Akrich 1992, Dourish 2001, Larivière et al. 2017 and van Doorn et al. 2017 would close the rest.
9. **How should Part 4 treat Barlow and Johnson 2026?** Cite it as the source of the contrast, or read it
