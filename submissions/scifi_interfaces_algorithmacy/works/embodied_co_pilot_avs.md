# Embodied co-pilot (AVs and human-agent teaming)

**Kind.** Emergent paradigm (sci-fi trope + contemporary semi-autonomous vehicles / HAT).

**Canonical references.** Semi-autonomous vehicles; Human-Agent Teaming (HAT) literature; sci-fi “read the mind of the machine you are inside.”

**Seed reading.** Drawn from [`../essay.md`](../essay.md) § “The Embodied Co-Pilot.”

## Interface paradigm

The algorithmic third party is embodied in the vehicle and sensor stack, not a screen or implant alone. Control shifts dynamically between human and machine. Unexpected braking or hesitation surfaces opacity at speed.

## Algorithmacy / triadic coordination

**Collaborative** (seed framing): human, algorithmic vehicle, and physical environment co-manage navigation. Stakes are safety and trust calibration. Folk theories fill the black box so the driver can decide whether to intervene. (Whether “environment” counts as a party or as world is an open modeling choice — see alternatives.)

## Cognitive integration

HAT handoffs; acute need to construct explanations under time pressure. Anthropomorphism (“the car sees / hesitates / is overwhelmed”) is the everyday theory-building of embodied algorithmacy.

## Historical analogy

Navigating complex routes with a black-boxed astrolabe: instrument mediates world-reading; opacity of the instrument is part of the skill.

## Modes of resistance

Manual override; developing and sharing folk theories; refusing automation levels that erase intervention rights.

### Dyad with a tool, not a triad
**Author:** seed contrast (import) · **Date:** 2026-09-20

A contrasting take: the AV is a tool in a driver–world dyad. Algorithmacy language overfits unless a second human (fleet dispatcher, remote safety operator, insurer) is coordinated through the same stack. Folk theories then look like ordinary tool-use literacy under uncertainty.

### Engagement layer as development-as-product
**Author:** Roger Hunt (drafted by agent) · **Date:** 2026-09-21

Under the tool reading the driver uses a finished vehicle; under the collaborative reading the driver and vehicle co-manage a road. The engagement-layer reading adds that every handoff, override, and folk theory is continuous co-development of the shared policy: an intervention on one car becomes training data for the next driver’s car, so the fleet the next driver meets is the sediment of prior engagement. There is no finished vehicle to use. The handoff interface is the development interface, and designing it as a product surface — smooth, explanatory, reassuring — hides the fact that the driver is a contributor to a policy shared with strangers.
