# Omniscient curator (recommender oracles)

**Kind.** Emergent paradigm (sci-fi trope + contemporary recommenders / matchmakers).

**Canonical references.** Invisible AI as matchmaker, career counselor, media filter; Spotify / TikTok / dating-app recommenders as near-term analogues; folk theories of algorithms as social being vs trainable machine.

**Seed reading.** Drawn from [`../essay.md`](../essay.md) § “The Omniscient Curator.”

## Interface paradigm

No developer console for the ordinary user. The algorithm operates in the background, filtering which profiles, songs, news, or opportunities appear. Engagement is behavioral: swipe, linger, click patterns as training signals.

## Algorithmacy / triadic coordination

**Curated.** The algorithm decides which humans are permitted to interact. Coordination between people is gated by an unexplainable third party. Competency is less coding than performative negotiation with an inscrutable oracle (“algorithmic pairing”).

## Cognitive integration

Opaque interaction driven by folk theories and intuition. Users cast the system as a punishing/rewarding social being or as a machine that can be strategically trained. Developer experience is absent from the user side.

## Historical analogy

Ancient oracles: interpreting ambiguous signs, propitiating an authority that speaks in filtered outcomes rather than explanations.

## Modes of resistance

Data obfuscation; strategic engagement patterns to “teach” or confuse the oracle; algospeak and off-platform channels when curation hardens into moderation (see [`algospeak_resistance.md`](algospeak_resistance.md)).

### Curator as market, not mediator
**Author:** seed contrast (import) · **Date:** 2026-09-20

A contrasting take: recommenders are matching markets with ranking rules, continuous with search and advertising, not a novel triadic competency. Folk theories are consumer superstition around a dyadic platform–user relation; the “other human” never enters a joint determination with the user through the system.
