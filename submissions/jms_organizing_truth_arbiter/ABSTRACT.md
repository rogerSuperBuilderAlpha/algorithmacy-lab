**Title:** Truth by Arbitration: How Evaluating Platforms Settle What Happened Between Two Parties

## Abstract
When a hotel and a guest disagree about a stay, or a seller and a buyer about a sale, whose account becomes the fact? On platforms, the answer is increasingly the system's. Its review, rating and ranking record settles what happened, and it displaces older apparatuses of evaluation such as inspection and accreditation. We theorize this as truth by arbitration. The system's record is produced under conditions neither party can inspect. Its rules are withheld, its information asymmetries are designed, and each party shapes its report in anticipation of the other and of the filter. The parties' own accounts carry no comparable authority, so a disputed verdict becomes a blame game between parties who cannot make the arbiter answer. Building on research on evaluative apparatuses and algorithmic control, we argue that the only counter-record available to the evaluated parties is the shared understanding they build between themselves outside the system's record. We specify how counter-records are made, kept and asserted, and when platforms admit them. We contribute an account of organizational truth in which authority over facts rests with an arbiter rather than with experts or stakeholders, and we ask what arbitration does when the parties are already polarized.

**Keywords:** algorithmic management; arbitration; evaluation; platforms; shared understanding; truth
