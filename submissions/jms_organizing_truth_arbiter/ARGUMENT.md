# Argument: the arbiter's record as organizational truth

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Angle sharpened against the OT paper (see FLAGS 2).]

**P1.** When a platform evaluates the joint conduct of two parties, its record of what happened becomes the account that counts. Ratings and rankings displaced older apparatuses of evaluation, and the parties' own accounts carry no comparable weight.
- *Sources:*
  - `hospitality_phygital/library/cards/scottorlikowski2012accountability.md` (ratings subvert hierarchical accountability)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/orlikowskiscott2023.md` (p. 1: "displacing institutional apparatuses from their positions of primacy and authority"; p. 3)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/leonardileavell2026.md` (p. 537: stakeholders "treated the outputs as binding forecasts")

**P2.** That record is produced under conditions the parties cannot inspect. Information asymmetries are deliberately created, norms are hidden, and the parties shape their own reports in anticipation of each other and of the filter.
- *Sources:*
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/kellogg2020algorithms.md` (p. 384)
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/rahman2019invisible.md` (abstract, p. v)
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/bolton2013engineering.md` (reciprocity in feedback; discussion-paper version)
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/eslami2019user.md` (p. 11)

**C.** Platforms organize truth by arbitration. The system settles which account of a joint event is fact. The only counter-record available to the parties is a shared understanding they build between themselves, outside the system's record. Organizational research on truth should therefore study how evaluated parties build, keep and assert such counter-records, and when platforms admit them.
