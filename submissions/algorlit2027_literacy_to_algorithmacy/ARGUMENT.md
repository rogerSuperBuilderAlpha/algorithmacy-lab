# Argument: from algorithmic literacy to algorithmacy

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. For the author's ruling.]

**P1.** Algorithmic literacy is defined for a person facing a system. Its dimensions (awareness, knowledge, critical evaluation, coping, creation) describe what one user knows and does about an algorithm. The field's two reviews find no cohesive construct and no human counterpart in the relation studied.
- *Sources:*
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/dogruel2021.md` (four dimensions)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/devito2021.md` (definition)
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/oeldorfhirsch2023what.md` (journal pagination; p. 693)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/gagrcin2026.md` (169 studies; no cohesive framework)

**P2.** The history of literacy shows that a competence named after the medium a person reads tells us little about the social relation the medium sets up. Literacy campaigns promised individual advancement that the social order did not deliver, and AI-literacy initiatives repeat that promise.
- *Sources:*
  - `berthon_ethics_course/literature/cards/graff1991literacy.md` (pp. 2, 321)
  - `berthon_ethics_course/literature/cards/kamperman2026ai.md` (pp. 35–36)
  - `berthon_ethics_course/literature/cards/ong1982orality.md` (p. 133)

**C.** Where an algorithm stands between two people and evaluates both, the competence that matters is not literacy about the algorithm. It is algorithmacy: the competence to coordinate with the other person through it. Algorithmic-literacy research should add the counterpart to its object, and literacy programmes should stop promising that knowledge of the system will deliver standing.
