# Spaces in the Stream: Algorithmacy and the Perception of Decision Spaces

Roger Hunt · one-page outline · theory article for *Human–Computer Interaction* · full draft about 22,000 words

**Argument.** LLM interfaces stream their output as text and so cast the supervisor as a reader: a digital *scriptio continua*. Platforms now pause the stream for plan approval, permission prompts and menus, but they place those stops for the platform's intent, which is to keep the work moving. I propose *algorithmacy*, the competence to navigate, evaluate and steer opaque, co-optive, triadic intermediaries. Its perceptual layer depends on marking *decision spaces*: points in the process where human intervention is functionally necessary to control intent.

**1. Introduction.** The literacy trap: proofreading the stream, then approving a plan the system wrote. The *scriptio continua* analogy and its limits. Scope is the perceptual layer only; marking is necessary but not sufficient. Gap: I found no framework that marks decision spaces inside the generative stream.

**2. Foundations.** Word spaces as cognitive offloading (Saenger; Johnson; Rayner et al., 1998); regressions repair comprehension (Booth & Weger; Schotter et al.); abrupt onsets and priority maps (Yantis & Jonides; Wolfe); event segmentation (Zacks et al., 2009); text as a stable object (O'Regan & Noë; Delgado et al.).

**3. A worked case and four literatures.** Claude Code, traced through its changelog and docs: task list (April 2025), plan mode (June 2025), more questions, then auto mode as the default (September 2026). Read through Johnson (2000), its stops are cues for a performance, not spaces for inspection. Pacing research varies only rate; automation research places advice on static displays; friction research places obstacles around the stream; gaze dissociates from judgment. Table 3.1 asks of each: who places the decision point?

**4. Theory.**
- 4.1 Definition; triadic coordination and co-optation (Stark & Vanden Broeck); lineage with oracy, literacy and numeracy; navigation = perception × mental model, after Hoover & Gough; contrast with algorithmic literacy.
- 4.2 Nomological network (Figure 4.1): stream properties → forward pull → narrowed look-back; marking → event boundary, look-back, fixations at the decision space; mental model multiplies with fixation; co-optation depresses navigation.
- 4.3–4.4 Six propositions; thirteen directional predictions (Table 4.2).
- 4.5 Operational rule: commitment points, branching junctures, core assertions, situation-model transitions, coded with inter-coder agreement.
- 4.6–4.8 Three mechanisms: attenuated forward pull, induced event boundaries, a preserved look-back window.
- 4.9–4.11 Moderators (placement, duration, form, mental model); gaze versus navigation, with falsification conditions; what marking is not (forcing functions, microboundaries, selective friction, concept suppression).

**5. Discussion.** 5.1 Study: three-condition within-subjects eye tracking (N = 42): continuous stream, yoked pauses at non-decision sentence boundaries, decision-space pauses; seeded errors; mental model measured first; reading rate calibrated per participant. The decisive test is decision-space pauses against yoked pauses. 5.2 Design implications by criterion. 5.3 Beyond text: coding agents and platform-mediated physical work. 5.4 Counterpoints: friction reactance; the New Literacy Studies critique of autonomous models.

**6. Conclusion.** Oversight is a question of where the interface lets a person intervene, not only of whether they can read the output.

**Contributions.** (1) A construct, algorithmacy, separated from algorithmic literacy. (2) A testable model of how streaming suppresses oversight and how marking restores it. (3) An operational rule and a study design that can falsify it.
