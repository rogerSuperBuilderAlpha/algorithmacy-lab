# cholim2026: How Source Attribution Visualization Shapes User Attention and Preference: An Eye-Tracking Study of Four AI Chatbot Layouts
Full citation: Cho, J., & Lim, D. (2026). Journal of Eye Movement Research, 19(4), Article 89. | DOI: 10.3390/jemr19040089 | Read: full text (PMC13513764, via browser, 2026-10-20; saved texts/cholim2026.txt)

Question the paper asks: How do four source-attribution layouts in AI chatbot answers (inline chip, card list, side panel, raw hyperlink) shape source discoverability, engagement, load and trust? (§1.5)

Claim (one sentence): Layout strongly changes how fast sources are noticed and how long they are looked at, but these attention differences did not carry into self-reported trust (§3.4, §4.1).

Method and data: Within-subjects, N = 23 (24 recruited, 1 excluded), 92 trials; Korean university students, regular AI users (§2.1). Static, simulated, non-clickable answer screens shown for a fixed 60 s each (§2.3, §5.3). Tobii Pro Spark 60 Hz (§2.4). Source AOIs merged per condition; TTFF, total fixation duration, fixation-time ratio, fixation count; pupil as exploratory (§2.5, Table 3). RM-ANOVA, Holm (§2.7). Content subtopic confounded with layout; order not balanced (§2.3, §5.2).

Key results:
- TTFF to sources: card list 0.96 s; inline 7.34 s; panel 11.17 s; hyperlink 15.65 s (Table 5; §3.2). ln-TTFF F(2.28, 50.1) = 11.47, ηp² = .343 (§3.2). Only card-list contrasts survive Holm.
- Dwell: panel 10.77 s and cards 9.11 s > inline 4.87 s and links 4.35 s (§3.3).
- No self-report measure (readability, flow, trust, intention) differed (§3.10).
- Within-participant r(TTFF, trust) = −0.20, p = .102; r(TDF, trust) = 0.10, p = .388 (§3.4). Authors: "Attention moved without moving judgment." (§3.4)
- Long dwell on panel/cards read as claim–source "mapping cost", not engagement (§4.1) — an interpretation, not a measured construct (authors say so, §4.1).
- Proposes Notice–Attend–Map–Act pathway (§4.6).

Controls and baselines: four layouts vs each other; no no-source baseline.
Stated limitations: static screens; no clicks; no source-correctness manipulation; order imbalance (panel first for 11/23); topic–layout confound; single-item Likert (§5).

My concerns (mine): (1) Stimuli did not stream — the study cannot speak to incremental text. (2) "Find information" in the proposal is broader than what was measured: TTFF to *source attribution elements*, not to answer content.

Quotes worth keeping, verbatim:
- "Attention moved without moving judgment." (§3.4)
- "the stimuli were static and non-clickable, this study measured the visual preconditions of verification (noticing and attending to sources) rather than verification acts themselves." (§4.1)
