# mangen2019: Comparing Comprehension of a Long Text Read in Print Book and on Kindle: Where in the Text and When in the Story?
Full citation: Mangen, A., Olivier, G., & Velay, J.-L. (2019). Frontiers in Psychology, 10, Article 38. | DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00038 | Read: full text (OA PDF).
Method: N = 50 (~24 years old); 28-page mystery story (~1 h) on Kindle DX vs print pocket book; tests of engagement, recall, locating events, plot reconstruction (Abstract; Method).
Key results: most measures identical across media; print readers better on chronology and plot reconstruction (Kendall distance, t(48) = 2.03, p < .05, η² = .08) (Results; Fig. 3). Locating events in the text correlated with plot reconstruction (R = −0.356, p < .02).
Theory: cites Gibson (1977) for affordances and O'Regan & Noë (2001) for sensorimotor contingencies (Introduction); paper offers "material placeholders" that off-load cognition (Discussion).
Scrolling: the authors deliberately did NOT test scrolling; they cite Hou et al. (2017) that scrolling "is known to impede readers' capacity to create an effective mental map of the text" (Introduction).
Corrections to the packet: no neuroimaging or parietal-cortex evidence here; the study compares page-turning devices, not scrolling or streaming.
Cited by me for: the empirical bridge from Gibson/O'Regan & Noë to reading media — spatial and kinesthetic cues support a cognitive map of text, and that map supports chronology.
