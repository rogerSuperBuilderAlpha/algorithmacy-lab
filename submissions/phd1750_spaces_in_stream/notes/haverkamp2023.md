# haverkamp2023: Is it the size, the movement, or both? Investigating effects of screen size and text movement on processing, understanding, and motivation when students read informational text
Full citation: Haverkamp, Y. E., Bråten, I., Latini, N., & Salmerón, L. (2023). Reading and Writing, 36(7), 1589–1608. (Online 27 Aug 2022; open access.) | DOI: 10.1007/s11145-022-10328-9 | Read: full text (Springer OA HTML via browser); supplementary materials not read.

Question: Do screen size (smartphone vs tablet) and text movement (scrolling vs paging) affect integrated understanding, strategic backtracking, and intrinsic motivation? (The present study)
Method: N = 145 Norwegian university students, 2×2 between-subjects; 1,492-word informational text as PDF; screen-recorded backtracking (κ = .87); written report scored for 11 idea units (κ = .82) (Method).
Key results (Results):
- Integrated understanding: paging 9.06 vs scrolling 7.86, F(1, 139) = 3.43, p = .066 — NOT significant (authors call it a trend).
- Backtracking instances: paging 3.53 vs scrolling 2.49, F(1, 140) = 6.43, p = .012, ηp² = .044.
- Backtracking distance: scrolling 2.88 vs paging 2.09 pages, F(1, 140) = 8.81, p = .004.
- Motivation: interaction only; scrolling on tablet more enjoyable.
- Backtracking–understanding correlation r = .15, p = .076 (Discussion).
Authors call for eye tracking to study rereading (Discussion).
My concerns (mine): reader-controlled scrolling, not system-driven auto-scroll; screen-recording cannot see regressions within a screen.
Cited by me for: digital "text movement" reduces strategic backtracking; the cognitive-map account (spatial location supports relocation). Note for Roger: the abstract's "tended to obtain better integrated understanding" rests on p = .066.
