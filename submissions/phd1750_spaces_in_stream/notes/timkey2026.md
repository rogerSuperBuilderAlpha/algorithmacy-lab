# timkey2026: Eye movements reveal a dissociation between prediction and structural processing difficulty in language comprehension

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 123(32), e2532230123. | DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2532230123 | Read: published abstract (Crossref record) + preprint (OSF 10.31234/osf.io/eq2ra_v1) abstract, measures and method sections; results read in part.
Claim (published abstract): with n = 368 readers of syntactically challenging sentences and 409 types of LM surprisal estimates, early disambiguation effects track surprisal, but disambiguation adds a rereading cost surprisal does not explain; "surprisal can capture routine structure-building, but not the cost of detecting or correcting errors in the structure-building process."
Preprint adds: readers "strategically target the words most useful to amend the structure" when rereading (Abstract); six LLM families (Methods). Preprint says 407 estimate types; published says 409.
Corrections to the packet: authors are Timkey et al. (the packet gives none); the paper concerns syntactic garden-path sentences — the step to "auditing logical flaws in LLM output" is the packet's extrapolation, not the authors'.
