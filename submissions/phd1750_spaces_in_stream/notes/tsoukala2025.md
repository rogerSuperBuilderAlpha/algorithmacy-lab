# tsoukala2025: The influence of text segmentation on garden path processing: evidence from self-paced reading and eye-tracking
Full citation: Tsoukala, A., Vogelzang, M., & Tsimpli, I. M. (2025). Language and Cognition, 17, e58, 1–26. | DOI: 10.1017/langcog.2025.10009 | Read: full text (CC-BY published version), §1–§7; supplementary materials not read.

Question: Do line breaks that coincide with vs clash with clause boundaries change how readers parse object–subject garden paths? (§1.4)
Claim: Line breaks act as "good" (helpful) or "bad" (misleading) cues to structure; aligned breaks facilitate processing, repeated clashes disrupt it and can hurt comprehension (§4.3; §7).
Method: 32 five-line poem-like texts; 2 × 2 (Line Completeness: lines before the critical verb complete vs "scissored"; Transitivity) (§2.2). Study 1 self-paced reading, N = 39 (§2.1). Study 2 eye tracking, EyeLink 1000 Plus, N = 29 (§3.1, §3.3). Study 2 displayed text cumulatively, one line appearing after the reader fixated a cross (§3.3). Measures: first-pass, rereading (regression), total time (§3.4).
Key results:
- Study 1: Incomplete-Intransitive accuracy lower than all other conditions (e.g., vs Complete-Intransitive OR = 0.38, 95% CI [0.19, 0.76]) (§2.5).
- Study 1: Complete-Intransitive disambiguating line read faster than other conditions (§2.5).
- Study 2: Line Completeness raised first-pass time on disambiguating region, d = 0.32, and total time, d = 0.41 (Table 5).
- Study 2: interaction p = .035 uncorrected, p = .103 FDR-corrected — not significant after correction (§3.5).
- Rereading (regressions) to verb and noun regions longer when verb turned out intransitive (§3.5; §4.2).
- Prior work: only 31% of lines in second-grade US texts ended in intact syntactic units (Levasseur et al., 2006, cited §6).
Limitations: imbalanced gender cue in questions; Study 2 power 52% for interaction (§5).
My concerns (mine): poem-like 5-line texts, not chat output; break placement is syntactic, not decision-based.

Cited by me for: closest evidence that the *placement* of visual breaks relative to meaningful units matters, separate from the *number* of breaks — the logic of Roger's decision-unit vs arbitrary-interval control. Also: their Study 2 cumulative line-by-line display resembles a stream.
