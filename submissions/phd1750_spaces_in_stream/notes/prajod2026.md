# prajod2026: Towards Gaze-Informed AI Disclosure Interfaces: Eye-Tracking Attentional and Cognitive Load While Reading AI-Assisted News
Full citation: Prajod, P., Cools, H., Röggla, T., Cesar, P., & El Ali, A. (2026). arXiv:2605.14999v1. PREPRINT (ACM template, venue not filled). | Read: §1–§4.2 in full; discussion read in part.
Method: 3 (disclosure: none / one-line / detailed; between) × 2 (news type) × 2 (AI role: editing / partial generation) mixed design; 40 recruited, 34 analysed, 30 for eye tracking; Tobii; static ~250–300-word news articles (§3).
Key results (§4.1–4.2):
- NASA-TLX and pupil diameter: no differences across disclosure conditions.
- Total fixation duration: one-line 58.70 vs detailed 49.46 (z = 2.544, d = 0.34, adj. p = .02); vs NO disclosure, the difference was significant only within lifestyle articles (z = 2.468, d = 0.41).
- Saccade counts: same pattern (one-line 221.62 vs detailed 187.33, z = 2.655, d = 0.35).
- Authors invoke Information-Gap Theory: brief labels alert readers without resolving uncertainty.
Correction to the packet: "compared to both no disclosure and detailed disclosures" holds overall only for detailed; vs no disclosure only in lifestyle articles (and trending for saccades).
My concerns (mine): static text, not streamed; disclosure labels, not decision boundaries.
