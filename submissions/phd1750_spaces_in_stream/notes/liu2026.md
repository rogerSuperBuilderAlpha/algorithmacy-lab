# liu2026: Behavioral Indicators of Overreliance During Interaction with Conversational Language Models
Full citation: Liu, C., Zhou, Q., Shen, X., Liu, X. B., Wu, T., & Chen, X. A. (2026). In Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). ACM. | DOI: 10.1145/3772318.3790332 | Read: §1–§5 opening and Table 3 in full (arXiv 2602.11567v1, CC-BY version); detailed per-pattern subsections and discussion read in part.

Question: Which mouse/keyboard interaction patterns correlate with overreliance on a conversational LLM that injects plausible misinformation? (§1)
Method: N = 77 university participants; GPT-4 chat platform with injected misinformation; three tasks (survival-item ranking, 700-word article summary, trip planning), 15 min each (§3; Table 2). Logged clicks, scrolls, keypresses, copy/paste, idleness via Chrome extension (Table 1). Transformer autoencoder + DBSCAN clustering on 10–60 s windows (§4).
Key results: 154 clusters → 54 core valid → 18 salient (§5). Low overreliance: careful reading at task start, fine-grained locating and post-paste editing. High overreliance: repeated whole-text copy-paste, frequent referring back to LLM, coarse navigation of "prominent areas", hesitation followed by acceptance (Table 3).
My concerns (mine): no eye tracking; behaviour inferred from mouse/keyboard; correlational.
Cited by me for: process-level (not outcome-level) markers of overreliance; "coarse locating" of prominent areas is the behavioural analogue of what Roger's gaze measures would capture. Supports measuring regressions/fixations at decision points as a process indicator.
