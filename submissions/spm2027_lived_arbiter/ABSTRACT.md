**Title:** The Lived Arbiter: Speaking to Another Person Before an Invisible Judge

## Abstract
Accounts of bureaucratic opacity usually describe one person facing an institution. On digital platforms, opacity is lived in a three-person situation. A host speaks to a guest and a seller writes to a buyer, and each is heard by a ranking algorithm that judges them both. This paper takes its framework from Simmel's sociology of the third. The mediator leaves the decision with the parties, while the arbitrator receives it from them. A third may also profit from the parties' division. The platform algorithm is an arbitrator that neither party chose and neither can see. Drawing on published interview studies of Airbnb hosts and of reviewers on a filtered review site, the paper describes how this arbiter is lived. Hosts report a "double negotiation", with guests and with partly transparent algorithms, and an anxiety that attaches to the unseen judge rather than to the guest. Reviewers who learn of a hidden filter begin to write for it as an audience. The paper argues that platform institutionality is experienced as a doubled address: every word to the other person is also a submission to the arbiter.
