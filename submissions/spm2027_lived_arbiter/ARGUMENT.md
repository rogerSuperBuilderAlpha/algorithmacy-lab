# Argument: the lived arbiter

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. For the author's ruling.]

**P1.** Simmel distinguishes the mediator, who leaves the decision with the parties, from the arbitrator, to whom the parties hand it over. A third can also benefit from the parties' division, or produce it.
- *Sources:* `dxd_foundation/literature/library/simmel1950sociology.md` (pp. 148, 151, 154, 162); `triadic_reduction/library/cards/simmel1902number2.md` (pp. 169, 172).

**P2.** A platform's evaluating algorithm is lived as an arbitrator that nobody chose and nobody can see. Hosts describe a double negotiation, with guests and with only partly transparent algorithms, and the anxiety that follows. Users who learn that a review filter exists begin to write for it as an audience.
- *Sources:* `dxd_foundation/literature/library/jhaver2018algorithmic.md` (abstract); `dxd_foundation/literature/library/eslami2019user.md` (pp. 11–12); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/burrell2016.md` (abstract depth).

**C.** Institutional opacity on platforms is lived as a three-person situation, not as a relation between one person and an institution. The other person is present, but only through a judge whose criteria are withheld. A phenomenology of platform institutions must describe this doubled address: speaking to another person while being heard by an arbiter.
