**Title:** An Exact Test for Triadic Coordination: Integrated Information as Shared Infrastructure for Organization Science

## Summary
Platform and algorithmic-management research often describes work as triadic. A worker, a counterpart and an algorithm are said to form a relation that cannot be reduced to pairs. The field has no shared test for that claim. We propose one, built as reusable research infrastructure. A coordination form is written as a small Boolean model in which each party's next state depends on the others. We compute integrated information over the minimum-information partition. If some cut along party lines leaves the form unchanged, the form is dyadic. If no such cut exists, the form is triadic. The classifier is built on PyPhi, and an AI-assisted research pipeline runs it. The pipeline takes a question through review, literature search, hypotheses fixed before computing, methods and execution, with a human approving each stage. Early results show why an exact test matters. In the complete family of 256 three-party strict-mediation forms, 24 are triadic. An intuitive rule, that a form is triadic when the mediator reads both parties, is necessary but far from sufficient: only 15% of forms that meet it are triadic. Time-series information proxies separate dyadic from triadic forms only near chance. During the development stage we will release the classifier with a model-writing guide. We will also add a module that turns a published case description into candidate models, and run the pipeline on a set of cases from the platform literature. The verdicts concern models, not organizations. The infrastructure's value is in making structural claims exact, comparable and open to challenge.
