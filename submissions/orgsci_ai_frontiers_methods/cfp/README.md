## Stages (verbatim)
- "Stage 1: Submit research proposal and package (Deadline: November 1, 2026)"
- "Stage 2: Engage in development process (January-May, 2027)". The workshop is at Duke on 19–20 Feb 2027, per the 9 Oct post. The July post said 27–28 Feb.
- "Stage 3: Finalization of research projects (Summer, 2027)"
- Final papers are "Nature-style ... between 2,500 and 4,500 words."

## Package (verbatim)
- "A short proposal: approximately 3-5 pages / 1,500–2,000 words plus references, which should describe the core intended scientific contribution. This should include a description of what you have done, what you hope to do, the key risks you face in fully executing your vision, and how organizational and social science would change if you are successful."
- "Technical appendix or supplement".
- "Research artifact: prototype and repository. Authors should provide a Git repository or equivalent artifact that includes a prototype or proof of concept for the work."
- Anonymity: "make sure that whatever artifacts you upload to that repo are anonymous"; "Make sure that your proposal does not have author names in it".
- Portal: mc.manuscriptcentral.com/orgsci, "Special Issue: AI-Enabled Frontiers".

## Scope (verbatim)
- Contribution types include "AI-enabled research loops under human direction", "reusable research infrastructure", "new forms of measurement" and "Synthetic social systems".
- "what we do not want is more obvious: conventional full-length papers with AI-related content or typical application."
- "Can this be theoretical? Yes, we welcome work that uses AI to build theory, provided the process is transparent and the theoretical contribution is clear."
