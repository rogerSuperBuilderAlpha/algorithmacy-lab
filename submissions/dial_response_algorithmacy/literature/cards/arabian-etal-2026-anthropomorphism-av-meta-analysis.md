## Summary
PRISMA systematic review + meta-analysis, 66 articles, 70 effect sizes, 5,903 participants. Finds designed anthropomorphic interfaces (name/voice/face) do NOT reliably improve trust; distinguishes this from cognitive-attribution anthropomorphism (spontaneously attributing agency/emotion/intentionality), which does relate to trust. Flags the safety downside: excessive anthropomorphism can induce overreliance and degrade vigilance.

## Relevance to draft.md
IMPORTANT CAUTION/COMPLICATION as well as support for §4.1: cuts against a naive "anthropomorphism = trust" reading, which strengthens the paper's actual claim (about spontaneous anthropomorphic EXPLANATION, not anthropomorphic interface DESIGN) — but the distinction must be drawn explicitly, or the paper risks contradicting its own cited meta-analysis.

## Key facts / candidate quotes
- Meta-analytic null: designed anthropomorphic interfaces don't reliably improve trust.
- Distinguishes perceptual anthropomorphism (voice/face/name) from cognitive-attribution anthropomorphism (agency/emotion/intentionality) — the latter is what relates to trust.
