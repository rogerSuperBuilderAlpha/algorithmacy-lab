## Topic and Rationale

This paper explores how we should design communication given the growing need to coordinate
through algorithmically mediated platforms. In other words, how should we design for
"algorithmacy," which is similar to asking how we should design for "literacy." It is a direct
response to Belousov et al. (2027), who developed an eight-type framework for user–technology
interactions with digitally induced altered states of consciousness (DIAL). They focus on the
brain–computer interface and measure "input discrepancy" to mark an altered state. They surveyed
25 sci-fi-influenced modes to distill their framework.

I will argue that they make several mistakes and will write a paper that effectively updates
(despite them publishing it only a few months ago) their conceptual model, interface assumptions,
sci-fi analysis, and ultimately provide an alternative framework and set of recommendations to
'design for the future of consciousness'.

## Connection to Course Concepts

**Perception** — Folk theories of algorithmic systems are perceptual models inferred from outputs
rather than from structure (Eslami et al., 2016). Belousov et al. (2027) focus on an altered state
as a discrepancy between sensory input and experienced content. That criterion tests the periphery
of perception and fails to address the coordinative co-optation of contemporary product design and
evolution. Furthermore, focusing on BCI as distinct from any other perception manipulation — such
as rhetoric or text — fails to consider what Dehaene and Cohen (2007) show: reading affects a
region of ventral occipitotemporal cortex, or the visual "word form" area. There is nothing
categorically different between BCI, an effective leaflet, or moving speech. At the same time, the
future of design will instead focus on algorithmacy-oriented modes, which we will explore using
the same (or a similar) sci-fi/speculative methodology.

**Cognition** — Algorithmacy is a competence, not a literacy additive. It is the competency to
navigate triadic coordinative cooptation. Instead of looking at outdated qualia, I propose
Integrated Information Theory's measure phi (Φ) as a replacement for their "altered state." Instead
of examining qualia, we examine changes in efficacy across a causal network when you partition
dyads (Albantakis et al., 2023). Irreducibility of cause–effect structure not only measures
"consciousness" more accurately, but also aligns with the triadic structure inherent to
algorithmacy.

**Human factors and design** — Ananny and Crawford (2018) showed that seeing a system is not
knowing it. At the same time, simply showing raw backend access can overload working memory. I
reject that novices should get abstracted heuristics while only experts engage structural
variation. The future of design is coordinative cooptation where the user is categorically
essential to the design process. The design object is the engagement layer — where reading and
rewriting the shared mediator is the fundamental mode rather than a product layer that hides the
mediator behind a distribution network.

## Preliminary Position or Direction

I'll refactor Belousov et al.'s 25 sci-fi novels and include some options I like — movies and TV
shows. Rather than focusing on the result, I'll be looking at how these worlds think about the
development of the platforms that cause those ends. Buried in each of these concepts are a variety
of ways that people engage and coordinate to develop and distribute these technologies. The most
interesting of these seems to respect or foreshadow an algorithmate society, rather than one
technologically determined by a few bad apples.

I'll refactor consciousness. Their claim based on the qualia model is incoherent and relatively
ancient. Instead, we look at irreducibility in causal networks. If the whole is greater than the
sum of the parts, then we call that consciousness, and we should be examining this not at an
individual level, but across the networks necessary to develop the technologies.

And finally, the framework for how we should design at this transitional moment must respect the
transition rather than try to preserve former notions. I still have to figure out what that
actually is, but it'll be some pastiche from the sci-fi movies I review.
