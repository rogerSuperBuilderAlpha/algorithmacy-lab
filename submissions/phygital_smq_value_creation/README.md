## Candidate angle [DRAFTED, for ruling]

**Working title:** Where the Customer Can Still Decide: Decision Spaces in the Phygital Service Journey.

- **Claim.** The call names a "privacy-convenience paradox" and a "perceived loss of control in highly automated smart spaces". Both are what an unmarked decision space feels like from the customer's side: the service decides at points the customer cannot see. The frontline employee, whom the call calls the "human anchor", is the actor who marks those points in a phygital servicescape.
- **Call topics answered:**
  - psychological and ethical impacts, including the privacy-convenience paradox and loss of control;
  - frontline employees as human anchors;
  - trust and relational metrics;
  - human-centric design principles.
- **Lab constructs used:**
  - decision spaces and marking (the Spaces in the Stream theory);
  - triadic coordination among customer, algorithm and employee;
  - the hospitality paper's augmentative vs substitutive distinction, cited rather than restated.
- **Possible evidence:** a scenario experiment that varies whether a service journey marks its decision points, with perceived control and trust as outcomes. It would reuse the design logic of Paper C's Study 2.
