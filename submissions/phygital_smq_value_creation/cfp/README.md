## Aim (compressed)
- **The aim.** PVC puts human experience, not technological transactions, at the centre of service innovation. The call roots it in the phygital research paradigm (Batat, 2024b), phygital service research (Batat, 2025), and Bitner (1992) and Lemon and Verhoef (2016).
- **Technology.** XR, AI, automation and smart sensors are catalysts. They are to be studied for their emotional, sensory and identity effects, not for speed or cost.
- **Methods.** The call encourages emic methods, including phyginography.

## Research questions listed (paraphrased)
1. **Design principles:** how PVC design principles support human-centric experience design and well-being.
2. **Emotional and sensory effects:** how human–technology interaction shapes emotional and sensory consumer behaviour in phygital servicescapes, and how to humanise it.
3. **Psychological and ethical impacts:** in particular the **privacy-convenience paradox and perceived loss of control in highly automated smart spaces**.
4. **Methods:** human-centric approaches such as phyginography.
5. **Frontline employees:** their role as **human anchors** in the transition to technology-infused service.
6. **Non-transactional metrics:** experiential satisfaction, relational trust and connection.
7. **Future trends:** including the experiential marketing mix (7Es).
8. **Interdisciplinary research** on PVC.
