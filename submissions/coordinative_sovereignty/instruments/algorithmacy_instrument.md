# Algorithmacy: a measurement instrument

Construct-development specification for algorithmacy, the competence to coordinate through an opaque algorithmic
mediator. Candidate items are proposals for the content-validity stage, styled after the closest validated
scales and written fresh; none is copied from a source. The instrument measures a respondent's competence with a
*named* platform on which the respondent depends, so that items refer to a concrete system rather than
algorithms in general.

## Construct and content domain

Algorithmacy is the integrated competence through which an actor coordinates with another party through an
algorithmic third party that interprets both sides and commits determinations neither controls. It has three
dimensions, fixed by the chapter.

- **Inferential** — modeling the behavior of a system one cannot see, forming and revising a working theory of
  how it sorts and optimizes from the evidence of its outputs.
- **Translational** — rendering one's own conduct into the terms the system reads, and reading the system's
  determinations back into consequences for oneself and one's counterpart.
- **Temporal** — holding a coherent strategy across a system whose rules shift without notice, adapting to
  undisclosed changes while keeping a course.

## Position against existing scales

Algorithmic-literacy scales measure awareness that algorithms operate and factual knowledge of how they work in
principle (Dogruel et al., 2022; Gran et al., 2020). Critical-data-literacy frameworks add reflection and
protective skill (Masur, 2020; Pangrazio & Selwyn, 2019). Algorithm sensemaking describes how workers form
theories of the system that manages them (Möhlmann et al., 2023). Algorithmacy shares the inferential ground
with sensemaking, and departs from all of these on two dimensions the literature has not operationalized:
translational competence (acting *in the system's terms*, not only understanding it) and temporal competence
(holding strategy *through undisclosed change*). Awareness and knowledge read the system from outside;
algorithmacy acts within it. The instrument must therefore discriminate from algorithm literacy, digital
self-efficacy (Murphy et al., 1989; Ulfert-Blank et al., 2022), and critical data literacy, converging with them
moderately and separating on the two novel dimensions.

## Candidate items

Response format: 7-point Likert, strongly disagree to strongly agree, referring to a named platform P the
respondent depends on. Items marked (R) reverse-scored. These are candidates for expert rating, not a validated
set.

### Inferential (model the unseen system)

1. I have a working theory of what P's system rewards and penalizes.
2. When my results on P change, I can usually infer what the system did.
3. I revise my understanding of P's system when it stops behaving as I expected.
4. I can predict how P will treat a change I make before I make it.
5. I mostly cannot tell why P's system produces the outcomes it does for me. (R)

### Translational (act in the system's terms, read outputs back)

6. I shape what I submit to P in the terms I believe its system reads, not only in terms that make sense to a
   person.
7. I can read a change in P's output back into a specific diagnosis of what I did or what P changed.
8. I know which of my actions P's system actually registers and which it ignores.
9. I translate my goals into moves that P's system will score well, even when those moves are not the obvious
   ones.
10. I present the same thing to P the way I would present it to a person. (R)

### Temporal (hold a strategy through undisclosed change)

11. I keep a coherent strategy on P even though its rules change without notice.
12. When P changes and tells no one, I detect it from the effects and adjust.
13. I plan for P to change the rules rather than assuming they will hold.
14. A silent change in P's system tends to disrupt me for a long time before I recover. (R)
15. I have a routine for re-learning P's system after it shifts.

## Dimensionality and validation

- **Content validity** — an expert panel (platform-strategy scholars and experienced platform-dependent
  operators) rates each item's fit to its intended dimension; items below threshold are cut or rewritten,
  targeting six to eight retained per dimension.
- **Dimensionality** — exploratory factor analysis on a first sample, expecting three correlated factors;
  confirmatory factor analysis on an independent sample against one-, two-, and three-factor models, with the
  three-factor model predicted to fit best. Moderate-to-high interfactor correlations are expected, since the
  three are facets of one competence; a second-order algorithmacy factor is plausible and should be tested.
- **Reliability** — internal consistency and composite reliability per dimension.
- **Convergent and discriminant validity** — moderate positive correlation with algorithm literacy (Dogruel et
  al., 2022) and digital self-efficacy, with the translational and temporal dimensions showing the weakest
  correlations to those scales, evidence they capture something the literacy measures do not. An
  average-variance-extracted / shared-variance comparison should establish discrimination from algorithm
  literacy specifically.
- **Criterion validity** — an inferential-accuracy task: elicit a respondent's predictions of how P will treat
  a set of moves, and score them against P's actual behavior; the inferential subscale should predict task
  accuracy, tying the self-report to a behavioral criterion.
- **Nomological validity** — algorithmacy should predict platform outcomes (visibility, sales, retention) and,
  in the chapter's larger model, an actor's coordinative sovereignty
  (`coordinative_sovereignty_instrument.md`), the sensibility being the precondition of the standing.

## Cautions

Self-reported competence risks over-confidence; the inferential-accuracy criterion task is the check on it, and
its inclusion is not optional. Items must name a concrete platform, because algorithmacy is competence with a
system, and a respondent may hold it for one platform and not another. Common-method bias is a live threat when
algorithmacy, coordinative sovereignty, and outcomes are all self-reported; separate the measurements and carry
a marker variable.
