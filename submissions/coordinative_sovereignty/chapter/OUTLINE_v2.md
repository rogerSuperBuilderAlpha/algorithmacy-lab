## Research question

For an actor whose economic coordination runs through an algorithmic intermediary it cannot feasibly leave, what form of sovereignty is available, and what competence does exercising it require?

## Thesis

Coordinative sovereignty. Where a mediator still binds after the parties it stands between could reach each other directly, the autonomy available to the coordinated actor is not independence and not control of the infrastructure but standing within the mediation: the power to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the governance of the arrangement. A counterfactual on the forbidden tie sorts the mediators an actor can leave from those it can only govern, and yields a priority rule: exit-restoring instruments against contingent mediators, voice-building instruments against necessary ones, applied function by function rather than firm by firm. The competence this standing requires, algorithmacy, is continuous with algorithmic literacy and discontinuous in its object; it is a precondition of exercising the standing, never a substitute for it. The dominant program, digital sovereignty, extends the literate model of accountability to data, infrastructure, and code, and for that reason cannot reach the layer where platform coordination binds; the two sovereignties vary independently, and a firm can be impeccably compliant and strategically captured.
