# Suggested reviewers for the IGI chapter

Prepared 2026-08-30 for the volume editor's request (Samuel Fosso Wamba, *Organizational Implications
of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI*). Chapter: *Algorithmacy and Standing: Coordinative
Sovereignty for the Platform Era* — Hunt (Bentley), Berthon (Bentley), Whitmer (Iowa).

## What the editor is asking for

An edited volume needs peer reviewers, and the editor does not know every subfield a chapter touches.
Contributors are asked to nominate people qualified to judge the chapter's argument. The editor is not
obliged to use them, does not tell the nominee who suggested them, and — in a double-anonymized volume —
the nominee does not learn the authors' names. A suggestion is a pointer to expertise, not a favor
being asked of a friend.

What to send: name, affiliation, institutional page or email, and one line on the expertise. Three names
is the standard ask.

Two constraints govern the list:

1. **No conflicts.** Not a coauthor, advisor, advisee, or colleague at Bentley or Iowa; not a recent
   collaborator; not someone already writing a chapter for this volume. This rules out Mareike Möhlmann
   (Bentley, algorithmic management), whose work §4 cites and who would otherwise be an obvious name.
2. **Coverage, not friendliness.** The chapter argues across three literatures — digital sovereignty,
   platform organizing, and republican political theory. One reviewer per side beats three reviewers
   who all like the same half of the argument. None of the three below is an ally of the thesis; each
   is positioned to find the weak joint in a different section.

## The three

### 1. Julia Pohle — WZB Berlin Social Science Center (Politics of Digitalization)

Tests §3, the claim that digital sovereignty is the wrong instrument for the computational layer. Pohle
wrote the field's standard conceptual treatment (Pohle & Thiel, 2020), which §3 cites, and since 2026 she
has led the Expert Commission on Digital Sovereignty for the European Commission's Joint Research Centre.
She is the person best placed to say whether the chapter's characterization of the regulatory strand is
fair or a straw man, which is the objection most likely to sink it.

- <https://www.wzb.eu/en/persons/julia-pohle>
- Risk: senior, with Commission commitments; she may decline.

### 2. Thomas Gegenhuber — Johannes Kepler University Linz (Chair, Managing Socio-Technical Transitions)

Tests §4 and §7, the co-optation argument and the institutional menu. Gegenhuber studies participation
formats in platform organizing, and §4 cites him on exactly the point at issue — that platforms offer
feedback channels while withholding governance authority (Gegenhuber et al., 2021). He sits on the
editorial board of *Information & Organization*, so he reviews this genre routinely, and his
organization-theory framing matches the volume's own.

- <https://www.jku.at/en/institute-of-organization-science/about-us/our-team/prof-dr-thomas-gegenhuber/>

### 3. James Muldoon — University of Essex

Tests §2, the normative apparatus. Muldoon and Raekstad (2023) brought republican non-domination to
algorithmic management, and the chapter cites them while correcting their framing: §4 says labor
republicanism treats the platform relation as dyadic subordination where the chapter reads it as triadic.
Handing the chapter to the scholar whose model it revises is the sharpest available test of whether the
revision holds.

- <https://www.essex.ac.uk/people/MULDO26500/James-Muldoon>

## Alternates, if the editor wants substitutes

- **Donato Cutolo**, IE University — platform-dependent entrepreneurs and complementor power asymmetries
  (Cutolo & Kenney, 2021, cited in §1 and §3). Mid-career and likelier to accept than the three above.
- **Hatim Rahman**, Northwestern Kellogg — opaque algorithmic evaluation and the "invisible cage,"
  cited across §3 and §4.
- **Kelley Cotter**, Penn State (College of IST) — algorithmic literacy and black box gaslighting,
  cited in §3 and §9; the right reader for the algorithmacy-versus-algorithmic-literacy distinction.
  Public CV lists kcotter@psu.edu.
