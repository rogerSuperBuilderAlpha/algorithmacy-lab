# Section-by-section review — "Algorithmacy and Sovereignty"

Phase A of the 2026-08 rebuild. Target: `chapter/chapter.md` at commit `c247e56` (13,463 words total,
11,203 body, 78 reference entries, abstract 148 words). Line numbers refer to that file.

This review does not re-derive what the ten-reviewer panel already adjudicated on 2026-07-03. Those twelve
spine fixes landed and are recorded in `reviews/SYNTHESIS.md` lines 117–147. What follows covers three
things the panel left behind: the findings it marked *author-only* and never closed, the findings it
recorded as executed that only half-landed, and the prose problems that survive the earlier style passes.

**Verdict.** The argument is sound and the contribution is real. Three things stand between it and a
submission that survives a hostile referee: an unanswered charge in §5, an overclaim in §10 that the panel
explicitly told the authors to remove, and roughly 1,500 words of argument said more than once.

---

## The three findings that matter most

### 1. Reviewer 2's charge is still unanswered — §5

The verbatim charge (`reviews/08_reviewer2.md`):

> the bypass test, as it appears in this chapter, is make-or-buy / brokerage substitutability… Until the
> chapter exhibits one borderline case where the naive counterfactual and the graded measure disagree,
> [it] reduces to "relational autonomy plus voice… with substitutability relabeled"

`SYNTHESIS.md` spine fix #7 prescribed the answer: claim the contribution as a **function-level,
rule-based, remedy-linked** classification, explicitly distinct from Burt's structural holes, Williamson's
make-or-buy, and essential-facilities doctrine. The synthesis then recorded §5 as executed.

It was not. `Burt` and `Williamson` appear only at lines 160 and 183, both in §3. `essential-facilities`
appears once, at line 646, in §7. **§5 — the section carrying the charge — contains none of the three.** A
referee who reaches §5.2 and thinks "this is make-or-buy" finds nothing that answers them.

The other half of the charge cannot be answered here and should not be attempted. A case where Φ and the
plain counterfactual diverge requires the formal apparatus, which this chapter deliberately carries none of
(removed per the author decision in `research/findings/review_rewrite.md`; formalism deferred to the
companion paper). The right move is to narrow the claim honestly, which leads directly to finding 2.

### 2. The "computable" overclaim survives in the conclusion — §10, line 819

> The chapter has argued that the line between mediators one can leave and mediators one cannot is
> **computable**…

Spine fix #7 ends: "stop implying 'computable' is delivered here." The editorial pass had already softened
the same claim inside §5.1 (line 390 now correctly attributes the measures to the companion paper). The
conclusion was missed. As it stands the chapter's last substantive paragraph promises exactly what the
volume editor and the hostile reviewer both told it to stop promising, and it does so in the position a
referee remembers.

### 3. The bearer of the construct is never named

`reviews/02_political_theory.md` called this "the one thing only the author can supply," and it was never
carried into the synthesis's author-only list, so it has sat silently open. `bearer` appears zero times.

The chapter slides between three referents without ever saying whether one construct covers them: a driver
(§3.1, a person), a marketplace seller (§4, a small firm), an independent software vendor (§1, §8, a firm).
§6's definition says "a coordinated actor." §8 shifts to "an organization's standing." The instruments in
`instruments/coordinative_sovereignty_instrument.md` are scoped to individual respondents.

For a volume titled *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty* this is not a philosophical
nicety. A referee will ask whether coordinative sovereignty is a property of persons that firms inherit by
aggregation, a property of firms that persons hold only derivatively, or one construct with two levels of
analysis. The chapter should answer, and the honest answer is available: the construct is defined by a
*relation* (standing within an unexitable mediation), and any actor that occupies that relation bears it,
which is why a driver and an ISV can both be described by it — but the measurement differs by level, and
saying so costs three sentences.

## Section by section

Each entry: **Claim** (what it asserts) · **Debt** (what it must establish) · **Delivery** (what it does,
and where it falls short) · **Keep** (what must survive the rewrite).

### Abstract — 148 words, lines 3–16

- **Claim.** Platform coordination introduces a relationship no prior communicative competence can govern;
  algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty answer it.
- **Debt.** Name the relationship precisely inside 150 words, with no citations.
- **Delivery.** The opening sentence is the best in the chapter — the compliance/helplessness gap stated
  concretely, in one breath. Two problems follow. Line 12–13 ends on meta-commentary ("the chapter surveys
  the institutions that could build such standing"), spending scarce words describing the chapter instead of
  making its claim. And line 8–9's "reads both sides of a coordination and commits outcomes neither side
  controls" opens the chapter's most-repeated formula (see duplication map).
- **Keep.** Sentence 1, intact.

### §1 Introduction — 622 words

- **Claim.** European regulation governs data, infrastructure, and code, and misses the layer where platform
  actors' outcomes are decided; that layer needs a different sovereignty.
- **Debt.** Show the gap is real and not a compliance failure; justify the word "sovereignty"; state the
  diagnostic that carries the argument.
- **Delivery.** The strongest section in the chapter. The five-instrument sweep followed by three concrete
  helpless actors (lines 27–34) earns the claim rather than announcing it, and the Bodin defense
  (lines 55–64) — sovereignty in the democratic sense, the construct *specifying* rather than stretching —
  is the panel's headline fix, correctly landed.

  Three defects. **(a)** Line 55 opens on the writing act: "The word 'sovereignty' needs defending here,
  because its classic sense resists exactly this use." State Bodin's objection and answer it; the reader does
  not need to be told a defense is coming. **(b)** Line 49 announces "This chapter names the reconceived form
  coordinative sovereignty," and line 508 announces it again in §6. Name it once. **(c)** The normative core
  is asserted and never defended. Line 48 says the sovereignty of independence "is empty" where the mediator
  cannot be left. The project's own thesis (AGENDA item 6) is stronger and more interesting — that demanding
  independence leaves an actor *less* sovereign, because pressing for a bypass that cannot exist spends the
  actor's leverage on nothing. The chapter never makes that argument, in §1 or anywhere.
- **Keep.** Lines 20–34 whole. The Bodin answer, restructured to lead with the objection.

### §2 Sovereignty and its sensibilities — 1,186 words

- **Claim.** Every historical sovereignty depended on a communicative competence that made authority legible
  and contestable; oracy, literacy, and an extended literacy mark three eras.
- **Debt.** Establish the pairing as more than analogy; show digital sovereignty is literacy extended rather
  than a new kind; justify the historical detour to a management reader.
- **Delivery.** The panel and the editor both asked for ~300 words out of §2.1–2.2. Nothing was cut; the
  section still runs 1,186.

  The dead weight is identifiable. Lines 86–95 form a paragraph that does nothing but tell the reader what
  the historical case is for: "Seeing the pattern in the oral case, where the stakes are plain and the
  analytics simple, prepares the eye for the platform case." That is meta-commentary plus ornament ("the
  pattern is naked," "prepares the eye") occupying ten lines. Lines 152–154 are a second roadmap sentence
  closing §2.3. Together they are ~180 words that assert the section's relevance instead of demonstrating it.

  An ambiguity also survives at line 139: "The one completed change in kind is the first, from speech to
  writing: a scribe is a different creature from a better speaker." The chapter's architecture intends
  exactly two changes in kind — oral → literate and literate → algorithmic — with the second still under way
  (`chapter/README.md` lines 34–36 record this as a deliberate fix). The sentence is therefore consistent,
  but "completed" is carrying that whole distinction alone, and a reader hits "the one … change in kind" and
  reasonably takes it to mean there is only one. Rephrase so the second transition is visible in the sentence
  that mentions the first.

  And the editorial pass's open flag is still open: *state what the historical frame buys that a direct
  account of opacity-plus-non-exit would not.* If §2 cannot answer that in two sentences, it should shrink to
  its load-bearing content, which is §2.3's claim that digital sovereignty is a reading competence.
- **Keep.** §2.2's persistence/portability/comparability triad (lines 99–101) — three properties, each
  carrying distinct content, doing real analytic work. §2.3's whole argument. Lines 141–150 on the
  characteristic error (meeting a new medium with the competence you already have) — that is the paragraph
  §2 exists to produce.

### §3 The platform turn — 1,746 words, the longest section

- **Claim.** Platform coordination runs through a mediator that is opaque and carries a firm's interest, and
  none of the three classical coordination mechanisms was built to govern it.
- **Debt.** Defeat "this is just hierarchy in new clothes"; establish the triad; show why reading fails.
- **Delivery.** Substantively the best-repaired section — every panel fix landed. The three-way separation at
  lines 179–194 (the coordination logic is old, the topology is old, the conjunction of opacity and interest
  is new) is precise and answers Kellogg and Vallas & Schor properly. §3.2's four blocking features are well
  built.

  What remains is redundancy and one ornament. §3.3's rent argument (lines 260–283) is the first of four
  statements of the same point (see duplication map). Line 208's "Simmel's tertius gaudens grown a machine"
  is the ornament to cut under the metaphor rule — *tertius gaudens* stays with a plain gloss, "grown a
  machine" goes. Line 217 "First, the object resists reading" through line 255 is strong and should not be
  touched.
- **Keep.** Lines 179–194 whole. §3.2's four features, in order. The Selznick concession argument
  (lines 164–169).

### §4 Algorithmacy — 1,194 words

- **Claim.** Algorithmacy is the integration of inferential, translational, and temporal competence inside
  the triad, and it differs in kind from algorithmic literacy.
- **Debt.** Distinguish from Dogruel, Pangrazio, Sander, DeVito, Eslami without dismissing them; show the
  integration is the novelty; show the change in kind.
- **Delivery.** The marketplace-seller vignette (lines 322–342) is the best concrete writing in the chapter.
  The order-halving morning, the diagnosis by elimination, the inferred delivery-speed reweighting, the
  recovery over a week — that passage shows the competence instead of defining it.

  **But it illustrates a dyad, and the section defines a triad.** Spine fix #5 said so explicitly:
  "Illustrate the *triad* (the counterpart present), not the seller-vs-ranking dyad." The buyer appears
  nowhere in the vignette. The seller is modeling a ranking system, not coordinating with a counterpart
  through one. This is the same defect the panel flagged, in the same place, and the fix is small: the
  counterpart has to be visible in the scene.

  The distributional argument (lines 344–354) is a genuine and underrated contribution — the competence costs
  money, so it concentrates where advantage already sits, and the mediator has an incentive to keep it
  scarce. It also contains the chapter's weakest-supported load-bearing sentence, line 347: "Opacity is a
  rent-preserving strategy," cited to Cutolo & Kenney (2021) and Zuboff (2019). Neither argues this. Cutolo
  and Kenney document power asymmetry and its consequences for platform-dependent entrepreneurs; Zuboff
  argues from a different frame entirely. The claim needs a source that actually makes it, or it needs
  softening to what the cited works support.
- **Keep.** The vignette, with the buyer written in. The distributional paragraph, re-sourced. The
  literate-merchant comparison at lines 331–332 (an analogy that carries argument, not decoration).

### §5 A diagnostic — 1,767 words, the core contribution

- **Claim.** A runnable counterfactual sorts mediators into necessary, contingent, partial, and reducible,
  and that sorting decides whether an actor's remaining power is exit or voice.
- **Debt.** Define the test; separate it from the factoring question; **distinguish it from the three
  neighbouring tests it resembles**; show it is actionable.
- **Delivery.** §5.1/§5.2's separation of the two counterfactuals is exactly right and was the panel's spine
  fix #3 — irreducible-as-wired does not entail necessary-under-bypass, and line 408's "Reading irreducibility
  as necessity is how a contingent gate passes for an unavoidable one" states the payoff cleanly. §5.4's
  portfolio argument is, with §8's independent-variation demonstration, the most teachable thing in the
  chapter: one firm, four kinds of mediator, four different managerial responses.

  Four defects. **(a)** The positioning paragraph against Burt / Williamson / essential facilities is missing
  — finding 1 above. **(b)** The OTA case (lines 489–501) carries four unsourced factual claims: the 15–30%
  commission range, the 2024 European rulings voiding price-parity clauses, the ~one-third direct-booking
  plateau, and the billboard effect. Every reviewer flagged this; `SYNTHESIS.md` line 82 calls it universal.
  The numbers are still bare. **(c)** The case stops halfway. It classifies two functions and says exit is
  right against one and a mirage against the other, then ends. It never names the instrument, so it
  demonstrates the classification without demonstrating the remedy link — which is precisely the part of the
  contribution claim that answers Reviewer 2. **(d)** The `partial` verdict has no decision rule. Line 424
  defines it, §5.4 assigns marketplace matching to it, and no sentence anywhere says what an actor facing a
  partial verdict should do. The editorial pass flagged this and proposed the answer in one line: contest the
  bypassable part, accept the integrating part.
- **Keep.** §5.1–5.2 whole. §5.4's four-way classification of one marketplace (lines 466–472). Line 408.

### §6 Coordinative sovereignty: a definition — 1,253 words

- **Claim.** Coordinative sovereignty is the power to shape the terms of a mediation one cannot leave, to
  contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in its governance.
- **Debt.** Define crisply; answer concept-stretching; show the neighbours do not already occupy the space.
- **Delivery.** The definition (lines 511–515) is clean and should not be rewritten. The Muldoon & Raekstad
  wedge (lines 565–570) is the sharpest paragraph in the section — worker ownership can change who sets a
  necessary mediator's rules and cannot make the mediator leaveable, so the problem outlives the change of
  owner. That single argument does more distinguishing work than the four-strand roll-call above it.

  The four strands (lines 520–549) remain a roll-call despite the panel breaking the anaphora. Each strand
  gets a paragraph of the same shape: name the theorist, state the doctrine, apply it. The reader reviewer
  flagged the over-stuffing and it was not resolved, only re-punctuated. Markell and Ostrom carry real weight;
  Pettit and Nedelsky are doing setup that could be compressed by half.

  The bearer question (finding 3) belongs here.
- **Keep.** The definition verbatim. The Muldoon & Raekstad wedge. The three clarifications at lines 575–585
  — rights-are-not-standing, transparency-is-not-standing, this-is-not-anti-platform — which are efficient
  defensive work against the three most likely misreadings.

### §7 Realizing coordinative sovereignty — 999 words

- **Claim.** Seven institutional forms could build effective voice, and the diagnostic sorts which applies
  where.
- **Debt.** Show each confers standing rather than protection; show the sorting works.
- **Delivery.** Still the uniform catalogue the editorial pass flagged: cooperativism, data trusts,
  bargaining, contestability, interoperability, oversight boards, regulation — each given name, function, and
  limit, in the same shape, seven times. The reader feels the template by the fourth.

  The genuinely good argument is buried at lines 640–650: interoperability is an exit instrument against a
  contingent mediator and a *voice* instrument against a necessary one, because mandated access to a facility
  no one can bypass does not let the actor leave, it forces the mediator to grant terms. That is the
  diagnostic doing real work, and it is the seventh item in a list rather than the section's spine.

  §7 also overlaps §8's policymaker paragraph substantially — both sort instruments by whether they build
  standing or stop at legibility.
- **Keep.** Lines 640–650 whole, promoted. The composition argument at lines 668–675 (a data trust supplies
  the leverage that makes a bargaining unit credible, and the data it pools feeds the audits contestation
  needs) — the only place the section stops cataloguing and starts arguing.

### §8 Implications — 1,167 words

- **Claim.** The two sovereignties vary independently, which matters differently to scholars, managers, and
  policymakers.
- **Debt.** Demonstrate independent variation with real cases.
- **Delivery.** Lines 688–701 are the demonstration and they work: an app developer or marketplace seller
  high on digital sovereignty and low on coordinative, against a platform worker under Directive 2024/2831
  who is the reverse. Both off-diagonal cells populated with real instruments. The panel named this a top
  strength.

  The openness/control reframe (lines 742–758) is the second strength — a closed national platform can secure
  digital sovereignty and reproduce co-optation under a domestic flag, an open federated one can deliver
  standing with no state control at all. `research/worked_cases.md` names these cases (WeChat, RuNet, Som
  Mobilitat, the fediverse); the chapter hedges them to "state-integrated national platforms" and "a
  member-governed mobility cooperative." The hedge costs concreteness for no gain — the worked-cases file
  already has them verified.

  Two currency risks: line 692's Apple €500m April 2025 fine (appeal status unknown as of this review) and
  line 696's "now benefits from" for a directive whose transposition deadline is December 2026.
- **Keep.** Lines 688–701 whole. The openness/control reframe, with the cases named.

### §9 Future research directions — 567 words

- **Claim.** Four directions, the first two prerequisite to the rest.
- **Delivery.** Directions 1 and 2 restate §8's measurement paragraph (lines 703–710) at greater length —
  same claim, same discriminant-validity target, same two constructs. Direction 3 (classify mediators, then
  measure response, then ask whether the response fits the type) is new and good. Direction 4 (does reported
  standing match afforded standing) is the most interesting idea in the section and gets the fewest words.

  Lines 802–805 promise a diffusion question — "whether algorithmacy can be democratized as literacy was" —
  and never say by what mechanism literacy diffused. `print capitalism`, `schooling`, and `compulsory` all
  appear zero times in the chapter. The media reviewer asked for the mechanism; the chapter still asks the
  question and supplies nothing to answer it with. Either name the mechanisms or drop the promise, because as
  written it invites a referee to observe that the chapter's closing question is unanswerable on its own
  terms.
- **Keep.** Directions 3 and 4.

### §10 Conclusion — 463 words

- **Delivery.** Three paragraphs doing two paragraphs' work, with the chapter's most consequential error at
  line 819 (finding 2). Lines 819 and 835 both open on "The chapter has argued / The chapter has offered" —
  the meta-commentary tic in its purest form, twice, in the last twenty lines. Line 832's "a new constitutive
  priesthood" is ornament.

  The diffusion paragraph (lines 826–833) is the best of the three and would be stronger still if §9 supplied
  the mechanism it gestures at.
- **Keep.** Lines 826–833, mechanism supplied, priesthood removed.
