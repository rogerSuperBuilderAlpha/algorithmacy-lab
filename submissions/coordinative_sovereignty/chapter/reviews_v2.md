# Second review round — the rewritten chapter

Read 2026-08-02 against `chapter.md` as it stands after the rebuild and the citation passes. Ten lenses, run
in one head rather than delegated. Findings ranked by damage.

**Verdict: minor-to-moderate revisions.** The argument is in much better shape than the pre-rebuild draft.
Three findings are serious, and one of them lands on the passage the rebuild was proudest of.

---

## 1. The OTA "test" overclaims, because the ruling it turns on postdates the data — §5.5

**This is the most damaging finding in the review, and it attacks the chapter's answer to Reviewer 2.**

§5.5 says Europe "has now run the exit move, which makes the case a test rather than an illustration," then
cites the September 2024 CJEU judgment, then produces distribution data running **2013 to 2023**.

The judgment is *after the last year of data*. The 2023 reference year cannot speak to a ruling handed down in
September 2024. As written, the chapter invites the obvious retort: you claim a natural experiment and then
report outcomes measured before the intervention.

What survives, and it is still a real finding: the parity gate was dismantled progressively and much of it
*does* fall inside the window. Booking and Expedia switched from wide to narrow parity in mid-2015; France,
Austria, Italy and Belgium legislated against parity clauses across 2015–2018; the German Federal Court of
Justice struck the narrow clauses in 2021. Over exactly that period agency share rose and direct share fell.
That is a decade of gate-opening followed by deepening dependence, which is the pattern the function-split
predicts and the substitutability reading does not.

**Fix:** stop resting the claim on the 2024 judgment. Anchor it to the 2015–2021 sequence, which is inside the
data. Say plainly that the CJEU ruling is too recent for the distribution data to speak to, and that the test
it sets up is one a later study can run.

**A second, related overclaim.** The chapter says the function-split "predicts that prices change and
dependence does not," and then reports that dependence *rose*. A rise is not what was predicted; it is
consistent with the prediction only in direction of non-decline. The rise itself needs another mechanism —
mobile search, consolidation among agencies, the 90% share the three largest hold. Concede that the
function-split predicts the *absence of the erosion* the rival reading expects, and does not by itself explain
the increase.

## 2. The bypass test may define discovery functions as necessary by construction — §5.2, §5.5

A hostile reader will press here and the chapter has no answer yet.

The test restores "the direct tie between the two parties the mediator stands between." §5.5 then applies it
to demand aggregation: "Restore the same tie for a traveler who has never heard of the hotel, and the tie
carries no information, because there was no relationship to restore."

If the two parties have never met, there is no tie to restore, so the mediator survives the counterfactual —
**for every discovery, search, or matching function whatsoever.** The test would then return "necessary" for
any mediator whose job is introducing strangers, which is most of what platforms do, and the classification
does no work in precisely the cases the chapter cares about.

The chapter needs to say what the counterfactual restores when there is no prior relationship. The defensible
answer is that it restores *the capability of direct contact*, not a particular existing tie: could this
traveler reach this hotel directly at comparable cost if no rule or architecture prevented it? For a
well-known chain, yes, and aggregation is contingent for that segment. For an unknown independent, no, and the
aggregation is doing integrating work no direct tie reproduces. That reading also explains why the same
function sorts differently for different actors, which is a feature and should be claimed as one.

Without this paragraph, §5.2's central operation is under-specified and §5.5's verdict looks assumed.

## 3. The only demonstrated divergence is a car dealership — §5.2

The node-versus-edge argument is the chapter's answer to Reviewer 2, and its one worked demonstration is a
franchised car dealer. That example is clean, and it is not a platform. A referee will note that the
chapter's central methodological claim is proved off-domain and ask whether it survives on the cases the
chapter is actually about.

**Fix:** add one platform instance. The App Store payment gate is the obvious candidate and is already in the
chapter. Delete the gate as a node and ask whether another actor could provide in-app payment on iOS: under
the platform's own rules, no, so every node test returns non-substitutable. Restore the developer-to-user tie
for payment — sideloading, external purchase links — and the gate's force evaporates. Node says necessary,
edge says contingent, and the DMA's anti-steering remedy is the edge test applied by a regulator. Two
sentences, and the claim is demonstrated on home ground.

## 4. Oracy does not support the unevenness claim — §2

§2 was re-aimed onto the distributional claim: each era shows "a competence for working it that was scarce
before it was common." §2.2 earns this (literacy was a clerical monopoly). §2.3 earns it. **§2.1 does not.**
Oracy in an oral order is near-universal — everyone speaks — so as written the first of the three cases
contradicts the frame the section was rebuilt to carry.

The historical answer is available and better: what was restricted in oral orders was not speech but *standing
to speak in the forms that bound* — who may address the assembly, who may swear a binding oath, who may
recite the law. The competence was general; the authorized performance of it was not. One clause fixes this,
and it strengthens the section, because it makes the pattern about authorized standing rather than raw skill —
which is exactly what coordinative sovereignty is about.

## 5. The 2021 German ruling is asserted without a source — §5.5

The chapter states that the Federal Court of Justice "held the agencies' narrow parity clauses unlawful in
2021" and cites nothing. Every other factual claim in §5.5 now carries a source. Either cite the decision or
attribute it more loosely to the national enforcement wave. Given that the docket could not be verified,
loosening is the safer move.

## 6. The payment-network example is too vague to teach — §5.4

"The same network operates a gate whose force comes from the rails both sides happen to share, which is
contingent." A reader cannot tell what gate this is. §5.4's whole value is that it is teachable. Either make
the gate concrete or drop the payment network and let the marketplace, which is fully worked, carry the
example alone.

## 7. Smaller findings

- **§6, the bearer.** "Where firm and members diverge the members' standing governs" is asserted. Pettit
  licenses the instrumental view; he does not settle the conflict case. Either argue it in a clause or mark it
  as a position taken rather than derived.
- **§4, "Note what she is not doing."** A small instructive aside that addresses the reader about the example
  instead of extending it. Cut the frame, keep the content.
- **§8.** "Further gatekeeper decisions have followed against other platforms" is a hedge standing in for an
  unverified fact. Acceptable, but it reads thin next to the precisely dated Apple decision beside it.
- **§10.** "Hotels learned the distinction expensively" inherits whatever §5.5 ends up claiming, and must be
  softened in step with finding 1.
- **§3.2.** Five blocking features is one more than a reader will hold. Features one and two (opaque, moving)
  are a natural pair and could be presented as one feature with two aspects, which would restore the four-part
  structure without losing adaptivity.

## What is working and must survive

- §1's opening: the five instruments and the three helpless actors, then Berlin and Anderson on why
  independence is the wrong demand. The normative core now arrives in the first 700 words.
- §3.3's rent argument, now properly sourced to O'Reilly et al. and Cohen, with the Armstrong mechanism the
  right way round.
- §4's eBay vignette. The buyer is present, the seller is managing two relationships at once, and the passage
  shows the competence instead of defining it.
- §5.2's node/edge distinction, subject to findings 2 and 3.
- §6's Schlager and Ostrom move. Proprietorship without title is exactly the right shape for a platform, and
  the Maine lobster fishers make it concrete.
- §8's two-construct demonstration and the Article 25 quotation.
- §9's diffusion answer. The finding that the expertise market teaches compliance is the best ending the
  chapter has had.
