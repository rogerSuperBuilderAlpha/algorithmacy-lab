# Seat 02 — Argument

**Manuscript:** Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era
**Venue:** IGI Global, *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI* (ed. Fosso Wamba)
**Standpoint:** Hostile structure. Does oracy → literacy → algorithmacy earn coordinative sovereignty? Does the §5 bypass test license the §6 definition and the §7–§8 institutional program? Press make-or-buy, concept-stretching, DeVito novelty, Hirschman mapping, and any “no relationship to restore.” Confirm the §6 predicate (necessary mediation) now travels.

**Recommendation:** Accept with nits. The definitional cascade holds. The bypass test licenses the scope condition and the exit/voice sort; it does not, and the chapter already says it does not, license the third clause of the definition. Three welds still over-earn: the §10 sentence that lets the historical sequence deliver necessity-gated sovereignty; the capability question’s “architecture” / “comparable cost” pair, which leans the test toward make-or-buy; and the claim that Williamson returns no strategy to a party inside the coordination, which is too strong for the firm bearer this volume is about. Do not reopen the diagnostic, the App Store node/edge demonstration, the capability-not-relationship cut, or the DeVito narrowing.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### 1. The historical sequence earns a competence. Section 10 lets it deliver the sovereignty.

**Claim.** Oracy → literacy → algorithmacy earns algorithmacy as the competence equal to a new coordination regime, and earns the claim that literate instruments do not reach the triad. It does not earn the scope condition (necessary mediation) or the three-clause definition (shape the terms, contest arbitrary power, share in governance). Those arrive from §5 and from the political-theory stack in §6. The architecture of the chapter is sound if the welds stay honest. The conclusion does not.

**Quote.**

> The competence equal to that medium is algorithmacy, and the standing it makes possible is coordinative sovereignty: effective voice within a necessary mediation, an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve, carried far enough to be a share in how that coordination is governed. (913–916)

**Why it damages this lens.** One “and” makes a two-step inference look like one. A referee can accept the competence story and reject the necessity gate, or accept the diagnostic and reject the historical pairing; §10 gives that referee a single sentence to treat as the chapter’s load-bearing claim. The introduction does not make this mistake. It states the pairing (40–45), introduces the triad the pairing does not cover (48–55), runs the counterfactual in miniature (52–55), and only then names coordinative sovereignty as standing within a necessary mediation (66–68). Section 4 ends by handing the check to §5 (387–388). The conclusion erases those joints.

A parallel, smaller leak sits in §4, before the diagnostic has run:

> Coordinative sovereignty, which algorithmacy makes possible, therefore concentrates where algorithmacy is already held. (372–373)

Algorithmacy is the precondition of *exercise* (§6, 679–684), not the scope condition. Under a contingent gate, the same competence improves exit and gaming, not coordinative sovereignty as defined. The next paragraph tees up leaveability (387–388), so the section self-corrects. The sentence still ascribes the construct to the competence without the gate.

**Fix.** Split §10 so history delivers the competence and the bypass delivers the scope. In §4, put the necessity gate on the concentration claim. Paste-ready text in Part 3.

### 2. “Architecture” and “comparable cost” lean the capability question toward make-or-buy.

**Claim.** The bypass test is a different *operation* from Williamson (edge, not node; lift a constraint, do not choose a governance form given the constraints). The *criterion* inside the capability question is a cost comparison, and “architecture” is ambiguous between an interface that forbids contact and the matching system that is the mediator’s work. The cases disambiguate in use. The question does not.

**Quote.**

> Could these two parties reach each other at comparable cost if no rule, contract, or architecture stood in the way? (428–429)

**Why it damages this lens.** If “architecture” means the no-contact interface (you cannot message the other party), the question stays an edge test. If it means the matching, ranking, or discovery system, lifting it deletes the mediator’s work and the test collapses into the node tests the chapter spent 442–471 distinguishing. “Comparable cost” is the right qualifier for the unknown-hotel case (562–564). It is also Williamson’s language. For the firm bearer this volume cares about — the hotel building a direct channel, the developer standing up an external checkout — restoring the capability *is* a boundary move. The chapter later says none of the neighboring tests “returns a strategy to the party inside the coordination” (481–483). A firm that can open a direct channel is a party that can move its boundary. That sentence is true of the worker and too strong for the firm.

The App Store demonstration itself is clean and should not be reopened (461–471): node test on the payment *rail*, edge test on the exclusivity *rule*, DMA anti-steering as the edge test run by a regulator. Parmigiani (2007) is used correctly in §5.4 for concurrent sourcing as a distinct strategy, not a hedge (546–548). The pressure is only that §5.2’s question and its “none of the three” closer do not yet talk as precisely as the App Store paragraph and the portfolio paragraph do.

**Fix.** Make “architecture” mean the block on contact. Let the TCE closer say what question the bypass asks first, and that a firm still needs that answer before it decides what to make. Part 3.

### 3. One sentence in §6 re-widens a construct the cascade closed.

**Claim.** The official definition is tight: autonomy proper to a necessary mediation (600–602). The cascade’s job was to stop “cannot exit” / “cannot readily leave” from becoming the official added property, and to keep lock-in as involuntary retention. That job is done in §1, §5.3, §6’s definitional paragraph, §9, §10, the bearer, Muldoon, and Key Terms. One later sentence in §6 lets the scope drift.

**Quote.**

> And the construct takes the mediation as given, often as necessary, and asks what autonomy is possible within it. (676–677)

**Why it damages this lens.** “Often” makes necessity typical rather than defining. Read charitably, the sentence is only refusing to wish the mediator away. Read as a scope rule, it lets coordinative sovereignty apply to any mediation an actor happens to remain inside, including a contingent gate held by lock-in — the case §5.3 and §9 just assigned to exit. This is not a failed cascade. It is a residue in the “what the construct is not” stretch.

**Fix.** “Takes a necessary mediation as given.” Part 3, under Finding 3.

## Part 2 — Seat-specific audit (joints and license)

### Does oracy → literacy → algorithmacy earn coordinative sovereignty?

**What the sequence actually earns.**

Every sovereignty requires a competence equal to the medium (40–45, 88–90, 128–130, 908–909). Oracy is participation in an oral order, unevenly authorized (95–108). Literacy lets subjects read and contest what the state writes (118–123); democratic sovereignty tracked the spread of the competence, not the invention of the medium (124–126). Digital sovereignty, in its regulatory strand, is that literate competence carried to data, infrastructure, and code (134–145). Platform coordination is a relationship the pattern does not cover: two parties who never meet, a system that reads both and commits determinations neither controls (48–51, 206–216). Reading is the wrong operation (51–52, 155–156). Algorithmacy is the competence indexed to that triad (302–310, 329–338). Coordinative sovereignty is the standing that competence can make effective — once, and only once, the mediation is necessary (66–68, 600–602, 679–684).

That is two arguments, welded in the title and in §10, distinguished in the body.

The first argument (competence equal to medium) would license a literate-style sovereignty over the new medium: model the system, hold it to account. That is not what §6 defines. Coordinative sovereignty is narrower and different: voice *inside* a mediation the open tie does not dissolve, including a share in governance. Necessity is not in the historical pairing. Share-in-governance is not in it either. Habermas’s literate public (42–45, 118–120) contests a state by reading. Algorithmacy contests a mediator by inference because the object will not hold still to be read (229–232). The chapter is honest that the operation has changed (“reading is the wrong operation,” 51–52) and that the capacities are continuous with algorithmic literacy while the regime is not (336–338, 379–383). The pairing therefore earns a *regime* change and a *competence* continuous with its neighbors. It does not earn a third sovereignty as specified.

**The DeVito concession leaves that pairing standing, just barely.**

DeVito (2021) already holds awareness, strategic use, and revision across changing platforms (312–316). Jarrahi and Sutherland, Sutherland et al., Dogruel et al., folk-theory HCI, and algorithmic-management fieldwork already describe the practice (316–327). The novelty claimed is the index to triadic coordination, “and nothing larger” (335–336). Street’s caution is parked on the competence, not the regime (382–385). Closed item 9 holds. The remaining joint is this: if the capacities are continuous and only the setting is new, algorithmacy is not a third competence in the sense oracy and literacy were. Literacy was a different operation on a different medium. Algorithmacy is the same kind of operation (theorize, translate, hold a course) on a different topology. Section 2.3’s “change of the same order in the regime of coordination” (147–149) is the salvage, and §4 delivers it. Do not reopen the coinage. Do not let §10 treat the coinage as if it had delivered the definition.

Curchod et al. (2020) illustrate the triad (340–344) and also show why discriminant validity is owed: those sellers are already doing DeVito’s folk theorization while managing a counterpart. Section 9 states the debt (857–865). That is the right place for it.

### Does the §5 bypass test license the §6 definition and the §7–§8 program?

**What the test licenses, tightly.**

1. Four kinds of mediator: necessary, contingent, partial, reducible (414–425).
2. The operation: restore the capability of direct dealing, not a prior relationship (427–429). The parties may never have met. Holds. Do not reopen “no relationship to restore.”
3. Factoring ≠ bypass (437–441). Irreducibility as currently wired is not necessity. Key Terms restore the grammar (1321–1322, 1369–1371).
4. Node ≠ edge (442–450, 461–471). Neighboring tests delete or substitute the mediator. The bypass leaves the mediator in place and adds a tie. Demonstrated on a platform: rail vs exclusivity rule. DMA anti-steering is named as the edge test run by a regulator (470–471).
5. Classification is per function and per dependent party, not per platform (434–435, 524–525).
6. Exit is available against a contingent (part of a) mediator; necessity does not produce voice; it forecloses exit and leaves voice as the residual lever (494–498, 511–521).
7. A portfolio wants a split strategy, not a diversified average (537–548).
8. Hotels: parity is contingent, aggregation is necessary for the traveler who would not have found the hotel; Europe opened the gate and dependence did not fall (560–583). The 2024 judgment is too late for the distribution data (585–588). The split is silent on the rise (587–588). An actor who treated the agency as one leaveable thing read the decade backwards (590–592).

That licenses the *scope* of coordinative sovereignty (it is the autonomy proper to the necessary case) and the *sort* of instruments (exit tools against contingent functions; voice tools against necessary ones). It licenses §8’s managerial procedure (815–820) and the hotels warning (819–820).

**What the test does not license — and what the chapter does instead.**

> A share in governance specifies what residual voice should amount to, and the counterfactual does not deliver it on its own. (602–603)

Correct, and load-bearing. Hirschman’s voice is complaint, protest, petition — residual leverage, not a seat. The third clause of the definition (share in governance) is imported from Markell (628–631) and from Schlager and Ostrom’s claimant/proprietor positions (635–642). Non-domination supplies “contest the mediator’s arbitrary power” (611–620). Relational autonomy supplies “the object of sovereignty is the shape” (622–626). The bypass says *when* those demands are the remaining ones. Political theory says *what they are*. That is a two-step license. It holds if the chapter keeps saying so.

Section 7 mostly delivers the weaker thing and is honest about it. Contestability “stops short of a share in how the system is governed in general” (750–751). Article 22 is case-level standing for natural persons (253–257, 747–749). DSA 20–21 and oversight boards are case-level channels, the latter bounded by the constituting platform (753–757). Interoperability against a necessary mediator is “access on regulated terms, which is voice” (693–696) — essential-facilities voice, not a share. Cooperative ownership is the nearest approach to the third clause, and §6 already said ownership does not make a necessary mediator leaveable (663–668, 721–725). The table (706–714) is a sort, not a constitution. The closer is right: no single instrument delivers the construct; the diagnostic “splits what current research treats as a single organizational capability” (765–767). Section 8 opens on that split with Fratini, Hummel, and Schmuntzsch in subject position (771–778). Cascade G and H hold.

The residual, not a new finding: §6 defines the construct at the Markell/Ostrom end; the available European instruments sit nearer Hirschman and Article 22. The chapter already measures them that way (759–761). Do not invent a collapse. Do not let a future edit drop 602–603, or the two-step license disappears.

### Make-or-buy, pressed

Williamson asks whether to perform a transaction internally or buy it, given the legal environment (447–448, 457–458). The bypass asks which constraints are load-bearing (458–459). Essential facilities asks whether a rival could duplicate a facility (447–449). Brokerage asks what a position yields given existing ties, or when a broker is essential to coalition value (444–446). The operations differ. That distinction holds, and the franchised dealer is the right first wedge (451–455): every node test keeps the dealer; the open manufacturer-to-buyer edge dissolves him.

Two pressures remain, both already in Finding 2.

First, once “comparable cost” is inside the counterfactual, necessity for the chapter’s *platform* cases is often “not cheaper to reproduce at current scale,” not “logically unreproducible by a dyad.” The clearinghouse, the many-to-one exchange, and the interpreter (416–418) set a harder bar than demand aggregation or App Store authorizing-and-trust work (467–469, 562–564). Partial is the right residual category for the middle of that continuum (424–425, 531–533). The binary that licenses two sovereignties is then a discretization of a cost comparison. Name it; do not pretend the hotel’s aggregator is a clearinghouse.

Second, for the organizational bearer the volume asked for, opening the bypass is often a make decision. Parmigiani (2007) is already on the page for concurrent sourcing (546–548). Use that adjacency to qualify “none of the three returns a strategy” (483), which is the overclaim. The worker who cannot move a firm boundary still needs a test the neighboring three do not run. The firm needs the same sort *first*, then make, buy, or both. That is a narrower, truer novelty claim.

### Concept-stretching (Bodin, Habermas, Repetto)

Closed item 1 holds. The objection is met in §1, not deferred (72–84). Bodin: supreme, perpetual, indivisible. Habermas (1996): popular sovereignty desubstantialized, “found in those subjectless forms of communication” (75–77). Coordinative sovereignty “adds two attributes to it, a necessary mediation and a standing carried by voice, and so narrows the term’s extension” (78–79). Specification, not stretching. Repetto (2025), on 240 works: accounts of multiple coexisting digital sovereigns “neither stretch the concept of sovereignty nor turn digital sovereignty into something different from sovereignty tout court” (80–83).

The remaining pressure, not a regression: Repetto’s quoted verdict is about multiple digital sovereigns — still sovereigns, usually states, firms, or communities controlling infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty inverts the bearer. It is the standing of the *coordinated*, “the same relationship from the other end” (662–663). Functional sovereignty and the platform sovereign theorize the platform’s authority (660–662). Repetto does not bless that inversion. Habermas’s sentence does more of the work than Repetto’s, because it already moved sovereignty from a seat to a standing to hold power to account through a medium (77–78). Say that more plainly if a referee presses, but do not reopen the naming. The industrial/grassroots strand is acknowledged (141–145, 652–655); digital sovereignty is not flattened to “centralize control.” Closed item 8 holds.

### Hirschman mapping

The mapping is to the actor–mediator relation (enrollment, co-optation), not to the two-party tie the bypass restores. That is why “no relationship to restore” and Hirschman can sit in the same section without contradiction. Exit is leaveability of the mediator. Voice is pressure on the mediator. The two-party tie is the counterfactual instrument, not the relationship under repair.

Three cuts are earned:

- Bypassability is the availability of exit (496).
- Necessity does not produce voice; it leaves voice as residual, available against a contingent gate too (496–498). No functionalist slide.
- Hirschman’s loyalty kept the voice-prone from leaving and thereby activated voice; involuntary retention (lock-in, multihoming costs, sunk investment) suppresses exit and activates nothing (508–509). That is the chapter’s best theoretical distinction. Keep it.

Hirschman’s own warning is used, not flattered: exit can substitute for voice; under a necessary mediator there is no exit to lend the critic leverage, so institutions have to supply the force (513–521). The extension from repair-voice (Hirschman on decline) to constitutional voice (a share in a going, rent-extracting concern) is Markell’s, and is marked (628–631). Do not attribute the third clause to Hirschman.

Muldoon and Raekstad (2023) are filed as voice/ownership, not exit (663–668, 721–725). Worker ownership can change who sets the rules and cannot make a necessary mediator leaveable. A driver-owned dispatch platform is still a necessary mediation. Closed item 7 holds. The leftover uses the predicate (668).

### Bearer, Pettit, Anderson

Whoever occupies the relation of being coordinated through a necessary mediation bears the standing; person and firm; a corporate body’s freedom is for the sake of its members; where the two diverge, the members govern (644–650; Pettit, 2016). Closed item 6 holds. E. Anderson (2017) is used twice, both times for the insufficiency of quit/exit against an authority that reconstitutes itself down the road (62–64, 668–670). The platform-work directive’s employment presumption is correctly split from Article 25: the presumption may move the worker into a hierarchy, which changes the coordination form; Article 25 is the voice clause inside the triad (802–807). That split keeps the construct from swallowing employment law.

### Predicate travel (the cascade’s extra job)

Official added property in §1: necessary mediation plus standing carried by voice (66–68, 78–79). “Where the mediator cannot be left” (57) is the setup, not the official property. “Cannot exit” is not reinstalled.

§6 definition: autonomy proper to a necessary mediation (600–602). “Cannot leave” is immediately subordinated as the name of that fact, and distinguished from lock-in and from irreducibility-as-wired (603–606).

Bearer: “coordinated through a necessary mediation” (644–646).

Muldoon leftover: “standing within a necessary mediation” (668).

§9: measured under “a necessary mediation, an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve”; lock-in is involuntary retention, exit column (867–873).

§10: “effective voice within a necessary mediation, an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve” (914–916). Same predicate as §6. The *weld* to the historical sequence is Finding 1; the predicate itself matches.

Key Terms, coordinative sovereignty: official wording plus “Irreducibility as the coordination is currently wired does not by itself establish necessity” (1318–1322). Irreducibility as its own term repeats the cut (1369–1371).

The only scope wobble is Finding 3 (“often as necessary,” 676) and the premature ascription at 372–373. Neither reinstalls “cannot readily leave.” Neither is a regression of the cascade. Both are nits on a predicate that otherwise travels.

### Scope honesty (computable / companion)

The companion develops measures for borderline and many-party cases (403–404, 887–889). This chapter carries the plain counterfactual (405–406). “Computable” is absent from the conclusion. Closed item 5 holds. Section 9’s fourth direction operationalizes afforded standing via the companion’s formal model (887–891). That is a research program, not a result delivered here. No over-promise found for this seat.

## Part 3 — Paste-ready revisions

Highest-value only. Author’s diction. No first person. Shorter or the same length.

### A. Section 10 — let the bypass, not the history, deliver the scope (913–916)

**Was:**

The competence equal to that medium is algorithmacy, and the standing it makes possible is coordinative sovereignty: effective voice within a necessary mediation, an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve, carried far enough to be a share in how that coordination is governed.

**Replace with:**

The competence equal to that medium is algorithmacy. Where a mediator still binds after the parties can reach each other, the standing that competence can make effective is coordinative sovereignty: voice within that mediation, carried far enough to be a share in how the coordination is governed.

### B. Capability question — keep the edge, drop ambiguous “architecture” (428–429)

**Was:**

Could these two parties reach each other at comparable cost if no rule, contract, or architecture stood in the way?

**Replace with:**

Could these two parties reach each other at comparable cost if no rule, contract, or interface forbade the contact?

(Matching, ranking, and discovery stay in the mediator. The question lifts the block on the tie, not the mediator’s work. “Comparable cost” remains, because the unknown-hotel case needs it.)

### C. TCE closer — true for the worker; qualify it for the firm (481–483)

**Was:**

Brokerage theory predicts the broker’s returns. Transaction cost economics prescribes a governance form to a party that can move its boundary. Essential facilities tells a court when to compel access for an excluded rival. None of the three returns a strategy to the party inside the coordination, and none of the three is run by that party.

**Replace with:**

Brokerage theory predicts the broker’s returns. Transaction cost economics tells a firm that can move its boundary whether to make or buy. Essential facilities tells a court when to compel access. None asks, from inside the coordination, which functions rest only on a forbidden tie. A firm still needs that answer before it decides what to make.

### D. Two one-line predicate nits (not a third argument; keep if touching those sentences)

**§4, 372–373, was:** Coordinative sovereignty, which algorithmacy makes possible, therefore concentrates where algorithmacy is already held.

**Replace with:** Coordinative sovereignty therefore concentrates where algorithmacy is already held, and only where the mediation is necessary.

**§6, 676–677, was:** And the construct takes the mediation as given, often as necessary, and asks what autonomy is possible within it.

**Replace with:** And the construct takes a necessary mediation as given and asks what autonomy is possible within it.

## Verdict

**Accept with nits.**

The definitional cascade holds. Necessary mediation is the official added property in §1, the definition in §6, the bearer sentence, the Muldoon leftover, the §9 inclusion rule, the §10 compression, and Key Terms. Lock-in is involuntary retention. Irreducibility-as-wired is not necessity. “Cannot readily leave” / “cannot exit” are not reinstalled as the official property. The §5 cuts that earlier panels closed are still on the page and still do the work: capability of direct dealing, not a prior relationship; node vs edge on the App Store rail and rule; factoring ≠ bypass; OTA sequence 2015–2021, 2024 marked late; computable gone; DeVito novelty narrowed to the triadic index.

The historical sequence does **not** earn coordinative sovereignty as defined. It earns algorithmacy and the claim that literacy cannot govern the triad. The bypass test licenses the scope and the exit/voice sort. Markell, Ostrom, Pettit, and relational autonomy license the content of voice, including the share the counterfactual “does not deliver… on its own” (602–603). Section 7–§8 are a repertoire sorted by that diagnostic, not a deduction from it. That two-step structure is the chapter being itself. The nits are places the chapter talks as if the steps were one, or as if the bypass were further from make-or-buy than the capability question’s own words allow.

**Single most important fix.** Revision A: split the §10 sentence so the bypass, not the oracy–literacy pairing, delivers the necessity gate.

**Biggest genuine strength.** The three cuts in §5.2 plus the loyalty/retention cut in §5.3. A referee who knows brokerage, TCE, and essential facilities can see a different operation. A referee who knows Hirschman can see why lock-in is not loyalty. The App Store paragraph is the demonstration that the dealer was not a toy.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** Whether “architecture” in line 428 was meant to include matching and discovery (the mediator’s work) or only the interface that forbids contact. The cases already run the second reading. If the first was intended, the node/edge distinction needs a sentence, not a word change.
