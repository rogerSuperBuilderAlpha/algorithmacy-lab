# Reviewer 10 — Smart Interdisciplinary Reader

**Manuscript:** Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era
**Venue:** IGI Global edited volume *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI* (ed. Samuel Fosso Wamba)
**Standpoint:** Intelligent reader who is not a specialist in digital sovereignty, political theory, or organization theory. Judges first-read experience: what lands, where the chapter loses the reader, which examples teach and which assume, whether the payoff is clear by the end.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

The chapter holds the impersonal academic register. Named agents carry active verbs ("Burrell distinguishes," "Hirschman drew," "the diagnostic decides"). No first-person voice appears in the prose body. Self-cites are anonymized as Author (2026). That register is taken as correct for the venue.

What this lens judges hard is whether a smart non-specialist finishes the chapter with the argument in hand — not whether every citation satisfies a field referee. The bar:

1. Does each major move land on a first read without prior fluency in Pettit, Selznick, or IIT?
2. Where does the prose lose the reader — density, jargon, assumed background, or fatigue?
3. Which examples teach the claim, and which name a world the reader is assumed already to know?
4. By §8–§10, is the payoff clear: what to do differently, and why the length was spent?

House-style tics (antithesis machine, self-narrating rigor, metronomic openers) are flagged only where they actually impede first-read comprehension. Closed findings from the brief are re-checked for regression; none of the ten is reopened as a live dispute unless the current text fails the fix.

## Part 1 — Theory and structure, §1–§10

Ranked by damage to a first read. Clean sections get one sentence and move on.

### Finding 1 (highest damage) — §5.1 loses the non-specialist before the payoff arrives

**What it claims.** Some coordinations "factor" and some do not; factoring is about whether a cut of a modeled system loses "causal information that exists only in the joint behavior of its parts"; the companion paper grades borderline cases with integrated-information measures; this chapter needs only the bypass counterfactual.

**What lands.** The *question* (is the coordination genuinely triadic?) is fair. The promise to defer formalism is the right promise.

**What fails for this lens.** The subsection opens the diagnostic with the densest sentence in the chapter for a general reader:

> "Whether a coordination factors is a question about its rules: does some cut of the modeled system lose causal information that exists only in the joint behavior of its parts?" (ll. 394–395)

"Factors," "cut," and "causal information" arrive without a plain gloss. A reader who has never seen systems modeling will stall here, then be told the companion paper holds the real measure — which reads as: the hard part is elsewhere, keep going. That is honest, and it still costs trust. The bypass test in §5.2 is the engine a neighbor can run; §5.1 is the vestibule that makes the engine look more technical than the chapter needs it to be.

**Damage rank.** Highest for this lens. A two-paragraph compression that states "some mediators are conduits; some constitute the joint outcome" and points to §5.2 would keep almost every general reader. The factoring vocabulary can stay as a pointer for specialists, one sentence, not the section's opening load.

### Finding 2 — §6 piles theory names past comfort; the definition itself is clear

**What it claims.** Coordinative sovereignty is standing within an unexitable mediation: shape terms, contest arbitrary power, share in governance. Four strands (non-domination, relational autonomy, Markell, Ostrom/Schlager) converge; neighbors (Floridi/Pohle, Bannerman, Pasquale/Cohen, Muldoon & Raekstad) mark the space.

**What lands.** The definition paragraph (ll. 586–590) is plain and memorable. The three clarifications (more than rights; not transparency; not anti-platform) teach. The bearer paragraph (person and firm via relation; Pettit 2016) is clear enough for a non-specialist. Closed finding 6 holds.

**What fails.** Between the definition and the clarifications, the reader meets Pettit, Skinner, Muldoon & Raekstad, Cicerchia, Mackenzie & Stoljar, Nedelsky, Markell, Ostrom, Schlager, Floridi, Pohle & Thiel, Roberts, Couture & Toupin, Repetto, Bannerman, Pasquale, Cohen, Anderson — a roll-call that is each correct and collectively fatiguing. A management or law neighbor will start skimming by Markell and resume at the clarifications. The "From republican theory it takes…" metronome of the July draft is gone (good); the *density* remains. The cooperativism filing as voice/ownership rather than exit (ll. 648–655) holds (closed finding 7).

**Damage rank.** Second. The construct survives a skim; the theory pile does not need to. Two nearest neighbors plus one sentence on the rest would keep the general reader awake through the section that names the chapter's product.

### Finding 3 — §2 earns its place late; §2.1–2.2 still feel like a detour until §2.3

**What it claims.** Sovereignty and communicative competence arrive together; oracy, literacy, then digital sovereignty as extended literacy; the literate response to platforms is more disclosure, which cannot reach the new layer.

**What lands.** Concrete images teach: "Law was what the elders could recite" (l. 95); persistence, portability, comparability (ll. 109–111); "a compliance officer is a reader" (ll. 147–148). The nesting error at the end of §2.3 is the load-bearing move — without it, §2 is throat-clearing; with it, the rest of the chapter has a reason to have walked through media history. Closed finding 1 (sovereignty naming / Bodin) is handled early in §1, not here, and holds.

**What fails.** A first reader who wants the platform argument will feel §§2.1–2.2 as delay. They are not wrong content; they are long for the work they do before the nesting pay-off. One modest trim per subsection would help without cutting the arc.

**Damage rank.** Third — fatigue, not confusion. The reader is not lost; the reader is waiting.

### Finding 4 — §4's literature situating outruns the vignette that teaches

**What it claims.** Algorithmacy is the competence for triadic algorithmic coordination; three parts (inferential, translational, temporal); novelty is the setting, not the parts; competence is unevenly held like capital.

**What lands.** The ordinary-week seller vignette (ll. 335–344) is the best teaching passage in the chapter for a non-specialist. "She has read nothing; there was nothing to read" (ll. 340–341) converts the abstract claim into a scene. "A competence distributed like capital distributes standing like capital" (ll. 361–362) sticks. Closed finding 9 (novelty narrowed; DeVito conceded) holds on a first read.

**What fails.** Before the vignette, roughly a page of adjacent constructs (DeVito, Jarrahi & Sutherland, Sutherland et al., Dogruel, Pangrazio & Selwyn, Sander, Eslami, Möhlmann, Bucher) asks the general reader to track a literature review whose point is "these exist; algorithmacy adds the triad." Specialists need that paragraph; neighbors need one or two names and the vignette. The change-in-kind close (ll. 364–376) is careful and slightly long; Street's caution lands, then the fluent-speaker-before-a-contract image re-earns the section.

**Damage rank.** Fourth. Not confusing — overstuffed before the teaching scene.

### Finding 5 — §7 is usable now (table helps) but still catalogues

**What it claims.** The same instrument changes character by mediator type; exit tools against contingent functions, voice tools against necessary ones; stack institutions for cumulative standing.

**What lands.** The opening claim (ll. 675–676) and the table (ll. 692–700) are the section's gift to a first reader. Interoperability as exit-or-voice depending on mediator type is the one institutional idea a non-specialist will remember. Closed finding 7's filing of cooperativism remains consistent here.

**What fails.** After the table, the prose still walks institution by institution in similar shape (name, function, limit). Content is right; rhythm dulls the middle of the chapter. A first reader who has the table could skim the paragraphs and lose little — which means the paragraphs are not earning full attention.

**Damage rank.** Fifth — boredom, not loss of argument.

### Sections that land cleanly for this lens

**§1 Introduction — strong.** The compliance-gap hook (ll. 28–35) and the ISV / merchant / creator triad teach the thesis before any theory arrives. Berlin and Anderson (ll. 56–61) are quick but concrete enough. The Bodin / Habermas / Repetto defense of the word "sovereignty" (ll. 69–80) is denser than the opening but meets a real objection early; closed finding 1 holds. A first reader finishes §1 knowing what the chapter will argue.

**§3 The platform turn — analytic high point of the front half.** Keeping logic, topology, and conjunction apart (ll. 173–192) is clarifying for a non-specialist who has heard "platforms are new" too often. The driver–rider triad (ll. 205–209) teaches without jargon. §3.2's five blockers of disclosure are a real enumeration; Burrell's third opacity and "facts without consequences" (ll. 232–234, 252–253) land. §3.3's app-store single-homing example (ll. 271–273) teaches competitive bottleneck better than the Armstrong name does. Selznick's formal co-optation (ll. 194–201) assumes a little org-theory memory but explains itself. Closed finding 8 (DS not flattened to centralize control) is not this section's job; the infrastructural strand appears later and holds.

**§5.2–5.5 The diagnostic after the vestibule — the chapter's original engine, and it teaches.** Once past §5.1, the bypass test is runnable in plain language. Closed finding 3 holds: "It does not restore a prior relationship. It restores the capability of direct dealing" (ll. 417–419). Closed finding 2 holds: node vs edge is demonstrated on the App Store payment gate (ll. 452–458), not only the car dealer. The hotel/OTA case (§5.5) is the stretch a first reader will retell: parity clause contingent, demand aggregation necessary, dependence deepened 19.7% → 29.6% while the gate opened (ll. 565–567). Closed finding 4 holds: 2024 CJEU marked as too recent for the distribution data (ll. 569–571). This is where length feels earned.

**§8 Implications — payoff crystallizes.** High digital / low coordinative (developer, Apple fine, P2B) against low digital / high coordinative arriving (platform-work directive) makes the two-construct thesis concrete. The managerial decision procedure (exit vs voice by mediator type, ll. 785–791) is what a practitioner came for. The openness/control reframe with WeChat vs CoopCycle (ll. 810–822) teaches without requiring field fluency. A first reader who reached here tired still gets the point of the chapter.

**§9 Future research — competent, skimmable.** Four measurement directions in dependence order. Nothing confuses; nothing insists. The democratization close (Paglayan; platform-expertise industry coaching compliance) is the one idea a general reader will care about.

**§10 Conclusion — payoff clear; arc recapped once too often.** The contingent/necessary line and the hotels sentence (ll. 891–893) close the argument. Closed finding 5 holds: no "computable" in the conclusion. The oracy→literacy→algorithmacy arc has already been told; the conclusion can afford to spend less of its space retelling it. The final charge on diffusion of algorithmacy lands.

## Part 2 — Style / slop audit

Register-aware. Exact quotes, failure type, direction of rewrite. Fewer, sharper.

### 1. Self-narrating the caution (house tic 2)

> "Two qualifications keep the case honest." (l. 569)

**Type.** Announces the virtue of stating limits instead of stating them.
**Rewrite.** Drop the frame; open on the limit: "The 2024 judgment postdates the last year of these data, so nothing here tests it."

> "One modest claim survives every qualification of that tradition:" (ll. 127–128)

**Type.** Milder instance of the same — narrates modesty.
**Rewrite.** State the claim: "A sovereignty and its competence form together, and no sovereignty can be contested by those who lack the competence its medium requires."

### 2. Antithesis pairing run as metronome (house tic 1 — allowed once, currently several)

The infrastructure-vs-standing contrast is the thesis, so recurrence is allowed. On a first read the twin-sentence shape becomes audible and the content starts to blur:

- "Digital sovereignty asks who controls the infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty asks what autonomy remains for the actor coordinating through it." (ll. 65–66)
- "Digital sovereignty is a literacy construct… Coordinative sovereignty is an algorithmacy construct…" (ll. 749–752)
- "Openness and control are properties of the infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty is a property of the coordinated actor's relation to it…" (ll. 820–822)

**Rewrite direction.** Keep the §8 literacy/algorithmacy pairing (sharpest). Rewrite the §1 pair so the contrast rides inside one sentence's logic rather than two mirrored clauses.

### 3. Demonstrative closer that labels rather than advances

> "The failure belongs to a modality, and naming it points at the modality that would succeed. Disclosure makes an object legible. What the coordinated actor needs is standing." (ll. 257–258)

**Type.** Mild — the last sentence earns its keep; the first narrates the turn.
**Rewrite.** Cut the first sentence; let "Disclosure makes… / What the coordinated actor needs…" stand alone.

### 4. What is *not* a problem for this lens

- Agentive voice is consistently good ("Burrell distinguishes," "the diagnostic decides").
- The July "From X it takes Y" four-deep anaphora is gone; §6 now varies entry ("Four strands… converge"; "Relational autonomy supplies"; "Markell adds"; "Ostrom supplies").
- Self-narrating "does work / pays / yields the payoff" language from the prior reader pass is largely absent from the current text.
- "Computable" does not appear in the conclusion (closed finding 5).

## Part 3 — Paste-ready revisions

Highest-value fixes for a first reader's path through the chapter.

### (a) §5.1 — plain vestibule; keep the companion pointer to one sentence

**Replace ll. 385–397 with something in this shape:**

> Model the coordination as three parties linked by fixed rules: a worker or seller, a mediator, and a counterpart. Ask whether the mediator is doing joint work the parties could not produce as a pair of separate ties. Some mediators are conduits. They relay between parties who remain separable, and an actor governs the arrangement by reading the interface and the terms. Other mediators are constitutive. They commit an outcome that depends on both parties at once and that neither could produce alone, and an actor governs such an arrangement, if at all, by algorithmacy.
>
> The companion paper develops formal measures that grade the borderline cases, where the answer is close or the parties are many (Author, 2026). The argument here needs only the runnable counterfactual of the next section.

### (b) §6 — keep definition and clarifications; thin the middle neighbors

**After the bearer paragraph (ends ~l. 633), compress the neighborhood to:**

> Most digital-sovereignty work asks who controls the infrastructure and answers with reclaimed control, whether by a state, a community, or a firm (Floridi, 2020; Pohle & Thiel, 2020; Roberts et al., 2021). The move is not always centralizing — Couture and Toupin (2019) document grassroots variants — and every variant still answers the ownership question. Coordinative sovereignty asks what standing the coordinated actor holds, without owning the infrastructure and without leaving it. Repetto (2025) treats that as innovation rather than dilution. Functional sovereignty (Pasquale, 2023) and the platform sovereign (Cohen, 2017) theorize the authority the platform exercises; this construct reads the same relationship from the other end. Muldoon and Raekstad (2023) recommend cooperative ownership; ownership can change who sets a necessary mediator's rules and cannot make the mediator leaveable, so standing within an irreducible coordination outlives the question of title.

(Bannerman can move to a citation cluster or drop; Cicerchia can stay earlier with the non-domination objection if a specialist referee needs it.)

### (c) §1 — one contrast, not a mirrored pair

**Replace ll. 65–67:**

> Digital sovereignty asks who controls the infrastructure; coordinative sovereignty asks what autonomy remains for the actor who must still coordinate through it. The two vary independently, and the rest of the chapter is the work of telling them apart.

### (d) §4 — one-sentence situating, then the vignette

**Compress the long adjacent-construct paragraph (ll. 305–320) to:**

> None of those three parts is new, and their integration is not new either. Algorithmic literacy and related constructs already describe awareness, folk theorization, and strategic use of systems that act on an actor (DeVito, 2021; Dogruel et al., 2022; Jarrahi & Sutherland, 2019). What algorithmacy adds is the setting: an actor working a system that is simultaneously matching it to a counterpart and scoring it on how that match goes.

Then let Curchod and the ordinary-week vignette carry the illustration.

### (e) §5.5 — drop the honesty announcement

**Replace l. 569:**

> The 2024 judgment postdates the last year of these data, so nothing here tests it; what the decade covers is the sequence from the 2015 abandonment of wide parity to the national enforcement of 2021, and the judgment sets up a test a later study can run.

## Verdict

**Recommendation: minor revisions** (accept-with-nits for a volume chapter aimed at an interdisciplinary audience).

**Single most important fix.** Rewrite §5.1 so the diagnostic opens in plain language. The bypass test is the chapter's gift to a general reader; the factoring vestibule currently makes that gift look like specialized apparatus the chapter has parked offstage. Two plain paragraphs and one companion pointer would remove the sharpest first-read stall.

**Biggest genuine strength.** The hotel/OTA worked case (§5.5) plus the ordinary-week seller vignette (§4). Together they teach the necessary/contingent split and the competence claim without requiring any of the surrounding literatures. A smart reader who remembers nothing else will remember that Europe opened a parity gate and hotel dependence deepened, and that a seller can infer a scoring rule from the movement of her own listings. Those two passages are why the length feels mostly earned.

**Does the payoff land by the end?** Yes. By §8 a first reader can say: compliance is not standing; some mediators you leave and some you only govern; check which with the bypass test; build exit tools or voice tools accordingly. §10 restates that cleanly. The arc is clear.

**Where the chapter loses a non-specialist (summary).** Briefly in §5.1 (systems jargon); by fatigue in §6's theory roll-call; by waiting through §2.1–2.2 and the §4 literature situating; by catalogue rhythm in §7 after the table has already done the work. None of these break the argument. All are editable without redesign.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** A second short worked case at the same grain as hotels/OTAs — one named platform function, one cited outcome after an exit-style remedy — so the diagnostic's teaching power does not rest on a single sector. The App Store payment-gate paragraph (ll. 452–458) is structurally clear and still illustrative rather than outcome-traced; only the author can decide whether a sourced post-DMA steering result is ripe enough to carry a paragraph of the hotels' weight.

**Length.** Roughly earned. The engine (§5.2–5.5), the vignette (§4), and the implications (§8) justify a long chapter. §2's front, §4's situating page, §6's middle, and §7's post-table catalogue are where a first reader feels the word count before feeling the return. Trim there; do not gut the diagnostic or the OTA case.
