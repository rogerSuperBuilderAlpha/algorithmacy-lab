## Step 0 — Register and bar

The manuscript is an impersonal academic chapter in APA 7, double-anonymized. Named agents and active voice predominate ("Ananny and Crawford show," "Armstrong named"). No first person. Self-cites appear as "Author, 2026," which this review does not treat as a prose defect.

What this lens judges hard:

1. Whether co-optation is attributed and read faithfully (Stark & Pais origin; Selznick narrowing; domination owned by the chapter).
2. Whether the political economy of mediation distinguishes **bottleneck power** from **bottleneck rent**, and whether Rochet–Tirole / Armstrong are cited for claims they support.
3. Whether the mediator is anthropomorphized (interest must sit with the firm; adaptivity with human reconfiguration).
4. Whether algorithmacy's novelty claim stays inside the triadic setting rather than erasing Möhlmann / Bucher / DeVito.
5. Whether the bypass / portfolio apparatus earns its keep on platform cases, not only old-economy brokerage.
6. Closed-item regressions from the brief (App Store node/edge; capability-not-prior-tie; OTA 2015–2021 anchor; "computable" out of the conclusion).

House style: antithesis and self-narrating honesty are light; remaining defects are repetition of one governance point and a few agentless or system-as-agent slips. Scope honesty: Φ stays in the companion; the chapter carries the plain bypass counterfactual. That boundary holds.

### §1 Introduction

**Claims:** Europe's digital instruments leave platform-dependent actors strategically helpless; sovereignty requires a medium-matched competence; independence is the wrong demand where the mediator cannot be left; coordinative sovereignty is voice within unexitable mediation; "sovereignty" is specification, not stretch (Bodin → Habermas → Repetto).

**Lands:** The ISV / merchant / creator triad (ll. 28–35) is the right platform-dependence hook, and Cutolo & Kenney (2021), Rahman (2021), and Rahman et al. (2024) land at first mention. Berlin and Anderson on why quitting/independence fails (ll. 56–61) give the normative core early. Sovereignty naming (ll. 69–80) closes the Bodin objection for this lens.

**Fails:** None load-bearing. Vignettes remain stylized rather than case-anchored at first use; acceptable for an introduction that §3–§4 earn.

### §2 Sovereignty and its sensibilities

Outside the core lane. Oracy now separates general speech from authorized standing (ll. 100–103) — the closed unevenness fix holds. Nesting claim in §2.3 (literate response = more disclosure) remains the sharpest mechanism sentence for why transparency regulation misses platform coordination. Clean for this lens.

### §3 The platform turn — weighted

**Claims:** Platforms as co-optation (fourth coordination principle); logic and triad topology are old; novelty is opaque + adaptive + firm-interested conjunction defeating disclosure; Selznick formal co-optation narrowed further; mediated triad; five disclosure failures; political economy of bottleneck power and rent.

**What lands (do not re-open as open wounds):**

1. **Logic / topology / conjunction triage** (ll. 173–192) still preempts "algorithmic management is just hierarchy" by conceding Kellogg et al. (2020) and relocating novelty. Faithful to how the field argues.
2. **Stark & Pais (2020)** now shares the co-optation typology with Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024). Priority-of-credit error from the prior panel is closed.
3. **Selznick narrowing** (ll. 194–201) is the chapter's best co-optation move: formal co-optation without even the forms. That owns a critical reading along Selznick's axis.
4. **Interest located in the firm** (ll. 186–188): "the interest it carries is not the algorithm's but the firm's: the owner pursues objectives of its own and retunes the system." This is the de-anthropomorphizing discipline the field requires.
5. **Vallas & Schor (2020)** *tertius gaudens* (ll. 167–170) and Burt/Obstfeld (ll. 179–181) show the topology is known. Good.

**Finding A (moderate) — Co-optation now mostly holds; one honesty sentence still missing.**  
Stark is no longer missing, and Selznick carries the domination-adjacent claim. What remains is attribution hygiene. Stark & Pais use co-optation as a coordination principle (enrollment and recombination of lateral activity), in a register more generative than extractive. The chapter's sentence fuses that typology to "enrolled into an algorithmic system without delegated authority…" and then moves straight into Selznick and, via §3.1–§3.3, into rent and invisible-cage domination. A Stark-attuned referee will accept the typology cite and still ask whether the extractive synthesis is Stark's or the chapter's. Two clauses after the Stark & Pais sentence — co-optation as coordination principle here; the political economy of interested mediation is developed in §3.3 — would close it. **Not a re-litigation of the old missing-cite finding; a residual honesty gap.**

**Finding B (moderate) — "Adaptive" remains the unsupported leg of the novelty conjunction.**  
"It is adaptive, reconfigured continuously and without notice" (ll. 185–186) carries one-third of what is "new" and has no citation at the point of claim. Burrell anchors opacity later; the firm anchors interest. Adaptivity needs the field's continuous-experimentation / algorithm-as-deployed decoupling literature (Christin's work on ethnography of algorithms; the premise in Möhlmann et al., 2023, that formal rules never catch the live system). Without it, a referee treats the third novelty feature as assertion.

**Finding C (minor–moderate) — Kellogg's contested terrain is still under-used.**  
Kellogg et al. (2020) appears for distinctiveness of algorithmic control (ll. 170–171) and as a triad cite (l. 209). The paper's load-bearing claim is that algorithmic control is a *contested terrain* met by resistance and gaming. That half *is* algorithmacy in the field — workers building theories and shaping conduct. Petre et al. (2019) on "gaming" appear in §4 (ll. 356–359); Kellogg's frame does not. Pulling contested terrain into §3 or §4 would ground algorithmacy in the chapter's best ally rather than leaving Kellogg as a "not just hierarchy" foil.

**Finding D (minor) — Residual system-as-agent slips.**  
§3.1: "The system stands between them and pursues objectives set by a firm" (ll. 208–209). The firm is named, but the verb still makes the system the pursuer. Prefer: "stands between them; a firm sets the objectives the system executes." Same discipline at l. 276: "A mediator optimizing for objectives of its own" — the operator optimizes; the mediator is the instrument.

Rosenblat & Stark (2016) remains absent for the driver/rider illustration. Less urgent now that the asserted ride-hail classification is gone, but still a conspicuous gap for the triad that opens §3.1.

### §3.2 Why disclosure cannot reach it

**Claims:** Five blocks — Burrell opacity; adaptivity; Ananny & Crawford no-channel; Cutolo & Kenney / Rahman no-leverage; Hildebrandt literate modality.

**Lands:** Burrell's third opacity stated correctly. Rahman's invisible cage (ll. 238–242) and Wood et al. (2019) flexibility-inside-control are faithful. Cutolo & Kenney single-homing-at-scale is the right dependence claim. The close — "What the coordinated actor needs is standing" (ll. 257–258) — is the section's payoff.

**Fails for this lens:** Features three and four remain near-duplicates (missing institution ≈ unreachable accountability). Five parts still tax a reader; collapsing one/two or three/four would tighten without loss. Not a theory error.

### §3.3 The political economy of interested mediation — weighted

**Prior panel's priority error is closed. The rent argument now holds.**

Current architecture (ll. 261–292):

1. Structural position → **bottleneck power** (Rochet & Tirole, 2003).
2. Conversion into rent depends on homing → Armstrong (2006) **competitive bottleneck**, with single-homing / multi-homing mechanics stated correctly.
3. App-store illustration: consumers single-home OS; developers multi-home and pay commission (ll. 271–273).
4. Faithful mediator would still hold bottleneck power; self-interested retuning compounds it (ll. 275–280).
5. Opacity preserves rent (O'Reilly et al., 2024; Cohen, 2017; Sekar & Siddiq, 2026).
6. Transparent bottleneck still a bottleneck; disclosure without standing does not liberate (ll. 290–292).

This is the distinction the prior review demanded: power (topological) versus rent (disciplined by outside options and homing). Armstrong, not Rochet–Tirole, names the competitive bottleneck. Single-homing maps onto Cutolo & Kenney platform dependence. The O'Reilly/Cohen opacity-as-rent-preservation line belongs here and strengthens the political economy without mis-citing two-sided-market models for "rent independent of any service."

**Finding E (minor) — Soft misattribution of the word "bottleneck" to Rochet & Tirole.**  
"that structural position gives the mediator bottleneck power (Rochet & Tirole, 2003)" (ll. 264–265). Rochet & Tirole supply the two-sided-market framework and pricing logic; "bottleneck" / competitive bottleneck is Armstrong's coinage, correctly used two sentences later. Resequence: R&T for two-sided mediation and cross-group externalities; Armstrong for bottleneck. Specialist nit, not the old contradictory claim.

**Finding F (nit) — Doubled "still a…" cadence** at ll. 290–292 survives. First clause earns its place; second restates Ananny in bottleneck clothes. See Part 2.

**Verdict on §3.3 for this panel:** Accept. The argument holds for a two-sided-markets referee. Do not reopen the closed "independent of any service" error.

### §4 Algorithmacy

**Claims:** Inferential / translational / temporal competence; parts and integration already in the literature; novelty is the triadic setting; eBay vignette; uneven distribution like capital; coordination-regime change-in-kind with Street's caution on the competence.

**Lands:** The spectatorship overclaim is gone. "None of those three parts is new, and the integration of them is not new either" (l. 305) plus "What algorithmacy adds is the setting" (l. 322) is the honest novelty claim. DeVito, Jarrahi & Sutherland, Möhlmann, Bucher, Dogruel, Eslami are positioned as neighbors, not spectators. Curchod et al. (2020) eBay vignette (ll. 329–333) and the seller week (ll. 335–344) are the chapter's best concrete pages — triadic competence shown, not defined. Cotter & Reisdorf (2020) and Petre et al. (2019) earn the capital-gradient claim. Opacity→rent cross-reference to §3.3 (ll. 359–360) is correct.

**Fails:** Temporal part remains the least distinctive relative to DeVito and Möhlmann; the text already concedes as much by locating novelty in the setting. No further cut needed if Finding C pulls Kellogg's contested terrain in as field base. Clean for this lens once that ally is used.

### §5 Diagnostic

**Claims:** Factoring vs bypass; necessary / contingent / partial / reducible; capability of direct dealing; node vs edge on dealer and App Store; Hirschman exit/voice; portfolio of mediators; OTA worked case.

**Lands:**

- Capability-not-prior-tie paragraph (ll. 417–426) closes the discovery-by-construction trap at the level of the test definition.
- App Store payment gate (ll. 452–458) demonstrates node/edge on a platform case. Closed item holds.
- Gu & Zhu (2021) (ll. 476–478): platforms may withhold the integration that would make them necessary — the chapter's best gesture toward **manufactured necessity**.
- Portfolio (ll. 507–524): payment authorize vs anti-steering; marketplace fulfill / match / gate / resell — teachable, and the right managerial claim.
- OTA qualifications (ll. 569–577): 2024 judgment postdates data; function-split predicts non-erosion, not the rise. Closed overclaim held in check.
- Hirschman exit-undermines-voice (ll. 498–504) engaged for the necessary-mediator case.

**Finding G (moderate) — §5.5 still speaks the closed under-specification.**  
"Restore the same tie for a traveler who has never heard of the hotel, and the tie carries no information, because there was no relationship to restore" (ll. 546–547). §5.2 already forbids this reading. Rewrite to the capability test: for that traveler, no direct path at comparable cost exists, so aggregation does integrating work the open tie does not reproduce. Same verdict, earned wording.

**Finding H (minor) — Manufactured necessity is named via Gu & Zhu but not driven through an algorithmic gate.**  
The App Store payment example shows a contingent gate held by platform rules — perfect raw material. One sentence tying that gate to design (anti-steering, contact suppression, identity holding) would connect §4's rent-preserving opacity to §5's classification: platforms invest in making contingent gates look necessary. Rosenblat & Stark (2016) remains the canonical cite if a ride-hail or contact-suppression clause is added; not mandatory if the App Store sentence does the work.

**Finding I (minor) — Portfolio / Hirschman coupling still thin.**  
Exit from contingent functions can drain collective voice on necessary ones (hotel that recovers book-direct margin has less reason to join a push on aggregation). §5.3 treats exit-undermines-voice for the monopolist comfort case; the portfolio coupling would make the managerial advice sharper. One sentence.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions** (accept-with-nits at a volume that already knows the digital-sovereignty literature; a specialist platform-studies referee would still want Findings A–B and G before press).

**Single most important fix:** Rewrite §5.5's aggregation sentence so it uses the capability-of-direct-dealing test already earned in §5.2 (Finding G). Leaving the old "no relationship to restore" phrasing invites the discovery-by-construction objection the rebuild closed.

**Biggest genuine strength:** The disaggregation of digital sovereignty (literacy over infrastructure) from coordinative sovereignty (algorithmacy / standing within unexitable mediation), made operational by a function-level portfolio diagnostic and sorted institutions — with §3.3's bottleneck-power / bottleneck-rent distinction and the App Store node/edge demo now fit for a platform-economics reader.

**What only the author can supply:** A verified primary cite for continuous algorithmic reconfiguration / experiment culture at the "adaptive" claim (Christin or equivalent), if Möhlmann/Kellogg alone are judged too indirect; and a decision whether to add Rosenblat & Stark (2016) for contact-suppression as manufactured non-bypassability, or to let the tightened App Store sentence carry that point alone.
