# Peer Review 03 — Flow (cohesion)

**Lens:** House cohesion only — tail-head, end-focus, rename, weld, reformulate, forward/backward hooks, grouped evidence. Not a synonym hunt. Not a theory redesign.
**Manuscript:** *Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era* (`chapter/chapter.md`; line numbers below).
**Venue:** IGI Global, *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI*.
**Standpoint:** A reader walking the thread entity from sentence to sentence and section to section. Joins that fail are quoted. The sovereignty register is uncalibrated; no letter grade.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

Impersonal academic chapter, APA 7, double-anonymized. Named agents and active voice. No first person. Self-cites as "Author, 2026" are not prose defects.

What this seat judges hard:

1. Tail-head at sentence joins and at section-to-section handoffs (the latter weigh as much as the former).
2. End-focus on structural sentences — parts, splits, verdicts must end on content, not a citation parenthesis.
3. Rename of the thread entity. Drumbeat of "sovereignty," "algorithmacy," or "mediator" is a defect only where a second name would have carried the same referent.
4. Forward hook at paragraph close; backward hook at the next open. An epigram that names nothing the next stretch walks is a failed close.
5. Parallel evidence grouped, not one finding per sentence for a run of six.

Weld-the-gloss and reformulate are judged where they decide whether a later sentence has anything portable to carry. Local stretches that already cohere are named in one sentence and left.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 — §1 closes the naming box; §2 restarts a different Habermas

**Claim.** The first section joint does not land. §1's last paragraph spends its end-focus on Repetto's "not a stretch" verdict. §2 opens on the 1962 pairing of authority and competence — a thread last live in the middle of §1, not at its close. The shared name "Habermas" is a false tail-head: §1's Habermas is 1996 desubstantialized popular sovereignty; §2's is the competence-and-authority pairing.

**Join.**

> End (§1, ll. 77–80): accounts that recognize multiple coexisting digital sovereigns "neither stretch the concept of sovereignty nor turn digital sovereignty into something different from sovereignty tout court," but "explore ways to innovate the concept of sovereignty as a whole" (Abstract).
>
> Start (§2, l. 84): Habermas established that a form of authority and a communicative competence arrive together.

**Why it damages this lens.** A citation-final structural sentence (the naming defense's verdict, closed on "(Abstract)") hands the next section nothing. The live thread that could have carried — "standing to hold power to account through the medium" (ll. 73–75) — is displaced. The reader who arrives at §2 must retrieve the competence pairing from ll. 37–43 across the independence argument, the definition, Bodin, Habermas 1996, and Repetto. Section-to-section handoff is the costliest join in the chapter because every later section inherits a restarted thread.

**Fix.** End §1 on who holds the competence that standing requires. Park Repetto mid-paragraph (subject position or before the last clause). Open §2 by naming that competence, not by invoking a second Habermas claim. Paste-ready: Part 3, Fix A.

### Finding 2 — §4 closes on an unread contract; §5 opens on a different question

**Claim.** The competence-to-diagnostic joint fails. §4 ends on an epigram (the literate citizen before a script she cannot read). §5 opens on which sovereignty the rules make available. Those are not the same thread.

**Join.**

> End (§4, ll. 384–386): A citizen with excellent legal and technical literacy who cannot model the system coordinating her stands where a fluent speaker once stood before a written contract in a script she could not read. She can perceive that something binds her, and she cannot work within it.
>
> Start (§5, ll. 390–391): Which sovereignty is available to a coordinated actor depends on a property of the coordination's rules, and the property can be checked.

**Why it damages this lens.** Mechanism 6: an epigram closes a paragraph; a hook opens the next one. "Cannot work within it" names a competence failure. "Which sovereignty is available" names a property of the mediator. The bind image does not hand §5 the bypass test, the two questions, or even the word "rules." The reader must invent the bridge (if she cannot work it, the next question is whether she can leave).

**Fix.** Make the last clause of §4 name the checkable property. Open §5 on that property. Paste-ready: Part 3, Fix B.

A related dead-end sits inside §4 and makes this joint worse. The unevenness paragraph closes on concentration of the construct (ll. 371–372); the next paragraph abandons it for the orality–literacy change-in-kind claim (l. 374). Quoted under Finding 5.

### Finding 3 — §5→§6 carries one word over a two-subsection gap

**Claim.** §5→§6 looks as if it lands and does not. The hotel close ends on a strategy-error ("mistook the necessary function for the contingent one"). §6 opens on reconceiving autonomy where the mediator is necessary. "Necessary" is the only shared item. The hotel, the decade, and the mistake are dropped. **Joins.**

> End (§5.3, ll. 516–517): Building those institutions is what the literate and digital forms of sovereignty leave untheorized.
>
> Start (§5.4, l. 521): A real platform is several mediators wearing one name, and the diagnostic classifies functions, not firms.

That is a clean failed hook: "institutions" / "untheorized" → "several mediators wearing one name." The forward hook toward the definition and the institutional program is spent on the portfolio.

> End (§5.5, ll. 586–588): an actor treating the agency as one leaveable thing would have read the direction of travel backwards, and a hotel that spent the decade trying to leave mistook the necessary function for the contingent one.
>
> Start (§6, ll. 592–593): Where the mediator is necessary and only voice remains, autonomy has to be reconceived.

**Why it damages this lens.** "Only voice remains" is not in the hotel close; it is imported from ll. 508–515. A one-word carry ("necessary") across two subsections is not tail-head. The case that earned the necessity verdict is treated as finished business rather than as the problem §6 must solve. A reader who believed the OTA walk was the chapter's empirical peak arrives at a definition that does not take the case as its backward hook.

**Fix.** Close §5.5 on the leftover problem (against a necessary function, exit is the wrong move and voice is what remains). Open §6 on that leftover, not on a free-standing "autonomy has to be reconceived." Paste-ready: Part 3, Fix C.

### Finding 4 — The standing thread is cut twice at the §3 / §4 seam

**Claim.** §3.2 earns "standing" as the portable restatement and then neither the next subsection nor the next section picks it up.

**Joins.**

> End (§3.2, ll. 263): Disclosure makes an object legible. What the coordinated actor needs is standing.
>
> Start (§3.3, l. 267): Attend to what the mediator captures and the stakes come into focus.

> End (§3.3, ll. 296–297): A transparent bottleneck would still be a bottleneck, and a disclosed objective the coordinated actor has no standing to contest is still an objective imposed on it.
>
> Start (§4, l. 301): Working a platform well takes a competence that reading does not supply.

**Why it damages this lens.** Mechanism 5 produced the right portable form at l. 263 — "standing" — and both subsequent opens refuse it. §3.3 restarts on surplus and bottleneck; it only returns "standing" as a trailing echo in its last clause. §4 then reframes the problem as "working a platform well," a how-to open after a political-economy close. Algorithmacy is the competence that makes standing real; the join never says so. The reader is asked to start §4 as if the prior close were about skill at using a platform, not about an imposed objective the actor cannot contest.

**Fix.** Open §3.3 on the standing gap just named (what the mediator captures is why standing, not disclosure, is the object). Open §4 on that same gap: standing of that kind is not a right to read; it is a competence the literate instruments do not supply.

§2.3→§3 is the contrast case and is clean: "leaves untouched the layer it cannot" (ll. 154–155) → "Disclosure is the modality European regulation reaches for, and platform coordination is the case where it fails" (ll. 159–160).

### Finding 5 — §4 neighbor parade is one finding per sentence; the next close drums the nouns and dies

**Claim.** The DeVito-concession paragraph (ll. 311–326) emits one neighboring construct per sentence for six sentences, then the unevenness paragraph ends on a three-noun drumbeat that the next paragraph does not take.

**Join (parade).** After "None of those three parts is new" (l. 311):

**Join (dead-end).**

> End (ll. 371–372): Coordinative sovereignty, which algorithmacy makes possible, therefore concentrates where algorithmacy is already held.

**Why it damages this lens.** Mechanism 8: several findings that support one point must be subordinated or grouped under named sub-heads. The point is "the parts are not new." The paragraph lists eight accounts as eight news. Subject-position cites (DeVito, Jarrahi) are the right end-focus move; the run still fails grouping. ## Part 2 — Cohesion audit

**1. Tail-head.** Inside paragraphs the chapter often does this well (ISV → "They still leave the merchant"; "the gap" at l. 37; "the pattern" at l. 45; "that enrollment" at l. 195; "The same properties that let the state govern" after Scott). **2. End-focus / citation-final structural sentences.** Verdict at ll. 33–35 ends on (Cutolo & Kenney, 2021; Rahman, 2021; Rahman et al., 2024); the next sentence recovers with "the gap," which is the right backward hook and should have been the end-focus of the verdict itself. Many other citation-finals are attribution sentences with the cite already in subject position (Habermas, Armstrong, DeVito) and are not defects.

**3. Rename.** "Sovereignty" in ll. 69–80 is the topic of a naming defense, but after "Sovereignty in that sense is the standing…" the later clauses can say *that standing*, *the title*, *the concept* instead of repeating the noun through Repetto's close. The clear drumbeat defect is l. 372 (Finding 5). Algorithmacy is otherwise used sparingly enough (eighteen hits in the file) that the problem is placement, not frequency.

**4. Weld.** First uses are welded: coordinative sovereignty at ll. 63–65; algorithmacy at ll. 302–303; constitutive / necessary / contingent / reducible / partial in §5.1–§5.2; co-optation along Selznick's axis at ll. 195–202. No weld failure at first mention.

The three-clause definition is re-welded at full length in the abstract, at ll. 63–65, and at ll. 597–599; §10 correctly compresses to "effective voice within a coordination one cannot leave." §6 should have carried that short form rather than re-glossing.

## Part 3 — Paste-ready revisions

Highest-value joins only. Drop in; do not re-argue the theory.

### Fix A — §1 last movement + §2 open (Finding 1)

Replace ll. 73–84 with:

> Sovereignty in that sense is the standing to hold power to account through the medium in which power is exercised, and it is the sense a literate public exercises over a state whose writing it can read. Coordinative sovereignty adds two attributes to it, a mediation the actor cannot exit and a standing carried by voice, and so narrows the term's extension. Repetto (2025), reviewing 240 works on digital sovereignty, treats accounts that recognize multiple coexisting digital sovereigns as innovations of the concept rather than stretches of it (Abstract). What those accounts still leave open is who holds the competence the standing requires.
>
> ## 2. Sovereignty and its sensibilities
>
> A competence that everyone has governs an authority everyone can contest. A competence held by a few governs an authority the rest merely endure. Under a mediation an actor cannot leave, that distribution decides everything, because an actor who cannot leave and cannot contest has nothing left.

(If the Habermas 1962 pairing must stay as §2's first move, open on the standing just named: "The standing just named is only as wide as the competence that works the medium. Habermas established that a form of authority and a communicative competence arrive together. The question he did not press is who holds the competence.")

### Fix B — §4 close + §5 open (Finding 2)

Replace ll. 384–391 with:

> A citizen with excellent legal and technical literacy who cannot model the system coordinating her stands where a fluent speaker once stood before a written contract in a script she could not read. She can perceive that something binds her, and she cannot work within it. Whether that bind is one she can leave is a property of the coordination's rules, and the property can be checked.
>
> ## 5. A diagnostic: necessary and contingent mediators
>
> Two questions do the work, and they are not the same question.

### Fix C — §5.5 close + §6 open (Finding 3)

Replace ll. 586–595 with:

> It is still enough to sort the two readings: an actor treating the agency as one leaveable thing would have read the direction of travel backwards. A hotel that spent the decade trying to leave mistook the necessary function for the contingent one, and against a necessary function only voice remains.
>
> ## 6. Coordinative sovereignty: a definition
>
> Where only voice remains, autonomy has to be reconceived. Independence has nothing to offer an actor who cannot leave, and reclaiming the infrastructure does nothing for the actor who must still coordinate through whatever infrastructure prevails.

Optional companion, §5.3→§5.4: end 5.3 on the portfolio ("The institutions to be built differ by function, and a platform is several functions wearing one name") so the institutions hook is not spent before the case.

## Verdict

**Minor.** The prose inside many paragraphs already coheres; the damage is at the spine joints, where a reader has to restart the thread.

**Single most important fix.** Rewrite the §1 close and the §2 open so the competence that standing requires is the tail §2's head names (Fix A). The Bodin–Repetto defense can stay; it cannot keep the last slot.

**Biggest genuine strength.** §5.1→§5.2 (restore the link → restore the tie) and the §5.5 walk from two functions through the decade to the Schegg split. The §4 seller week is the chapter's cleanest local cohesion.

**Only the author can supply.** Which thread §2 is meant to inherit — the naming defense or the competence pairing. Placement cannot be fixed without that choice. This seat cannot make it.
