# Argument — Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era

**Venue:** IGI Global, *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI* (ed. Samuel Fosso Wamba)  
**Manuscript:** `chapter/chapter.md` (canonical; not `chapter_v2.md`)  
**Standpoint:** Hostile structure. Does the historical series earn the construct? Does the §5 bypass test license the §6 definition and the §7–§8 program? Press make-or-buy / essential facilities, sovereignty-stretching, DeVito novelty, Hirschman mapping, “no relationship to restore,” and abstract / §1 / §6 / Key Terms consistency.

**Recommendation: minor revisions.** The spine holds. Closed items 1–9 hold; item 10 is present for this seat. No regression of “no relationship to restore,” of the OTA dating fix, or of “computable.” The damage that remains is a predicate mismatch at the headline definition, a thinner-than-claimed wedge for the edge test, and two abstract overclaims. None of those sinks the argument; each is a weld the chapter already has the vocabulary to fix.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 (highest) — §6 defines the construct with a predicate §5.2 has just forbidden

**Claim.** The bypass test licenses a *sort* — exit against a contingent mediator, residual voice against a necessary one — and that sort licenses the §7 table. It does not license the §6 definition as written. The definition is keyed to *irreducibility* and to *cannot leave*, two predicates the chapter itself separates from *necessity*. The third clause of the definition (a share in governance) is a Markell/Ostrom specification of residual voice, not a diagnostic entailment.

**Quote.**

> Coordinative sovereignty is the autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated: the power to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the governance of the arrangement one cannot leave. (ll. 597–599)

> Factoring asks whether the coordination is triadic as it stands. The bypass asks whether it would stay triadic if an external constraint were lifted. A marketplace gate that today carries every buyer–seller match is irreducible as the system is currently wired, and it still dissolves the moment a rule forbidding direct contact is repealed. Reading irreducibility as necessity is how a contingent gate passes for an unavoidable one. (ll. 436–440)

> Irreducibility describes a coordination as it stands and does not by itself establish that a mediator is necessary. (ll. 1352–1353)

> What holds many actors inside a contingent mediation is involuntary retention: lock-in, multihoming costs, sunk investment. (ll. 503–504)

Key Terms then repeat the §6 wording (“autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated,” ll. 1301–1303) and, two entries later, define a necessary mediator as the one that “cannot be exited” (ll. 1328–1330). Three predicates are in play:

| Predicate | Chapter’s own meaning | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Irreducible / constitutive | Triadic *as wired*; does not factor | §5.1; Key Terms *Irreducibility* |
| Necessary | Survives restoration of the capability of direct dealing | §5.2; Key Terms *Necessary mediator* |
| Cannot leave | No exit in practice | Abstract, §1, §6, §10; also involuntary retention under a contingent gate, §5.3 |

The argument needs the middle row. The definition uses the first and the third. By the chapter’s own warning at ll. 436–440, an actor can be “irreducibly coordinated” through a contingent gate. By §5.3, an actor can be unable to leave a contingent mediator. Coordinative sovereignty, if it is the autonomy that remains when the open tie *does no work*, is proper to necessity. Written as proper to irreducibility, it applies to the marketplace gate the diagnostic was built to unmask. Written as proper to “cannot leave,” it applies to lock-in the chapter has just named involuntary retention and assigned to the exit column.

The third clause is a separate license gap. Necessity forecloses exit and leaves voice as the residual lever (ll. 492–494, 507–516). That licenses *some* voice institution. It does not entail proprietorship without title, a share in how the coordination is authored, or Markell’s “place in the determination” (ll. 621–624). Contestability of the case, a complaint channel, or a protection against the worst conduct would still be voice. §7 knows the difference — “a share in the governance of the arrangement or only a protection against its worst conduct” (ll. 751–753) — and then treats the stronger reading as what the definition “supplies.” The diagnostic supplies the occasion. The four strands of §6 supply the content. Those are not the same license.

§7–§8 are in better shape. The table at ll. 699–707 is licensed by the sort: the same instrument changes character by mediator type. That is what the bypass test actually buys. The hotels lesson in §8 (ll. 807–809) is licensed by §5.5. The two-construct demonstration in §8 (high digital / low coordinative, and the reverse) is licensed by the independence claim in §1 and §6, not by the bypass test, and does not need to be.

**Why it damages this lens.** A volume referee who accepts the diagnostic can still reject the headline construct as mis-specified, because the chapter has handed that referee the distinction. The institutional program can survive a rewrite of §6; the definition cannot survive its own Key Terms.

**Fix.** Define the construct as proper to a *necessary* mediation — an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve. Use “cannot leave” only as the Hirschman consequence of that fact, and say so in a clause that keeps involuntary retention in the contingent column. Mark the share-in-governance clause as a specification of residual voice, not as what the counterfactual returns. Align Key Terms *Coordinative sovereignty* with that wording. Leave the §7 table alone.

§2, §3, and §4 are clean for this finding. They do not use the mismatched predicate.

### Finding 2 — The App Store demonstration runs node and edge on two objects; the make-or-buy charge dies as node-substitution and survives as edge-substitutability

**Claim.** The node-versus-edge distinction is stated clearly and is demonstrated on a platform case, so closed item 2 holds. The demonstration does not quite prove what it claims. The node test is asked of the store’s payment *rail*; the edge test is asked of the exclusive-rail *rule*. §5.4 already splits a payment network that way. The edge test therefore survives Burt / Williamson / essential facilities (those delete or substitute a node) and remains closer to ancillary-restraints-plus-edge-substitutability than the “operation differs from each” sentence allows.

**Quote.**

> Take the gate an application store holds over payment inside an application. Delete it as a node and ask whether another payment rail could practicably reach the same users on that device at comparable cost: the installed base, the store's default position, and the cost of teaching users a second checkout all answer no, so every node test returns a mediator that cannot be replaced. Restore the developer-to-user tie for payment, through sideloading or a link to an external purchase, and the gate's force disappears, because it never rested on integrating work. Node says necessary and edge says contingent, on the same function, in the same firm. (ll. 460–466)

> A payment network authorizes transactions, a determination that depends jointly on the merchant's request and the issuer's approval and that neither could produce alone, which is necessary integrating work. The same network enforces a rule against steering the customer toward a cheaper method at the till, a gate whose force comes from the card both parties already hold, which is contingent. (ll. 523–527)

The dealer example (ll. 451–458) is clean: the dealer is both the node and the occupant of the exclusive territory, so deleting the node and opening the manufacturer-to-buyer edge are two operations on one object. The App Store paragraph changes the object mid-sentence. “Another payment rail” tests whether IAP-as-system is substitutable — a node question, and one on which Apple’s fraud, refund, tax, and parental-control stack can be pleaded as integrating work. “Sideloading or a link to an external purchase” tests whether the *prohibition* on a direct payment edge is load-bearing — a rule question, and contingent by construction once the rule is the thing under test. “On the same function” is therefore not earned. “Because it never rested on integrating work” is stipulated, not returned by the test.

That matters for the neighbor charge. Ll. 441–449 are right that brokerage, make-or-buy, and essential facilities operate on a node. The bypass test does not collapse into those. It does collapse, operationally, into the question whether an alternative edge is available once a constraint on who may deal is lifted. That is edge-substitutability under a relaxed legal or architectural environment. The chapter already names the nearer relative: ancillary restraints “already runs this counterfactual on contract terms” and “the diagnostic generalizes that operation from clauses to a mediator’s functions” (ll. 469–476). The honest novelty is who asks, and what is returned (a strategy for the dependent party, not a finding of legality). That is a real repositioning. It is not a different operation from the three named neighbors *and* from the neighbor the next paragraph concedes.

§7 then reimports essential-facilities logic as a *voice* instrument against a necessary mediator (ll. 687–691). That is coherent if EF is a remedy for a facility the open tie does not dissolve, not a test of necessity. The weld holds. The stretch is calling compelled access “voice” in Hirschman’s sense; it is closer to common-carriage regulation. One clause would keep the table.

**Why it damages this lens.** The edge test is the chapter’s answer to Reviewer 2. If the one on-domain demonstration compares two objects, a referee who knows the DMA/Apple file will say the disagreement was arranged. The make-or-buy charge then returns in its residual form: the qualitative question is still “could they deal directly at comparable cost,” which is make-or-buy asked of the relationship once the constraint is a parameter.

**Fix.** Run both operations on the exclusive-rail *rule*, or split the App Store the way §5.4 splits the payment network: authorization / trust stack as necessary or partial; anti-steering as contingent. Drop “on the same function” unless both tests address the same object. Soften “the operation differs from each” to match the ancillary-restraints paragraph that follows.

The rest of §5.2 (capability of direct dealing, actor-relative classification, factoring ≠ bypass) is clean. §5.3 is clean for this finding.

### Finding 3 — Abstract and §10 do not carry the body’s own concessions

**Claim.** Two load-bearing sentences in the abstract, and the compressed definition in §10, walk back distinctions the body has earned.

**Quote.**

> Governing it takes a different competence, algorithmacy, and a different sovereignty. (l. 10)

> The novelty claimed against these accounts is that index and nothing larger. (ll. 334–335)

> A counterfactual sorts mediators an actor can leave from those it can only govern and decides which institutions to build. (ll. 12–13)

> the standing it makes possible is coordinative sovereignty: effective voice within a coordination one cannot leave. (ll. 902–903)

“Different competence” is the claim §4 retires. DeVito is conceded; the parts and their integration are conceded; what remains is the triadic index and the competence-to-standing weld (ll. 328–337, 673–678). Key Terms already state that cleanly (ll. 1309–1310). The abstract does not.

“Decides which institutions to build” overstates the diagnostic’s resolution. The counterfactual sorts *classes* of instrument — exit-restoring against a contingent function, voice-building against a necessary one. It does not choose among data trusts, bargaining, contestability, and oversight boards. §7 is honest: “the diagnostic is what tells a policymaker where to put each one” (ll. 757–758). “Where to put” is the licensed verb. “Decides which… to build” is not.

§10’s compression drops the share-in-governance clause that §6 and Key Terms treat as definitional and that Finding 1 has already marked as a specification rather than a deduction. If the clause stays in §6, the conclusion cannot silently retire it. If the clause is marked as specification, §10’s shorter form is the better official wording — but then abstract, §1, §6, and Key Terms have to follow it.

§1’s working definition (ll. 63–64) is closer to the abstract (“standing within a coordination one cannot exit”) than to §6 (“autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated”). That is the same three-predicate problem as Finding 1, visible at the four sites a copyeditor will collate.

**Why it damages this lens.** The abstract is what a volume referee reads first. A competence-novelty claim the body has withdrawn, and a “decides” the body has already softened to a sort, invite a review that the chapter then spends §4 and §7 talking down.

**Fix.** In the abstract, index algorithmacy to the triad rather than calling it a different competence; replace “decides which institutions to build” with a sort. Align §1, §6, §10, and Key Terms on one official wording after Finding 1 is applied. Word budget on the abstract is already tight (≤150); the replacements below are written to fit.

## Part 2 — Joints and license audit

### J1. Oracy → literacy → algorithmacy → coordinative sovereignty

**Partially earned; not ornamental; not a deduction.**

The series earns two things. First, the medium–competence–accountability pairing (Habermas in §1 and §2; literacy as clerical monopoly; digital sovereignty as extended literacy on the regulatory strand). That pairing is why a firm can satisfy GDPR / AI Act / DMA / DSA / NIS2 and still be helpless: those instruments extend literacy, and platform coordination is not a text. Second, algorithmacy as a third *competence*, indexed to a third coordination regime (presence and memory; persistent document; opaque adaptive mediator). §4’s kind-claim pin keeps the change of kind on the regime, not on the mind. That is load-bearing. Cut §2 and the competence claim becomes a rename of algorithmic literacy without a reason the volume’s readers must accept.

The series does not earn coordinative sovereignty. Oral and literate sovereignties are a public holding a polity to account through the medium. Coordinative sovereignty is a dependent party holding a private mediator to account from inside a coordination the open tie does not dissolve. Bearer and object both move. §1 already says the pattern “does not cover” platform coordination (ll. 45–46) and introduces the bypass question as what sets the situation apart. The historical frame is setup for the competence and for the sovereignty *register* (“standing to hold power to account through the medium,” ll. 73–75). The construct’s content is licensed later, and only in part (J2).

Key Terms *Communicative competence* (“each paired with a form of sovereignty,” ll. 1313–1314) is the one sentence that over-pairs. The pairing is analogical. It should not read as a series that delivers the fourth term.

### J2. §5 bypass test → §6 definition

**Occasion and sort, yes. Definition as written, no.**

What the test licenses:

- A classification of functions (necessary / contingent / partial / reducible) for a given dependent party, not of platforms once and for all (ll. 433–434).
- The Hirschman consequence: necessity forecloses exit; voice remains, including against contingent mediators, and is the only lever where the mediator is necessary (ll. 492–494).
- The occasion for reconceiving autonomy when independence is a bypass that does not exist (ll. 54–61, 592–595).

What it does not license:

- The predicate “irreducibly coordinated” (Finding 1).
- The predicate “cannot leave” as a synonym of necessity (Finding 1; involuntary retention is the counterexample the chapter supplies).
- The third clause, share in governance (Markell / Schlager & Ostrom). Residual voice can be thinner than proprietorship. That is a position, and §6’s bearer paragraph already knows how to mark a position taken (ll. 642–643). The share clause needs the same mark.

Capability of direct dealing (ll. 426–434) holds. The test is not necessary-by-construction for discovery functions. Actor-relativity is claimed as a feature and should be kept. The residual in §5.5 — “a traveler who would never have learned that the hotel exists” (ll. 558–560) — still writes the verdict into the traveler’s description. The question the capability reading requires is whether that traveler could reach that hotel at comparable cost through some other architecture (search, destination marketing, brand). The current wording is no longer the old bug; it is a softer circularity. Not a regression. Not promoted.

### J3. §5 → §7–§8 institutional program

**Licensed as a sort. Not licensed as a complete institutional theory.**

The table is the payoff the diagnostic actually earns: interoperability, anti-steering, contestability, bargaining, data trusts, cooperatives, and oversight boards change character by mediator type. §8’s managerial rule — do not spend exit on a necessary function, do not concede a contingent one — is the same sort applied to the firm. The hotels paragraph points at a case the chapter has already scoped honestly.

What §7–§8 add beyond the sort (which stack, for which bearer, at which level of the case or the rule) is not returned by the counterfactual. The chapter does not claim it is, except in the abstract’s “decides” (Finding 3). No further finding.

### J4. Make-or-buy / essential facilities

**Node charge answered. Edge-substitutability charge standing. Ancillary-restraints neighbor owned.**

Survives as against Williamson and essential facilities in the form the chapter attributes to them: those hold the network of who may deal fixed and delete or substitute a node (ll. 446–458). The bypass test relaxes the constraint on the edge. That is a different *question*.

Does not survive as a different *operation* from “could these parties transact directly if the prohibition were lifted?” That is edge-substitutability with the legal or architectural environment as a parameter. A TCE reader can say comparative institutional analysis already treats the environment as a parameter. The chapter’s remaining wedge is who asks and what is returned (ll. 476–479). Keep that wedge; do not rest it on the App Store paragraph as currently written (Finding 2).

Essential facilities is correctly rejected as a *test* in §5.2 and correctly reimported as a *remedy* in §7. No inconsistency there.

### J5. Sovereignty-stretching (Bodin / Repetto / specification)

**Closed item 1 holds. Residual is scope, not absence.**

§1 meets Bodin with Habermas’s desubstantialized popular sovereignty and claims specification: two added attributes, narrower extension (ll. 69–76). Repetto is quoted on innovation rather than stretching (ll. 77–80) and reused in §6 (ll. 650–652). The defense is present and early.

The residual: Repetto’s finding is about accounts that recognize multiple coexisting *digital* sovereigns — still answers to who controls infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty asks a different question from the other end of the relation (ll. 654–657). Using Repetto to cover that further step licenses the *word* more than the *construct*. The Habermas gloss (“standing to hold power to account through the medium”) is doing the real work. That is specification of an already-generalized sense, not of Bodin, and it is enough. Do not reopen. Do not ask Repetto to carry more than multiple digital sovereigns.

### J6. DeVito novelty

**Holds.** Triadic setting plus competence → standing. Parts and integration conceded (ll. 311–326). “The novelty claimed against these accounts is that index and nothing larger” (ll. 334–335). §6 states algorithmacy as precondition of *exercise*, not of formal endowment (ll. 676–678). The abstract’s “different competence” is the only regression of tone, not of the body (Finding 3).

### J7. Hirschman mapping

**Holds. Best joint in the manuscript.**

Necessity does not produce voice; it forecloses exit and leaves voice as the residual lever, available against contingent mediators too (ll. 492–494). Voice is “the only autonomy left exactly where it commands the least leverage” (ll. 514–515). Loyalty is distinguished from involuntary retention: Hirschman’s loyalty kept the voice-prone from leaving and activated voice; lock-in “suppresses exit and activates nothing” (ll. 504–505). Exit-as-substitute-for-voice and the comforted monopolist are on the page (ll. 509–513). No is/ought slide from the structural fact to “the right move.” This is the mapping the 2026-08-02 close-out claimed, and it is still there.

### J8. Abstract / §1 / §6 / Key Terms

**Not internally consistent.** Covered by Findings 1 and 3. Official wording should be chosen once and copied. Recommended official wording, after Finding 1: standing (not “autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated”) to shape terms, contest arbitrary power, and — marked as specification — hold a share in the governance of a *necessary* mediation.

## Part 3 — Paste-ready revisions

Highest-value only. Apply Finding 1 first; Findings 2 and 3 are independent of each other.

### 3.1 — §6 definition (ll. 597–601), plus the matching Key Term

Replace:

> Coordinative sovereignty is the autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated: the power to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the governance of the arrangement one cannot leave. Its currency is effective voice — terms that hold because the coordinated actors have the standing to make them hold.

With:

> Coordinative sovereignty is the autonomy proper to a necessary mediation: the power to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and — a specification of residual voice, not a return of the counterfactual — to hold a share in the governance of an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve. “Cannot leave” here names that structural fact. It does not name lock-in under a contingent gate, which section 5.3 already treated as involuntary retention and assigned to exit. Irreducibility as the coordination is currently wired is a different property, and section 5.2 separated the two. The construct’s currency is effective voice: terms that hold because the coordinated actors have the standing to make them hold.

Key Terms *Coordinative sovereignty* (ll. 1301–1304), aligned:

> **Coordinative sovereignty:** The autonomy proper to a necessary mediation. It is the standing to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the governance of an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve. Its currency is effective voice. It is borne by whatever actor occupies that relation, individual or organizational. Irreducibility as-wired does not by itself establish the condition; necessity does.

### 3.2 — App Store paragraph (ll. 460–467)

Replace with a split that matches §5.4:

> The same divergence appears on a platform, which is where it matters. Take an application store’s control of payment inside an application. Delete the store’s payment rail as a node and ask whether another rail could practicably reach the same users on that device at comparable cost: the installed base, the default checkout, and the cost of teaching users a second rail all answer no, so every node test returns a mediator that cannot be replaced. That result is about the rail. It is not yet a verdict on the exclusive-rail *rule*. Restore the developer-to-user payment edge — sideloading, or a link to an external purchase — and the rule’s force disappears, because a prohibition on a direct tie is a constraint, not integrating work. The rail may still do authorizing and trust work the open edge does not reproduce, which is the split section 5.4 runs on a payment network: necessary or partial as a rail, contingent as a gate. Node and edge disagree on the gate. They need not disagree on the rail. The Digital Markets Act’s anti-steering obligations are the edge test run by a regulator on the gate.

Then, at ll. 441–442, replace “the operation differs from each” with “the operation differs from the first three; the nearer relative is named below.”

### 3.3 — Abstract, two clauses (keep ≤150 words)

Replace “Governing it takes a different competence, algorithmacy, and a different sovereignty.” with:

> Governing it takes algorithmacy — those competences indexed to the triad, not a new set of capacities — and a different sovereignty.

Replace “A counterfactual sorts mediators an actor can leave from those it can only govern and decides which institutions to build.” with:

> A counterfactual sorts mediators an actor can leave from those it can only govern and indicates which class of institution to build.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions.**

**Single most important fix.** Rewrite the §6 definition and the Key Terms entry so the construct is proper to *necessary* mediation, and mark share-in-governance as a specification of residual voice. That is the weld between the diagnostic and the headline claim.

**Biggest genuine strength.** The §5.2 factoring/bypass distinction together with the §5.3 Hirschman mapping: necessity forecloses exit; voice is the residual lever and is weakest where it is the only one; loyalty activates voice, involuntary retention activates nothing. That is the argument the rest of the chapter is entitled to run.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** Whether the third clause (share in governance / Schlager–Ostrom proprietorship) is the specification of voice this chapter wants, or whether contestability-without-share would suffice for the volume’s institutional program. The diagnostic will not decide it. Hunt et al. (2025) and companion-paper scope remain author-side if a referee asks; they are not argument defects in this text.
