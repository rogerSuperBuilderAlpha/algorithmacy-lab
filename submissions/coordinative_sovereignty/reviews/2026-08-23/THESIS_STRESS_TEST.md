# Thesis stress test — Algorithmacy and Sovereignty — 2026-08-23

**Question tested:** does the chapter's thesis survive an independent analysis of the literature?
**Design.** Five analysts worked blind: none was shown the chapter, the construct names, the diagnostic, or any project file. Each received only a neutrally worded research question ("for actors who cannot feasibly stop using an algorithmic platform intermediary, what kind of autonomy, power, or accountability is available, and through what mechanisms?") with six neutral sub-questions, and was instructed to answer from the published literature of one discipline: organization theory and organizational sociology; platform economics and IO; political theory; law and regulation; communication, HCI, and sociology of work. Each ran 10–27 searches and cited only works verified this session. Their full reports are in `blind_panels/`. This file maps what they independently produced onto the chapter's thesis, component by component. Convergence between a blind panel and the chapter is evidence the thesis tracks the literature rather than curating it; divergence is a finding.
**Limits.** Consensus was unavailable (quota) and the open APIs rate-limited, so panels leaned on Wiley full text plus open-web verification; each report states its coverage limits. Blindness is imperfect in principle (an analyst could stumble on the public algorithmacy material), but no panel's citations or vocabulary show contact with it.

## Component analyses

### T1. Fourth coordination mode

The organization theory panel, unprompted, called it the field's "near-consensus... that the algorithmic platform is not adequately described as market, hierarchy, or network" and reached first for Stark and Pais's co-optation, quoting the fourth verb the chapter builds on. That is direct blind confirmation of the chapter's §3 anchor. The same panel, and the economics panel independently, then supplied the rivals: meta-organization (Kretschmer et al.), organized markets and partial organization (Kirchner and Schüßler; Ahrne and Brunsson), evaluative infrastructures (Kornberger et al.), private regulator (Boudreau and Hagiu), and contested relational structures (Schüßler et al., with the explicit line that platforms "are different things at the same time"). The blind result therefore matches the earlier gap sweep exactly: the substance of §3 is the field's, and its exposure is presenting Stark as the lone predecessor. The verdict is that T1 holds as a claim about the world and fails as a claim of priority; the fix is positioning, not retreat.

### T2. The triad

Four panels described a three-party structure without being asked to choose. Organization theory: "the field is emphatic that the structure is at least three-party," citing the triangular geometry and Curchod's seller-buyer-platform study. Communication: "the field explicitly treats the structure as triadic, not dyadic." Law: "emphatically three-party... in its positive description," with the sharp addition that regulation carves the triangle into regulated bilateral legs and that labour law deliberately collapses it into employment. Economics: "irreducibly three-party," and here is the caution, because the panel dates that recognition to Rochet and Tirole 2003, where two-sidedness is defined by the failure of the Coase theorem across the sides. The chapter's dyadic-inheritance claim is therefore field-specific: true of the communication, literacy, and much of the algorithmic-management literatures, false of platform economics, and split within political theory, where the labor-republican wing (Muldoon and Raekstad) treats the algorithm as the boss's instrument in a two-party relation while Lazar's intermediary power is three-registered and includes "power between mediatees." The chapter should state the inheritance claim with that index and cite Lazar and labour law's collapse move as the two live alternatives. The law panel's observation that the employment presumption is a deliberate de-triangulation is worth a sentence in §8: recharacterization is a rival remedy to standing within the triad, and the chapter already half-says this.

### T3. Disclosure fails; the gap is standing

This is the thesis's strongest component, and the convergence is striking because each panel reached it by a different mechanism. Organization theory: opacity is a design choice and a control resource (Rahman; Rosenblat and Stark), and disclosed metrics provoke reactivity (Espeland and Sauder), so "transparency invites gaming, opacity produces arbitrary domination." Economics: information asymmetry defeats disclosure duties, equilibrium incentives run to obfuscation (Peitz and Sobolev 2025), and even perfect wage transparency coexists with monopsony power near elasticity 0.1 (Dube et al. 2020), so the field moved "from information remedies to power remedies." Political theory: the problem is uncontrolled power, not hidden information, so disclosure governs how power is used without removing it. Law: the "transparency fallacy" (Edwards and Veale), the Commission's own P2B review (a third of platforms disclosing, mediation "virtually unused"), and Kaminski and Urban's condition that transparency is "an enabling condition rather than a substitute" for a duty to hear and decide. Communication: Ananny and Crawford plus platform delegitimation of bottom-up knowledge as gaming. The chapter's abstract sentence, that disclosure makes an object legible while the actor needs standing, is independently derivable from all five literatures. Two enrichments the panels add: the reactivity dilemma (Espeland and Sauder) gives §3.2 a mechanism it currently lacks for why platforms cannot simply disclose, and Dubal's ban argument marks a boundary the chapter should note, that for some practices (personalized wage-setting) the field's answer is prohibition, not standing.

### T4. The competence

Organization theory and communication independently produced the competence literature the chapter builds on, under its own names (algorithmic competencies, folk theories, gossip, literacies, score management), with the chapter's own qualifications: real but fragile, revocable at the next update, socially produced, unequally distributed, and stigmatized by platforms as gaming. Blind confirmation extends to the distributional claim (Koo and Eesley's rural sellers "particularly poor at adjusting"; Bishop's gossip networks; Zhou et al.'s peer-support antecedent). Two qualifications survive as findings. First, the economics panel judges participant savvy "largely zero-sum across competing sellers": if rank-seeking mainly redistributes position among the coordinated, then democratizing the competence raises no one's aggregate standing, and §9's democratization question needs that possibility stated, because it cuts against the literacy analogy (mass literacy was not zero-sum). Second, the political theory panel holds that under a republican analysis "freedom that depends on outwitting the master is still domination," which is not a refutation but a blind restatement of the chapter's own precondition-not-sufficiency claim in §6; it confirms that the chapter is right to make competence necessary for exercise rather than constitutive of standing. The comm panel's Bucher point (imaginaries shape conduct even when wrong) repeats the Heiland challenge to §9's accuracy criterion.

### T5. The sorting

The exit-first, voice-fallback architecture was reproduced blind in four fields. Economics states it as its own structure: "where exit is feasible, protect it... where exit is structurally infeasible, move to the regulated toolkit," with escapability determined by network effects, homing and switching costs, data feedback, and contractual restraints, and with parity clauses analyzed precisely as instruments that close the exit door. Law runs the same fork ("the law shifts from protecting the exit option to disciplining the terms of staying") and supplies a doctrinal cousin of the bypass test: abuse-of-dependence regimes turn on whether the weaker party has "sufficient and reasonable alternatives." Political theory has the fork as a named internal debate (Taylor's exit republicanism against the Anderson wing) and resolves it the chapter's way: where exit is structurally unavailable, stop relying on it. Organization theory adds the master variable, dependence, and communication adds turnover evidence that where exit is feasible it is the dominant response and platforms "solicit neither loyalty nor voice" (Charles and Gunderson 2024), which independently supports the chapter's Hirschman worry that voice is weakest exactly where it is the only lever. What no panel produced is the chapter's own contribution: a checkable counterfactual run on an edge rather than a node, applied function by function. The nearest structures are the legal alternatives test (node-flavored), Hagiu and Wright's leakage model (the platform's side of the same counterfactual), and Li and Choi's bridge decay. The diagnostic's novelty survives the blind test; its two flagged weaknesses also survive: contingency is manipulated over time (the treacherous turn), and escapability partly tracks the actor's position outside the platform (Schor), which the chapter's per-actor relativization in §5.2 accommodates but should cite.

### T6. Standing where exit is foreclosed

The convergence here is the headline result. Each panel, asked only what remains when leaving is not feasible, produced the chapter's §7 inventory: contestation and appeal rights, collective bargaining and organization, data pooling and worker-led audits, interoperability as regulated access, cooperative ownership at the ambitious margin, and each independently judged individual and informational mechanisms insufficient without collectivization. Law compressed it into a phrase the chapter could quote as an independent formulation of coordinative sovereignty's neighborhood: the dependent actor's realistic good outcome is "accountable, contestable, collectively counterbalanced governance rather than escape." Economics closed with a sentence that is nearly the chapter's thesis stated from outside: "for the truly unavoidable intermediary, the dependent actor's autonomy is not self-generated; it is institutionally conferred, by regulators and collective institutions, or not at all." On ownership, three panels independently reached "neither necessary nor sufficient" (economics, law, communication), organization theory reached "one route among several rather than the definition of success," and political theory mapped the three-way split the chapter already engages, with no one defending sufficiency. The chapter's §6 claim that worker ownership does not make the mediator leaveable was reproduced by the political theory panel via Muldoon and Raekstad's own concession. Two additions: the effectiveness record for case-level contestation is worse than the chapter implies (Vaccaro et al.'s "Facebook does what it wants"; Gegenhuber et al.'s "microphones, not megaphones"; the P2B review), which strengthens the chapter's insistence that case-level voice is not yet a share in governance; and the countervailing-power literature (Galbraith through Gaudin, and the Australian bargaining code with final-offer arbitration) supplies a formal condition for when collective voice actually moves terms, which §7 currently lacks.

### T7 and T8

The blind design did not put the sovereignty vocabulary in play, so the independence of the two sovereignties was not directly tested. Indirect signs point the chapter's way: no panel treated control of infrastructure as the dependent actor's goal, law named the condition "regulated dependence," and the political economy panels located power outside the ownership question. The historical pairing of sovereignty with communicative competence produced nothing in any panel; the fields simply do not carry the claim, for or against. T8 therefore remains the chapter's own construction, exposed only to the already-flagged secondary-orality objection (Venturini) and the great-divide caveats, and the chapter is right to hold it as framing rather than as a finding.

## Divergences that require text changes

1. §3: share the fourth-form space (T1). Confirms the gap sweep's C3–C6; no new drafting beyond what LITERATURE_GAPS.md already specifies.
2. §1 and §4: index the dyadic-inheritance claim to the communication and literacy traditions, and concede that platform economics has been triadic since Rochet and Tirole 2003 and that labor republicanism remains deliberately dyadic. One sentence each.
3. §9: state the zero-sum possibility for the competence, against the literacy analogy, citing Koo and Eesley and the reputation-investment literature. This is the one genuinely new theoretical challenge the blind test surfaced.
4. §3.2: add the reactivity mechanism (Espeland and Sauder 2007) and note the prohibition boundary (Dubal 2023).
5. §7: add the countervailing-power condition (Gaudin 2018; Fumagalli and Motta 2008; the Australian code as the worked case of voice with teeth), and the empirical hollowness of current appeal channels (Vaccaro et al. 2020; Gegenhuber et al. 2021), which supports the case-level versus rule-level distinction the chapter already draws.
6. §8: name recharacterization (the employment presumption as deliberate de-triangulation) as a rival remedy to standing within the triad.

## Bottom line

The thesis holds up. The load-bearing chain, triadic coordination that disclosure cannot govern, an exit-or-voice fork decided by escapability, and standing as the residual good whose ownership is neither necessary nor sufficient, was reproduced independently by four to five blind disciplinary panels, twice in formulations close enough to quote against the chapter. What the blind test adds to the fix list beyond the earlier audit and sweep is small and specific: the priority claims must be shared (fourth form, triad), the democratization question must face the zero-sum finding, and §3.2 and §7 each gain one mechanism (reactivity; the countervailing-power condition). The components that found no echo, the historical competence-sovereignty pairing and the named construct itself, are exactly the ones the chapter offers as its contribution, and the panels' silence there is the shape a genuine contribution should have: the fields walk up to the gap from five directions and none of them fills it.
