# Blind panel 1 — Organization theory and organizational sociology (2026-08-23)

Analyst saw only the neutral research question in blind_brief.md; no chapter, constructs, or project files.

## Q1 verdict
Near-consensus: the algorithmic platform is a distinctive coordination mode or organizational form, not market, hierarchy, or network. Anchors independently produced: Vallas & Schor 2020 ("distinct type of governance mechanism"); Stark & Pais 2020 (fourth verb "co-opt"; Möbius topology; triangular geometry); Kretschmer et al. 2022 (meta-organizations); Karanović et al. 2020 (new form of organizing, Puranam problems); Kirchner & Schüßler 2019 and Aspers et al. 2022 (organized markets); Robinson et al. 2025 (market and hierarchy interwoven by task); Kornberger, Pflueger & Mouritsen 2017 (evaluative infrastructures). Structure: "emphatic that the structure is at least three-party" (Stark & Pais; Curchod et al. 2020 customers as relays of algorithmic control; Öborn et al. 2024; Healy & Pekarek 2024); rival reading Schüßler et al. 2021 (contested relational structures: "platforms can do different things at the same time because they are different things at the same time").

## Q2 verdict
Deep skepticism of transparency: opacity is a design choice (Rahman 2021 invisible cage), information asymmetry is the control resource (Rosenblat & Stark 2016), "no one is accountable" under algorithmic accounting (Stark & Pais 2020), and any disclosed metric provokes reactivity (Espeland & Sauder 2007 AJS). Dilemma: transparency invites gaming, opacity produces arbitrary domination. Working conditions: disclosure designed backward from outcomes (Geng 2023). Alternatives: advance notice, third-party dispute resolution, standing for associations (Cutolo & Kenney 2021); worker-led audits from pooled data (Calacci & Pentland 2022); reputational scoring of platforms (Fairwork; Graham et al. 2025).

## Q3 verdict
Named competences: algorithmic competency (Jarrahi & Sutherland lineage; Zhou et al. 2025 scale), working the algorithm via affordances (Bellesia et al. 2023), reactivity tactics (Rahman 2021), resistance co-constituted with control (Cameron & Rahman 2022), algoactivism (Kellogg et al. 2020), opportunistic agency (Öborn et al. 2024). Effectiveness: real but fragile, decays on criterion change; stratified by dependence (Schor et al. 2020); some felt autonomy is neo-normative control (Morales & Stecher 2023; Galière 2020).

## Q4 verdict
Exit a spectrum (multihome, partial disintermediation, bypass) governed by network effects, non-portable reputation, customer-relationship control, unilateral terms; dependence the master variable (Cutolo & Kenney 2021 via Emerson). Platforms defend: punish multihoming (Chung et al. 2025), kill-zone copying (Zhu & Liu 2018; Motta & Shelegia 2025). Disintermediation feasible where repeated, local, trust-based (Öborn et al. 2024; Fitz & Scheeg 2025; Maffie 2023 "becoming pirates", plus cognitive blockage: acquired dependence). Policy: portability/interoperability, effects conditional (Kim 2026; Bourreau et al. 2026).

## Q5 verdict
Platform voice channels are "microphones, not megaphones" (Gegenhuber, Ellmer & Schüßler 2021 Human Relations); voice migrates outside: algoactivism, litigation, protest (Kellogg et al.; Dasgupta et al. 2025; Rafélis de Broves et al. 2024); subordinated agency antagonism without employment (Wood & Lehdonvirta 2021); recruiting the state = "regulated dependence" (Karanović et al. 2020); intermediate institutions needed (Maric, Schüssler & Thäter 2025). Cooperativism: genuine advantages, persistent capital/network-effect/governance barriers (Ghirlanda & Kirov 2024; Bunders et al. 2022). Data pooling (Calacci & Pentland). Contestation rights discussed mostly as a gap (Cutolo & Kenney). Workers want voice plus flexibility (Martindale, Wood & Burchell 2024).

## Q6 verdict
No single name: countervailing power (Vallas & Schor; Cutolo & Kenney), effective voice / functional work system (Gegenhuber; Maric), accountability (Stark & Pais; Besio et al. 2024). Ownership: neither sufficient nor strictly necessary; one route among several. Non-domination vocabulary sits at the border with political philosophy (Ferretti 2020), not the field's core.

## Overall answer
The actor inside an unleavable algorithmic intermediary is inside a genuinely novel governance form; available autonomy is real but residual and reactive; durable influence lies almost entirely in mechanisms operating on the platform from outside the dyad: collective organization, regulation, sectoral institutions, pooled data and audits, reputational scoring, portability, and at the margin cooperative ownership. No purely individual, informational, or intra-platform mechanism suffices.
