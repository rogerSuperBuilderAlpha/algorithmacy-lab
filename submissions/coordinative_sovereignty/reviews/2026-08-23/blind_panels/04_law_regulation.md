# Blind panel 4 — Law and regulation of platforms, EU and comparative (2026-08-23)

Analyst saw only the neutral research question in blind_brief.md.

## Q1 verdict
Settled position: the platform is a private regulatory order that doctrine initially failed to see. Cohen 2017 UC Davis L Rev (private ordering plus reordering of bedrock rights; functional sovereignty; utility analogy) opened full text. Rahman 2018 Cardozo (infrastructural goods, private power over necessities). Velasco 2026 ELJ: formal independence "conceal[s] a situation of factual subordination"; Art. 102 dominance framework cannot capture relational power. Bostoen & Petit 2024 "treacherous turn": induce relation-specific investment, then flip terms once dependence locks. Adams-Prassl 2019: algorithmic management as concentrated employer functions with disclaimed status. Structure: emphatically three-party in positive description; instruments carve the triangle into regulated bilateral legs (P2B; consumer law; DMA); labour law deliberately COLLAPSES the triangle via the employment presumption in Directive 2024/2831 (Countouris & De Stefano 2025). Digital constitutionalism evaluates platform rule-making by rule-of-law criteria (Suzor 2018; Frosio & Geiger 2023).

## Q2 verdict
First-generation transparency underdelivers. Edwards & Veale 2017 "transparency fallacy". Empirical vindication: COM(2023) 525 P2B review — about a third of services list ranking parameters, vague where present, 42.4% low alignment, fifteen member states enforcing. Bostoen & Petit: sunlight premise fails in tipped markets — the informed dependent has nowhere to go. Leerssen 2023 CLSR: dynamic personalized ranking defeats individual counterfactuals ("demotion" unprovable); propose systemic transparency (researcher access, analytics, changelogs). Convergent condition: transparency must couple to actionability — "an enabling condition rather than a substitute" for a duty to hear and decide (Kaminski & Urban 2021 Columbia L Rev). Alternatives when disclosure fails: ex ante design duties, collective informational rights vested in representatives (Adams-Prassl et al. 2023 ELLJ blueprint), audits and DSA Art. 40 access, and prohibition where no disclosure suffices — Dubal 2023 Columbia L Rev: nonwaivable ban on algorithmic wage discrimination.

## Q3 verdict
Doctrinal field largely silent on participant literacies; the socio-legal competence is legal mobilization, unevenly distributed (Cowan 2004 MLR; Trautner et al. 2013 Law & Policy). Paradigm platform case: ADCU/Worker Info Exchange wielding GDPR Arts. 15 and 22 collectively — Amsterdam Court of Appeal, April 2023, Uber and Ola ordered to disclose and explain automated deactivation decisions. Distribution bleak: P2B review documents low rights-awareness among protected business users. Implicit remedy: institutionalize the competence in collectives (representatives), not train individuals.

## Q4 verdict
Escapability jointly determined by market structure and legal-contractual restraint, with doctrine for both. DMA literature: contestability absent where network effects leave "no realistic alternatives" (Crémer et al. 2023 Yale J Reg). Restraints: C-264/23 Booking.com — parity clauses not ancillary restraints; DMA Art. 5(4) anti-steering restores the disintermediation route. Non-escapability is itself a doctrinal test: abuse-of-economic-dependence and relative-market-power regimes (DE, FR, IT, JP, KR) turn on "sufficient and reasonable alternatives" (Velasco 2026; GRUR International 74(3):217 2025). Prescriptive split: exit merely costly → portability/interoperability (GDPR Art. 20, DMA Arts. 6(9)–(10), Data Act; effects real but modest, Graef); exit structurally unavailable → regulate the ongoing relationship (P2B floor, DMA conduct, dependence doctrines): the law shifts from protecting exit to disciplining the terms of staying.

## Q5 verdict
Layered catalogue with verdicts. Contestation/appeal: P2B Arts. 11–12, DSA Arts. 17/20/21, GDPR Art. 22; design argument: platforms are "private courthouses" needing separation of legislative/executive/judicial functions (Van Loo 2021 U Chi L Rev); Kaminski & Urban due-process architecture; Bayamlıoğlu 2021 R&G. Practice weak: automated generic complaint responses, mediation "virtually unused" (COM(2023) 525). Collective bargaining decoupled from employment status: 2022 Guidelines exempting solo self-employed; "negotiating the algorithm" (De Stefano 2019; De Stefano & Taes 2023), partially codified in Directive 2024/2831 algorithmic-management chapter. Data pooling: bottom-up data trusts (Delacroix & Lawrence 2019), DGA cooperatives, Uber/Ola-style litigation collectives; Dubal warns rights are outrun by changing models. Interoperability as continuing access. Structural: separation of platforms and commerce (Khan 2019); utility toolkit (Rahman 2018). Cooperativism mainly in adjacent literatures. Verdict: architecture has raced ahead of demonstrated effectiveness; individual channels systematically underused relative to collective and regulatory ones.

## Q6 verdict
Multiple names, no winner: fairness and contestability (DMA telos, Crémer et al.); legitimacy under the rule of law (digital constitutionalism — accountable government BY the platform, not liberation from it); countervailing power (labour and competition strands); anti-domination register imported from republican theory via the utility revival. Ownership: mainstream neither necessary nor sufficient (regulated private ownership with obligations and procedure); structuralist minority (Khan, Rahman) necessary where the platform competes with dependents; cooperativist sufficiency marginal in law proper; Dubal's fourth view: some practices no distribution of power legitimates.

## Overall answer
Independence is not on offer; law supplies "regulated dependence" in four layers: recharacterization (naming the relation to unlock protective law), procedure (contestable administration), collectivization (individual rights fail; aggregate them), structure (separation, utility duties, prohibition). All sides share the premise that the platform is a governor and the realistic good outcome is accountable, contestable, collectively counterbalanced governance rather than escape.

## Coverage limits
High confidence on EU regulatory literature and US platform-power canon (many opened full text). Moderate where record-only (Suzor, Delacroix & Lawrence, De Stefano, Khan, Rahman, C-264/23, 2022 Guidelines). Wiley skew for passage-level access; CMLR/CLSR/US reviews via open web. Q3 an interpretive synthesis. Non-EU/US coverage limited to East Asian dependence doctrines plus one China piece (He 2022 P&I).
