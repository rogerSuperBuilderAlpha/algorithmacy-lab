# Blind panel 3 — Political theory and political philosophy (2026-08-23)

Analyst saw only the neutral research question in blind_brief.md.

## Q1 verdict
Dominant move: deny the relation is market at all; redescribe as government/private power. Anderson 2017 private government template; Muldoon & Raekstad 2023 "algorithmic domination" with the algorithm as the bosses' tool — for labor republicans the structure is fundamentally two-party. Second strand genuinely three-party: Lazar 2025 P&PA "Governing the Algorithmic City", intermediary power in three registers including power BETWEEN mediatees (platform settles constitutive norms of relations between other parties); historically novel combination of structural scale with agential real-time adaptability. Third strand sovereignty-flavored: Pistor 2020 Constellations (digital power wielders rival statehood); D'Cunha 2021 ELJ (Tech Leviathans with a "supreme court" but no accountability); Aytac 2024 "quasi-public domination"; Rahman & Thelen 2019 (ecosystem control). Split verdict on parties: two-party dominates labor republicanism, three-party the governance-of-social-relations literature.

## Q2 verdict
Transparency necessary but radically insufficient because the problem is uncontrolled power, not hidden information (Anderson; Muldoon & Raekstad: disclosure governs how power is used, does not remove it). Vredenburgh 2022 JPP right to explanation grounded in informed self-advocacy (explanation for contestation, not as end). Lazar: procedural legitimacy insufficient without the authority question. Aytac: where users cannot exit, notice/consent "normatively insignificant"; contestatory-democratic institutions instead. Hoeksema 2023: assent, not consent, under structural dependency. Pistor: individual data rights cannot bite because value lies in aggregation. Dawson 2022: accountability beyond legality (openness, non-arbitrariness, effectiveness, publicness).

## Q3 verdict
Field largely silent on literacies as a category. Epistemic asymmetry theorized as a component of domination, not a skill deficit (Muldoon & Raekstad computational inequalities); Vredenburgh's informed self-advocacy the nearest normative competence, owed by institutions rather than acquired unaided. Borrowed empirics (algoactivism, out-of-app agency) read as evidence of agency but not an answer to domination: "freedom that depends on outwitting the master is still domination."

## Q4 verdict
Named internal debate on exit. Exit wing: Taylor 2017 *Exit Left* (state's job is real exit: competition, lower switching costs). Larger wing: exit fails under (i) structural dependence — switching changes the dominator, not the domination (Anderson; Muldoon & Raekstad); (ii) network effects/monopoly (Aytac; Pistor); (iii) non-portable stakes (Mannan & Schneider 2021 Exit to Community); (iv) consumer-side political entrenchment (Culpepper & Thelen 2020). Hoeksema: assent. Prescription: where feasible make exit cheap; where not, stop relying on it and build voice, contestation, or ownership (Anderson's ranking of voice over exit representative).

## Q5 verdict
Layered menu: (1) collective organization and the strike (Gourevitch 2016 right to strike; Sharratt 2019 unions + basic income; degraded by algorithmic redesign, Mendonça & Kougiannou 2022); (2) workplace democracy/codetermination (Landemore & Ferreras 2016; Frega, Herzog & Neuhäuser 2019); (3) contestation and appeal (Aytac contestatory mechanisms; Vredenburgh explanation as precondition; Lazar procedural legitimacy + democratic authorization); (4) ownership change: platform co-ops eliminate bosses but face competition and internal managerial domination (Muldoon & Raekstad concession); exit-to-community (Mannan & Schneider); platform socialism, plural multi-scalar ownership (Muldoon 2022); (5) structural regulation: utility treatment, middleware/interoperability (Fukuyama et al. 2021; Rahman); breakup skepticism (Pistor); (6) data pooling: negative result at individual level, points to collective data institutions. Field candid that effectiveness evidence is thin.

## Q6 verdict
The name exists: freedom as non-domination (Pettit via Hasan 2021 reconstruction). Live dispute: dyadic vs structural non-domination (Hasan 2021 PPQ; Gädeke 2020 JPP; Hoeksema). Companions: Lazar's liberty + relational equality + collective self-determination; Dawson accountability; countervailing power (Rahman & Thelen; Culpepper & Thelen); democratic voice (Anderson; Landemore & Ferreras). Ownership three ways: exit-republicans neither necessary nor sufficient; contestatory theorists not necessary; socialist republicans necessary but not sufficient. No one defends sufficiency.

## Notable silences
No political-theory treatment found of seller/developer dependence specifically; examples overwhelmingly gig workers and social media users. Q3 largely silent. Data cooperatives and interoperability thin in the field proper.
