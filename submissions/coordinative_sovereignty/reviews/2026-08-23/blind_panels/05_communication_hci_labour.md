# Blind panel 5 — Communication, media studies, HCI/CSCW, sociology of work (2026-08-23)

Analyst saw only the neutral research question in blind_brief.md.

## Q1 verdict
Near-consensus: distinct hybrid governance form combining market rhetoric with hierarchical control through code. Vallas & Schor 2020 (new governance mechanism); Kellogg, Valentine & Christin 2020 (new contested terrain; 6 Rs); Parth & Bathini 2021 (direct control by other means); Robinson et al. 2025 (rejects the binary; blends by task); Morales & Stecher 2023 (neo-normative control); Nieborg & Poell 2018 (platformization; contingent commodity). Explicitly triadic: triangular worker–platform–client relationship (Öborn et al. 2024); customers' ratings do the controlling (Duggan et al. 2023); Wood & Lehdonvirta 2021 "subordinated agency" — formal independence from clients, structural subordination to the platform; creators–platforms–audiences–advertisers (Duffy & Meisner 2023).

## Q2 verdict
Distinctively skeptical; opacity is itself a mode of power. Ananny & Crawford 2018 (transparency ideal fails); Burrell 2016 (three opacities; disclosure remedies only the first); Rosenblat & Stark 2016 (information asymmetry as soft control); Alacovska et al. 2024 (algorithmic paranoia; affective governance); Petre, Duffy & Hund 2019 (platform paternalism delegitimizes bottom-up knowledge as gaming). Conditions: outcome-targeted, coupled to power (Geng 2023). Alternatives adversarial and collective: Turkopticon (Irani & Silberman 2013 CHI); Shipt pay-data pooling (Calacci & Pentland 2022); everyday algorithm auditing (Shen et al. 2021); Fairwork public scoring (Graham et al. 2020, 2025).

## Q3 verdict
Signature contribution under several names: algorithmic competencies (Jarrahi & Sutherland 2019; Zhou et al. 2025 validated scale: understand, embrace, leverage, remediate; predicted by peer support); folk theories and the algorithmic imaginary (Bucher 2017; Eslami et al. 2015); visibility game (Cotter 2019); algorithmic gossip (Bishop 2019); algorithm literacy (Zhang & Liu 2024 JASIST); score affordances (Bellesia et al. 2022). Effectiveness deliberately ambivalent: real but bounded and revocable; visible gaming punished. Distribution unequal: socially produced, thin among the isolated, older adults, and marginalized creators who pay extra "literacy labor" (Duffy & Meisner 2023; Karizat et al. 2021).

## Q4 verdict
Formal freedom to leave coexists with material dependence. Schor et al. 2020: dependence stratifies; escapability is a function of the worker's position outside the platform, not platform design. Charles & Gunderson 2024 JTSB: exit is the dominant response where feasible; platforms solicit neither loyalty nor voice. Out-of-app bypass where work is relational and local (Öborn et al. 2024). Complementor multihoming (Abolfathi 2025); mutualism degrades to commensalism (Gastaldi et al. 2024). Creators: exit mostly illusory; audiences non-portable; deplatformed migration collapses reach (Rogers 2020); algorithmic precarity (Duffy 2020). Determinants: outside options, asset specificity of audience/reputation, network effects, relational vs standardized work. Policy: portability and social-graph interoperability (Vergne 2025).

## Q5 verdict
Layered repertoire, sober verdicts. Individual: everyday resistance in the app's seams (Reid-Musson et al. 2020), score management, identity-based algorithmic resistance (Karizat et al. 2021). Collective: worker communities as seedbeds (Maffie 2020); geographically varying organizing (Johnston 2020); Dynamo's documented friction (Salehi et al. 2015); counter-audit data pooling. Appeals hollow in practice: "at the end of the day Facebook does what it wants" (Vaccaro, Sandvig & Karahalios 2020); mass-reporting victims unsupported (Meisner 2023); TikTok visibility moderation opaque (Zeng & Kaye 2022). Cooperativism promising but capital/scale-constrained (Ghirlanda & Kirov 2024); risks entrepreneurial activism substituting for movement-building (Sandoval 2020). Platforms counter-organize: intraplatform algorithmic changes fragmenting solidarity (Mendonça & Kougiannou 2023); churn and voluntariness narratives suppress solidarity (Newlands et al. 2018; Permana 2025). Antagonism emerges from dependence even without employment (Wood & Lehdonvirta 2021). Regulatory complements necessary (Maric et al. 2025).

## Q6 verdict
Three coexisting vocabularies: worker voice and countervailing collective power (IR wing; Fairwork's five principles including fair management and representation); algorithmic accountability and meaningful contestability (Ananny & Crawford; Vaccaro et al.); preserved everyday agency and organized resistance capacity (Bonini & Treré 2024). Creator studies: stable non-arbitrary visibility ending algorithmic precarity. Ownership: cooperativist tradition close-to-necessary; field's larger position neither necessary nor sufficient (Sandoval on ownership without movement power; voice/regulation strand on disciplining private owners).

## Overall answer
Autonomy is not the formal independence platforms advertise (a control strategy) but three stacked capacities: interpretive power (literacies; fragile, unequal, revocable), associational power (communities, unions, counter-infrastructures; actively countered by platforms), institutional power (appeal rights largely hollow as implemented; certification; interoperability; sectoral bargaining; ownership transformative only when paired with collective power). Dependence, not the algorithm per se, converts platform coordination into domination; the converging prescription is accountability-with-voice from below plus policy that makes exit credible.
