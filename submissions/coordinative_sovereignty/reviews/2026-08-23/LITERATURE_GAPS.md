# Literature gap sweep — Algorithmacy and Sovereignty — 2026-08-23

**Manuscript:** `chapter/chapter.md` (18 Aug 14:06). Companion to `EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md` (same date), which audited the sources already in the text. This file covers what is not in the text.
**Method:** seven parallel sweeps by strand (algorithmic management and literacy; platform power and economics; digital sovereignty and EU regulation; political theory of platforms; coordination theory and media history; the chapter's worked cases; competing constructs and term ownership). Each ran 12–27 queries. Every item below was verified to exist this session by DOI landing page, publisher or repository record, Scholar Gateway full text, or API record; confidence is stated. Items already cited or already proposed in `EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md` were excluded.
**Database limits.** Consensus is exhausted for August (resets 1 Sept). OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, and Crossref APIs rate-limited or refused the proxy for most of the run, so no cited-by trails ran and no citation counts were pulled; coverage outside Wiley titles (Scholar Gateway) relied on targeted title searches. A follow-up pass on 1 Sept with Consensus and OpenAlex should pull cited-by for the eight collision items in §1 below. Elsevier tourism journals and Sage/T&F political theory 2024–26 are the thinnest patches.

This is a review-only pass. No edits were made to the manuscript.

## Verdict

The chapter's reference list is strong on its own spine (Stark, Kellogg, Möhlmann, DeVito, Pettit, Hirschman, Ostrom) and thin where neighbouring fields have already built constructs on the same object. Eight works collide directly with the chapter's core claims and a referee in the relevant field will expect each to be positioned against, not merely cited. Beyond those, the sweep found roughly thirty works a field referee would mark as missing and a dozen that contest specific claims. The most exposed section is §3: three literatures (meta-organization, partial organization, evaluative infrastructures) already name platforms as a form beyond markets, hierarchies, and networks, and the chapter presents co-optation as if Stark were the only predecessor. The second most exposed is §4's novelty claim, because Möhlmann et al. (2021, MISQ) already fuses matching with control, which is the chapter's "matches it to a counterpart and scores how that match goes."

## 4. Term ownership (for Key Terms and anonymization)

"Algorithmacy": zero hits in Crossref bibliographic search and the Wiley corpus; web search returns only the author's Substack and algorithmacy.org. The coinage is safe to claim, and Key Terms should say so while naming the established neighbours: algorithmic literacy (Dogruel; Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum), algorithmic competency (Jarrahi & Sutherland, scaled by Zhou et al. 2025), algorithmic competence (Savolainen & Ruckenstein 2024), coding literacy (Vee 2017), computational literacy (diSessa 2000), data infrastructure literacy (Gray, Gerlitz & Bounegru 2018).

"Algorithmic sovereignty" has two owners, neither close: state geopolitics (Liang & Li 2025, P&I 17(3)) and Indigenous data governance (Brown et al. 2024, Data Science Journal 23). "Coordination rights" is Paul's. "Infrastructural power" is Mann's, applied to platforms by Valdez (2023, R&G 17(1)) and Rahman & Thelen (2019). "Algorithmic citizenship" is Cheney-Lippold's (2016). "Technological citizenship" is Frankenfeld (1992), updated by Gardenier et al. (2024). "Relational sovereignty" is Bannerman (cited). "Platform sovereignty" is not an established title-term; the idea lives under Cohen, D'Cunha (2021, ELJ), Lehdonvirta, and Törnberg (2023, BD&S). "Computational sovereignty", "network sovereignty", "mediated autonomy", and "data agency" have no established literatures. "Digital self-determination" (Verhulst 2023, Data & Policy 5) is the nearest rival on the data side.

## 5. Research gaps confirmed by the sweep

No peer-reviewed study yet quantifies post-2024 Booking DMA compliance effects on hotel dependence; Pantazi 2026 and Lasio et al. 2025 (NET Institute WP 25-07) are the frontier. No academic analysis of the P2B repeal's protection gap beyond Nolteernsting, who argues the opposite. No empirical work on DGA intermediaries conferring bargaining standing. No study of algorithmic competence concentrating in large versus small dependent firms (Klawitter & Hargittai is the nearest, at the seller level). No 2024–2026 EJPT or Political Theory piece on platforms surfaced beyond those already cited or proposed, which suggests §6 is current in that venue. The creator-expertise market sub-strand is thin after Bishop 2020 and MacDonald 2023.
