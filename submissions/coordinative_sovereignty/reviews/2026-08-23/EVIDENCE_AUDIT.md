## Verdict

The citation circuit holds (0 orphans, 0 ghosts, all 109 entries plus 2 cases exist). The evidentiary layer underneath it does not yet meet the two house rules. Four factual errors need correction before submission, roughly twenty load-bearing sentences carry no source at all, and about thirty more rest on a single source where a verified second exists. Most of the unsourced material is legal or historical description that the chapter states correctly but does not cite: the five EU instruments named in §1 are characterised, quoted by article, and contrasted throughout without a single reference-list entry among them. Fixing that alone closes a third of the findings.

Two items from the 17 Aug panel's "do before press" list are still in the text: `Gagričin` (two occurrences) and the §8 opening cite that asks Fratini and Hummel to operationalize organizational digital sovereignty.

## 5. Legal and time-sensitive status as of 23 August 2026

Case T-438/25 (Apple v. Commission) is pending; no judgment or order located. The General Court's 8 July 2026 ruling against Apple concerns the 2023 gatekeeper designation, not the anti-steering fine; do not conflate. On 18 August 2026 the Commission welcomed Apple's revised business terms (effective October 2026) and said it is not considering periodic penalties. "Pending" remains correct.

Directive 2024/2831: deadline 2 December 2026. No member state has notified full transposition. Germany drafting (May 2026); Netherlands Platform Work Act in consultation (21 Aug 2026). The chapter's sentence holds with a date stamp.

COM(2025) 837 (Digital Omnibus, data): still a proposal. A blocking minority led by Germany and Poland prevented a Council general approach in late June 2026; Parliament's ITRE/LIBE position is due after the summer. The tabled text still repeals P2B (Art. 10(1), with cross-referenced provisions surviving to 31 Dec 2032 under Art. 10(2)) and folds the DGA into the Data Act, converting the mandatory intermediary regime into a voluntary one. "Would repeal" remains the right tense. Do not confuse with the Digital Omnibus on AI, which is now Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (see E3).

COM(2026) 178 exists (28 April 2026, with SWD(2026) 123). Booking.com gatekeeper designation: 13 May 2024 (IP/24/2561), four months before the 19 September 2024 judgment, as the chapter says.

Essential facilities: the CJEU narrowed Bronner in *Lithuanian Railways* (C-42/21 P, 2023) and *Alphabet/Android Auto* (C-233/23, Feb 2025: indispensability not required where the platform was built to be opened). Cite Bronner as the classic formulation and add a clause.

GDPR Art. 22: *SCHUFA* (C-634/21, 7 Dec 2023) treated a credit score as a "decision"; *Dun & Bradstreet Austria* (C-203/22, 27 Feb 2025) widened the explanation right. "Threshold is high" is no longer safe without qualification.

Anonymization: a web search for "algorithmacy" returns only the author's Substack and algorithmacy.org (conference, 28–31 Oct 2026), both defining the term as the chapter does. A referee searching the term deanonymizes the chapter in one click. Nothing can be done about the term itself; make sure no §4 wording points to either site.

## 8. Research gaps

G1. No study shows algorithmic competence concentrating in large platform-dependent *firms* versus small ones. The evidence is citizen-level (Cotter & Reisdorf; Gran et al.; Chung & Wihbey, where the gradient is uneven across countries) and a paid expertise market for creators (Bishop 2020). The §4 concentration claim is an inference and should say so. This is the clearest empirical opening the chapter could claim for the companion work.

G2. No peer-reviewed source quantifies how often major platforms retune ranking or matching systems. Koo & Eesley document one major change; Kohavi et al. document experimentation practice; "continuously" remains inference. DSA Art. 40 researcher-access studies may eventually supply it.

G3. No source documents a ranking platform scoring the existence of disputes regardless of outcome. The documented analogue is card-network chargeback monitoring; eBay's published rule is the opposite.

G4. No academic analysis yet exists of the substantive protection gap the P2B repeal would open (business-user framing, search engines, sub-threshold services). The chapter could claim this as its own contribution rather than asserting "the only duty".

G5. No source itemises assignment, deactivation, and ranking as bargained terms in any concluded platform agreement. Case material (Danish Hilfr/3F) was not verified this session.

G6. No empirical study of whether DGA-registered intermediaries confer bargaining standing; Carovano & Finck is doctrinal.

G7. "Mobile search" as a driver of OTA share growth: no source found; Schegg 2024 gives no causal account.

G8. The Goody/Ong "great divide" on which §2 and the "two changes in kind" rest is contested (Street 1984; Finnegan 1988; Halverson 1992). §4 concedes this for individual cognition; §2 does not. One sentence in §2 closes it.

G9. The literacy-to-democracy link (§2.2, §10) is contested by Graff's literacy-myth work and sits in tension with Paglayan as the chapter itself cites her.

G10. Dasgupta et al. 2025: whether the paper invokes Hirschman could not be confirmed from the abstract.

G11. Prior use of "algorithmacy": no independent academic usage found via web search; Consensus was unavailable and Google Scholar could not be fetched, so the search is not exhaustive.
