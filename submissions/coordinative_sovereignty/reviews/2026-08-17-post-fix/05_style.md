The register is already correct: impersonal APA chapter, zero first person, named agents in active voice. The Annals first-person rule does not apply. This seat judges habit, not voice: decorative antithesis, self-narrating rigor, metronomic punchy openers, agentless passives where a named agent is available, unearned definitional contrasts, BrE residue, Key Terms parallelism, and recurrence of `the argument` / `this chapter` meta frames. ### 1. Key Terms last sentence of coordinative sovereignty is a garden-path (L1319–1320)

**Claim.** The §0g B weld put the irreducibility/necessity distinction into the glossary and the sentence broke. H's tokens are gone; this is the new problem the weld introduced.

**Quote.**
> Irreducibility as a coordination is currently wired does not by itself establish the condition; necessity does. (L1319–1320)

**Why it damages style.** The body has the readable form: "nor irreducibility as the coordination is currently wired" (L606–607). Dropping `the` and standing the clause up as a subject produces a garden-path a volume editor will trip on. "The condition" then has no nearby noun. A glossary is the last place a definition should have to be re-parsed.

**Fix.** Keep the distinction; restore the body's grammar. See R1.

### 2. One leftover unearned contrast at the §3 open (L160–161)

**Claim.** Morning Finding 4. Not in §0g H, so not a failed fix — still the clearest decorative antithesis in the body.

**Quote.**
> The failure is not a shortfall of political will or a gap in drafting. It follows from what the coordinated actor is enrolled in. (L160–161)

**Why it damages style.** The positive sentence does the work. The `is not a` foil denies two motives nobody has yet offered. Earned neighbors (Berlin at L58, Street at L384, Ostrom at L635) stay.

**Fix.** Delete the middle sentence. See R2.

### 3. §2.2 still tells the reader the point matters (L124)

**Claim.** The morning panel discounted "One modest claim survives" (L129) and missed the clause in front of it. The paragraph performs its own importance before stating the claim.

**Quote.**
> Literacy was a clerical monopoly for most of its history, and the point matters more than the celebration usually allows. (L124–125)

**Why it damages style.** "The point matters more" is an emphasis marker in the sentence. The next sentence already states the point: those who could write governed those who could not. Cut the warrant; keep the monopoly and the tracking claim.

**Fix.** See R3.
