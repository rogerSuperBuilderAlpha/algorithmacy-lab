## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

The morning content spine was applied. Abstract standing-gap, Fratini sorting, §8 employment-presumption split, §7 DSA Arts. 20–21, Art. 13(3) threshold, and the cut of “further gatekeeper decisions” all hold in the live text. ### Finding 1 (damage: medium) — §8’s managerial paragraph restores a predict/contest vacuum the abstract and §3.2 no longer claim

**Claim.** After the §0g abstract fix, the chapter’s thesis is standing after disclosure and thin channels. The Implications paragraph that tells a manager what compliance buys still states the stronger gap.

**Quote.**

> A firm that invests in regulatory compliance secures a digital position and acquires no ability to predict or contest the algorithmic determinations that set its market outcomes. (813–814)

The same section has just treated the Platform-to-Business Regulation as more than disclosure:

> That Regulation does more than disclose, since it also requires internal complaint-handling, offers mediation, and gives representative organizations standing to bring proceedings, which makes it one of the few instruments to attach a channel to a disclosure. (787–789)

**Why it damages this lens.** A section delivers what it promises. §8’s independent-variation case is high digital sovereignty beside low coordinative sovereignty: compliance without standing. That case does not need a total inability to predict or to contest. Prediction is the algorithmacy the next paragraph correctly assigns to capability building (822–827). Contestation, thinly, is what P2B Arts. 11–12 and DSA Arts. 20–21 already are; §7’s table now says so (711). “No ability to predict or contest” is the abstract’s old vacuum, relocated. A referee who accepted the rewritten first sentence (5–6) and then hits this line will treat Implications as forgetting the concession the chapter just made.

**Fix.** Keep the exposure. Replace the vacuum with the standing gap the rest of the chapter argues. One sentence: compliance secures a digital position and does not confer standing over the determinations that set market outcomes.

### Finding 2 (damage: low) — one leftover “irreducible” after the definitional weld

**Claim.** §6, Key Terms, and §10 now key the construct to necessary mediation. The Muldoon paragraph still uses the predicate §5.2 separated from necessity.

**Quote.**

> Worker ownership can change who sets a necessary mediator's rules and cannot make the mediator leaveable. A driver-owned dispatch platform still coordinates its drivers through a system no single driver can bypass, so the question of standing within an irreducible coordination outlives the question of who holds the title. (666–669)

**Why it damages this lens.** The case is right: a driver-owned dispatch is still a necessary mediator. “Irreducible” is the wrong term of the pair §5.2 made load-bearing — as-wired non-factorability, which a contingent gate can also have (440–442). The weld at 601–607 and the Key Terms entry (1316–1320) already police that distinction. One word in a neighboring paragraph lets it slip.

**Fix.** “standing within a necessary mediation” (or “within a coordination the open tie does not dissolve”). Do not reopen the definition.

No other Part 1 findings. Clean stretches are in Part 2.
