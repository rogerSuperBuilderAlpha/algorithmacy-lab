**Venue:** IGI Global, *Organizational Implications of Digital Sovereignty in the Age of AI* (ed. Samuel Fosso Wamba)  
**Manuscript:** `chapter/chapter.md` (canonical live text after HANDOFF §0g; not `chapter_v2.md`)  
**Standpoint:** Hostile structure. Does oracy → literacy → algorithmacy earn coordinative sovereignty? Does the §5 bypass test license the §6 definition and the §7–§8 institutional program? Press make-or-buy / essential facilities, concept-stretching, DeVito novelty, Hirschman mapping, residual “no relationship to restore,” node vs edge on the App Store, whether §6 keys to *necessary* mediation, whether share-in-governance is marked as specification, and whether the abstract matches the body.

**Recommendation: accept-with-nits.** The spine holds. The morning panel’s three license defects — headline definition keyed to irreducibility / cannot-leave, App Store node and edge run on two objects, abstract overclaiming competence-novelty and institutional choice — are closed at the sites the fix named. That is one weld, not a new argument. No major. Do not reopen the morning findings as if they were still live.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 — The definitional weld landed at §6 and Key Terms; the cascade did not

**Claim.** Coordinative sovereignty is now properly keyed, at the headline definition, to a *necessary* mediation — an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve — and the share-in-governance clause is marked as a specification of residual voice rather than a return of the counterfactual. That is the weld the morning argument seat required. Four later or satellite sites still treat “cannot leave,” “cannot readily leave,” or “irreducible” as if they named the same condition. The most damaging of those is §9, which states the retired predicate as “the condition that defines the construct.”

**Quote.**

> Coordinative sovereignty is the autonomy proper to a necessary mediation: the power to shape the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the governance of an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve. A share in governance specifies what residual voice should amount to, and the counterfactual does not deliver it on its own. "Cannot leave" names that structural fact. It does not name lock-in under a contingent gate, which section 5.3 assigned to exit as involuntary retention, nor irreducibility as the coordination is currently wired, which section 5.2 separated from necessity. (ll. 601–607)

> The second is a validated instrument for coordinative sovereignty. Its standing dimensions — freedom from the mediator's arbitrary power, effective voice over the terms of mediation, and a share in the coordination's governance — would have to be measured under the condition that defines the construct: a mediator the actor cannot readily leave. (ll. 868–871)

> the standing it makes possible is coordinative sovereignty: effective voice within a coordination one cannot leave, carried far enough to be a share in how that coordination is governed. (ll. 913–914)

> whoever occupies the position of being coordinated through a mediation it cannot leave bears coordinative sovereignty. (ll. 645–647)

> the question of standing within an irreducible coordination outlives the question of who holds the title. (ll. 668–669)

> The alternative is coordinative sovereignty: standing within a coordination one cannot exit (ll. 65–66)

Key Terms *Coordinative sovereignty* (ll. 1316–1320) follows the §6 wording, including the irreducibility caveat. HANDOFF §0g claimed that “the Key Terms entry and §10’s compressed definition follow the same wording.” Key Terms does. §10 does not. §9 was not on the claimed-fix list; after the definition moved, it is the site that now contradicts the definition in so many words.

The four leftover predicates are not interchangeable, and the chapter has already said so.

| Predicate | Chapter’s own meaning | Licensed use after the fix |
|---|---|---|
| Irreducible / constitutive | Triadic *as wired*; does not factor | §5.1; Key Terms *Irreducibility*; not the construct |
| Necessary | Survives restoration of the capability of direct dealing | §5.2; §6 definition; Key Terms *Necessary mediator* |
| Cannot leave / cannot exit | Hirschman consequence of necessity; also the surface of lock-in | Licensed only as the consequence of the open tie doing no work |
| Cannot readily leave | Switching-cost / lock-in inclusive | Involuntary retention; contingent column; not the construct |

§9’s inclusion rule (“cannot readily leave”) pulls in the locked-in actor under a contingent gate — the case §5.3 named involuntary retention and assigned to exit. An instrument built on that rule would not discriminate the construct §6 defined. Discriminant validity against Spreitzer then tests the wrong contrast: “unexitable external mediation” (l. 872) is still the lock-in predicate, not necessity. The two-step design two paragraphs later (ll. 879–884) already classifies mediators first; that design is the right inclusion rule, and it is not the one §9 announces as definitional.

§10 is the official compression a volume referee reads last. “Cannot leave” without the §6 caveat returns the predicate the diagnostic was built to unmask. “Carried far enough to be a share” is the right mark for the third clause; it should sit on the necessity wording, not on the retired one.

The bearer sentence and the Muldoon leftover are the same slip inside the section that just taught the distinction. “Cannot leave” in the bearer paragraph, two screens after the caveat, invites a reader to treat the caveat as withdrawn. “Irreducible coordination” in the cooperative-ownership paragraph is the exact confusion §5.2 forbade: a driver-owned dispatch that no single driver can bypass is a *necessary* mediator, not merely an as-wired triad. §1’s working definition (ll. 65–67) and the Bodin attributes (“a mediation the actor cannot exit,” ll. 77–78) are sequential introduction, not a second official wording — lower damage, still the sites a copyeditor will collate against abstract, §6, Key Terms, and §10.

**Why it damages this lens.** A referee who accepts the diagnostic and the new §6 can still reject the measurement program as operationalizing lock-in, and can still quote the conclusion as evidence that the chapter did not keep its own distinction. The institutional program does not depend on these sentences. The headline construct now does, and these sentences undo it.

**Fix.** Copy the §6 predicate to §9’s inclusion rule, to §10’s compression, to the bearer sentence, and to the Muldoon leftover. Leave the §6 definition and the Key Terms entry alone. Optional: bring §1’s working definition onto “necessary mediation” / “open tie” so the four official sites match. Do not reopen share-in-governance as a deduction; the specification mark at ll. 603–604 is the right mark and should travel with the wording.

## Part 2 — Joints and license audit

### J1. Oracy → literacy → algorithmacy → coordinative sovereignty

**Partially earned; not ornamental; not a deduction.**

The series earns two things. First, the medium–competence–accountability pairing (Habermas in §1; literacy as clerical monopoly; digital sovereignty as extended literacy on the regulatory strand, with the industrial strand acknowledged and set aside). That pairing is why a firm can satisfy GDPR / AI Act / DMA / DSA / NIS2 and still be helpless: those instruments extend literacy, and platform coordination is not a text. Second, algorithmacy as a third competence, indexed to a third coordination regime (presence and memory; persistent document; opaque adaptive mediator). §4’s kind-claim pin keeps the change of kind on the regime, not on the mind. Cut §2 and the competence claim becomes a rename of algorithmic literacy without a reason the volume’s readers must accept.

The series does not earn coordinative sovereignty. Oral and literate sovereignties are a public holding a polity to account through the medium. Coordinative sovereignty is a dependent party holding a private mediator to account from inside a coordination the open tie does not dissolve. Bearer and object both move. §1 already says the pattern “does not cover” platform coordination (ll. 47–48) and introduces the bypass question as what sets the situation apart. The historical frame is setup for the competence and for the sovereignty *register* (“standing to hold power to account through the medium in which power is exercised,” ll. 75–76). The construct’s content is licensed later, and only in part (J2).

Key Terms *Communicative competence* (“each paired with a form of sovereignty,” ll. 1328–1330) is still the one sentence that over-pairs. The pairing is analogical. It should not read as a series that delivers the fourth term. Not promoted; the body no longer treats it as a deduction.

### J2. §5 bypass test → §6 definition

**Occasion, sort, and — now — the headline definition. Cascade incomplete (Finding 1).**

What the test licenses:

- A classification of functions (necessary / contingent / partial / reducible) for a given dependent party, not of platforms once and for all (ll. 435–436).
- The Hirschman consequence: necessity forecloses exit; voice remains, including against contingent mediators, and is the only lever where the mediator is necessary (ll. 497–499).
- The occasion for reconceiving autonomy when independence is a bypass that does not exist (ll. 56–63, 597–599).
- The §6 definition as now written: autonomy proper to a *necessary* mediation; “cannot leave” as the name of that structural fact, not of lock-in; irreducibility separated (ll. 601–607).

What it still does not license:

- The third clause, share in governance (Markell / Schlager & Ostrom). Residual voice can be thinner than proprietorship. The chapter now says so (ll. 603–604). That mark must travel; §7 already treats share-versus-protection as a measure of how much voice, not as what the counterfactual returns (ll. 760–762).
- §9’s “cannot readily leave” as the defining condition (Finding 1).

Capability of direct dealing (ll. 428–436) holds. The test is not necessary-by-construction for discovery functions. Actor-relativity is claimed as a feature and should be kept. The residual in §5.5 — “a traveler who would never have learned that the hotel exists” (ll. 563–565) — still writes the verdict into the traveler’s description. The question the capability reading requires is whether that traveler could reach that hotel at comparable cost through some other architecture (search, destination marketing, brand). The current wording is no longer the old bug; it is a softer circularity. Not a regression. Not promoted.

§5.1’s closer (“The plain counterfactual that follows is enough here: restore a direct link… and see whether the mediator still binds,” ll. 406–407) previews the bypass as if it answered the factoring question. §5.2 then says the two answers need not agree. Read as a handoff into §5.2, it is clumsy. Read as the test of triadicity, it would misclassify a contingent-but-irreducible gate as not-triadic and drop the as-wired layer algorithmacy still has to work. The next subsection repairs it. Tightness, not a failed distinction. Not promoted.

### J3. §5 → §7–§8 institutional program

**Licensed as a sort. Not licensed as a complete institutional theory.**

The table is the payoff the diagnostic actually earns: interoperability, anti-steering, contestability, bargaining, data trusts, cooperatives, and oversight boards change character by mediator type. §8’s managerial rule — do not spend exit on a necessary function, do not concede a contingent one — is the same sort applied to the firm. The hotels paragraph points at a case the chapter has already scoped honestly.

What §7–§8 add beyond the sort (which stack, for which bearer, at which level of the case or the rule) is not returned by the counterfactual. The chapter does not claim it is. The abstract’s verb is now “names the institutional class available” (ll. 13–14), which matches “the diagnostic is what tells a policymaker where to put each one” (ll. 767–768). The morning’s “decides which institutions to build” is gone.

Compelled access against a necessary mediator is filed as voice under essential-facilities logic (ll. 695–698). Coherent if essential facilities is a *remedy* for a facility the open tie does not dissolve, not a *test* of necessity. The stretch is calling compelled access “voice” in Hirschman’s sense; it is closer to common-carriage regulation. The table still sorts. One clause would keep the neighbor honest. Not promoted.

### J4. Make-or-buy / essential facilities

**Node charge answered. Object-split on the App Store now holds. Residual edge-substitutability owned, not closed.**

Survives as against Williamson and essential facilities in the form the chapter attributes to them: those hold the network of who may deal fixed and delete or substitute a node (ll. 450–460). The bypass test relaxes the constraint on the edge. That is a different *question*.

The App Store demonstration no longer arranges the disagreement. The node test is asked of the payment *rail* (installed base, default checkout, cost of a second rail). The edge test is asked of the exclusivity *rule* (sideloading, external-purchase link). The paragraph now says the rail may still do authorizing and trust work the open edge does not reproduce, and points at the §5.4 payment-network split: necessary or partial as a rail, contingent as a gate (ll. 462–472). “On the same function” is gone. “Because it never rested on integrating work” is gone; the remaining “because” is definitional of a prohibition, not a stipulated verdict on what in-app purchase integrates. Closed item 2 holds as claimed.

Does not survive as a different *operation* from “could these parties transact directly if the prohibition were lifted?” That is edge-substitutability with the legal or architectural environment as a parameter. A TCE reader can say comparative institutional analysis already treats the environment as a parameter. The chapter’s remaining wedge is who asks and what is returned (ll. 478–484), and the nearer relative is now named rather than denied: ancillary restraints “already runs this counterfactual on contract terms” (ll. 474–478). “The operation differs from each” (l. 444) is immediately qualified by “A closer relative comes later.” Keep the wedge; do not rest novelty on a different operation from the neighbor the next page concedes.

Essential facilities is correctly rejected as a *test* in §5.2 and correctly reimported as a *remedy* in §7. No inconsistency there.

### J5. Sovereignty-stretching (Bodin / Repetto / specification)

**Closed item 1 holds. Residual is scope, not absence.**

§1 meets Bodin with Habermas’s desubstantialized popular sovereignty and claims specification: two added attributes, narrower extension (ll. 71–78). Repetto is quoted on innovation rather than stretching (ll. 79–82) and reused in §6 (ll. 657–659). The defense is present and early.

The residual: Repetto’s finding is about accounts that recognize multiple coexisting *digital* sovereigns — still answers to who controls infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty asks a different question from the other end of the relation (ll. 656–657). Using Repetto to cover that further step licenses the *word* more than the *construct*. The Habermas gloss is doing the real work. That is specification of an already-generalized sense, not of Bodin, and it is enough. Do not reopen. Do not ask Repetto to carry more than multiple digital sovereigns. The leftover “cannot exit” in the two added attributes (ll. 77–78) is Finding 1’s cascade, not a return of stretching.

### J6. DeVito novelty

**Holds.** Triadic setting plus competence → standing. Parts and integration conceded (ll. 313–327). “The novelty claimed against these accounts is that index and nothing larger” (ll. 336–337). §6 states algorithmacy as precondition of *exercise*, not of formal endowment (ll. 683–685). The abstract no longer calls algorithmacy a different competence; it indexes “those capacities” to the triad (ll. 10–11). “No prior competence reaches it” (l. 10) is the regime claim — literacy does not reach the triad — not a withdrawal of the DeVito concession. Closed item 9 holds in the body and now holds in the abstract.

### J7. Hirschman mapping

**Holds. Best joint in the manuscript.**

Necessity does not produce voice; it forecloses exit and leaves voice as the residual lever, available against contingent mediators too (ll. 497–499). Voice is “the only autonomy left exactly where it commands the least leverage” (ll. 519–520). Loyalty is distinguished from involuntary retention: Hirschman’s loyalty kept the voice-prone from leaving and activated voice; retention “suppresses exit and activates nothing” (ll. 509–510). Exit-as-substitute-for-voice and the comforted monopolist are on the page (ll. 514–518). No is/ought slide from the structural fact to “the right move.” This is the mapping the 2026-08-02 close-out claimed, and it is still there.

### J8. Abstract / §1 / §6 / Key Terms / §10

**Headline sites now consistent; satellite sites not.** Covered by Finding 1. Official wording should be the §6 / Key Terms form: standing (autonomy proper to a *necessary* mediation) to shape terms, contest arbitrary power, and — marked as specification — hold a share in the governance of an arrangement the open tie does not dissolve. Abstract already uses the open-tie wording. §1, bearer, §9, and §10 should follow it.

## Verdict

**Accept-with-nits.**

**Single most important fix.** Rewrite §9’s inclusion rule so the instrument is built under *necessary* mediation, not under “cannot readily leave.” That is the one leftover that redefines the construct. Carry the same predicate to §10 and the two §6 leftovers.

**Biggest genuine strength.** The §5.2 factoring/bypass distinction, the App Store rail/rule split, and the §5.3 Hirschman mapping, now joined to a §6 definition that uses the predicate those sections earned. Necessity forecloses exit; voice is the residual lever and is weakest where it is the only one; loyalty activates voice, involuntary retention activates nothing; share-in-governance is a specification, not a deduction. That is the argument the rest of the chapter is entitled to run.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** Whether the third clause (share in governance / Schlager–Ostrom proprietorship) remains the specification of voice this chapter wants. The diagnostic will not decide it; HANDOFF §0g already records the decision to keep the clause and mark it. Hunt et al. (2025) and companion-paper scope remain author-side if a referee asks; they are not argument defects in this text.
