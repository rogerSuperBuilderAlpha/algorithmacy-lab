## PART 1 — Itemized pass

### 1A. Grammar, agreement, punctuation, typos

1. **`badly-shaped` — hyphenation error (genuine).** §6, para on relational autonomy: "a badly-shaped one diminishes it." An -ly adverb is never hyphenated to its participle (APA/CMOS). Fix to **`badly shaped`**. (Note: `well-shaped` in the same sentence is correct — "well" is not an -ly adverb.) This is the one unambiguous typo in the body.

2. **Number style — `fifteen to thirty percent`.** §5.4, travel case: "an online travel agency that charges fifteen to thirty percent commission." Both numbers are ≥10; APA 7 uses numerals: **`15% to 30% commission`** (or "15 to 30 percent"). "roughly a third" later in the same paragraph is a fraction and correctly stays spelled out.

3. **`companion` referent is named three ways.** The self-citation (Author, 2026) is called "the companion literature" (§1), "the companion work" (§4, §9), and "the companion paper" (§5, twice). All point to the same anonymized source. Standardize to one term (recommend **"the companion work"**) for consistency. Not an error, but a copyedit house-keeping fix.

4. **`algorithmate few` (§10, twice: "an algorithmate few"; cf. "algorithmate few" para).** "algorithmate" is a coined adjective paralleling "literate." It is deliberate and internally consistent, but it appears only in §10 with no earlier signposting. CONFIRM the coinage is intended; if kept, it is fine as is.

5. **Em dash spacing — global style decision, not per-instance errors.** The manuscript uses **spaced em dashes** throughout ("logic — price, authority, trust — is old"; and ~10 more: §3, §4 close, §5.3, §5.4, §6, §8 twice, Key Terms "exit and voice"). APA 7 / US house style sets em dashes closed up (—). This is internally consistent, so it is one global find-replace for the typesetter, not a list of defects. Flag for the volume's house style: closed em dash or keep spaced.

6. **Heading case mismatch.** Chapter title is title case ("Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era"); all section and subsection headings are sentence case ("The platform turn: coordination through an opaque, interested mediator"). Section headings are internally consistent sentence case — fine — but confirm against the IGI template, which often wants title case for level-1 headings. Global decision, not per-heading errors.

No subject-verb, tense, or comma-splice errors were found in the body. Spot checks that pass: "the economics of two-sided markets ... *calls* a competitive bottleneck" (singular subject/verb, §3.3); "Direct-booking shares climb ... and then plateau" (plural agreement, §5.4); "The market-conduct remedies of 2025 reached ... and mandated ..." (§8).
