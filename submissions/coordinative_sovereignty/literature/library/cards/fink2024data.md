## Summary
This is a glossary entry in Internet Policy Review's concepts series, and its function is definitional. Fink defines data cooperatives as a type of data intermediary that uses long-established cooperative models and the regulatory affordances now available in several jurisdictions to manage data with or on behalf of members, the data producers, either for the members' own benefit or for trade with external queriers, the data users. The entry situates the construct against platform capitalism, where large firms accumulate economic power by centralising data, and presents cooperatives as an alternative organisational form grounded in the International Cooperative Alliance principles of democratic governance and member control, which lets users or workers own data collectively and share in the gains from it. It distinguishes data cooperatives from other data intermediaries such as trusts and fiduciaries, and notes that the form's governance commitments are what separate it from a commercial data broker that happens to serve individuals.
