## Summary
Mügge observes that EU policy invokes digital sovereignty for artificial intelligence, but that AI sovereignty can serve diverse and conflicting goals and so obscures the tensions within actual EU strategy. He proposes three trade-offs. First, does AI sovereignty pit the EU against other AI powers, or citizens against large technology companies? Second, is it meant to lift the EU's position in a putative AI race, or to defy that competitiveness logic? Third, is it meant to benefit European citizens, or does it carry a global responsibility? He then maps the Commission's AI strategy since 2018 and the AI Act negotiations onto these dimensions. The finding is that EU AI policy prioritizes jurisdictional independence over citizens' sovereignty, embraces a global race logic, and largely neglects its effects on people outside the EU. This orientation feeds rising geoeconomic tension, and Mügge insists it reflects political choices rather than a natural consequence of technological change.
