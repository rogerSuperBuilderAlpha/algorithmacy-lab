## Summary
The article studies how freelancers on digital work platforms such as Upwork and Fiverr cope with algorithmic management that is opaque and changes quickly. Taking a sociomateriality perspective and focusing on worker agency rather than experience alone, the authors analyse 12,294 scraped comments from an online community of digital freelancers. They find that workers develop direct and indirect "anticipatory compliance practices", among them undervaluing their own work, staying under the radar, curtailing outreach to clients and keeping emotions in check, in order to secure continued participation on a platform that functions as a shadow employer. The contribution is threefold: workers engage in practices aimed at pacifying the algorithm, these practices constitute extra work, and through them workers co-construct the very power of the algorithm they are responding to. The setting is remote knowledge work rather than ride-hailing or delivery, which widens the empirical base for algorithmic management beyond location-based gig work.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §4 as the last item in a run on folk theorization: workers "shaping their conduct in advance to pacify a system they cannot see (Bucher et al., 2021)". The role is support for the claim that competence under algorithmic coordination is exercised prospectively, on an inferred rather than disclosed system. Strand 1 places the paper after Bucher (2017) on the algorithmic imaginary and beside Möhlmann et al. (2023) on informal theories, and its finding that compliance practices co-construct algorithmic power is also the cleanest empirical statement of the recursivity the chapter asserts. Note that the authors are Eliane Bucher and colleagues, not Taina Bucher of the 2017 piece; the two are easily conflated in a citation run.
