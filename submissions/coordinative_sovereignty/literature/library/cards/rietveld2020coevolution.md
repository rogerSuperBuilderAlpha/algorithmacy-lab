## Summary
Rietveld, Ploog and Nieborg ask how a platform's growing dominance affects the complement producers on which its success depends. Multisided platforms such as Apple's App Store and Valve's Steam become more dominant as more end users and complementors join, yet little is known about what the dominance trajectory does to complementors. A multiple case study of four market-leading platform ecosystems over several years discovers that as a platform gains dominance, the sponsor's governance strategies shift from broadly supporting the complement population to becoming more selective and more oriented to end users. Those shifts track changes in complementor outcomes: value created at the ecosystem level rises, but average demand for individual complements falls and becomes progressively concentrated, while complement prices decline and complementors' costs rise. The authors note that digital platforms can alter their technical infrastructure seamlessly to extend and solidify dominance, which makes these dynamics especially salient for digital ecosystems.
