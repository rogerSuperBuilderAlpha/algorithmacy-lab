## Summary
Cotter contributes to the literature on what people understand about algorithms by naming a specific form of understanding, practical knowledge of algorithms. She defines it as awareness located where discursive meaning-making meets situated action: not knowing facts about how an algorithm works but knowing how to move through algorithmically shaped environments while guided by the values and frameworks of one's social group. The argument is grounded in an empirical study of BreadTube, a leftist YouTube community, through analysis of community discourse and practice around YouTube's recommendation system. The study shows how practical knowledge forms, circulates, and functions within that community, including how members discern and disclose what they take the system to reward. The central result is a reframing of algorithmic knowledge as collective, practical, and normatively loaded rather than as individual technical comprehension.
