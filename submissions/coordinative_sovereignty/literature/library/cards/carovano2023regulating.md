## Summary
Carovano and Finck examine the Data Governance Act's new regulatory regime for data intermediaries, which the Commission introduced to raise the competitiveness of the European data economy by building trust in data-sharing mechanisms. The article is doctrinal. It introduces the category of data intermediary, sets out the DGA's notification, neutrality and structural-separation requirements, and tests the assumptions behind them. The authors argue that the Act imposes requirements not all intermediaries can meet and entrenches a particular techno-organisational form of intermediation that may not be economically viable; they question whether the rules are necessary and proportionate given the freedom to conduct a business; and they uncover inconsistencies and loopholes between the DGA, the GDPR, the draft Data Act and the Digital Markets Act. Their conclusion is that, although the DGA's aims are laudable, imprecise postulations and internal incoherence, together with friction against the broader framework, hinder achievement of its objectives.
