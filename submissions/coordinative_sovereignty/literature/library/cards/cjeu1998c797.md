## Summary
The Court answered a preliminary reference about whether Mediaprint, holding the only nationwide early-morning home-delivery scheme for daily newspapers in Austria, abused a dominant position by refusing to carry a small rival's paper. Applying Magill and assuming for argument that the reasoning could extend to any property right, the Court set the conditions under which a refusal to supply is abusive: the refusal must be likely to eliminate all competition from the requesting party, must be incapable of objective justification, and the service must be indispensable, in that there is no actual or potential substitute. On the facts none of this held. Other distribution methods existed, nothing technical, legal or economic made it impossible or unreasonably difficult for another publisher to build its own scheme, and it is not enough to show that duplication is unviable for a small paper; the test is whether a second scheme would be viable at a circulation comparable to the incumbent's. This is the classic essential-facilities formulation in EU law.

## Use in the chapter
Section 5.2 states that "Competition law's essential-facilities doctrine asks whether an excluded rival could viably duplicate the facility it seeks access to," and later contrasts the court's node test with the chapter's edge test. Section 7 mentions the doctrine again. The audit (U4) notes the doctrine is described three times and never cited and proposes Bronner paragraphs 41 to 46 as the primary source, with Evrard 2004 or Graef 2019 as commentary. Role: legal instrument. The chapter should add that the CJEU has since narrowed Bronner, most recently in Alphabet/Android Auto (C-233/23, 2025), where indispensability is not required when the platform was built to be opened.

## Key quotations
"not only that the refusal of the service comprised in home delivery be likely to eliminate all competition in the daily newspaper market on the part of the person requesting the service and that such refusal be incapable of being objectively justified, but also that the service in itself be indispensable to carrying on that person's business, inasmuch as there is no actual or potential substitute in existence for that home-delivery scheme." (para. 41)
"it is not enough to argue that it is not economically viable by reason of the small circulation of the daily newspaper or newspapers to be distributed." (para. 45)
"it would be necessary at the very least to establish ... that it is not economically viable to create a second home-delivery scheme for the distribution of daily newspapers with a circulation comparable to that of the daily newspapers distributed by the existing scheme." (para. 46)
