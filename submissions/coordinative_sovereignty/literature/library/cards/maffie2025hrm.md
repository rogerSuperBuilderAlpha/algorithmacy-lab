## Summary
Maffie and Lakhani ask how human resource practices affect organisational outcomes in the gig economy, combining multi-sided market theory with strategic HRM's ability, motivation, opportunity (AMO) framework. The strategic threat they isolate is multi-homing: because gig workers face low switching costs, they can work for rival platforms at once, so retention means preventing multi-homing rather than turnover. Drawing on original survey data and qualitative interviews from the US ride-hail industry at a critical moment in Uber-Lyft competition, they find that the introduction of HR-like practices, delivered without an employment relationship (training through driver mentors, tipping and bonuses, fairer customer-rating appraisal, dispute resolution, discretion over when and how to work), reduces drivers' tendency to multi-home. Lyft's early dispute-resolution and in-app tipping drew drivers to multi-home in its favour against Uber's first-mover advantage. The discussion argues that the gig "service triangle" is multiplied, letting workers move between triangles on the relative favourability of the manager-worker and customer-worker relations, and calls for context-specific HR constructs in service work.
