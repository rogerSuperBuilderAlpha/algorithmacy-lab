## Summary
The paper asks how online freelancers manage the double bind of gig work: platforms promise flexible, autonomous work and some relief from precarity by helping workers find work consistently and securely, but they also introduce their own demands and constraints. Drawing on 20 interviews with online freelancers, 19 interviews with corresponding clients, and a first-hand walkthrough of the Upwork platform in which the authors transacted as client and freelancer, the authors identify a set of critical literacies they call gig literacies: the competencies that emerge around online freelancing, spanning the ability to work the platform's profiles, ratings, and search, to manage client relationships through and around the platform, and to assemble a working routine from the platform's affordances. The methods note that the division between the client and freelancer groups is not clear-cut, since most clients interviewed also sought work through Upwork as freelancers or had done so.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §4: "Sutherland et al. (2020) describe 'gig literacies' from a study" of freelancers and clients, as one of the named precursors the chapter assembles for its algorithmacy construct. Role: support for the claim that the literature already names workers' platform competencies. The audit (O10) flags the chapter's wording: the interviews were paired, not joint, so "interviewed freelancers and their clients together" overstates; the abstract's own phrase is "corresponding clients". Related audit item S11 pairs it with Jarrahi and Sutherland 2019, whose "algorithmic competencies" the chapter also cites (with O11's 'coined' softened to 'introduced').
