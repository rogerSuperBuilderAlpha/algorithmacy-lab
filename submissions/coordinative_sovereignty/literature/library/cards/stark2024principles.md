## Summary
The essay, its title a deliberate echo of Taylor's Principles of Scientific Management, develops a theory of algorithmic management in relation to changes in the topology of organisation in the twenty-first century. Its starting point is that many of the most valuable actors, assets and activities are no longer inside the firm but in an entanglement of information flows, practices and users, which raises the problem of how to manage what one does not employ or own. The authors' answer is a fourth mode of coordination: where actors in hierarchies command, in markets contract and in networks collaborate, on platforms they are co-opted, and co-optation is carried out by algorithmic management. They compare the ideology and practices of algorithmic management with those of scientific management and of the more recent collaborative management, arguing that it operates within a different organisational form, articulates a different ideology, and addresses different managerial problems with different governance principles along different lines of accountability. The piece is theoretical, with no new empirical material.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §3: "Stark and Pais (2020) and Stark and Vanden Broeck (2024) read platforms as a fourth term," beside markets, hierarchies and networks. The chapter takes the co-optation formula from here and then narrows it along Selznick's axis. The Literature Gaps report notes that Kornberger et al.
