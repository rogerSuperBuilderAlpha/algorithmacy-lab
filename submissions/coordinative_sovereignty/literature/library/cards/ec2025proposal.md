## Summary
The Digital Omnibus (data) is a Commission proposal of 19 November 2025 to simplify the EU digital acquis. It amends the GDPR, the ePrivacy Directive, the Data Act, NIS2, and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive, and repeals four instruments: the free flow of non-personal data regulation, the Platform-to-Business Regulation 2019/1150, the Data Governance Act 2022/868, and the Open Data Directive. The explanatory memorandum justifies the P2B repeal by arguing that the Digital Markets Act and Digital Services Act have "largely overtake[n]" its provisions. The DGA's regimes for data intermediation services and data altruism are moved into a new Chapter VIIa of the Data Act, with the intermediary regime turned from a mandatory notification scheme into a voluntary one and the structural separation requirement replaced by functional separation plus conditions. The text also reworks GDPR definitions and cookie rules. It remains a proposal; a Council general approach was blocked in June 2026.

## Use in the chapter
Cited twice. §7: "the November 2025 Digital Omnibus proposal would repeal that Act and move the data-intermediation rules into the Data Act (European Commission, 2025a)". §8: "The Commission has since proposed to repeal it as part of a wider simplification package (European Commission, 2025a), which would remove the only duty in European law to disclose the main parameters of ranking". The audit confirms the tense and the repeal, but Nolteernsting 2025 contests "only duty" (DSA Art. 27 and UCPD impose near-identical duties), so the §8 sentence needs softening. Do not confuse with the Digital Omnibus on AI, now Regulation 2026/1744. Role: legal instrument.
