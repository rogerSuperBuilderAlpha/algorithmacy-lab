## Summary
Gowder argues that the major platforms have repeatedly failed to govern their users, and that the failures (he lists stolen elections, refused vaccines, counterfeit respirators in a pandemic, and genocide) follow from a structural incapacity: no central management can handle the complexity of the platforms' user bases, product lines, and conflicting incentives. The remedy he proposes is to adapt institutional tools from political science and democratize the platforms. Democratic structures, on his account, do two things a corporate hierarchy cannot: they let informed participants share knowledge about the problems they actually face, and they let leaders recruit third parties to expand their decision-making capacity. The book proceeds through chapters on what platforms are, how their governance developed, the knowledge and self-control problems they face, Meta's Oversight Board as a partial experiment, and practical routes to democratic implementation. It is normative political theory applied to platform governance, published open access.
