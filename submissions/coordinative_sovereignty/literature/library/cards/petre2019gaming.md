## Summary
Petre, Duffy, and Hund examine how platforms discipline the visibility strategies of cultural workers. As metrics reconfigure cultural production, journalists, musicians, photographers, and content creators pursue visibility through search-engine optimisation, reciprocal linking, and automated engagement boosting, and platforms routinely condemn such practices as gaming the system. The authors analyse three episodes in which Google, Facebook, and Instagram accused producers of algorithmic manipulation, drawing on 105 news articles and the platforms' own user guidelines. They find that the line between illegitimate manipulation and legitimate strategy is nebulous and tracks the platforms' material interests, while the language used to draw it is strongly moral, casting system-gamers as dishonest and the platform as a neutral guardian of authenticity and integrity. They name this dynamic platform paternalism and conclude that gaming accusations are a mechanism by which platforms legitimate their power, to the detriment of less established producers who lack the resources to stay on the right side of a shifting line.
