## Summary
De Gregorio's book is about rights and powers in the digital age and is an attempt to reframe the role of constitutional democracies in the algorithmic society. It proceeds in two parts. The first reconstructs why digital constitutionalism emerged in Europe, tracing the Union's movement from a digital liberalism that shielded platforms from liability toward an active constitutional posture that reads platform power as a threat to fundamental rights and democratic values. The second offers a normative framework and asks whether European constitutionalism adequately protects rights and constrains unaccountable private power in algorithmic contexts. The central claim is that European digital constitutionalism is a distinct reaction to platform powers, and that its promise depends on extending constitutional guarantees to the exercise of private algorithmic power rather than only to the state.
