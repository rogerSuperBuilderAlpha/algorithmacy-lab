## Summary
The paper provides quasi-experimental evidence on the effect of removing price parity clauses, using the full removal of Booking.com's clauses in France in 2015 following the Loi Macron. Parity clauses prevent sellers from charging lower prices on alternative channels; their removal should allow hotels to price their own sites below the agency. Comparing French hotels listed on Booking.com with hotels in neighbouring countries before and after the intervention, the authors find significant price decreases in the short run but a more limited effect in the medium run. Hotels with a more complex organisational structure, such as chains and larger properties, reduced their prices more substantially than independents. The conclusion is that the intervention benefited a subset of consumers using Booking.com, and that the capacity to exploit the freedom the removal created was unevenly distributed across hotels.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. Audit S20 proposes it, with Hunold et al. (2018), as the source for §5.5's unsourced prediction that removing parity should let hotels undercut the agency on their own sites. Both papers find that hotels did lower prices after removal, while the chapter's data from Schegg (2024) show that the agencies' share of overnight stays still rose. The audit asks the chapter to acknowledge the price effect, because the combination, prices moved but dependence did not, is the strongest evidence for the portfolio reading. Role: second source.
