## Summary
Muldoon and Raekstad offer a republican theory of algorithmic domination and apply it to ride-hail and food-delivery work. Empirical studies of the gig economy have raised concerns about algorithmic management, and the authors argue that certain algorithms facilitate new relations of domination by sustaining a socio-technical system in which a company's owners and managers dominate workers. On their definition, an individual is subject to algorithmic domination if and only if they are subject to a dominating power whose operations are determined, in whole or in part, directly by an algorithm. The algorithm is not itself the dominator; the owners and managers who coordinate through shared beliefs and joint intentions to deploy it are, which keeps the account within the agent-centred republican tradition. They argue that algorithmic management narrows workers' room to negotiate schedules and imposes more stringent control, and they favour cooperative ownership as a remedy while acknowledging its limits.

## Use in the chapter
Cited twice in §6. First as the direct application of non-domination to algorithmic management, "treating it as a socio-technical system in which owners and managers hold arbitrary power". Second for the remedy: "Muldoon and Raekstad (2023) recommend cooperative ownership, and that remedy locates the wedge", since they acknowledge that domination can survive a change of owner.
