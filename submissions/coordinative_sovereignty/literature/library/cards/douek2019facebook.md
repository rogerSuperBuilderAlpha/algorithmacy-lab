## Summary
Douek treats Facebook's then-proposed Oversight Board as one of the most ambitious constitution-making projects of the modern era and asks what it can and cannot be. It cannot hear enough cases to answer calls for due process in individual content decisions, and it cannot become a source of globally accepted speech norms for a worldwide platform. Its real value lies between those extremes. The article argues for two more modest functions: exposing weaknesses in Facebook's policy-formation process, removing blind spots and inertia in the legislative process behind the Community Standards; and serving as an independent forum for public reasoning about disputed decisions, through which members of a pluralistic community can come to accept rules they disagree with. The analysis is institutional and doctrinal, drawing on constitutional and administrative law, and it derives design recommendations (stable infrastructure, institutional humility) from the functions it identifies.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter. The Evidence Audit (S31) proposes it as a second source for the §7 sentence on platform-run oversight boards, currently resting on Klonick alone, and notes that the chapter's plural "oversight boards" overstates: one such board exists, so the chapter should write "Meta's Oversight Board." Douek also supports the chapter's point that the board's limit is dependence on the platform that constitutes it, since she argues it cannot provide individual error correction at scale. Role: second source for the oversight-board claim.
