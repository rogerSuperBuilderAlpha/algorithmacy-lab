## Summary
The authors examine four models of data governance emerging in the platform society alongside the dominant model of corporate platforms collecting and exploiting personal data at scale. They argue that small businesses, public bodies and civil society also take part in data governance and identify four models from their practices: data sharing pools, in which actors pool data under agreed terms; data cooperatives, owned and governed by the people whose data they hold; public data trusts, in which public bodies steward data for public benefit; and personal data sovereignty, in which individuals control access to their own data through intermediaries. They propose a social-science conceptualization of data governance built on the notion of data infrastructure, asking who holds the power to decide how data are used and for whose benefit. Their conclusion is that civil society and public bodies are key to democratizing data governance and redistributing the value produced through data. The paper is conceptual with illustrative cases.
