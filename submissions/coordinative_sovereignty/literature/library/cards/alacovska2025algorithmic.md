## Summary
The article introduces the concept of algorithmic paranoia to name the negative affective experience that arises when gig workers perceive algorithmic management as non-transparent, arbitrary, and retaliatory. Drawing on the sociology and organisation-studies literature on organisational paranoia, the authors treat paranoia as a structurally produced, relational affect rather than an individual disorder. Empirically the study rests on an abductive analysis of 53 in-depth interviews with creative freelancers (illustrators, graphic designers, visual artists) on gig platforms, supplemented by threads from an online occupational forum. Workers read sudden drops in reputation scores, lost clients, non-payment, and suspensions as signs of malevolent intent from the algorithm, clients, and co-workers, and respond with hypervigilance and self-protective pre-emptive actions. The central result is a self-reinforcing loop between perceived abusive management, negative affect, and defensive action. The authors argue that what other studies read as resistance is better understood as adaptive coping that further entrenches labour control.
