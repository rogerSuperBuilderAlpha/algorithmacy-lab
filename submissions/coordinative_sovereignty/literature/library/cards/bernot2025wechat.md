## Summary
Bernot traces how Tencent's WeChat moved from supporting the Chinese party-state's information control to becoming an integrated module of its public security apparatus. The article introduces two capabilities, "public security WeChat" (official police accounts delivering services and collecting reports) and "WeChat policing" (functions on mobile police devices for intelligence gathering, surveillance of targeted persons and incident reporting), and documents their spread from 2012 to more than 50,000 police accounts by 2017. The method is a multi-modal dataset of 53 government procurement records supplemented by archived news, read through an infrastructure-studies lens. The findings show provincial variation driven by top-down digitisation mandates under the Internet+ programme and by local budgets, with localities turning WeChat into a "software-as-a-police-service" platform that police bureaus can configure without bespoke infrastructure. The discussion frames this as the "infrastructurization of platforms", the reverse of the platformisation of infrastructure familiar from liberal settings, shaped by technonationalist policy that treats WeChat as critical infrastructure.

## Use in the chapter
§8 cites it in the sentence "WeChat is woven into Chinese public administration to the point of hosting police modules (Bernot, 2025), and its users hold no standing within a system the state governs securely." The first clause is well supported by the article's procurement evidence. The second clause is the chapter's inference: Bernot describes surveillance integration, not the standing of users, so the audit (O16) asks that the standing claim be marked as the chapter's own and (S32) that Plantin and de Seta (2019) be added as a second source for the WeChat case. Role: support for the empirical premise of the "control without standing" example.
