## Summary
Collin rereads Goody's body of work on the consequences of literacy and separates two strands in it: arguments that tend toward technological determinism, in which literacy causes uniform change, and arguments in which literacy is a force that shapes and is shaped by the social processes it meets. He reviews the sociocultural critique (Street, Scribner and Cole, Brandt and Clinton) and grants its objection to the first strand, but argues that in rejecting Goody wholesale the critics lost concepts needed to explain literacy's role in transforming societies, and that their misreading leads the field to underestimate the distinct force of literacy and overestimate local culture. He proposes an interaction model in which changes in literacy are both conditions of and conditional upon changes in other domains, and closes with a reworked notion of genre as the place where literacy meets other social processes. Street replied in 2014, and the two synthesized a joint model.

## Use in the chapter
Strand 5 lists it as optional for §2 and §4. It is useful to the chapter in two ways. It licenses the chapter's §2 use of Goody at the level of coordination regimes while conceding Street's point about cognition, which is the exact move §4 makes with "Street's caution applies to the competence rather than to the regime". And it gives the chapter a citable middle position on the determinism debate that G8 in the audit says §2 currently lacks. Role: optional support; pair with Collin and Street 2014 and Halverson 1992.

## Key quotations
"researchers working to advance understandings of the wide-ranging effects of literacy take care to avoid technological determinist arguments, in general, and Goody's arguments, in particular. In this article, I contend that these and other researchers, in their haste to avoid technological determinist thinking, have missed what is good in Goody." (Introduction)
"how they misread Goody limits the depth of the answers they provide to questions about literacy and its relations to culture, economics, politics, and other spheres of existence." (Introduction)
