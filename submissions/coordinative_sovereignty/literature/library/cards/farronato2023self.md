## Summary
The paper asks whether Amazon favours its own brands, such as Amazon Basics, in marketplace search results. The authors collect micro-level consumer search data through a custom browser extension installed by a panel of study participants, which lets them observe search positions, search behaviour and product characteristics as real users see them rather than through scraped or synthetic queries. Controlling for observable product characteristics, they find that Amazon-branded products are ranked higher than comparable third-party products. The size of the effect is expressed relative to paid placement: the prominence given to Amazon's brands is 30 to 60 percent of the prominence granted to sponsored products. The paper is a short conference contribution and does not attempt a welfare analysis; its contribution is a credible measurement of self-preferencing in ranking using data from the consumer's side of the screen, which is exactly where the platform's discretion over allocation is exercised and is otherwise opaque.
