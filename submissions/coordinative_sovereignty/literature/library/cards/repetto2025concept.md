## Summary
Repetto reviews 240 works on digital sovereignty, coding each for disciplinary area, the actors to which sovereignty is ascribed and the type of relation among actors that the work conceptualizes. The question is whether the proliferation of digital sovereigns (states, Indigenous peoples, individuals, companies, the EU) stretches the concept in Sartori's sense. His finding is that the literature has not stretched sovereignty so much as fragmented into clusters, each organized around a sovereign of choice, siloed within disciplinary boundaries and avoiding conversation with the others; each cluster preserves the unitary and legitimacy-bearing character of traditional sovereignty by ignoring coexistence or framing it as zero-sum. The works that do acknowledge multiple coexisting digital sovereigns, or that treat digital sovereignty as arising from interaction among actors, neither stretch the concept nor turn it into something different from sovereignty tout court; they innovate the concept by problematizing its unitary assumptions. He argues for recognizing sovereignty's relational nature and for conversation among the clusters.

## Key quotations
"Those works that acknowledge the possibility of multiple coexisting digital sovereigns, or that see digital sovereignty as something that arises from the interactions among multiple actors, neither stretch the concept of sovereignty nor turn digital sovereignty into something different from sovereignty tout court: instead, they explore ways to innovate the concept of sovereignty as a whole and problematise our long-held assumptions about it." (Abstract)

"Each cluster avoids conceptual stretching by forming its own understanding of digital sovereignty and by limiting interaction with works belonging to other clusters." (Discussion)
