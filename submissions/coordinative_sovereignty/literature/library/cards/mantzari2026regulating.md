## Summary
Mantzari builds a normative framework for the fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory access obligation in Article 6(12) of the Digital Markets Act, with Apple's iOS App Store as the central case. Her diagnosis is that although the DMA couples contestability with fairness, enforcement has concentrated almost entirely on contestability and avoided the harder question of what a fair access fee is. Because network effects and developer lock-in may prevent effective contestability from materialising, the Commission will be forced to confront the substantive fairness of app-store commissions. Surveying FRAND principles from standard-essential patents, EU competition law and telecoms regulation, she finds none transposable directly to ecosystems built on intangible assets and reciprocal value creation. She proposes instead a principles-based approach with four propositions: gatekeepers should not be remunerated for network-effect rents; FRAND must reflect the reciprocal value developers generate; access pricing should account for the net exchange of value between platform and developer; and fees should reflect long-run average incremental costs. Together these offer a method for operationalising fairness under Article 6(12).
