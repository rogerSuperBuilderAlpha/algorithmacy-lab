## Summary
The paper analyses two symmetric strategic platforms that set fees to a local monopolist merchant and cash-back rebates to end users, under a no surcharge rule (NSR) that forbids the merchant from charging the platforms' customers more, a restraint imposed by some card networks and more recently seen in online travel booking. Each platform can gain transactions by widening the spread between its merchant fee and its user rebate beyond its rival's, because the merchant cannot steer customers away and the rebate pulls them in. That incentive yields non-existence of a pure-strategy equilibrium in many natural environments, a result the authors show is robust to the extensions they try. In some circumstances a mixed-strategy equilibrium exists in which platforms choose fee structures that induce the merchant to accept only one platform with equal probability, a form of monopolistic market allocation. The introduction situates the analysis in the Amex litigation, where the district court's one-sided reading of merchant fees was reversed on two-sided grounds and affirmed by the Supreme Court in Ohio v. American Express (2018). The conclusion notes the relevance to online booking sites when they rebate users while merchants face no surcharge restraints.
