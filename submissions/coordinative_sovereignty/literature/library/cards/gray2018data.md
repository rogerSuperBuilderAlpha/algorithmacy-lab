## Summary
Gray, Gerlitz and Bounegru start from a UN report's call for global data literacy as a way to realise the data revolution and argue that the notion should be widened. Beyond numerical and technical competence in reading and working with datasets, they propose data infrastructure literacy: the ability to account for, intervene around and participate in the wider socio-technical infrastructures through which data is created, stored and analysed. Drawing examples from open data, online platforms, data journalism and activism, they show that such infrastructures are not neutral and that their design shapes what can be known and done. The authors argue that data literacy initiatives should cultivate sensibilities drawn from science and technology studies, sociology and politics, and from public engagement with digital systems. The result is a programme for collective inquiry, experimentation and intervention in which infrastructures are not only read but questioned, reshaped and repurposed for public interests different from those they were built to serve.
