## Summary
The Platform-to-Business Regulation sets the first EU-wide rules for the relationship between online intermediation services and search engines on one side and the business users that reach consumers through them on the other. Recital 2 states the premise: these services can be crucial for the commercial success of the undertakings that depend on them, and the platform economy only delivers if business users can trust the intermediaries they deal with. The operative provisions require plain and intelligible terms and conditions with notice of changes (Article 3), reasons for restriction, suspension and termination (Article 4), and disclosure in the terms of the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for their relative importance (Article 5). It also obliges larger providers to run an internal complaint-handling system (Article 11), to name mediators (Article 12), and gives representative organisations and public bodies standing to bring proceedings (Article 14). The Regulation applied from 12 July 2020 and is the subject of a repeal proposal in COM(2025) 837.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in prose in §1, §3.2, §7 and §8 as "the Platform-to-Business Regulation obliges platforms to disclose the main parameters of ranking" and, in §8, as the instrument that "does more than disclose" by attaching complaint-handling, mediation and representative standing to disclosure. The Evidence Audit (U5) asks for a formal citation to Articles 3(2), 5, 11, 12 and 14 and for Recital 2 to ground the SME-exposure claim (U16). Role: legal instrument; the chapter treats it as one of the few European duties that builds a channel rather than only a disclosure, and notes the proposed repeal.

## Key quotations
"Online intermediation services can be crucial for the commercial success of undertakings who use such services to reach consumers." (Recital 2)
"Providers of online intermediation services shall set out in their terms and conditions the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those main parameters as opposed to other parameters." (Article 5(1))
"The proposed changes shall not be implemented before the expiry of a notice period which is reasonable and proportionate to the nature and extent of the envisaged changes and to their consequences for the business user concerned." (Article 3(2))
