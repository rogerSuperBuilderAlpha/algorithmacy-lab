## Summary
This is the introduction to a special issue on EU digital sovereignty. The authors observe that the language of digital sovereignty has become ubiquitous in Europe but that there is little systematic knowledge of whether the discourse is matched by policy change. The contributions, using qualitative and quantitative methods, trace discursive and policy change across nine major areas of EU digital policymaking. The finding is asymmetric: policy has shifted toward more control over the digital in every area examined, but the shift is only sometimes accompanied by a turn in discourse toward digital sovereignty. The authors explain the gap by ideational trade-offs: invoking sovereignty carries costs that vary by venue, policy area and member state, so actors sometimes pursue control without the word and sometimes use the word without corresponding policy. They place the pattern against current geopolitical and geo-economic pressures and offer the issue as a map of how EU digital policy and its discursive politics have changed.
