## Summary
The study asks whether participation in online travel agencies pays for hotels, a question the authors frame as controversial: hotels value the bookings OTAs deliver but resent the commissions. Using property-level income statement data from 644 US hotels, they test the association between OTA engagement and occupancy, revenue per available room and EBITDA. At the aggregate level OTA participation correlates positively with all three, so intermediated demand raises both the top line and profit. Disaggregating by hotel class changes the picture: intermediaries drive top-line revenue across all property types, but economy hotels show lower profitability despite higher revenues, so the commission burden outweighs the demand gain at the low end. The conclusion is that OTA participation is not advantageous for every segment and that the value of aggregation depends on a hotel's price point and on the share of demand it could have captured directly.
