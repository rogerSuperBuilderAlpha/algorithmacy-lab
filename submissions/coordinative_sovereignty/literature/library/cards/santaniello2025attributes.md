## Summary
Santaniello treats digital sovereignty as a pivotal element of contemporary politics and, from a constructivist standpoint, proposes a conceptual framework for studying the policy discourse around it. The framework is built on five attributes that characterise sovereignty claims in the digital sphere: adversariality, multiversity, latency, instrumentality and hypocrisy. These serve as analytical categories for reading digital sovereignty as a discursive resource deployed in political struggles over control of digital networks. Digital sovereignty emerges as a contested concept at the centre of competition among nation-states, regional blocs, corporations, epistemic communities and social groups. The paper stresses the contradictory and fragmented character of the claims, showing how they intersect with wider geopolitical ambitions, and situates them within global power rivalries and historically determined social relations. The result is a critical understanding of digital sovereignty that embraces its contested and evolving nature rather than seeking a single definition. The paper is conceptual; no original empirical corpus is reported in the abstract.
