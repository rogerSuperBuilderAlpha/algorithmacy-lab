## Summary
Paglayan proposes a theory of mass education as a state-building tool deployed when mass violence threatens the state's viability. After experiencing widespread internal violence, national leaders come to see primary schooling as a means of teaching obedience and securing social order, not as a concession to popular demand or a product of democratization. The argument is tested in two ways: a pattern analysis of primary-schooling expansion following civil conflicts in Europe and Latin America, and a detailed case study of Chile after the 1859 civil war, where the government expanded schooling in the rebellious provinces specifically to instil deference to authority. The contribution is to reverse the usual direction of causation between schooling and democracy, placing the expansion of public school systems before and apart from the extension of the franchise and attributing it to order-seeking rather than to enlightenment or redistributive motives.
