## Summary
Couldry and Mejias argue that the contemporary data economy is best understood not as a new frontier but as the continuation of historical colonialism by other means, which they call data colonialism. Where historical colonialism appropriated land, resources, and bodies, data colonialism appropriates human life itself so that data can be continuously extracted from it for profit. The ideologies of connection and personalization play the role that civilizing narratives played earlier, rationalizing the appropriation as a benefit to those appropriated. The book traces how this extraction feeds a new stage of capitalism in which the organisation of life is oriented to maximum data value and in which wealth and power concentrate in a small number of firms. The work is theoretical and synthetic, drawing on postcolonial theory, political economy, and media studies rather than on a single empirical case, and closes by considering what resistance to this order might require.
