## Summary
The article builds an integrative framework for technological citizenship under digitization. It maps how digital technologies affect citizens across three spheres, private, social, and public, and examines the active roles citizens can play in shaping those impacts, drawing on liberal, communitarian, and republican strands of political theory to specify what citizenship requires in each sphere. Technological citizenship is the capacity of citizens to navigate and shape digitization across the three domains, both seizing the opportunities digital technologies present and managing their risks, through private choices, community participation, and political engagement. Empowerment and resilience are the two enabling conditions the framework centers. Building on Frankenfeld's founding account of technological citizenship as rights and obligations toward technologies that govern one's life, the authors update the concept for the digital condition and argue for policies that support citizens' participation in safeguarding public values.
