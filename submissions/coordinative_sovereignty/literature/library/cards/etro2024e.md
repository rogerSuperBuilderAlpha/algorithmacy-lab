## Summary
Etro surveys the economics of e-commerce platforms with particular attention to the antitrust debate on Amazon. The hybrid marketplace model monetises through fees on hosted third-party sellers and margins on own-brand products, and the survey organises recent theoretical and empirical work on endogenous marketplace structure, the welfare effects of the dual mode, and recommendation algorithms associated with self-preferencing. The passages opened cover fee discrimination across product categories (Tremblay 2022), where restricting the platform's ability to discriminate raises consumer welfare, and the platform's incentive to enter with own products, which tends to intensify competition and lower fees on sellers; and the Prime subscription model, which turns the marketplace into something closer to an access-funded two-sided platform and which the literature reads less as lock-in than the public narrative suggests. The survey's conclusion is cautious: the trade-offs are complex, and one cannot easily conclude that Amazon's entry is biased to expropriate sellers or that banning dual mode, self-preferencing or copycatting would benefit consumers.
