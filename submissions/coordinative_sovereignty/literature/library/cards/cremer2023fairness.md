## Summary
The article, part of a Yale Journal on Regulation special issue by economists and policy experts on competition in digital markets, examines the two objectives the Digital Markets Act sets for itself, fairness and contestability, and asks how the Commission should interpret them when implementing the Act's obligations on gatekeepers. The authors read contestability, following Recital 32, as the ability of non-dominant firms to overcome barriers to entry and expansion to the benefit of users, and fairness as the organisation of economic activity so that users reap the just rewards for their contributions to economic and social welfare. They argue that the two objectives are intertwined rather than separate, since unfair conduct by a gatekeeper typically also entrenches its position, and they work through the Act's obligations (self-preferencing, data access, interoperability, anti-steering) to show how each serves one or both goals. The piece is doctrinal and economic, with no empirical component, and is written to guide enforcement.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter. The Literature Gaps report proposes it as a must for §7, "to disambiguate the DMA's 'contestability' (market entry) from Alfrink's (challenging a decision)." The chapter uses Alfrink's sense when it says a contestable system is open to dispute throughout its lifecycle, and the DMA uses the word for something else entirely, the ability of rivals to enter and expand; a referee in law or economics will expect the chapter to say so. Role: support, as the authoritative statement of the DMA sense.

## Key quotations
"contestability...should be understood as the ability of non-dominant firms to overcome barriers to entry and expand to the benefit of users" (p. 990)
Fairness is "the organization of economic activity to the benefit of users in such ways that they reap the just rewards for their contributions to economic and social welfare" (p. 981)
Recital 32: "contestability should relate to the ability of undertakings to effectively overcome barriers to entry and expansion" (quoted at p. 978)
