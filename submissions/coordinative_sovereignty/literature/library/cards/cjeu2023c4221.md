## Summary
The Court of Justice dismissed Lithuanian Railways' appeal against the General Court's finding that it abused a dominant position by removing a section of track that competitor Orlen needed to route freight through Latvia. The judgment matters for essential-facilities doctrine because the appellant argued that the Commission should have applied the Bronner conditions, above all indispensability. The Court restated those conditions (paragraph 79) and then held that they did not govern this case for two reasons. First, the destruction of infrastructure is distinct from a refusal of access and constitutes an independent form of abuse (paragraph 81). Second, the Bronner criteria exist to protect a dominant firm's incentive to invest in its own infrastructure, so they do not apply where the infrastructure was financed by public funds and the dominant undertaking is not its owner (paragraph 87). The Court also upheld the General Court's use of unlimited jurisdiction to reduce the fine. The judgment narrows the reach of Bronner rather than overruling it.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited. The audit (§5 note) observes that the CJEU "narrowed Bronner in Lithuanian Railways (C-42/21 P, 2023) and Alphabet/Android Auto (C-233/23, Feb 2025)" and advises the chapter to "cite Bronner as the classic formulation and add a clause". The relevant passage is §5.2, where the bypass test is compared with essential-facilities doctrine, which "asks whether an excluded rival could viably duplicate the facility". The role is legal instrument: it shows that indispensability is not required where the facility was publicly funded or built to be opened, which is close to the chapter's own distinction between a mediator whose position rests on its own integrating work and one held in place by an external constraint. Use it as a one-clause qualification, not as a worked case.

## Key quotations
"the destruction, by a dominant undertaking, of infrastructure must be distinguished from a refusal of access, as provided for in the case-law arising from the judgment of 26 November 1998, Bronner" (para. 81)

"those criteria do not apply, in a situation such as that in the present case, where the infrastructure in question was financed by means not of investments specific to the dominant undertaking, but by means of public funds and that undertaking is not the owner of that infrastructure." (para. 87)
