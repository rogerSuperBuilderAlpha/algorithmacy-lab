## Summary
Mannan and Pek investigate the dynamics of worker-member participation in platform worker cooperatives, drawing on interviews with 21 founders and senior leaders. They find some successes and many challenges, and build theory about how four distinctive features of platform worker cooperatives shape participation: the facilitation of multihoming, the physically untethered nature of the work, the relatively high importance of scale as a strategic imperative, and the relatively low importance of initial member investment. Their central claim is that the platform organisational model and the worker-cooperative model are in tension when combined. Corporate platforms thrive on porous boundaries and dynamically adjusted worker supply, whereas cooperatives depend on a sharp inside-outside distinction and on serving members; the result is that cooperatives face a dilemma between not offloading risk onto members and remaining competitive with well-resourced rivals. Untethered work can also enable participation through virtual tools. The paper is qualitative and theory-building.
