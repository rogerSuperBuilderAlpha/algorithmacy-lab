## Summary
The paper asks what the optimal clearing arrangement is for bilateral financial contracts in which assessing counterparty credit risk matters for efficiency. In the model, borrowers face limited commitment and hold private information about their own creditworthiness, and lenders choose between central and bilateral clearing. A central counterparty lets lenders mutualize counterparty risk, which diversifies exposure and can raise efficiency, but the very insurance it supplies weakens lenders' incentives to acquire and reveal information about borrowers. Bilateral clearing preserves those incentives: when information acquisition is not incentive compatible under central clearing, participants may prefer bilateral arrangements, either to deter strategic default or to economize on collateral. The central result is a trade-off in which the choice between clearing structures turns on whether information acquisition remains incentive compatible, so transparency, collateral requirements, and clearing architecture are jointly determined rather than independent design choices.
