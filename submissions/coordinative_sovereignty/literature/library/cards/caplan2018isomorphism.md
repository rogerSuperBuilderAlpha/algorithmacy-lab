## Summary
Caplan and boyd ask how algorithms and data-driven technologies, when enacted by an organization such as Facebook, induce similarity across a whole industry. Using organizational sociology and neoinstitutional theory, they trace the bureaucratic roots of big data and algorithms and argue that algorithms, like bureaucracies before them, have been positioned as the cure for subjective decision-making. The empirical case is news media's dependence on Facebook's News Feed. Following DiMaggio and Powell's dictum that the more an organization depends on another, the more similar to it it becomes, the authors show how Facebook, by defining and redefining what counts as relevant or valuable information, increasingly writes the rules that news organizations must follow, producing what they call algorithmic isomorphism. Organizational contexts are embedded into algorithms, which are then embedded into other organizations' practices. The paper concludes that treating technical practices as organizational and bureaucratic lets questions of accountability and decision-making be reframed.
