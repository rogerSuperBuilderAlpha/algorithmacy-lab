## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the current draft. The audit (U13) proposes it for §2.1, where the chapter's oral-order sentences ("law was what the elders could recite, an oath bound because the community witnessed it") and the claim that authorized speech was restricted by age, sex, birth and office currently carry no source. Bloch's edited volume, on formalized oratory and the constraints that ritual speech forms place on what can be said in councils and assemblies, is the anthropological anchor for the claim that in an oral order the competence was general but the authorized exercise of it was not. The role is support; Goody (1986, chapters 3 and 4) is the audit's companion proposal. The audit (O6) also notes that neither Goody nor Ong uses the word "oracy", so the sentence that introduces Bloch should not attribute the term to them.
