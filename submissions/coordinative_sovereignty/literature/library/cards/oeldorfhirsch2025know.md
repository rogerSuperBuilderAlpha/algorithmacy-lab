## Summary
Oeldorf-Hirsch and Neubaum review the rapidly growing empirical literature on how literate users are about the algorithms that shape their use of communication technology, especially social media. They find a field marked by great diversity in how it theorizes and measures understanding of algorithms, owing partly to the black-box character of the systems themselves. The review summarizes the state of knowledge on algorithmic literacy under four headings: its definitions, its development, its measurement, and current theorizing on human-algorithm interaction. On that basis they propose a research agenda with four directions: balancing users' expected literacy against developers' responsibility for transparency; methods for engaging users in raising their literacy; developing the affective and behavioural facets of literacy beyond awareness and knowledge; and addressing the new algorithmic divide between those who can and cannot work such systems. The central result is a consolidated map of a fragmented construct.
