## Summary
Kude and Huber study how complementors in enterprise software ecosystems respond when the platform owner makes moves that damage their positioning, whether by restricting access to resources such as APIs and relationships or by increasing competitive overlap through encroachment on the complementor's market. They integrate the cooperation and competition perspectives into a move-response framework and test it in a fourteen-year longitudinal qualitative study of four platform partnerships, analysing 21 move-response instances. Three response archetypes emerge: insist, pivot and detach. Over time complementors combine these into three patterns. Progressive diverging diversifies beyond the focal ecosystem at multi-homing cost but reduces dependence. Adaptive oscillating uses each move as an occasion to diversify within the ecosystem. Persistent insisting restores the prior position but deepens dependence and leaves the complementor exposed to the next move. The discussion notes that platform owners are unlikely to listen to any single complementor and suggests complementor associations as a channel for voice.

## Use in the chapter
Audit S1 proposes it for §1's opening examples, where the chapter's independent software vendor depends "for its entire access to customers, on an application store's ranking and review decisions" but the cited sources (Cutolo and Kenney; Rahman 2021) concern freelancers rather than ISVs. Kude and Huber supply the enterprise-ISV evidence, including unannounced API retirements and encroachment. Role: second source for §1; also relevant to §5.3, since the three patterns map the chapter's exit, multihoming and loyalty options, and to §7, where the authors themselves reach for collective voice as the remedy.
