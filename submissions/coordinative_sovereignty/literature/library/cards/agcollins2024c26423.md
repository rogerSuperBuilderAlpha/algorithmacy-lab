## Summary
The Advocate General's Opinion precedes the Court's 19 September 2024 judgment on Booking.com's price parity clauses and sets out the German procedural history on which the chapter's §5.5 narrative rests. Paragraphs 4 to 9 trace the sequence: the Bundeskartellamt's 2010 investigation of HRS and its December 2013 prohibition of wide parity clauses, upheld by the Düsseldorf Higher Regional Court on 9 January 2015; Booking.com's July 2015 replacement of wide clauses with narrow ones restricting only the hotel's direct channel; the Bundeskartellamt's 22 December 2015 decision that the narrow clause also infringed competition law; the Higher Regional Court's 4 June 2019 reversal treating narrow clauses as ancillary restraints needed to prevent free riding; and the Bundesgerichtshof's 18 May 2021 judgment overturning that reversal because the clauses restricted competition and were not objectively necessary. Paragraph 13 records the divergence across member states, with Belgium, France, Italy and Austria prohibiting both forms. The Opinion then reasons toward the conclusion that parity clauses are not ancillary restraints under Article 101(1) TFEU.

## Use in the chapter
§5.5 states that "national enforcement had narrowed the clauses by 2021" and that "several member states legislated against the clauses". The audit (E4, U2) flags the first phrase as pointing the wrong way: the 2021 event is the BGH upholding the prohibition of even the narrow clause, so the sentence should read that German enforcement had by 2021 condemned the narrow clause too. This Opinion, paras 4 to 9 and 13, is the audit's proposed locator for the whole parity-clause history, alongside Wang and Wright (2020) and Calzada, Manna and Mantovani (2022) for the national statutes. Role: legal instrument supporting the §5.5 chronology; it also supplies the BGH citation (KVR 54/20) that the chapter currently lacks.

## Key quotations
Paragraph 6 records that in July 2015 Booking.com "terminated the wide price parity clauses" and replaced them with narrow ones (para. 6, as rendered in the EUR-Lex text opened).
Paragraph 8 records the Higher Regional Court's 2019 finding that narrow clauses were "necessary to avoid free-riding" (para. 8).
The Bundesgerichtshof's 18 May 2021 judgment held the narrow clauses restricted competition and were not objectively necessary (para. 9, paraphrase of the text opened; verbatim wording not captured).
