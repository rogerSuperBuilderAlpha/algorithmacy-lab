## Summary
The study is the 2024 edition of an annual survey of hotel distribution channels in Europe conducted by the Institute of Tourism at HES-SO Valais-Wallis for HOTREC, the European hospitality trade association, reporting on the reference year 2023. It measures the share of overnight stays generated by each booking channel, with direct channels (phone, email, walk-in, the hotel's own website) against online travel agencies, global distribution systems, tour operators and others, and tracks the series back to 2013. Direct channels remain the largest group at 50.9 percent of overnight stays in 2023 but have lost ground over the decade, while the OTA share rose from 19.7 percent to 29.6 percent. Within the OTA channel, concentration has increased: Booking Holdings' share rose from 60 percent in 2013 to 71 percent in 2023, and the top three agencies, Booking Holdings, Expedia Group and HRS, account for about 90 percent. The study is descriptive and does not offer a causal account of the shift.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §5.5: "Between 2013 and 2023, the agencies' share of European hotel overnight stays rose from 19.7% to 29.6% while direct bookings fell from 57.6% to 50.9% (Schegg, 2024)." The role is support for the chapter's central empirical observation in the OTA case, that dependence deepened after the parity gate came open. The audit (U17) recommends that the next sentence about consolidation among the agencies cite Schegg itself for the Booking Holdings 60 to 71 percent figure, and that the unsourced "mobile search" explanation be cut, since the study gives no causal account (G7). Because this is a trade-association report, the chapter should keep the descriptive use and let Hunold et al. (2020) and Ennis et al. (2023) carry the causal claims.
