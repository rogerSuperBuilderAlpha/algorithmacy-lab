## Summary
The press release announces that on 13 May 2024 the Commission designated Booking Holdings as a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act for its online intermediation service Booking.com, while deciding not to designate X Ads and TikTok Ads and opening a market investigation into the rebuttal submitted for the online social networking service X. The decisions followed the notifications the three companies made on 1 March 2024 on reaching the quantitative thresholds in Article 3 DMA and the Commission's 45-working-day review. Booking received six months to comply with the DMA obligations, which for an online intermediation service include the Article 5(3) prohibition on preventing business users from offering the same products at different prices through other channels, the rule that captures price parity clauses. The release is the primary source for the date and legal basis of the designation; its substantive effect on parity clauses is treated in Pantazi (2026).

## Use in the chapter
§5.5 states that the CJEU held parity clauses not ancillary in September 2024 "four months after Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act", without a citation. The audit confirms the date (13 May 2024, four months before the 19 September 2024 judgment) and proposes this release as the source. Role: support for a dated factual claim; it also anchors the chapter's observation that the competition-law route (C-264/23) and the regulatory route (DMA Art. 5(3)) closed on the parity gate within the same year. The audit separately asks that the stray hyperlink on "Booking.com" in that sentence be removed.
