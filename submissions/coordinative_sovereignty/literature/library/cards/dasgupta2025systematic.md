## Summary
This systematic review takes stock of worker voice research in the platform economy, where digital labour platforms act as technological intermediaries connecting customers to independent freelancers for on-demand tasks. The authors observe that the dominant disciplinary adaptations of voice have moderated and sidelined the more revolutionary potential Hirschman envisioned, and that the literature has focused on employee voice inside conventional organizations. Platform work lacks the shop floor that traditionally facilitated bottom-up voice, yet workers find new ways to express voice matching their nontraditional arrangements. From the reviewed studies the authors theorize a grassroots voice mechanism: voice constituted from below, outside the employing organization's formal channels, emerging from structured antagonism, power asymmetry, and the indeterminacy of the effort bargain. They argue this conceptualization shifts the discourse on voice beyond the confines of the organization and redraws HRM's boundaries to remain current as platform arrangements spread and influence traditional workplaces.
