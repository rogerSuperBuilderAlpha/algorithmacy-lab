## Summary
The book analyzes a connective world in which platforms have penetrated the heart of societies, disrupting markets and labor relations, circumventing institutions, and transforming social and civic practices and democratic processes. It pursues two questions: how do platform mechanisms work and to what effect are they deployed, and how can platforms be made to incorporate public values and benefit the public good? The authors dissect the platform ecosystem's mechanisms (datafication, commodification, selection) and trace intense struggles between competing ideological systems and contesting societal actors, market, government, and civil society, over who is responsible for anchoring public values such as privacy, accuracy, safety, security, fairness, accessibility, democratic control, and accountability. Four sectors carry the analysis: news, urban transport, health care and health research, and education, each examined at local, ecosystem, and geopolitical levels, including the conflicts between global platform markets and national governments that later European regulation would take up.
