## Summary
Tretter contributes to the debate on what sovereignty means in the digital sphere by taking contact tracing apps as a paradigmatic case. Starting from recent work that describes digital sovereignty as a network of different, overlapping exercises of power rather than a single locus, he conducts a narrative review of the literature on these apps to show what that network looks like in practice. In the contact-tracing context, sovereignty is best understood as a complex web of three actors, nations, big-tech companies and individuals, each exercising forms of power against or on behalf of the others in order to claim sovereignty for itself and to weaken or strengthen the claims of the others. The Apple and Google exposure-notification framework, which constrained what states could build, is the central illustration. He argues that the results generalize beyond the apps and can inform discussions of the regulation and governance of the digital generally.
