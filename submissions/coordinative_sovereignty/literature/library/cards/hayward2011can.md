## Summary
Hayward starts from an apparent tension between theories of democratic deliberation and democratic theories of multicultural accommodation and argues that many on both sides converge on an ideal of political freedom understood as non-domination. Her contribution is to split that ideal in two. Inter-agentive non-domination obtains when every participant in a power relation is free from rule by others who can set its terms; systemic non-domination obtains when the terms of the power relation itself are responsive to those they affect. The two do not covary: a relation can be free of any dominating agent and still be governed by terms that no one affected can change, and a relation with identifiable rulers can have terms that are responsive. Because they come apart, institutions and practices must be built to promote both. The article is a work of normative political theory, with no empirical component, and it is written against the neorepublican tendency to require an agent behind every domination.
