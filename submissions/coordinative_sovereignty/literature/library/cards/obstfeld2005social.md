## Summary
Obstfeld introduces the tertius iungens, "the third who joins", as a strategic orientation toward one's social network in which a person connects disconnected others or fosters new coordination among people already connected. He sets it against the tertius gaudens of structural-holes theory, where the broker's advantage comes from keeping parties apart and playing them off. Using a multimethod study in the engineering division of an automotive manufacturer, combining survey measures of network structure and orientation with qualitative material on innovation projects, he finds that individuals with a tertius iungens orientation, dense networks, and access to diverse knowledge are more involved in innovation. The paper's wider point is about agency: network position is not destiny, since the same bridging position can be worked divisively or conjunctively, and the choice matters for what organizations get out of their brokers.
