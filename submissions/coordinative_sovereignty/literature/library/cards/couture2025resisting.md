## Summary
Written for the Policy & Internet special section "Unthinking digital sovereignty", this narrative review examines discourses and practical proposals that resist or challenge the digital sovereignty of established states. Two groups are studied. The first is what the authors call civil society: non-governmental, non-profit organizations and collectives, often inspired by anti-capitalist, autonomist and anarchist ideas, that build self-managed digital infrastructures on free and open-source software and hardware. The second is Indigenous peoples and organizations that have developed a robust discourse of data sovereignty over knowledge and data relating to them. The method is an interpretive, non-systematic review guided by one rule: engage with sovereignty as the actors themselves define it. Four contributions follow. Autonomy and self-determination matter beyond state-centric models; digital sovereignty has cultural dimensions, especially in resisting ongoing colonization; openness is a point of tension between the two perspectives; and the concept of sovereignty itself, still tied to the Westphalian state, may be inadequate to these claims. The authors conclude that sovereignty functions for these groups as a generative term for a quest for greater autonomy and control.
