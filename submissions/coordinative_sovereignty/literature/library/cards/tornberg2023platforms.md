## Summary
Törnberg asks whether platformization brings a new form of capitalism or merely intensifies neoliberal trends, and answers with regulation theory: digital capitalism is a set of continuities that produce discontinuities. Three arguments follow. First, digital capitalism continues longer-running post-Fordist trends of financialization, digitalization and privatization, which converge in the emergence of digital proprietary markets, owned and regulated by transnational platform companies. Second, because the platform model is founded on monopolizing regulation, platforms come into direct competition with states and public institutions, pursuing a set of technopolitical strategies to claim the power to govern; the outcome is a geographically variegated institutional transformation rather than a uniform one. Third, while proprietary markets continue existing trends, they bring new pressures and affordances that produce discontinuities in social regulation. The paper is theoretical and synthetic. It does not use the phrase "platform sovereignty", but it describes platforms seeking to operate on the same level as states, as sovereign managers and regulators of markets.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited. Strand 7 lists it as SHOULD for §3.3 and §6 and records that "platform sovereignty" is not an established title-term; Törnberg is one of the works (with Cohen, D'Cunha, Lehdonvirta) where the idea lives without the label. In §3.3 it supports the political economy of interested mediation, platforms as private regulators of the markets they own. In §6 it helps the chapter position coordinative sovereignty against the platform-as-sovereign idea: the chapter concedes the platform governs and asks what standing the governed hold. Role: support, and a construct to position against.
