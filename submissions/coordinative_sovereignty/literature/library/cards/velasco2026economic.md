## Summary
Velasco argues that EU competition law should recognise economic dependence as a concept in its own right, beyond the dominance-based framework of Article 102 TFEU. The dominance paradigm is built on a horizontal, market-wide notion of power and leaves vertical relational power, exercised over trading partners who lack equivalent alternatives, largely to private law as a matter of contractual fairness. Drawing on bargaining theory and the theory of vertical competition, the paper holds that such relational power can distort the competitive process below the dominance threshold, especially on non-price parameters. Comparative analysis shows that several member states, following Germany's relative market power, and Japan and South Korea through abuse of superior bargaining position, already regulate this, with clear functional convergence. At EU level the DMA addresses only collective dependence on designated gatekeepers, and national regimes survive through Article 3(2) of Regulation 1/2003, producing fragmentation. In the context of the ongoing evaluation of Regulation 1/2003, Velasco proposes a European concept of economic dependence that would permit case-by-case intervention and restore coherence, in line with the Treaties' constitutional values.
