## Summary
The article asks how and why platform workers respond to the novel organizing solutions platforms impose, and how those responses vary with the regulatory environment. Using resource dependence theory, the authors study Uber drivers through topic modelling of drivers' online forum posts, complemented by a qualitative analysis of triangulated sources. They find that workers do not simply succumb to the organizing solutions imposed on them; they also actively oppose them or supplement them with arrangements of their own. The central result is that the pattern of response depends on local regulatory structure, because regulation changes the mutual dependence and balance of power between platform and workers. Where regulation bears directly on both drivers and the platform, the relationship tends toward mutual dependence; where regulation reaches drivers only through the platform, responses turn more antagonistic. The paper contributes to work on new forms of organizing by bringing the worker, previously absent from platform-regulation research, into the analysis.
