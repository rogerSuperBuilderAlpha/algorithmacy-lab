## Summary
Wals and Schinkel model the combination of two contract terms used by hotel booking platforms: a narrow price parity clause, which forbids the hotel to undercut the platform on its own website, and a best price guarantee, under which the platform refunds consumers who find a lower price elsewhere. Building on Wang and Wright's framework, they show that a narrow parity clause combined with a low-hassle best price guarantee leaves only the price parity and monopoly equilibrium, in which consumers are worse off than if no platform operated at all. The guarantee deters entry by a rival platform, because any lower price a rival induces is matched by refund, while the narrow clause removes price competition from the hotel's direct channel. An equally efficient entrant could in principle enter, but network effects make that outcome unstable. The authors conclude that the combination calls for a competition policy aimed at the contract pair rather than at wide parity alone.
