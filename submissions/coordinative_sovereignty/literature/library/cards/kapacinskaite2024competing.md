## Summary
Kapacinskaite and Mostajabi contrast traditional market entry with entry by a platform owner into a market occupied by its own complementors, and ask how complementors respond across platforms. They hypothesise that generalists, complementors that also operate on another platform and therefore face low repositioning costs, shift effort away from the focal platform, while specialists, whose effort is concentrated on one platform, double down on it. Using Apple's launch of the Files app on the App Store as the entry event, they find support for both predictions: multihoming developers moved effort toward the competing platform and single-homing developers intensified their commitment to Apple. Comparing Apple's entry with entries by other large firms such as Microsoft and SanDisk, they find that only the platform owner's entry elicits a strong complementor response. The paper contributes to competitive and corporate strategy by showing that complementor heterogeneity governs the allocation of effort when the platform becomes a competitor.

Not yet cited. Strand 2 lists it as a should for §5.3, where the chapter reads exit through multihoming and argues that an actor who can hold more than one platform faces a contingent gate. The paper shows the other half: the single-homing complementor, with no outside option, responds to the owner's encroachment by deepening dependence rather than leaving.
