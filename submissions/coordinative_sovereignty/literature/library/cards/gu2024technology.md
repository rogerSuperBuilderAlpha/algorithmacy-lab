## Summary
Gu asks how communication technology shapes disintermediation, the practice of buyers and sellers who meet on a platform taking their transaction off it. The identification comes from a natural experiment: the blockade of Skype in mainland China cut off a communication channel that the freelance marketplace under study could not monitor. Comparing affected and unaffected users, she finds that restricting the hard-to-monitor channel reduced disintermediation by about 18 percent. The effect concentrates where the gains from bypass are largest: time-sensitive work, communication-heavy projects and specialized roles, and among cost-conscious participants such as experienced users and individuals rather than enterprise clients. The paper's lesson for platforms is that vulnerability to disintermediation is partly a function of the communication infrastructure around the platform, so it can be managed in platform design and anticipated in market expansion. For the present purpose, the finding shows that the availability of a direct tie is itself something the environment, or the platform, designs.
