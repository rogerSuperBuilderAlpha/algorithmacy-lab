## Summary
This conceptual paper surfaces the paradigmatic and ideological assumptions shaping algorithmic management research. The authors identify two sets, jointly labeled algo economicus: an ontology of algorithms, on which HRM algorithms are non-human entities out there with material agency apart from human intervention, and an ontology of management, on which algorithmic management is a form of control aimed at maximizing economic or shareholder value. They document how these assumptions run through the field's definitions and framings, from control-centered definitions and Foucauldian panopticon metaphors to readings of platforms as hyper-Taylorist, and argue that even work moving past labor process theory stays inside the economist paradigm of value creation. These assumptions produced advances but also blind spots that hamper new ways of studying HRM algorithms. The paper's avenues forward call for research foregrounding human agency in the construction and use of algorithms and a humanistic, wellbeing-oriented perspective on what algorithmic management is for.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter's current text. The sweep proposes it for §3.1 and §3.3 as a contesting source. The chapter's interested mediator presupposes exactly what this paper names as an assumption: that the algorithmic mediator is a control instrument maximizing the firm's objectives (§3.3 grounds opacity in rent preservation). Lamers and colleagues argue this algo economicus framing is a paradigm choice with blind spots, not an established fact, so the chapter should either defend the assumption for its platform cases or concede its scope. Citing it shows §3's political economy is adopted with awareness of the field's self-critique.
