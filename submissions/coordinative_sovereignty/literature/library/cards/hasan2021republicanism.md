## Summary
Hasan asks what domination is and answers against the leading republican view. On the dyadic conception, a person is dominated when subject to the unconstrained power of another particular agent: master over slave, husband over wife, boss over worker. Hasan argues that domination is better understood structurally, as domination by institutions: the institution of slavery dominates the slave, patriarchy the wife, labour law the worker. Structural domination is the more abstract relation in which social, economic and legal institutions both enable dyadic domination and are sustained by it. The claim is that the dyadic conception misdescribes the power dynamics of the very paradigm cases that motivate republican theorising, because what makes the boss's power arbitrary is the legal and economic structure that backs it rather than the boss. The article is analytic political philosophy and engages Pettit and the labour republicans directly.
