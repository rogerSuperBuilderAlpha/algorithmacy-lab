## Summary
Cameron and Rahman study how the shift from managerial to algorithmically mediated customer control changes the relationship between control and resistance. On platforms, customers rate workers and algorithms tally those ratings to govern future opportunities, so control is distributed across platform, customer, and worker. Drawing on a comparative ethnography of two of the largest ride-hailing platforms, they find that this arrangement has expanded the service encounter so that control and resistance extend well beyond the execution of a task. Workers have the most latitude to resist early in the labour process and must change tactics as their ability to resist shrinks in each later stage. The paper therefore examines resistance before, during, and after a task. Its second result is that workers' everyday interactions with customers can influence and manipulate the algorithm in ways the platform cannot always observe, which exposes a limit of algorithmically mediated customer control.
