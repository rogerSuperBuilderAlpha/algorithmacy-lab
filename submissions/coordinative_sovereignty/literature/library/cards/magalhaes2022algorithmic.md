## Summary
Magalhães argues that algorithmic resistance can take a form the literature rarely considers: political disengagement. Drawing on interviews with ordinary Brazilian users of Facebook, he finds that some people stop acting politically on the platform as a way of avoiding an algorithmic visibility regime they experience as demeaning their civic voice. Users give three reasons. Some assume that algorithms, by creating bubbles, make their citizenship useless, so speaking is pointless. Some judge that being seen on Facebook requires unacceptable sacrifices of their values and wellbeing. Some describe the distress of achieving political visibility and then being unable to control it. Magalhães treats this withdrawal as a genuine if ambiguous form of resistance, since it evades the regime without contesting it, and he situates it within Brazil's process of autocratization, where disengagement from platform politics has costs beyond the individual. The central result is that much of what counts as resistance leaves platform power untouched.
