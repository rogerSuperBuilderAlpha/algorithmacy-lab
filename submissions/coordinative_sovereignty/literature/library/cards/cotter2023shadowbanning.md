## Summary
Cotter introduces "black box gaslighting" to name a second-order opacity problem that the standard "black box" framing misses. Beyond secrecy and technical complexity, platforms can exploit their perceived epistemic authority over their own algorithms to discredit what users have learned through experience, eroding users' confidence in their own knowledge and undermining the legitimacy of their critique. The case is Instagram's denial that shadowbanning exists, studied through interviews with seventeen influencers who believed their visibility had been suppressed and through online discourse around the dispute. The analysis shows how the platform's flat denial, set against the influencers' inferential evidence from reach and engagement data, made the users' claims look paranoid even when well grounded. Cotter concludes that platforms hold a structural advantage in epistemic contests over the legitimacy of critique, and argues for recognising both the incompleteness of platform claims to truth and the standing of user knowledge about algorithms.
