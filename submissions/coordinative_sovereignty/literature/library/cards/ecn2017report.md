## Summary
The report records a monitoring exercise run in 2016 by ten national competition authorities and the Commission, coordinated through the European Competition Network, after Booking.com and Expedia replaced their wide rate-parity clauses with narrow ones in mid-2015 and France and Germany went further. It combines an electronic survey of hotels across the participating Member States with data requests to the major online travel agencies and to metasearch sites, and econometric analysis of prices and availability. The central findings are that the parity changes had produced limited behavioural change: nearly half of responding hotels did not know the clauses had changed, most had not knowingly price-differentiated between OTAs, commission rates of the three major OTAs ranged from ten percent to above twenty percent and had not clearly moved, and the evidence did not conclusively show harm from the clauses. The heads of the ECN took the results into account in deciding against further coordinated action and in favour of continued monitoring.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §5.5 as the source for the commission band: "which the European Competition Network's monitoring exercise put at 10% to above 20% of each booking (European Competition Network, 2017)". Role: support for the factual premise of the hotel and OTA worked case. The report also supplies a second, uncited finding that bears on §5.5's argument that lifting the parity constraint opens exit only in principle: 47% of hotels did not know the clauses had changed and more than 80% did not alter their behaviour, which the chapter could use beside Wals and Schinkel (2018) and Mantovani et al. (2021).
