## Summary
This is the Commission's statutory review of the Digital Markets Act, delivered on 28 April 2026 under Article 53 of the Regulation and accompanied by a staff working document. It takes stock of designation and enforcement in the Act's first years, assesses whether the obligations in Articles 5 to 7 have delivered contestability and fairness, and considers whether the scope should be extended. On messaging, the report finds that the interoperability obligation has had effect: alternative messaging services can now interoperate with WhatsApp and Facebook Messenger, and this has already brought new providers into the market, including a European SME offering new functions. On social networking, the Commission declines for now to extend horizontal interoperability. It gives three reasons: an external contractor's study showed mixed evidence and greater technical complexity than for messaging; demand for such interoperability from both end users and business users was limited; and the messaging obligation has not applied long enough to draw final conclusions. The report therefore reads as a cautious consolidation rather than an expansion of the DMA.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §7: "the European Commission (2026) reports that messaging interoperability has brought new providers into the market, including a European SME, while judging it too early to extend the same obligation to social networking". The chapter's paraphrase matches the text opened this session (sections 4.1 and 4.4). Role: legal instrument and official evidence for the interoperability discussion, where the chapter distinguishes what interoperability does against a contingent mediator (restores exit) from what it does against a necessary one.
