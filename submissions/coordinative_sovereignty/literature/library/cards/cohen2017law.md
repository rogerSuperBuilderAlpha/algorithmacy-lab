## Summary
Cohen's article maps patterns of legal-institutional change in the platform-driven economy. Its premise is that the platform is not only a new business model, social technology, or infrastructural formation but the core organizational form of the informational economy: platforms do not enter or expand markets, they replace and rematerialize them. The first part traces the evolution of the platform as a mode of economic reorganization and the way platforms restructure exchange and information flow. The second examines how actions by and on behalf of platform businesses are reshaping legal entitlements and obligations, including the treatment of algorithmic logics as zones of exclusivity and the strategic use of opacity in litigation. The third part describes the challenges platform intermediation poses for existing regulatory institutions and the institutional responses emerging in reply, closing with a section that asks whether platforms are becoming emergent transnational sovereigns. The argument is that legal institutions have systematically facilitated, not merely reacted to, the platform economy.

## Use in the chapter
Section 3.3 cites it for the operation of opacity: platforms "extracting transparency from users but shielding basic operational knowledge" (p. 154) and deploying opacity to defeat class identification (p. 182). Section 3.2 groups it with Pasquale on disclosures that reproduce the standing gap, and §6 names "the platform sovereign (Cohen, 2017)" as a theory of platform authority that coordinative sovereignty views from the other end. Role: support on all three. Audit O14 flags that Cohen's phrase is "emergent transnational sovereigns," confirmed this session as a section heading at p. 199, so the §6 wording should be adjusted. Strand 7 lists Cohen among the works where the idea of platform sovereignty lives without being a title term.
