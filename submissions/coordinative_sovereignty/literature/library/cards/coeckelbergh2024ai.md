## Summary
The book argues that artificial intelligence, as currently developed and deployed, does more than serve authoritarian regimes: it erodes the principles on which liberal democracies rest, including freedom, equality and the conditions for informed public judgment. Coeckelbergh treats AI as a political technology rather than a neutral tool, tracing how recommendation, surveillance and automated decision shape what citizens know, how they deliberate, and whether they can hold power to account. The book's constructive part holds that technical fixes are insufficient; it calls for more democratic technologies, new political institutions suited to an AI-saturated public sphere, and a renewal of education and culture so that AI serves the common good. It is a short work of political philosophy aimed at a broad readership, and the reviews located this session treat it as a synthesis of the author's earlier work on AI ethics and political philosophy rather than as a new empirical study.
