## Summary
Plantin, Lagoze, Edwards and Sandvig bring together two approaches that emerged separately to characterize new digital objects: infrastructure studies, rooted in the history and sociology of large technical systems, and platform studies, rooted in media and software studies. Through case studies of the Open Web, Facebook, and Google they show what each perspective captures. Infrastructure studies explains the evolution of shared, widely accessible systems of the kind governments often provide or regulate in the public interest; platform studies captures how communication and expression are enabled and constrained by programmable digital systems. Their thesis is a double movement: platform-based services acquire characteristics of infrastructure, becoming essential, taken for granted, and hard to leave, while new and existing infrastructures are built or reorganized on the logic of platforms, modular, proprietary, and governed by their owners. The article proposes the combined framework, often summarized as platformization of infrastructure and infrastructuralization of platforms, for future case studies.
