## Summary
Hirschman's book, as the publisher description opened this session presents it, studies decline rather than growth: how members and customers respond when a firm, organisation or state deteriorates. He distinguishes two responses. Exit is for the customer to switch to a competing product or the member to leave; voice is for members or customers to agitate and exert influence for change from within. The book questions the efficiency of the competitive mechanism, which relies wholly on exit, in a class of important situations, and argues that exit often undercuts voice while failing to counteract decline, because the most quality-sensitive customers leave first and take their complaints with them. Loyalty is introduced as the factor that retards exit and allows voice to play its role. The interplay of the three concepts is applied across economic, social and political cases, including the chapter on how a monopolist can be comforted by the availability of an alternative that siphons off its critics.

## Use in the chapter
§5.3 opens with "Hirschman (1970) drew the distinction for organizations in decline" and later says "Hirschman's own analysis blocks any easy confidence in it. He observed that exit does not reliably strengthen voice and can substitute for it ... so a monopolist can be comforted by an alternative that removes the critics." The audit (U21) finds the paraphrase faithful but unlocated and asks for chapter locators (ch. 3, ch. 5, ch. 7). Role: support; the exit/voice distinction is the spine of the necessary/contingent mediator diagnostic. The audit also notes (S16, S17) that Maffie (2023) explicitly carries Hirschman's exit into gig work and should be cited beside Dasgupta et al. (2025).

## Key quotations
