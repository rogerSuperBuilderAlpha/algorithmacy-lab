## Summary
This open-access edited volume follows the earlier New Digital Work, which addressed digital sovereignty at the workplace, and extends the theme to companies and organizations and to entire industry branches. The editors' aim is to broaden the view from what digital work means for workers and their workplaces to what digital sovereignty means for the firm as an actor that must control its data, infrastructure, and code while depending on external providers. The contributions are interdisciplinary, drawn from computer science, economics, social science, human resource management, organizational psychology and human factors, mechanical and industrial engineering, and law, and they address common challenges to organizational digital sovereignty alongside suggestions, best-practice examples, and sector perspectives. Chapter 1, by Hartmann, Shajek and Schmuntzsch, introduces and structures the concept of digital sovereignty for companies and other organizations. The volume is the German applied-research strand of the digital sovereignty debate, oriented to capability and self-determination in the use of digital technology rather than to state control.
