## Summary
Parmigiani observes that transaction cost economics, neoclassical economics and the capabilities literature all depict firm boundaries as the outcome of a dichotomous make-or-buy choice, and that none explains why firms concurrently source, that is, simultaneously make and buy the same good. The study combines the three organizational-economics theories to predict when firms make, buy, or concurrently source, and tests the predictions with a survey of small manufacturing firms (powder-metal component makers). Support is found for aspects of all three theories: volume uncertainty, technological uncertainty, performance ambiguity and the firm's and suppliers' expertise each shape the choice. The central result is that concurrent sourcing is a distinctly different choice with its own logic rather than a point along a make-to-buy continuum or a hedge between two options. The article established "concurrent sourcing" as a construct and prompted later work on plural sourcing.

## Use in the chapter
§5.4 cites it for the portfolio view of platforms: "Firms that both make and buy the same input are not hedging between two options but pursuing a distinct strategy with its own logic (Parmigiani, 2007), and an actor that fights one function of a platform while depending on another is pursuing that same distinct strategy." Role: support by analogy; the abstract's finding that concurrent sourcing is "a distinctly different choice" rather than a point on a continuum is exactly what the chapter borrows. The analogy from sourcing a good to contesting one function of a mediator while relying on another is the chapter's own extension and should be presented as such.
