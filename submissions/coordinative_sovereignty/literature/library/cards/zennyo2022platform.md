## Summary
A theoretical model of platform encroachment in which a platform not only intermediates between consumers and third-party sellers but also sells its own first-party products, choosing between a fair search engine, under which all products are equally likely to appear in results, and a biased one, under which the first-party product is more likely to appear. The counterintuitive result is that biased encroachment leads the platform to impose a lower commission on sellers, which lowers the equilibrium price and attracts more consumers, and increased consumer participation can raise seller participation through indirect network externalities. The mechanism is that own-content bias raises the platform's profit per consumer, so it competes harder for consumers by cutting the commission. The model thus complicates the presumption that self-preferencing straightforwardly harms the platform's two sides, while confirming that the platform's control of the search engine is the lever that reallocates surplus between itself and its complementors.
