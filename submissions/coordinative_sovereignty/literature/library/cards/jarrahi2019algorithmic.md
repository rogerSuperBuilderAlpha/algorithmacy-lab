## Summary
The paper studies how gig workers understand and appropriate the algorithms that manage them. Data-driven algorithms let digital labor platforms automatically manage transactions between thousands of workers and service recipients, and the resulting information asymmetries leave workers uncertain about how work is assigned and evaluated. Drawing on a study of Upwork users, the authors show that workers are not passive recipients of algorithmic management: they develop a working literacy of the platform's automated features and use it to preserve professional autonomy inside algorithmic constraints. The paper introduces algorithmic competencies for this practical knowledge, the learned capability to make sense of automated features, to interpret how the algorithms function, and where useful to work around or manipulate them strategically. The contribution is to shift attention from what algorithmic management does to workers toward the competencies through which workers understand, navigate, and appropriate the systems managing them.
