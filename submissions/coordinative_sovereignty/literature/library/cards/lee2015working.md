## Summary
Lee, Kusbit, Metsky and Dabbish study how software algorithms that assign, optimize, and evaluate human work change the experience of workers, in the setting of Uber and Lyft when both were new. They coin the term algorithmic management for software algorithms that assume managerial functions together with the institutional devices that support them in practice. The study is qualitative: interviews with drivers, analysis of online driver forums, and observation of the apps. Findings are organized by the three managerial functions the algorithms performed: assigning work, providing informational support, and evaluating performance. The paper describes how drivers responded to each, and how they used online forums to make sense of the algorithm's features collectively. It closes with implications and future work. The paper's lasting contribution is the term itself and the observation that algorithmic management lets companies oversee very many workers at scale while its impact on workers had been unexplored.
