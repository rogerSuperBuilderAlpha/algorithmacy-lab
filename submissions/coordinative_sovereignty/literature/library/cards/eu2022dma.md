## Summary
The Digital Markets Act establishes ex ante obligations for providers of core platform services that the Commission designates as gatekeepers on the basis of size, user numbers and entrenched position. Its aim, stated in the title, is contestable and fair markets in the digital sector. Article 5 lists self-executing obligations, including the ban on wide and narrow parity clauses in Article 5(3) and, in Article 5(4), the duty to let business users communicate and promote offers to end users acquired through the platform and conclude contracts with them outside it, the anti-steering rule. Article 6 lists obligations susceptible to further specification: Article 6(4) on third-party app stores and sideloading, Article 6(5) banning self-preferencing in ranking, indexing and crawling, and Article 6(7) on interoperability with operating-system features. Article 7 adds interoperability duties for number-independent messaging. The Commission enforces through specification decisions, non-compliance decisions and fines, with the first non-compliance decisions issued in 2025.

## Use in the chapter
The chapter names the DMA in §1, §5.2, §7 and §8 without a reference-list entry; audit row U5 asks for an APA legal entry and article pinpoints at first mention: Arts. 5(4), 6(4), 6(5), 6(7), 7. Row U8 ties §5.2's app-store bypass discussion (sideloading, external purchase links) to Arts. 5(4) and 6(4) and to the Commission's April 2025 Apple decision. Role: legal instrument. In §7 and §8 the DMA is the chapter's chief example of regulation that opens exit against a contingent gate rather than conferring standing over a necessary function.

## Key quotations
"The gatekeeper shall allow business users, free of charge, to communicate and promote offers, including under different conditions, to end users acquired via its core platform service or through other channels, and to conclude contracts with those end users, regardless of whether, for that purpose, they use the core platform services of the gatekeeper." (Art. 5(4))
"The gatekeeper shall not treat more favourably, in ranking and related indexing and crawling, services and products offered by the gatekeeper itself than similar services or products of a third party." (Art. 6(5))
