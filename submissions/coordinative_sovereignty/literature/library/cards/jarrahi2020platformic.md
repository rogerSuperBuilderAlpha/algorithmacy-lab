## Summary
Jarrahi, Sutherland, Nelson and Sawyer study Upwork, a platform for knowledge-intensive freelance projects, through forum discussions, interviews with workers, and walkthroughs of the platform. They propose "platformic management" as a concept wider than algorithmic control: the platform manages work through a sociotechnical assembly of rules, interface affordances, reputation systems and escrow, not only through algorithms. Their central finding is a paradox. The platform enables the autonomy that gig workers want, by letting them choose projects and clients, while also serving as a means of control that keeps transactions viable and protects the platform against disintermediation. They conceptualize Upwork as a boundary resource, a term from software ecosystem research, that facilitates worker independence and constrains worker behavior at once. The paper is one of the earlier studies to name disintermediation, the worker and client leaving the platform to deal directly, as a structural threat the platform's design is built to prevent.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited; the chapter cites Jarrahi and Sutherland 2019 for "algorithmic competencies" in §4. Strand 1 lists this paper as a near miss for §3. Its relevance is twofold: it widens the object from algorithmic to platformic management, which fits the chapter's argument that the mediator is a firm rather than an algorithm, and it identifies disintermediation as the thing the platform's controls guard against, which is the chapter's bypass test seen from the platform's side (compare Hagiu and Wright 2024 on leakage).
