## Summary
Nolteernsting analyses the EU's ranking transparency rules since 2020, which require online platforms to disclose the main parameters of ranking separately to business users, under Article 5 of the P2B Regulation, and to consumers, under Article 7(4a) of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, with a parallel duty in Article 27 of the Digital Services Act. The article is doctrinal. It compares the scope, content and addressees of the provisions and argues that they are substantively identical rather than merely similar, so that the parallel regimes create redundancy and regulatory confusion. It then asks whether the transparency interests of commercial users and consumers genuinely differ and concludes that the two groups share equivalent information interests and protection needs. On that basis, and drawing on the guiding concepts and legislative intent behind the provisions, the author proposes consolidating the fragmented rules into a single "platform-to-user" (P2U) regulation with consistent standards that eliminate duplication while keeping an appropriately balanced level of transparency.

## Use in the chapter

The gaps file marks it MUST for §8, where the chapter says that repeal of the P2B Regulation "would remove the only duty in European law to disclose the main parameters of ranking to the business users being ranked" and that the DSA's recommender transparency "runs to a platform's users and does not replace that duty." Nolteernsting directly contests the "only duty" claim: near-identical ranking-disclosure duties exist in UCPD Art. 7(4a) and DSA Art. 27, and she argues the P2B duty is redundant. The chapter can hold its narrower point (the P2B duty is the one addressed to business users and the one paired with a channel, mediation and representative action) but must drop the superlative.
