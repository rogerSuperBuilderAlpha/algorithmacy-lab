## Summary
Vallas and Schor review the sociology of platform work by sorting the literature into four metaphors: platforms as entrepreneurial incubators that free workers from bureaucracy, as digital cages that tighten control, as accelerants of precarity, and as chameleons that adapt to whatever regulatory environment they face. Each captures something; none explains the combination of loose attachment and tight control that gig platforms display. Their alternative is to treat platforms as permissive potentates: organizations that externalize responsibility and control over economic transactions onto workers and customers while retaining concentrated power over the rules, the data and the allocation of work. On this view the platform is a distinct governance mechanism, neither market, hierarchy nor network, and it raises regulatory problems that existing employment and competition categories do not fit. They close by noting that struggles over platform regulation remain open and hard to predict as the platform economy spreads.
