## Summary
Maffie asks how and why platform workers quit. Interviews with 84 ride-hail drivers show that platforms use information asymmetries to downplay the true cost of a gig: the benefits (freedom from a boss, instant income, schedule control) arrive immediately, while costs such as vehicle depreciation accrue slowly, so some workers come to rely on ride-hail income before they understand what it costs them, a condition he calls "acquired dependence". Workers who cannot simply leave respond by building off-app "pirate" taxi operations, using the app to source repeat customers whom they then serve directly. A survey of 330 drivers confirms that greater acquired dependence predicts more pirate activity. The theoretical contribution is to Hirschman's framework: independence is a distinct response alongside exit and voice, in which workers disintermediate the platform from inside while continuing to use it, and what looks to the firm like quitting is in fact the transfer of its clients to a rival the worker has built.
