## Summary
Hummel and colleagues review 341 publications to establish what is meant by data sovereignty in settings where new data-driven technologies confront individuals, organisations, and states with the problem of retaining control over their data. They count the frequency of related notions (data, digital, and cyber sovereignty), map the agents the term concerns, the contexts in which it appears, and the values it invokes. The sample shows considerable divergence and occasional unclarity about intended meanings. In response the authors propose a conceptual grid that systematises the dimensions and connotations of the term. Each dimension relates in some way to meaningful control, ownership, and other claims to data articulated by agents ranging from individuals to countries. They conclude that data sovereignty alludes to a nuanced mixture of normative concepts including inclusive deliberation and recognition of the fundamental rights of data subjects. The work is a scoping review rather than an operationalisation.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §2.3 for the definition of digital sovereignty as "the ability of states, organizations, and communities to exercise control over their digital infrastructures, data, and technologies (Floridi, 2020; Hummel et al., 2021; Pohle & Thiel, 2020)", which the review supports. Also cited in §8: "Fratini et al. (2024), Hummel et al. (2021), and Schmuntzsch et al. (2025) operationalize organizational digital sovereignty". The audit (E6) rejects that use: Hummel et al. is a data-sovereignty review and does not operationalize organizational digital sovereignty; replace in §8 with Hartmann, Shajek and Schmuntzsch 2025 and Moschko et al. 2025 and keep Hummel in §2.3. Role: support for §2.3 only.
