## Summary
Tomasello's commentary asks how the notion of civics should be rethought in the digital age and within the infosphere. The first part examines the effects of surveillance capitalism and datafication on the idea of civics, noting that platform algorithms are often opaque, profit-driven and tuned to reward behaviours that increase online presence, and that identities are assigned through continuous data processing, which is the condition Cheney-Lippold named algorithmic citizenship. He argues that users' data rights should sit at the centre of any digital civic initiative. The second part turns to the notions of political community and membership that digital civics presupposes, and argues that the diverse values and virtues that shape civics across the world cannot be reduced to a single source, so the spatial scope, or political geography, of the community a digital civic initiative addresses has to be defined rather than assumed. The piece draws on Bauman's liquid society to frame the instability of membership online.
