## Summary
Venturini revives Ong's notion of secondary orality to conceptualize online conspiracism as a creative, if monstrous, response to the attention economy of social media. Combining the classic literature on oral cultures with current research on online subcultures, he takes conspiratorial folklore seriously as a cultural form and develops a programme of research into its features and into what the abstract calls its surprising adaptation to the attention regime of digital media. The implication for media theory is that platforms revive an oral register of communication rather than extending the literate one. The article sets out a research programme for a sociology of such online monsters rather than reporting a single empirical study.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter; strand 5 and the gaps report list it under Contests for §2 and §4.
