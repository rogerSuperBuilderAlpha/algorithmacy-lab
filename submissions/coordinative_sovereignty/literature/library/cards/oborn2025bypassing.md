## Summary
The paper studies four gig platforms in the Swedish basic service sector and finds that algorithmic management there is limited and widely bypassed. All three actors in the triangular relationship deviate from the logic of app-based competitive bidding over work. Workers, frustrated by insufficient work and asymmetric information when bidding, contact platform administrators and clients directly outside the app to gain advantage and avoid the matching process's failures. Corporate clients prefer to direct work to particular workers rather than accept algorithmic allocation; individual clients strike out-of-app spot deals. Platform administrators intervene by hand to fix digital market failures and to keep their position as intermediary against the danger of being cut out by bilateral side-deals. The authors frame worker non-compliance through Emirbayer and Mische on agency and Williamson on opportunism as "opportunistic agency," antagonistic to platform capital but ultimately serving the sale of labor power. The extended lag between contracting and performance in this sector, unlike ride-hail or delivery, is what makes bypass feasible; the paper offers this as a corrective to claims that algorithmic management is pervasive and omnipotent.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. Strands 1 and 7 both list it under contests for §5.2. The chapter's bypass test asks whether a mediator keeps its grip once a direct tie is restored; Öborn et al. show a case where the tie was never closed and all three parties, including the platform's own staff, routinely use it, and where the platform's response is manual intervention to stay in the triad. Role: contests the assumption that the platform mediator is a binding third party in every gig setting, and supplies evidence that whether a mediator is necessary or contingent depends on the market's timing structure, which the chapter can absorb as support for its "sorts functions for a given dependent party" clause.
