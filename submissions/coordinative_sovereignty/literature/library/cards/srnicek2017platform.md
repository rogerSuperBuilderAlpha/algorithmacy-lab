## Summary
Srnicek's short book argues that the platform is the emerging business model of contemporary capitalism, produced by the long downturn since the 1970s and the search for new sources of profit after the dot-com crash and the 2008 crisis. Data has become the raw material of the economy, a resource to be extracted, refined and used, and the platform is the organisational form built to extract it, because it positions the firm as the intermediary between groups and records their interactions. He distinguishes five types: advertising platforms such as Google and Facebook, cloud platforms such as Salesforce, industrial platforms such as GE and Siemens, product platforms such as Rolls-Royce and Spotify, and lean platforms such as Uber and Airbnb. Network effects and cross-subsidisation push platforms toward monopoly, and their strategic tendencies are the expansion of extraction, positioning as a gatekeeper, convergence of markets and the enclosure of ecosystems. He concludes that many lean platforms are unsustainable and that firms will either find new means of extracting surplus or revert to more traditional forms.
