## Summary
The authors note that the EU increasingly invokes digital sovereignty to safeguard European values, yet the term has no settled definition. They analyse how EU institutional actors use it and map the policies enacted under it. Reviewing 180 EU webpages that mention digital sovereignty between March 2020 and March 2021, they propose a definition centred on legitimate, controlling authority over digital infrastructure and identify five policy areas: data governance, constraints on platform power, digital infrastructure, emerging technologies and cybersecurity. Data governance shows the most progress; GAIA-X is treated as a federated cloud initiative that is not itself a provider and that counts Amazon and Google among its founding participants; data localisation is discussed against the Free Flow of Data Regulation's prohibition on member-state localisation mandates. The authors conclude that existing measures do too little on competitiveness and private-sector regulation to secure the values the discourse invokes.
