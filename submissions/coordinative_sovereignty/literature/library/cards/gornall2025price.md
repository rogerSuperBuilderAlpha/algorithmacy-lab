## Summary
This three-page case note reports and assesses the Court of Justice's judgment of 19 September 2024 in Case C-264/23, Booking.com v 25hours Hotel Company Berlin, on the price parity (most-favoured-nation) clauses that online travel agencies impose on hotels. The authors distinguish wide clauses, which require the hotel to keep its price on the platform no higher than on any channel, from narrow clauses, which forbid only undercutting on the hotel's own direct channel, and they set out the competing accounts of the clauses as a defence against free-riding or as a restriction of hotels' pricing freedom. Their reading of the judgment is that the Court held the clauses not to be ancillary restraints, so that they cannot escape Article 101(1) as objectively necessary to the platform's service, and that the Court stressed the two-sided structure of the market when directing the national court on market definition under the Vertical Block Exemption. The note is descriptive and doctrinal, written for practitioners.
