## Summary
Geradin and Katsifis examine whether Apple's conduct in operating the App Store breaches competition law and how the concerns could be addressed. The App Store is the only channel through which developers can distribute apps on iOS, and by 2019 it carried more than fifty billion dollars of consumer spending. The purpose stated in the abstract is to examine whether that is so and, if it is, how the concerns can be addressed. The article is a long-form legal analysis, running to more than eighty pages, rather than an empirical study; only the abstract was opened this session, so the structure of the argument (market definition, the payment and steering rules, remedies) is not summarized here beyond what the abstract states.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter; strands 2 and 6 rank it MUST/SHOULD for §5.2 and §8. In §5.2 the app store is the chapter's example of a contingent gate (in-app payment, anti-steering) bolted to a necessary function (distribution on iOS), and in §8 the Apple fine under the DMA is the closed empirics. Geradin and Katsifis supply the pre-DMA antitrust analysis of exactly that separation. Role: support.
