## Summary
Kalantzis and Cope argue that generative AI is before anything else a technology of writing, a machine that writes, and that in a world-historical frame its arrival is as momentous for literacy and education as movable type and the printing press, and in its own way as problematic. The first part sets this claim historically and theoretically, noting that the technology entangles the unnatural language of code with natural language and that its writing is multimodal. The second part argues that literacy in the time of AI needs a revised grammar, drawing on the authors' multiliteracies theory of meaning as design, and contends that the traditional canon of literacy teaching was already anachronistic before generative AI made it worse. The third part reports an experimental application that uses generative AI in support of writing and learning. The conclusion proposes cyber-social literacy learning, a complementary relation between a human writer and a machine, and redirects the purpose of literacy teaching from utility to human growth.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. Strand 5 lists it as optional for §4, where the chapter defines algorithmacy against literacy and draws on the New Literacy Studies. Kalantzis and Cope offer the literacy-education field's own account of what changes when writing is machine-produced, and their insistence that the change is comparable to print supports the chapter's media-regime framing while keeping the competence at issue a literacy rather than an algorithmacy.
