## Summary
Sander proposes critical big data literacy as a concept that extends beyond basic data skills to awareness of, and critical reflection on, big data systems and their societal implications. The argument is that while media, digital and data literacy frameworks have been refined to match changing technologies, people's understanding of big data practices, including collection, analytics, automation and prediction, has only recently attracted attention, and citizens need to be able to recognise risks and benefits and to scrutinise the structural and systemic level of datafication. The empirical part identifies roughly forty online resources that aim to foster such literacy and reports a qualitative multi-method study of how citizens responded to a selection of them across three points in time. Interactive, accessible tools with appealing visualisations and constructive advice, such as the web series Do Not Track, had positive effects on awareness and attitudes. The article closes with recommendations for future literacy initiatives.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §4: data literacy "adds the reflexive and tactical capacity to identify how one's data are used and to act on it (Pangrazio & Selwyn, 2019; Sander, 2020)".
