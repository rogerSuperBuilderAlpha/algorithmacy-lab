## Summary
The article examines mass reporting, the targeted and often automated flagging of a creator's content by coordinated groups in order to trigger takedowns and account bans, as a form of networked harassment. Drawing on 18 in-depth interviews with creators on Instagram, TikTok, Twitch and YouTube who had been mass reported, most of them from marginalised groups or producing stigmatised content, together with an analysis of the platforms' community guidelines, Meisner uses a grounded-theory approach to trace three dimensions of the phenomenon: manipulating reporting infrastructures, removing posts and punishing creators, and reinforcing platform opacity and precarity. The central argument is that the infrastructures platforms present as democratic tools for community governance have been weaponised, so that the channels meant to protect creators from harassment become the channels through which they are harassed, while automated flagging also disrupts the few routes creators have for reaching platform support. The study broadens creator-governance research beyond recommendation algorithms to the human-operated flagging apparatus.
