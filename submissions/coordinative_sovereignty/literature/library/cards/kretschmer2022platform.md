## Summary
Kretschmer, Leiponen, Schilling and Vasudeva characterize platform ecosystems as a distinctive organizational form that enables innovation and economic efficiency, and they name the form: meta-organizations, organizations of organizations that are less formal and less hierarchical than firms yet more closely coupled than traditional markets. To function, a platform must coordinate multiple participants whose interests are not all aligned, without the employment contracts of a firm or the arm's-length contracts of a market. The article develops the strategic implications: the platform owner governs through design levers such as architecture, rules of access and participation, pricing, and the selective provision of boundary resources, and it must balance the autonomy that draws complementors against the control that keeps the ecosystem coherent. The piece sets an agenda for platform strategy research by asking how these organizational features shape competition among platforms and between platforms and their complementors. It is the strategy literature's closest analogue to the chapter's fourth coordination form.

Both the literature gaps (C3) and strands 2 and 5 rank it a must for §3, where the chapter adopts Stark and Pais's co-optation as a fourth term beside market, hierarchy, and network, and for §5.4's portfolio of mediators. Role: construct to position against. The meta-organization describes "coordination of legally autonomous actors without hierarchical authority or market contracts," which is exactly the space the chapter assigns to co-optation, so §3 must say why co-optation, enrollment by an interested and opaque mediator, is not meta-organization, coordination by design levers from the owner's point of view. The difference is standpoint and interest: the meta-organization frame takes the orchestrator's perspective and treats misalignment as a design problem, while co-optation takes the enrolled actor's and treats it as a standing problem.
