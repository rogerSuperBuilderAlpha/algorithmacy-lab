## Summary
Gu and Zhu study the tension a platform faces between building trust across its two sides and being bypassed once that trust is high enough for the sides to transact off-platform and avoid its fees. They exploit a randomized controlled trial run on an online freelance marketplace that varied a trust-enhancing feature and track both hiring and subsequent disintermediation. Enhanced trust increases the likelihood that high-quality freelancers are hired, a gain for the platform. But when the trust level is sufficiently high, it also increases disintermediation, and the revenue lost offsets the gain from better hiring. The authors identify heterogeneity across clients and freelancers in their propensity to take the relationship off-platform and discuss strategies platforms can use to soften the trade-off, such as bundling services that are hard to replicate outside the platform. The result gives a marketplace a reason to stop short of the integration that would make it indispensable.

## Use in the chapter
Section 5.2 cites it in "Gu and Zhu (2021), in a field experiment on a freelance marketplace, find that the platform's own trust-building raises the rate at which parties transact around it, which gives a mediator a reason to withhold the very integration that would make it necessary." Role: support for the claim that necessity is not fixed and that the mediator can choose its own contingency. Audit S16 asks that the conditional be added, since the effect appears only once trust is sufficiently high; the abstract confirms that wording. The paper is also the natural companion to Hagiu and Wright (2024) on leakage and Gu (2024) on the Skype blockade, both approaching bypass from the platform's side.
