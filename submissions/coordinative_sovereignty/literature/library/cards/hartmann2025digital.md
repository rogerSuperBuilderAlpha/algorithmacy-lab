## Summary
The introductory chapter frames the fourth volume in a series on digital sovereignty in the economy, this one devoted to the organizational level. The editors build their analytical frame from three kinds of capital: human capital, the knowledge and competencies of employees; structural capital, the organizational structures that enable access to and development of knowledge; and relational capital, the organization's external relationships. They then propose two control aspects as the pillars of sovereignty, confidence that intended outcomes can be achieved and freedom to pursue a range of options, and cross the three capitals with the two aspects to produce a six-cell matrix for analysing and designing an organization's digital sovereignty. The chapter surveys the volume's contributions, which come from computer science, economics, the social sciences, organizational and work psychology, human factors, mechanical and industrial engineering and law, and presents the result as an interdisciplinary framework for the sovereignty challenges facing companies and other organizations.
