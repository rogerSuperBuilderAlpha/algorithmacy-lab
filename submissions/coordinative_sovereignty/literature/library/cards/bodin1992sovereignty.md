## Summary
The volume presents, in Julian Franklin's translation and with his introduction, the four chapters of the Six livres de la république that carry Bodin's theory of sovereignty, the first complete English rendering of those chapters since 1606. The publisher's description states the core doctrine: sovereignty is indivisible, and the entire power of the state must be vested in a single person or group. Bodin reached this position in the setting of the French wars of religion, and the description notes that the crisis of authority led him to an absolutist reading of the monarchies of his day, designed to remove any lawful ground for resistance. The chapters also treat the marks of sovereignty, the distinction between sovereign and magistrate, and the forms of state. The edition belongs to the Cambridge Texts in the History of Political Thought and carries a chronology and bibliography.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §1: "Bodin made sovereignty supreme, perpetual, and indivisible (Bodin, 1576/1992)." The chapter uses Bodin as the benchmark against which a standing that is shared, partial and exercised by complaint looks like a misuse of the term, and then argues that the democratic tradition already dissolved that objection. Role: support for the conceptual history, and the source of the objection the chapter answers.
