## Summary
Wood, Graham, Lehdonvirta, and Hjorth evaluate job quality in the remote gig economy, the provision of digital services through online labour platforms to clients anywhere. Focusing on workers in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa, they draw on 107 semi-structured interviews across six countries and a cross-regional survey of 679 workers. Across country contexts and types of work they find that algorithmic control, exercised through rating and reputation systems rather than direct supervision, is central to how the platforms operate. Algorithmic management tends to give workers high flexibility, autonomy, task variety, and complexity, which are the good-gig features. The same control mechanisms, however, also produce low pay, social isolation, unsocial and irregular hours, overwork, sleep deprivation, and exhaustion, because workers must stay available and responsive to protect their ratings. The paper is a landmark mixed-methods study and the source for the claim that platform flexibility is real but operates inside algorithmic control.
