## Summary
diSessa argues that the computer can transform learning, and science education in particular, not as a tool for more efficient instruction but as the basis of a new literacy that changes how people think. The book, written for a general reader with high-school science, draws on his work as scholar, technologist and teacher and on the Boxer programming environment, which was built to investigate what he calls computational literacies. He sets out a learning theory in which intuitive knowledge is the platform on which students build scientific understanding, explains why computers are powerful catalysts for that building, and discusses the material and social conditions under which a new literacy could spread. A central normative claim is the call for two-way literacies, in which everyone is a creator as well as a consumer of dynamic, interactive expressive forms. The book's literacy is expressive and educational: a capacity to write in computational media, not a capacity to read the systems that coordinate one.
