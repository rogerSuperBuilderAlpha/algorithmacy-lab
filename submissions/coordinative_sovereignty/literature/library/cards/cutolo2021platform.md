## Summary
Cutolo and Kenney introduce the platform-dependent entrepreneur (PDE) as a category that platform research has overlooked: the far more numerous firms that build businesses inside a platform's ecosystem. Drawing on Emerson's power-dependence theory, they argue that the power asymmetry between platform and ecosystem member is intrinsic to the economics and the technical architecture of digital platforms rather than a contingent abuse. Winner-take-most dynamics make platforms quasi-monopolies that leave PDEs few alternatives; the platform sets and changes the rules, controls the data and the interface, and can enter the PDE's market. The article is conceptual, drawing on cases across marketplaces, app stores and content platforms. It catalogues the risks PDEs face (rule changes, demotion, disintermediation, competition from the platform) and the strategies available to them, including multihoming, building off-platform assets and collective action, while noting that such countermeasures are difficult or costly, especially for smaller firms with fewer capabilities.

## Use in the chapter
Cited three times. §1 uses it for the claim that market positions are "decided somewhere else, inside the algorithmic systems through which the platform chooses what to surface and what to bury (Cutolo & Kenney, 2021; ...)." §3.2 states that PDEs "operate under power asymmetries that diversification cannot relieve, because in many sectors no alternative platform exists at the required scale (Cutolo & Kenney, 2021)"; the audit (S7) flags this as overreach and asks for softening to the source's "often quasi-monopolies, leaving few alternatives," with multihoming as a costly mitigation. §8 uses it for the firm that complies with the GDPR and DMA "yet still have no leverage over the ranking and review algorithms." The audit also proposes it for U11 (cost of modelling the system) and U16 (SME exposure), in each case with the firm-scale claim marked as inference.
