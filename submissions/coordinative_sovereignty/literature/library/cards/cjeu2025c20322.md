## Summary
The Court of Justice, on a reference from Austria, ruled on what a data subject is owed under Article 15(1)(h) GDPR when subject to automated decision-making under Article 22. The case arose after a mobile operator refused a contract on the basis of a credit assessment by Dun & Bradstreet Austria, and the data subject sought "meaningful information about the logic involved". The Court held that the controller must describe, in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, the procedure and principles actually applied in using the person's data to reach the result, such as a credit profile. The explanation must let the person understand which personal data were used and how, but it does not require disclosure of the algorithm itself. The Court further held that a controller cannot refuse disclosure merely by invoking trade secrets; the allegedly protected information must be given to the supervisory authority or court for a case-by-case balancing of rights. The judgment materially widens the practical content of the explanation right.

## Use in the chapter
Proposed by the evidence audit (U10) for §3.2, where the chapter says of Article 22 "Its threshold is high, its practice thin". The audit notes the judgment "widened the explanation right" and that "high threshold" is outdated after SCHUFA and this case, and proposes rewording to "its threshold is contested and its enforcement record thin". Cite beside cjeu2023c63421 and Binns and Veale. Role: legal instrument.

## Key quotations
- The controller must provide "relevant information and in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, the procedure and principles actually applied in order to use, by automated means, the personal data concerning that person with a view to obtaining a specific result, such as a credit profile."
