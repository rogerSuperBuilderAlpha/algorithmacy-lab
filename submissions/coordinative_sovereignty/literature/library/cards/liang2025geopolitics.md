## Summary
The article brings geopolitics into platform-governance research, which has concentrated on moderation, misinformation and privacy. Through two cases, China's 2021 regulatory action against Didi Chuxing and the United States' attempts to ban or force the divestiture of TikTok, the authors analyse policy documents and media discourse to show that national security and economic competitiveness are the drivers of state action against platforms. They identify two mechanisms of sovereign control: algorithmic sovereignty, the assertion of authority, including extraterritorial authority, over a platform's algorithmic operations and their ownership, and data localization, the enforcement of territorial limits on cross-border data flows. From these they build a framework of four governance models defined by the interplay of security imperatives and competitiveness priorities. The conclusion stresses convergence: both states use territorially grounded regulation to control algorithms and data while projecting authority beyond their borders, China by insulating domestic assets, the United States by domesticating a foreign platform.
