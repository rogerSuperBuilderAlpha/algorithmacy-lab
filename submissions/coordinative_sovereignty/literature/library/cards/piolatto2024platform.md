## Summary
The paper models why the leading online travel groups hold both a revealing platform, which discloses the identity of the hotel (Booking.com, Expedia.com), and an anonymous one, which does not (Priceline, Hotwire), when the anonymous channel offers discounts and degrades matching. Two horizontally differentiated firms sell directly and through a platform that enlarges their pool of consumers, pays a two-part tariff, and operates under a narrow price-parity clause. The platform chooses whether to add an anonymous channel. The result is that adding it is always profitable, for two reasons: it implements price discrimination, as the opaque-selling literature had shown, and it improves rent extraction by breaking the link between the revealing-channel price and the firms' outside option of direct sales, while under linear pricing it also reduces double marginalisation. The anonymous channel always harms consumers, through worse matches and more surplus extracted; the welfare effect is ambiguous, since market expansion may offset information loss.
