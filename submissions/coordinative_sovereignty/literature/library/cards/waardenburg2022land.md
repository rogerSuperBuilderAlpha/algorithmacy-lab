## Summary
A 31-month ethnographic study of the Dutch police's implementation of a learning algorithm predicting crime incidents, offering one of the first empirical accounts of algorithmic brokers. Intelligence officers were tasked with brokering between a machine learning community and a user community by translating the algorithm's outputs to police management. As knowledge brokers they performed different translation practices over time and enacted increasingly influential brokerage roles: messenger, conveying predictions; interpreter, enriching predictions with contextual knowledge; and curator, replacing algorithmic outputs with their own informed judgments. Triggered by an impassable knowledge boundary yielded by the black-boxed machine learning, the brokers eventually acted like kings in the land of the blind and substituted the predictions with their own judgments, a shift fed by the absence of explanations for the system's predictions and the organization's growing trust in the brokers. The paper contributes a dynamic, power-attentive account of brokerage and translation in the age of learning algorithms.
