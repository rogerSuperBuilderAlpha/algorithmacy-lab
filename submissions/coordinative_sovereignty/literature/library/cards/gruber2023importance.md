## Summary
The article starts from the observation that using the Internet means encountering algorithmic processes that shape what a user sees, that people's algorithm skills vary widely, and that users build folk theories of these processes which guide their behaviour. What had been little studied is whether such skills let people use algorithms to their benefit and avoid harms. Drawing on 83 in-depth interviews from five countries about how people research and search for products and services online, the authors find that awareness of personal data collection helps people understand algorithmic processes, so data awareness is a route into algorithm awareness. But understanding does not translate into influence: the options platforms offer for controlling the degree of customisation are so limited that even skilled users cannot change algorithmic output much. The paper closes with research-design implications drawn from the diversity of its sample.
