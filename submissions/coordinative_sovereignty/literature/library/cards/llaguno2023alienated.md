## Summary
Llaguno argues that contemporary social and political philosophy has discussed dependence in two separate registers, care and vulnerability studies on one side and the critique of capitalism, including labour republicanism, on the other, and that neither captures the objective character of dependence under capitalist relations. Drawing on Marx's notion of objective dependence, the article distinguishes three forms it takes, the objectivity of exchange or money, of capital, and of machinery, and then, using Jaeggi's account of alienation as a relation of relationlessness, argues that what is wrong with modern dependence is not that it is objective but that it is alienated: we are the makers of our social relations and at the same time dominated by them and structurally prevented from changing them. The conclusion is that critique should target alienated objective dependence rather than dependence as such, that domination here has no agent behind it in the way republicans require, and that independence as presently experienced is itself a form of alienation, so that a free society would begin from dependence rather than deny it.
