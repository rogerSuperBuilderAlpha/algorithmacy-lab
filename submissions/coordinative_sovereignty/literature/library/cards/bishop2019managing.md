## Summary
Bishop studies how beauty vloggers, whose feminised output places them outside conventional spheres of technical expertise, nonetheless become skilled managers of algorithmic visibility. Drawing on an ethnography of beauty vloggers and industry stakeholders, she identifies the collaborative and directive processes through which algorithmic expertise is formulated and sustained, and names the result algorithmic gossip: communally and socially informed theories and strategies about recommender algorithms that are shared and acted upon to secure financial consistency and visibility on algorithmically structured platforms. The central finding is that gossip is productive. Talk within the community informs and supports concrete practices, such as uploading frequently and producing feminised beauty content, that are believed to perform well on YouTube. Bishop argues that taking gossip seriously offers a resource for understanding how algorithms work and have worked over time, and for seeing how perceptions of algorithms, accurate or not, shape what creators make.
