## Summary
Kenney, Bearson and Zysman measure how far online platforms have insinuated themselves into the economy and then examine what that pervasiveness means for power. The first part maps the presence of platforms as intermediating organizations across all US service industries at the six-digit NAICS level, finding that 70 per cent of service industries, representing over 5.2 million establishments, are potentially affected by one or more platforms. The second part is a firm-level case study of Amazon, showing how the macro pattern is expressed by a single mega-platform whose growth trajectory has carried it into, and transformed, existing industries and sectors. The authors conclude with limitations and a research agenda. The contribution is primarily descriptive and measurement-oriented: it gives a defensible figure for the share of establishments, overwhelmingly small, that now stand in a dependent relation to at least one platform.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited. The audit (U16) proposes it beside Cutolo and Kenney (2021) and P2B Recital 2 as support for the §8 claim that the repeal of ranking disclosure "hits hardest for small and medium-sized enterprises." The establishment count is the evidence that the population exposed to platform intermediation is largely made up of small firms; it does not itself show that smaller firms suffer more from opacity, which remains an inference (gap G1). Role: second source for SME exposure, to be cited for scale rather than for differential harm.
