## Summary
The paper evaluates the best price clauses that online travel agents imposed on hotels, using meta-search price data on nearly 30,000 hotels across several countries, and exploits differences in the clauses across agencies and the abolition of Booking.com's narrow clause in some countries. The authors find that the clauses shape both the pricing and the availability of rooms across online channels. Where Booking.com does not apply a narrow clause, hotels list their offers on the agency more often and promote their own direct online channel more actively. The abolition of the narrow clause is associated with the direct channel of chain hotels more often carrying the strictly lowest price. The results indicate that parity clauses did restrain hotels from undercutting the agency on their own sites, and that removing them shifted relative prices toward the direct channel, at least for hotels with the organisational capacity to manage channels.
