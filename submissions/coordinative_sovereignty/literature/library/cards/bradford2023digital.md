## Summary
Bradford's book, written from the publisher description opened this session, describes an intensifying contest among three digital powers, the United States, China and the European Union, each advancing a competing model for governing the digital economy and each trying to extend its model's reach. The American model is market-driven and built around free expression and innovation; the Chinese model is state-driven and built around control and surveillance; the European model is rights-driven and built around the protection of fundamental rights and democracy through regulation. The argument develops the author's earlier "Brussels effect" thesis into a comparative account of regulatory export, examining how the three models clash on data, content, competition and AI, how companies navigate the conflicts, and how the contest shapes the options of other states. The summary here is limited to what the catalogue page shows; the chapter's specific claim about the degree of European control should be checked against the book's chapters on the European model before the superlative is retained.
