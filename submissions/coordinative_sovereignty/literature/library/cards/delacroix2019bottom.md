## Summary
Delacroix and Lawrence propose data trusts as a legal mechanism through which individuals can pool the rights they hold over their personal data and have them exercised collectively by a trustee bound by fiduciary duties. Their starting point is that neither regulatory constraint of the GDPR kind nor individual data ownership returns to individuals the power that stems from aggregated data. Rather than a single top-down regime, they envisage an ecosystem of bottom-up trusts, each embodying different values and risk appetites, among which individuals could choose, so that data governance ceases to be "one size fits all". The article is doctrinal and institutional in method, drawing on English trust law and on the economics of data aggregation. Its central claim is that a trust gives a collective the bargaining position no individual data subject has while leaving the data-using organisations in place and changing the terms on which they obtain their input.
