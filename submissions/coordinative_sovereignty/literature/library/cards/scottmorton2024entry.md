## Summary
Scott Morton analyses how the EU Digital Markets Act could open the two mobile app stores to entry and competition. The Act obliges Apple and Google to share the technical interface specifications that developers and rival stores need, to permit alternative stores on the handset, and to allow developers to bypass the legacy stores altogether through web downloads to the device. She anticipates price competition that erodes the prevailing 30 percent commission, together with competition in features and variety: specialised stores for children, for sustainability-minded users, or for public services, and entrants such as Epic offering alternative payment models. The paper treats privacy and security as the central justification gatekeepers will raise and argues that Commission enforcement must balance genuine protection against dangerous services with the contestability the Act is meant to create. It is a policy working paper in the economics of platform competition rather than an empirical study.
