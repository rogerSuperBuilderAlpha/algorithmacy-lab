## Summary
Pettit asks what ideal the international order should aim at, assuming that states remain a permanent feature of the world. His answer extends the republican ideal of freedom as non-domination from individuals to peoples. Peoples already organized under non-dominating, representative states should pursue a twin goal. First, they should arrange matters so that each enjoys non-domination in relation to every other people and to the multinational and international agencies that act upon them. Second, they should do what is possible and productive to help less fortunate peoples achieve representation in non-dominating states of their own and to bring them into a non-dominating international order. Pettit presents the position as standing midway between a utopian cosmopolitan ideal of global justice and a sceptical ideal of non-intervention, and he gives effective, representative states a special role as the agents through which peoples act. The paper is the clearest statement that the corporate body to which Pettit extends non-domination is a people organized as a state, not an arbitrary collective.
