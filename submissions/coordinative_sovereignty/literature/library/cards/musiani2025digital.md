## Summary
This perspective piece takes stock of research conducted under the lens Musiani earlier named infrastructuring digital sovereignty, grounded in science and technology studies and infrastructure studies. The aim is to move digital sovereignty beyond its strictly legal interpretations and its rhetorical uses in political discourse and to understand it from within, as an ongoing series of negotiations, practices, struggles and controversies embedded in infrastructures and in their creation, development and maintenance. Musiani reports on two recently started projects, one French and one global and interdisciplinary, that seek an empirical and theoretical account of digital sovereignty as a set of processes and co-constructed techniques, practices and norms in the making. The contribution is programmatic rather than empirical: it restates the 2022 agenda, identifies where the infrastructural reading has travelled, and argues that self-determination in digital environments is enacted through the concrete arrangements of infrastructures rather than declared by states or granted by regulation.
