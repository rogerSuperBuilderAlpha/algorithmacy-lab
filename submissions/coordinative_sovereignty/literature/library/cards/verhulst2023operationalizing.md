## Summary
Verhulst argues that datafication, the transformation of life into data for private or public benefit, offers real public value through open data and data collaboratives but is constrained by three asymmetries that fall hardest on vulnerable and marginalised groups: data asymmetries, information asymmetries, and agency asymmetries. These limit human potential practically and psychologically, produce disempowerment, and erode trust in technology. Existing remedies, open data and consent, and alternatives under discussion, data ownership, collective ownership, and personal information management systems, are each found wanting. He therefore proposes a principle and practice of digital self-determination (DSD), sketches its characteristics, and concedes that study and practice of DSD are in their infancy. The paper is a conceptual and agenda-setting piece, and it closes by calling for a research framework to examine how DSD can address the asymmetries and inequalities in data and in society that it treats as the key public-policy challenge of the era.
