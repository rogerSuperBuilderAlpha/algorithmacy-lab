## Summary
The study develops and validates the first scale of algorithmic competency (AC) for on-demand labour platform workers, defined as their understanding of the platform algorithms that assign and evaluate their work and their ability to adapt to and navigate those algorithms. Drawing on sociotechnical systems theory, digital competence and algorithm literacy, and on interviews, the authors generate 14 items and refine them to 12 across five samples of Chinese ride-hailing and food-delivery workers (DiDi, Ele.me, Meituan), using exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis. AC has four dimensions: understanding, embracing, leveraging and remediating algorithmic management. Peer social support and cognitive job crafting predict AC, and AC explains variance in customer-oriented service behaviour and identification with gig work beyond related constructs. The authors distinguish AC from algoactivism: where resistance aims to circumvent control, AC is agency exercised to align the system with the worker's interests. They note that China's distinctive on-demand economy limits generalisability.
