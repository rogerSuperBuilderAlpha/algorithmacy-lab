## Summary
Rouvroy and Berns name algorithmic governmentality: a mode of government that works through the statistical treatment of massive data rather than through norms addressed to subjects. They describe it in three stages, the collection of data stripped of context, the mining of that data for correlations that reconstruct singular cases without reference to any general norm, and the anticipatory action on conduct through profiles. The decisive feature is that this government produces no subjectivation: it bypasses reflexive human subjects and feeds on infra-individual data that are insignificant in themselves, then acts on environments and possibilities before any subject can take a position. The emancipatory question they pose concerns the disparate, the irreducible difference between beings, which statistical governance flattens. They argue that individuation through relation, in Simondon's sense, depends on preserving that disparate, and that resistance to algorithmic governmentality must be sought in what escapes the profile rather than in rights claims addressed to it.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter; strand 5 ranks it SHOULD for §3.2 and §4. In §3.2 it supplies the strongest statement of why disclosure cannot reach algorithmic coordination: if the system bypasses subjects and acts on correlations, there is no norm to disclose. In §4 it is the counter-case to algorithmacy, since Rouvroy and Berns doubt that a subject can form a working theory of a system that does not address it. Role: support for the opacity diagnosis, with a challenge to the competence remedy that the chapter should answer.

## Key quotations
"Le datamining, articulé à des finalités de profilage, reconstruit, suivant une logique de corrélation, les cas singuliers émiettés par les codages, sans pour autant les rapporter à aucune « norme » générale" (p. 165)

"La gouvernementalité algorithmique ne produit aucune subjectivation, elle contourne et évite les sujets humains réflexifs, elle se nourrit de données infra-individuelles insignifiantes en elles-mêmes" (p. 173)
