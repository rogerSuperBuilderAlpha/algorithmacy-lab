## Summary
Gawer's essay asks what the digital revolution has done to organization and management. Her claim is that digital innovation allows value to be created and captured in fundamentally new ways, and that the platform enterprise together with its surrounding ecosystem has become the defining organizational form of the digital age. She reviews the competitive and innovative consequences of that shift and identifies a central tension: the distributed creation of value by many complementors and users is what allowed platform firms to emerge, yet the models those firms adopted concentrate the capture of value in the platform owner. That concentration, she argues, is the source of public concern about market dominance, privacy and labor practices. The essay ends by calling for research on ecosystem governance and on the accountability of platform organizations, treating them as new social institutions rather than only as business models.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. Strand 5 lists it as a MUST for §3 and §5.4. The gaps report identifies §3 as the most exposed section because three literatures (meta-organization, partial organization, evaluative infrastructures) already name platforms as a form beyond market, hierarchy and network, while the chapter presents co-optation as if Stark were the only predecessor. Gawer is the most direct statement that platforms and ecosystems are the dominant organizational form, and her distributed-creation, concentrated-capture tension is the organizational-theory version of the chapter's interested mediator. Role: support, and a predecessor to be acknowledged in §3 before the chapter claims novelty; in §5.4 her ecosystem framing sits beside Jacobides et al. 2018 and Baldwin on where a platform's functions come apart.
