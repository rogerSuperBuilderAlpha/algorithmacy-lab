## Summary
van Angeren, Miric and Özalp study how control over strategic bottlenecks in platform ecosystems shifts over time and what that means for regulation. In an ecosystem, the platform owner but also other actors can gain control over bottlenecks that govern how others create and capture value, and such control brings the ability to exert market power. The authors examine the evolution of the console gaming industry from 1990 onward, tracing shifts in bottlenecks, in ecosystem interdependencies and in strategic acquisition patterns. Over that period the bottleneck moves from the platforms to distributors, back to the platforms, and then onto tool developers (game engines), with M&A activity following the bottleneck. The contribution is an evolutionary framework for thinking about the migration of bottlenecks and interdependencies within ecosystems, with the regulatory implication that rules which fix on the platform owner as the locus of power may miss where the bottleneck has moved.
