## Summary
This practitioner article translates the then-new economics of two-sided networks into strategy advice. Platforms serving two distinct user groups whose value depends on each other's participation enjoy returns to scale that conventional value chains do not, yet many firms fail at building them because they manage them as ordinary products. The authors set out three problems. Pricing: which side to subsidize and which to charge, depending on each side's price sensitivity, quality sensitivity and multi-homing behaviour. Winner-take-all dynamics: whether a market will tip to one platform, which turns on multi-homing costs, network effect strength and user preferences for differentiation, and whether to share or fight for the platform. Envelopment: the threat that an adjacent platform bundles one's functionality into its own. The contribution for scholarship is its accessible statement that the feasibility of users holding more than one platform at once governs whether a platform's position is contestable.
