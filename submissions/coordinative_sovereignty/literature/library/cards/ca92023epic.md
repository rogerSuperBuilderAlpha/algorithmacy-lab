## Summary
The Ninth Circuit's April 24, 2023 decision resolved cross-appeals from Epic's antitrust challenge to Apple's App Store rules. The court affirmed the rejection of Epic's Sherman Act claims against Apple's app-distribution restrictions and its in-app payment requirement: although the district court erred in parts of its market-definition and contract analysis, the errors were harmless because Epic failed to establish viable less restrictive alternatives under the rule of reason. On the state-law side, the court affirmed the district court's judgment for Epic under California's Unfair Competition Law and upheld the injunction against Apple's anti-steering provision, the rule barring developers from directing users to purchasing options outside Apple's in-app payment system. The decision thus separates the lawful architecture of the store from the unlawful prohibition on telling users about a cheaper direct path, leaving the payment rail intact while opening the informational edge between developer and user.
