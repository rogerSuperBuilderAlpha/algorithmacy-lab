## Summary
Robinson, Jarrahi, Keegan and Meijerink study how digital labour platforms apply algorithmic management (AM) to HR activities and how those systems embed both market and hierarchy principles in shaping worker control and autonomy. Prior work frames AM in binary terms, matching and autonomy on the market side against control and hierarchy on the other. Using an inductive qualitative design, the authors analyse 33 semi-structured interviews with platform workers and documentary data from 23 platforms, and they find that platforms dynamically calibrate market and hierarchy approaches in response to task characteristics such as complexity, urgency, autonomy and interdependence, balancing flexibility, oversight, discretion and incentives. Matching can carry hierarchical features when platforms decide who is visible, and control can carry market features when exercised through ratings and incentives. The "limbo" of the title is the strategic territory between market and hierarchy that platforms occupy, claiming to be markets while controlling workers through algorithms that enforce standards hierarchically.

## Use in the chapter
§3 cites it for the claim that algorithmic management interweaves three logics: "algorithmic pricing that clears supply and demand, algorithmic command that assigns and deactivates, algorithmic reputation that scores and ranks, interwoven task by task within a single platform; Robinson et al., 2026." The audit (S3) notes that the article covers market and hierarchy only and does not theorize reputation or evaluation as a third logic; it recommends adding Vallas and Schor (2020) or Rahman (2021) for algorithmic reputation. Role: support for the task-by-task interweaving of market and hierarchy; the three-logic formulation is the chapter's and should not be attributed to Robinson et al. alone.
