## Summary
Andersson Schwarz argues that digital platforms are not merely software media but governing systems that control, interact and accumulate, and that they solidify markets, understood as social networks of exchange that leave few data traces, into infrastructure, understood as material arrangements of traceable activity. The article is conceptual. It first sets out a schematic overview of digital infrastructures of governance and the systemic mechanics they produce, then argues that the shifting emphases of different disciplines are only partly normative and are largely the product of different disciplinary lenses: information systems and design research attends to direct, technical inter-platform affordances, while critical political economy attends to transnational, geopolitical formations of platform capitalism. The interplay among these mechanics is summarized in the concept of "platform logic," which the author offers to correct what he calls simplistic theoretical conclusions about platforms and to describe the forms of domination the platform model produces.
