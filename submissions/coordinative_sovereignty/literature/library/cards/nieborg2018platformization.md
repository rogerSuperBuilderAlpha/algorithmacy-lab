## Summary
Nieborg and Poell define platformization as the penetration of the economic and infrastructural extensions of online platforms into the web and, through it, into the cultural industries. Combining business studies, political economy and software studies, and using news and games as their sectors, they argue that platformization moves cultural production from two-sided market structures toward complex multisided configurations controlled by a few platform corporations. Producers must adapt continually to changes in platform governance that affect curation and pricing, while new platform services also open opportunities. The central theoretical claim is that the cultural commodity becomes contingent: modular in structure, and continuously reworked in response to datafied audience metrics and platform decisions. The article is conceptual with sectoral illustration and has become the anchor for the platformization literature in media studies.
