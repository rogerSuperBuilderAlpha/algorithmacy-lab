## Summary
Jacobides, Cennamo and Gawer offer a theoretical account of why platforms and ecosystems have become ubiquitous, by asking what value they add. They treat the two constructs as related structures through which constellations of economic actors engage as they seek to create and capture value, and they propose that each emerges as a response to distinct market failures, identifying the specific externalities that platforms and ecosystems help overcome (coordination among complementors, demand-side network effects, and the like). The article's distinctive move is to identify the post hoc, endogenous failures that these structures in turn generate: functional failures, where the orchestrator's design no longer serves the system, and distributional failures, where value created collectively is captured by the orchestrator at the expense of participants. The conceptual contribution is a symmetrical framing in which the same features that solve one set of externalities produce a second set, with implications for strategy, governance and regulation.
