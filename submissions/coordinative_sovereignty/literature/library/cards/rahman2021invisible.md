## Summary
Rahman studies how freelance workers on a labour platform contend with an opaque third-party evaluation algorithm, and with what consequences. Prior research showed that people experience third-party evaluations as control because they align their behaviour with the criteria to win resources and recognition, but that work concerned evaluations with transparent criteria. Drawing on more than three years of interviews, archival data and observation as a registered platform user, Rahman finds that the platform introduced an opaque rating algorithm to differentiate meaningfully among freelancers' scores. Workers experienced it as control yet could not align their actions with criteria they could not identify. They responded with two kinds of reactivity: experimenting with tactics to raise their scores, such as varying project types and contract lengths, or withdrawing to preserve their scores by limiting engagement. Which response they chose depended on how much they relied on the platform for income and whether their scores had fallen. Rahman names the resulting condition an invisible cage, a form of control in which the criteria for success, and changes to them, are unpredictable.
