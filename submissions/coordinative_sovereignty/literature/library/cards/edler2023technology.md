## Summary
The article responds to the intensification of technology-based competition between states and its entanglement with broader political and value-system conflict, which has unsettled the post-Cold War assumption that international technological interdependence is mutually beneficial. Against that backdrop the authors set out a concept of technology sovereignty suited to innovation policy. Their definition treats sovereignty as state-level agency within an international system, a capacity for governmental action, rather than as territorial control or self-sufficiency. Technology sovereignty so understood should serve two innovation-policy ends, sustaining national competitiveness and building the capacities needed for transformative policy. The paper positions the concept between naive globalism and autarkic isolation, acknowledging that collaboration carries risks and that some interdependencies are cooperative and worth keeping, and closes with policy suggestions derived from the framework. It is conceptual, drawing on the innovation-policy literature rather than on new data.
