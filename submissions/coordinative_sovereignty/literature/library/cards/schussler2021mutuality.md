## Summary
This is the introduction to a Socio-Economic Review special issue, and it advances a view of digital platforms as dynamic and relational rather than as a fixed organizational type. The authors propose that an archetypal transaction platform is made of three canonical social relationships that exist in tension: mutuality, the sharing and reciprocity of the early "sharing economy"; autonomy, the freedom and independence that draw many earners to platforms; and domination, the exercise of power and control that drives many platform owners and managers. All three are present in varying degrees on every platform. By conceptualizing platforms as contested relational structures, they aim to connect prior attempts to classify what platforms are with the diverse empirical studies of what platforms do in particular contexts, and they conclude that platforms can do different things at the same time because they are different things at the same time. The piece is conceptual and frames the empirical papers that follow it.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited, though a legacy entry exists; gap C6 rates it MUST for §3 and §6, found independently by strands 2 and 5. In §3 the chapter reads the platform through Simmel's tertius gaudens as the third who profits from standing between two others and "runs it as a system". Schüßler et al. contest that single reading: domination is one of three relational registers, and participant agency (autonomy, mutuality) is built into the structure rather than opposed to it. The role is position against. The chapter should state that coordinative sovereignty is a claim about standing inside the domination register, and that the other two registers are where the "felt empowerment" §6 warns against tends to live. The paper also links to Schor et al. (2020) on dependence as the variable that conditions autonomy.
