## Summary
The report studies whether listing a hotel on a third-party online travel agent (OTA) raises bookings the hotel receives through its own channels rather than through the OTA itself, an effect the author names the billboard effect. The setting is a set of JHM Hotels properties listed on Expedia, and the design compares reservation volume through non-OTA channels in periods with and without an Expedia listing. The central finding is that the listing produces a measurable increase in direct bookings, estimated at 7.5 to 26 percent of incremental reservations across the four properties examined. The mechanism is exposure: travelers discover a property on the agency's site and then book directly, so the agency works as an advertising surface as well as a sales channel. The report is an industry research note rather than a peer-reviewed article, which bears on how much weight the estimate can carry; later work cited in the chapter has not reproduced the effect consistently.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §5.5 in the Booking.com case: "C. K. Anderson (2009, 2011) named the first of these the billboard effect." The chapter uses it to establish that an agency performs a necessary demand-aggregation function for hotels, separate from the contingent rate-parity function, which is the premise of the portfolio argument in §5.4. It is support, with the chapter already noting that Beritelli and Schegg (2016) did not reproduce the effect; the July bib's doubt about the 7.5 to 26 percent range is resolved by the repository record.
