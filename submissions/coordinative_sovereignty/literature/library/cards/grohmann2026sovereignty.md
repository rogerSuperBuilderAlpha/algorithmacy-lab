## Summary
The article introduces "sovereignty-as-a-service" to name the way Microsoft, Amazon and Google/Alphabet have redefined digital sovereignty through their sovereign-cloud programmes. Using critical discourse analysis of official corporate materials released in 2022 and 2023, the authors show how the firms answer regulatory pressure, especially in Europe, by offering modular products that frame sovereignty as a technical, legal and infrastructural property that can be configured and bought. The inversion is the point: sovereignty is no longer something exercised over platforms by states or publics but something provisioned by platforms on corporate terms. The authors read this as discursive capture that empties the concept while remaining consistent with the Californian Ideology, turning a political claim into a purchasable, proprietary service. The piece is short, in the journal's crosscurrents format, and its contribution is conceptual rather than empirical beyond the discourse sample.
