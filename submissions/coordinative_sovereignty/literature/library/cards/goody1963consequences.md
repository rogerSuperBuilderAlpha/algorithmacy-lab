## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the current draft; the chapter anchors §2.1 in Goody (1977) and Ong (1982). Strand 5 marks this article MUST for §2.1 and §2.2 because it is where the claim that alphabetic literacy in Greece underwrote a critical, democratic public originates, which is the claim the chapter's §2.2 ("citizens govern themselves to the degree they can read what institutions write") restates for the modern state. The role is support, but it is also the article the critics target: the audit (G8) notes that the Goody and Ong "great divide" is contested by Street (1984), Finnegan (1988) and Halverson (1992), and that §4 concedes this for cognition while §2 does not. Citing Goody and Watt in §2 therefore obliges the chapter to add the one closing sentence the audit asks for. The audit (O6) also records that the term "oracy" is Wilkinson's, not Goody's.
