## Summary
The summary publishes the Commission's first non-compliance decision under the Digital Markets Act. It finds that Apple, designated as gatekeeper for its App Store online intermediation service, breached Article 5(4), which obliges a gatekeeper to allow business users, free of charge, to communicate and promote offers to end users acquired through the core platform service and to conclude contracts with them. Apple imposed technical restrictions on developers' ability to steer users to offers outside the App Store and charged a recurring commission of 17% or 10% on transactions concluded within seven days of a link-out. The Commission imposed a fine of EUR 500 million and ordered Apple to bring the infringement to an end within 60 calendar days by allowing developers to steer and conclude contracts without restriction and free of charge, with periodic penalty payments of up to 5% of average daily worldwide turnover for continued non-compliance. The decision is dated 23 April 2025 and the summary appeared in the Official Journal on 16 June 2025.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §8: "the European Commission fined Apple €500 million in April 2025" for restricting developer steering (European Commission, 2025b), with the appeal noted as Case T-438/25. The chapter uses it to show that enforcement has reached platform conduct without reaching the ranking layer, the first strand of high digital sovereignty beside low coordinative sovereignty. Audit §7.7 asks for the missing space before "(OJ" and suggests the full OJ title and the 16 June 2025 date. Role: legal instrument.

## Key quotations
Article 5(4) requires the gatekeeper to "allow business users, free of charge, to communicate and promote offers ... to end users acquired via its CPS ... and to conclude contracts with those end users." (Summary, as quoted in the OJ text)
