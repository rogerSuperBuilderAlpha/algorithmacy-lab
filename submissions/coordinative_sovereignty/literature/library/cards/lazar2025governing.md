## Summary
Lazar argues that political philosophy must update its picture of social relations for the information age. The "Algorithmic City" is the network of algorithmically mediated social relations made possible by computing, the internet and machine learning; its characteristic agents are algorithmic intermediaries, go-betweens that are dynamic and adaptive, able to monitor the relations they mediate and intervene in real time, which is what lets them act at such speed and scale. Lazar distinguishes three dimensions of their power: power over mediatees, exercised by those who design and deploy the systems; power between mediatees, whose mutual relations the intermediary reshapes; and, over time, power to reshape broader social structures. He then brings the tools of analytic political philosophy to bear on justifying governing power, distinguishing proper authority, procedural legitimacy and substantive justification, and argues that algorithmic governance differs from governance by law in ways that may make it harder to justify. The stakes are assessed by degree, scope and concentration; scope is especially wide, since intermediaries govern domains the state leaves alone. He is sceptical of projects to "perfect" governance by algorithm and of alignment programmes that pursue substantive justification while ignoring legitimacy and authority.

The gaps report lists it first (C1) among works that collide with the chapter's core claims: same object, algorithmic intermediaries governing mediated social relations, framed as the legitimacy and authority of "intermediary power". Role: construct to position against, in §1 and in one paragraph of §6. The relation to state: Lazar asks what would legitimate the intermediary's governing power; coordinative sovereignty asks what standing the coordinated actor holds regardless of whether the intermediary is legitimate. The two are complementary, and the chapter can say so, but a referee in political philosophy will expect the contrast to be explicit. Lazar's "power between mediatees" also maps onto the chapter's triad and could be cited in §3.1.
