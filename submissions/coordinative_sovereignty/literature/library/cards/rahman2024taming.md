## Summary
Rahman, Karunakaran and Cameron review research on multisided platforms that emphasises how platform owners accumulate power over producers and customers and argues that such power needs to be balanced by accountability. They organise the literature into two perspectives. The bottom-up, emergent perspective studies collective action by lower-powered actors, gig workers and app developers among them, to improve rule adequacy and push back against owners, and its overarching concern is procedural fairness. The top-down, institutional perspective emphasises preventing extractive opportunism and keeping a level playing field through legal, regulatory and governance change, and its concern is largely distributive fairness. Both have limits, especially given the informational asymmetries among platform actors. Synthesising across literatures, the authors propose an accountability framework that accounts for both procedural and distributive fairness, on the premise that multisided platforms require accountability systems, and they set out an approach to enforcing platform accountability that could rebalance power between high- and low-powered actors.
