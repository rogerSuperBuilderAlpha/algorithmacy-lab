## Summary
The study questions whether hotels should over-optimize their distribution channel mix by picking the single best-performing channels. Its premise is that travelers do not encounter channels in isolation; they navigate a network of information presented in one networked environment, so the channels a hotel occupies interact rather than compete cleanly. Using multiple regressions on samples of Swiss hotels drawn from surveys run between 2009 and 2012, the authors estimate the drivers of online booking volume. The central result is that the estimated number of online bookings depends on the number of channels a hotel occupies rather than on which channels they are: breadth of presence, not selection of a premium channel, predicts bookings. The managerial recommendation is to maximize share of shelf and build on interdependencies and network effects across channels, instead of concentrating on any one intermediary or treating a single listing as the source of direct-booking lift.
