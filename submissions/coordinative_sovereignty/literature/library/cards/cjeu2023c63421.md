## Summary
The Grand Chamber judgment of 7 December 2023 answers a reference from the Wiesbaden Administrative Court about credit scoring by SCHUFA, a German credit reference agency. The Court holds that the automated establishment by a credit information agency of a probability value about a person's future ability to meet payment commitments is itself "automated individual decision-making" within Article 22(1) GDPR, where a third party to whom the score is transmitted draws strongly on it to establish, implement or terminate a contract with that person. The reasoning, in paragraphs around 73, is that a narrower reading would leave a gap in protection: the agency that produces the score would escape Article 22 because it makes no decision, and the bank that decides would escape because the decisive step happened elsewhere. The ruling therefore moves the threshold of Article 22 upstream to the scoring step, expanding the reach of the prohibition and the attached rights to human intervention, expression of a view and contestation.

## Use in the chapter
§3.2 says of Article 22 GDPR that "Its threshold is high, its practice thin, and its protection stops at the natural person". The audit (U10) flags "threshold is high" as unsourced and outdated after this judgment and Dun & Bradstreet Austria (C-203/22, 27 February 2025), and proposes rewording to "its threshold is contested and its enforcement record thin", citing both judgments with Binns and Veale (2021) for the difficulty of the threshold. Role: legal instrument; it supports the chapter's point that Article 22 is case-level standing in hard law while correcting the claim about its narrowness. The §7 repetition of the Article 22 description should be aligned with the same wording.

## Key quotations
"Article 22(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ... must be interpreted as meaning that the automated establishment, by a credit information agency, of a probability value based on personal data relating to a person and concerning his or her ability to meet payment commitments in the future constitutes 'automated individual decision-making' within the meaning of that provision, where a third party, to which that probability value is transmitted, draws strongly on that probability value to establish, implement or terminate a contractual relationship with that person." (Operative part, point 1)
