## Summary
Aloisi argues that algorithmic management has extended employer monitoring and control far beyond what the standard employment relationship anticipated, and that the traditional protections of labour law have been hollowed out by data-driven managerial technologies. The article is doctrinal and EU-focused. It works through how two bodies of EU law, data protection and non-discrimination, can be combined as substantive and procedural safeguards that rebalance the informational asymmetry between employers and workers and render automated decisions transparent and contestable. The distinctive move is the third pillar announced in the title: collective rights. Because data are relational, Aloisi argues, individual rights cannot on their own restrain algorithmic misuse; workers' representatives must be involved in designing digital HR policies, and organized groups are the actors best placed to challenge improper applications. The central result is a layered regulatory model in which individual transparency rights are backstopped by collective participation in the governance of the systems themselves.
