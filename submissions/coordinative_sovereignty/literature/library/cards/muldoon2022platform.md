## Summary
Muldoon argues that whoever controls the platforms controls the future, and sets out a programme he calls platform socialism: the organisation of the digital economy through social ownership of digital assets and democratic control over the infrastructure and systems that govern digital life. The book diagnoses how large technology firms have taken control of digital infrastructure and developed techniques for extracting value from users, and argues that private control of that infrastructure lets companies shape the structural conditions of politics. Against both corporate control and state monopoly, Muldoon draws on guild-socialist traditions and on contemporary experiments in platform cooperativism and new municipalism to propose democratically governed platforms at the scale appropriate to each service, local for ride-hailing and delivery, necessarily larger and federated for global social networks. The book is a political manifesto with historical and case material rather than an empirical study, and it closes with a roadmap for transition.
