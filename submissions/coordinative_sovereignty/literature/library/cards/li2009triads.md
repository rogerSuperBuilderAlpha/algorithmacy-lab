## Summary
Li and Choi apply structural-hole reasoning to the triad that every outsourcing arrangement involves: the buyer, its supplier and the buyer's customer. In manufacturing outsourcing the buyer remains a bridge between supplier and customer before, during and after the arrangement, because the product passes through it. In services outsourcing the structure changes, since the supplier delivers the service directly to the buyer's customer. Before outsourcing the buyer is the bridge; during implementation the bridge begins to decay as the customer comes into direct contact with the supplier; after implementation the bridge is in effect transferred to the supplier, which now enjoys the leverage that comes with occupying the bridging position. The authors argue that an unmanaged bridge transfer has serious performance implications for the buyer and that the buyer should continue to interact actively with and monitor the supplier so that the supplier cannot solidify its position. The paper is conceptual, building propositions from social network theory for supply-chain management.
