## Summary
Grabher and König argue that digital platforms disrupt not only industries but the scholarly narratives about where capitalism is heading, which tend to split between reading platforms as the ultimate marketisation of social life and reading them as seeds of a post-capitalist alternative. To get past that binary they adopt Karl Polanyi's framework of embeddedness and the double movement. They identify three drivers of platform emergence: a technological driver (cloud computing, big data and algorithms), a scientific driver (network theories that do not merely describe but actively construct the social reality of platform markets), and a regulatory driver (the state, which enables and constrains platform growth). The argument is that platforms, like the historical markets Polanyi studied, are socially constructed rather than natural, and that their eventual shape will be settled by political contestation over how platform economies are embedded in societal and regulatory structures rather than by the technology alone. The piece is theoretical, written for an economic-sociology audience.
