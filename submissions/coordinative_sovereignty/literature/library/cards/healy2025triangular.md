## Summary
Healy and Pekarek argue that platform gig work is created and contested in a triangular relationship among platforms, workers, and consumers, and that evidence on the third corner is thin. Using two Australian public opinion surveys run five years apart, in 2017 and 2022, they track how consumers' views of gig work shifted across a period that included the first two years of the pandemic. Platform use rose markedly, yet many consumers felt conflicted about gig workers' conditions and about typical platform labour practices. The authors find an enduring tension in consumer views between acceptance that platforms benefit workers to some extent and misgivings about workers' vulnerability to harm. Centring consumers, they argue, enriches triangular conceptions of platform labour relations by showing how consumers mediate the interests of platforms and workers, shaping how gig work manifests and who benefits. They also extract practical themes for campaigns to improve gig workers' rights.
