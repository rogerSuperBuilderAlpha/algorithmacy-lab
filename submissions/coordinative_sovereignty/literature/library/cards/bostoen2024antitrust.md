## Summary
Bostoen and Petit examine the "treacherous turn," in which a platform sets advantageous terms to attract demand and reverses them once demand has become sticky, a strategy the platform literature labels "open early, closed late." They ground the harm in transaction cost economics: opportunism is profitable where relation-specific investments lock users in, and such investments are the norm in the platform economy. The article is doctrinal. It shows that the micro-rules of antitrust liability (excessive pricing, refusal to supply, disruption of supply), together with contract law and sectoral regulation, do not adequately capture the strategy in either the EU or the United States. The authors propose a narrow quasi-per se rule: where a platform with market power in its prime market takes a treacherous turn that adversely affects trading partners or potential competitors and could not reasonably be expected, the turn is presumptively unlawful unless the platform advances a plausible business justification, and the remedy freezes the previous terms in place for existing users.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited. Two strands (3 and 4) and the gaps file propose it for §5.2 and §5.3, and strand 4 lists it under "contests." The chapter's bypass test treats contingency and necessity as a property of a mediator at a point in time, yet Bostoen and Petit show that platforms manipulate that contingency over time, opening direct ties early and closing them late once investments are sunk; the gaps file concludes that "the bypass test needs a temporal clause." It also bears on §3.2, since the reversal of terms is exactly the kind of change that disclosure duties record after the fact but do not prevent. Role: contests the static form of the bypass test; its proposed freeze remedy is a candidate row for the §7 table of channels.

## Key quotations
"Digital platforms can take a 'treacherous turn', where they initially set advantageous terms and conditions only to reverse them once demand has become sticky." (Abstract)
"Treacherous turns are innocuous in the absence of market power. Users subject to a treacherous turn can leave for competing platforms. The strategy is self-defeating. By contrast, when a user is locked into a platform (due to relation-specific investments or because the market has tipped), a treacherous turn becomes a profit-maximising strategy." (Conclusion)
"If the platform is not able to develop a credible justification, a remedy can be imposed to freeze the previous conditions of supply. The platform remains free to apply a different policy to new users." (Conclusion)
