## Summary
The study treats a platform's recommendation algorithm as an orchestration mechanism that steers complementors at scale. Using two quasi-natural experiments on a food-sharing platform that upgraded its recommender twice, from a location-based baseline to a popularity-based algorithm (PopRec) and then to a personalization-based one (PersRec), the authors analyse more than 1.7 million observations of complementor behaviour and revenue. PopRec led complementors to concentrate on a few offerings; PersRec encouraged new product introduction; each shift came at the expense of the other. On distribution, PopRec cut the revenues of prior superstars and raised those of long-tail complementors, improving equality as measured by Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients, while PersRec widened inequality by benefiting superstars disproportionately. The authors frame visibility as an algorithmic reward for which complementors compete, extending Rietveld et al.'s selective promotion from managerial to algorithmic decision, and close on the need for algorithmic accountability in platform orchestration.
