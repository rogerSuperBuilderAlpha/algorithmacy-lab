## Summary
Bonini and Treré argue against the picture of algorithmic systems as monolithic forces acting on passive users. Drawing on a multilingual digital ethnography spanning Italy, Mexico, China and other sites, they show people negotiating, gaming and repurposing platform algorithms in three domains: delivery couriers working around dispatch and rating systems, cultural workers competing for visibility on social media, and activists using algorithmic tactics for political ends. The organizing concept is algorithmic agency, defined as the reflexive capacity to influence algorithmic outcomes, which they split into tactical agency (improvised moves by resource-poor users) and strategic agency (moves from positions of institutional or economic power). Resistance is everyday and often individual rather than organized, and it coexists with dependence on the platforms resisted. The book is open access and has been reviewed in IJoC, ICS and NTWE (Lata 2025).

## Use in the chapter
Strands 1 and 7 list it as a MUST for §4 and §6 as a construct to position against. "Algorithmic agency" is a second-tier collision with the chapter's term: it names the same observed competence the chapter calls algorithmacy but treats it as resistance rather than as a competence that can be held without standing. The chapter's §6 argument that competence without accountability leaves domination intact is the point of contrast, and Magalhães 2022 (resistance as disengagement) is the critic to cite beside it.
