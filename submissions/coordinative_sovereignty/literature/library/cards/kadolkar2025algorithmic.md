## Summary
Kadolkar, Kepes, and Subramony systematically review the cross-disciplinary literature on algorithmic management of gig workers, which they find fragmented in its conceptualisation and measurement of the construct and lacking consensus on which dimensions drive worker outcomes, through which mechanisms, and under what boundary conditions. Using natural-language-processing topic modelling across management, computer science, sociology, law, and operations research, they extract twelve topics and integrate them into an input-process-output framework. On that basis they offer a comprehensive definition of algorithmic management as a multidimensional system through which platforms automate task assignment, performance evaluation, and behavioural regulation, specify its key dimensions, and trace the main mediating pathways from each dimension to outcomes such as well-being, performance, and resistance. The paper closes with a research roadmap from an organisational-behaviour perspective. It serves as the consolidating review of the field as of 2024.
