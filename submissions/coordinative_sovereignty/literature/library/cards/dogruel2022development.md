## Summary
The article develops and validates a self-report instrument for algorithm literacy among internet users, motivated by the spread of algorithmic decision-making and the argument that such literacy is needed to navigate digital environments. The instrument has two related components: awareness of algorithm use, that is, knowing that and where algorithms operate, and knowledge about algorithms, that is, understanding what inputs they take and how they select, prioritize and present content. Validation used item response theory in two phases: 331 participants evaluated 46 items, which were cut to 32, and a second sample of 1,041 German internet users tested the reduced set, yielding eleven items per subscale. Both dimensions correlated positively with self-reported coding ability and predicted responses to algorithmic content-filtering scenarios. The scale is deliberately limited to the cognitive dimensions; critical evaluation, coping and creation skills are discussed in the literature review as further dimensions but are not measured.
