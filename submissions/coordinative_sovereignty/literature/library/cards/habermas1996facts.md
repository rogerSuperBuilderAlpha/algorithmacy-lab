## Use in the chapter
Cited in §1, in the answer to the Bodinian objection that sovereignty must be supreme and indivisible. The chapter writes that "Habermas (1996) describes popular sovereignty as desubstantialized" and quotes "sovereignty is found in those subjectless forms of communication" (pp. 486–487), from Appendix I, "Popular sovereignty as procedure". Role: support for the move that lets a shared, partial, complaint-borne standing count as sovereignty at all.
