## Summary
Rader and Gray ask how people make sense of algorithmic curation and how awareness of it might change their behaviour in the system. They analyse open-ended responses to a survey question asking whether respondents believe their Facebook News Feed shows every post their friends create; 505 people completed the survey and 464 cases entered the analysis. Responses spanned a wide range of beliefs and causal inferences, from the belief that the feed shows all possible posts from friends to the recognition of automated filtering by an algorithm. The authors classify these beliefs and argue that each carries different potential consequences for user behaviour in the system. Because user behaviour is both input to and constrained by the algorithm, these patterns of belief have consequences for the system as a whole.
