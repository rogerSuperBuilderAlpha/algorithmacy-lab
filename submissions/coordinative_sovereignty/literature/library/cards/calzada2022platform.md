## Summary
Calzada, Manna and Mantovani build a model of two horizontally differentiated online travel agencies and two hotels to ask when agencies adopt price parity clauses and how those clauses change hotels' listing decisions. Without parity, hotels trade off the extra demand from multihoming against the higher margin from singlehoming, and segmentation arises only when hotels are close substitutes. Parity clauses let agencies raise commissions, which makes multihoming less attractive and makes segmentation, each hotel listing on a different agency, more likely. Agencies adopt the clauses when competitive pressure is high, either between agencies or between hotels, because they keep commissions up even when the market segments. Consumers and hotels generally lose. The introduction documents the enforcement record: the Bundeskartellamt prohibitions of HRS (2013) and Booking.com (2015), and legislative bans in France (August 2015), Austria (2016), Italy (2017), and Belgium and Sweden (2018).

## Use in the chapter
The evidence audit (U2) proposes this as the source for the §5.5 sentences "Major agencies abandoned wide parity clauses in 2015 under pressure from national competition authorities" and "several member states legislated against the clauses in the years that followed". Role: support for the parity history, with the instruction to omit Sweden from the list.
