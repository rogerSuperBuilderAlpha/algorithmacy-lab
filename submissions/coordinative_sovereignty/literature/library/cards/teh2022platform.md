## Summary
Teh models the non-price governance of marketplaces by platforms such as Amazon, Airbnb and eBay, which act as regulators of the markets they create. The framework asks when a profit-maximising platform has an incentive to design its governance, the rules that determine how much sellers compete, in a welfare-enhancing way. The central result is that the platform's governance design can be distorted toward inducing either insufficient or excessive seller competition, and which way it tilts depends on the fee instrument the platform uses: under per-transaction commissions the platform's revenue is tied to seller prices, so it may dampen competition, whereas under participation fees it may push competition harder than is efficient. The results are illustrated with micro-founded applications to the platform's control over seller entry, its information provision and recommendations, its quality standards, and its search-design choices. The paper supplies a formal reason why the same governance lever can be used for or against the coordinated sellers depending on how the platform is paid.

In §3.3 it supports the claim that the interested mediator's choices about ranking, entry and quality standards are shaped by its own revenue structure rather than by the parties' interests; in §5.4 it supports the portfolio reading, since the fee instrument attaches to a function and the distortion follows the function.
