## Summary
Gagrčin, Naab and Grub conduct a systematic integrative literature review of 169 studies across the social sciences and humanities on algorithm literacy, motivated by the concern that research on individuals' understanding of algorithms is expanding rapidly without a cohesive framework. They organize the field around its key conceptualizations of algorithm literacy and around the endogenous, exogenous and personal factors that shape it. Their theoretical proposal is that existing findings can be framed as experiential learning cycles, in which users encounter algorithmic outputs, form and test beliefs about them and adjust their use, and they argue that this framing is useful for thinking about how algorithm literacy is acquired. The review closes with a research agenda: defining the core competencies relevant to algorithm literacy, standardizing measures, integrating subjective and factual aspects of literacy, and adopting task- and domain-specific approaches rather than a single general construct.
