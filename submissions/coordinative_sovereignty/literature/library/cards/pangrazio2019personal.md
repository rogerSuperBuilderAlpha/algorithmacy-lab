## Summary
Pangrazio and Selwyn start from the premise that the capacity to understand and control one's personal data is now a crucial part of living in contemporary society, and they argue that the prevailing responses, data-safety education on one side and data-science skills on the other, both miss what ordinary people need. In their place they propose personal data literacies grounded in the critical literacies tradition, where digital data are understood as socially situated and context dependent rather than as a technical object to be secured or analysed. The framework has five domains: data identification, data understandings, data reflexivity, data uses, and data tactics, moving from recognizing what data are generated through understanding how they are processed, to reflecting on their personal and social implications, to putting them to use and, finally, to tactical action that resists or repurposes data practices. The article positions personal data literacy as an educational priority while acknowledging that data generation and processing involve socio-technical structures that exceed individual responsibility.
