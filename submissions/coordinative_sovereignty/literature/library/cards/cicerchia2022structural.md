## Summary
Cicerchia intervenes in the neo-republican debate over whether domination must be the intentional exercise of arbitrary power by an agent. Critics of the standard definition argue that it cannot capture structural domination, the unfreedom that arises unintentionally from the workings of social systems such as capitalist labor markets. Cicerchia's reconciliation holds that structural domination involves both agency and unintended consequences: the labor process gives individual agents incentives to create and sustain structures whose effects dominate, even where no one deliberately intends domination, while those agents' intentions are themselves shaped by their positions within the structure. Domination is therefore simultaneously agential and structural, neither a purely intentional act of oppression nor an impersonal systemic force, but the interplay between decisions and the constraining conditions those decisions reproduce. The labor market is the worked example: workers face a dominating structure that operates through, and is answerable to, identifiable agents.

## Use in the chapter
