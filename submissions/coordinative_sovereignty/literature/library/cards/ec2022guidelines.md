## Summary
These Commission guidelines state when collective agreements by solo self-employed persons fall outside, or will not be enforced against, the Article 101 TFEU cartel prohibition. The Commission reasons that certain solo self-employed persons are in a situation comparable to workers: they face dependence on counterparties for access to customers and receive take it or leave it terms with little negotiating leverage. The section on digital labour platforms defines them as services provided at a distance by electronic means at the recipient's request, where the organisation of individuals' work is a necessary and essential component rather than ancillary aggregation. Paragraph 31 draws the operative conclusion that collective agreements between solo self-employed persons and digital labour platforms relating to working conditions fall outside the scope of Article 101 TFEU. The guidelines thereby remove the competition-law obstacle that had made platform workers' collective bargaining legally precarious across the Union.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter's current text. Audit S30 proposes it for §7, where the chapter writes that collective bargaining "has recently acquired a statutory anchor" but currently supports the sentence with the platform-work directive alone. Paragraph 31 is the load-bearing pinpoint: it is the instrument that takes solo self-employed platform workers' collective agreements on working conditions outside Article 101, which is the competition-law half of the anchor the directive's Article 25 voice clause presupposes. Role: legal instrument supporting the §7 claim that bargaining over how assignments, deactivation, and ranking are governed is legally available.

## Key quotations
"collective agreements between solo self-employed persons and digital labour platforms relating to working conditions fall outside the scope of Article 101 TFEU" (para. 31).
