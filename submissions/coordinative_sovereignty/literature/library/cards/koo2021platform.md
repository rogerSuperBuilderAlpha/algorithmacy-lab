## Summary
Koo and Eesley study how sellers on a major online retail marketplace respond to a governance intervention made through design. Platform research treats design changes, such as modifications to ranking algorithms or interfaces, as a way of regulating participant behaviour, but the effectiveness of a change depends on how participants respond, which varies with their context. The authors focus on one contextual difference, rural versus urban location, and exploit a substantial redesign of the marketplace. They find that rural sellers were considerably less able to adapt to the redesign than urban sellers, producing an enduring performance gap, and attribute the weaker adaptation to rural sellers' more limited access to rich informational resources, which they call the offline interface. The paper's contribution is to show that platforms frequently change their design rules, that those changes have heterogeneous and unintended consequences for governance, and that digital inequality among platform-dependent sellers is reproduced by the ability to read and adjust to such changes.
