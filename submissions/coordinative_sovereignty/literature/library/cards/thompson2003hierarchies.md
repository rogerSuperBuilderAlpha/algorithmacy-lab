## Summary
Thompson examines how the word "network" has been deployed across the social sciences and whether networks constitute a distinct mode of coordinating social and economic activity alongside hierarchies and markets. The book offers a commentary on the idea of the network as it appears in organisational sociology, economics, political science, and social theory, compares the three forms on a preliminary basis (hierarchies coordinate through authority, markets through price, networks through trust, reciprocity, and shared norms), and then tests the limits of the network concept by asking how networks are governed, whether they can be designed, and where the metaphor stops explaining.
