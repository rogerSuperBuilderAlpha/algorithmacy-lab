## Summary
This Cornell Center for Hospitality Research report replicates and extends Anderson's 2009 study of the billboard effect, the lift in a hotel's own direct bookings that follows from being listed on an online travel agency. Using reservation and search data from InterContinental Hotels Group properties across 2008 to 2010, Anderson tracks what happens to a hotel's brand-site bookings when it appears on Expedia and when it does not. The expanded analysis finds that being listed on Expedia raised total reservation volume by roughly 7.5 to 26 percent depending on the hotel, and that for each reservation an IHG hotel received through Expedia the brand's own website received between three and nine additional reservations. The report reads the agency's listing as an advertising and discovery channel as much as a sales channel: travelers use the agency to search and compare, then book direct. It is practitioner-oriented and descriptive rather than causal, and later work has not consistently reproduced the effect.
