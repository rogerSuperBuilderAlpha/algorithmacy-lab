## Summary
Ziewitz asks how those who are measured by a ranking system respond to the pressure to do well on it without being accused of gaming. Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork with search engine optimization consultants in the United Kingdom, he shows that raising a website's visibility requires constant judgement about where the line falls between acceptable optimization and illegitimate manipulation. That line is not fixed in the algorithm; it is drawn and redrawn by the search engine operator through guidelines, penalties and public statements, and it is deliberately ambiguous. Practitioners therefore perform what he calls ethical work: managing the moral ambiguity the operator creates, justifying their techniques to clients and to themselves, and anticipating how the operator will classify them. The conclusion is that the ethics of optimization has become a central site of governance in contemporary ranking systems, with the operator governing conduct through the threat of the gaming label rather than through rules alone.
