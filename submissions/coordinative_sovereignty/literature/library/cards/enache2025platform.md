## Summary
Enache and Rhodes model a platform that matches buyers and sellers who then transact repeatedly, charging per-transaction fees while offering users a convenience benefit. They compare a regime in which all transactions must run through the platform with one in which the parties can disintermediate, taking the relationship off-platform after the initial match. The threat of disintermediation reshapes pricing: the platform front-loads its fees, charging more for early transactions before the parties can leave. More strikingly, the platform can benefit from the possibility of disintermediation, because users' choices about when to leave screen their private valuations of the platform's convenience, letting the platform price discriminate. The welfare conclusion is that users cannot be assumed to gain from an open exit: the fee adjustments that the exit option induces may leave buyers worse off despite their new freedom to transact directly.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter's current text. The sweep proposes it for §5.2 and §5.3 as a contesting source. The chapter's bypass test treats restoring the direct tie as the move that dislodges a contingent mediator, and §5.3 treats exit as the sovereignty that opening the tie makes available. This paper contests the inference that opening the tie transfers leverage to the dependent party: with repeated transactions the platform re-prices around the open edge, front-loading fees and using exit behavior as a screen, so a formally bypassable mediator can retain its take. The chapter's diagnostic should note that bypassability changes the fee path, not necessarily the distribution of surplus.
