## Summary
Drawing on interviews with 77 high-performing eBay business sellers in France and Belgium, the authors examine the power asymmetries that customer evaluations create in online work settings. They identify three mechanisms. Customer ratings open visibility gaps that align the interests of buyers and the platform owner against the seller, so that an implicit coalition forms between the two parties the seller depends on. Algorithms mediate relations and constrain human agency, because ratings feed into search rank and account standing through rules sellers cannot see or contest. Sellers nonetheless exploit algorithmic knowledge strategically to recover agency, contacting buyers before negative feedback is filed and managing how the system reads them. The study reports that negative reviews, though infrequent, produced anxiety and a sense of vulnerability. The contribution is a triadic account of algorithmic power in which the platform governs through the customer rather than directly.
