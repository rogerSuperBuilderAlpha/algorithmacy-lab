## Summary
Berndsen reviews the academic literature on derivative central counterparties (CCPs), drawing on 176 peer-reviewed articles published between 1983 and the end of 2020 and organizing them around three fundamental questions: when a CCP should be introduced in a derivatives market, whether a CCP's prefunded financial resources and recovery tools are sufficient, and what incentives produce good CCP governance. The introduction sets out what makes a CCP distinctive: unlike an automated clearinghouse that nets payments while leaving bilateral exposures intact, a CCP interposes itself between the two trading counterparties through novation, so that each party's exposure is to the CCP rather than to the other. The review finds that whether central clearing reduces aggregate counterparty exposure depends on four calibratable factors, that the adequacy of the default waterfall is a normative rather than empirical matter, and that mandatory clearing is self-fulfilling once markets have moved to it. The author discloses board roles at LCH.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited. The audit (U19, §5.2) asks for a source behind the clearinghouse example in the bypass test: "A clearinghouse that guarantees both sides of a trade ... does something two parties dealing directly could not do for themselves, and handing them a direct line dislodges nothing" (§5.2). Berndsen is proposed as the open-access source; the alternative is Antinolfi, Carapella and Carli (2022). The role is support for the claim that novation is integrating work no direct tie reproduces, which is the chapter's paradigm of a necessary mediator. The introduction's contrast between netting-only clearinghouses and CCPs that substitute themselves as counterparty is exactly the distinction the chapter needs, and the finding that mandatory clearing becomes self-fulfilling is a useful caution: necessity can be partly manufactured by rule.

## Key quotations
"a CCP clears one or multiple asset classes by interposing itself between two trading counterparties through the legal process of novation or open offer. The bilateral exposures between the two trading parties are replaced by a bilateral exposure between the seller and the CCP and between the CCP and the buyer." (Introduction)

"Mandatory central clearing in combination with UMR as per the current G20/FSB policy, is a self-fulfilling policy in the sense that once the market has moved to central clearing, it becomes optimal to keep it that way as all four factors move in favor of central clearing." (Conclusions)
