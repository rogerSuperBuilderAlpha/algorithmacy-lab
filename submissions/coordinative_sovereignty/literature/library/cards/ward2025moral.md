## Summary
Ward, Davis, Mackenzie and Jones study the mass migration of users from Twitter to Mastodon after Musk's 2022 acquisition through a mixed-methods analysis of migration-related tweets. Read through symbolic interaction, both platforms are mediated moral enterprises and the migrating users are moral entrepreneurs in Becker's and Cohen's sense, making claims in words and actions about how platforms ought and ought not to operate. The authors recast moral enterprise for platform settings as a tripartite process of idealization (framing a platform as the answer to a named ill, as Mastodon's founder framed decentralization against Twitter's centralization), materialization (building the ideal into architecture) and actualization (users enacting it). The findings show three pressures that Twitter's affordances imposed on a migration organized on Twitter itself, naturalization, attention and pace, each tending to discourage disconnection or to centralize the newcomers on a few large instances, and they show how well-networked users countered those pressures with explainers and patience. The paper extends moral entrepreneurship to platform proprietors as well as users.

## Use in the chapter
Section 8 says "federated social networks give users both exit to another instance and a voice in local governance (Gehl & Zulli, 2023)." The audit (S34) notes that in Gehl and Zulli governance is at the administrator level and user voice is variable, recommends rewording to "the possibility of voice," and proposes Ward et al. 2025 as the second source. Role: second source for the federated-network example, and the evidence that whether voice is realized depends on the migration's dynamics: the large instances that absorbed the influx restricted sign-ups and strained their volunteer administration, so exit was real and voice was uneven. That supports the chapter's own qualification that "structure enables voice without ensuring it."
