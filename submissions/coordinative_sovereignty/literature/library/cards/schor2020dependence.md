## Summary
Against the two dominant accounts of platform labor, precarity through employment classification and technological control through algorithms, both of which predict relatively common worker experiences, the authors find heterogeneity. Drawing on 112 in-depth interviews with workers on seven platforms (Airbnb, TaskRabbit, Turo, Uber, Lyft, Postmates, and Favor), they show that satisfaction, autonomy, and earnings vary significantly across and within platforms because platform labor is weakly institutionalized. The variable that explains the variation is dependence: workers who rely on platform income to pay basic expenses fare worse than supplemental earners, who are more satisfied and higher-earning, which suggests platforms free-ride on conventional employers. Platforms are also hierarchically ordered in what providers can earn, conditions of work, and their capacity to produce satisfied workers. The authors call for a new analytic approach centered on labor force diversity, connections to conventional labor markets, and worker dependence.
