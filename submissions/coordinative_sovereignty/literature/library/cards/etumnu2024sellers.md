## Summary
Etumnu and Malone study Amazon's BuyBox, the box on a product page that displays a single seller when many sellers offer the same item. Using a choice model and a panel of more than 290,000 grocery products from five Amazon country sites, they estimate which seller attributes predict winning the BuyBox. The dominant factor is competitive pricing: being the lowest-priced seller over a 180-day window substantially raises the chance of assignment. Low current prices, low sales ranks, and minimal out-of-stock percentages also matter. The authors read these results through Amazon's dual position: it competes with its own third-party sellers and with outside retailers, so the algorithm rewards sellers who keep the marketplace price-competitive and well stocked. The paper frames the findings as guidance for food and beverage firms on how to operate inside a platform whose allocation rule is opaque but partly inferable from outcomes.
