## Summary
Plantin and de Seta treat WeChat as a case of a non-Western digital service whose success came first from platformization and then from the infrastructuralization of its platform model, as it absorbed payments, identity, public services and commerce. They argue that this trajectory reflects China's techno-nationalist media regulation and an increasingly explicit cyber-sovereignty agenda rather than market dynamics alone. Using technical documentation, business reports, interviews and observation, they situate WeChat in the history of Chinese ICT infrastructure and examine WeChat Pay's role in setting new monetary standards. They close by asking whether the techno-nationalist model of a state-aligned platform that becomes infrastructure foreshadows how platforms will be regulated elsewhere. The article extends Plantin's earlier work on infrastructure studies and platform studies to the Chinese case.

## Use in the chapter
Audit S32 proposes it as the second source beside Bernot (2025) for §8's WeChat sentence: "WeChat is woven into Chinese public administration to the point of hosting police modules." Plantin and de Seta supply the structural account of how WeChat became infrastructure under a cyber-sovereignty agenda, which supports the chapter's claim that a closed national platform without user voice reproduces co-optation under a domestic flag. Role: second source; Sivetc (2021) is the RuNet parallel.
