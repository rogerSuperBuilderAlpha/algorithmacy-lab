## Summary
The paper models how a platform that matches buyers and sellers can defend against disintermediation, the practice of parties meeting on the platform and completing the transaction off it to avoid commission. It studies the platform's two levers, pricing and information. Several results run against intuition. A platform facing a high disintermediation threat may do best by raising commissions when the information environment is rich, concentrating its revenue on the transactions it can secure. Raising sellers' switching costs can lower platform revenue, because sellers pass the cost through to buyers. On information, the platform benefits from disclosing partial rather than complete information about buyer risk, because full information reduces the value of the protection the platform offers and strengthens the incentive to transact off-platform. Upfront fees prevent disintermediation but raise less than commissions when seller quality is heterogeneous. The setting is analytical, in the operations-research tradition.
