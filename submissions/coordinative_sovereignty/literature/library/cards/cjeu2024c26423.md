## Summary
On a reference from the Amsterdam District Court in damages litigation between Booking.com and German hotels, the Second Chamber ruled on two questions. First, whether wide and narrow parity clauses in contracts between hotel reservation platforms and accommodation providers escape Article 101(1) TFEU as ancillary restraints. The Court restated the test: a restriction is ancillary only if the main operation would be impossible without it, and being harder or less profitable is not enough. On that test, narrow clauses, which forbid hotels from undercutting the platform on their own sites, do not appear objectively necessary to the platform's economic viability, and neither kind of clause is an ancillary restraint, though they may still be assessed for actual effects and exemption. Second, on market definition under the Vertical Block Exemption, the Court held that the substitutability between online intermediation services and other sales channels must be examined concretely rather than assumed.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §5.2, where the chapter quotes the ancillary restraint test, "the implementation of the main operation … would be impossible in the absence of the restriction in question" (Case C-264/23, para. 53), as the legal analogue of its bypass test, and in §5.5, where it reports that "parity clauses, wide and narrow alike, are not ancillary restraints" (§5.5). Role: legal_instrument. The audit confirms the para. 53 locator and the substance of the §5.5 sentence.

## Key quotations
"In order for a restriction to be classified as 'ancillary', it is necessary to establish, first, whether the implementation of the main operation, which is not anti-competitive in nature, would be impossible in the absence of the restriction in question. The fact that that operation is simply more difficult to implement or even less profitable without the restriction concerned cannot be deemed to give that restriction the 'objective necessity' required in order for it to be classified as 'ancillary'." (para. 53)

"narrow parity clauses which prohibit only partner accommodation providers from offering to the public on their own online channels overnight stays at a rate lower than that offered on the hotel reservation platform ... do not appear to be objectively necessary to ensure the economic viability of the hotel reservation platform." (para. 63)
