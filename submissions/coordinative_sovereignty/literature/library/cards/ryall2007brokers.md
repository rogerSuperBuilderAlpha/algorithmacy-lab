## Summary
Ryall and Sorenson ask whether standing between parties can confer a competitive advantage and whether any such advantage survives when actors form relations strategically. Using a biform game, in which actors first choose relations and then bargain over the value a coalition creates, they show that if relations are taken as exogenous a broker can enjoy an advantage only under three conditions: it faces no substitutes either for the connections it offers or for the value it creates, it intermediates more than two parties, and interdependence does not lock it into a particular pattern of exchange. If instead actors form relations in light of the expected future value of those relations, profitable intermediation positions arise only under strict assumptions about unilateral action. The paper thus formalises when a broker is essential to the value a coalition creates and when it is merely present, and it draws implications for firm strategy and for empirical brokerage research.
