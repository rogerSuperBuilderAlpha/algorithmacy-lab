## Summary
Gillespie's chapter asks what it means for algorithms to have become the means by which publicly relevant information is selected and presented, and sets out six dimensions along which "public relevance algorithms" carry political weight. Patterns of inclusion concern which data are gathered and made ready for the algorithm. Cycles of anticipation concern the platform's effort to know and predict its users, and the users' reciprocal effort to anticipate the algorithm and shape their conduct accordingly. The evaluation of relevance concerns the criteria, typically hidden, by which results are judged appropriate. The promise of algorithmic objectivity concerns the presentation of the procedure as impartial. Entanglement with practice concerns how users reshape their activity to suit the algorithm. The production of calculated publics concerns how algorithmic presentation of a public shapes that public's sense of itself. The argument is that these systems are sociotechnical institutions whose legitimacy needs examination in the way that editorial judgment once did.
