## Summary
Kellogg, Valentine and Christin synthesize the interdisciplinary literature on algorithms at work through Edwards's (1979) idea of the workplace as contested terrain, in which managers deploy production technologies to extract value and workers resist. They find that algorithmic control operates through six mechanisms they call the 6 Rs: employers use algorithms to direct workers by restricting and recommending, to evaluate them by recording and rating, and to discipline them by replacing and rewarding. Four further claims follow. Labor process theory exposes problems hidden by the largely positive view of workplace algorithms. The technical capacities of algorithmic systems produce a form of rational control distinct from the technical and bureaucratic control employers have used for a century, being more comprehensive, instantaneous, interactive and opaque. New algorithmic occupations are emerging around these systems. And workers resist individually and collectively through tactics the authors call algoactivism. The review is conceptual and maps an agenda rather than reporting new data.

## Use in the chapter
Section 3 cites it: "Kellogg et al. (2020) likewise find algorithmic control distinct from the technical and bureaucratic control," and §3.1 cites it with Möhlmann et al.
