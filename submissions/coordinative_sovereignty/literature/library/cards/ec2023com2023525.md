## Summary
The Commission's first preliminary review of the Platform-to-Business Regulation, three years after it became applicable, reports on how its transparency and redress provisions have been implemented in practice. On ranking, a study of platforms' terms and conditions found that only around a third of online intermediation services listed information on their main ranking parameters, and that where they did the descriptions were general enough to fall short of what the Regulation expects. On internal complaint handling, the figures reported by the large platforms diverge so widely (Apple reporting a dozen complaints, Google millions) that the Commission concludes providers interpret the notion of a complaint very differently. Mediation, which the Regulation offers as a contractual option, is virtually unused: only several dozen cases were reported, and only 25 of 179 large platforms had named the required mediators in their terms. The Commission does not recommend revision, holding that the rules can work if applied fully and correctly, and proposes enforcement improvements instead.

## Use in the chapter
Not yet cited; strand 3 and the gaps file rate it MUST. It is the official evidence behind two claims the chapter already makes. In §3.2 the chapter says the P2B Regulation "obliges platforms to disclose the main parameters of ranking" and that the literate wager "mostly fails"; this report shows that only about a third even disclose. In §8 the chapter says the Regulation "offers mediation, and gives representative organizations standing", "making it one of the few instruments to attach a channel to disclosure", and §7 calls the practice of such channels "thin"; the report's "virtually unused" mediation figure is the evidence for thin. The role is support. The Omnibus repeal of P2B discussed in §8 should be read against this report's conclusion that the rules need enforcement rather than revision.
