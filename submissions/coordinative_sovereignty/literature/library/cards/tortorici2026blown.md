## Summary
An in-depth case study of The Drivers Cooperative (TDC), the driver-owned ride-hailing platform in New York City, examining the challenges multistakeholder platform cooperatives face. Drawing on 16 semi-structured interviews, a group interview with five board members, six months of participant observation during 2024 fieldwork, and extensive desk research, the paper traces how TDC's growth efforts produced a fracture between its cooperative membership and its platform infrastructure, conceptualized as the Blown Head Gasket Effect. Limited access to capital, political divergence among stakeholders, and temporal pressure drove the fracture: the cooperative spent roughly $600,000 on app development in 2022 that drivers judged a mistake, tensions ran between management and drivers and between engineers demanding market-rate pay and drivers protecting wages, and by October 2023 the cooperative existed separately from its app. By summer 2024, TDC retained fewer than 50 full-time drivers against earlier publicity of 12,000 recruited. The paper closes with TDC's legislative achievements and recommendations for practitioners and policy.
