## Summary
Chung and Wihbey ask how understanding of social media algorithms is distributed across sociodemographic groups and countries, and whether that knowledge has behavioural consequences. They field surveys in the United States (N = 1,415), the United Kingdom (N = 1,435), South Korea (N = 1,798) and Mexico (N = 784), measuring factual algorithmic knowledge and the predictors of it. The United States shows the highest overall algorithmic knowledge, followed by the United Kingdom, Mexico and South Korea. The socioeconomic gradient is uneven: education predicts algorithmic knowledge in South Korea and Mexico but has no significant effect in the United States or the United Kingdom, and gender is a notable predictor in South Korea, where men score higher. The authors also find that algorithmic knowledge is associated with actions against misinformation, and they draw out implications for algorithmic literacy interventions. The contribution is comparative rather than theoretical: it is one of the few studies to put the "knowledge gap" about algorithms on a cross-national footing.
