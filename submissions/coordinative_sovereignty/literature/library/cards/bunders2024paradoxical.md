## Summary
The article asks why platform cooperatives, which promise worker ownership and governance as an alternative to investor-owned gig platforms, remain rare despite slow but steady growth in Europe. Using desk research on 48 platform cooperatives and 16 in-depth interviews with founders, the authors apply paradox theory to the start-up phase, asking how founders manage competing demands. Extending work on interorganisational paradoxes, they show that systemic tensions in the gig economy (between flexibility and security, autonomy and control) motivate the founding of cooperatives as a coping response, so the paradoxes are generative. Yet the same tensions, interacting across levels during development, can produce failed market entry, so the paradoxes also cut against the organisations they bring into being. The study therefore explains both why cooperatives keep appearing and why they keep failing, and addresses the puzzle of establishing organisations that are themselves permeated by the tensions they respond to.
