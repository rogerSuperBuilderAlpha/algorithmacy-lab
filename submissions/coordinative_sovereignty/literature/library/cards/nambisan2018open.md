## Summary
This editorial essay for a special issue argues that two trends, open innovation and platformization, have jointly transformed entrepreneurship. Platforms supply boundary resources and market access that let entrepreneurs build as complementors, but in doing so the entrepreneur's entire business comes to depend on a core platform that stands between it and its customers. The authors coin "contingent competencies" for skills whose exercise is scaffolded by the platform and can be destroyed by a change the platform owner makes. They illustrate with third-party Amazon merchants, who handle much of the 45 percent of US e-commerce passing through Amazon and are "at the mercy of the Amazon recommendation algorithm", with app developers whose delisting could be devastating even with multihoming, and with YouTubers whose revenues were cut by policy changes. Platforms ease entry but concentrate power over the conditions of entrepreneurial action, and the essay anticipates a revival of antitrust to protect entrepreneurs from the platforms that enable them. The conclusion states that never before has so much entrepreneurial decision-making been at the mercy of an external entity.
