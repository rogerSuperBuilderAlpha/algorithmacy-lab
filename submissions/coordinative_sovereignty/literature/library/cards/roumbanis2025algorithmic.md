## Summary
Roumbanis takes the concept of mediation, which he treats as essentially relational, and applies it to the spread of AI technologies through social life. He begins from relational thinking in sociological theory and postphenomenology but departs from their non-substantialist bent, taking a substantialist position and defining what he calls the ecstatic dimension of relationality, reinterpreted through the notion of "being-toward". The main resource is Sartre's socio-materialist account of mediation, especially the "practico-inert field", the sedimented material environment that shapes how groups form and how serial relations among strangers are reproduced. The final part proposes a metaphorological approach, reading human-AI relations through three metaphors, "vampire objects", "bewitched quantities" and "frozen voices", each tied to an empirical phenomenon: algorithmically generated synthetic data, high-frequency trading, and AI recruitment chatbots. The upshot is that algorithmic systems act as mediating third parties that reshape group formation and serial reciprocity, with alienating effects.

The chapter's §3.1 describes the mediated triad in which both parties pass through an opaque, interested third. The role is support from social theory: Roumbanis offers a philosophical account of algorithmic systems as third parties in the Sartrean sense, which gives the chapter's structural claim a theoretical lineage outside organization theory and platform economics. The fit is loose, since his mediation is ontological rather than coordinative, and his examples (synthetic data, HFT, hiring chatbots) are not platform triads.
