## Summary
MacDonald studies YouTube videos in which self-styled algorithm experts promise to reveal how the platform's recommendation and monetization systems really work. Although the lore they teach is speculative, the videos function as market devices that construct YouTube's visibility markets, and they do so to the platform's benefit in four ways: they legitimize the platform's narrative of algorithmic objectivity, they teach creators to calculate the value of content through the platform's metrics, they enroll creators in building and governing audiences for the platform, and they moralize the difficulties of creator life in ways that keep creators engaged. The argument is that lore videos are performative rather than descriptive: in teaching calculative practices and audience governance they reproduce platform meritocracy, so the expertise market that appears to empower creators against the algorithm in fact channels their effort into compliance with the platform's objectives.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §9, in the discussion of whether algorithmacy can be democratized: "Studies of the platform-expertise industry find it coaching producers to comply with platform objectives, translating opacity into advice about how to please the system (Bishop, 2020; MacDonald, 2023)".
