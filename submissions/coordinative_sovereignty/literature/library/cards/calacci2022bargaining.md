## Summary
The paper reports the design and deployment of the Shipt Calculator, a tool built with non-profit worker organisations that lets app-based delivery workers on Shipt pool and share their pay data. The authors ground the design in the history of worker inquiry and describe the challenges of building technologies that are worker-centric rather than platform-centric. The empirical core is an audit of Shipt's 2020 change from a transparent commission formula to an opaque "black box" pay model: using data contributed by 140 workers in summer 2020, the authors find that almost half of workers experienced an unannounced pay cut even though average per-order pay rose, and that many earned below minimum wage on some orders. The contribution is methodological as much as empirical: aggregated worker data can reverse-engineer a pay algorithm that no individual worker can see, which the authors frame as "Digital Workerism", a route toward a more balanced platform economy in which workers hold independent knowledge of the systems that manage them.
