## Summary
Lehdonvirta argues that digital platforms have become institutions with a power comparable to states. The early internet's insecurity led companies such as Amazon, eBay, Apple and Upwork to build marketplaces that restored order at scale by making and enforcing rules, and the result is a set of empires in the cloud ruled with little accountability, whose authority is not bound to territory but follows their users wherever they live. Users and workers cannot easily leave because viable alternatives often do not exist, and antitrust and decentralisation have proven weak remedies. Told through the careers of founders such as Bezos, Kalanick and Nakamoto and of resisters such as Kristy Milland of Mechanical Turk, the book chronicles the recapitulation of economic, political and social institutions inside platforms. Its prescription is to treat platforms as polities: protect the proto-democratic institutions that users and developers are already building, and adopt a charter of fundamental rights that limits platform rulers as constitutions limit states.
