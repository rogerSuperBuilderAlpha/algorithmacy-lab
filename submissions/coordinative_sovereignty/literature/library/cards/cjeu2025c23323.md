## Summary
The Grand Chamber answered a reference from the Italian Consiglio di Stato arising from the Italian competition authority's fine on Google for refusing to make Android Auto interoperable with Enel's e-mobility app. The Court held that a dominant undertaking's refusal to ensure interoperability between its platform and a third-party app can be an abuse under Article 102 TFEU even where the platform is not indispensable to the app's commercial viability, provided the platform was developed to be opened to third parties rather than for the dominant firm's own use and interoperability would make the app more attractive to users. In that setting the strict indispensability condition of Bronner does not apply; the analysis turns on whether the refusal restricts competition on the merits and harms consumers. The Court also addressed the assessment of effects, objective justifications including security and the absence of a ready template, and market definition in emerging sectors.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. The Evidence Audit (U4) notes that the chapter describes the essential-facilities doctrine three times in §5.2 and §7 without citing it, and recommends citing Bronner as the classic formulation with a clause noting the 2025 narrowing in Android Auto: indispensability is not required where the platform was built to be opened. Role: legal instrument. It matters for the chapter's necessary-versus-contingent distinction because it shows the Court treating a platform's openness by design as the trigger for a duty to deal, which is close to the chapter's own test of whether a mediator was built to introduce strangers.
