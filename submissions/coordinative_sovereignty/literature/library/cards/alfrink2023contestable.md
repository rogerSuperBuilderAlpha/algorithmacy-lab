## Summary
The article starts from the observation that automated decision-making by AI systems raises concerns of fairness, legitimacy and accountability, and proposes that such harms can be guarded against by making systems contestable by design, meaning responsive to human intervention throughout the system lifecycle and not only after a decision has been taken. The authors treat contestable AI as a small but growing research field whose findings require translation before practitioners can use them, and they argue that design frameworks are a proven way to convey intermediate-level, generative design knowledge. Using qualitative-interpretative methods and visual mapping of the literature, they extract sociotechnical features and development practices that contribute to contestability and synthesise them into a framework of five system features and six development practices. The framework is meant to let designers build systems in which affected parties can review, challenge and override determinations, with human controllers able to intervene before machine decisions take effect.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §7 for the definition of contestability: "a contestable system is open and responsive to dispute throughout its lifecycle" (Alfrink et al., 2023). The chapter uses it as support for the distinction between explainability (reasons) and contestability (a channel to challenge), and then locates contestability as voice at the level of the case rather than a share in governance. The Literature Gaps report suggests pairing it with Crémer et al. (2023) to disambiguate the DMA's market-entry sense of "contestability" from Alfrink's decision-challenging sense, which the chapter currently leaves implicit.
