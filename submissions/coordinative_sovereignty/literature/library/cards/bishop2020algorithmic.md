## Summary
Bishop studies self-styled algorithmic experts who build brands and careers by selling theories of algorithmic visibility to YouTube creators. These intermediaries sit between established industries and content producers and teach optimization strategies meant to manage the risk of algorithmic invisibility. Drawing on a three-year ethnography centred on two prominent figures, Matthew Patrick (MatPat) and Matt Gielen, she shows that although the experts present themselves as adversaries of YouTube who run objective experiments on the recommender, their advice in practice teaches creators to comply with the platform's business model. Their expertise rests on what Bishop calls algorithmic lore, subjective narratives that mix data with assumption rather than settled knowledge. The central result is twofold: a paid market in algorithmic expertise exists and coaches compliance, and its meritocratic framing hides structural inequalities on the platform by offering individualized solutions to problems that marginalized creators face systemically.
