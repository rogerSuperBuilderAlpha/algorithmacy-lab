## Summary
The article addresses the confusion produced by indiscriminate use of the term common-property resource, which has been applied alike to resources owned by governments, to unowned resources and to those managed by communities of users. Schlager and Ostrom replace the single label with a framework that distinguishes property-rights regimes by the bundle of rights their holders have. The rights are access, withdrawal, management, exclusion and alienation, and holders are classified by cumulative bundles: authorised users hold access and withdrawal; claimants add management; proprietors add exclusion; owners add alienation. The point is that meaningful collective governance can exist without full ownership, since management and exclusion rights are held by claimants and proprietors who cannot sell the resource. They apply the schema to empirical cases, including inshore fisheries and the Maine lobster industry, to show that different bundles function differently in practice and that institutional analysis must identify which rights are actually held rather than assume ownership.
