## Summary
Duggan and colleagues argue that "gig work" aggregates arrangements that differ in ways that matter for employment relations and HRM, and propose a three-way classification by technological feature: capital platform work (renting assets), crowdwork (online task markets) and app-work (locally delivered services allocated through an app). Focusing on app-work, they examine algorithmic management as the mediating tool through which platforms form the working relationship, assign work and manage performance while denying that an employment relationship exists. The piece is conceptual and sets out a research agenda. Its central structural observation is that app-work is triangular: the platform, the worker and the customer are all parties, the customer's ratings do supervisory work, and the app itself stands in for the manager, which complicates psychological contracts, voice and collective organization. The authors note that control is "always-on" and that platform-run training or appeals substitute for HR processes.

## Use in the chapter
Strand 1 lists it as a MUST for §3 and §3.1, supporting the chapter's triangular platform, worker, customer structure and the claim that the coordinated parties are enrolled without the contracts that mark employment or exchange. Role: support. It sits beside Vallas and Schor (2020) on platforms as a distinct governance mechanism and Kellogg et al. (2020) on algorithmic control, and Healy and Pekarek (2025) extends the triangular point.
