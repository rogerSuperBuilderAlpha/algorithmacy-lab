## Summary
Burrell and Fourcade review how the pairing of very large datasets with algorithmic processing has reshaped core social institutions. They organize the field around three developments. First, the rise of a coding elite that controls the means of digital production while a dispersed and precarious cybertariat supplies labelled data, moderation and gig labour. Second, the spread of mathematical optimization into education, medicine, finance and criminal justice, which entrenches an actuarial logic of decision. Third, the algorithmic intermediation of communication, which changes how people interact and think. The piece is a synthetic review rather than an empirical study. Its closing section cautions against inflated promises for artificial intelligence while insisting that the entanglement of algorithms with social organization and subjectivity is now deep. The class framing is the distinctive contribution: algorithmic competence is distributed along a line between those who write systems and those who are written by them.
