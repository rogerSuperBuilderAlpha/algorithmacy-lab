## Summary
Duffy and Meisner study how social media creators experience the algorithmic systems that enable or thwart their visibility. Against the hype of the creator economy and the critique of its exploitation and overwork, they ask how creators drawn from historically marginalized identities and stigmatized content genres perceive and live with algorithmic (in)visibility. Thirty in-depth interviews show a shared understanding that platforms enact governance unevenly, through formal means such as human or automated content moderation and through informal means such as shadowbans and biased algorithmic boosts. Creators' understandings feed into practices that range from self-censorship to concerted efforts to circumvent algorithmic intervention. The article closes by arguing that the regimes of discipline and punishment that structure the social media economy systematically disadvantage marginalized creators and cultural expression deemed non-normative. It belongs with Cotter's and Bishop's work on visibility as the currency that platform governance allocates, and it adds the distributional point that the allocation tracks existing marginality.
