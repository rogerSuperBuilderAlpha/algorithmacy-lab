## Summary
Ahrne and Brunsson argue that organization studies has restricted the concept of organization to formal organizations and has described everything outside them with other concepts, chiefly institutions and networks. They propose a broader concept in which organization is any order based on decisions, and they distinguish complete organization, which commands all the elements of organization (membership, hierarchy, rules, monitoring, and sanctions), from partial organization, which rests on only one or a few of those elements. Partial organization, they claim, is common and characterizes much of the contemporary global order, including standards, rankings, and meta-organizations. The article is conceptual rather than empirical. It discusses how partial organization arises, how and why institutions and networks sometimes become organized, and what organization does to change, transparency, and accountability. The central result is a vocabulary in which markets and networks can be partly organized without becoming firms, which is the move later applied to digital platforms by Kirchner and Schüßler.
