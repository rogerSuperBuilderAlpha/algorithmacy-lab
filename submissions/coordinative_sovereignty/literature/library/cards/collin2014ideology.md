## Summary
This is a published exchange between Brian Street and Ross Collin over the "interaction model" of literacy that Collin had proposed in an earlier RRQ article. Collin's model, built as both complement and counter to Street's ideological model, defines literacies as technologies that coevolve with sociocultural processes, and it adapts some of Goody's arguments about literacy's technological dimensions. Street replies that the interaction model repackages the technicist model that he and other socioculturalists refuted, and illustrates the point with MOOCs and Web 2.0 schooling initiatives that cannot change literacy through technology alone. Collin answers that the interaction model rejects both technological and cultural determinism: neither technology nor ideology fully determines literate practice, and digital literacies should be studied as complex technologies interacting with social relations, institutions, labour processes and mental conceptions. The two close with a synthesis that accounts for multiple processes, denies inherent priority to any one, and foregrounds the sociocultural not as ontology but as a political manoeuvre against technicism.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited. Strand 5 lists it as optional for §2 and §4. Its value is methodological: the chapter pairs forms of sovereignty with the media that carry them (Innis, Eisenstein, Goody and Watt) and could be read as technologically determinist; Collin and Street's synthesis gives a ready-made statement of how to hold that a medium conditions a competence without letting the medium determine it. It also sits beside Finnegan and Collins and Blot as part of the literacy-studies critique of the great divide that §2.1 and §4 should acknowledge.
