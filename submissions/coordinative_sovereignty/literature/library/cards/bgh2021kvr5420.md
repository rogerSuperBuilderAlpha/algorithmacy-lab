## Summary
Summary written from the Bundeskartellamt notice and two commentaries opened this session, not from the decision text. On 18 May 2021 the Federal Court of Justice (Kartellsenat) overturned the Düsseldorf Higher Regional Court, which had held Booking.com's narrow best price clause to be a lawful ancillary restraint needed to prevent hotels free-riding on the platform, and reinstated the Bundeskartellamt's 2015 prohibition. The narrow clause barred hotels from offering lower rates on their own websites while leaving them free to undercut on rival platforms. The court held that the clause was an appreciable restriction of competition under Article 101(1) TFEU and German law, was not an exempt ancillary restraint, and did not benefit from the Vertical Block Exemption. Booking.com had already stopped using the clause in Germany during the proceedings. The decision is the national precursor of the Court of Justice's 2024 ruling in C-264/23.

## Use in the chapter
Proposed by the evidence audit (E4) for §5.5, where the chapter writes that "national enforcement had narrowed the clauses by 2021". The audit flags this as the wrong direction: the 2021 event is the BGH upholding the Bundeskartellamt's 2015 prohibition of even the narrow clause, so the sentence should read that by 2021 German enforcement had condemned even the narrow clause. Role: legal_instrument. The chapter knows the decision only through the AG Collins Opinion in C-264/23 (paras 7, 9); this card supplies the primary reference.
