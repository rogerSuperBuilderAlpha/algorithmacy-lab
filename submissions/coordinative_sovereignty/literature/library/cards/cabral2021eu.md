## Summary
The report is the independent economic opinion of a panel convened by the Joint Research Centre on the Commission's then-proposed Digital Markets Act. It begins from the finding of several earlier reports that very large online platforms act as gatekeeping intermediaries between businesses and consumers, and that classic competition tools have struggled to deal with their conduct in time. The panel endorses the DMA's structure: designation of gatekeeper platforms and a list of ex ante obligations, complementing rather than replacing competition enforcement. The body of the report weighs the network effects that make large platforms valuable against the anti-competitive practices their position enables, examines novel conduct in advertising and mobile ecosystems, and addresses the information asymmetry between regulator and platform that implementation will have to overcome. It is policy analysis grounded in the existing economic literature rather than new empirical work, and it is a compact statement of the mainstream economic case for ex ante platform regulation.
