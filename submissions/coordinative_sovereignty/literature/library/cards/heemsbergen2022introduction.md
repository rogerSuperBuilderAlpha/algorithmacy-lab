## Summary
This editorial introduces a special issue of Media International Australia on algorithmic antagonisms. The editors frame the field around three terms. Resistance covers the tactics by which users, workers and communities push back against algorithmic systems, from refusal and obfuscation to collective organizing. Reconfiguration covers changes to how computational systems themselves are built and run. Renaissance names the moments in which alternative ways of living with computation become thinkable. The contributions they introduce range across tactical media, data activism, labour organizing against algorithmic management and artistic intervention, set within debates on computational justice and the democratic governance of technology. The piece is programmatic rather than empirical; its value is as a map of the resistance vocabulary and of the authors who own it. Magalhães's article in the same issue, which argues that much algorithmic resistance amounts to political disengagement, is the internal critic.
