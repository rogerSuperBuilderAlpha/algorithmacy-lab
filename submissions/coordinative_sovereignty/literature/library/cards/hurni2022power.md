## Summary
Hurni, Huber and Dibbern address what they call the power paradox of software platform ecosystems: the technological and structural position of the platform owner puts every complementor at the mercy of the owner's actions, yet the ecosystem's success depends on complementors' self-determination and the innovations they bring from outside. Through an exploratory multiple-case study of six platform partnerships, they find that power unfolds as a reciprocal process of three interlocking cycles in which both sides take an active role. A central cycle repeatedly evaluates whether the complementor accepts or rejects the owner's domination; thriving partnerships sustain it over time, which requires the complementor to adapt how it wields its own power as the partnership's needs change, and the owner sporadically to redefine the ecosystem framework while using coercion and manipulation to favour particular partnerships. Complementors in turn entice the owner through what the authors term over-subjectification. The study reframes platform power as dynamic and negotiated rather than fixed.
