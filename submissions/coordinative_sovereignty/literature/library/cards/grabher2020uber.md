## Summary
This editorial introduction to an Exchange in Environment and Planning A confronts the platform conception with the global production networks framework that has dominated the analysis of business-to-business relations. The authors note that the sharing economy debate built around Uber and Airbnb has produced much insight into peer-to-peer platforms while the digital transformation of business-to-business interaction has drawn less attention. They organize the comparison along four dimensions: value, shifting from owning assets to granting access; governance, shifting from make-or-buy to employ-or-enable; management, shifting from the back end to the front end; and labour, shifting from jobs to gigs. The piece is conceptual. Its central contribution is to translate the platform form into the vocabulary of economic geography and to mark where the GPN framework's assumptions about lead firms, contracts, and upgrading no longer hold when coordination runs through a platform.
