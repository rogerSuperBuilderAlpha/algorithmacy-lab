## Summary
The authors ask how platform workers make sense of algorithmic management when the system is opaque to them. Drawing on a case study of Uber drivers and on Weick's theory of enactment, they develop the concept of algorithm sensemaking, a cognitive process with three components: focused enactment, in which workers act on the system to see what it does; selection modes, through which they choose among interpretations; and retention sources, from which they keep and revise working theories. Sensemaking is presented as the mechanism linking workers' perception of tensions in the algorithmically managed workplace, for instance between the promise of flexibility and the reality of control, to the behaviors they then adopt. The contribution is to the algorithmic management literature in information systems: it gives a theoretical account of how workers build and revise informal models of a system they cannot inspect, rather than treating them as passive subjects of control.
