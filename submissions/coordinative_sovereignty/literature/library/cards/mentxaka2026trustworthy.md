## Summary
The article proposes a conceptual framework that sorts the effects of AI on democracy into seven risks, among them the subversion of autonomy, power asymmetries and the erosion of trust, and seven conditional contributions, among them accessibility and transparency. Each risk is mapped onto the seven requirements of the EU High-Level Expert Group's 2019 guidelines for trustworthy AI, then onto governance responses and the actors responsible for them. Two worked examples apply the scheme to electoral misinformation and to AI-assisted allocation of public services. The authors present it as a synthesis of recent literature rather than a study, state that it has not been empirically validated, and concede that the mappings do not show that the proposed measures work. Transparency is the requirement they assign most often, to six of the seven risks, while the responsible actors they name are all institutions: regulators, platforms, providers, auditors, courts and civil society.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the chapter. It is a current and well-placed instance of the dominant view that §3 criticizes, where the chapter holds that "absent procedural standing, information disclosure yields no actionable accountability" (§3). The framework concedes that opacity is institutional and strategic, yet answers it mainly with explanations and transparency portals, and it lists no role in which the coordinated person holds standing inside the mediation, although its public-service example does recommend appeals. Role: a construct to position against, with the caution that its scope is democratic institutions rather than platform coordination.
