## Summary
Klawitter and Hargittai ask whether people who depend on algorithmic systems recognize how those systems affect them and whether they can act on that knowledge skilfully. They interview creative entrepreneurs across the United States who sell handmade goods through online marketplaces and social media, and examine how far these sellers understand that algorithms shape the visibility of their wares and sales success. Participants show varying levels of what the authors call algorithmic skills or know-how. Many recognize that algorithms decide who sees their listings, but only some know how to set things up so as to raise their chances of reaching buyers. The distribution of this skill is uneven across the sample. The authors argue that the sellers' experience illuminates how internet users generally struggle with opaque and complex systems, and they position algorithmic skill as a dimension of digital inequality.
