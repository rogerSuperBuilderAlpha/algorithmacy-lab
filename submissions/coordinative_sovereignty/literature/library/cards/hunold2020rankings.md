## Summary
Hunold, Kesler and Laitenberger investigate whether online travel agents assign hotels worse positions in their search results when those hotels set lower prices elsewhere, on their own websites or on rival agents. The question follows directly from the removal of price-parity clauses in several European countries: once a hotel can no longer be contractually bound to give the OTA its best price, the OTA may still enforce parity through the ranking algorithm. The paper uses hotel-level data on prices across channels together with observed rank positions on the major OTAs, and the strand report records its finding that rank is better when a hotel's other-channel prices are higher, so that the gate migrated from the contract into the algorithm. The consumer-protection angle in the title follows: if a ranking that consumers read as a relevance signal is in fact rewarding pricing conduct that raises prices on other channels, the ranking misleads. Only the opening sentence of the abstract was seen this session; the findings described here are as summarised in the strand 6 report and LITERATURE_GAPS.
