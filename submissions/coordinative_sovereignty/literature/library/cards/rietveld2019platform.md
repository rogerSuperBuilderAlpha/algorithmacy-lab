## Summary
The article argues that platform sponsors have both the incentive and the opportunity to manage the overall value of their ecosystems, and that selective promotion of individual complements is a central instrument for doing so. Through promotion a sponsor can reward successful complements, draw attention to underappreciated ones, shape consumers' perception of the ecosystem's depth and breadth, induce loyalty among powerful complementors, time promotion to lift sales in slow periods, and reduce competitive interaction among complements. The authors develop hypotheses about when a platform will promote a given complement and test them on data from the console video-game industry in the United Kingdom. They find that platforms do not simply promote the best complements; they invest strategically in ways that manage trade-offs in ecosystem value. The contribution is to treat visibility allocation as a governance lever rather than a neutral reflection of quality, which reframes ranking and featuring as choices made in the sponsor's interest.
