## Summary
Fitz and Scheeg study how small and medium-sized enterprises that sell through dominant digital platforms cope with the power asymmetry of that relationship. Using a multiple case study of 14 SME value providers on transaction-based platforms, they document the challenges such firms encounter, including exploitative relationships, eroded entrepreneurial autonomy, surveillance pressure, unreliable and arbitrary partnership terms, increased dependence, and the devaluation of the SME's contribution. Coping practices cluster into disintermediation (prioritising direct marketing and sales), multi-homing and partnering with alternative or more equitable platforms, individual resistance tactics, establishing relationships with platform outsiders, and collectivising counter-power in interest groups. The authors identify three relationality types in how SMEs see platforms: as partners, as tools, and as a necessary evil. The background section, drawing on Cutolo and Kenney, lists algorithmic ranking, platform competition on its own platform, changing terms, and the threat of delisting as inherent risks.

## Use in the chapter
Proposed by the evidence audit for §1 (S1: second source for the ISV, merchant, and creator examples; "SMEs, delisting threat") and for §3.2 (S7: the chapter overstates Cutolo and Kenney when it says "no alternative platform exists at the required scale" and "diversification cannot relieve"; the audit says soften and add Fitz and Scheeg, whose cases show multi-homing and disintermediation as real if costly coping practices). Role: second source for the dependence claims in §1 and §3.2; also a mild corrective.

## Key quotations
- "The findings reveal that power asymmetry between dominant DPs and SMEs creates significant challenges, including exploitative relationships, loss of autonomy, surveillance pressures, and devaluation of SME contributions, while SMEs cope through disintermediation, multi-homing, individual resistance tactics, and striving for more equitable DP models." (Abstract)
- "Inherent threats for SMEs include separation of platform-dependent businesses from their customers, algorithmic management through ratings, rankings, and recommendations, DP owners entering competition on their own platform, changing terms of participation, and finally the threat of getting delisted or banned from a platform." (Background)
