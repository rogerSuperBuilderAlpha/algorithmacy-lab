## Summary
Written from the publisher description only. Zuboff's book names and analyses "surveillance capitalism," which she presents as an unprecedented form of power in which firms claim human experience as free raw material, render it as behavioural data, and use the surplus to build prediction products sold in markets for future behaviour. The publisher describes the book as the first detailed examination of that form of power and of the quest by powerful corporations to predict and control our behaviour. The argument runs from the discovery of behavioural surplus at Google, through the extension of extraction to the physical world, to what Zuboff calls instrumentarian power, which works by tuning and herding populations rather than by coercion. Its relevance to the chapter is narrow: the observation that the objectives encoded in a platform's systems are the firm's, and that those objectives can diverge sharply from the interests of the people whose behaviour the systems shape.

## Use in the chapter
§3.1 cites it for the claim that the platform's system "optimizes for the firm's ends, which can diverge sharply from the interests of the workers, sellers, and users enrolled in it (Zuboff, 2019)." The audit (S5) rates the fit as loose, since Zuboff's subject is consumer data extraction and behavioural prediction rather than the management of workers and sellers through ranking, and recommends Rahman, Karunakaran and Cameron (2024), already in the list, as the primary source.
