## Summary
Anderson's Tanner Lectures argue that the modern workplace is a form of private government: employers hold sweeping, largely unaccountable authority over workers' speech, dress, manners and much of their off-duty conduct, and public discourse has no vocabulary for this because it treats the labor contract as an exchange between free equals. She traces the egalitarian hopes that early market advocates from Locke and Smith to Lincoln attached to free labor, shows how the Industrial Revolution broke the link between market freedom and personal independence, and argues that the ideology of free markets making workers free has survived the facts that refuted it. The publisher's description records that one in four American workers describes the workplace as a dictatorship. The volume includes responses from David Bromwich, Tyler Cowen, Ann Hughes and Niko Kolodny, and Anderson's reply. Her central remedy is to name employer authority as government and ask what constitutional limits and worker voice it should be subject to.
