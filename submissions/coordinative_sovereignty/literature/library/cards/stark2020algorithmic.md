## Summary
Stark and Pais argue that the platform is the distinctive organizational form of the early twenty-first century and give it a coordination verb of its own: where actors in markets contract, in hierarchies command and in networks collaborate, platforms co-opt assets, resources and activities that are not part of the firm. This creates a distinctive managerial problem, how to manage value-creating activity that happens on the platform but not in the firm. Their answer is algorithmic management in a triangular geometry: platform owners co-opt the behaviour of providers and users, enrolling both in management practices without any delegation of managerial authority. Ratings and other activities of providers and consumers, undertaken on their own behalf, are translated algorithmically into rankings and calculating devices that circulate through feedback loops the authors call twisted rather than circular, so that at each fold accountability can be deflected and denied. Where Scientific Management legitimated a new managerial class, algorithmic management is reshaping that class, and its organizational asymmetries connect to regulatory coalitions of owners, investors and consumers.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §3 with Stark and Vanden Broeck (2024): "Stark and Pais (2020) ... read platforms as a fourth term: where actors in hierarchies command, in markets contract, and in networks collaborate, on platforms they are co-opted". The role is support for the fourth-form framing. The audit (S4) proposes it also for §3.1, where the triad is currently attributed to Kellogg and Möhlmann, who do not frame it as triadic; the "triangular geometry" sentence is the direct source for the chapter's mediated triad. The gaps file (C4) notes that Kornberger, Pflueger and Mouritsen (2017) state the fourth form three years earlier and should be cited beside Stark. The "twisted feedback loop" in which accountability is deflected is also the closest statement in the literature to the chapter's §3.2 claim about standing.
