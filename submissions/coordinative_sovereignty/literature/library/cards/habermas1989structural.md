## Summary
Written from the publisher description and the chapter's own account. Habermas's first major book is a historical-sociological study of the origins, nature and evolution of the bourgeois public sphere: the realm, emerging in eighteenth-century Britain, France and Germany, in which private persons came together as a public to reason about matters of common concern and, through print, coffee houses and salons, to subject state authority to the test of public argument. The public sphere mediated between civil society and the state and made public opinion a source of legitimacy. The second half of the book traces the sphere's "structural transformation" under mass media, organized interests and the welfare state, as critical publicity gave way to manipulative publicity and the reasoning public to a consuming one. The publisher presents it as Habermas's most concrete historical work and as foreshadowing his later concerns with legitimation and communication.

## Use in the chapter
§1 cites it for the chapter's founding pairing: "Habermas (1962/1989) traced the pairing for the literate state, where private persons reasoning together in print constituted a sphere in which public power could be called to answer." §2.2 repeats the point: "A literate public could read what the state wrote, lay one pronouncement beside another, and apply critical reason to authority (Habermas, 1962/1989)."
