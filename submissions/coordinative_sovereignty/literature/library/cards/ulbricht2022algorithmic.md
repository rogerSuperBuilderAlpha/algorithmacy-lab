## Summary
The article is the editors' synthesis of a Regulation & Governance special issue assessing Yeung's 2017 concept of algorithmic regulation as a mode of social coordination and control. The authors identify three changes since the concept was proposed: the explosion and diversification of scholarship on algorithms and data across disciplines, the increased take-up of algorithmic regulation in the public sector, accelerated by the pandemic, and growing awareness of its hazards together with recognition of the need for limits. They then draw out the themes that emerge across the contributions, what is distinctive about the concept, its strengths and limits, and its relation to the wider field. The conclusion is that the concept is valuable and maturing: it has become an analytical bridge that supports cross-disciplinary work, enriching its early skeletal form and permitting context-sensitive analysis of algorithmic regulation in concrete settings while also enabling critical reflection on the legitimacy of existing and proposed regimes. The concept covers both regulation through algorithms and regulation of them.
