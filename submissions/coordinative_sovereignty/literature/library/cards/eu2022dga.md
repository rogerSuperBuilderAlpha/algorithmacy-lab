## Summary
The Data Governance Act lays down conditions for the re-use of certain categories of protected data held by public sector bodies, a notification and supervisory framework for providers of data intermediation services, a framework for the voluntary registration of entities that collect and process data made available for altruistic purposes, and a European Data Innovation Board. The part that matters for the chapter is Chapter III on data intermediation services. Recital 32 explains the purpose: to increase trust in such services, in particular as regards the use of data and compliance with the conditions set by data subjects and data holders, the Regulation creates a Union-level framework of highly harmonised requirements for their trustworthy provision. Intermediaries must be structurally separated from any other service, may not use the data they intermediate for their own purposes, and must act in the interest of the data subjects and data holders whose data they handle. The Act thus gives a registered legal form to the collective intermediaries, including data cooperatives, that the chapter discusses as a route to standing.

## Use in the chapter
§7 states: "The European Data Governance Act has since created a registered category of data intermediary, and the November 2025 Digital Omnibus proposal would repeal that Act and move the data-intermediation rules into the Data Act." Audit U5 requires a reference-list entry and article pinpoints (Chapter III, Articles 10 to 15) at the first mention. The chapter's own caveat, that evidence on whether the mechanism delivers standing "is not yet in", is consistent with LITERATURE_GAPS, which finds no empirical work on DGA intermediaries conferring bargaining standing. Role: legal instrument.
