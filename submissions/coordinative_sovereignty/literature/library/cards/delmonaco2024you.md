## Summary
The paper studies shadowbanning, the practice by which platforms reduce the visibility of content without notifying the user, from the side of marginalised users who experience it and whose claims platforms often deny. Using qualitative surveys and interviews, the authors examine how these users make sense of unclear reductions in reach, develop folk theories about what triggers suppression, and try to prove that it is happening. They find that users collaboratively develop and test algorithmic folk theories, pooling observations and running informal experiments, a practice the authors name collaborative algorithm investigation. Participants reported frustration, reduced engagement, an inability to post certain content, and financial consequences, and many attributed their shadowbans to deliberate suppression of queer, non-white and other marginalised creators. Some adapted their posting behaviour to avoid further suppression. The paper closes with design and policy recommendations on transparency and appeal for visibility moderation.
