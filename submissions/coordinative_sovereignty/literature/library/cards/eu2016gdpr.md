## Summary
The General Data Protection Regulation is the EU's general framework for the processing of personal data, in force since 25 May 2018. It sets out principles of lawful processing, a catalogue of data-subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection), obligations on controllers and processors, the supervisory-authority and consistency architecture, and an administrative-fine regime. Article 22(1) gives the data subject the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal or similarly significant effects; paragraph 2 carves out contractual necessity, legal authorisation, and explicit consent; paragraph 3 requires, in the contract and consent cases, safeguards including at least the right to obtain human intervention, to express a view, and to contest the decision. Article 15(1)(h) adds a right to meaningful information about the logic involved. Recent CJEU judgments (SCHUFA, Dun & Bradstreet Austria) have expanded the reach of these provisions.

## Use in the chapter
Characterised in §1 and §3.2 and throughout without a reference-list entry. §3.2: "Article 22 of the GDPR belongs to a different family: it gives a natural person subject to a solely automated decision the right to obtain human intervention and to contest the outcome." §7 repeats the point as "European data-protection law sets a floor". The audit (U5) asks for a formal entry and pinpoint citations to Art. 22(1) and (3); U10 asks that "Its threshold is high" be reworded in light of the two judgments. Role: legal instrument.

## Key quotations
- "The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her." (Article 22(1))
