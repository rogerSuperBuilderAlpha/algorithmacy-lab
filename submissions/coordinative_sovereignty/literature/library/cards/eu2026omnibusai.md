## Summary
Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, adopted 8 July 2026, published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and in force from 27 July 2026, is the "Digital Omnibus on AI." It amends the AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) together with the machinery and aviation regulations to simplify implementation of the harmonised AI rules. For the chapter the material change is Article 1, point (5), which replaces Article 4 on AI literacy. The former duty on providers and deployers to "take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy" becomes a duty to "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of staff and others operating AI systems on their behalf, with an express proviso that the obligation "does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual." A new paragraph 2 tasks the Commission and Member States with supporting compliance, particularly for SMEs, through published practical examples, and paragraph 3 tasks the AI Board with recommendations drawing on European competence frameworks and setting common objectives.

## Use in the chapter
§9 states that "the AI Act obliges providers and deployers to ensure a level of AI literacy among staff," citing it as an early compulsory analogue of the competence the chapter calls algorithmacy. The audit (E3) marks this as outdated: after 27 July 2026 the duty is to support the development of AI literacy, not to ensure it, and the regulation expressly disclaims any guaranteed level. The sentence should be reworded and should cite Regulation 2024/1689 Art. 4 "as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744." Role: legal instrument. Do not confuse it with the data Digital Omnibus, COM(2025) 837, which remains a proposal and is the instrument that would repeal the P2B Regulation and the DGA.

## Key quotations
"Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf ... This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual." (Art. 1(5), new Art. 4(1))
"The Board shall adopt recommendations, taking into account European competence frameworks, to support the Commission and Member States in the promotion of AI literacy required under paragraph 1, including by setting out common objectives." (Art. 1(5), new Art. 4(3))
