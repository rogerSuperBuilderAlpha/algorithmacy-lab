## Summary
Loh and Elsas-Nicolle ask how the entry of a rival platform changes the strategies of an incumbent's complementors, who face a trade-off: entry threatens the indirect network benefits they enjoy on the incumbent, but it also lets them escape intense within-platform competition. They study the Epic Games Store's December 2018 entry into PC game distribution, until then dominated by Steam, and examine two complementor choices: whether to multihome by joining Epic, and whether to keep participating in Steam's platform-wide sales promotions, the incumbent's main orchestration tool. The trade-off does not resolve uniformly. Complementors with weaker strategic resources, independent developers, were more likely to multihome and became less responsive to Steam's orchestration; complementors more reliant on network effects, multiplayer developers, were less likely to multihome and became more responsive. The authors conclude that platform competition reshapes complementor motivations heterogeneously, so that a platform's success does not benefit all its complementors equally and not all take an exit when one opens.
