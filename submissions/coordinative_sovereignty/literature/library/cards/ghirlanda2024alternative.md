## Summary
Ghirlanda and Kirov present what they describe as the first comprehensive systematic literature review of platform cooperativism, the idea, coined by Trebor Scholz, that platforms can be run on cooperative principles of democratic governance and shared ownership. Their review organises a dispersed, interdisciplinary literature through stakeholder theory, mapping how workers, users, founders, and communities can be brought into an extended, decentralised, and heterarchical governance structure and where strategic complementarities between them lie. A second aim is to reconnect the platform cooperativism debate with the older theory-of-the-firm literature on the competitive advantages and weaknesses of cooperatives relative to capitalist firms. The authors argue that multi-stakeholder platform cooperatives could, under certain conditions, combine efficiency with equitable outcomes, while also cataloguing the challenges the cooperative model carries (capital, scale, degeneration). They note the field is still emerging and their review is intended as a baseline for updates.
