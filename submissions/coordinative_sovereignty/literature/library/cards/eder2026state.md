## Summary
The post, written by staff of the certified dispute-settlement body User Rights and the consultancy Tremau, takes stock of the first full year of out-of-court dispute settlement under Article 21 of the Digital Services Act. Nine bodies have been certified across eight member states and five have published transparency reports; together those five handled tens of thousands of complaints across more than a dozen platforms and all 27 member states. On the merits, the bodies frequently find against platforms: User Rights reports that 84.4 percent of concluded proceedings judged the platform's original decision unjustified, and Appeals Centre Europe decided for the user in 59 percent of reviewable cases. The central finding is that redress depends on platform participation, which is uneven: TikTok implemented body recommendations in roughly 90 percent of cases while Meta platforms implemented around 13 percent, with complainant satisfaction tracking the same gap. The authors conclude that the mechanism delivers meaningful redress where platforms engage, and that participation is where progress is most needed.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the current draft. The gaps file and strand 3 mark it MUST for §7, where the chapter says the DSA's "internal complaint-handling and certified out-of-court dispute-settlement bodies (Articles 20–21) institutionalize case-level contestation at statutory scale" without evidence on whether the channel delivers. The role is support, and specifically the only first-year data on whether DSA contestation produces outcomes; the 13 to 90 percent implementation spread is exactly the "thin case-level channel" the §7 table describes and shows that a channel with standing on paper still depends on the platform's willingness to honour it. It is grey literature by participants in the system, which the chapter should signal. It pairs with the Commission's P2B review (COM(2023) 525) on mediation being virtually unused.
