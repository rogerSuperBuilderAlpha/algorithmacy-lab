## Summary
The authors extend Māori data sovereignty principles to algorithms. Their starting point is the spread of data technologies and algorithms used for decision-making and decision support in Aotearoa New Zealand, and the need for frameworks that maximise the opportunities while minimising the risks. For algorithms that use Māori data the risks are heightened by systemic biases inherent in data and in algorithm development. Because an algorithm can be framed as a particular use of data, existing data frameworks can be extended to cover it. The paper therefore reworks the well-known Māori data sovereignty principles and sub-principles to address responsible algorithms from a Māori perspective, yielding the Māori algorithmic sovereignty principles. The core idea is that algorithms that use Māori data or are applied to Māori individuals are subject to the laws and governance structures of Māori. The paper defines the idea, presents the updated principles, and sets out a strategy for detecting and minimising bias, including what it calls colonising bias, in the algorithm development process.
