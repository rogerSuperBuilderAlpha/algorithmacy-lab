## Summary
The Ninth Circuit's December 2025 opinion reviews the district court's finding that Apple was in civil contempt of the 2021 anti-steering injunction from the original Epic litigation. Apple had responded to the injunction by permitting links to outside purchase options while charging a 27 percent commission on purchases made through such links and imposing design restrictions that made the links unattractive. The court affirmed the contempt finding, holding that Apple willfully set a commission rate that in practice made every alternative to its own payment system economically non-viable, and it affirmed the findings of bad faith. It reversed, however, the district court's permanent prohibition on any commission for linked-out purchases, treating a zero-commission rule as an impermissible civil contempt sanction rather than a coercive remedy. The court remanded for the district court to set a framework under which Apple may charge a reasonable, non-prohibitive commission tied to its actual costs, while insisting that Apple must be able to purge its contempt by compliance.

Strand 6 and the gaps report list it as a MUST for §5.2 and §8 because the opinion draws the chapter's own split: the prohibition on steering developers to an outside purchase edge is struck down as a gate, while a priced rail for the work Apple actually does (cost-based commission) survives. Section 5.2 already argues that "the rail may still do authorizing and trust work the open edge does not reproduce," so the case is direct non-EU corroboration. Role: legal instrument. It also qualifies §8's remark that enforcement reaches platform conduct without reaching the ranking layer: here a court reached the payment gate but left the commission question to a cost-based remand.
