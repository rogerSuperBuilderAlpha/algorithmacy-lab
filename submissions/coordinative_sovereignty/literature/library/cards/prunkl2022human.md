## Summary
This short comment piece observes that current AI policy recommendations disagree about what the risks to human autonomy are, and argues that the disagreement stems from the unexamined complexity of the concept of autonomy itself. Prunkl separates strands that policy documents run together: autonomy as rational self-government, autonomy as the capacity to act on one's own values, the difference between the exercise of autonomy in a choice and the possession of the capacity for it, and the distinction between direct threats (manipulation, deception, nudging) and structural threats (dependence on systems that remove the occasion for choice). Her conclusion is that governance solutions must be adapted to the particular dimension of autonomy at stake rather than invoking the value as an undifferentiated good, and that policymakers must confront the philosophical complexity before designing protections. The piece is conceptual and brief; it functions as a map of the autonomy literature for an AI-governance readership rather than as an argument for a single position.
