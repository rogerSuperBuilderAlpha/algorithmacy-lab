## Summary
Van Doorn and Badger argue that gig work under financialized platform capitalism is governed by what they call dual value production: the monetary value of the service a worker delivers is augmented by the use value and speculative value of the data produced before, during and after the job. App-governed gig workers are thus conduits in software systems that turn urban activity into machine-readable data and produce data as an asset class. The argument rests on the authors' own ethnographic and autoethnographic work: van Doorn spent two years on food delivery and domestic cleaning platforms in New York, Berlin and Amsterdam, working as a courier and cleaner, and Badger did nine months of delivery work in London and eighteen months inside a grassroots union organizing gig workers. They reject the human-capital framing in which workers could accumulate their own data, survey collectivist strategies for data-centric worker empowerment, and close by identifying the meta-platform, the venture and sovereign-wealth capital that bankrolls and coordinates platforms across sectors, as the most formidable obstacle to worker power.
