## Summary
Smith and Burrows examine the influence of neoreactionary (NRx) thinking, associated with Curtis Yarvin, Nick Land, Peter Thiel and Patri Friedman, on contemporary political debate as it appears in what they call "architectures of exit". Their case is Urbit, a digital architecture built on NRx ideas, which they read as capturing how post-neoliberal politics imagines freedom and sovereignty through the micro-fracturing of nation-states into "gov-corps". They trace the development of NRx philosophy and place it within recent political and technological change in order to theorize the significance of exit as manifested in the notion of "dynamic geographies". Their conclusion is that even if programmes such as Urbit never succeed, they and speculative ventures like seasteading express broader post-neoliberal NRx imaginaries that were prefigured a quarter century earlier in The Sovereign Individual. The article is a cultural-theoretical reading of a political current and its software, not a study of platform dependence.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited; cut from the chapter on 2026-08-01. The July bib note explains why: the article's "exit" is the neoreactionary project of leaving the nation-state through sovereign software, whereas the chapter's exit is Hirschman's, the coordinated actor's power to leave a contingent mediator. The word overlap is the only connection, and citing it in §5.3 or §6 would invite the wrong reading. The role is optional at most: it could appear in Key Terms as a neighbouring and opposed use of "sovereignty" and "exit", to mark that coordinative sovereignty is not a secessionist construct. Mueller (2020) is the better cite if the chapter wants a critic of sovereignty talk in cyberspace.
