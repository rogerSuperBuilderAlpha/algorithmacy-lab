## Summary
Scholz's pamphlet criticizes the so-called sharing economy of Uber, Airbnb and Mechanical Turk as an extractive model that concentrates wealth in platform owners while shifting risk onto contingent workers without protections. Against it he proposes platform cooperativism: digital marketplaces and service platforms owned and democratically governed by the workers, users or municipalities who depend on them, combining the cooperative tradition with internet technology. He sets out ten principles for a fair labour platform, among them collective ownership, decent pay, transparency, appreciation of work, co-determined design, a protective legal framework and a rejection of excessive surveillance, and catalogues early examples such as Loconomics, Stocksy and Union Taxi. The closing sections discuss obstacles: financing, governance, design capacity and scale, with the suggestion that cooperatives might target smaller local niche markets. The text is advocacy rather than research and was later expanded in Scholz's 2023 book Own This!

## Use in the chapter
Section 7 cites it: cooperatives "would give coordinated actors a share in governing the mediation by making them owners (Scholz, 2016)," followed by "The limits are scale and capital." Role: support for ownership as a route to standing. Audit S27 flags that the scale-and-capital limit is only loosely present in Scholz (the report mentions financing and niche markets but does not analyse the limit) and proposes Mannan and Pek (2024) for it; strand 4 notes that Scholz (2023) updates the 2016 text and that Bunders and De Moor (2024) and Ghirlanda and Kirov (2024) supply the empirical record.
