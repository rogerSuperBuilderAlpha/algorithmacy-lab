## Summary
Mariotto and Verdier model a monopolistic platform that intermediates between sellers and consumers while sellers retain the option of selling directly. The platform delivers a higher-quality service to consumers than the direct channel and may generate efficiency gains for sellers, and the question is whether it prefers to impose price parity, forbidding sellers from charging less on their own channel. They show that the platform imposes parity when consumer heterogeneity is high relative to the service quality of the seller side. The restriction lowers the total transaction fee paid by consumers who buy from sellers with low transaction costs, so consumers buying from such sellers pay a lower purchase price, while the effect on average consumer surplus can go either way. The paper places parity clauses inside a competition between the platform and the merchants it serves over who supplies the sales service, which ties the parity question to the bypass question rather than treating it as pure rent extraction.
