## Summary
Where network studies usually infer employees' agency from observed changes in ties, Yang and colleagues asked employees directly whom they wished to collaborate with more closely to do their jobs better. The setting is a bespoke advanced-manufacturing firm (141 respondents), and the analysis uses logistic regression QAP with MRQAP and ERGM robustness checks on the directed formal workflow network. The argument is that formal structure creates workflow dependencies as people gather inputs from others, and that individuals want to alter their networks to manage those dependencies in two ways: strengthening ties with existing formal partners, or bypassing, forming ties outside the prescribed workflow. Tie-strengthening was sought with partners already trusted to deliver reliable, high-quality inputs. Bypassing ties were chosen to reduce dependence on upstream alters, either by picking a structurally equivalent alternative or by reaching two steps upstream to close disadvantageous structural holes; a tie that disintermediates several brokers at once was desired more than one that closes a single hole. The discussion connects this to structural-hole decay and broker disintermediation, listing awareness of being brokered by someone profiting from the position (Hahl et al. 2016) among the known triggers, and adding formal dependence as a further one.
