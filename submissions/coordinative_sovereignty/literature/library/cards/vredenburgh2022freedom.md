## Summary
Vredenburgh identifies a neglected normative dimension of algorithmic opacity in the workplace and the labour market. The standard case for explanation is instrumental: workers need explanations to contest decisions or protect their interests. She argues that explanations of algorithms and of algorithmic decisions are also of non-instrumental value, because explanations of the structure and function of parts of the social world are the basis on which people reflectively clarify their practical orientation toward the institutions that play a central role in their lives. Understanding is, on this account, a precondition of a certain kind of freedom at work. Using that account, she diagnoses distinctive normative defects that reliance on AI can encourage in workplaces and economic institutions: where workers cannot understand the systems that allocate, evaluate and direct their work, they are alienated from those institutions in a sense that survives even fair outcomes. The paper is analytic political philosophy and sits beside her companion piece on the right to explanation.

## Use in the chapter
Not cited in the current draft. Strand 4 marks it MUST for §4 and §6. The role is support for the chapter's claim that algorithmacy, the competence to read and act on an opaque coordinating system, is not merely useful but constitutive of standing: Vredenburgh supplies the philosophical argument that understanding has non-instrumental value for freedom, which the chapter currently asserts through the literacy analogy alone. In §6 it also bridges the republican account of domination, which is about control, to Markell's involvement dimension, since alienation from institutions one cannot understand is a failure of involvement rather than of protection. The chapter should note that her subject is the worker, so extension to the platform-dependent firm is its own step.
