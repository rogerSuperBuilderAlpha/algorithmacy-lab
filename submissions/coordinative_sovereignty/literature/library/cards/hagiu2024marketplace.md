## Summary
Hagiu and Wright model leakage, the problem a marketplace faces when buyers use it to discover a seller or to obtain conveniences and the seller then takes the transaction off the marketplace to avoid its fees. With heterogeneous buyer switching costs the model yields partial leakage in equilibrium: some transactions stay on the platform and some leave. The paper then evaluates the instruments a marketplace can use to limit leakage and shows when each is profitable and how each affects welfare: investing in transaction benefits that make on-platform dealing more attractive, restricting communication between buyers and sellers, charging referral fees rather than transaction fees, imposing price-parity clauses, inducing competition among sellers, and removing sellers who encourage leakage. The central result is that leakage-prevention instruments are substitutes for one another and that a platform's choice among them depends on the distribution of switching costs and on whether its value lies in discovery or in transaction services.
