## Summary
Cotter studies conscious, instrumental engagement with algorithms as a way into the extent and limits of algorithmic power. Through a thematic analysis of online discussions among Instagram influencers she finds that the pursuit of influence takes the form of a game whose rules are encoded in the platform's algorithms. Influencers' interpretations of Instagram's algorithmic architecture act as the lens through which they read and mechanize those rules. Two interpretations dominate, each combining what influencers glean about the algorithms with discourses already present in influencer communities about authenticity and entrepreneurship. The article argues that attending to the visibility game makes visible the interdependence of users, algorithms and platform owners, and shows that algorithms structure user behavior without unilaterally determining it. It belongs with Bucher and Petre et al. in the line of work on how producers model an opaque ranking system and adjust their conduct to it.

## Use in the chapter

(2019) for the claim that platforms treat attempts to work the system as illegitimate "gaming" and that "the accusation falls hardest on producers who are not already" established. The audit (S13) marks this as single-sourced and proposes Cotter 2019 as the second source. Role: second source for the gaming and visibility claim, and a further instance of producers building folk models of a platform's ranking. It is also the natural companion to the chapter's §4 account of algorithmic literacy as a practice rather than a survey construct.
