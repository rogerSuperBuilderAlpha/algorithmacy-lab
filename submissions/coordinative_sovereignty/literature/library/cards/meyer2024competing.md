## Summary
Meyer, Kerkhof, Cennamo and Kretschmer challenge the assumption that platforms benefit smaller firms by widening reach and visibility. That logic holds only if the platform's function is to match consumer preferences to complement characteristics. They argue that platforms also broker attention toward some complements at the expense of others, and that this attention mechanism dominates where complement characteristics cannot be observed before consumption. In such settings complementors with larger scale and broader scope capture attention, because they are better known, reputationally cued, and become the default option on a crowded platform. The authors formalize and test this in online news aggregators, which feature newspapers of different sizes and scope. Large, generalist newspapers benefit most from being on an aggregator, while small, focused outlets perform better when not featured on the aggregator at all. The finding reframes platform participation as a contest for attention whose terms the platform sets and which the already large tend to win.
