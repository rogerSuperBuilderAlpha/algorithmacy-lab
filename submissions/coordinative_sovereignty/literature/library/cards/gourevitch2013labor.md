## Summary
Gourevitch reconstructs the position of nineteenth-century American labor republicans, who argued that wage labor was incompatible with republican liberty and that cooperative production was needed to secure liberty in economic as well as political life. He then defends that position against contemporary neo-republicanism. His charge is that neo-republican remedies for economic domination, chiefly a universal basic income and a guaranteed right of exit from any particular employer, cannot reach the structural domination that a labor market as a whole imposes on those who must sell their labor; exit from one employer delivers the worker to another with the same powers. The concept of structural domination is what makes the labor republican tradition relevant to modern labor markets. On this reading freedom requires more than escape from labor: it requires control over productive assets and a share in the organization of the workplace, which is an inclusion claim rather than only a protection claim.

## Use in the chapter
Section 6 uses Markell (2008) for the point that non-domination "protects without including" and E. Anderson (2017) for the point that the right to quit does not dissolve employer authority. The audit (S23) finds each single-sourced and proposes Gourevitch 2013 as a second source that covers both: the insufficiency of exit and the demand for a share in control. Role: second source for both claims. Gourevitch also supplies the structural-domination vocabulary the chapter uses when it argues that a ranking system's power can be arbitrary even though the system is not an agent, which links to Cicerchia 2022 and Hasan 2021.
