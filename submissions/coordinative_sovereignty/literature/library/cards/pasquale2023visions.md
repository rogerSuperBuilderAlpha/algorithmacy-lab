## Summary
Only the opening of the abstract was visible this session: the chapter "describes a shift from territorial to functional sovereignty online, as private firms increasingly assume the prerogatives of governan[ce]." The contrast it names is between territorial sovereignty, the state's claim to govern data and networks within its borders, and functional sovereignty, the condition in which platform firms exercise governance-like prerogatives over the parties dependent on the functions they control. The body of the chapter and the remainder of the abstract were not opened, so the argument beyond that framing is not summarized here.

## Use in the chapter
Cited in §3.2 with Cohen 2017 for the standing gap that disclosure cannot close, and in §6: "Functional sovereignty (Pasquale, 2023) and the platform sovereign (Cohen, 2017) theorize the authority platforms exercise over the coordinated. Coordinative sovereignty looks at the same relationship from the other end." Role: support, and the nearest predecessor construct. The chapter's move is to shift the subject of sovereignty from the platform, which holds functional sovereignty, to the coordinated actor, who seeks standing within it; the card reader should see Cohen 2017, Lehdonvirta 2022 and Törnberg 2023 for the same platform-as-sovereign framing, and strand 7's note that "platform sovereignty" is not owned as a title term.
