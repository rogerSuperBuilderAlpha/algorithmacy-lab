## Summary
A survey of 1,014 hotels in Austria, Germany, and Switzerland examining how many distribution channels hotels use and what role each plays as new intermediaries enter the market. Hotels operate an average of 8.06 offline and online channel categories; traditional booking methods remain significant, but roughly one fifth of reservations originate entirely online, and hotels work with an average of 3.61 online travel agencies. The study finds an oligopolistic OTA market structure, with Swiss and German hotels more dependent on OTAs than Austrian ones, and a cluster analysis identifies four distinct portfolio strategies: multi-channel, electronic, real-time, and traditional distributor approaches. The paper's contribution for the platform-dependence literature is to measure dependency directly, channel by channel and country by country, showing that reliance on a few dominant intermediaries coexists with wide but shallow channel portfolios that do not, in practice, dilute the OTAs' hold.

## Use in the chapter
Audit S19 proposes it for §5.5, where the chapter's claim that "later work has not consistently reproduced" the billboard effect rests on Beritelli and Schegg (2016) alone. Role: second source for the hotel-OTA worked case: it documents empirically the dependency on an oligopolistic OTA tier that the chapter's necessary-mediator diagnosis presupposes, and its portfolio evidence shows that multihoming across many channels does not restore exit when demand aggregation is concentrated in a few agencies. Pair it in the text with Beritelli and Schegg 2016 and O'Connor 2025.
