## Summary
The Platform Work Directive is the first EU instrument addressed to algorithmic management as such. Chapter II establishes a rebuttable legal presumption of employment: where facts indicating direction and control are found, the relationship between a digital labour platform and a person performing platform work is presumed to be employment, with the burden on the platform to rebut (Art. 5). Chapter III regulates automated monitoring and decision-making systems for all platform workers regardless of status: limits on the data that may be processed, transparency and information duties (Art. 13), human oversight, a right to human review of significant decisions with written reasons, and health and safety obligations. Chapter IV provides for information and consultation of worker representatives. Chapter V covers enforcement and dispute resolution (Art. 25), including rights for representatives to act on workers' behalf, and Article 29 fixes transposition by 2 December 2026. The directive thus attaches both a status remedy and a procedural accountability regime to the algorithmic mediator.

## Use in the chapter
Section 8 cites it as the EU instrument that reaches algorithmic management directly, with the note that no member state had fully transposed it at the time of writing. Role: legal instrument. The audit (U18) asks for a dated tracker and an "as of August 2026" stamp; Ius Laboris 2026 is the proposed source. Aloisi 2024 is the scholarly companion on regulating algorithmic management at work, and the chapter's §7 table of remedies should distinguish the status presumption (exit from the contractor category) from the Chapter III rights (voice within the mediation).

## Key quotations
"The contractual relationship between a digital labour platform and a person performing platform work through that platform shall be legally presumed to be an employment relationship where facts indicating direction and control, in accordance with national law, collective agreements or practice in force in the Member States and with consideration to the case-law of the Court of Justice, are found." (Art. 5(1))
