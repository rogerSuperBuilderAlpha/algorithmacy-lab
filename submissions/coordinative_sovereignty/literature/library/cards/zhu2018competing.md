## Summary
An empirical study of when a platform owner enters its complementors' product spaces and competes against them, using two rounds of data collection on Amazon.com. Amazon entered 3% of third-party sellers' product spaces over a ten-month period, and entry is more likely for successful spaces, products with higher sales and better reviews, and less likely for spaces requiring greater seller effort to grow or already using Amazon's fulfillment service, showing that complementors' platform-specific investments shape the owner's entry decisions. Using propensity-score matching, the authors find that entry increases product demand and reduces shipping costs for consumers, but discourages affected third-party sellers from subsequently pursuing growth on the platform. The discussion notes that sellers lack the market power to retaliate or deter entry, and that direct entry is only the most visible form of squeezing complementors, alongside fee increases and terms-and-conditions rejections used by other platforms.
