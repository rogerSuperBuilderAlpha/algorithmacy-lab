## Summary
Klonick examines how the major private platforms, Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube, moderate the speech of their users, and argues that they have become a new kind of governor of online expression. Drawing on interviews with former and current content-moderation staff and on internal documents, she reconstructs the people, rules, and processes involved: the origins of moderation policy in American free-speech norms carried by early lawyers, the elaborate internal rule systems and their revision in response to public pressure, and the layered human and automated processes by which rules are applied at scale. She characterises these as systems of governance shaped by three forces, American free-speech culture, corporate responsibility and commercial interest, and user expectations, and argues that while the platforms are responsive to users in some ways, they remain largely unaccountable, outside both state-action doctrine and any meaningful procedural framework. The piece is foundational for the platform-governance literature.
