## Summary
Bucher asks where and how ordinary people meet algorithms: in what situations they become aware of them, how they make sense of systems that are largely hidden, and whether that awareness changes how they use the platform. The study draws on tweets about the Facebook algorithm and interviews with 25 ordinary users, and from those personal stories develops the notion of the algorithmic imaginary, the ways of thinking about what algorithms are, what they should be, and how they function. The central argument is that the imaginary is not only productive of moods and sensations, such as the unease of seeing an old photo resurface, but plays a generative role in moulding the Facebook algorithm itself, because users act on their beliefs and the system learns from those actions. Examining how algorithms make people feel is therefore presented as essential to understanding their social power, and the paper is a founding reference for later work on folk theories and algorithmic literacy.
