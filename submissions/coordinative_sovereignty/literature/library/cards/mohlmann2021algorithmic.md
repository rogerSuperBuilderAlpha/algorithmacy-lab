## Summary
The paper theorizes algorithmic management on online labor platforms as operating along two dimensions at once: matching, through which the platform accommodates market needs by pairing workers with counterparties, and control, through which it monitors and tightly disciplines how platform work is executed. Where prior research attended mainly to optimized matching, the authors show the two dimensions are conjoined in practice. Using a qualitative grounded-theory study of Uber drivers' perceptions, supplemented by interviews with Uber executives and engineers, they find that in the context of both algorithmic matching and algorithmic control workers experience tensions around work execution, compensation, and belonging, and that these tensions trigger two families of worker response, market-like behaviors and organization-like behaviors. The contribution is a grounded theory of algorithmic management in which the platform's market-making and its managerial control are a single fused apparatus rather than separate functions.
