# Deep research: sovereignty, autonomy, and the platform condition

<!-- run: 2026-07-01 | three parallel sweeps: previous forms; digital-sovereignty variants; platform power & near-synonyms -->

The project asks whether there is a kind of autonomy proper to a world in which most coordination runs
through platforms one cannot leave. This report maps what sovereignty and autonomy have meant, what digital
sovereignty means now, and where a coordination-native construct would sit. It reads the field along one axis:
whether a tradition treats autonomy as something to **preserve against** outside forces or as something
**constituted through** relationships.

## Previous forms divide into preservationist and relational

The dominant line is preservationist. Bodin's sovereignty is supreme and indivisible [bodin1576], Hobbes's is
the undivided authority that ends the war of all against all [hobbes1651leviathan], and Westphalian
sovereignty makes non-interference the mark of a state. Rousseau relocates sovereignty to the people
[rousseau1762] and Mill relocates liberty to the individual [mill1859liberty], and both keep the shape:
autonomy is the absence of external control, and to be sovereign is to stand apart. Berlin's negative liberty
[berlin1958] states the form cleanly. The historical scholarship shows the preservationist ideal was always
partly a myth — Osiander demonstrates the Westphalian system was retrospectively invented [osiander2001westphalia],
and Krasner calls state sovereignty "organized hypocrisy," honored as often in the breach [krasner1999sovereignty].

A second line treats autonomy as constituted through relationships. Pettit's republican freedom is
non-domination, the absence of another's arbitrary power, which is a relational and institutional condition
rather than mere non-interference [pettit1997republicanism; skinner1998liberty]. Nedelsky's relational
autonomy holds that persons become autonomous with others, through the relationships and laws that structure
them, so the vocabulary of boundaries and independence mistakes what autonomy is [nedelsky2011;
mackenzie2000relational]. Markell sharpens the target: non-domination is insufficient because it secures
against arbitrary power without securing a share in the relationships that constitute one — it wants
involvement, not only protection [markell2008]. Indigenous data governance states the relational claim
institutionally, as authority exercised through responsibility within relationships rather than as exclusive
control [carroll2020care]. This second line is the precursor the project builds on.

## Digital sovereignty centralizes control

Digital sovereignty, in its several variants, is a preservationist move applied to the digital. The European
framing seeks control over infrastructure, data, and standards to reduce dependence on foreign firms
[floridi2020digital]. Data sovereignty asserts a jurisdiction's or a community's authority over data.
Technological sovereignty seeks control of the stack through open source. Cyber sovereignty, in the
China-Russia model, treats the network as national territory and partitions it. Platform or private
sovereignty names the authority platforms themselves exercise over billions through moderation and interface —
the "new governors" [klonick2018governors], the "lawless" rule of hidden terms [suzor2019lawless], the new
utilities that transmit, gate, and score [rahman2018utilities], the private government that rules without a
vote [anderson2017private], the stack whose control of interfaces is a sovereignty distinct from territory
[bratton2015stack]. Across every variant the move is the same: a state, a jurisdiction, a community, or a firm
**reclaims or asserts control** over a digital domain. The locus is centralizing and the frame is territorial
or proprietary.

This is the sharp contrast the project needs. Digital sovereignty asks who controls the infrastructure.
Coordinative sovereignty asks what autonomy an actor coordinating **through** that infrastructure can have.
The first centralizes authority; the second distributes agency under mediation it does not seek to own.

## Platforms restrict autonomy through dependence, not only interference

The empirical platform literature shows the restriction is relational and dependence-based. Algorithmic
management controls dispersed workers who never meet a manager, and republican theory reads it as domination,
a standing arbitrary power rather than episodic interference [muldoon2022domination]. Platforms are contested
relational structures holding mutuality, autonomy, and domination in tension at once [schussler2021relational].
The variable that predicts a worker's autonomy is dependence — the supplemental earner is freer than the one
who must have the platform's income [schor2020dependence]. The felt autonomy platforms grant is itself a form
of control, the "autonomy paradox" of flexibility paired with tightening constraint [wood2019goodgig]. Platform
power reaches consumers and politics, not only workers [culpepper2020platform], and platforms are studied as
infrastructure precisely because exit is not available [plantin2018infrastructure; vandijck2018platform]. The
condition these describe is not a loss of independence to be restored; it is a bind of coordination one cannot
leave.

## The gap and the near-synonyms

No established construct names an actor-centric autonomy exercised through irreducible coordination. The
closest neighbors mark the space without occupying it. Benkler's networked freedom locates liberty in peer
coordination rather than isolation [benkler2006wealth]. Bannerman's relational sovereignty reads platform power
through a relational lens but stays at the level of communications law and states [bannerman2024relational].
"Networked sovereignty" and "digital self-determination" appear as alternatives to digital sovereignty but keep
the state as the primary actor. Ostrom's commons governance is the strongest institutional precedent for
collective self-rule under interdependence, though it concerns shared resources rather than mediated
coordination [ostrom1990commons]. Hirschman's exit-voice-loyalty supplies the hinge the project will use: where
exit is available, the old sovereignty of independence works; where exit is foreclosed, only voice remains, and
voice within an unleaveable coordination is what a new construct must name [hirschman1970exit].

"Coordinative sovereignty" as a term is not established in this literature. The project's contribution is to
occupy the gap the relational tradition opened and the platform condition made urgent, and to give it the one
thing the political-theory literature lacks and this lab holds: a formal test of when a mediator can be left
and when it cannot.
