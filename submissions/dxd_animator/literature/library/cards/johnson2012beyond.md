## What it argues
A review of the tools of choice architecture: how the number of options, defaults, attribute translation, sorting and partitioning change what people choose.

Key claims:
- Decision designers cannot avoid influencing choice; every presentation is a choice architecture.
- Tools fall into two groups: structuring the choice task and describing the options.

## Relation to the argument
Gives DXD a working vocabulary for 'surfacing key decisions', and a warning that the same tools can serve the user's path or the seller's.
