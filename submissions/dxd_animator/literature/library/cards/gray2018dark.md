## What it argues
A content analysis of 118 practitioner-shared examples of dark patterns, grouped into five strategies: nagging, obstruction, sneaking, interface interference and forced action.

Key claims:
- Dark patterns put shareholder value ahead of user value.
- The five strategies map onto failures at Norman's gulfs of execution and evaluation.

## Relation to the argument
The named counter-model in Roger's framing ('heavens forbid dark patterns'). Useful as a list of what DXD must not do, and as evidence that some dark patterns test well on usability.
