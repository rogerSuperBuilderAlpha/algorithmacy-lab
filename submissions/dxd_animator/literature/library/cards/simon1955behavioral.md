## What it argues
Simon replaces the all-knowing 'economic man' with a chooser who has limited information and limited computing power, and who settles for an option that is good enough.

Key claims:
- Real choosers simplify: they search a limited set and stop at an acceptable option (satisficing).
- The structure of the environment shapes what a limited chooser can do.

## Relation to the argument
The base claim for pillar 1: the user is a bounded human, not an optimiser. A DXD interface helps someone reach a good-enough decision rather than maximising a metric for them.
