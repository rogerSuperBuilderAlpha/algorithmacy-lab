## The problem

Digital experience used to be about a designer guiding a user to an end that both of them agreed
on, mostly. I want to buy a raincoat, and an online retailer designs a clear, simple flow for me to
get one.

Today that end is less clear. When people work with agents such as Claude or Grok Bot, they are often
making something themselves. Say it is raining and I need a plan for getting to work. If I land on a
site that pushes me towards raincoats, but while talking it through with my agent I worked out that I
need an umbrella, then no one gets what they want.

So the key measures are not conversion rate, stickiness, or, worse, dark patterns that trick me into
buying a raincoat. They are about me getting myself to the decision, and then being shown ways to find
and buy what I decided on. The experience is how I make that decision. The design problem is how the
designer surfaces the key decisions that move me along whatever path I need to take.

We call this **Decision Experience Design (DXD)**.

## What stays fixed

Chat and AI interfaces fall well short of this today (as of October 2026), and they will change a lot.
Three things hold regardless.

1. **The user is the human: cognitive, biological, emotional.** There is a separate body of research
   on agent experience, the agent as user. This call sets it aside.
2. **The user's environment is a platform: opaque, cooptive, and triadic.**
3. **The design move is an aesthetic shift.**

## Why animation

When we engage an LLM we are engaging an animation. Animators design experiences that are:

- **Opaque.** There are, quite literally, missing frames.
- **Cooptive.** At some point in watching an animation you lock in, and the gaps disappear.
- **Triadic.** When I watch an actor, *I* am watching an *actor*: a dyad. When I watch animation,
  *I* am watching a *character* moved by an *animator*.

To deliver Decision Experience Design, this conference proposes learning the core methods of the
animator and applying them.

## Topics

We welcome work that takes up any of the following.

- **The human deciding.** How people reach decisions with an agent: bounded attention, preferences
  that are built while choosing, working out the problem before the purchase, the cost of making many
  choices, trust and reliance.
- **Beyond conversion.** What DXD measures instead of conversion and engagement; how it differs from
  choice architecture, nudging, persuasive technology and dark patterns.
- **The platform.** How opacity, cooption and a third party (the platform, the model provider, the
  retailer) shape the user's path to a decision.
- **The animator's methods.** What timing, anticipation, staging, closure between frames, limited
  animation and character design offer an interface that has to surface decisions; where the analogy
  breaks.
- **Character and agency.** How users read an agent as a character, and what follows when someone
  else is moving it.
- **Cases and prototypes.** Interfaces, studies or critiques that show a decision being surfaced well
  or badly.

## Out of scope

Agent experience (designing for AI agents as users). Work that aims only to raise conversion or
engagement.
