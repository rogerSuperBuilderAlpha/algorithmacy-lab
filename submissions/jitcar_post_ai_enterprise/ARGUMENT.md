# Argument: the internal third party

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Angle sharpened against the OT paper (see FLAGS 2).]

**P1.** When a firm installs generative AI in appraisal, hiring or peer collaboration, the system stands between two of the firm's people. The firm is then at the apex as the operator, and it is also one party's principal. The manager is the firm's agent; the employee is the firm's subordinate.
- *Warrant:* the manager–GenAI–employee cases Hinds and von Krogh describe have this shape, and the firm chooses and configures the system.
- *Sources:* `lima_pdw/literature/cards/hindsvonkrogh2024.md` (pp. 4, 6); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/starkvandenbroeck2024.md` (the operator at the apex, p. 14).

**P2.** In that position the firm cannot treat the competence to coordinate through the system as each worker's private skill. The firm controls what each party can see and who may question the verdict. Studies of AI in organizations show brokers and experts taking on the interpreting work.
- *Sources:*
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/waardenburg2022.md` (brokers; abstract depth)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/leonardileavell2026.md` (pp. 516, 537: artificial certainty erodes expert authority)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/lebovitz2022.md` (engagement with opaque AI; abstract depth)

**C.** In the post-AI enterprise, algorithmacy is a firm-level capability that the firm must build and allocate. The firm decides who interprets verdicts, who can specify intent to the system, who tracks model updates, and which party can contest. Because the firm is both operator and principal, how it allocates these roles determines whether internal AI restores or dissolves accountability between its people.
