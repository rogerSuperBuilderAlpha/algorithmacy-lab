**Title:** The Internal Third Party: Allocating Algorithmacy in the Post-AI Enterprise

## Abstract
Generative AI now stands between people inside the firm. It can draft a manager's appraisal of an employee, screen candidates for a hiring manager, and summarize one colleague's work for another. In each case the system evaluates a relation between two of the firm's people, and the firm holds two roles at once. As operator, it chooses and configures the system. As principal, it employs one of the parties as its agent. This paper argues that this double position changes what organizations must do about AI-enabled decisions. On platforms, the competence to coordinate through an evaluating system is left to each participant. Inside the firm it cannot be, because the firm controls what each party sees and who can question a verdict. We call this competence algorithmacy. It has three practices: interpreting an ambiguous result, specifying intent so the other party can read it through the system, and tracking model changes. We argue that the post-AI enterprise must allocate each practice to a role. Someone must interpret verdicts for both parties, someone must hold the right to specify intent to the system, and someone must track updates. Drawing on published studies of knowledge brokers who translate algorithmic outputs, and of experts whose authority erodes when AI presents uncertain projections as certain, we propose an allocation framework and four propositions. The framework treats contestability as a role assignment, not only as a system feature. Firms that leave the three practices unallocated will find that internal AI deflects accountability between their people instead of supporting it.

**Keywords:** generative AI; algorithmacy; accountability; performance appraisal; knowledge brokers
