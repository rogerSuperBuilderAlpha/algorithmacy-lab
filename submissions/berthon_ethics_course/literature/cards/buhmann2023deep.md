## Summary
A BEQ argument from political CSR that AI firms are obliged to take part in deliberation about AI, organized as
"distributed deliberation" across expert venues, the public sphere and AI mini-publics (Figure 1, p. 162). It separates
strategic, expert and true opacity (pp. 150-152).

## Key facts it relies on
- Strategic opacity has motives that include "avoiding accountability and evading regulations" (p. 151).
- "Expert opacity can also arise inadvertently through attempts at disclosure and transparency that overwhelm citizens"
  (p. 151).
- Corporate engagement may "constitute merely a means of deflection" (p. 167); the authors answer that ethics washing
  may be an early stage toward substantive CSR (p. 167).

## Critical notes
- **Literacy thread:** expert opacity is defined at "the intersection of system complexity and 'technical literacy'"
  (p. 151, after Burrell 2016), which gives the course a BEQ source that ties opacity to literacy.
- **Fifth failure (returns promised):** the optimistic reading of ethics washing (p. 167) is a promise of later returns;
  compare Kamperman and Graff (2026).
