## Summary
The EU expert group's statement of "trustworthy AI": four ethical principles (respect for human autonomy, prevention of
harm, fairness, explicability) turned into seven requirements (p. 14) and an assessment list. Soft law: "Nothing in
this document shall create legal rights nor impose legal obligations towards third parties" (p. 6).

## Key facts it relies on
- Procedural fairness "entails the ability to contest and seek effective redress against decisions made by AI systems
  and by the humans operating them" (p. 13).
- "Whenever an AI system has a significant impact on people's lives, it should be possible to demand a suitable
  explanation of the AI system's decision-making process." (p. 18)
- "Certification can however never replace responsibility." (p. 23)
- "Note that transparency cannot prevent non-discrimination or ensure fairness, and is not the panacea against the
  problem of scoring." (p. 34)

## Critical notes
- **Principles approach:** the primary instance of what Mittelstadt (2019, card `mittelstadt2019principles`) criticizes
  as principles without enforcement; the document says itself that it binds no one (p. 6).
- **Literacy thread:** it asks the affected person to "demand" an explanation (p. 18), which leaves the reading to that
  person. It says nothing about literacy's history.
- Cite the guidelines directly rather than through Mentxaka et al.'s (2026) paraphrase.
