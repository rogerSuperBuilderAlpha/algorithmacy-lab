## 2. Tier A: Lexical (words and stock phrases)

Wikipedia's framing applies throughout: "One or two of these words appearing in an edit may be
coincidental, but an edit… introducing lots of them, lots of times, is one of the strongest tells"
(WP L383, "High density of 'AI vocabulary' words"). It also warns that the list is to be "taken as
literally as possible: a word being overused by AI does *not* imply that its synonyms are also
overused" (WP L394).

### 2.4 Kobak top excess style words (2024): reported vs computed

**Reported in the paper** (kobak p. 2, §2.1): *delves* r = 28.0; *underscores* r = 13.8; *showcasing*
r = 10.7; *potential* δ = 0.052; *findings* δ = 0.041; *crucial* δ = 0.037. Also reported: 379
excess style words in 2024, of which 66% were verbs and 14% adjectives (p. 3); lower bound on
LLM-processed 2024 abstracts 13.5% (p. 4).

**Computed this session** from `yearly-counts.csv.gz` using the paper's §4.3 formula
(p = (a+1)/(b+1); q = p₂₀₂₂ + 2·max(p₂₀₂₂ − p₂₀₂₁, 0); r = p/q; δ = p − q). The script reproduces
all six reported values exactly. Output is saved at
`ai_tics/kobak_repo/computed_excess_2024_style_words.txt`.

- By ratio r (rarer words): delves 28.0, underscores 13.8, delved 12.2, showcasing 10.7, meticulously 10.4, delve 7.9, intricacies 7.6, underscoring 7.5, intricate 7.4, surpassing 7.1, delving 6.9, commendable 6.8, underscore 6.7, excels 5.8, garnered 5.3, underscored 5.1, intricately 5.0, realm 5.0, encompassed 4.9, renowned 4.9, grappling 4.9, groundbreaking 4.8, emphasizing 4.7, encompassing 4.4, necessitating 4.3.

## 4. Tier C: Distributional patterns

| Pattern | Reported direction and size | Source and locator | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sentence-length variance / burstiness** (lower in LLM text) | Kutyła: LLM texts show "lower perplexity and burstiness values… and more consistent sentence lengths" (Polish student reports, 2,400 pairs). Jan: within-text SD of sentence length 4.1 (AI) vs 7.9 (human), U = 2,140, p < .001 (illustrative-data caveat). Terčon: "sentence lengths in AIGT tend to vary much less" (Zindela 2023; Desaire et al. 2023). Zanotto: machine text "exhibit[s] comparatively low variability" across features | kutyla abstract; jan Table 2, p. 679; tercon §4.2.2; zanotto §4.2, Fig. 1 | **Consistent direction**, though the magnitudes come from weak or abstract-only sources |
| **Mean sentence length** | **No consensus.** Zanotto: human 23.5 words vs ChatGPT 19.3–20.0 and GPT-4 18.2–19.1 (Table 1), yet "we do not notice a clear pattern" across models (§5.1). Jan: AI 22.4 vs human 19.6 (illustrative). Bao: syntactic complexity (including mean sentence length) fell faster in arXiv abstracts after ChatGPT (§4.1). Terčon: "little consensus regarding whether LLMs produce longer sentences than humans or vice versa" | zanotto Table 1, §5.1; jan Table 2; bao §4.1; tercon §4.2.2, §6 | **Do not use the mean as a tell** |
| **Homogenised complexity (variance in writing complexity)** | What is homogenised: the variance of a composite writing-complexity score, the mean of Simpson index, Shannon entropy, average dependency link length, type-token ratio and hapax legomena (Methods §1.7). LLM rewriting cut that variance by **21–50%** (p ≤ .05) across datasets and models (abstract). Examples: arXiv 0.0090 → 0.0064–0.0074; Reddit 0.0091 → 0.0045–0.0076 (Table 3). **Exception:** Llama 3 with the "Syntax Grammar" prompt *raised* arXiv variance to 0.0111. Observational: post-ChatGPT slope declines in arXiv (β = −0.0014, p < .001), Reddit (β = −0.0021, p < .001) and Patch news (β = −0.0022, p = .049) (Table 1). LLM rewriting also cut trait-prediction F1 by 6% on average (Study 2) | sourati abstract; Table 1; Table 3; Methods §1.7; Study 2 | Strong. It is a corpus-level effect, not a per-text tell |

## 5. Use for drafting

How to read this section. Each line gives a rule phrased as what to do instead. **[INSPECT]**
marks an item that overlaps a legitimate academic or BEQ convention: treat any hit as a tell to
check in context, not a ban. **[AVOID]** marks an item with no academic function that the evidence
ties to LLM output. Wikipedia's own caution applies: "do not merely treat these signs as the
problems to be fixed; that could just make detection harder" (WP L9). The underlying faults are
vagueness, unearned significance and unsupported attribution. Surface substitution does not fix
them.

**Conflict with the user's writing-style rules (flagged, not resolved).** The global CLAUDE.md
says "Cut agentless passives." Reinhart finds GPT-4o and 4o-mini use agentless passives at
**about half the human rate** (53% and 51%, d ≈ −0.56; SI Tables S5–S6). Removing agentless
passives therefore moves a text *toward* the GPT-4o profile on that one feature. The rule may
still be right on clarity grounds; the evidence here does not make agentless passives an AI tell.
The same applies to hedges, which LLMs also under-use (reinhart SI Table S5; tercon §4.2.7).

### Lexical

1. Significance words (*pivotal, crucial, vital, key role, testament, underscores its importance*): state the specific consequence and who bears it. **[INSPECT]** *Crucial* and *key* have literal uses.
2. *Delve, showcase, intricate, meticulous, tapestry, realm, interplay, multifaceted, garner, commendable, groundbreaking*: use the plain verb or noun (*examine, show, complex, careful*), or cut. **[AVOID]** in BEQ prose, except *interplay* and *complex* where they name a real relation.
3. High-δ academic words (*potential, findings, insights, comprehensive, notably, particularly, within, across, enhance*): keep them when they do work. They are ordinary scholarly words, and only their density marks LLM text. **[INSPECT]**
4. *Additionally* to open a sentence: link by content (repeat the key noun, or use the relation the argument needs). **[INSPECT]** The word is legitimate, but WP calls it a weak tell on its own.
5. *Serves as / stands as / represents / functions as* in place of *is*: write *is*. Write *has* for *boasts/features/offers*. **[AVOID]** except where *represents* means representation.
6. *Associated with / in connection with*: state the relation (authored, funded, argued, employed). **[INSPECT]** In empirical claims, *associated with* is correct statistical hedging.
7. *It is important to note that; plays a crucial role in; in the context of*: delete the frame and state the point. **[AVOID]** the first two; **[INSPECT]** *in the context of*.
8. Promotional adjectives (*vibrant, rich, profound, renowned, groundbreaking*): replace with evidence of the quality, or cut. **[AVOID]**
9. Coverage puffery ("widely cited," "extensively discussed in the literature"): say what the cited work argues. **[INSPECT]** A brief positioning sentence is normal in a literature review.

### Rhetorical and syntactic

10. Present-participle tails (", highlighting…", ", underscoring…", ", reflecting…", ", contributing to…"): prefer a finite clause with a named agent, or a new sentence that makes the claim and earns it. **[INSPECT]** Participial clauses are grammatical and sometimes the right subordination; the tell is the evaluative tail that adds significance without evidence.
11. Nominalisation density (*the implementation of the regulation of…*): turn nominalisations back into verbs with agents where the agent matters, which is often in ethics, since responsibility attaches to agents. **[INSPECT]** Technical nominals (*accountability, legitimacy*) are terms of art.
12. Phrasal-coordination pairs and triads ("ownership, consent, and dignity"): keep a list only when every member is argued for. Otherwise name the one that matters. **[INSPECT]** Analytic triplets are allowed by the user's style guide; check that each item carries weight.
13. "Not X but Y" / "not just X but Y" / "Y rather than X": use it only when someone actually holds X, and cite them. Otherwise state Y directly. **[INSPECT]** Contrast with a cited position is the backbone of philosophical argument; the tell is the strawman contrast with no holder.
14. "X should be treated as … rather than …"; "this does not by itself establish…": write the claim the evidence supports, at its strength, once. **[INSPECT]** Scope statements are legitimate in limitations sections.
15. Didactic disclaimers ("It is important to remember…"): cut. **[AVOID]**
16. Summary closers ("In conclusion," "Overall," paragraph-final restatement): end on the paragraph's last new move, or the hook into the next. **[INSPECT]** The formal Conclusion section of a BEQ article is conventional, and Kobak et al. themselves open their final paragraph with "In conclusion" (kobak p. 6). The tell is the paragraph-level restatement.
17. "Despite these challenges, X continues to…" outlook endings: end on the open problem as an open problem. **[AVOID]**
18. Vague attributions ("scholars argue," "critics have noted," "researchers"): name the author and cite the page. **[AVOID]** unattributed; **[INSPECT]** "a growing literature" when it is followed by citations.
19. Metacommentary ("In this section we will discuss…"): **[INSPECT]** A one-sentence roadmap and first-person method statements ("I argue," "I define") are standard BEQ practice and match the user's style guide. The tell is the content-free announcement ("we will explore the unique characteristics…").
20. Em dashes: use them for real glosses and interruptions, and vary with commas, parentheses and colons. **[INSPECT]** The user's style guide allows em dashes; WP dates em-dash overuse to 2022–Sept 2026 and reports it as model-specific (higher in Claude, per a study WP cites).
21. Elegant variation: repeat the defined term. In a theory paper, a key term should not drift into synonyms. **[INSPECT]** (historical tell; also a reader-confusion fault).
22. Hedges: hedge once, at the claim that needs it. Do not strip all hedges to sound un-AI: LLMs under-use hedges (reinhart; tercon). **[INSPECT]**
23. Agentless passives: name the agent where responsibility is the point (ethics). Do not treat a passive as an AI tell (see the flagged conflict above). **[INSPECT]**
24. *Because*, *be* as main verb, first-person pronouns, contractions, clausal coordination, analytic negation: GPT-4o under-uses all of these (reinhart SI Tables S5–S6). Plain causal *because* and first-person method statements are human markers, so keep them where the register allows. **[INSPECT]** BEQ rarely uses contractions.
25. Cliché, redundant exposition, purple prose, vague generality (Chakrabarty): cut what the reader can infer, and replace generalities with the case, the clause or the number. **[AVOID]**
26. False ranges, rhetorical questions, colon reveals: no source read here supports these as tells. Judge them on clarity alone. **[INSPECT]**

### Distributional

27. Sentence length: vary it with the argument, so that dense sentences alternate with short ones. Low within-text variance is the tell (kutyla; jan; tercon); the mean is not. **[INSPECT]**
28. Paragraph shape: let paragraph length follow the move. (No evidence was found on uniform paragraph length; this rule rests on the user's style guide alone.) **[INSPECT]**
29. Lexical diversity: use the field's precise terms and the case's own vocabulary, but do not cycle synonyms (see 21). Evidence on the direction of lexical diversity is mixed. **[INSPECT]**
30. Connectives: use the relation word the logic needs (*however, therefore, because*) and avoid repeating the same opener. Do not count connectives as a tell: the evidence says LLMs use fewer basic connectives (bao) or no more conjuncts than humans (reinhart). **[INSPECT]** Relation connectives are core academic cohesion.
31. Formulaic bundles (*it is important to note that, in the context of, plays a crucial role in*): cut or replace with the specific claim. **[AVOID]** the first and third; **[INSPECT]** the second.
32. Homogenised complexity: no per-text rule follows from Sourati, whose finding is corpus-level. The drafting implication is to keep the authors' own idiolect when polishing, because LLM polishing narrows it (Table 3). **[INSPECT]**
