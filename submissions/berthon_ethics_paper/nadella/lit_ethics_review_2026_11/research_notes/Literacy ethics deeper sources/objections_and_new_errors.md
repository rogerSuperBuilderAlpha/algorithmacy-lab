## Which errors lack a contemporary objection, and what objections exist?

### Takeaway
Against the existing review, errors 5, 9, 12, 13 and 14 looked thinnest, but the library already holds strong
contemporaries for 5 (Las Casas, Oviedo), 9 (Wharton 1807) and 10 (Wilson 1917). This session adds an affected
or official contemporary for 12 and 13 (Du Bois 1903; Zitkala-Ša), and an inspector's objection for 14 (Matthew
Arnold's 1867 report, against payment by results). Error 14 thus gains its first contemporary: the metric
certified reading that had not been learnt. Errors 3 and 6 still rest on secondary or institutional voices.

**Error 5, unanswerable notice (library cards, not re-read)**
- Las Casas calls the Requerimiento "cosa es de reir, ó de llorar por mejor decir", and asks what force a command has
  "que se hace á gente que la lengua en que se dice no entiende" — Las Casas, *Historia de las Indias*, tomo IV (ed.
  1876), Bk III ch. 57, pp. 162–163; lib. card `lascasas1876historia`.
- "creer que entendieron ellos cosa del requerimiento es falsísimo" (ch. 59, p. 167); the parties read it "á los
  árboles" (ch. 67, pp. 198–199) — same card.
- Oviedo, an officer on the 1514 expedition, to Pedrarias: "estos indios no quieren escuchar la teología deste
  requirimiento, ni vos tenes quien se la dé á entender" — Oviedo, *Historia general*, tomo III, Lib. XXIX cap. VII,
  p. 31; lib. card `oviedo1853historia`. A participant's objection, dated by Oviedo to 14 June 1514 (p. 29).
- Inference for the writer: error 5 has the best-documented contemporary objection in the whole set; the review's
  row should cite these two cards.

**Error 9, restriction by reader class**
- Wharton, HC 13 June 1807, asked whether ministers and churchwardens should "hold a critical examination in order
  to ascertain who were fit to be exalted to the counting-house, and who were only fit for the plough?" — HC Deb 13
  June 1807 vol 9 c. 801; lib. card `hansard1807parochial`.
- Du Bois reports the reigning view, to reject it: "an education that encourages aspiration ... is the privilege of
  white men and the danger and delusion of black" — W. E. B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903), ch. VI "Of the
  Training of Black Men" [Project Gutenberg #408](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/408/pg408.txt) (partial;
  Gutenberg has no page numbers; chapter located by roman-numeral heading). Elision mine; the cut words are "that
  sets the loftiest of ideals and seeks as an end culture and character rather than bread-winning,".

**Error 10, competence as cover (library card)**
- Wilson rejects the literacy test as "a radical change in the policy of the Nation which is not justified in
  principle", because "Tests of quality and of purpose can not be objected to on principle, but tests of opportunity
  surely may be" — Woodrow Wilson, veto message of H.R. 10384, "THE WHITE HOUSE, January 29, 1917"; lib. card
  `wilson1917message`. Wilson's "tests of opportunity" also names error 11's mechanism: a test of what one was
  allowed to learn.

**Error 11, the contemporaneous standard (new)**
- J. S. Mill, 1861, makes literacy a condition of the vote but ties it to a prior duty: "Justice demands, even when
  the suffrage does not depend on it, that the means of attaining these elementary acquirements should be within the
  reach of every person" — Mill, *Considerations on Representative Government* (1861),
  [Gutenberg #5669](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/5669/pg5669.txt) (partial; chapter on the extension of the
  suffrage, heading not checked).
- Same passage: "If society has neglected to discharge two solemn obligations, the more important and more
  fundamental of the two must be fulfilled first; universal teaching must precede universal enfranchisement." Mill is a
  designer, not an objector; he states in 1861 the condition the self-justifying gate breaks.

**Error 12, selective provision by usefulness (new)**
- Du Bois, 1903: "Mr. Washington distinctly asks that black people give up, at least for the present, three
  things,— First, political power, Second, insistence on civil rights, Third, higher education of Negro youth" — *Souls*,
  ch. III "Of Mr. Booker T. Washington and Others",
  [Gutenberg #408](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/408/pg408.txt) (partial). The sentence continues: "and
  concentrate all their energies on industrial education, and accumulation of wealth, and the conciliation of the
  South."
- Du Bois, ch. V "Of the Wings of Atalanta": "The function of the university is not simply to teach bread-winning, or
  to furnish teachers for the public schools or to be a centre of polite society" — same source.
- Du Bois, ch. VI: "Teach workers to work,—a wise saying; ... Teach thinkers to think" — same source. He accepts
  industrial training and objects to its being the whole of provision for one race.

**Error 13, literacy taught for docility (new)**
- Du Bois, ch. III, reporting the case he rejects: "it has been claimed that the Negro can survive only through
  submission" — *Souls*, [Gutenberg #408](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/408/pg408.txt) (partial).
- Zitkala-Ša (Yankton Dakota, a pupil and later a teacher in Indian schools) on the schools: "few there are who have
  paused to question whether real life or long-lasting death lies beneath this semblance of civilization" —
  "An Indian Teacher Among Indians", in *American Indian Stories*,
  [Gutenberg #10376](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/10376/pg10376.txt) (partial). The book's imprint date and
  any earlier magazine printing were not checked this session; date the testimony only from a checked edition.
- Same essay: "For the white man's papers I had given up my faith in the Great Spirit. For these same papers I had
  forgotten the healing in trees and brooks." Her mother, she writes, "would have said the white man's papers were not
  worth the freedom and health I had lost by them" — same source. "The white man's papers" is her term for literate
  schooling, so the objection is to literacy's terms of delivery, not to reading.

**Error 14, mismeasured progress (new; the strongest find)**
- The designer, Robert Lowe, introducing the Revised Code: "If it is not cheap it shall be efficient; if it is not
  efficient it shall be cheap" — HC Deb 13 February 1862 vol 165 c. 229 (column 230 begins in the next sentence),
  [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1862/feb/13/education-the-revised-code-o-regulations)
  (partial: Lowe's statement and all other speakers scanned). The other speakers that day (Disraeli, Walpole,
  Pakington, Cecil, Kinnaird, Newdegate, Caird, Grey) raised procedure and cost, not the measure.
- The objector, Matthew Arnold, HM Inspector of Schools: "it is now found possible, by ingenious preparation, to get
  children through the Revised Code examination in reading, writing, and ciphering, without their really knowing how
  to read, write, or cipher" — Arnold, General Report for the Year 1867, in *Reports on Elementary Schools 1852–1882*
  (1889), p. 124, [archive.org `reportsonelement00arnouoft`](https://archive.org/details/reportsonelement00arnouoft)
  (partial; page from the OCR running heads).
- Arnold, same report, pp. 120–122: "making two-thirds of the Government grant depend upon a mechanical
  examination, inevitably gives a mechanical turn to the school teaching, a mechanical turn to the inspection" — same
  source.
- Arnold, General Report for 1869, p. 136: "The school examinations in view of payment by results are, as I have said,
  a game of mechanical contrivance in which the teachers will and must more and more learn how to beat us" — same
  source [OCR mended: "oi", "hy"].
- Kay-Shuttleworth, 1861, reporting school managers' objection before the Code took effect: the Code "overlooks the
  value of all this indispensable preliminary moral and intellectual training. It treats it as no part of the work
  done. It discourages it because it cannot test it, and therefore excludes it from all aid" — [J. Kay-Shuttleworth],
  *Letter to Earl Granville, K.G., on the Revised Code* (1861),
  [archive.org `lettertoearlgra00shugoog`](https://archive.org/details/lettertoearlgra00shugoog) (partial; OCR lines
  2708–2721, printed page not located; [OCR mended] heavily, so check against a page image before use). Author
  attribution rests on the archive.org record, not on the title page.

**Error 8, a weak addition**
- The same Kay-Shuttleworth passage says the Code would "turn out dull scholars, sluggards, and truants, though the
  fault might be in the want of local civilisation and home training" — same source and caveats [OCR mended]. The
  sentence's subject falls outside the extract, so read the page before using it for blame.

**Boundary B, compulsion and an enforced standard (new affected-person voice)**
- Zitkala-Ša on her first day at a mission boarding school: "now I was only one of many little animals driven by a
  herder"; at the cutting of her braids, "Then I lost my spirit" — "School Days of an Indian Girl", ch. II "The Cutting
  of My Long Hair", *American Indian Stories*,
  [Gutenberg #10376](https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/cache/epub/10376/pg10376.txt) (partial).

### Inferences
- Error 14 is now an ethical error, not only a statistical artefact. Arnold, an inspector inside the system, said in
  1867 that the grant-earning examination certified reading that had not been learnt. That answers the existing
  review's caveat on Barakat. It is also the closest historical match to the AI analogue "counting trainings or scale
  scores as protection".
- Lowe and Arnold make a matched pair, designer and objector in one institution, like Branch's Congress for error 1.
- Du Bois serves three errors (9, 12, 13). The report should note that he is objecting to a Black leader's strategy
  as well as to white policy, so "contemporary objection" here is intra-community argument, not only protest at a
  ruling class.
- Mill (1861) and Wilson (1917) give the paper contemporaneous *standards*, not just complaints: provision before
  gate (Mill) and quality not opportunity (Wilson). Both suit the paper's "recognised in its own time" test.

### Gaps
- **Error 3**: no quoted contemporary found this session; the 1381 material rests on a modern historian's paraphrase.
- **Error 6**: only an institutional evaluation (UNESCO/UNDP 1976), no named contemporary; not searched further.
- **Error 13, British case**: Lovett and Collins, *Chartism* (1840), the obvious working-class objection to schooling
  for docility, was not accessible (HTTP 401 on the only archive.org copy found).
- **Error 5, presumption of reading the law**: I meant to test Bentham's attack on unknowable judge-made law, but the
  archive.org title search for "Truth versus Ashhurst" returned nothing; not found.
- Zitkala-Ša's dates and the Mill chapter heading need checking against a paginated edition.

## Further error categories: is there scholarship, and is each distinct?

### Takeaway
Of the five candidates, only **charging for the medium** has both scholarship and contemporaries on file, and even it
is contested; it fits best as a sub-case of custody (4) or the gate (1). **Literacy demanded in a language the person
does not speak** is well documented but is a device of errors 5 and 10, not a new error. **Deferral** is the temporal
form of error 11, with Mill and Du Bois as contemporaries. **Surveillance via documents** and **displacement of
oral or vernacular competence** found no usable scholarship in this session's searches. I recommend no new numbered
error; three sub-entries are defensible.

### Cited Findings

**Charging for the medium (taxes on knowledge)**
- Hewitt's history (secondary, lib. card `hewitt2013dawn`, via a review) finds the press taxes mixed in purpose:
  revenue, political suppression and trade interest. The trade groups rejected the radical phrase "taxes on
  knowledge" (review para. 3). The card concludes the price-as-gate reading is "real but mixed in purpose".
- Collet Dobson Collet, campaigner and historian of the repeal, records the association quoting the publisher Charles
  Knight against the paper duty: "During the same period I have paid fifty thousand pounds paper duty, which sum has
  become a double charge to me by the inevitable operation of a tax upon the raw material" — Collet, *History of the
  Taxes on Knowledge* (London: T. Fisher Unwin, 1899), p. 39,
  [archive.org `historytaxesonk00collgoog`](https://archive.org/details/historytaxesonk00collgoog) (partial; which of
  the two volumes the scan holds is unclear from the OCR). This is a producer's cost complaint, not a reader's.
- Judgment: distinct in mechanism (price, not permission), but the record on file is a trade and radical campaign
  whose moral framing Hewitt disputes. Treat as a sub-case of 4 ("custody by price") or 1, not a fifteenth error.

**Literacy demanded in a language the person does not speak**
- Lake (lib. card `lake2006mississippi`) traces the test's change "from ability to fill out an application form in
  English to a dictation test in any European language" (p. 213). Natal's 1897 bill went "one step further" because
  its targets were "perfectly well able to read and write in their own language"; Chamberlain recommended the Natal
  Act "as a model to the Australians in 1901" (p. 221).
- Las Casas on the Requerimiento: a command to "gente que la lengua en que se dice no entiende" (lib. card,
  pp. 162–163).
- OpenAlex search ("literacy test language immigration dictation Natal", 19 Nov 2026) also returned "Legal transfers
  of restrictive immigration laws: a historical perspective" (2016),
  [doi:10.1017/s0020589316000373](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0020589316000373), open-access PDF listed; **title only,
  not read**.
- Judgment: not distinct. In Natal the foreign language disguised a racial target (error 10); in the Requerimiento it
  made notice unanswerable (error 5). List it as a named device under both.

**Deferral ("not yet ready") of rights pending literacy**
- Mill, 1861: "universal teaching must precede universal enfranchisement" (source above).
- Du Bois, 1903, against Washington's call to give up political power and higher education "at least for the
  present" (source above).
- Judgment: the temporal form of error 11. Mill's own condition (teaching first) is the test that separates a just
  deferral from a self-justifying one. A sub-entry under 11, not a new error.

**Surveillance via registers, passes and identity documents**
- Castillo Gómez and Martínez (existing review) say writing meant "taxes, punishment, and forced labor" for many
  subalterns (p. 22); that is the nearest support on file.
- Two OpenAlex searches ("pass laws documents literacy South Africa identification"; "identity registration literacy
  colonial surveillance writing", 19 Nov 2026, top 6 each) returned nothing relevant.
- Judgment: unestablished. The searches put "literacy" in the query, which likely hid work filed under identification
  or documentation history. On present evidence it overlaps error 7 (writing turned on the unlettered) and error 3.

**Displacement of oral or vernacular competence**
- The only material is testimony: Zitkala-Ša, "For these same papers I had forgotten the healing in trees and brooks"
  (source above).
- Judgment: overlaps boundary B. An objection to writing *as such* (the Phaedrus line) would also break the paper's
  guardrail that the critique is of design and allocation, not of the competence (existing REVIEW, "Agreement").

### Inferences
- The list of 14 plus B holds up. The candidates are devices or forms of existing errors, and naming them as
  sub-entries keeps the list's claim that each error is a distinct design move.
- If the author wants a fifteenth, "custody by price" is the only candidate with both a mechanism of its own and a
  documented contemporary campaign; it needs a reader-side source, not a publisher's.

### Gaps
- No scholarship read on identity documents or pass systems as literacy's instrument. The next step is a title search
  for the standard histories of South African and colonial Indian identification, not a "literacy" keyword search.
- No reader-side contemporary for the taxes on knowledge; the unstamped press (e.g. the *Poor Man's Guardian*,
  indexed in Collet at vol. i, pp. 32, 42) was not read.
- The 2016 legal-transfers article was found but not read.
