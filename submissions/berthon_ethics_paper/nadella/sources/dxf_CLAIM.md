# Claim — provisional

*Stated by Claude on 2026-10-12 from the author's instruction. It is a claim under test. The author
rules on its final form after `literature/facets/T_adjudication.md`.*

## The thesis as the author gave it

The move from *scriptio continua* to spaces between words was the critical user-experience move that
ignited literacy. Algorithmacy needs a move of the same kind. Satya Nadella's "Models as Insider Risks
in the Super Intelligence Era" proposes the opposite.

## The thesis in the form the research tests

1. **Precedent.** Scribes changed the page so that the reader no longer had to find the words. The work
   moved from the reader to the artefact.
2. **Consequence.** One of two readings holds, and the research decides which:
   - *H-cost.* The move lowered the cost of reading and widened the group who could read without long
     training.
   - *H-cause.* The move caused the spread of silent reading and, through it, of wider literacy.
3. **Transfer.** Coordination through an algorithmic third party is today in the condition of unspaced
   script: only the trained can do it. Decision Experience Design is the discipline that looks for the
   move that takes the work off the person.
4. **Foil.** Nadella's answer to opaque models is observation, logging and containment by the
   organisation. On the author's reading it asks people to read more, keeps a two-party picture of
   principal and contained tool, and separates authority from engagement.

## Do not claim

- That word separation created silent reading, unless the research supports it.
- That a design change restructures cognition (standing ruling; see `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §3).
- That the remedy for algorithmacy is helping users read the algorithm (standing ruling).
- That Nadella argues for transparency alone. He says it is "not sufficient or dependable".
- Any figure or source from the Gemini essays that a facet has not confirmed.
- Novelty for the analogy before facet Z reports.
