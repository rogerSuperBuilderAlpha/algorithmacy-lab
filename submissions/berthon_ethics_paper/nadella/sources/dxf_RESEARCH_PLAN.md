## 1. What the paper is

A paper for PHD1750-3 / HF700-1, Foundations in Human Factors (Bentley, Fall 2026), by Roger Hunt. It
replaces the DIAL response (`../dial_response_algorithmacy/`) as the course paper and is meant to found
Decision Experience Design (DXD; `../decision_experience_design/`).

The author's argument, in his words (2026-10-12): "We need to be reviewing the hisotry of the critical UX
move converting scriptura continua to spaces between words which ignited literacy. we will need a
similar move here." He contrasts this with Satya Nadella's post of 10 Oct 2026, "Models as Insider Risks
in the Super Intelligence Era", which he reads as "the exact opposite of the soltion and what
algorthmacy proposes".

His earlier statement of the same idea, which the paper keeps verbatim
(`../dial_response_algorithmacy/draft.md:263`): "Word separation did not train the populace to read
*scriptio continua*; it changed the interface so that reading no longer required the training."

**This run produces research and an outline. Nobody drafts the paper; the author writes it.**

## 2. Terms

- **Algorithmacy.** The author's current wording (`../algorithmacy_ot/manuscript/PAPER.md:19-21`): "the
  sensibility to navigate opaque triadic coordinative cooptation", with three competencies: interpret a
  variable counterpart's intentions from an otherwise innocuous output; specify intent so meaning
  survives the opaque, adaptive mediator; keep track as governance shifts. The triad is two human
  parties and an algorithmic third party that reads both and commits determinations neither controls.
- **Decision Experience Design.** Published Part 1
  (`../decision_experience_design/published/2026-09-30_decision-experience-design.md`, lines 22, 34,
  142, 150): design for choices brokered through an adaptive, opaque algorithmic intermediary; its
  expertise is structural intervention (counter-delegation, structural refusal, bounded operating
  envelopes), not turning users into decoders.
- **Engagement layer.** `../dial_response_algorithmacy/corpus/ENGAGEMENT_LAYER.md:9`: the layer at
  which people meet a system that is still being made, where steering is the ordinary mode of use.
- **Scriptio continua.** Writing without word boundaries. Both spellings are in scholarly use: Saenger
  writes *scriptura continua* (1997, p. 69), as the author does; Parkes, Heilmann and most classicists
  write *scriptio continua*. *(Corrected 2026-10-12: an earlier version of this line called the
  author's spelling non-standard.)*

**Author's answer of 2026-10-12 on the history.** "Let the research decide." The third ruling is
therefore open for this paper: the research carries two hypotheses and the author rules afterwards.

- **H-cost (design precedent).** Word separation moved parsing work from the reader to the page. It
  lowered the cost of reading and widened the group who could read without long training. It did not
  create silent reading and did not by itself spread literacy.
- **H-cause (ignition).** Word separation was a cause of the spread of silent reading and, through it,
  of wider literacy.

**Author's answer on Nadella.** The paper opposes his post on three counts:
1. *Legibility paradigm.* He answers opacity with more reading: observe, log, audit.
2. *Keeps the dyad.* He treats the model as a contained insider under the organisation's authority.
3. *Containment versus engagement.* He separates the supply of intelligence from authority over it;
   algorithmacy proposes an engagement layer where the user reads and rewrites the shared mediator.

## 4. Four problems the research must settle

1. **"Ignited literacy" outruns the evidence in hand.** `../decision_experience_design/part2_ethics/
   literature/facets/E_reading_history_meaning.md` concludes that spacing "lowered a cost" and "widened
   the group". Readers of unspaced scripts read fluently. Continental adoption ran to the late tenth
   century; mass literacy came much later.
2. **Word spacing is itself a legibility remedy** (Parkes's "grammar of legibility"), yet the first
   ruling rejects legibility and the paper opposes Nadella as the legibility paradigm. Candidate
   distinction: spacing moved work from reader to artefact; observability asks the human to read more.
   Does that distinction hold in the human-factors literature?
3. **Nadella is not a clean opposite.** He says chain-of-thought transparency "alone is not sufficient
   or dependable", puts controls outside the model (close to "bounded outputs" and to structural
   refusal), and treats the model as an actor. Steelman, then convergences, then divergences.
4. **"One similar move" may be the wrong shape.** Parkes describes a complex of conventions over
   centuries. Part 1 line 118 says algorithmacy engages a coordination, not a medium.

## 7. Facet file format

Two files per facet in `literature/facets/`: `<stem>.md` and `<stem>.bib`. Model:
`../decision_experience_design/part2_ethics/literature/facets/E_reading_history_meaning.md`.

`<stem>.md` has, in order:
1. A header line: facet, date, "Agent-drafted. The author has not reviewed it, and none of the text is
   the author's."
2. **What this facet covers** and **the question the synthesis answers**.
3. **Legend for read status**: *read in full*, *read in part* (say which pages), *abstract only*,
   *not accessible*.
4. **(a) Search log**: a numbered table of source, exact query, hits, notes. Log failures.
5. **(b) Entries**, one per source: full reference, bib key, how you verified it exists, read status,
   what it argues, quotations with page or section locators, and its bearing on H-cost, H-cause or the
   four problems (supports, complicates, refutes).
6. **(c) Synthesis** by question, ending with a list of claims the sources contradict.
7. **(d) Gaps and unverified leads**, including every source you could not open and where a human with
   library access would find it.

`<stem>.bib`: one entry per source, key in the form `surnameYEARfirstword` (reuse an existing key when
the source is already in a repo bib), with a DOI, arXiv ID or stable URL, and a `note` giving read
status.

## 8. Rules

- Report what the sources say, including when they sink the thesis. A facet that confirms everything is
  suspect.
- Quote exactly, with a locator. Paraphrase anything you read only as an abstract and say so.
- Never invent a DOI, a page number, a quotation or a statistic. Write "not found" instead.
- Resolve every DOI against Crossref (`https://api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`) and compare the title.
- Write only inside `submissions/dxd_foundation/literature/facets/`, and only your own two files. Do
  not edit any other arm. Do not run git.
- Do not use the Chrome browser tools; several agents run at once and would collide. List sources that
  need a browser in section (d) and the lead will open them.
- Do not try to get around a paywall or a bot wall. Record the block.
- Tools that have worked: Crossref, OpenAlex (`https://api.openalex.org/works?search=`), Unpaywall
  (`https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/<doi>?email=` with the address in `org_frontier/research/
  oa_acquire.py`), Europe PMC, arXiv, HAL, author copies by `curl` and `pdftotext`, archive.org
  (including its full-text search and borrowable books), Open Library, Perseus, Consensus (at most
  three calls at a time; on a rate-limit error wait 30 seconds), WebSearch and WebFetch.
