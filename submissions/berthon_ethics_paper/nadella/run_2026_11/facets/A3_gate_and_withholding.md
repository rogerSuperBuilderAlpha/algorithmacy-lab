## 3. What each STRUCTURE section can rest on

- **3.2 The gate.**
  - Kousser 1974, ch. 2 (partial-text): the eight-box laws and the secret ballot as "de facto literacy tests"; Table 2.2 turnout estimates for three states; the understanding clause and convention speeches stating discriminatory intent; registration returns.
  - Ragas 2007 (full-text): a Peruvian literacy gate imposed in 1896 on a population that had voted without it; oral voting and prepared ballots in the earlier period.
  - Gingerich 2019 (abstract): the same ballot-design mechanism in Brazil's Second Republic, reported as measured and intended.
  - Heckelman 1995 (abstract): a competing explanation for turnout falls after the secret ballot.
  - Not covered by this facet: Alabama 1901 as a literacy test in practice beyond Kousser's convention quotes, *Katzenbach*, *Gaston County*, Australia's dictation test (other facets).
- **3.3 The withheld competence.**
  - **Thinly supported.** Only Rasmussen's opening paragraph (abstract) links the 1740 South Carolina Act to the Stono rising. The statutes' provisions, enforcement and the way people learned anyway are not carded here. Williams 2005, Cornelius 1991, Willis 2023 and Monaghan 1988 were not reachable. Margo's review card (existing) is the only source in the library for the statutes' stated motive.
- **Counter (a design that includes).** Shani (Introduction only): India's 1951-52 franchise for an electorate described as mostly illiterate. Fujiwara (supplement only): the electronic machine cuts blank and null votes, with photographs, feedback and digit cues for voters who read poorly. Ragas shows oral voting. None of the three measures the full effect: Fujiwara's headline estimates are in the unread article, and Shani's Introduction has no account of ballot symbols.

## 5. Claims in STRUCTURE.md, ARGUMENT_AND_OUTLINE.md or the ledger that these sources contradict

- None found. STRUCTURE 3.2 gives "Brazil 1881" for the gate, and these sources cover other Brazilian dates (1945-1964, 1998), so they neither support nor contradict it.
- A check for the later drafting (not a contradiction): Kousser ch. 2 says the Southern literacy qualifications came with escape clauses, and Kousser reports that white registration was also depressed (p. 62), so the gate cut white as well as Black voters. A section that describes the test as aimed only at one group should say so with this in view.
