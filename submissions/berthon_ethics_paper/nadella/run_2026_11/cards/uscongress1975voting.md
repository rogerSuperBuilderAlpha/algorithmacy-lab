## Summary

Read in full: the seven-page Act as printed in the Statutes at Large. The OCR is poor in places, with spaced
letters and margin notes in the text, so quotations below come only from clean lines. Title I extended the
preclearance coverage period and, by striking the 6 August 1975 end date from section 201(a) of the 1965 Act, made
the ban on tests or devices permanent; section 201 itself is not reprinted here. Title II found voting discrimination
against language minorities "pervasive and national in scope," tied it to unequal schooling and "continuing
illiteracy in the English language," and redefined English-only election materials as a "test or device" where a
single language minority exceeds five percent of voting-age citizens. Covered jurisdictions must provide every
registration and voting notice, form, instruction, assistance and ballot in the minority language as well as in
English, or orally where the language is unwritten. Title III (new section 203) imposed the same bilingual duty,
until 6 August 1985, wherever a language minority exceeded five percent and its illiteracy rate exceeded the
national rate, with illiteracy defined as failure to complete the fifth primary grade. Title IV gave aggrieved
persons standing, allowed attorney's fees, and ordered Census registration surveys.

## Key facts it relies on

- Title I, s. 102 strikes the words limiting the ban to the period before 6 August 1975 from section 201(a) of the 1965 Act, which the long title describes as making "permanent the ban against certain prerequisites to voting." 89 Stat. 400 [paraphrase]
- Congress's finding: voting discrimination against "citizens of language minorities is pervasive and national in scope." 89 Stat. 401, s. 203, new s. 4(f)(1) [quote]
- Congress named the cause as the states' own: minority citizens "have been denied equal educational opportunities by State and local governments, resulting in severe disabilities and continuing illiteracy in the English language." 89 Stat. 401, s. 4(f)(1) [quote]
- "where State and local officials conduct elections only in English, language minority citizens are excluded from participating in the electoral process." 89 Stat. 401, s. 4(f)(1) [quote]
- English-only notices, forms, instructions, assistance and ballots count as a "test or device" where more than five percent of voting-age citizens belong to a single language minority. 89 Stat. 401-402, s. 4(f)(3) [paraphrase]
- The duty: a covered jurisdiction "shall provide them in the language of the applicable language minority group as well as in the English language"; where the language is oral or unwritten, only oral instructions and assistance are required. 89 Stat. 402, s. 4(f)(4) [quote]
- Title III trigger: illiteracy rate of the group above the national rate, where "illiteracy means the failure to complete the fifth primary grade." 89 Stat. 403, new s. 203(b) [quote]
- The Census Director's determinations "shall not be subject to review in any court." 89 Stat. 403, s. 203(b) [quote]
- Title IV, s. 401 lets "an aggrieved person" sue alongside the Attorney General; s. 402 allows a reasonable attorney's fee to a prevailing party other than the United States. 89 Stat. 404 [paraphrase]

## Critical notes

- Form of correction (question 3): the work moved onto the instrument (the ballot and every election material made
  in the voter's language, or oral where the language has no script), plus a prohibition (the permanent ban on
  tests or devices). It is the clearest statutory case in this facet of the medium changed at the point of use.
- The Act names the wrong in its own findings and assigns it to "State and local governments" (s. 4(f)(1)); the
  committee reports (H.R. Rep. 94-196, S. Rep. 94-295) were not read.
- Limits: the bilingual duty is triggered by census thresholds, not by an individual's need; Title III's duty
  expired in 1985 under this text (later extended, outside this card); determinations are unreviewable. Section
  201 of the 1965 Act, which carries the literacy-test ban itself, is amended here but not printed, so this card
  does not quote the ban.
- Bearing: supports 3.1 and 7-D07 (the page takes on the reader's work); supports 7-D02 (permanent prohibition);
  3.2 (the gate closed by statute); 3.7 (the language standard named as an exclusion by Congress).
