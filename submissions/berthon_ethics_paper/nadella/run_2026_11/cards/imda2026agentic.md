## Summary

Read: cover, contents and pp. 3-6, 11, 13-14, 25-30, 42-44, 46-49 and 52 of 53 pages; the rest searched, not read. A Singapore public body's framework of "emerging best practices" (p. 13) with four dimensions: assess and bound risks upfront, make humans meaningfully accountable, implement technical controls and processes, and enable end-user responsibility. It addresses the organisations that deploy agents. It does name "end users", and it splits them into those who interact with agents (customer service and HR agents, "mostly external-facing") and those who integrate agents into their own work. For the first group it asks the deployer for transparency: say it is an agent, what it may do, how data is used, and whom to contact. It is worded as what organisations "should consider" and states no legal duty and no right of action. It names no remedy or compensation for a person harmed.

## Key facts it relies on

- Accountability sits with the deployer: "The organisations that deploy agents and the humans who oversee them remain accountable for the agents' actions." [quote] (p. 25, 2.2)
- Under "Outside the organisation" the duties run between firms: organisations should "Clarify distribution of obligations in any terms and conditions or contracts between the organisation and the external party" [quote] (p. 28). The same page, under "End users", says organisations "should ensure that users are provided sufficient information to hold the organisation accountable" [quote] (p. 28); it states no mechanism.
- Logging and halting are for the deployer: organisations should "stop agent workflow and escalate to a human supervisor" when failures are detected [quote] (p. 43, 2.3.3). They should "Maintain complete audit trails by ensuring that problematic agent trajectories and failures cannot be deleted" [quote] (p. 44).
- Disclosure to people who interact with agents is the nearest the document comes to the person acted on: "Declare upfront in the user interface that the users are interacting with agents at the point of interaction" [quote] (p. 47, 2.4.2)
- The same page asks for sight of the limits: "Inform the users on the range of actions and decisions that the agent is authorised to perform and make." [quote] (p. 47)
- It asks for a route to a human: "Provide users with the respective human contact points who are responsible for the agents, whom the users can alert if the agents malfunction or if they are dissatisfied with a decision." [quote] (p. 47)
- Users may set their own limits: "Where appropriate, allow users to set their own approval thresholds and boundaries for agent actions based on their individual risk tolerance, beyond organisation-defined limits." [quote] (p. 47, in the 2.4.2 list for users who interact with an organisation's agents). On p. 29 ("User-defined") the users meant are those the agent acts for: "agents may act on behalf of users who have different risk appetites" [quote] (p. 29).
- Workday's recruiter-agent example describes transparency toward HR users, with the agent not making hiring decisions [paraphrase] (p. 48). The candidate screened is not addressed.
- Liability appears once, as a trigger for human approval: "actions that may trigger liability" [quote] (p. 29). Whole-text search found no redress, compensation or insurance provision. [paraphrase] (absence claim; search of the saved text)

## Critical notes

Contests the paper's claim in part: this public-body framework does ask deployers for disclosure, an account of the agent's range of action, and a human contact for the person dissatisfied with a decision, which is more than the vendor frameworks in this facet give. Limits: written as what organisations "should consider" (pp. 25, 46), with no legal duty stated, and the word "voluntary" does not occur in the text; the contact point is for users, and the framework's examples concentrate on staff and customers of the deployer; no standing, no loss cover; logs are for the deployer's monitoring, not for the person. Read depth is partial, so claims about parts not read (pp. 7-10, 12, 15-24, 31-41, 45, 50-51, 53) are not made.

Bearing: Person acted on gets: record no (audit trails are the deployer's, pp. 43-44); brake partial (a human contact to alert, p. 47; "where appropriate" the user's own approval thresholds and boundaries for agent actions, p. 47; no stop right); envelope partial (range of actions and decisions disclosed to users who interact with agents, p. 47); standing partial (contact point where "dissatisfied with a decision", p. 47; "sufficient information to hold the organisation accountable", p. 28; no right to contest); loss cover no (p. 29 mentions liability only as an approval trigger; p. 28 allocates obligations by contract between firms). Structure sections 4.3, 4.4-stop, 4.4-contest: narrows the claim, because disclosure and escalation to a human are present; 4.4-record and 4.4-loss: supports.
