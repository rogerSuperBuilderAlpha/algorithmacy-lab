## Summary
Read in full. This three-page piece, adapted from a conference talk (note 1), is Graff's survey of where literacy studies stands. He says the field embraces two more-or-less opposing positions, "many literacies" and alarm at "dangerously low levels of literacy", and that practice and context replaced presumptions of literacy's "unmediated powers". He describes the relabeling of rhetoric and composition programs around literacy as "the lure of literacy" for currency, enrollment and funding. He argues that the field lacks "an adequate political economy of literacy" and that the United States may have "literacy deficits and literacy surpluses" at once. He holds that after decades of criticism of literacy's autonomous power, "familiar and new hierarchies have returned". The piece is programmatic and gives no historical evidence of its own.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Practice and context, explored in a variety of contexts and traditions, replaced presumptions of the unmediated powers and advantages of literacy." [quote] p. 15.
- "This act represents what I call “the lure of literacy” for currency and relevance, and enrollments and funding." [quote] p. 15.
- "Similarly, and, more seriously, we lack an adequate political economy of literacy." [quote] p. 16.
- "In the United States today, we may suffer simultaneously and incommensurably from literacy deficits and literacy surpluses." [quote] p. 16.
- "The literacy needs of a “knowledge economy,” we easily forget, do not bring employment and rewards to all those in search of fair work and pay, regardless of their ability to read and write across different media and different languages." [quote] p. 16.
- "After several decades of sharp criticism of the autonomous, independent powers of literacy, both familiar and new hierarchies have returned." [quote] p. 17.
