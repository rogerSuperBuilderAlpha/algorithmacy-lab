## Summary

Abstract only; paraphrase. Schwartz argues that the study of Ottoman printing took its frame from the European experience of print, and that this frame still distorts the scholarship. She sets a factual overview of printing among Ottomans against its historiographical portrayal. She then traces the rumor that two Ottoman sultans banned print: it began in early modern Europe, Ottomans adopted it in the nineteenth century, and twentieth-century historians fixed it.
