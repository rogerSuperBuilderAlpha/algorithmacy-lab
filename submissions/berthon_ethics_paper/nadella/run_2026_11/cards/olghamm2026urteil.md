## Summary

Read in full: header, Tenor and paras 1-118; Anlage K1, which the Tenor incorporates, is not reproduced on the
database page. A registered consumer association sued a cosmetic-treatment firm whose website chatbot, asked on
3 April 2025, answered that the firm's two physician directors were specialists in plastic and aesthetic surgery,
which they were not. The firm argued that the chatbot answered autonomously, by statistical calculation, so its
outputs were not the firm's own commercial acts, and that consumers know AI answers need fact-checking. The Higher
Regional Court held the chatbot's answers to be the firm's own commercial acts under § 2(1) no. 2 UWG, because the
firm operated it, set its frame of action and could have it reprogrammed (as it did, with a prompt instruction and a
keyword filter); the „Black-Box-Problem“ did not change this. In the alternative it held that the firm would also
answer on duty-of-care or Störer principles, since the court found it reasonable to expect the firm to prevent the
false answers before first putting the chatbot into service (paras 82-95), and it left open the case of answers provoked by
a tendentious question (para 96). It rejected the fact-checking defence as resting on no rule of experience. The firm
was enjoined (fine up to EUR 250,000 per breach) and ordered to pay the association EUR 260 in warning costs. The
page header marks the judgment final ("rechtskräftig"), although the Tenor and para 118 grant leave to appeal.

## Key facts it relies on

- Tenor and para 2: the claimant is an association entered in the list under § 4 UKlaG, suing for an injunction and warning costs; the defendant is ordered to pay "an den Kläger 260,00 Euro". Para 112: the sum is the association's flat-rate warning costs (§ 5 UKlaG with § 13(3) UWG). [quote; paraphrase]
- Para 6 (date and question in para 5): on 3 April 2025 the chatbot answered "Ja, A und B sind Fachärzte für plastische und ästhetische Chirurgie." The judgment does not say who put the questions; the transcript is the claimant's exhibit, Anlage K1 (Tenor; para 43). Para 5: the chatbot also let visitors book appointments, and each quoted answer ends "Möchten Sie einen Termin vereinbaren?" (paras 6, 8, 10). [quote; paraphrase]
- Para 12: after the warning letter the firm deactivated the chatbot and added "einer Prompt-Anweisung zur neutralen Antwort bei Fragen mit dem Begriff „Facharzt“ sowie eines nachgelagerten Keyword-Filters". Para 13: the firm admitted the answers were false and called them a "bedauerliches technisches Versehen", but gave no cease-and-desist undertaking. [quote; paraphrase]
- Para 30 (the firm's case): the chatbot works "autonom und werde nicht im Einzelnen gesteuert oder kontrolliert, so dass dessen Äußerungen nicht auf eine menschliche Willensentschließung zurückzuführen seien." [quote]
- Para 30 (the firm's case): a third company developed, configured and trained the chatbot on the firm's instruction, using only the firm's own website texts and FAQs, which held no false statement on the directors' qualifications. Para 71: the court treats the deployment as resting on the decision "der Beklagten und des in ihrem Auftrag tätigen IT-Dienstleisters (§ 8 Abs. 2 UWG)"; para 94: the firm answers for that agent under § 8(2) UWG. [paraphrase; quote]
- Para 70: "In Anbetracht dessen stellt der Chatbot (lediglich) ein technisches Mittel dar, dessen sich die Beklagte zur Kommunikation mit potentiellen Kunden/Patienten bediente und über das sie hinreichende Steuerungsgewalt besaß." (The chatbot is merely a technical means the firm used to communicate with potential customers and patients, over which it had sufficient control.) [quote; translation by the drafter, amended]
- Para 71: the „Black-Box-Problem“ does not change the result; the chatbot does not differ materially in law from the contract-document generator in BGH I ZR 113/20 (2021, cited at para 69). [paraphrase]
- Para 73: the use of AI, including chatbots, constitutes commercial acts "seines Betreibers, weil dieser hinreichenden Einfluss auf das System hat und es in Gang setzt" (of its operator, because the operator has sufficient influence on the system and sets it going), consistent with art. 2(d) of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. The court adopts this from the commentary it cites (Köhler; Alexander; Dornis, GRUR 2023). [quote; translation by the drafter; paraphrase]
- Para 77: the AI chatbot is not a third party; the firm is liable for its own infringement, not for a duty of care over a third party's. [paraphrase]
- Paras 82-95 (alternative ground): even on duty-of-care or Störer principles the firm would answer. Para 91: "Zudem war es der Beklagten - entgegen ihrer Ansicht - auch zumutbar, bereits vor der ersten Inbetriebnahme des Chatbots sicherzustellen, dass dieser keine unzutreffenden Auskünfte zu der Facharztqualifikation der bei ihr tätigen Ärzte/innen, insbesondere ihrer Geschäftsführer, gibt." (It was also reasonable to expect the firm, before first putting the chatbot into service, to make sure it gave no false information on the specialist qualification of its doctors.) Para 93 sets stricter standards for conduct in health matters; para 94 says the firm or its agent should have seen that the chatbot "unzutreffende Antworten halluzinieren könnte". [quote; translation by the Fable reader; paraphrase]
- Para 96: the court does not decide whether the same holds for false answers "die durch eine tendenziöse Fragestellung des Nutzers provoziert werden"; the questions here were neither tendentious nor suggestive. [quote; paraphrase]
- Para 104: the firm's claim that consumers know chatbots err and need a fact-check rests on no rule of experience; "Vielmehr trifft es zu, dass ein Großteil der angesprochenen Verbraucher in besonderer Weise auf die Richtigkeit der computergenerierten Antwort vertraut, da Maschinen im Allgemeinen als weniger fehleranfällig als der Mensch wahrgenommen werden." The court adds that a firm whose customers distrusted AI answers would not have put a chatbot on its site. [paraphrase; quote]
- Para 106: the firm's technical fix did not remove the risk of repetition; the court names the cease-and-desist undertaking with a penalty as the usual way to remove it, and the firm gave none. [paraphrase]
- Tenor and para 118: leave to appeal (Revision) granted for fundamental importance. [paraphrase]

## Critical notes

- Limits: unfair-competition law, injunctive relief only; no damages for any individual, no finding on contract or tort, and the decision concerns a chatbot's statements, not an agent's transactions (the chatbot could book appointments, para 5, but no booking is in issue). The court reserves the case of answers provoked by a tendentious question (para 96). A first-instance judgment of a regional appellate court; the court itself granted leave to appeal, and the "rechtskräftig" marker suggests no appeal was lodged (not checked further). A date of 30 Sept 2026 for its final status comes from a search result for a secondary report, not from a saved file; nothing in this card rests on it.
- Relation to `moffatt2024air`: the same attribution to the firm in a different legal order (Germany, collective consumer action rather than an individual small claim); Moffatt awarded damages to the customer, this court an injunction to an association. Para 104 matches Moffatt para 28's rejection of a duty to double-check (checked against the Moffatt card), and para 70's ground (operator control shown by its own fix) is a design-based reason the Moffatt card does not record.
- Strongest use against the paper (the card's inference): existing law already attributes an agent's statements to the deployer and refuses to put the reading burden on the consumer, so the "presumption of reading" pairing (STRUCTURE 3.4, 4.4 "who carries the loss") is weaker for misstatements than the paper says. The alternative ground goes further: the court expects the deployer to prevent a foreseeable false answer before first use (paras 91-95), and attributes the developer's work to the deployer (paras 71, 94).
- What it leaves standing: no consumer is a party or is named, and nothing goes to any person who read the answers; standing ran to a listed association, and the remedy was an order for the future. The firm's fix (prompt plus keyword filter) was the firm's alone, and it did not end the claim (para 106).
- Person acted on gets: record — no (the transcript in evidence is the claimant's own exhibit, Anlage K1; the firm admitted the answers, para 13); brake — no; envelope — no; standing — partial (through a qualified consumer association, paras 2 and 58, §§ 3(1) no. 1 and 4 UKlaG); loss cover — no (the EUR 260 is the association's warning costs, Tenor and para 112).
