## Summary

Read in full: the five-page Act as printed in the Statutes at Large, an OCR'd scan whose margin notes break into
the text in places. The Act extended the Voting Rights Act's preclearance regime and rewrote the bailout standard
for covered jurisdictions (s. 2). It restated section 2 as a results test with a "totality of circumstances"
standard (s. 3). It extended the language-minority provisions (s. 4). Section 5, effective 1 January 1984, added a
new section 208, "Voting Assistance": a voter who needs help to vote because of blindness, disability or inability
to read or write may be helped by a person of the voter's choice, other than the voter's employer or union officer
or agent. The Act names who may not be the helper; it names no duty on the state to provide one and sets no check
on what the helper does. It contains no statement of purpose; the legislative-history note points to S. Rep.
97-417 and H.R. Rep. 97-227, which were not opened.

## Key facts it relies on

- Section 208 is headed "VOTING ASSISTANCE" and added to title II effective 1 January 1984. 96 Stat. 134-135, s. 5 [paraphrase]
- Who is covered: "Any voter who requires assistance to vote by reason of" blindness, disability, or inability to read or write. 96 Stat. 135, s. 208 [quote]
- Who helps: "assistance by a person of the voter's choice, other than the voter's employer or agent of that employer or officer or agent of the voter's union." 96 Stat. 135, s. 208 [quote]
- The verb is permissive ("may be given"); the section creates a choice of helper for the voter, not a state-provided helper. 96 Stat. 135, s. 208 [paraphrase]
- Section 2 as amended bars any voting qualification or practice "imposed or applied by any State or political subdivision in a manner which results in a denial or abridgement of the right of any citizen of the United States to vote on account of race or color." 96 Stat. 134, s. 3 [quote]
- Bailout requires, among other things, that the jurisdiction have engaged in "constructive efforts, such as expanded opportunity for convenient registration and voting for every person of voting age." 96 Stat. 132, s. 2(b)(4) [quote]
- The legislative history lists S. Rep. No. 97-417 and H.R. Rep. No. 97-227 (not read). 96 Stat. 135 [paraphrase]

## Critical notes

- What the usual summary overstates: s. 208 is often described as a right to assistance. The text gives the voter
  the choice of helper and excludes the employer and the union; it does not oblige an election official to supply a
  helper, and it says nothing about checking that the help is faithful.
- The exclusions name the two parties most likely to have power over the voter, so the text itself treats a
  helper chosen by the stronger party as a risk; the Act gives no reason, and the committee reports were not read.
- Form of correction (question 3): a helper owed to the weaker party, chosen by that party, with the stronger
  parties barred from the role.
- Contemporary naming of the wrong is not in the statute; it would be in S. Rep. 97-417 (Congress.gov or a
  government-documents library).
- Bearing: supports 3.1 and 7-D04 (assistance by a person of the weaker party's choice, a working legal precedent);
  7-D07 only weakly (the medium is unchanged; a person reads for the voter).
