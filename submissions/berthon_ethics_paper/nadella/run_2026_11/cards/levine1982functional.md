## Summary

Abstract only; paraphrase. Levine, a British sociologist, argues that the concept of functional literacy has shaped adult basic education since the Second World War. He says the concept's appeal hides flawed assumptions and ambiguities. He offers a critique of the existing notion and proposes a different approach that places literacy competencies in their social context.
