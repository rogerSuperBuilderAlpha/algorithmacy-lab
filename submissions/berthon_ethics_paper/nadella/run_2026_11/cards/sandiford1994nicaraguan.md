## Summary

Abstract only; paraphrase. The article follows up women graduates of the 1980 Nicaraguan National Literacy Crusade, which UNESCO honoured in 1981 and which was claimed to have taught three quarters of the country's illiterate to read and write. The abstract reports that a decade later a significant share of the graduates could no longer read or write, and that many of those who could had already attended school as children. From census and survey figures the authors estimate that about 9 per cent of the population became literate solely through the 1980s adult-education interventions. The abstract leaves other benefits, such as child health and survival, as unquantified.
