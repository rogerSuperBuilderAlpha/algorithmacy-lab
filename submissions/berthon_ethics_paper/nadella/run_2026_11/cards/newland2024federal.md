## Summary

Read: the front matter, the Assistant Secretary's letter, the recommendations and the survivor testimony on language, about twenty pages of 106. Vol. II reports the Department of the Interior's second-stage investigation of the federal boarding school system. It says the schools were used to pursue a policy of forced assimilation over a century and a half. It recommends that the United States acknowledge a national policy of forced assimilation carried out by removing and confining Indian children and placing them in the system. It reports survivor testimony that punishments for speaking Native languages were among the most common shared experiences. A text search of the whole volume for "literacy" and "learn to read" found no hit, so the report does not blame the teaching of reading.

## Key facts it relies on

- Forced assimilation as the policy. [quote] p. 6: "these schools were used to pursue a policy of forced assimilation over a century and a half."
- The wrong is named as policy and method. [quote] p. 22: "a national policy of forced assimilation of Indian children, and carrying out this policy through the removal and confinement of Indian children from their families"
- Punishment for Native languages. [quote] p. 87: "The punishments for speaking Native languages instead of English, even when children could not understand or speak English, commonly involved their mouths being washed with lye soap or varying types of corporal punishment that ranged in severity."
- Recommendation 1 asks the government to repudiate forced assimilation and to affirm the right to maintain languages. [paraphrase] p. 22.
- The word "literacy" does not occur in the extracted text. [paraphrase] whole volume, text search.

## Critical notes

A federal agency report issued by the party that ran the schools; survivor statements are anonymised and drawn from the Road to Healing sessions, not tested against school records in the passages I read. Vol. I (card newland2022federal) holds the historical findings; this card covers Vol. II only. Contested by those who read the policy as one of education in part; the report does not engage that view. Not a record of a rule: the passages read do not give a statute or regulation text requiring English.
Bearing: supports 3.7 (blame falls on removal, confinement and language suppression, not on teaching reading).
