## Summary

Read: the Introduction only (pp. 1-20), the open sample on Cambridge Core; the body chapters were not opened. Shani argues that India's universal adult franchise was a deliberate act of the state's own making, prepared from November 1947 by a small Constituent Assembly Secretariat and carried through in the first elections of 1951-52. She sets this against the colonial franchise, which was narrow and tied to property, education and gender, and against the claims of colonial officials and some Indian legislators that adult suffrage was impracticable. The Introduction stresses the scale: an electorate of about 173 million, most of it poor and illiterate. It does not say how illiterate voters were to mark a ballot, so the card cannot speak to the ballot symbols that the PLAN lists as a correction. Its use for this facet is as a counter-case: a state enfranchised a population it knew to be mostly unable to read, and treated literacy as no bar.

## Key facts it relies on

- The colonial franchise: "The small and divided electorate was based mainly on property, as well as education and gender qualifications" (p. 3) [quote].
- The 1935 Government of India Act extended the vote to "a little more than 30 million people, about one-fifth of the adult population" (p. 3) [quote].
- "British officials unfailingly argued that universal franchise was a bad fit for the people of India" (p. 3) [quote].
- In the 1930s inquiries, colonial administrators and Indian representatives in the provincial legislatures called adult suffrage "impracticable at present" and "administratively unmanageable" (p. 4) [quote; Shani is quoting committee and other documents, in her notes 12-13].
- The roll preparation expanded the electorate "more than five fold to over 173 million people, 49 per cent of the country's population" (p. 4) [quote].
- About 85 per cent of the newly enrolled had never voted for a legislative assembly, and "a vast majority of whom were poor and illiterate" (p. 4) [quote].
- The first elections ran from 25 October 1951 to 21 February 1952 (p. 4) [paraphrase].
- Among the administrative challenges was "an electorate that was 85 per cent illiterate" (p. 12) [quote].

## Critical notes

- Introduction only. Shani's account of how the roll was built is in the chapters that follow, which this card does not cover. The Introduction says nothing on ballot symbols, on assistance at the polls, or on how illiterate voters cast ballots, so it gives no support to the claim that symbol ballots were a design correction.
- The book's own claim that the franchise was India's "own" act rather than a colonial legacy (p. 1) is an interpretation that Shani contrasts with views of democracy as an inheritance of the Raj (p. 12). A reader who weighs the evidence should take the claim as hers.
- The 85 per cent illiteracy figure is stated without a source in the passages read; the card carries it as her statement.
- Who contests: the colonial officials and legislators of the 1930s, whose view she reports, argued that universal franchise was impracticable (p. 4).
- Bearing: counter (a franchise that ignored literacy for 173 million people); supports the existence of a course not taken in the literacy-test cases of 3.2, without evidence on the mechanism.
