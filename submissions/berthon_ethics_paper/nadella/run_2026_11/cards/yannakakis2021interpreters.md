## Summary

Abstract only; paraphrase. Yannakakis studies the General Interpreter, an office that linked Native lords and municipal authorities to colonial ecclesiastical and civil jurisdictions in Spain's American empire. Her case is Villa Alta, Oaxaca, in the late seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, outside the viceregal courts where interpreters are better studied. She draws on Native-language petitions, notarial records, letters and memoriales written by Native scribes, the Spanish translations interpreters made of them, and first-instance cases on land, idolatry, murder, theft and village elections. She argues that patronage, economic cooperation and exploitation, and inter-ethnic networks shaped what interpreters did, and she asks how those who sought justice saw the office.

## Key facts it relies on

- The General Interpreter was an office that made translations of Native-language petitions into Spanish for the courts. [paraphrase] (abstract)
