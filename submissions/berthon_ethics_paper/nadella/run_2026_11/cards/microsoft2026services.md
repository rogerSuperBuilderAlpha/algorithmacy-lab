## Summary

Read: the clauses on conduct, work accounts, liability, AI services and disputes, located by searching the full
extract. The agreement governs Microsoft's consumer services, Microsoft Copilot among them, in the version effective
30 September 2026. Its Code of Conduct names "AI services that make decisions, or take actions, autonomously",
and binds the account holder to follow the rules when using them. Section 14(s) on AI services bars using them for
consequential decisions about any person "without appropriate human oversight", makes the user "solely responsible"
for responding to third-party claims about that use, and states that Microsoft stores inputs and outputs to monitor abuse.
Section 13 caps damages at one month's fee (or $10 for free services), and §15 sends U.S. disputes to individual
arbitration with a class-action waiver. For work or school accounts, the domain owner may control the account and
access its data.

## Key facts it relies on

- Version: "Published: July 30, 2026"; "Effective: September 30, 2026". header [quote]
- "You are accountable for your conduct and content when using the Services." §3 [quote]
- Code of Conduct reaches agentic use: "including AI services that make decisions, or take actions, autonomously or with varying levels of human intervention, you will follow these rules". §3(a) [quote]
- Work or school accounts: "the owner of the domain associated with your email address may be notified of the existence of your Microsoft account and its associated subscriptions, control and administer your account, and access and process your Data". §4(c) [quote]
- Liability cap: the exclusive remedy is "direct damages up to an amount equal to your Services fee for the month during which the loss or breach occurred", or up to $10 if the Services are free; no other damages. §13 [quote / paraphrase]
- "AI services are not designed, intended, or to be used as substitutes for professional advice." §14(s)(i) [quote]
- "You may not use the AI services to discover any underlying components of the models, algorithms, and systems." §14(s)(ii) [quote]
- Record kept by Microsoft: "Microsoft will process and store your inputs to the service as well as output from the service, for purposes of monitoring for and preventing abusive or harmful uses or outputs of the service." §14(s)(v) [quote]
- "You are solely responsible for responding to any third-party claims regarding Your use of the AI services in compliance with applicable laws". §14(s)(viii) [quote]
- The user may not use AI services "To make decisions or take actions without appropriate human oversight that may have a consequential impact on any person’s legal position, financial position, life opportunities, employment opportunities, or human rights". §14(s)(ix)(1) [quote]
- Local law: "YOU MAY HAVE CERTAIN RIGHTS UNDER YOUR LOCAL LAW. NOTHING IN THESE TERMS IS INTENDED TO AFFECT THOSE RIGHTS, IF THEY ARE APPLICABLE." §12 [quote]; the §13 limits apply "To the maximum extent permitted by law". §13 [quote]
- No rights for outsiders: "Except for section 15 (arbitration and class action waiver) these Terms are solely for your and our benefit; they aren’t for the benefit of any other person, except for Microsoft’s successors and assigns." §16 [quote]
- U.S. disputes: "you and we agree to binding individual arbitration before JAMS under the Federal Arbitration Act"; class and representative actions are not allowed. §15 [quote / paraphrase]

## Critical notes

A consumer agreement: enterprise use of Microsoft 365 Copilot and Foundry runs under other terms (see
microsoft2026microsoft for the Enterprise AI Services Code of Conduct). The live page was read, not an archived
version; earlier versions were not compared. The human-oversight duty in §14(s)(ix)(1) is laid on the account
holder. Its nine prohibited uses may be compared with the AI Act's art. 5; this card did not compare them.
People outside the contract are named in several places: §3(a) bars harm to children and to "others" and breaches of
the rights and privacy of "others" (rules ii, vii, viii, ix); §14(s)(vii) bars "misleading others"; and eight of the
nine restrictions in §14(s)(ix) name persons (for example "any person" in (1), "natural persons" in (5); (7), on
scraping facial images, names none). In each case they get the user's obligation, not a right against Microsoft, and
§16 says the Terms "aren’t for the benefit of any other person". The cap and the arbitration clause are qualified by
local law in general words only (§12, §13); the clauses read do not say in which jurisdictions they give way. Duplicates in part microsoft2026copilot on who answers
for actions.
Person acted on gets: record no (Microsoft stores inputs and outputs for its own abuse monitoring, §14(s)(v));
brake no; envelope no; standing no (the Terms are "solely for your and our benefit", §16); loss cover no (the user is
"solely responsible" for responding to third-party claims, §14(s)(viii); the user's own remedy against Microsoft is capped, §13).
