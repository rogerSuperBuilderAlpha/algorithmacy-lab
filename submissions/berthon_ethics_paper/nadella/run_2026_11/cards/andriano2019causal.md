## Summary
Read in full (not the online appendix). The authors use the universal primary education reforms of Malawi (1994) and Uganda (1997) as an instrument for mothers' schooling, combining Demographic and Health Survey data with district-level reform intensity. In Malawi each additional year of maternal education lowers a child's probability of dying before age 5 by 10%, and in Uganda the odds are 16.6% lower. They suggest that financial barriers to care, attitudes to health services and rejection of domestic violence may be among the pathways. They find no evidence that quality of schooling rose after the reforms. The design isolates the expansion in schooling, not literacy, and covers two countries.

## Key facts it relies on
- "few studies have successfully identified a causal relationship between maternal education and under-5 mortality." [quote] p. 1765
- "In Malawi, for each additional year of maternal education, children have a 10 % lower probability of dying" [quote] p. 1765
- "we found no evidence that the quality of education provided improved after the reforms." [quote] p. 1784
- "The UPE policy was meant to increase the quantity of education, and this analysis considered only the effects of the expansion in school participation" [quote] p. 1787
- "The lack of detailed information about the efficiency of the two reforms, local administrative wastage, and corruption" is named as a limit. [quote] p. 1787

## Critical notes
- Page numbers are the journal's printed numbers, confirmed by the running head on PDF page 7 ("1771"); PDF page n is printed page 1764+n.
- Identification rests on district-level reform intensity and birth cohort as instruments; the authors give the exclusion restriction and test it (p. 1784), and note one reform per country only (p. 1787). Its estimates are local to women whose schooling the reform changed.
- Schooling, not literacy; a reform that waived fees. This bears on the paper's duties, since a state expanding access and a state gating access are different positions.
- Bearing: narrows 2.1; counter-evidence of benefit with causal identification. Does not touch the five failures.
