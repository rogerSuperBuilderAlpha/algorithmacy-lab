## Summary

Read in part, from a public Internet Archive capture (6 May 2015) of AustLII's text, with AustLII's paragraph numbers.
The High Court dismissed, five to two, an appeal from the Full Federal Court, which had upheld Olney J's determination
that native title did not exist over land and waters along the Murray and Goulburn rivers. The trial judge had called
the 1880s writings of the squatter Edward Curr the most credible source on traditional law and custom. He gave the
claimants' oral testimony less weight than Curr's record. He also treated an 1881 petition by 42 Aboriginal people,
many of them resident at or connected with Maloga mission, as their own evidence that traditional observance had
ended, while accepting that the missionary Matthews had played a part in composing it. The joint judgment held that
weighing the evidence was for the trial judge and denied that he started from the premise that writing beats oral
testimony. It rested dismissal on findings that the claimants' forebears had ceased to occupy their lands under
traditional law and custom. Gaudron and Kirby JJ would have remitted the case, finding that Olney J erred in law by
requiring a "substantially maintained" connection with the land. Callinan J listed the lack of a written language
among the claimants' disadvantages of proof and said that the spoken word of forebears is at risk of distortion in
transmission.

## Key facts it relies on

- Trial judge, quoted at para 62: the oral testimony "being based upon oral traditional passed down through many generations extending over a period in excess of two hundred years, less weight should be accorded to it than to the information recorded by Curr" ("traditional" is in the source). para 62 [quote]
- The joint judgment's answer: "The conclusion the primary judge reached did not begin from the impermissible premise that written evidence about a subject is inherently better or more reliable than oral testimony on the same subject." para 63 [quote]
- Curr was one of the first squatters in the claim area (1841-1851); his books appeared in 1883 and 1886. para 59 [paraphrase]
- The trial judge treated the 1881 petition as "positive evidence emanating from the Aboriginals themselves," while accepting that Matthews "would have played a part in the composition and presentation of [it]." para 68 [quote]
- Matthews' Maloga mission (from 1874) suppressed indigenous languages and traditional practices. para 65 [paraphrase]
- Holding: the forebears "had ceased to occupy their lands in accordance with traditional laws and customs and that there was no evidence that they continued to acknowledge and observe those laws and customs. Upon those findings, the claimants must fail." para 96 [quote]
- Dissent: Olney J's view that a connection must be "substantially maintained" was "an error of law affecting the reasoning process" that led to the "tide of history" finding. para 123 [quote]
- Callinan J: the claimants faced, "by reason of the lack of a written language and the absence therefore of any indigenous contemporaneous documents, the need to rely extensively upon the spoken word of their forebears, which, human experience knows, is at risk of being influenced and distorted in transmission through the generations ..." para 143 [quote]

## Critical notes

- What the usual summary overstates: the case is often cited as courts preferring the coloniser's written record
  to Aboriginal oral tradition. The trial judge did weigh Curr above oral testimony (para 62), but the joint
  judgment of three Justices called that premise impermissible, denied that the trial judge began from it (para 63)
  and decided on discontinuity of observance (para 96). The
  record-over-voice reading rests on the trial judge's weighing, not on the High Court's holding.
- The written evidence that told against the claimants was in part their own petition, composed with a missionary
  who suppressed their languages (paras 65, 68). The joint judgment adds that the claimants themselves tendered the
  petition, as part of a history of efforts to obtain land, and made no attack on its accuracy at trial (para 68).
- Not read: Olney J's trial judgment and Callinan J's reasons beyond para 146; the Full Court
  (Black CJ dissenting) is known only through this judgment.
- Form of correction (question 3): none; the claim failed. Statutory context only: s 82 of the Native Title Act,
  as it stood when the application was made and the evidence led, freed the Court from rules of evidence and
  required it to take account of Aboriginal cultural concerns; it was narrowed before judgment (paras 144-145), and
  Olney J conducted the trial and weighed the evidence under the earlier form (para 146).
- Bearing: supports 3.4 with care (the trial judge's weighing and the mission-made petition); 4.4-record; counter
  (the joint judgment calls the written-is-better premise impermissible; Callinan J, in the majority, discounts oral
  transmission at para 143).
- Corrected by V-A-2: Callinan J's quotation (para 143) was closed at "forebears." where the sentence continues with his view that oral transmission "is at risk of being influenced and distorted in transmission through the generations"; restored with an ellipsis, since the omitted clause is itself a judicial preference for the record over the voice. Note also para 59: the claimants themselves tendered Curr's writings.
- Corrected at the Fable read (V-A-2-1, 2026-11-18): the Summary had the appeal lying against Olney J's determination; it lay from the Full Federal Court, which had dismissed the appeal from him (para 98). The petitioners were 42 Aboriginal people "many of whom" were resident at or connected with Maloga, not all "at Maloga mission" (para 67; the dissent says "some", para 106). "The High Court majority" on the written-is-better premise is the joint judgment of Gleeson CJ, Gummow and Hayne JJ (para 63); McHugh J concurs in the result on the findings (para 135). The note on the petition ended in a gloss that was not the Court's; it now carries what para 68 says. The s 82 note said "as first enacted"; the judgment says only that it was in that form when the application was made (para 144).
- Opened at the Fable read, outside the drafter's pages: Callinan J returns to oral and written evidence. At para 155 he reports that Olney J "generally preferred, but did not rely exclusively upon," Curr's writings to conflicting oral evidence. At para 190 he rejects Black CJ's criticism, says orally transmitted accounts with "potential richness and strength" may meet and refute contemporaneous written records to the contrary, and holds that the primary judge was right to find they did not do so here. He would dismiss the appeal (para 192).
