## Summary

Read in part, from a public Internet Archive capture (6 May 2015) of AustLII's text, with AustLII's paragraph
numbers. The High Court dismissed, five to two, an appeal against Olney J's determination that native title did not
exist over land and waters along the Murray and Goulburn rivers. The trial judge had called the 1880s writings of
the squatter Edward Curr the most credible source on traditional law and custom. He gave the claimants' oral
testimony less weight than Curr's record. He also treated an 1881 petition by 42 Aboriginal people at Maloga
mission, which the missionary Matthews helped compose, as their own evidence that traditional observance had ended.
The joint judgment held that weighing the evidence was for the trial judge and denied that he started from the
premise that writing beats oral testimony. It rested dismissal on findings that the claimants' forebears had ceased
to occupy their lands under traditional law and custom. Gaudron and Kirby JJ would have remitted the case, finding
that Olney J erred in law by requiring a "substantially maintained" connection with the land. Callinan J listed the
lack of a written language among the claimants' disadvantages of proof.

## Key facts it relies on

- Trial judge, quoted at para 62: the oral testimony "being based upon oral traditional passed down through many generations extending over a period in excess of two hundred years, less weight should be accorded to it than to the information recorded by Curr" ("traditional" is in the source). para 62 [quote]
- The joint judgment's answer: "The conclusion the primary judge reached did not begin from the impermissible premise that written evidence about a subject is inherently better or more reliable than oral testimony on the same subject." para 63 [quote]
- Curr was one of the first squatters in the claim area (1841-1851); his books appeared in 1883 and 1886. para 59 [paraphrase]
- The trial judge treated the 1881 petition as "positive evidence emanating from the Aboriginals themselves," while accepting that Matthews "would have played a part in the composition and presentation of [it]." para 68 [quote]
- Matthews' Maloga mission (from 1874) suppressed indigenous languages and traditional practices. para 65 [paraphrase]
- Holding: the forebears "had ceased to occupy their lands in accordance with traditional laws and customs and that there was no evidence that they continued to acknowledge and observe those laws and customs. Upon those findings, the claimants must fail." para 96 [quote]
- Dissent: Olney J's view that a connection must be "substantially maintained" was "an error of law affecting the reasoning process" that led to the "tide of history" finding. para 123 [quote]
- Callinan J: the claimants faced, "by reason of the lack of a written language and the absence therefore of any indigenous contemporaneous documents, the need to rely extensively upon the spoken word of their forebears." para 143 [quote]

## Critical notes

- What the usual summary overstates: the case is often cited as courts preferring the coloniser's written record
  to Aboriginal oral tradition. The trial judge did weigh Curr above oral testimony (para 62), but the High Court
  majority expressly rejected that premise (para 63) and decided on discontinuity of observance (para 96). The
  record-over-voice reading rests on the trial judge's weighing, not on the High Court's holding.
- The written evidence that told against the claimants was in part their own petition, composed with a missionary
  who suppressed their languages (paras 65, 68): a record made under the stronger party's hand and read as the
  weaker party's voice.
- Not read: Olney J's trial judgment and Callinan J's reasons beyond para 146; the Full Court
  (Black CJ dissenting) is known only through this judgment.
- Form of correction (question 3): none; the claim failed. Statutory context only: s 82 of the Native Title Act,
  as first enacted, freed the Court from rules of evidence and required it to take account of Aboriginal cultural
  concerns, and was narrowed before judgment (paras 144-145).
- Bearing: supports 3.4 with care (the trial judge's weighing and the mission-made petition); 4.4-record; counter
  (the apex court denies the written-is-better premise).
