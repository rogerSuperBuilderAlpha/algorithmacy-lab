## The three errors that would have done most damage

1. saunders1971anglia, p. 7: Lord Wilberforce quoted as saying English law places dependence "on the level of
   education and prudence of the signer." with the rest of the sentence ("and on the honesty and competence of his
   professional adviser") dropped. The paper would have made English law rest the risk on the illiterate signer
   alone, against the speech's own words.
2. yorta2002members, para 143: Callinan J quoted only for the claimants' "disadvantage" of having no written
   language; the omitted clause says oral transmission "is at risk of being influenced and distorted". The paper's
   counter-reading (the apex court rejected written-over-oral) would have ignored a member of the majority who
   discounts oral testimony in terms.
3. dittmar2011information, p. 1155: entry cost given as "a full set of movable type" being "a large share of a
   printer's output"; the source says a complete set of equipment cost 4 to 10 years of a craftsman's wages. A
   fabricated-looking figure in the print-monopoly section.
