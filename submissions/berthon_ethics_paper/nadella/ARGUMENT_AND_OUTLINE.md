## What changed from v2

- **No two stages.** The scribes' correction and the print-era failures are two recurring *choices*, not two periods
  (ruling 3). CA is now stated by beneficiary: no move in the architecture carries the work onto the artifact *for the
  second party*. "Print stage" names England's press regime (1557–1695), the clearest case of the second choice.
- **The history classifies; it does not condemn by itself** (ruling 14). The verdict today rests on two present
  routes: Microsoft's own professed standards, and a present political standard (non-domination, met by Lazar's
  legitimacy account, which reaches the same place).
- **The law gives more than v2 said** (ruling 1). GDPR art. 22, *Dun & Bradstreet*, AI Act arts. 26(11), 85, 86,
  Reg. B and the California and Colorado rules give some second parties notice, explanation and contest *after* a
  decision. The gap is narrower and now located precisely.
- **Responsibility in the terms falls on the principal, not the person acted on** (ruling 10). The second party's
  burden is shown by *Moffatt* and by the documents' silence.
- **The duties name their bearer and their precedent honestly** (rulings 19–20). Five of six have a precedent; D08 is
  a proposal.
- **A fifth question**: who carries the loss (ruling 21).
