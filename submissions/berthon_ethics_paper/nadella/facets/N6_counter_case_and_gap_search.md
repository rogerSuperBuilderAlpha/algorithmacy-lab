# N6: The counter-case against CA and CB, and the gap search

*Facet N6 (round 2), 2026-10-24. Agent-drafted. The author has not reviewed it, and none of the text is the author's.*

## What this facet covers

Part 1 builds the strongest sourced case against CA (Nadella's course repeats the print stage's failures) and CB
(the six duties), ranked by the damage left after the cheapest repair, each item with what survives. It takes the
six lines of attack the brief names: (a) the moves are corrections; (b) the print institutions served the bound
party or authors; (c) a paired failure meets none of A3's three standards; (d) a changed condition removes the
shared property; (e) objections O01–O08 in their strongest published form; (f) historicist attacks on the A7
bridge. Part 2 asks whether anyone has already drawn the print, licensing, Stationers, scribal, notary or literacy
analogy against AI vendor governance or against the post, and reports the post's reception, 10–24 Oct 2026.

**The question the synthesis answers.** After the strongest objections, which premises of A and B still hold, in
what narrowed form, and is the analogy new?

**Premises and outline sections served.** A2–A8, CB (B1, D06–D08); outline sections "Standards of judgement",
"The present case", "Objections"; the introduction's gap sentence.

## (c) Synthesis

### Q1. Are most of Nadella's moves corrections? (line a)

For the enterprise, several are. The post and its siblings move custody away from the producer of the medium (the
model provider) to the organisation that uses it (P3, P5, P17; 2026-07-12 ¶¶5, 12, 16), demand consent at the
boundary, refuse to outsource responsibility (P3), separate controls from the model (P11–P12; N1 conceded P11–P12 as
spacing's kind of move), forbid self-verification (P14, P18), and name scaffolding as the product-design aim
(2025-12-29 ¶4). Measured against literacy's record, that is the shape of a correction: the work and the risk move
away from the weaker party in the vendor–buyer relation. Microsoft's Copyright Commitment shows the vendor already
carrying one class of output risk for that buyer. On this reading A4 misidentifies the bound party: in Nadella's frame
the bound party is the enterprise, and his course serves it.

What the corpus never does is name the customer, applicant or recipient the enterprise's agents act on, except as the
recipient of disclosure in P20. The corrections all run upward (provider to buyer), none outward (buyer to the person
acted on). The counter-case therefore succeeds against A4 as written ("Nadella's moves settle the same four things the
print-stage way") and fails against A4 restated for the person acted on.

### Q2. Did the print institutions serve the bound party or authors? (line b)

They served authors and producers in part, and the record of that is stronger than round 1 logged. Johns concedes the
patentees "had a point" (p. 112), places licensing inside regimes "designed to protect good works as well as to hinder
bad" (p. 119), and adds that no Protestant state before 1695 lacked a press regime (p. 119): there was no unregulated
press for contemporaries to prefer. Cedillo-Lazcano reads the Stationers as successful self-regulation that seeded
statute. N2's 1642 order requiring authors' consent points the same way. None of these sources shows the institutions
serving the reader bound by the text, or the barred reader of 1543; Cedillo-Lazcano never mentions readers. The
sink-condition for A2 ("serving the bound party without outside force") is not met. Johns's null-set point does real
damage elsewhere: a failure verdict cannot rest on comparison with a better contemporary regime, because none existed.

### Q3. Which paired failures meet none of A3's standards? (line c)

On round-1 cards, four of the six failures meet at least one standard: custody (Commons 1695; Milton), the one-sided
record (the Easton clerk dispute, Israeli on the testimonio), the burden of reading (Maugham L.J.'s regret in
L'Estrange, p. 405, is a contemporary objection from the bench), and the gate (1546 *Supplication*; literacy-test
litigation). Two are weak:
- **Disclosure in place of understanding (M3)** fails in its strong form (N3: grade cards) and, after Calo, holds only
  where disclosure is offered as the bound person's own safeguard with no outside reader holding standing.
- **Testing the system, never anyone's understanding (M7)** has no literacy-era objector in any facet. Its standard is
  professed only by later corrections (the 1883 jurat rule; art. 193) and, in the present, by Microsoft's own T1.3 and
  A5.4. As an A3 failure it drops out; as a broken present-tense profession it survives (N1 C7, N5 support 1).
- **Competence withheld, then oversold (M11)** meets A3 through Dakar and UNESCO 2015, but Nadella's corpus is not
  silent (2025-12-29 ¶4), and Cuba and Nepal are counter-instances (N3). The pairing shifts from "silence" to "a
  scaffolding promise with no owed, withdrawn or checked form", which is the D08 inversion and no longer the H04 failure.

### Q4. Does a changed condition remove the shared property? (line d)

Three candidates do partial damage. **The enterprise seat:** staff and enterprise customers are not "bound" as
signers were. The enterprise negotiates terms, can switch models (2026-07-12 ¶16), and receives vendor indemnities;
consumers acted on have ordinary-law remedies where a duty of care exists (Moffatt, paras 26–29). **Speed and scale:**
Kolt (pp. 343, 371) and Nadella himself (P10) hold that human monitoring cannot keep up, which removes the reader on
whom the burden-of-reading failure turned but also removes the reader on whom the reader-based corrections (the notary
reading aloud, the jurat's check, art. 193) depended. That damages CB's D06 more than CA. **Consent and choice of
vendor:** real for the enterprise (Nadella's "Choice"), thin for the person acted on (K12, O01; no new source changes
this). Nadella's concessions (P9, P19–P20) remain as N5 and facet G left them. Kugelberg and Kugelberg's point that AI
changes the practice it enters was carried in N5; I found no new source that sharpens it beyond Kolt.

### Q5. The objections in their strongest form (line e)

- **O07 (disclosure respects autonomy).** Its strongest form is Calo's, and it is not about the reader: disclosure
  disciplines the discloser and arms outsiders (pp. 1060–1061, 1064). P20 ("share learnings industrywide") and AI
  Act art. 73 (to authorities) fit that model. M9 survives only as "notice to those affected, with the outside readers
  being the industry itself" (FMF, N4) and no standing for the affected.
- **O02 (the law already provides).** Moffatt adds a consumer case to N4's GDPR and PLD: where a duty of care exists,
  the tool is no shield and the double-check burden fails. It does not reach non-customers, non-monetary harm, or
  harms nobody can trace to the agent; and it gives a remedy after the fact, not a voice before.
- **M2 / political CSR.** Scherer and Palazzo's position (via Lindman pp. 145–146) legitimates P6's industry standards
  where states lag. Lindman et al. (p. 152) and N4's composition counts answer it.
- **O08 (responsibilization).** No source stronger than Docherty and Valderrama Barragán (V6) was found; Nadella's own
  "scaffolding" (2025-12-29 ¶4) makes the objection sharper, because CB asks Microsoft to do what its CEO already
  claims to do.
- **O01, O03–O06.** No new published form found beyond V6 and N5. Grote and Paulo and Scherer and Palazzo could not be
  upgraded (blocked).

### Q6. Historicist attacks on A7 (line f)

N5 showed that the internal standard binds a present actor only through standards it shares or professes. Williams's
relativism of distance attacks the other half: appraising the Stationers or the 1543 Act as unjust is a notional
confrontation and, on his view, inappropriate (Gowans §7). Cedillo-Lazcano shows the same history run the other way,
licensing self-regulation by its eventual codification. Johns's null set removes the period contrast class. Together
they leave the paper unable to rest any verdict on its own appraisal of the past. What remains is the record of
contemporaries' objections and broken professions (A3), which were real confrontations for those who made them, plus
Microsoft's present professions. Skinner's "no perennial problems" argument, the canonical form of this attack, could
not be opened.
