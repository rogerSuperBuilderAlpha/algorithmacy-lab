## What I found reading each whole

**Diamantis.** Claim first, then the case. Long sentences carry evidence, and short ones turn the argument ("When
Goliath wins, things are different."). He names his own moves in the first person ("I argue", "I recently took my
son") and never uses it to carry a claim. He concedes where it costs him most, and his conclusion opens on
"For all I have said, contextual integrity may still be the best theory available." He turns the opponent's own
example against it (the library card). His one reader-directed device, "put yourself in a neutral judge's shoes",
runs for two paragraphs and stops. The prose is the best of the three by a distance, and it is the 2025 BEQ house
voice.

**Kim & Routledge.** The skeleton fits us best: the argument in premise form in the opening, cases (Apple Card,
Target), a separate objections section with an analogy/disanalogy test, duties on firms. The prose is the weakest of
the three. Sentences stack qualifications. "We believe" carries claims. The paper comments on itself ("which we hope
adds some concreteness to our philosophical discussion"), which house rule 4 forbids. The text has typos ("ax ante",
"philsophically", "assurerance"). Using it as a model would import the problem you asked me to fix.

**Kingston & Carnovale.** The best model for one move: the duties paragraph that admits the duties "might sound
overly ideal" and then names a regime that already has them. Table 2 is the form our duty table needs. Elsewhere the
prose is serviceable, with long nominal subjects (the sample's first sentence has a 25-word subject). §1 is a review
of other philosophers, which has no counterpart in our paper.

## Recommendation

**Diamantis 2025.** You asked for a model of sentence structure and prose density. It has the best sentences, it is
current BEQ, and its thesis has the same shape as ours: a design that looks protective works, in practice, for the
stronger party. Its §3 gives the form for our §3 (and, as a recorded departure, for each historical moment in §2:
one subsection, one mechanism, one worked case).

What it costs:
- §2 history has no counterpart. I would write it in his §3 form and record the departure.
- His objections sit inside the mechanisms and the conclusion. Either we fold ours in (BEQ-normal), or we keep §4 and
  record it as a departure.
- His conclusion offers three refinements; ours offers six duties with precedents. That section will run long against
  its counterpart.
- His citations sit in footnotes, so his sentences end on content. With author-date in the text, I would move cites to
  subject position. Switching to Chicago notes is the other option: BEQ asks authors to state which Chicago system they use, and both
  Diamantis and Kim & Routledge publish with notes.
