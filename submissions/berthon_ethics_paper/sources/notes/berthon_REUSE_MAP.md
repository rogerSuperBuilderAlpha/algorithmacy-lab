# Reuse map — what the two drafts give the ethics paper

*Built by Claude on 2026-10-13 by reading both drafts in full (the Part 2 reference list excepted). None
of this is author text. Line numbers refer to the copies in `sources/`.*

**The new question.** Where did the global community fail on ethical grounds as literacy spread, and
which of those mistakes recur in the transition to algorithmacy?

**Tags.** *Carries* — the passage serves the new question as it stands. *Carries, flagged* — it serves
the question but something in it needs the author's ruling or a re-read of its source. *Out* — it
argues something this paper does not need.

**What neither draft holds.** A documented ethical failure of the literacy transition. The history in
both is about how print and the page worked, and it stops before mass literacy. The research round
(`RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §5) supplies that half.

## Flags the author should see before the outline

1. **Berthon.** `design_ethics_DRAFT.md:120` quotes Berthon and Pitt (2019, p. 136), "business models are
   designed to make us mindless", and then sets James Williams against the mindfulness remedy. The
   draft's library card (`../algorithmacy_design_ethics/literature/library/berthon2019.md`) reads the
   column as a design-side diagnosis with a subject-side prescription. The draft also carries a note
   that Berthon is at the author's institution. He is now the reader. The passage is unchanged here.
2. **The history's standing.** `part2_ethics_DRAFT.md:117` says "the history is analogy and bears no
   explanatory weight". The new paper asks the history to show wrongs. The author rules after the
   research (`SOURCE.md`).
3. **The literacy promise.** `design_ethics_DRAFT.md:106` treats literacy as the case where
   comprehension did yield standing; line 85 of the same draft declines to claim literacy rewarded the
   literate. The two lines pull apart, and the new paper's thesis sits in the gap.
4. **Unsourced sentences that the new paper would lean on.** `design_ethics_DRAFT.md:93` (oral
   instruments failing to govern print), `:116` (enclosure) and `:117` (deskilling and automation
   bias) cite nothing.
5. **Read depth.** Clanchy, Eisenstein, Street, Scribner and Cole, Finnegan and Halverson are cited in
   the design-ethics draft from secondary or bibliographic cards; Graff's card is description-level;
   Saenger and Parkes are snippets. None is quotable until re-read.
