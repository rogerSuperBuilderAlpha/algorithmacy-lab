## 2. Claims in the outline that the new sources narrow

1. **"No settled counterpart to these protections" (introduction, abstract, 5.3).** Two bodies of law
   supply counterparts.
   - *EU law.* The Platform Work Directive (2024/2831) requires a human to decide any termination
     (Art. 10(5)) and gives written reasons and review (Art. 11); Member States transpose it by 2
     December 2026. The Digital Services Act forbids complaint decisions taken "solely on the basis of
     automated means" (Art. 20(6)). GDPR Art. 22(3) gives a right to human intervention and to contest.
     All quotations verified against the Official Journal texts ●.
   - *Contract law.* Unconscionability (*Williams v. Walker-Thomas*, 1965 ●), notice (*Specht v.
     Netscape*, 2002 ●, from an excerpt) and the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015, ss. 62 and 68 ●.
   - *What remains true.* No rule requires anyone to read, explain and certify a person's
     understanding before the act; the reviewer under the EU rules is the platform itself; the DSA's
     outside bodies "shall not have the power to impose a binding settlement" (Art. 21). A narrower
     claim the sources support: no independent, binding check at the point of decision. The wording
     is Claude's.
   - *Caution.* *Hill v. Gateway* (1997) says "A contract need not be read to be effective". The
     court's remark that reciting terms is costly and ineffectual is an aside, not a holding on a
     read-aloud rule.
2. **"Zero leverage" (5.1; now "may give ... little leverage").** Dubal (2023, p. 1984 ●): "Knowledge
   alone will not end or mitigate the precarities of digitalized pay." Cameron and Rahman (2022 ◐) and
   Cameron (2024 ●) find workers gain some covert leverage. The current wording fits both.
3. **Declared operating envelopes (6.3).** Mandated disclosure fails in the evidence: "only one or two of every 1,000 retail
   software shoppers access the license agreement" (Bakos et al., 2009 working paper ●); disclosures are
   "almost always ignored" (Ben-Shahar and Schneider, 2011 ●). The paper has to say why an envelope is
   a commitment that binds the operator and not a notice the user must process. Predictability also
   does not end domination (Lovett, Stanford Encyclopedia ●).
4. **Counter-delegation as the modern notary (6.4).** The Restatement (Third) of Agency, as Kolt (2025 ●) quotes it: "At present, computer programs are
   instrumentalities of the persons who use them." So a user's agent does not carry a
   notary's duties by analogy, and it is a new third party with its own interests. Prior lineage: Li
   et al. (2022 ●, a position paper) on end-user assistants for gig workers; Citron (2008 ●) on audit
   trails; Delacroix and Lawrence (2019 ○) on data trusts. Dubal finds workers' own data collection
   has not changed firm practice.
5. **Structural refusal (6.2).** Exit and consent are the weak answers to platform power (Hoeksema,
   2023 ◐). The principle needs a duty on the operator behind it.
6. **"Deliberate" exclusion (3.1).** A gate with a stated exclusionary purpose is documented for the
   US South and for Natal in 1897 (Lake, 2006 ●). Elsewhere a literacy rule could widen a franchise:
   Italy's 1882 law (reference entry ◐; check the statute).
7. **Brazil.** 1881 is right (the Saraiva Law, Decreto 3.029, art. 8). Engerman and Sokoloff date the
   rule to the 1891 constitution and overlook the decree.

## 3. Aspects the outline does not yet have

Each is a paragraph topic, with where it would go.

1. **A moral frame for what the five practices share (new 2.x, then §4 and §6).** Republican
   non-domination: arbitrary power held by whoever holds the test, the access or the record. It needs
   no proof of intent and no collective agent, and it leaves the three internal standards as the test
   of "failed". Pettit (1997; 2012) ○ must be read in the original; Muldoon and Raekstad (2023 ◐)
   apply it to Rosenblat and Stark's Uber drivers. Limit: that literature treats power as company
   over worker, and has nothing on one intermediary binding two coordinating parties.
2. **Why the past is judged internally and the present is not (2.3, 6.1).** A philosopher will press
   this. One answer, which is Claude's proposal: apply the same three tests to the present. Sources
   for internal critique: Walzer (1987 Tanner lectures ●), people who act badly "create and sustain the
   standards by which … they know themselves to act badly" (p. 42). Fricker (2010 ●) gives an
   "availability" test for blame across distance.
3. **Two models of responsibility (2.5, 5.4, 6.1).** Liability for the historical cases; Young's
   social-connection model (2006 ●; 2011 ○) for designers and operators, which assigns responsibility
   by position and without proof of intent. Limit: her model is forward-looking and cannot say
   "failed"; McKeown notes users keep a share.
4. **Epistemic injustice for the record over the voice (3.3, §4 record row).** Fricker (2007 ○).
   Symons and Alvarado (2022 ◐) describe an algorithm's score outranking the supervisor who knew the
   facts. Limit: hermeneutical injustice has no agent, which pulls against 2.5.
5. **Record over voice is a legal choice (3.3).** Messick (1993 ●): classical shari'a evidence law
   treated documents as no evidence, only witnesses. The outline's cases show courts choosing the text.
6. **Who sets the language of the test and the record (3.1 or 3.6).** Natal's test in "any European
   language" for people "perfectly well able to read and write in their own language" (Lake ●); the
   Valuev circular (1863) and Ems Ukaz (1876) against Ukrainian print (Moser, 2017 ●; Danylenko ●);
   classical Chinese in the examinations (Ye, 2003 ●, on who was barred from sitting).
7. **The gate outside the Anglo-American record (3.1, 7.1).** Literacy rules in the first
   constitutions of most Latin American republics (Engerman and Sokoloff, working paper pp. 23–25 ●).
   Their text says Chile's voters "more than tripled" when the rule was dropped in 1874; their own
   Table 3 shows about a doubling. Use the table.
8. **Withholding outside the United States (3.2).** Spanish royal orders of 1531, 1536 and 1543
   keeping fiction from the Indies (Leonard ●); the Irish penal laws on schooling (Parnell, 1808 ●, a
   secondary account; get the statute); caste, with Phule's testimony of 1882 that Mahars and Mangs
   were "practically excluded from all schools owing to caste prejudices" as the contemporary
   objection the paper's standard asks for (● as reproduced online; check a printed edition).
   Many of these bans leaked. Record the order and its ground, not an effect.
9. **The third party who profits, and who pays him (2.1, 5.3).** Simmel on the mediator and the
   *tertius gaudens* (Wolff, 1950, p. 154 ●); Stovel and Shaw (2012 ◐) on brokerage. The notary's duty
   to both parties and to the weaker one: International Union of Notaries, principles of 2004 ●. That
   text is a profession's statement of its own ideals; the reviews of Burns report notaries serving
   powerful clients.
10. **The platform's two roles (5.3).** It is the drafter and counterparty (standard-form contracts:
    Kessler, 1943; Rakoff, 1983; Radin, 2013; Kim, 2013, all ○) and also the supposedly neutral third
    party. The outline treats these as one.
11. **Fiduciary duty as the road not taken (6.1).** Balkin (2016 ●) proposes information fiduciaries;
    Khan and Pozen (2019 ●) reply that "A fiduciary with sharply opposed loyalties teeters on the edge
    of contradiction". This supports the paper's structural turn.
12. **Enforcement (6.5).** Principles without enforcement do little (Mittelstadt, 2019 ◐; Green, 2021
    ◐). Dubal's remedy is prohibition. The paper should say who holds the claim: user, regulator or
    public.

## 4. Objections to the paper's own term

Two, both for 2.1. Kamperman and Graff (2026, p. 35) fault "literacy" used "uncritically as a
metaphor". A reader trained on Street will add that a word ending in "-acy" names a skill and so
invites the autonomous model. The answer the outline already gestures at is that algorithmacy names a
relation among three parties.

  web search).

  US law on algorithmic management. Whether Benjamin (*Race After Technology*) draws the literacy-test
  parallel could not be checked; do not attribute it.
