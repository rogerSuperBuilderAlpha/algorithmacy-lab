## 1. Verdict

The present-day half of the paper maps five historical positions onto platforms and derives three design duties, and its one genuinely strong move is the observation in §4.4 that the platform collapses the roles of contracting counterparty and adjudicator. It is not ready for *Business Ethics Quarterly* or for a law-and-technology venue: the empirical layer over-reads its three platform sources, four reference-list entries do not exist as cited, §5.3 reproduces an uncited preprint's equation verbatim, and §7 contradicts §4–§5 on whether explanation and user tactics are remedies. The single most serious problem is the safeguards claim that the abstract, contribution 3 and §4.4 all rest on: "the complete absence of independent procedural safeguards" is false as a statement about the law in force, and the arm told the author so (`../LITERATURE_TO_ADD.md` §2 item 1) before this draft reinstated the unqualified wording.

## 2. Findings, most serious first

### F1. The safeguards claim is false as stated and was already narrowed once — **must fix**

**Where.** Abstract ("stripping away the independent procedural buffers"); contribution 3 ("platforms operate without any independent, binding counterpart"); §4.4 ("the complete absence of independent procedural safeguards", "Zero External Procedural Buffers or Independent Explanations", "Modern algorithmacy is historically unique: it places society's most critical economic transactions into the hands of a self-interested scribe who is legally permitted to review their own work").

**What is wrong.** EU law now contains statutory counterparts to each of the three safeguards the draft lists, and contract law contains counterparts to *non est factum*. The arm verified the following against the Official Journal (`../literature/facets/M2_algorithm_studies.md` §5.2–5.3, confirmed in `../literature/audit/AUDIT_05.md`):

- Directive (EU) 2024/2831, Art 10(5): any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate "shall be taken by a human being"; Art 10(2): oversight staff "shall have the competence, training and authority necessary ... including for overriding automated decisions" and are protected from dismissal for doing so; Art 11(1): written reasons "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect"; Art 11(2)–(3): review within two weeks, then rectification or "adequate compensation". Transposition deadline 2 December 2026.
- Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (DSA), Art 20(6): complaint decisions "not solely on the basis of automated means"; Art 21: any user may take the dispute to an out-of-court body certified by a national regulator, though that body "shall not have the power to impose a binding settlement".
- Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 (P2B), Art 4 (reasons, 30 days before termination), Art 12 (two named "impartial and independent" mediators).
- GDPR Art 22(3): "at least the right to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller, to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision". AI Act Art 86: a right to "clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure".
- Contract law: *Williams v. Walker-Thomas* asks whether each party, "considering his obvious education or lack of it, [had] a reasonable opportunity to understand the terms" (`M3_intermediaries_records.md` l. 122); *Specht v. Netscape* on notice; UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 s. 62 (unfair term "not binding").
- Outside statute: the App Drivers & Couriers Union won worker status at the UK Supreme Court and used GDPR Arts 15 and 22 to litigate data and profiling; an Amsterdam appellate court in April 2023 ordered Uber to explain how driver data is used in its pricing (Dubal 2023, pp. 1980–1984, `M2` ll. 60–64; local text `~/iit-playground/berthon_ethics_local/pdfs/M2/dubal.txt` ll. 591–600 confirms the ADCU passage).

So labour courts, data-protection authorities, DSA Art 21 bodies, P2B mediators, class actions and collective bargaining all exist as checks outside the platform. The draft's argument that the EU "human in the loop" "fall[s] prey to the same flaw" because the reviewer works for the platform is right about the *first-line* reviewer (PWD Art 11(2): "the digital labour platform shall respond"; GDPR Art 22(3): "on the part of the controller") and wrong as a description of the whole system, which adds an external layer after it. "Under brutal performance quotas" has no source in the draft or the arm. "Historically unique" and "legally permitted to review their own work" do not survive: the first review is the platform's by statutory duty, and an outside review follows.

**Narrower claim that survives** (the arm's wording, `LITERATURE_TO_ADD.md` §2 item 1, which the author accepted into the Google Doc and this draft dropped): no rule requires anyone to read, explain and certify a person's understanding *before* the act; the first-line reviewer under every EU instrument is the platform itself; the external bodies are ex post, recent, regional, partly not yet in force, and either non-binding (DSA Art 21), voluntary (P2B Art 12) or slow (courts). That is "no independent, binding check at the point of decision", and it is a claim worth making. Green (2022) adds that human oversight policies "provide a false sense of security" (`M2` l. 91), which is the evidence the draft's "same flaw" paragraph needs.

**Fix.** Replace "complete absence", "Zero External Procedural Buffers", "historically unique" and "legally permitted to review their own work" with the narrowed claim, in all four places. Add the article numbers above and the PWD transposition date. Source or cut "brutal performance quotas". Cite Green (2022) for the weakness of human review and Dubal (2023, p. 1984) for the finding that the rights have not changed firm practice.

### F2. Article 22 is not a "human in the loop" requirement, and *Hill v. Gateway* cuts against the paper — **must fix**

**Where.** §4.4: "Recent regulatory efforts to require a 'human in the loop'—such as Article 22 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation"; "a non-negotiable form contract that no human reads (Hill v. Gateway 2000, 1997)".

**What is wrong.** Art 22(1) is a prohibition: "The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing" (`M2` l. 109). Human intervention appears only in 22(3), as a remedy the controller must offer where an exception applies. It is a right not to be decided by a machine at all, with an ex post right to a human on request, not a design rule that puts a human in the loop. *Hill v. Gateway* holds that "Competent adults are bound by such documents, read or unread" and, in passing, that if a seller's staff had to read the terms aloud "the droning voice would anesthetize rather than enlighten" (`M3` ll. 114–116). It supports "binds unread". It does not say "no human reads" (that is Bakos et al., `M3` l. 132: "only one or two out of every thousand retail software shoppers"), and its aside rejects the read-aloud remedy on which the paper's notary analogy rests. The arm ranked *Hill* first among the works the paper must answer (`M3` §(i)).

**Fix.** Describe Art 22 as a prohibition with exceptions and a 22(3) remedy. Cite Bakos et al. (2009/2014) for non-reading. Keep *Hill* but say what it holds and answer its aside: why a declared envelope or a counter-agent does what oral recitation did not.

### F3. The §5.3 equation is reproduced verbatim from an uncited preprint — **must fix**

**Where.** §5.3: "The mathematical dynamics of this collapse are formalized by the Human Identity and Autonomy Gap (HIAG) framework ... $dA_t = \mu(I_t) A_t dt + \sigma_A A_t dW_t^A$ ... \sigma_A > 0 defines autonomy volatility, and W_t^A is a standard Brownian motion"; the "transparency paradox" attributed to Pasquale (2015); the "verification tax" in the §5.3 diagram and the §7.2 table "(HIAG)".

**Source found.** The equation is Equation 1 of Margondai and Mouloua, "The Transparency Paradox in Explainable AI: A Theory of Autonomy Depletion Through Cognitive Load", arXiv 2601.13973, submitted 20 January 2026, whose text reads "where μ(I_t) is the drift function, σ_A>0 is autonomy volatility, and W_t^A is standard Brownian motion" and defines I* as "the transparency level above which cognitive load systematically depletes autonomy" (§2.2). That paper credits "The Human Identity and Autonomy Gap (HIAG) framework (Margondai et al., 2025)" as its "conceptual foundation" (§1.1); the HIAG paper itself is a systematic review in the *Proceedings of the HFES Annual Meeting* 69(1), 1403–1408 (2025), which I saw only as a search record. The draft's sentence and symbol definitions match the preprint word for word, and neither work is in the reference list. The preprint does not cite Pasquale (2015) and does not use the phrase "verification tax"; "transparency paradox" in the draft's sense is the preprint's title, not Pasquale's (Pasquale is unread in the arm, `../literature/references.bib` l. 2923). The term of art in the legal literature is Edwards and Veale's "transparency fallacy" (2017, p. 67, `M2` l. 24).

**Is the model meaningful as stated?** No, on three counts. (i) Geometric Brownian motion has the closed form $A_t = A_0 \exp\big((\mu-\sigma^2/2)t + \sigma W_t\big)$, which is strictly positive for every finite t; it cannot reach an "absorbing barrier" at zero. The preprint knows this and places its absorbing boundary at a positive level, "A_t = B(WM)" (§2.5), which the draft omits. With constant negative drift the process decays toward zero asymptotically; nothing is "driven" anywhere in finite time. (ii) Nothing is measured: A_t has no unit or instrument, I_t conflates a rate ("token streaming speeds") with an object ("feature-attribution tables"), and I* is uncalibrated. The preprint's own validation is Monte Carlo simulation against its own closed-form solutions; it reports no human data and says empirical validation is "currently underway". (iii) It adds nothing to the ethics argument. The claim that disclosure overloads and so fails is already made in words with evidence the arm holds (Ben-Shahar and Schneider 2011, pp. 647, 672, 731; Bakos et al.; Edwards and Veale p. 67; `M3` ll. 137–141). Dressing that claim in an SDE converts a normative point into a pseudo-empirical one a FAccT referee will strike.

**Fix.** Either cite Margondai and Mouloua (2026, arXiv 2601.13973) and Margondai, Willox and Mouloua (2025) for the equation with the quotation marks the verbatim definitions require, and state that the model is theoretical and unvalidated, or cut the equation, the diagram and "HIAG" from §5.3 and §7.2. Remove "verification tax" or supply a source. Re-attribute "transparency paradox", or use Edwards and Veale's "transparency fallacy" with its page.

### F6. The three duties are presented as new where law and literature already supply them, and the objections raised are not answered — **must fix**

**5.2 Structural refusal.** "backed by a legal guarantee that choosing the non-algorithmic pathway will carry no penalty or loss of standing (Zong & Matias, 2024)": Zong and Matias's abstract proposes "a framework for data refusal from below ... from the standpoint of people who refuse" with four facets (autonomy, time, power, cost); it proposes no legal guarantee. Prior art the draft must position against: GDPR Art 22(1) itself (a right not to be subject to a solely automated decision); PWD Art 7(1) (bans on processing emotional state, private conversations, off-duty data and protected inferences, `M2` l. 103); NYC Local Law 144 (bias audit "within one year of the use of the tool", posted summary, notice "10 business days prior to use", per the DCWP page I opened, which mentions no right to an alternative process); Dubal's "nonwaivable peremptory ban" (`M2` l. 66); and Hoeksema's point that exit and consent are the weak answers (`LITERATURE_TO_ADD.md` §2 item 5). Cameron's finding that "choice-based consent can mask the more-structurally problematic elements of the work" is the objection to refusal-as-choice, and the draft cites Cameron without meeting it. Feasibility: "manual underwriting ... at comparable processing speeds" names no bearer of the cost; in US credit, Regulation B already requires "a statement of specific reasons" for adverse action (12 C.F.R. § 1002.9, `M3` l. 153), which is the nearest existing duty.

**5.3 Declared operating envelopes.** "binding pay floors per minute and mile" are not a proposal; two cities enforce them. Seattle's App-Based Worker Minimum Payment Ordinance (SMC 8.37) set $0.45 per minute, $0.77 per mile and $5.20 per offer for 2025 and $0.47/$0.80/$5.34 for 2026 (Office of Labor Standards page, seen through a search summary); New York City's minimum pay for app-based restaurant delivery workers reached $21.44 per hour before tips on 1 April 2025 (DCWP release, seen through a search summary). PWD Art 9(1)(b)(iii) already requires disclosure of "the main parameters ... and the relative importance of those main parameters", which is the workable wording for what an envelope discloses without disclosing the model; P2B Art 5(6) and Uber and Ola's security objection (Dubal p. 1983) are the trade-secret replies the draft must answer. "Mathematically guaranteed performance boundaries": for a learned or continuously retrained dispatch system there is no proof of a model property; a guarantee can only be (a) a rule enforced outside the model, as the pay floors are (computed and topped up after the fact), or (b) a runtime monitor that blocks outputs outside the envelope. The draft should say which, because (a) is law and (b) is design, and only (b) is the operator's duty. "explicitly bounds the maximum dispatch radius before a shift begins (Rosenblat & Stark, 2016)": Rosenblat and Stark describe blind acceptance; they propose no radius bound. Finally, the envelope is a disclosure unless something binds the operator; the arm's note stands: "The paper has to say why an envelope is a commitment that binds the operator and not a notice the user must process" (`LITERATURE_TO_ADD.md` §2 item 3), and the draft's own §5.3 evidence (Ben-Shahar and Schneider) is the objection.

**5.4 Infrastructural counter-delegation.** Prior art the draft does not cite: Li et al. (2022), a position paper proposing "end-user-programmable intelligent assistants" for gig workers (`M2` l. 134); Citron (2008, p. 1305): "automated systems should generate audit trails that record the facts and rules supporting their decisions" (`M3` l. 186); Calacci and Pentland's worker-centric audit tools; and, in practice, Driver's Seat and WeClock, which Dubal finds "useful for workers who have been able to contest unfair suspensions or terminations" while the problem remains "primarily a structural problem, not necessarily one that is rooted in control over and legibility of data" (pp. 1985–1986, `M2` l. 65). The draft cites Viljoen (2021) and Delacroix and Lawrence (2019) for user-side agents; Viljoen's "horizontal" data relation is between data subjects whose data is pooled, not an agent acting against a platform (`M3` l. 171), and Delacroix and Lawrence is unread in the arm. Kolt: the draft's "legally an instrument of its principal rather than an independent public officer (Kolt, 2025)" compresses Kolt's footnote 26, which quotes the Restatement (Third) of Agency § 1.04 cmt. e, "a computer program is not capable of acting as a principal or an agent ... At present, computer programs are instrumentalities of the persons who use them", and then notes the Restatement's "at present" and its single supporting citation (local `kolt.txt` ll. 465–476). The "independent public officer" contrast is the draft's. Kolt's actual issue for §5.4 is loyalty: "ensuring that those agents act in the best interests of their users" (`M2` l. 138), which raises who supplies the agent. §5.4 names the CFAA, trade secrets and agency law and then asserts that policy "must carve out protected legal standing"; that is the objection restated, not answered. Two design gaps: a user-side log records what the user's device was shown, not the platform's internal state, so it cannot by itself decide a pay dispute; and declaring that arbitration "is decided against" the user's record reverses record-over-voice rather than curing it unless someone certifies the record, which is where the diagram's "clearinghouse" would matter and the text never defines it.

**Fix.** For each duty, cite the enacted or proposed counterpart above, state what the duty adds, and answer the named objection (consent manufacture; trade secret and gaming; agent loyalty and supply). Replace "mathematically guaranteed" with the enforcement mechanism meant. Correct the Zong and Matias and Kolt attributions.

### F7. §7.1 contradicts §4.2 and §5.3, and its technical claims are not supportable — **must fix**

**Interpretability Layer.** §4.2: "Explaining the internal weights of an algorithmic model does nothing to cure domination". §5.3: "Providing a user with an uncurated stream of model weights is the modern equivalent of ... scriptio continua." §7.1: the engine "extracts the specific representations activated during an inference pass" by sparse autoencoders and circuit tracing to "restore cognitive coherence". The third sentence proposes the thing the first two condemn. On the technical claim: Anthropic's own account of attribution graphs says "our attribution graphs provide us with satisfying insight for about a quarter of the prompts we've tried", that the replacement model "doesn't attempt to replace the attention layers of the original model", that error nodes "cannot be interpreted", and that the case studies are existence proofs the authors "cannot guarantee" generalise (transformer-circuits.pub, "On the Biology of a Large Language Model", introduction and method overview). The methods also require white-box access to weights and activations, which the platform holds and which §4.4 calls a "Proprietary Black Box"; and dispatch and screening systems are not in general transformer language models. The layer therefore presupposes the operator's cooperation and would deliver the "cognitive dumping" §5.3 rejects.

**Provenance Layer.** C2PA's explainer defines a manifest as "information about the provenance of an asset consisting of one or more assertions that are digitally signed", signed "with the private key of the software or hardware that performed the operations"; it states that "provenance information alone cannot tell you whether the digital content is true, accurate or factual", that "Adding provenance to an asset is optional", and that manifests can be removed. It says nothing about "training lineage" or "who owns it" beyond an optional creator assertion about AI-training permission. It is a media-asset standard signed by the creator's own tools; it does not notarise a dispatch or a screening decision, and the diagram's "EU Art. 22 / C2PA Provenance / Irrevocable Transaction Ledger" clearinghouse combines a data-subject right, a media standard and an undefined ledger into a body with no author, funder, jurisdiction or power. The "Independent Regulatory & Public Notarial Clearinghouse" is the paper's remedy and the text never says who it is.

**Mediation Exposure and Autonomy Layers.** "the interface highlights the proxy variable in real time" presupposes the operator knows and discloses the proxy; "parametric controls and manual overrides" on the operator's system are the operator's design choice. If the operator builds the engine, it is the voluntary code §6.3 says cannot work; if a regulator mandates it, it is §5.3's envelope under another name. The engine has no owner.

**Fix.** Either cut §7.1 or recast it as the operator's duty under §5.3 and say who builds and enforces it; remove the interpretability layer or reconcile it with §4.2 and §5.3; correct the C2PA description to asset provenance and drop "training lineage" and "who owns it"; define the clearinghouse or delete it from the diagram.

### F8. §7.2 reinstates the user's burden the paper rejects — **should fix**

§5 opens: "ethical remedies directed at the user fail." The abstract: "educational remedies fundamentally misdiagnose structural extraction." §7.2: "Students must learn to identify hidden proxy variables in administrative datasets, audit models for feedback loops, trace digital provenance across distributed networks, and deploy obfuscatory tactics when systems become coercive." That is AI literacy with a critical adjective, and "obfuscatory tactics" is the move §4.3 says the plastic intermediary "rewrites itself" to neutralise. The reconciliation available is Oeldorf-Hirsch and Neubaum's own concession that "algorithmic literacy should not be solely the responsibility of the social media user" (preprint p. 23, `M2` l. 45): a pedagogy can coexist with operator duties if the paper says the duty sits with the operator and the pedagogy is secondary. The draft does not say so, and the §7.2 table's "(HIAG)" cell inherits F3. Fix: state the division of responsibility, drop "obfuscatory tactics" or answer §4.3, and remove the HIAG cell.

### F9. The triad is not applied consistently, and the diagram turns it into a dyad — **should fix**

The definition (§1 para 3, §2.1): two human parties coordinate through an automated third that reads both and binds both. The §2.1 diagram labels flank B "Employer / Capital" and the arrow from the intermediary to B "Extracted Surplus". In ride-hail the second human is the rider; the platform is the capital and sets the pay, which makes it the counterparty as well as the third (the arm's "two roles", `M3` ll. 16, 254; `LITERATURE_TO_ADD.md` §3 item 10). Labelled as drawn, the figure is a labour–capital dyad with an algorithm as management tool, which is Muldoon and Raekstad's and Stark and Vanden Broeck's frame (their abstract: "on platforms they are co-opted"), not a triad; and Stark and Vanden Broeck, cited for the triad definition, do not state it in the abstract, the only part the arm has read. The examples: hiring vendor between applicant and employer, a true triad and the paper's best case; a credit bureau between borrower and lender, a true triad the paper does not use, whereas "a lending algorithm" (§5.3) is the lender's own model, a dyad; content moderation ("flags a user's post as hate speech", §4.4) is user versus platform unless the audience is the second party, which the draft does not say; "consumers scroll through algorithmic feeds" (§2.2) is a dyad. Does the paper need the triad? Yes: the notary and second-interpreter analogies, and the "self-reviewing scribe" point, only work if the platform stands between two others; if it is the counterparty-drafter, the governing law is adhesion and unconscionability (`M3` §Q3), which the draft does not engage. Fix: relabel flank B as the second user (rider, employer, lender), move "capital" into the intermediary box, state the two-roles point in §4.4 as the paper's own, and restrict examples to cases that fit or say which do not. Simmel's question, who pays the third (`M3` l. 206), would do the work in one sentence.

### F10. Winner and Verbeek are stretched — **should fix**

Winner does use "inherently political technologies" for "man-made systems that appear to require or to be strongly compatible with particular kinds of political relationships" (reprint, introduction), and he draws on Mumford's "authoritarian and democratic technics". But the clean "no other way" case is his, and it is the atom bomb: "The internal social system of the bomb must be authoritarian; there is no other way." For nuclear power he quotes Denis Hayes ("safe reliance upon nuclear power ... may be possible only in a totalitarian state") and "a contemporary writer", and in his own voice he hedges: "nothing is 'required' in an absolute sense", with the political effect arising "once artifacts such as nuclear power plants have been built and put in operation" (reprint, "Inherently Political Technologies" and closing section). The draft's "Nuclear power plants ... demand ... hierarchical military command ... They cannot be operated democratically" merges the bomb passage with Hayes. "Hyperscale deep learning models ... operate as authoritarian technologies (Winner, 1980)" is an attribution Winner could not have made; it is the author's application, and Winner's own test (does the system *require* the arrangement, or is it merely *compatible* with it?) is the question §6.2 asserts an answer to without argument; Winner calls arguments of this kind "much more troublesome". Verbeek: "cyborg relation or composite relation" are not in *What Things Do* (2005) as far as the arm knows; they are the subject of Verbeek, "Cyborg intentionality: Rethinking the phenomenology of human–technology relations", *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 7 (2008), 387–395 (Crossref record opened; text not read), and "background relations and hermeneutic relations" are Ihde's typology as Verbeek presents it, which needs a page. On "script", the arm verified the lineage Akrich (1992, p. 208) to Latour to Verbeek, with no Verbeek text using "invisible scripts" (`sources/part2_ethics_DRAFT.md` l. 99). Fix: give Winner's hedge, attribute the nuclear claim to Hayes as Winner reports it, argue rather than assert the "requires" step for compute, cite Verbeek 2008 for cyborg and composite relations, and cite Akrich for the script.

### F11. The abstract and §4.4 reinstate two corrected facts — **should fix**

"sworn notaries" (abstract, contribution 3, §4.4) and "*non est factum* ... that historically restrained human scribes" (abstract, contribution 3) were flagged in `AUTHOR_DRAFT_CHECK.md` A3 and A4 and struck from the Google Doc: no source in the arm says "sworn", and in *Foster v Mackinnon* the misreader was the counterparty to the bill, not a scribe. Both wordings are back. §4.4's "Civil law traditions established the sworn notary as an independent public officer, legally bound to remain neutral, read instruments aloud, explain their implications to both sides" is closer to the UINL 2004–2005 principles ("act both impartially and independently, outside any State hierarchy"; "safeguarding both parties", `M3` ll. 191–192) than to the 1503 Pragmática, which says the notes are read "presentes las partes e los testigos" (`AUTHOR_DRAFT_CHECK.md` C12). Fix: cite UINL for the modern duty, the Pragmática for the 1503 rule in its own words, and drop "sworn" and the *non est factum*-restrained-scribes claim.
