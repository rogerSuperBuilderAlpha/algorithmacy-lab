## What Claude flagged before drafting (for the author)

- **The post does not say disclosure is sufficient.** It says chain-of-thought transparency is "not sufficient or
  dependable" (P9), and most of its seven principles are controls, not disclosures (model diversity P14,
  independent controls P17, containment P19). Its disclosure-type moves are CoT transparency (P9), "tamper-proof
  human readable evidence" (P15) and incident disclosure "to those affected" (P20). The defensible charge: every
  safeguard the post offers is a text to be read or a control held by the deploying organisation; the people the
  systems act on appear once, as recipients of disclosure after failure (P20), and nothing in the post builds their
  capacity. The v4 draft makes that charge, and the author can sharpen it.
- **The post's "us" is the enterprise**, not the end user (DXD ruling D5 adopted this reading). "The user" in the
  three wrongs is therefore two people: the organisation's staff who must read the evidence, and the people the
  systems act on.
- **The oracy-to-literacy transition** is a different period from v3's (print era and mass literacy). It runs from
  medieval England's shift to written proof (Clanchy) and the colonial treaties, where v3's "record over the voice"
  already sits. v3's other four practices carry over where they show the three wrongs.
- **Drafting.** His standing rule is that he writes the prose. Here he asked for "the entire paper for review", as
  he did for v2. Claude drafts it, marks it as Claude's, and keeps his wording where v3 carried it.
