# Version 4: outline

**Working title.** *Read to Them and Bound: Disclosure, Burden, and the Missing Scaffold from Oracy to Algorithmacy*

**Thesis (the author's, stated for the paper).** Nadella's insider-risk post is the most careful statement yet of how a
frontier-AI firm thinks a deploying organisation should govern models. Its ethical design stops at legibility: every
safeguard it offers is either a control held by the organisation or a text for someone to read, and the people the
systems act on appear once, as recipients of a disclosure after failure. That design repeats three mistakes of the
transition from oracy to literacy: it places the burden of understanding on the person who must read; it keeps that
person in a literacy-based experience when the medium has changed; and it does not scaffold the person's growth into
the competence the new medium demands, which this paper calls algorithmacy.

**What the record forces (narrowings built in).**
- The post does not say disclosure is sufficient (P9 calls CoT transparency "not sufficient or dependable"), and it
  rests mainly on controls (P11–P12, P14, P17, P19). The charge is that its *ethical design toward people* is
  exhausted by legibility. (V1)
- The early law of the transition treated the *signature* as sufficient (*L'Estrange*, *Bailey*), not disclosure. The
  read-over, the interpreter's certificate and the translation mandate were later partial reforms. The post stands at
  that reform stage; history shows further steps were possible: a knowledge check, risk moved to the drafter, a change
  of medium, and teaching to goal. (V3)
- Claims are about institutions, not minds (Finnegan; Street; Goody's own disavowal). (V2)
- Scaffolding is not a new duty in business ethics (Scherer et al. 2023; Vredenburgh 2022), and it can become
  responsibilization (Willis 2008; Docherty & Valderrama Barragán 2026; Martin 2019 p. 845). The contribution is the
  historical case for the duty and its location inside the system, paired with the operator's retained accountability. (V5, V6)

## 1. Introduction (≈1,400 words)
- Open on the post (10 Oct 2026): the trust architecture, "separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over
  it" (P5), "tamper-proof human readable evidence" (P15), the closing line (P21). Its reach (13M views at capture).
- What it offers, sorted: controls held by the organisation; texts to be read. Who the "we" is. Where the affected
  appear (P20). What it lacks: anything that builds a person's capacity. Microsoft's own application card says users
  "should still make sure to review the accuracy of the answers".
- The question: when a competence becomes a condition of standing, what does ethical design owe the person who lacks it?
- The move: the oral-to-written transition faced the same question; the Requerimiento (1513) as the emblem — a text read
  aloud, a demand to understand, the burden clause "á vuestra culpa", and a notary's certificate that it was read.
- Thesis; three wrongs; the narrowings; three contributions; roadmap.

## 2. Framework (≈1,500)
- 2.1 Algorithmacy: the triad (from v3) and the competence it demands — coordinating through a party that reads both
  and acts; why that competence is formed by acting, not by reading (Collins et al.: invisible process; Bainbridge).
- 2.2 Three wrongs defined. *Burden*: who must do the work of understanding (Solove; Selbst & Barocas). *Medium*: a
  safeguard in the form of a text to be read, for a process that is not a text (Ong: the computer "maximizes
  commitment of the word to space"). *Scaffold*: Belland's definition; telling, job aids and permanent support excluded.
- 2.3 Standard: internal critique (Walzer; Moody-Adams), condensed from v3; domination as description (Pettit/Lovett), one paragraph.
- 2.4 Not a great divide: institutions, not minds (Finnegan, Street, Goody); Plato's Thamus as the oldest statement of
  the two objections that survive without any cognitive claim (275a–b, 275e).

## 3. The three wrongs in the passage from speech to writing (≈3,000)
- 3.1 The burden. Requerimiento (Las Casas, Oviedo, Israeli, Helps). Duty to read: *Upton* 1875, *L'Estrange* 1934,
  *Bailey* 2004, *Morales* 2008. The neck verse: the examiner set the text (Cross, Gabel). Quo warranto: the burden to
  produce a writing (Clanchy, cautions).
- 3.2 The medium. Agreements made in speech, bound in writing: *Marshall*, *Delgamuukw*, Waitangi, Treaty 6's medicine
  chest (Morris pp. 218 vs 355; *Badger* ¶52). Israeli's mechanism: a dialogical procedure moved to people who could
  not answer kept its form. The read-over as ritual (CPR PD 32 para 7.1: "appeared to understand").
- 3.3 The missing scaffold. Withholding (v3: anti-literacy acts, Auld/Douglass; Cross: teaching a felon to read was
  indictable; Oviedo's cage). Who scaffolded: freedpeople's native schools (Alvord), the Swedish church (Johansson),
  Cuba 1961 (Bhola) — and whose interests scaffolding served (More; Brandt; the primer).
- 3.4 What the law did when it took the danger seriously, rarely and late: Pragmática 1503; Spanish notarial art. 193
  "cabal conocimiento … atendidas las circunstancias"; probate r. 13 knowledge check; *Jones v Meehan* construction for
  the hearer; second interpreters (Hagedorn). Sudore: 28% → 98% with teach-to-goal.
- 3.5 Four stages, not two: signature; disclosure; check and shift of risk; capacity. Counter-evidence (writing also
  widened standing; intermediaries often served the unlettered).

## 4. Echoes in the post (≈2,400)
- 4.1 What the post asks for, read fairly: controls; texts; P20; P9's own caveat. Where it exceeds the law (P20 vs AI
  Act Art 73). Microsoft's 2022 Standard T1.3 asks more than the post.
- 4.2 The burden: "human readable evidence" for whom? Staff (Bainbridge's impossible task; Green's uninterrogated
  assumption; Elish's crumple zone). Users (application card; "shared responsibility"). The Requerimiento's echo:
  evidence that it was read, not that it was understood.
- 4.3 The medium: the safeguard is a text about a process that is not one. Evidence that form decides (Vasconcelos:
  written explanation no better than none; salient marker 0% overreliance; Calo). The orality objection answered (Ong
  p. 133; Marz; Heyd).
- 4.4 The missing scaffold: nothing in the post; Microsoft's competence offer is courses outside the product (Smith
  2025); the EU AI Act Art 4 runs to staff and was weakened by the Omnibus to "support the development of". Sudjianto:
  "An experienced model validator does not become experienced by reading validation reports." Nadella's own Dec 2025
  call for "scaffolding for human potential".

## 5. What ethical design owes instead (≈2,200)
- 5.1 Move the burden to the drafter: knowledge check before binding; construe for the hearer; users answerable only
  after the operator meets its own obligations (Helberger et al.). Keep v3's independent party (short).
- 5.2 Change the medium: from records to practice — in-task, salient, interactive; Calo's visceral notice; Kulesza's
  explanatory debugging; declared operating envelope (v3) as what a person can act on without reading the model.
- 5.3 Scaffold toward algorithmacy: in-task, contingent, fading; owed by the operator; paired with avenues to act
  (Nussbaum). Evidence is modest (Kaiser et al. 0.10 SD; Kulesza +52%).
- 5.4 Objections: (i) the post is for enterprises; (ii) disclosure respects autonomy (Calo; Solove's consent dilemma;
  Berthon et al. "necessary, but not sufficient"); (iii) scaffolding is responsibilization or paternalism (Willis;
  Docherty; Martin; Scherer et al. on paternalism); (iv) the history is only analogy.

## 6. Limits (≈500)  ·  7. Conclusion (≈450)
- Return to P21: the system that lets us trust the model least; the question the post leaves: what lets the people it
  acts on need to trust it less.
