# Wertli, J., Schötzau, A., & Palmowski-Wolfe, A. (2023). The influence of age on eye movements during reading in early elementary school children. *Klinische Monatsblätter für Augenheilkunde, 240*(4), 591–598.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1055/a-2045-7271 · **Read depth:** full_text (open-access manuscript text from PMC10129412 via Europe PMC XML; no page numbers, so locators are section names; the author list and volume come from the PMC record's metadata) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed article · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** cross-sectional eye-tracking of reading aloud, 127 recruited children aged 7 to 12 from grades 2 to 5 in four primary schools in German-speaking Switzerland; 118 analysed (58 boys, 60 girls; mean age 9.8, SD 1.24; 63 native German speakers); two IReST texts of about 137 words, SMI RED 250 tracker at 250 Hz · **Parties modeled:** person–artefact (child reader and text) · **Facets:** D · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
Wertli and colleagues build normative eye-movement data for early readers. They ask how age, school grade, gender and mother tongue affect reading speed, saccades, fixations and errors when children read two standardized IReST texts aloud (Abstract; Methods).

Results (Results; Table 2):
- Median reading speed by grade rose from 50.1 words per minute in grade 2 to 74.06 (grade 3), 93.41 (grade 4) and 102.2 (grade 5). Median saccades per word fell from 2.52 (grade 2) to 1.05, 1.16 and 1.19; median fixations per word from 2.21 to 1.29, 1.44 and 1.36. Median reading errors per word fell from 0.05 to 0.04, 0.03 and 0.03.
- By age: 57.1 (SD 17.27) words per minute at age 7, 72.11 (SD 24.62) at 9, 92 (SD 26.24) at 10, 102 (SD 25.41) at 11. Parameters did not change reliably from age 9 and grade 3 on.
- Mother tongue: native German speakers in grade 2 needed fewer saccades per word (p = .008) and fixations per word (p = .03) and made fewer errors; the authors report no significant difference by grade 3 for the eye-movement measures, and a speed gap in grades 2 and 3 that had closed by grade 5 (the overall effect of mother tongue on speed was not statistically significant). Gender had no reliable effect. School made no difference.
- The Discussion sets the fifth-grade median against young German adults at 179 words per minute on an IReST text, which makes the children "1.75 times slower."

In the Introduction the authors state, from secondary sources, that "The saccades are typically forward movements of 7 – 9 characters," and that competent adult English readers have a perceptual span of 3 to 4 letter spaces to the left and 14 to 15 to the right of fixation, smaller in beginners and in dyslexic readers.

## Relation to the argument
The paper has no text on word spacing, and no premise or ruling in the argument map rests on its data. The project uses it as an open source for the typical saccade length. That figure appears in the Introduction as a statement the authors cite to their ref. 2, Rayner (1985), "The role of eye movements in learning to read and reading disability" (not to Rayner 1986 or 1998), and I read the card as confirming only that Wertli et al. say it, as the facet already records ("Secondhand"). It also gives a mildly useful context for D2 and D6: children in the first grades need about two fixations per word, adults far fewer, so a convention that lowers the cost of locating words would matter most in the early years of learning. That inference is the project's, not the authors'.

## Caution
Reading aloud in children aged 7 to 12 with the same grade-6 text for all, with few 7- and 12-year-olds (six each); the authors note aloud reading adds roughly 50 ms to fixations relative to silent reading. A study of normative data, not of spacing. The perceptual-span and saccade figures are restated from other work and should be cited to the original (Rayner 1998; McConkie & Rayner 1976, which the paper cites as ref. 14 with ref. 15 for the perceptual span).
