## What it argues

A working group chaired by Guy Debelle (Reserve Bank of Australia) and Paul Fisher (Bank of England) says the problem lies in the structure around the WM/Reuters 4 pm fix more than in its formula. Passive funds and some corporates ask dealers to fill orders at the fix price. Dealers accept as principal and hedge in the window, which produces the volume spike. That "creates optics of dealers 'trading ahead' of the fix" and "an opportunity and an incentive" to influence the rate, "allegedly including by collusion" (p. 2). The verdict: "it is the incentive and opportunity for improper trading behaviour of market participants around the fix, more than the methodology for computing the fix (although the two interact), which could lead to potential adverse outcomes for clients" (p. 2). The group says its work "was completely independent of the various conduct investigations" and that it saw none of their evidence (p. 1).

Its data test the volume story. For all seven currencies the fix minute is the day's largest volume spike, in most cases at least 10 times the mean minute (p. 16). Volatility does not follow: the highest average one-minute volatility is the 8:30 ET US data release, and the rise at the fix is "not especially large" (p. 18). The group adds that participants reported higher volatility in past years at month- and quarter-ends (p. 18).

It describes a "self-reinforcing dynamic": indices benchmark to the fix, trackers transact at it, dealers agree to it, and the market settles on it (p. 13; Fig. 2, p. 14). Index providers show "little appetite" to switch (p. 16). The IOSCO review found WM's oversight "informal and insufficiently tailored" and noted that "WM is not currently subject to regulatory oversight" (p. 43). One staff member chose the members of its oversight group (p. 46). The review checked WM's answers only against documents WM supplied (PDF p. 43).

Of the 15 recommendations, the main ones widen the one-minute window (median feedback: five minutes, pp. 3, 24), add data sources, create a named user group, price fix trades transparently, bar dealers from sharing positions, tighten and enforce codes of conduct, and ask index providers and asset managers to check fitness and best execution (pp. 3–4, 23–31). The group does not recommend banning fix trading because that "would require legislation or direct regulatory action" (p. 27).

## Relation to the argument

D8 and facet J: FX as the comparator for who holds a convention. The fix has a private administrator with no regulator (p. 43), and users adopt it because the market has "naturally coalesced" around it (p. 16). The remedy is a set of recommendations to the administrator and market bodies, with acceptance expected. Legal force "may require legislation" (p. 28), and the group notes that "the authorities" may conclude regulatory change is needed (p. 4). It says the window width should not be "dictated by the authorities" (p. 24). On facet J's Q2 (notice, voice, correction): "Very few market participants we spoke to fully understood the methodology ... Nor did they have any contact with the WM company" (p. 26); the answer is a user group. This is a convention nobody in particular owns and nobody could refuse, which fits ruling D8's answer that the paper does not name a holder.

Facet check: facet J says the change "was proposed by a body of regulators and market participants". Appendix 2 (pp. 33–34) lists only central banks, regulators, the ECB, IOSCO and the FSB secretariat; market participants were consulted (p. 1). Facet J's corrected line, that the report does not say dealers broke a convention, agrees with the text ("allegedly", p. 2).

## Caution

A consensus document of officials, not a study. The data are one six-month window, seven currencies, two inter-dealer platforms (much trading happens elsewhere, p. 16, n. 15) and averages, so rare days are invisible. The IOSCO review is a desk exercise on self-assessment under a "tight schedule" (PDF p. 45) and gives no ratings. The recommendations forecast acceptance; they report no results.
