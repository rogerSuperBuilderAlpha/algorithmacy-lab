# Cui, Y. (2023). Eye movements of second language learners when reading spaced and unspaced Chinese texts. *Frontiers in Psychology, 14*, Article 783960.

**Identifier:** doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2023.783960 · **Read depth:** full_text (open-access publisher PDF, 13 pages, journal pagination; I read the text, tables and discussion and did not read the figure data or the second half of the reference list) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed journal article · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** eye-tracking study of passage reading with 24 intermediate learners of Chinese (English L1, third- or fourth-year students at a US university) and 20 native-speaker controls; four versions of four passages of about 350–400 characters (unspaced, word-spaced, non-word-spaced, pinyin); Latin-square design; EyeLink 1000; mixed ANOVAs · **Parties modeled:** person–artefact · **Facets:** D · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
Cui asks whether inserting spaces between words in Chinese helps second-language learners, using full paragraphs rather than single sentences (p. 4). Comprehension did not differ across conditions for either group (L2 mean 4.33 of 5; native 4.55; Friedman tests p = .58 and .38, p. 6), so the author reads any difference as one of processing, with a ceiling-effect warning.

Native readers: spacing did not change fixation measures, and total passage times were 31.4 s unspaced and 34.5 s word-spaced (Table 4, pp. 6–7); only skip rate differed, with fewer skips on word-spaced text (10.6 percent against 40.2 percent unspaced; p. 6–7). Pinyin was far harder for them. L2 learners: total passage time was 169.0 s unspaced, 159.5 s word-spaced, 181.2 s non-word-spaced and 162.3 s pinyin (Table 4, pp. 6–7). The word-spaced against unspaced gap was not significant (ps > .11), and word-spaced against non-word-spaced gave p = .023 against an alpha of .007 that Cui set for these measures (Table 5 note, p. 8). Word-spaced text produced the fewest fixations per word (2.78) and fewest regressions (37.0), and the shortest regression-path duration (906 ms). Fixations and regressions were significantly lower only against the non-word and pinyin versions, and the regression-path contrasts (p = .009 against non-word, .023 against unspaced) missed her alpha (pp. 7–8). Landing position did not differ for learners under spacing in single-fixation cases (p. 8).

Cui's reading: the benefit of spacing was partially confirmed for L2 learners, mainly in discourse-level processing (p. 11). Her limits: "Given the relatively small sample size and the homogeneous native language background of the L2 participants in this single-experiment study, the results may not be generalizable" (p. 12). Learners reported the unspaced text easiest, yet 21 of 24 said that, once they saw the spacing followed English-like word units, "they felt it helpful for them to read faster later" (p. 11). She adds that spacing is "not the only one option": alternating word colours would leave the script unchanged (p. 12).

## Relation to the argument
It bears on P4 (learners gain most, on mixed evidence). The learner effect here is directional and mostly non-significant, and the comprehension scores sit at ceiling. The abstract's claim that learners "generally spent less time" with word spaces rests on a numerical gap of about 9.5 s in 160 s that failed the author's own tests. So this study fits the "mixed" grade the map already gives P4 and does not move it up. The native-reader null replicates Bai et al. (2008) on materials the author calls easy for natives.

The pinyin result and the learners' own report give a small point for the paper: learners' sense of what is easy (unspaced) and their eye-movement record (word-spaced no worse, sometimes better) disagree. That is a reminder that self-report and measures can part, and has no bearing on the ancient case.

Facet check: facet D's learner row (Cui 2023 listed as support, "Helps") overstates the result. The significant contrasts are against non-word and pinyin versions; the unspaced-against-word-spaced contrast is not significant.

## Caution
24 learners from one university, one native language, passages within their level, mixed models with an alpha of .007 and several effects around p = .02–.04. The conclusion paragraph and the abstract state benefits more strongly than the tests. Cite it as suggestive and mixed, and cite Shen et al. (2012) for the proficiency pattern.
