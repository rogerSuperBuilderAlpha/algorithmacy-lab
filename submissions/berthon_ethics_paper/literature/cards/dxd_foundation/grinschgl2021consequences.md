## What it argues

In the Pattern Copy Task a person rebuilds a layout of 12 (or 8) object images while only one of the model and workspace windows is visible; more switches to the model mean more offloading. Experiment 1 raised the cost of offloading with a 2-s lockout. Without the lockout people opened the model more (3.61 against 2.31 openings), finished faster (49.33 s against 55.06 s) and later remembered fewer identity-location bindings in a surprise test (5.91 against 7.07 of 12, t(170) = −2.63, ηp² = .04) (Table 1; Results). Within conditions, offloading and later memory correlated at |r| ≥ .51 (Table 2), and mediation analyses put the lockout's effect on memory through offloading rather than rehearsal time (Table 3). Experiment 2 added an announced test: informed participants offloaded less and remembered more; bindings ranged from 4.20 (no lockout, uninformed) to 6.28 of 8 (lockout, informed) (Table 4). Experiment 3 forced half the participants to offload maximally: forced and uninformed participants remembered 1.84 bindings of 12 against 4.18 when forced but informed and 4.69–5.18 under free choice (Table 8), so a learning goal "could almost completely counteract the negative impact of offloading" (abstract). The conclusion: "Cognitive offloading increases immediate task performance but also diminishes subsequent memory performance for the offloaded information" (Conclusion), and resources released by offloading "are rather 'lost'" without a goal to learn (Conclusion). Citing Moritz et al., the authors add that "what users learn in this context appears to be how to effectively utilise the offloading device rather than solving the problem at hand" (The trade-off of cognitive offloading).

## Relation to the argument

**The abstract's "internalize text."** If word separation offloaded boundary detection from the voice to the page, this literature predicts that what is offloaded is practised less and retained less. The abstract claims the opposite effect, internalisation. The two may not conflict, because the scribes' move still passes every word through the reader, and the paper's memory test is for the offloaded items themselves; but the draft cannot cite offloading research for an internalisation benefit without saying that the measured direction is the reverse. This is the facet's clearest "cuts against" for that clause.

**Exposure "lowering a cost has costs."** With `sparrow2011google` it gives the cost its size: about one binding in six at N = 172, with strong within-person correlations.

**Facet E link.** The desirable-difficulties reading (self-generated difficulty improves learning) is the same mechanism facet E cites from Soderstrom and Bjork.
