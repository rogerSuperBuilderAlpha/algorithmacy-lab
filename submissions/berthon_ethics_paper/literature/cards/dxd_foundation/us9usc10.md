# 9 U.S.C. § 10 — Same; vacation; grounds; rehearing (Federal Arbitration Act).

**Identifier:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/10 · **Read depth:** full_text (Cornell LII reproduction of the current Code text with amendment notes; whole section read) · **Source-tier:** statute · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** none · **Parties modeled:** two parties + third party (parties to an arbitration and the arbitrators they are bound to) · **Facets:** J · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
Section 10(a) lists the grounds ("In any of the following cases"; the text does not itself say they are exclusive, which is case law) on which the federal district court "wherein the award was made may make an order vacating the award upon the application of any party to the arbitration": (1) "where the award was procured by corruption, fraud, or undue means"; (2) "where there was evident partiality or corruption in the arbitrators, or either of them"; (3) arbitrator misconduct, in refusing to postpone a hearing on sufficient cause, refusing to hear pertinent and material evidence, or "any other misbehavior by which the rights of any party have been prejudiced"; and (4) where the arbitrators "exceeded their powers, or so imperfectly executed them that a mutual, final, and definite award upon the subject matter submitted was not made." (b): if the award is vacated and the time to make it has not expired, the court may direct a rehearing. (c): a person who is not a party but "adversely affected or aggrieved" by an award under 5 U.S.C. § 580 (administrative dispute resolution) may seek vacatur if the use of arbitration or the award is "clearly inconsistent" with the factors in 5 U.S.C. § 572.

The notes trace the text to the 1925 Act (ch. 213, § 10, 43 Stat. 885), with amendments in 1990, 1992 and 2002; the 2002 change moved former (a)(5) into (b) and added the (c) numbering.

## Relation to the argument
It shows that even a private tribunal chosen by the parties is held to a disinterest rule by the state: an award tainted by "evident partiality" can be set aside, so the legal order polices the neutral that parties bring in themselves. That supports the facet's reading, premise P10 and ruling D8: where a third party decides between two others, law attaches a condition to its position. The same text shows how thin the check is. Vacatur is on application of a party, after the award, on four narrow grounds listed in (a); there is no ground for an unfair term, only for corruption, partiality, misconduct and excess of power. For platforms that require arbitration (Suzor reports six of fourteen sets of terms do; `suzor2018digital`, p. 8), this is the review the user keeps.

## Caution
The statute's wording is the only content: it does not say what "evident partiality" requires (that is case law), and it covers awards under the Federal Arbitration Act only. LII copy; verify against the official Code before print.
