## What it argues

The report counts what the Board did in the first quarter of 2022. It is a statement by the Board, not an evaluation by an outsider.

**Intake.** "Where users have exhausted Meta's appeals process, they can challenge the company's decision by appealing eligible content to the Oversight Board" (p. 5). Because of tool limits, the Case Selection Team counted submissions by hand, so the figures are "an estimate and preliminary" (p. 5). An estimated 479,649 user cases arrived, the most in any quarter, up from 288,440 in Q4 2021 ("around two-thirds"), and more than 1.6 million from October 2020 to March 2022 (p. 5). With Meta's four referrals the total is about 479,653 (p. 4). Of the user cases, 94% appealed to restore content and 6% to remove other users' content (p. 5); 45% came from the United States and Canada and 22% from Europe (p. 6); 99.2% concerned Facebook and 0.8% Instagram (p. 7).

**Funnel.** The Case Selection Committee longlisted 29 user cases (p. 7), shortlisted 20 (p. 9), and announced three selections: two from users and one Meta referral (pp. 4, 11). Meta's legal team checks the shortlist for eligibility, which "does not include a re-review on the merits"; "in practice, Meta has also assessed whether its original decision ... was correct or not" (p. 9). Meta judged its own decision wrong in 14 of the 20 shortlisted cases, a rate of 70%, against 39% (51 of 130) for shortlisted cases from Q4 2020 to Q4 2021, and reversed itself in all 14 (10 restorations, 4 removals) (p. 9). The Board calls the sample small and says it seeks hard cases, yet "is raising with Meta the questions this poses for the accuracy of Meta's content moderation and the appeals process" (p. 9). Annex I summarises the 14; in most, the Board writes "Without explanation from Meta on either the removal or the restoration, it seems ..." (pp. 23–25).

**Decisions.** "The Board's decisions are binding, and Meta must implement them within seven days of publication" (p. 12). The Board decided two cases and overturned Meta in both (p. 12). One (2021-015-FB-UA) was a user appeal; the other (2021-016-FB-FBR) was a Meta referral. Both were assigned in October 2021 and published on 1 February 2022; they took 105 and 109 days against a 90-day target, an average of 107 days from assignment to implementation (p. 15).

**Policy.** The Board issued one policy advisory opinion and 22 recommendations in total: 12 on content policy, 7 on enforcement, 3 on transparency (p. 18). Meta said it would implement 8 "fully", 2 "in part", was "assessing feasibility" on 7 and would take "no further action" on 5 (p. 19). Across all 108 recommendations to date, implementation was demonstrated by published information for 16, partly for 2; "progress reported" for 44; Meta claimed but did not show implementation for 25; declined after assessment 7; omitted, declined or reframed 14 (p. 21). The Board says it "continues to lack data to verify progress on or implementation of the majority of recommendations" (p. 21). Meta answered 53 of the Board's 62 questions (85%), partly answered 8 and left 1 unanswered (p. 15).

## Relation to the argument

For D8 the Board is the platform world's nearest thing to a court, and the numbers show what kind. It reviews a chosen docket: about 479,000 user cases came in, 20 were shortlisted, 3 were announced as selected, and the 2 decisions published that quarter came from cases assigned in October 2021. Each decision binds only on its own content, within seven days. Policy recommendations bind nobody; Meta implements some, declines some, and the Board cannot verify most. The Board's gate and its data both come through Meta (Meta's legal review, p. 9; the Case Management Tool "created by Meta", p. 2). So the convention is still held by the owner, with an outside voice added.

Annex II adds a point for the reasons comparison. The Board recommended in 2021, repeatedly, that users "always" be told the specific rule enforced against them (for example Armenians in Azerbaijan #1, p. 26; Breast cancer #2, p. 27; Nazi quote #1, p. 28; South Africa slurs #1, p. 51). Each stood at "progress reported" in Q1 2022. That suggests, as an inference and not a stated finding, that giving reasons was not Meta's own convention before the Digital Services Act made it a legal duty (`eu2022dsa`, Art. 17).

## Caution

The Board describes itself. The 479,649 count is a manual estimate the Board calls "preliminary", and the report does not say how many of those cases were eligible. One quarter is a snapshot; nothing after March 2022 is in this file. The report cannot show how Meta's own appeals process treats users before the Board.
