## What it argues
Nadella says 2026 will be "pivotal", with capability outpacing "our current ability to use it to have real world impact", which he calls "model overhang" (paras 1 and 3). Three things "we still need to get right" follow in a list. First, "A new concept that evolves 'bicycles for the mind' such that we always think of AI as a scaffolding for human potential vs a substitute." He adds: "What matters is not the power of any given model, but how people choose to apply it to achieve their goals", and calls this "the product design question we need to debate and answer" (para. 4). That item also asks for a new "theory of the mind" that "accounts for humans being equipped with these new cognitive amplifier tools as we relate to each other." Second, "We will evolve from models to systems": "rich scaffolds that orchestrate multiple models and agents; account for memory and entitlements; enable rich and safe 'tools use'" (para. 5). Third, deliberate choices about diffusion, because "For AI to have societal permission it must have real world eval impact" (para. 6). He closes with outcomes "for each of us" and computing as "empowering people and organizations" (para. 7).

## Relation to the argument
