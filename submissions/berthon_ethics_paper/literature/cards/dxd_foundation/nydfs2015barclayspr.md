## What it argues

The release announces that Barclays will pay "an additional $150 million penalty" and is "terminating" its Global Head of electronic automated flow trading "for misconduct related to automated, electronic foreign exchange (FX) trading through its 'Last Look' system." With the May 2015 action the total FX penalty paid to the Department reaches $635 million. Acting Superintendent Albanese says the case "highlights the need for greater oversight and action to help prevent the misuse of automated, electronic trading platforms on Wall Street, which is a wider industry issue that requires serious additional scrutiny."

The body restates the consent order (`nydfs2015barclays`) in near-identical wording, under headings: how Last Look works, how Barclays rejected client orders, and how it communicated with clients. It repeats the stipulated points: Last Look applied to all FIX/API trades; "almost entirely in reference to the profit or loss the trade would bring to the Bank"; clients met with "NACK" and vague answers; three 2011 emails (two from a managing director) telling staff to "obfuscate and stonewall" and to "just blame it on the weekend IT release." It also reports that after the investigation began Barclays made the filter symmetrical in September and October 2014, but that 7% of trading volume stayed asymmetric until August 2015. It also reports (the release has no paragraph numbers; the earlier "¶26" locator belongs to the order and was not checked here) that some sophisticated customers who monitored rejection rates raised concerns, and Barclays "sometimes adjusted hold times and thresholds to decrease rejection rates."
