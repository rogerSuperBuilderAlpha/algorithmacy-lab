## What it argues

The FCA fined five banks £1,114,918,000 ($1.7 billion) for "failing to control business practices in their G10 spot foreign exchange (FX) trading operations": Citibank £225,575,000, HSBC £216,363,000, JPMorgan Chase £222,166,000, RBS £217,000,000 and UBS £233,814,000. Without the 30% settlement discount the total would have been £1,592,740,000. The fines are "the largest ever imposed by the FCA, or its predecessor." Its investigation lasted 13 months with over 70 enforcement staff. Barclays was left for a continuing investigation.

The finding: between 1 January 2008 and 15 October 2013 "ineffective controls at the Banks allowed G10 spot FX traders to put their Banks' interests ahead of those of their clients, other market participants and the wider UK financial system." Traders "shared information about clients' activities which they had been trusted to keep confidential and attempted to manipulate G10 spot FX currency rates, including in collusion with traders at other firms." Chat groups bore names such as "the players" and "1 team, 1 dream," and used code names for clients. The traders aimed at the 4pm WM/Reuters fix, calculated over a one-minute window (30 seconds either side of 4pm), and at clients' stop-loss orders. The release stresses that "there are no specific rules governing the unregulated spot FX market," yet "the importance of managing risks . . . through effective systems and controls is widely recognised in industry codes." It sets up an "industry-wide remediation programme" in which senior management must "attest that this work has been completed." It cites parallel penalties: CFTC over $1.4 billion in total and OCC $700 million on two banks, and a CHF 134 million disgorgement by FINMA from UBS.

## Relation to the argument

A dated case in which the conventions of a wholesale market (the fix window and the dealers' conduct) were gamed by the parties who relied on them, and which regulators corrected after the fact. Under ruling D8 it supports the comparison that FX is "not a gentler holder": the FCA concedes there were no specific rules, relied on industry codes, and used enforcement and a supervisory attestation scheme, not a client-side right. For premise P10 it shows interested parties inside a convention bending it to their positions. The FSB's later widening of the fix window (see `fsb2015progress`) is the convention-level response.

## Caution

A press release, not the Final Notices; fine amounts and findings are the FCA's characterisation of settled cases. The release covers voice-desk G10 spot trading on the voice desks based in London, or only in Zurich in UBS's case, per note 7, not electronic trading. It gives no estimate of client losses.
