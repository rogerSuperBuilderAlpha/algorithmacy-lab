## What it argues
§ 87(1) gives the works council a right of co-determination in fourteen matters, "soweit eine gesetzliche oder tarifliche Regelung nicht besteht" (in so far as no statute or collective agreement governs them). No. 6 is "Einführung und Anwendung von technischen Einrichtungen, die dazu bestimmt sind, das Verhalten oder die Leistung der Arbeitnehmer zu überwachen"; the official translation renders it "the introduction and use of technical devices designed to monitor the behaviour or performance of the employees." Other numbers touch platform work too: no. 10 (pay arrangements, including "deren Änderung", their modification), no. 11 (performance-related pay rates), and no. 14 (structuring of mobile work done by means of information and communication technology).

§ 87(2) supplies the fallback: "Kommt eine Einigung über eine Angelegenheit nach Absatz 1 nicht zustande, so entscheidet die Einigungsstelle. Der Spruch der Einigungsstelle ersetzt die Einigung zwischen Arbeitgeber und Betriebsrat." (If no agreement is reached, the conciliation committee decides; its award takes the place of an agreement between employer and works council.)

§ 76 sets up that committee. It has equal numbers of members appointed by each side and "einem unparteiischen Vorsitzenden" (an impartial chair) whom both must agree on, or whom the labour court appoints (§ 76(2)). In matters where its award replaces agreement, it acts on the application of one side (§ 76(5)), decides "nach billigem Ermessen" (at its reasonable discretion) with due regard to the establishment and the employees affected, and either side may challenge an excess of discretion in the labour court within two weeks (§ 76(5)).

## Relation to the argument
**Ruling D8 and premise P12.** P12 says the convention moved toward the weaker party only under outside force. § 87(1) no. 6 with § 87(2) is that force in statutory form: the interface owner (the employer) cannot settle a monitoring system alone, and a deadlock goes to a body with an impartial chair. On D8's question of who holds the convention, the statute's answer is joint custody, with a third party to break ties, which is a candidate the paper can name. It is the one arrangement in facet O that binds the owner on paper.

**What it does not show.** The statute covers employees of an establishment with a works council. Most platform workers are not employees, and facet O's one platform study (`neumann2025authority`, abstract only) reports co-determination kept out of "the core of the algorithmic work organisation." The text also gives the council a say over monitoring devices, not over the rules by which an algorithm allocates work, unless those fall under another number (nos. 1, 10, 11 or 14). Nothing here measures effects.

**Use in DRAFT_v2:** Line 439 says § 87(1) no. 6 "grants works councils co-determination over technical systems designed to monitor employee behavior or performance," and that "An employer cannot introduce or modify such a system without works-council agreement or a binding decision of the conciliation committee (§ 87(2))." The first sentence is supported by § 87(1) no. 6 and matches the official translation. The second goes beyond the words I read in two respects. (1) No. 6 names "Einführung und Anwendung" (introduction and use); "modify" is not in it, though no. 10 uses "Änderung" for pay methods. (2) § 87(2) says the committee's award replaces the agreement; it does not itself say the employer "cannot introduce" a system without one. That rule is how German labour courts are commonly said to read co-determination, but I did not read the case law, so the prohibition rests on the statute's structure plus an unverified doctrinal reading. Suggested check for the author: cite a commentary or a Bundesarbeitsgericht decision for the prohibition, or rephrase to the statute's own terms. Facet O (synthesis) makes the same "cannot introduce" statement.
