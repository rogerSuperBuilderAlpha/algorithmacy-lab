# Seattle Office of Labor Standards. (2024, November). *Model notice of deactivation* (based on requirements of SMC 8.40.070) [Form].

**Identifier:** https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/LaborStandards/Notice_of_Deactivation_Regular_form.pdf · **Read depth:** full_text (two-page agency form, revised 11/2024, whole document read; not the ordinance) · **Source-tier:** government model form (regulator guidance) · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** none · **Parties modeled:** two parties + third party (app-based worker and network company, with the city's labor-standards office as regulator) · **Facets:** J · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
The form is a template that a company fills in to notify an app-based worker that their account will be deactivated or limited. It restates, in blanks and bracketed instructions, what the Seattle App-Based Worker Deactivation Rights Ordinance (SMC 8.40) requires.

Timing: "Network companies are required to provide app-based workers with notice of deactivation 14 days before the date of deactivation and again on the date of deactivation" (p. 1). Language: notices must be issued "in any language that the company knows or has reason to know is the worker's primary language" (p. 1).

Contents the company must supply:
- the reasons, which "must include portion of Company's policy that was violated and specific incident(s) that violated the policy";
- the records considered, which "must include date, time, and location of all incidents supporting the deactivation decision";
- the length of the deactivation, and how the worker can remedy it (p. 1);
- the right to challenge under SMC 8.40, with "at least until ____ (90 days from date of notice)" to do so; the company "has up to 14 days to respond with a decision on the challenge," and "All records of your challenge and any responses will be provided to you within 14 days" (p. 2);
- "all available methods of contact for a worker to initiate a challenge" and a link or instructions to the company's deactivation policy (p. 2).

The form ends with a certification: "I certify under penalty of perjury under the law of Washington State that, to the best of my knowledge, true and accurate copies of the records ___ (Company) relied upon to deactivate the worker are [attached to this notice]/[can be downloaded here]," signed by a named officer with title, date and place (p. 2). The footer gives the office's contact details.

## Relation to the argument
A concrete, recent case of a government fixing procedural conventions on a platform: advance notice, reasons tied to a named policy clause and dated incidents, access to the evidence, a stated challenge route and time limits, and a signature under penalty of perjury. In the paper's terms, these are the court-like conventions that Suzor (`suzor2018digital`, pp. 7–8) finds missing from platform terms (reasons, stable rules, appeal), now required by a city for one category of platform. It supports ruling D8 in two directions: the convention does bend toward courts when a public body imposes it, and the platform did not volunteer it. It leaves the decider where it was: the form says the company "has up to 14 days to respond with a decision on the challenge" (p. 2), so the first-instance reviewer of a deactivation is still the company that made it. Nothing on the form requires an independent reviewer; that is the contrast with `tumey1927ohio` and `us455usc`, where the interested party may not judge.

Facet check: matches the facet's quotations. The facet notes the ordinance text was not found; that is still true here, and this form states only the minimum of what the company must put in the notice. Several of the form's duties (the 90-day window, 14-day response) are given as the company's duties under the ordinance; the ordinance itself is the authority for them.

## Caution
An agency's sample form, not the law: the legal duty and its remedies are in the ordinance, which was not read. Revised 11/2024, so it reflects the rules at that date. No data on use or effect. The company fills the blanks, so the form shows what must appear, not what companies write.
