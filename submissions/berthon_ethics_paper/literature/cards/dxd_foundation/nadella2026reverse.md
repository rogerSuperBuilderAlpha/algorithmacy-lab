## What it argues
Arrow's information paradox says a seller who reveals information to sell it has already given it away (para. 2). Nadella reverses it: "the buyer risks giving away knowledge, just in order to use what they bought" (para. 3). A firm pays "once with money, and again with something even more valuable: the proprietary knowledge you must reveal" (para. 4), and "the information asymmetry becomes increasingly skewed" because the seller learns about the buyer while the buyer learns little back (para. 5). Models learn from "exhaust": "the prompts people write, the tools agents use, and especially the corrections people make when the model is wrong" (para. 8). "In consuming intelligence, you are creating intelligence. And what you create should belong to you" (para. 9). He objects that providers claim fair-use rights to train on public data yet restrict distillation and "reserve the right to learn from customer usage" (para. 10). The remedy is a "hard boundary across which nothing crosses, not even the intelligence exhaust, without consent" (para. 12), built through five items (para. 14): Control (private evals; ownership of memory, traces and feedback), Capability (learning environments inside the tenant), Choice (an orchestration layer "decoupled from any single model"), Cost, and Compound.

## Relation to the argument
Rulings D4, D5, D8; premise P10. This is the clearest statement in the series of an interest that differs between provider and customer: the provider's learning infrastructure takes value from the customer's usage, and the remedy is a boundary the customer controls. The party protected is the enterprise. The people who make corrections are named as the source of the exhaust, and the firm, not those people, is the owner. The post supports the paper's reading that Nadella's unit is the organisation (D5), and it shows he accepts that a provider and its customer can have opposed interests (no shared-interest assumption, cf. P10). The counterpart for the paper's triad, a third party served by the mediator, still does not appear.
