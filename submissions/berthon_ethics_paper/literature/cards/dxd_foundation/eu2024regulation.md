## What it argues
Article 14(1): high-risk AI systems "shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons". 14(2): oversight "shall aim to prevent or minimise the risks to health, safety or fundamental rights" that arise under intended use or reasonably foreseeable misuse. 14(3): measures are "commensurate with the risks, level of autonomy and context of use", built in by the provider or identified by the provider for the deployer to implement. 14(4): the system must be provided so that assigned persons can, "as appropriate and proportionate", (a) understand its capacities and limits and monitor it for anomalies, (b) "remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output ... (automation bias)", (c) interpret the output, (d) decide not to use it or to "disregard, override or reverse the output", and (e) "intervene in the operation ... or interrupt the system through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state". 14(5): for systems under Annex III point 1(a), no action may follow an identification unless "separately verified and confirmed by at least two natural persons with the necessary competence, training and authority"; the two-person rule may be waived for law enforcement, migration, border control or asylum where law finds it disproportionate. The page banner says the Article applies from 2 December 2027 (Annex III systems) or 2 August 2028 (Annex I systems), "according to Article 113(c)". That banner and the related-recitals line (66, 73) are the site's.

## Relation to the argument
Premise P11 and P12. Article 14 is the statutory counterpart to the post's P19 stop mechanism: 14(4)(e) combines a stop button with a halt to a safe state, and 14(4)(b) names automation bias, so the law that requires a halting person also states that this person may over-rely. It differs from the post in two ways. First, 14(1) makes interface tools a design duty of the provider, which is a design-side requirement of the kind the paper wants for other parties. Second, 14(2) names the risks to "fundamental rights" of affected people as the aim, although the actors are the overseers the deployer assigns, so the affected person is protected but never acts. The Article scopes itself to "high-risk" systems, so it does not cover a general platform mediator.
