## What it argues

The N400 is a scalp-recorded electrical response, "an event-related brain potential response linked to meaning processing" (abstract). Kutas and Hillyard found it in 1980 by giving undergraduates seven-word sentences, one word a second, 75% sensible and 25% ending oddly; the odd endings produced no P3b but a broad negativity peaking near 400 ms (Discovery). The authors define it as a relative negativity measured against a reference and a baseline, usually shown as a difference wave: a "monophasic negativity between 200 and 600 ms, largest over centro-parietal sites" (Names and definitions). They treat the label as a heuristic for a pattern of sensitivities, not a single localisable process.

What moves it: amplitude, "becoming smaller when factors rendered information more expected and thus easier to process" (Component characteristics), correlating at r = .9 with the word's cloze probability (Functional sensitivity). A capitalised but expected word and simple grammatical errors ("All turtles have four leg") produce no N400; music deviations produce a P3b-like response (Functional sensitivity). Pictures, faces, gestures and arithmetic errors do, so the authors read it as a marker of meaning processing and not "language" (Words, pictures, and other meaningful stimuli; Math). Pseudowords and illegal letter strings produce N400s that vary with orthographic neighbourhood size, and word recognition "is extended over time, with critical aspects only beginning to take place around 200 ms after word onset" (Word recognition). Masking a prime weakens N400 priming: "masking of primes has been found to attenuate but not to eliminate N400 effects" (Debate: Attention and the N400).

On theory they set an integration view, a pre-recognition view and a middle view, and argue none fits the whole record. Their own proposal: the N400 window is an "electrical snapshot" of stimulus-driven activity meeting the current state of multimodal long-term memory (Theories of the N400). ERPs are "a direct measure of neocortical activity that tracks brain states continuously and instantaneously" (Functional sensitivity).

## Relation to the argument

Thin, and only through facet D, table row 12. The essay quoted there put an N400 result under "fMRI studies". The review confirms the N400 is an ERP measure; hemodynamic imaging appears only as independent evidence on the semantic network, and fMRI appears once, for memory encoding (Localization/neural source; Recognition Memory). The facet's correction stands.

Beyond that the review does not help. A text search finds no discussion of word spacing, word boundaries, segmentation, or degraded or unspaced text. The nearest findings, that expectancy moves amplitude and masking weakens priming, concern meaning access and cannot support a claim about perceptual segmentation (premise P5 rests on other sources). The review also gives no basis for "degraded text triggers increased N400". Claude's reading: cite it for the terminological point only.

## Caution

A review by two investigators in the field: the field's view, no new data. The manuscript may differ from the print. It reports N400 effects in more than a dozen languages (Language learning and bilingualism) but gives no sample sizes. It says nothing on whether unspaced text changes the N400.
