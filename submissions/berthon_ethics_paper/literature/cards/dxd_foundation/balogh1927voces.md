## What it argues

Balogh poses four questions (p. 87): can it be shown "daß das Altertum alles, was wir heute stumm für uns lesen, stets laut las?"; how far does this hold for the Middle Ages; was writing also a voiced act; and "was die Ursache unseres modernen 'verstummten' Lesens?" He starts from Lucian's book collector who reads "so schnell, daß die Augen den Lippen immer zuvorlaufen" (p. 84), Wieland's 1789 note, Nietzsche's aphorism, and Augustine on Ambrose, the "locus classicus" (p. 85). Norden in 1898 had one passage; by 1927 "etwa zwanzig Stellen" (p. 87), to which Balogh adds material collected since 1913 (p. 87; the first version appeared in Hungarian in 1921, n. 7). He concedes the shape of the evidence: because a habit taken for granted leaves no record, loud reading is attested chiefly through "Abnormitäten", cases where someone was forced to read silently or was stopped from reading aloud (p. 88). Suetonius's *taciti* for Augustus's knights "beweist klar, daß das normale Lesen laut vor sich ging" (p. 90); Horace's Nasica and the *tacite legere* of the will follow (pp. 90–91).

In the second part the page itself has a voice: "pagina loquitur aut silet, littera sonat" (p. 203); *legere et audire* is a fixed formula in which "das akustische Element ... vorherrschend" (p. 207). The excursus on pp. 226–228 is the one that matters for the abstract. Balogh reports the modern "inner speech" literature (Huey 1908, Javal 1907, O'Brien 1921) and holds that "Ein lautloses Lesen kann sich die Wissenschaft nämlich nur in Begleitung einer (mehr oder minder artikulierten, aber trotzdem lautlosen) inneren Sprache vorstellen", that inner speech is "nichts Anerzogenes, sondern etwas Ureigentümliches", and that silent reading "nur durch Unterdrückung instinktiver Lautäußerungen entstehen konnte" (p. 227). Then: "Das Lesen des antiken Buches haben die ungegliederten Zeilen, die sog. 'scriptio continua', ungemein erschwert" (p. 227). He explains why with eye-movement findings (five or six stops per line, ten to twelve letters per jump, the stops set by word gaps and punctuation): "Diese künstlichen Gliederungsmittel standen dem Altertum noch nicht zur Verfügung. Somit genügte es nicht die Buchstaben abzulesen ... das rasche Erkennen des ganzen Wortbildes war unerläßlich. Dieser Umstand machte die Fertigkeit des Lesens von einer langen Übung abhängig" (pp. 227–228), with Quintilian on the divided attention of eye and voice (p. 228).

On cause and date he is explicit. He has no evidence past the sixteenth century, and "Ich halte es für wahrscheinlich, daß die Buchdruckerkunst auch in dieser Hinsicht wesentliche Veränderungen verursacht hat. Die Druckerpresse hat ... mit der regelmäßigen Abgrenzung des Wortbildes und mit der Ausschaltung der so lästigen mittelalterlichen Abkürzungen ein rasches und leichtes Lesen ermöglicht" (p. 236). Silent reading belongs to "der Beschleunigung und Mechanisierung des Schreibens und Lesens" (p. 237), and to journalism (pp. 237–238).

## Relation to the argument

The origin of the reading-aloud consensus is also, on these pages, the first statement of the spacing argument, and it does not say what the abstract says.

- **Supports** "unspaced text forced ... sound": Balogh is the strongest voice for voiced reading as the ancient norm, and he links *scriptio continua* to difficulty (p. 227).
- **Cuts against** the mechanism. His own account of why unspaced lines were hard is whole-word recognition and "lange Übung" (pp. 227–228), a trained visual skill, not a need to hear the boundary. The voice, for Balogh, is instinct suppressed by moderns, not a parsing tool.
- **Cuts against the agent and date.** He credits the printing press, c. 1500, with "regelmäßige Abgrenzung des Wortbildes" and the end of abbreviations (p. 236), and names no Insular or Carolingian change. The abstract's seventh-century origin for the "freeing" has against it the founder of the thesis it builds on. Facet C's finding that historians of literacy do not credit spacing gains a 1927 ally here.
- **For ruling D2** (the author reviews the debate): Knox's 1968 refutation (`knox1968silent`) answers the pages I did not read; Busch (2002) endorses Balogh's summary. This card settles only what Balogh says about spacing and cause.
