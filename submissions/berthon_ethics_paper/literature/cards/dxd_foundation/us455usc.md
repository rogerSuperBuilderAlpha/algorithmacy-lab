# 28 U.S.C. § 455 — Disqualification of justice, judge, or magistrate judge.

**Identifier:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/455 · **Read depth:** full_text (Cornell LII reproduction of the current Code text with Historical and Revision Notes and amendment notes; subsections (a)–(f) read in full) · **Source-tier:** statute · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** none · **Parties modeled:** two parties + third party (litigants and a judge who must step aside when interested) · **Facets:** J · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
Subsection (a) sets the general standard: "Any justice, judge, or magistrate judge of the United States shall disqualify himself in any proceeding in which his impartiality might reasonably be questioned." (b) lists mandatory grounds: personal bias or prejudice concerning a party, or personal knowledge of disputed facts (b)(1); prior service as a lawyer in the matter, or a former practice partner's service (b)(2); prior government service as counsel, adviser or witness, or an expressed opinion on the merits (b)(3); the judge's, spouse's or minor child's financial interest "in the subject matter in controversy or in a party to the proceeding, or any other interest that could be substantially affected by the outcome" (b)(4); and a relative within the third degree who is a party, officer or trustee of a party, a lawyer in the case, or a likely material witness (b)(5).

(c) obliges the judge to inform himself of his own and (by reasonable effort) his family's financial interests. (d) defines "proceeding," "fiduciary" and "financial interest," which means "ownership of a legal or equitable interest, however small," with carve-outs for mutual funds the judge does not manage, offices in charitable and civic bodies, mutual-insurance policies and government securities, unless the outcome could substantially affect the value (d)(4). (e) bars waiver of the (b) grounds by the parties; for the (a) ground alone, waiver is allowed only "preceded by a full disclosure on the record of the basis for disqualification." (f) lets a judge keep a case after substantial time was spent if the financial interest, found late and not one the outcome could affect, is divested.

The notes show the text rests on a 1911 rule (the 1948 revision) and that the 1974 amendment "expanded applicability" and "inserted provisions relating to waiver"; the 1988 amendment added (f). The 1948 revision note says relationship to a party's attorney was included "in conformity with the views of judges cognizant of the grave possibility of undesirable consequences resulting from a less inclusive rule."

## Relation to the argument
Statutory counterpart to `tumey1927ohio`: where *Tumey* sets the constitutional floor (a direct, substantial pecuniary interest), § 455 sets a much wider statutory duty, using an appearance test ("might reasonably be questioned") and a count that includes "however small" interests. It is the courts-side evidence for premise P10 that the law makes the third party show disinterest. Two features bear directly on the paper: subsection (e) bars the parties from waiving the (b) grounds, so the convention is not at the parties' disposal (a platform's terms are the reverse: the user "agrees"), and (a) allows waiver only after full disclosure on the record, a rule on disclosure that sets the limits of what disclosure can cure — relevant to ruling D3.

The limit the facet already notes stands: disqualification is by the judge's own act ("shall disqualify himself"), so the safeguard depends on the interested party noticing, and it covers federal judges only.

## Caution
Federal judges only; state judges and arbitrators are not covered. A duty written as self-disqualification; the text gives no enforcement mechanism for a judge who does not step aside (the revision notes point to other sections for reassignment, not for challenge). Pin to the official Code before print.
