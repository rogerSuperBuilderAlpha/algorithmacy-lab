## What it argues
The study tests the "neuronal recycling" claim that the visual word form area (VWFA), as it grows with literacy, takes over cortex that processed faces, houses and tools. Hervais-Adelman and colleagues show participants sentences, nonwords, false fonts, faces, houses, tools and checkerboards in Devanagari-reading adults who range from fully illiterate to highly literate (Introduction; Methods, "Participants").

Findings, all from the Results section:
- Responses to sentences and nonwords rise with literacy in a network that includes a fusiform peak at MNI (−42, −55, −10), a few millimetres from the peak reported by Dehaene et al. (2010) at (−44, −50, −14).
- Individual peak locations near the VWFA do not shift with literacy: the authors searched radii of 10, 20, 30 and 40 mm and found no lateral displacement, though the response at those peaks kept rising with literacy (slopes 0.030–0.035, t89 = 4.5–4.9, all P < 0.001).
- The share of left ventral-temporal voxels preferring nonwords over other categories rises with literacy (slope 0.0036, t89 = 3.023, P = 0.003); no category-selective extent for faces, houses or tools shrinks (all P > 0.17).
- Representational similarity between faces and nonwords inside the VWFA rises with literacy (slope 0.003, t89 = 2.603, P = 0.011).
- After training, sentence responses rise near the VWFA (peak at −39, −49, −10), but responses to faces, houses, tools, false fonts and checkerboards do not change over the six months. A similar post-training change is not seen for nonwords, which the authors say does not let them rule out that the sentence effect reflects words learned during training.

The authors' summary: "acquisition of literacy in childhood recycles existing object representation mechanisms but without destructive competition" (Abstract). In the Discussion they describe "superposition without subsequent competition".

## Caution
Adults who learned to read late, in Devanagari (an alphasyllabary), so the results may not carry to children learning an alphabet. Sex was imbalanced across literacy levels (illiterate participants were mostly women: two men among the 29 trainees); the authors discuss it as a limitation. The post-training maps use P < 0.001 uncorrected at voxel level. The null on training effects for other categories covers only six months. The paper does not measure reading speed, silent reading or the effect of word spacing, so it cannot be cited for any claim about scriptio continua.
