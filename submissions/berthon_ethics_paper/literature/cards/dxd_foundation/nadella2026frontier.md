## What it argues
Nadella says AI is "the first time we can create a real cognitive loop between people and digital systems" (para. 2). A firm must build "human capital" (the "knowledge, judgment, relationships, ingenuity, and pattern recognition of its people") and "token capital" ("the firm's AI capability it builds and owns") (para. 4). Human capital grows more valuable as token capital grows: "human agency will be the driver of token capital growth", because "Humans will set ambitious goals" and "Without human direction, you have compute running in circles" (para. 5). The opportunity is "building a learning loop on top of models" (para. 6): "You can offload a task, or even a job, but you can never offload your learning." Control is tested by whether "A company should be able to switch out a 'generalist' model without losing the 'company veteran' expertise" (para. 7). The loop uses private evals, private reinforcement-learning environments and a queryable knowledge base (para. 8), and "becomes the new IP of the firm", a "hill climbing machine" (para. 9). He warns against "ceding value to a few models that eat everything they see", calls it politically intolerable, and compares it with the hollowing-out of industrial economies in the first phase of globalisation (paras. 10–11). He asks for "a frontier ecosystem, not just a frontier model" (para. 12).
