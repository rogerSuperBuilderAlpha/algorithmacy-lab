# Özkan, Z. G., & Perea, M. (2026). Word-initial capitalization preserves lexical processing in unspaced text. *Scientific Reports.*

**Identifier:** doi:10.1038/s41598-026-67473-2 · **Read depth:** full_text (accepted, unedited manuscript marked "Article in Press"; received 30 April 2026, accepted 14 August 2026; no journal pagination, so locators are sections and PDF page numbers, and the final version may differ) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed article (in press); the study was preregistered (the authors say a preregistration exists on OSF; I did not open it) · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Design and sample:** 2 × 2 within-subject eye-tracking experiment (interword spacing: spaced or unspaced × target-word frequency: high or low) on 120 Spanish sentence frames, every word capitalised in both spacing conditions; 60 University of València psychology students (43 female, mean age 21.8), 1,800 trials per condition · **Parties modeled:** person–artefact (skilled reader and text) · **Facets:** D · **Read:** 2026-10-13

## What it argues
Özkan and Perea ask whether a cue other than a gap can mark word boundaries well enough for readers of unspaced text. Their cue is the initial capital, as in hashtags such as #ClimateChangeAction. Both conditions keep every word capitalised ("Martha Was Very Happy…" against "MarthaWasVeryHappy…"), so the contrast isolates the gap itself (Introduction, PDF p. 3; Materials, PDF p. 5). They use the word-frequency effect as the marker of lexical processing: if capitals do the segmenting, the frequency effect should not grow when spaces go.

Results (Results; Tables 2–3, PDF pp. 8–9, 15–17):
- Global cost remained. Total reading time 2,790 ms unspaced against 2,609 ms spaced; mean fixation 206 against 198 ms; 13.5 against 13.1 fixations (Table 2).
- Local frequency effects were additive with spacing. For first-fixation duration, frequency 214 against 203 ms, spacing 211 against 206 ms, interaction b = 0.02, 95% CrI [−0.01, 0.05], BF01 = 31.76. Gaze duration: 351 ms unspaced against 305 ms spaced; interaction b = −0.01, BF01 = 32.92. Total viewing time: 549 against 496 ms; interaction BF01 = 20.07.
- Initial landing position moved toward the word's start without spaces: 33.9% of word width against 36.6% (b = 0.15, 95% CrI [0.06, 0.24]). The authors convert this to about 2.5 against 2.7 character widths.
- Skipping probability: no clear effect (3.94% unspaced against 5.01% spaced).

The authors' reading: "initial capitals provide useful word-onset information, but interword spaces provide additional visual and oculomotor cues that capitalization alone does not fully restore" (Abstract). They suggest the gap may also reduce lateral masking of boundary letters (Discussion, PDF p. 11).

## Relation to the argument
It supports the second half of premise P3: marking boundaries by other means recovers part of the loss. In this study, capitals preserve the lexical side (no extra frequency penalty) and not the oculomotor side (landing shifts, longer reading). The point matters for ruling D6 (a complex of conventions, with a search for the one doing the most work): the space bundles several jobs, boundary marking, word-extent information, and release from lateral masking, and capitals replace only the first. For premise P5 it gives a case where an artefact feature (capitals) carries a segmentation job otherwise left to the reader, with the residual cost measured.

Facet check: the facet's abstract-level reading ("initial capitals restored lexical processing but not all of the oculomotor cost") overstates one thing. The design has no lowercase spaced baseline and no unspaced lowercase condition, so it cannot show what capitals restore relative to ordinary text; it shows only that the frequency effect is the same size with and without spaces once capitals are present. The authors say as much: the design "does not test any additional cost or benefit that word-initial capitals themselves may impose relative to ordinary lowercase spaced text" (Discussion, PDF p. 11). The global reading-time cost also remained (about 7%, by my arithmetic from Table 2).

## Caution
Sixty Spanish students reading single-line sentences with one target word each; one frame set. Landing positions are reported in pixels as a share of word width (Arial is proportional). The conclusion that capitals "preserve" lexical processing rests on Bayes factors for a null interaction, with weakly informative priors the authors chose. A final published version may change numbers or wording. The paper is about skilled adult readers of a spaced script, not learners or historical readers.
