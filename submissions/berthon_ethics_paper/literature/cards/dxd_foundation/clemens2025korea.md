# Clemens, W. C. (2025). Review of V. D. Cha & R. Pacheco Pardo, *Korea: A new history of South and North* (Yale University Press, 2023). *Journal of East Asian Studies, 25*, 159–162.

## What it argues
About 80 words of this roughly 2,000-word review touch literacy; the rest concerns US policy toward North Korea under George W. Bush. The literacy passage sits in Clemens's praise of the book's treatment of the Japanese colonial period (p. 159). He reports that the authors "three times mention the Korean 24-letter alphabet hangeul sponsored in 1443 CE by King Sejong the Great to facilitate literacy by serving as an alternative to the logographic Sino-Korean Hanja." The next sentence says Hangeul "was initially denounced and disparaged by the Korean educated class." Clemens then says the book "might have devoted more space to efforts to spread literacy and learning", adds that "Christian missionary schools fostered hangeul" in the late nineteenth century, and gives two numbers: "by 1912 literacy in Korea was only 20 percent. It rose to over 60 percent by 1940" (p. 159). He also gives school counts of 2,000 in 1911 and over 5,000 in 1938, which he ties to the book's pp. 23–24 (p. 159).

## Relation to the argument
It supports only two statements in facet C: that Hangul was disparaged by the educated class, and that literacy was low in 1912 and much higher by 1940. It is the thinnest source in the Korea set. The 1912 and 1940 figures are Clemens's own sentences. He cites no census or table, and the review does not say that the book gives them. The two sentences before them (the "denounced and disparaged" line) are also unattributed: the text does not say the book reports them. The only claim the review plainly ties to the book is the "three times mention" sentence about Sejong and literacy.

Read against Becker and Won (`becker2024conquering`), the figures at least agree in order of magnitude. The 1930 census gives a town mean of 209 Korean-literate residents per 1,000 (male 344, female 70; p. 7 there), which lies between 20 percent in 1912 and 60 percent in 1940. That is a consistency check, not a verification, because Becker's number is a town-level mean of Korean-language literacy and Clemens's is unsourced.

Facet check: facet C says the figures are "the review's statements about the book." My reading is weaker: the figures are the reviewer's own, unsourced, and not marked as the book's. Do not describe them as what Cha and Pacheco Pardo report.
