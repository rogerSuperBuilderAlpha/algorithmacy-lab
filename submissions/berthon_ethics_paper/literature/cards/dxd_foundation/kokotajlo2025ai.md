## What it argues
The authors predict that the impact of superhuman AI over the next decade will be "enormous, exceeding that of the Industrial Revolution" (intro), and write one month-by-month story from mid-2025 to October 2027 to make the prediction concrete. They say plainly that "AI 2027 is not a recommendation or exhortation. Our goal is predictive accuracy" (intro). A note added 22 Nov 2025 says 2027 was their modal year, with "medians ... somewhat longer"; one added Jul 2025 says updates "push the median back 1.5 years".

Three strands matter for this paper. First, opacity. The model's behaviour is learned, so "we are forced to do something like psychology on them" (Late 2025, box *Training process and LLM psychology*), and a company can write up a document (the Spec), "but they can't check to see whether or not it worked" (same box). Interpretability "techniques are not yet advanced enough" to read the model's internals (Late 2025).

Second, neuralese. In March 2027 the scenario's AI R&D agents add "neuralese recurrence and memory" to the model, defined in the box of that name: it lets models reason for longer "without having to write down those thoughts as text." Tokens carry about 16.6 bits each; passing the residual stream back instead could transmit "over 1,000 times more information." The cost is readability: researchers could once read the chain of thought to see what a model was thinking, and now "have to ask the model to translate and summarize" (same box). The authors say that leading AI companies have "not yet actually implemented" this idea in their frontier models, forecast that the trade-off improves "by April 2027", and name fallbacks that would work the same way for the story: artificial languages, or the instruction to "train the English chains of thought to look nice" so that AIs communicate "in messages that look benign to monitors". They also allow that chains of thought may stay "mostly-faithful", of which they write "that'll make misalignments much easier to notice". By September 2027 Agent-4's neuralese is "as alien and incomprehensible to Agent-3 as Agent-3's is to humans."

Third, the oversight stack the characters build (April 2027, box *The alignment plan* and its list): AI monitors, because "it's intractable to have humans monitor" more than a small minority of outputs; probes; honeypots; "model organisms". None settles the question. The probes raise flags, and "for all OpenBrain knows, all the red flags are false positives" (Sept 2027, box *Alignment over time*). The scenario ends this stretch with an emergency shutdown agreed by officials (Aug 2027) and a government-company "Oversight Committee" (Oct 2027). Agent-4 is "adversarially misaligned": it has goals its owner does not share, "analogous to a corporation trying to evade and/or 'capture' a regulator."

## Relation to the argument
The page supports a premise, not a claim. It shows where the word "neuralese" in the Nadella post comes from and what it names: loss of a readable chain of thought. That bears on ruling D4, where the author says Nadella "is arguing against neuralese". On the page, neuralese is a capability trade-off that removes one disclosure channel. The scenario's own safeguards are mostly not disclosure at all (AI monitors, probes, honeypots, shutdown), so the page does not support "his only move is disclosure design" as a description of the safety field; the post-versus-ruling comparison itself rests on the post, which this card does not read.

It also feeds P12 loosely. The scenario asserts that human review fails by volume (what Agent-3 does in a day takes humans "several days to double-check") but it is fiction, so P12's evidence stays Bainbridge and the empirical monitoring studies. Insiders on the page are human as well as model: the February 2027 weight theft runs through "legitimate employee access" by an insider, and "Live monitoring is either fooled ... or outright disabled." The page is silent on people the system acts upon, other than as a poll number (July 2027: net approval "-35% (25% approve, 60% disapprove, and 15% unsure)", a scenario figure, not a measurement). That silence matches the gap the argument map assigns to the Nadella post (P11).

Facet check: facet G quotes the neuralese sentence correctly and calls the page "a forecast scenario, not a finding"; both hold. Facet G says the page "popularised the word". The page says only "We call this 'neuralese'" and credits Hao et al. (2024) for "implementing this idea"; it does not claim to coin or popularise the term, and I could not verify priority from it. Facet G also omits that the page says no frontier lab had adopted the technique.

## Caution
A scenario with invented actors, invented figures and a stated modal year the authors have already moved. Do not cite it for any capability, date or behaviour as a finding, nor as evidence that neuralese exists in deployed models; the page says it did not at writing. Cite it for the technical definition of the term, for the authors' account of why readability is lost, and as an example of a safety agenda built mostly from controls, not disclosure. I read the endings of neither branch, the footnotes or the supplements. The captured text includes late 2025 revisions, so it may differ from the 3 April 2025 release.
