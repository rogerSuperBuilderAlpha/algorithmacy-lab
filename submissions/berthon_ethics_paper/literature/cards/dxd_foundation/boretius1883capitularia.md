## What it argues

Chapter 72 of the *Admonitio generalis*, addressed to priests ("Sacerdotibus"), asks "ut scolae legentium puerorum fiant" (that schools of reading boys be set up), then runs on: "Psalmos, notas, cantus, compotum, grammaticam per singula monasteria vel episcopia et libros catholicos bene emendate" (p. 60, ll. 3–4). The sentence has one verb, the imperative "emendate" (correct), and it governs the whole list; nothing in it says "teach". Boretius's apparatus (p. 60, n. y) records the variants "emendent" and "emendatos"; the 2012 editors judge the imperative best attested and treat a "discant" (let them learn) found in part of the Ansegisus tradition as an interpolation (Mordek et al. 2012, p. 225, n. 178), and their translation reads the list as things to be corrected in the catholic books. The reason given: "quia saepe, dum bene aliqui Deum rogare cupiunt, sed per inemendatos libros male rogant" (p. 60). The boys are not to corrupt the books "vel legendo vel scribendo", and "si opus est euangelium, psalterium et missale scribere, perfectae aetatis homines scribant cum omni diligentia" (p. 60).

So the chapter orders that books be corrected, and that, when a gospel book, psalter or missal must be copied, grown men copy it with all care. It names the harm (people pray badly from uncorrected books) and supervises copyists by age and diligence. It says nothing about layout or word separation. Whether it touches script or punctuation turns on "notas": the 2012 editors gloss it as script signs "im weitesten Sinne", so probably letters and not only punctuation marks, accents, abbreviations, diacritics or Tironian notes (p. 225, n. 176), and Steinová (2015) contests the word's sense.

## Relation to the argument

**P10 and ruling D8.** This is the clearest text in the library of a ruler ordering correct books and careful copying. It supports the draft's "authority with standing over the scribe" for the Carolingian church, and it shows the scribe as a worker to be supervised, not as a party with an interest of his own.

## Caution

Read in OCR of the 1883 MGH edition, c. 72 and its neighbours only. The newer edition (Mordek, Zechiel-Eckes and Glatthaar, MGH Fontes iuris 16, 2012) numbers the chapter 70 (p. 222); its Latin of the passage matches Boretius's in substance (orthography apart, e.g. "sepe", "evangelium"). Only that chapter of the 2012 edition was seen, not its introduction. Steinová (2015) says the passage has "seemingly corrupt grammar and ambiguous vocabulary" (quoted from the abstract via the O facet file; S2 could not re-open that abstract, as Crossref carries none), so the gloss above should not be built on word by word. The text concerns correction of books, not the page's layout.
