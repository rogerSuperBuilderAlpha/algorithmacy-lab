## Summary

Read: pp. 6, 8-11, 22 and 87 (the end of the Assistant Secretary's letter, the opening of section 1, the first recommendation and the passage on Native languages), seven pages of 105, with a text search of the volume; the Fable read added the pages listed in the header. Vol. II reports the Department of the Interior's second-stage investigation of the federal boarding school system. It says the schools were used to pursue a policy of forced assimilation over a century and a half. It recommends that the United States acknowledge a national policy of forced assimilation carried out by removing and confining Indian children and placing them in the system. It reports that grief and trauma from the loss of Native languages was one of the most prominent shared experiences of survivors, and that punishment for speaking Native languages commonly involved lye soap in the mouth or corporal punishment (p. 87). Its findings state that the system discouraged or prevented the use of Native languages through punishment (finding 10, p. 93). A text search of the whole volume for "literacy" and "learn to read" found no hit; the volume does not discuss the teaching of reading.

## Key facts it relies on

- Forced assimilation as the policy. [quote] p. 6: "these schools were used to pursue a policy of forced assimilation over a century and a half."
- The wrong is named as policy and method. [quote] p. 22: "a national policy of forced assimilation of Indian children, and carrying out this policy through the removal and confinement of Indian children from their families"
- Punishment for Native languages. [quote] p. 87: "The punishments for speaking Native languages instead of English, even when children could not understand or speak English, commonly involved their mouths being washed with lye soap or varying types of corporal punishment that ranged in severity."
- Recommendation 1 says the government "should" issue a formal acknowledgment and apology, and "could" formally repudiate forced assimilation and affirm the right of Native people to maintain their cultural identities and languages. [paraphrase] p. 22.
- Added at the Fable read. The report's own finding on language, from government records. [quote] p. 93 (finding 10): "The Federal Indian boarding school system discouraged or prevented the use of American Indian, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian languages or cultural or religious practices through punishment, including corporal punishment."
- Added at the Fable read. Compulsion. [quote] p. 92 (finding 2): "children were induced or compelled by the U.S. Government to experience the Federal Indian boarding school system"
- The word "literacy" does not occur in the extracted text. [paraphrase] whole volume, text search.

## Critical notes

A federal agency report issued by the party that ran the schools; survivor statements are anonymised and drawn from the Road to Healing sessions, not tested against school records in the passages I read. Vol. I (card newland2022federal) holds the historical findings; this card covers Vol. II only. The card names no published critic of the report. The report itself records Indian education as a treaty right (finding 7, p. 93) and prints one participant's account of what a school meant to those who attended it (p. 86). Not a record of a rule: the passages read do not give a statute or regulation text requiring English.
Bearing: supports 3.7 (the wrongs named are removal, confinement, forced assimilation and punishment for Native languages; the volume says nothing of the teaching of reading).
