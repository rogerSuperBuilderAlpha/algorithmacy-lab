## Summary

Read in full. Microsoft's president and chief legal officer announce that Microsoft will defend paying commercial
customers sued for copyright infringement over the use of its Copilots or their output, and pay adverse judgments or
settlements, on condition that the customer used the guardrails and content filters built into the products and did
not try to generate infringing material, which includes giving a Copilot no input the customer lacks the rights to
use. The stated reason is that Microsoft charges for the Copilots, so legal issues from their use should be "our
problem rather than our customers’ problem", extending two decades of patent indemnity. The commitment covers paid
versions of commercial Copilot services and Bing Chat Enterprise, including Microsoft 365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot;
the post names no free or consumer service. An update note (dated 01.05.2024 on the page) records that on 15 Nov
2023 the commitment was renamed the Customer Copyright Commitment and extended to Azure OpenAI Service customers who
implement required mitigations. The post acknowledges authors' concerns and says Microsoft, not customers, should
address them.

## Key facts it relies on

- The promise: "if you are challenged on copyright grounds, we will assume responsibility for the potential legal risks involved." para. 2 [quote]
- The mechanism and its condition: "we will defend the customer and pay the amount of any adverse judgments or settlements that result from the lawsuit, as long as the customer used the guardrails and content filters we have built into our products." para. 3 [quote]
- The reason: "if their use creates legal issues, we should make this our problem rather than our customers’ problem." para. 5 [quote]
- On authors: "We are sensitive to the concerns of authors, and we believe that Microsoft rather than our customers should assume the responsibility to address them." para. 6 [quote]
- On authors, same paragraph: "Yet it is critical for authors to retain control of their rights under copyright law and earn a healthy return on their creations." Para. 12 lists "authors and artists and their representatives" among those Microsoft says it will listen to and work with. [quote]
- The commitment makes use of Microsoft's filters a condition: "Our new Copilot Copyright Commitment requires that customers use these technologies". para. 7 [quote]
- Scope: "specifically for paid versions of Microsoft commercial Copilot services and Bing Chat Enterprise". para. 8 [quote]
- Conditions: "customers must use the content filters and other safety systems built into the product and must not attempt to generate infringing materials, including not providing input to a Copilot service that the customer does not have appropriate rights to use." para. 8 [quote]
- "This step represents a pledge to our customers that the copyright liability of our products is ours to shoulder, not theirs." para. 11 [quote]
- Update note: from 15 Nov 2023 renamed the Customer Copyright Commitment and extended to Azure OpenAI Service outputs; eligibility requires the customer to have implemented required guardrails and mitigations. update note [paraphrase]

## Critical notes

Microsoft-authored. The post states no contract clause: it says the commitment "extends our existing intellectual
property indemnity support" (para. 3) and that Microsoft has "published more details" for customers (para. 10),
without a link in the saved page; the update note links a Microsoft Learn page on the required mitigations for Azure
OpenAI Service. None of those documents was opened, so where the binding terms sit is not established here. It is a
case of a provider carrying a loss for the deploying customer, conditioned on the customer's use of the provider's
own controls, and limited to paying commercial customers and one kind of claim (copyright by third parties). Authors
are the outside party named; the post states no remedy for them. In its one scenario a third party sues the customer
(para. 3): Microsoft defends the customer against that claim and pays "any adverse judgments or settlements", so a
claimant who wins or settles is paid by Microsoft. It contrasts with microsoft2026services §14(s)(viii), where the
consumer account holder is "solely responsible" for responding to third-party claims, and with microsoft2026copilot,
where the user is "solely responsible" for Actions taken at the user's request. The update-note text in the extract
has broken word spacing, so it is paraphrased, not quoted.
