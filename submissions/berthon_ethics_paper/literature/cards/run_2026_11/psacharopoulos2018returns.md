## Summary
Read: abstract and pp. 4-7; the other pages were searched for key terms only. The two authors (one at Georgetown University, one at the World Bank; title page) review 1,120 estimates of the return to schooling from 139 countries. They report a private return of about 8.8% per extra year of schooling in the 705 Mincerian estimates, which cover 1950 to 2014, and say it has hardly changed over decades. They discuss ability bias, the credential or screening explanation, and whether the estimates are causal. They report, straight after describing Card's (2001) natural-experiment test, that by and large schooling is linked to higher earnings through productivity rather than screening, while saying the debate on external validity and causality continues. The data concern years of schooling, not literacy, and describe average earnings returns (private in the pages read; the abstract also reports social returns), not what any person was forced to read or sign.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Based on 705 estimates, over the years 1950 to 2014, the private rate of return to an additional year of schooling is 8.8 percent" [quote] p. 5
- "One issue with the Mincerian method of estimating returns to education is missing variables, e.g., ability bias." [quote] p. 4
- "By and large, while some evidence of weak screening is revealed, education is generally associated with higher earnings due to productivity rather than to screening." [quote] p. 4
- "The debate is ongoing about the external validity and causality of rate of return estimates." [quote] p. 5
- The abstract says women's average returns are higher and that returns are higher in low-income countries. [paraphrase] Abstract

## Critical notes
- The authors treat the screening (credential) reading as the main rival to the productivity reading and find it weak (p. 4). A stronger credential reading would fit the gate (3.2) better; this paper does not take that side.
- The pages read report private earnings returns to schooling; the abstract adds social returns and differences by gender, country income and sector of employment. It is not about literacy, and the pages read say nothing on who was refused entry.
- Page numbers are the paper's printed numbers (PDF page minus two for the cover and abstract pages).
- Bearing: narrows 2.1 (counter-evidence: schooling pays, on average, in earnings); does not touch the five failures.
- The PDF spells the second author "Harry Antony Patrinos"; the card follows the Crossref record ("Anthony").
