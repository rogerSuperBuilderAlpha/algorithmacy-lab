## Summary
An investigative report by a Brussels NGO that campaigns on corporate lobbying, so an interested party, not neutral. It is the only source I found that counts who sits on CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, the committee drafting the AI Act's harmonised standards. From LinkedIn self-reports and other online sources it identified 143 people claiming JTC 21 involvement: 54 from corporations and 24 from consultancies (78, or 55 per cent), 23 from academia and think tanks (16 per cent), 13 from civil society (9 per cent). The methodology annex says the list is not complete and relies on self-reporting; the Commission cited "more than 200 experts" and no official statistics. For this paper the report's Microsoft findings matter most: four current Microsoft employees on the committee; "At least three national delegations are regularly headed by officers working for Microsoft", including Ireland's NSAI, which co-hosted a JTC 21 plenary at Microsoft's Dublin office in February 2024; and ISO/IEC 42001 "had been edited in SC42 by Microsoft's" representative, who also served as JTC 21 editor on the item. The JTC 21 chair is quoted saying 42001 "refers to an organisation and its processes, whereas the AI Act refers to products". The report quotes the Commission conceding that "the industry still represents the majority of participants". National mirror committees show the same pattern: AFNOR more than half industry and 4 per cent civil society; BSI two civil-society members of 89. Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Through online analysis Corporate Europe Observatory found 143 people who claim to be involved in the JTC21 committee."
- "54 of the identified JTC21 members represent corporations and 24 represent consultancies. Together, these 78 individuals make up more than half (55 per cent) of the identified JTC21 members."
- "only 23 representatives (16 per cent) came from academia and think tanks, and 13 from civil society (9 per cent)."
- "Corporate Europe Observatory identified four current (and one recent) Microsoft employees on the committee."
- "At least three national delegations are regularly headed by officers working for Microsoft."
- "The 42001 standard had been edited in SC42 by Microsoft's [named employee], who also serves as the JTC21 editor on this item." (Name omitted here; it is in the source.)
- Quoting the JTC 21 chair: "42001 refers to an organisation and its processes, whereas the AI Act refers to products. These do not fit together very well".
- Quoting the Commission's written reply: it "acknowledge[s] that the industry still represents the majority of participants", which is "a functional feature of standards which are highly technical".
- "Strategic decisions and votes on the draft standards are taken by the representatives of national standard-setting organisations".
- Methodology annex: "It is clear this is not a full membership list of the JTC21 committee"; "our analysis relies on self-reporting".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The Commission's later reply to the Ombudsman (`europeancommission2025reply`, pdf p. 13) reports the JTC 21 Inclusiveness task group's March 2025 questionnaire as showing "civil society organisations, public authorities, academics and independents represent more than half of the experts in JTC 21". The two counts conflict; neither is an official membership roll (the Commission says it holds none).
- The report's account of a "big push from the US, from Big Tech" for 42001 rests on one anonymous expert.
