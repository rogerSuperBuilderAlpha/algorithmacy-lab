## Summary
Bartha surveys criteria for analogical arguments. Commonsense guidelines (count similarities and differences),
Hesse's material criteria, and Gentner's structure-mapping theory (systematicity) all rest on overall similarity,
which "is not a reliable guide to plausibility" (§3.5.2). His own articulation model asks first for the "prior
association" in the source (what connects the shared features to the projected one) and then for "no critical
disanalogy". The legal example (Donoghue v Stevenson extended to a negligent lift repair but not to a workman who
could inspect a defective crane) shows the method in a normative domain (§2.1, Example 10). Section numbers serve
as locators (web page). Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- Schema: S is similar to T in known respects; S has Q; therefore T has Q or Q* (§2.2). "The argument form is
  ampliative" (§2.2). "nobody has ever formulated an acceptable rule, or set of rules, for valid analogical
  inferences" (§2.4).
- Guideline (G7): "The relevance of the similarities and differences to the conclusion ... must be taken into
  account" (§3.1).
- Against structure mapping: "Greater systematicity is neither necessary nor sufficient for a more plausible
  analogical inference." ... "There is no short-cut via syntax." (§3.4)
- Articulation model: "Prior Association. There must be a clear connection, in the source domain, between the known
  similarities (the positive analogy) and the further similarity that is projected to hold in the target domain";
  "Potential for Generalization ... there must be no critical disanalogy between the domains." (§3.5.2)
- Hesse's no-essential-difference condition: essential properties of the source must not be "part of the negative
  analogy" (§3.3.3).
- Precedent: stare decisis is justified pragmatically; "It pre-supposes a common set of social values"; "If a
  consensus emerges that a past case was wrongly decided, later judgments will distinguish it" (§4.4).

## Where the argument uses it
- A6: **refines, and narrows**. Sharing a named property (who holds the record) is not enough. The property must be
  the one that made the historical practice a failure under the benchmark (the prior association), and the target
  must lack a critical disanalogy. This is the refutation brief's "shared-property mapping is insufficient for
  normative transfer": it is insufficient unless the property is the ground of the source verdict.
- A7: **supports the form of the bridge**. A7 already says "unless something has changed that removes the property
  the failure turned on"; that is Bartha's two conditions stated as a premise. The precedent paragraph (§4.4) adds a
  limit: carrying a past verdict forward "pre-supposes a common set of social values". A7 therefore needs the
  present actor to share the value the verdict rested on (see `walzer1988interpretation` upgrade, lecture III).
- The Donoghue example is a template for A8: the duty fails to carry where the person could inspect (Farr v Butters),
  which is the paper's question of whether the person acted on can read the log or hold the brake.
