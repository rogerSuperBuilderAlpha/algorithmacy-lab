## Summary
Two non-profits asked the Commission for four harmonised standards (three toy-safety standards and a nickel test method) that CEN sold behind a paywall. The Grand Chamber annulled the Commission's refusal. It held, following *James Elliott Construction* (C-613/14, 2016, para 40), that a harmonised standard published in the OJ "forms part of EU law owing to its legal effects" (para 70), and that the rule of law requires free access to EU law, which gave an overriding public interest in disclosure (paras 73, 81). The Court's reasoning on bindingness is the passage the argument needs: although compliance with harmonised standards "is not compulsory", the presumption of conformity is one of their "essential characteristics" (para 74), and it "may prove difficult, or even impossible, for economic operators to have recourse to a procedure other than that of compliance" (para 75). Anyone who wants to challenge the presumption "must demonstrate that that product or service does not meet that standard or, alternatively, that that standard is not fit for purpose" (para 76). One of the four standards was "manifestly mandatory" because REACH required it as the test method (para 79).

I also opened *James Elliott Construction* (C-613/14, 27 October 2016; local copy `cjeu_elliott/62014CJ0613.txt`, sha256 9128a1c2...) and confirmed para 40: a harmonised standard published in the OJ "forms part of EU law"; and paras 59 and 61 (the answer to question 3): the presumption of fitness for use does not apply when a national court assesses a private contractual requirement.

## Key facts it relies on
- Para 70: "a harmonised standard, adopted on the basis of a directive and the references to which have been published in the Official Journal of the European Union, forms part of EU law owing to its legal effects".
- Para 71: "harmonised standards may be binding on the public generally as long as they themselves have been published in the Official Journal".
- Para 73: "even if the development of those standards is entrusted to a body governed by private law, only the Commission is empowered to request that a harmonised standard be developed"; the Commission supervises, finances and decides "to publish, not to publish or to publish with restriction the references".
- Para 74: "although Regulation No 1025/2012 provides, in Article 2(1) thereof, that compliance with harmonised standards is not compulsory, products which comply with those standards benefit ... from a presumption of conformity ... That legal effect ... is one of the essential characteristics of those standards".
- Para 75: "it may prove difficult, or even impossible, for economic operators to have recourse to a procedure other than that of compliance with such standards, such as an individual expert report, in the light of the administrative difficulties and additional costs arising therefrom".
- Para 76: "any natural or legal person who wishes effectively to challenge that presumption in respect of a given product or service must demonstrate that that product or service does not meet that standard or, alternatively, that that standard is not fit for purpose."
- Para 79: "compliance with harmonised standards is not generally mandatory", but the nickel standard "is, in the present case, manifestly mandatory".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The Commission's reply to the Ombudsman (2025, pdf p. 12; `europeancommission2025reply`) reads the ruling narrowly: draft standards and "experts contributions, minutes or agendas of meetings should not be considered to form part of EU law", so no overriding public interest attaches to them.
- Gornet and Maxwell (2024, pp. 11-12; `gornet2024european`) read the AG's opinion as saying "the whole architecture of the EU standardisation system presupposes that all actors use hENs".

## Where the argument uses it
- A4, M2 (P6 "industry standards"): narrows a premise. "Standards bodies bind no implementer" (RESEARCH_PLAN M2) is too strong for EU harmonised standards. Once cited in the OJ they are part of EU law and in practice the only economical route to conformity. The defensible claim: private, industry-weighted bodies draft text that the law then makes the default; the affected person meets it only as a presumption to rebut. For non-harmonised standards (ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF), the original premise holds.
- Refutation sought (binding standards): found, for the EU harmonised route only.
