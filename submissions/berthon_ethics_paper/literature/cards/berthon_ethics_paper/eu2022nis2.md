## Summary
NIS2 Art 23 is the cybersecurity incident regime for essential and important entities. Its main line runs to the authority: an early warning to the CSIRT or competent authority within 24 hours, an incident notification within 72 hours, a final report within a month (Art 23(4)). It also reaches the people served, more than the AI Act does. Entities notify "the recipients of their services" of significant incidents "likely to adversely affect the provision of those services" (Art 23(1)), and tell recipients threatened by a significant cyber threat "any measures or remedies that those recipients are able to take" (Art 23(2)). The authority may inform the public or require the entity to (Art 23(7)). "Remedies" here means self-help steps the recipient can take, not legal remedies against the entity; Art 23(1) adds that "The mere act of notification shall not subject the notifying entity to increased liability." A significant incident includes one that "has affected or is capable of affecting other natural or legal persons by causing considerable material or non-material damage" (Art 23(3)(b)).

## Key facts it relies on
- Art 23(1): "Where appropriate, entities concerned shall notify, without undue delay, the recipients of their services of significant incidents that are likely to adversely affect the provision of those services." "The mere act of notification shall not subject the notifying entity to increased liability."
- Art 23(2): entities "communicate, without undue delay, to the recipients of their services that are potentially affected by a significant cyber threat any measures or remedies that those recipients are able to take in response to that threat."
- Art 23(3)(b): an incident is significant if "it has affected or is capable of affecting other natural or legal persons by causing considerable material or non-material damage".
- Art 23(4)(a), (b), (d): early warning "within 24 hours"; incident notification "within 72 hours"; final report "not later than one month after".
- Art 23(7): where "public awareness is necessary ... or where disclosure of the significant incident is otherwise in the public interest", the CSIRT or competent authority "may, after consulting the entity concerned, inform the public about the significant incident or require the entity to do so."
