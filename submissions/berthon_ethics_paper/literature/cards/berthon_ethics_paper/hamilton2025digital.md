## Summary
Hamilton applies Pettit's and Skinner's republican liberty to digital advertising and social-media algorithms and
finds "digital domination" at two levels: individual (behaviour and ideas shaped by systems users cannot hold to
account) and political (multinational platforms beyond any one state's reach). The remedy is "antipower":
mechanisms by which individuals hold algorithms and their developers accountable, from user controls to
supranational institutions. Locators are preprint pages; the published chapter's pagination was not seen. Crossref
gives the chapter title as "Digital Domination".

## Key facts it relies on
- Definition via Pettit (1996, p. 578): domination as the capacity to interfere "with impunity and at will" in
  another's choices (p. 4).
- Republican liberty travels across eras: from Machiavelli and Locke through early modern feminists to basic income;
  "Republican liberty has, then, been applied to whatever kinds of domination develop" (p. 5).
- Not new: to the objection that mass media posed the same threat, "I have no disagreement here" (p. 9).
- Exit is not antipower: opting out has "extreme costs" and others' use still shapes the polity (p. 10).
- Vendor-granted controls ("Not Interested", "Ask App Not to Track") "should be recognized, as they directly respond
  to algorithmic power and create antipower for users" (p. 10).
- Conclusion: "individuals must have mechanisms to hold algorithms (and those who develop them) accountable"
  (abstract, p. 1; conclusion, pp. 13-14).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Treats vendor-granted controls as antipower (p. 10). On Lovett's procedural view a control the firm can withdraw
  at will is not a resilient constraint, and on Pettit's democratic view it is not control by the affected
  (`lovett2026republicanism` §2.2). Hamilton does not address this.
- Cases are social media and advertising, not agentic or enterprise LLM tools. Hamilton surveys Sparks and Jayaram
  (2022), who argue rule by automation can *reduce* domination under the right conditions (p. 6), a counterpoint
  the paper should name.

## Where the argument uses it
- A1 (the four design questions): supports the republican reading of "who holds the brake" and "who may contest":
  domination is the unaccountable capacity, exercised or not.
- A7: **supports** the claim that a republican standard binds present actors without depending on any one era's
  professions: the tradition is explicitly applied across eras to new forms of domination (p. 5), and the author
  concedes earlier media posed the same kind of threat (p. 9).
- A5/CB: the "Not Interested" passage (p. 10) is the strongest published statement that vendor-declared controls
  count. The paper's A5 claim (an envelope declared to the deployer alone) needs Lovett's resilience condition to
  answer it.
