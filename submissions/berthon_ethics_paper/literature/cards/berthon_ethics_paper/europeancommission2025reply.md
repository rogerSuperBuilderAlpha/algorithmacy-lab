## Summary
The Commission's answer to the Ombudsman inquiry opened on Corporate Europe Observatory's complaint about JTC 21. The text is undated in the copy read; its latest internal date is 28 August 2025. It concedes three things the argument needs. First, the Commission holds no data on who drafts the AI standards: "The Commission does not hold official statistical data" on the shares of industry, civil society and others, and the JTC 21 member list "does not include the members' professional affiliation" (pdf p. 13); DG CONNECT "does not have access to or hold documents that indicate the affiliation of the experts" (p. 1). Second, conflict-of-interest rules are kept out on purpose: applying the Commission's expert-group rules "would prevent industry stakeholders who are themselves providers of AI systems ... to participate" (p. 16). Third, the standards were late: the Commission is "aware and concerned by the delay" and "could issue guidance to fill the gaps" (p. 9); no AI Act harmonised standard had been cited in the OJ at the time of writing (p. 12). It defends industry's role as "central" because standards need "highly technical profiles and industry expertise" and their "main users ... are private-sector organisations" (p. 13). It reports a March 2025 JTC 21 questionnaire showing that civil society, public authorities, academics and independents "represent more than half of the experts" (p. 13). On *Public.Resource.Org* it holds that only standards cited in the OJ form part of EU law, so drafts and meeting records do not (p. 12).

## Key facts it relies on
- pdf p. 1: DG CONNECT "does not receive agendas and minutes directly and does not have access to or hold documents that indicate the affiliation of the experts".
- pdf p. 9: "the Commission is aware and concerned by the delay in the development of harmonised standards for the AI Act"; "At this stage, the Commission is not planning to develop common specifications as per the meaning of Article 41 AI Act. However, the Commission could issue guidance to fill the gaps caused by the delay of the standardisation work."
- pdf p. 12: "as long as a reference of a harmonised standard is not published in the OJEU, compliance with the harmonised standard ... does not give presumption of conformity ... and thus does not produce legal effects"; "no harmonised standards following a standardisation request for the AI Act has been cited in the OJEU for this legislation".
- pdf p. 13: "The Commission does not hold official statistical data as regard the participation of industry, private consultancies, other private entities, public authorities, civil society organisations and academic institutions"; the questionnaire results "show that civil society organisations, public authorities, academics and independents represent more than half of the experts in JTC 21"; "The role of the industry is therefore central in the standardisation process".
- pdf p. 13 n. 34: JTC 21 folders are "only accessible through a personal login and password"; five DG CNECT.A2 staff have access.
- pdf p. 16: expert-group conflict rules, "If applied to JTC 21, this obligation would prevent industry stakeholders who are themselves providers of AI systems in the meaning of Article 3(1) AI Act to participate"; "the ESOs are private law entities and ... their organisational structure and rules of procedure are internal matters."
- pdf p. 15: Annex III organisations "still face challenges", from "the long duration of processes and the associated cost of participation".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Corporate Europe Observatory (2025; `corporateeurope2025bias`) counts 55 per cent corporate and consultancy participants among 143 self-reported members; the Commission's questionnaire figure points the other way. Neither is an official roll.

## Where the argument uses it
- A4, M2 (P6): supports. The public authority that will cite the standards in the OJ does not know who wrote them, declines conflict-of-interest rules because they would exclude the providers, and calls industry's role "central". That is the guild's position, stated by its regulator.
- Narrows: the Commission keeps the Art 41 fallback and reviews drafts, so the public authority is not absent.
