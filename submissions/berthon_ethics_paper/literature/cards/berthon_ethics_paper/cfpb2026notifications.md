## Summary
Regulation B, the implementing rule of the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, requires a creditor that takes adverse action on a credit application to notify the applicant in writing and either state "specific reasons for the action taken" or disclose the applicant's right to obtain them (§ 1002.9(a)(2)). The statement "must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action", and a bare reference to internal standards or a failed score "are insufficient" (§ 1002.9(b)(2)). This is the oldest US rule that gives the person acted on by an automated decision a record of reasons. The CFPB's 2022 and 2023 circulars applying it to complex algorithms were withdrawn in May 2025 (web search only; the Federal Register notice was not opened); the regulation itself stands in the eCFR text of 1 October 2026 read here.

## Key facts it relies on
- § 1002.9(a)(1): "A creditor shall notify an applicant of action taken within: (i) 30 days after receiving a completed application concerning the creditor's approval of, counteroffer to, or adverse action on the application".
- § 1002.9(a)(2): the adverse-action notification "shall be in writing and shall contain a statement of the action taken; the name and address of the creditor; a statement of the provisions of section 701(a) of the Act; the name and address of the Federal agency that administers compliance with respect to the creditor; and either: (i) A statement of specific reasons for the action taken; or (ii) A disclosure of the applicant's right to a statement of specific reasons within 30 days, if the statement is requested within 60 days of the creditor's notification."
- § 1002.9(b)(2): "The statement of reasons for adverse action required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) of this section must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies or that the applicant, joint applicant, or similar party failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system are insufficient."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Web search (not opened as a source): the CFPB withdrew 67 guidance documents effective 12 May 2025, including Circular 2022-03 on adverse-action notices for decisions "based on complex algorithms" and Circular 2023-03. The withdrawal concerns guidance; the rule text read here is unchanged.
- The notice is a record of reasons after the decision; it carries no right to a human decider and no brake.

## Where the argument uses it
- A8 ("remedies after the harm, not voice"): **narrower**. For US consumer credit, the second party has had a record of reasons since 1977 (ECOA) in the form the paper's duty 3 asks for; she has no brake and no independent decider from this rule.
- CB duty 3 (counter-delegation; her own record): a present-day precedent, limited to credit.
