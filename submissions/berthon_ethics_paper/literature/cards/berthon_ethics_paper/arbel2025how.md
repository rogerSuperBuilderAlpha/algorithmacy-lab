## Summary
Abstract-only card, kept to make the gap search honest. Arbel and Becher test current LLMs as "smart readers" of
standard-form contracts and privacy policies, the sequel to their "Contracts in the Age of Smart Readers" (George
Washington Law Review 90(1): 83-146, 2022; SSRN 10.2139/ssrn.3740356). Paraphrase of the abstract: smart readers cut
contract length by about two-thirds, cut reading time by about a quarter of an hour, bring college-level text to a
fifth-grade reading level, and keep most essential information, but sometimes misstate legal terms or present
information misleadingly, so they cannot replace a lawyer; for the mass of daily transactions the authors conclude
that smart readers "have arrived" and call for a paradigm change in how the no-reading problem is treated. Verified:
Crossref (pp. 336-374, issued 2025-03-31); OpenAlex abstract. No quotation is carried, since no copy was opened.

## Key facts it relies on
- (paraphrase) The chapter treats the "no-reading problem" (the doctrine's "duty to read" meeting consumers who do not
  read) as a problem LLMs can relieve on the consumer's side.
- (paraphrase) Its error finding: smart readers occasionally miscommunicate legal terminology and present information
  erroneously.

## Where the argument uses it
- **Gap search / novelty (lines 68-70).** The "burden of reading" meets LLMs in this literature before the draft, but
  with the opposite valence: the LLM is cast as the reader's aid, and the duty to read is kept while its cost falls.
  The draft's claim that the architecture "leaves her the reading" (line 50) must meet this: a vendor can answer that
  the medium now reads itself to the person. The reply the record offers is that a smart reader run by the drafter is
  the Requerimiento's own interpreter; a smart reader the person brings is D04. Verdict: **contested** for the
  burden-of-reading pairing unless the draft says who runs the reader.
- **D07.** Smart readers are a change of medium at the point of use, which shows the duty is feasible, and shows the
  draft must distinguish operator-run from user-run readers.
