## Summary
Systematic review of twenty studies of teach-back (the clinician asks the patient to restate what was explained), the
practice the draft uses, through Sudore et al. (2006), as the modern form of "check understanding". The review finds
positive primary outcomes in 19 of 20 studies across knowledge, behaviour and readmission measures, but reports that how
teach-back was implemented is "not well described", and notes heterogeneity of settings and outcomes. It supports the
feasibility of D06 and shows that the check is cheap; it does not show that checks survive scale or who bears the cost
when the check fails. Page numbers are PLoS's own (1-18), confirmed in the local PDF.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 11: "Although there was variability among studies in relation to study populations, settings and outcomes, 19
  studies (95%) reported positive findings for primary outcome measures."
- p. 11: "To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review appraising the translation of teach-back into clinical
  practice. We found that implementation of teach-back is not well described in the literature."
- p. 14: "Teach-back is effective across a wide range of settings, populations and outcome measures, although
  implementation techniques are not well described."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Glaser et al. (2020, Medical Decision Making, doi 10.1177/0272989X19896348), the updated systematic review of
  interventions to improve comprehension in informed consent, could not be opened (SAGE and PMC served 403); it is
  the source a reviewer would cite for comprehension staying low after interventions.
- Teach-back is done by a clinician with a patient in front of her; D06 asks an operator to gather evidence about a
  population of customers. The review offers no evidence on that scaled form.

## Where the argument uses it
- **D06 (draft lines 527-530).** Feasibility of the "check understanding" half is supported at the bedside and
  uncontradicted by any failed precedent found. The unsupported part is the transfer from one patient to a population
  acted on by agents at speed (Kolt's changed-condition objection, N6 rank 6). Verdict: D06 **holds, narrower**: the
  check is a working precedent for individual encounters; the "carry the risk" half must do the work where no encounter
  occurs.
