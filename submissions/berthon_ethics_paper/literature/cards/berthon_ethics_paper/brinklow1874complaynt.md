## Summary
Cowper's EETS edition (1874) of two pamphlets by Henry Brinklow (writing as "Roderyck Mors"). The *Complaynt* (dated by the editor about 1542) accuses the bishops of trying to stop the Great Bible, of imprisoning one Porter in 1540 "for readyng in the Byble", and of trying in the Parliament of 1541-2 to discredit the translation, in order "to shutt it vp from the peple". The *Lamentacyon* (1545) complains that the rich citizens of London will not have the Bible in their houses "nor suffre their seruawtes to haue it". Both are contemporary objections to the withholding of the English Bible from the people. Neither names the 1543 Act; the first predates it, and the second blames householders and clergy, not the statute.

## Key facts it relies on
- "How they bare men from readyng the Byble! Yea & Boner, bysshop of London, shamyd not in the yere a thowsand fyue hundreth and forty, to preson one Porter and other for readyng in the Byble" (p. 54; OCR "hare" for "bare").
- The bishops, "euyn in the begynnyng of the last Parlament, in the yere a thousand fyue hundreth and .xli", said the Bible "is not ryghtly translated, and that it is ful of heresys" (p. 54).
- "I cannot blame you, though ye fyght thus agaynst God and his Word, to shutt it vp from the peple." (p. 54).
- The *Lamentacyon*, 1545: "the greate parte of these inordinate riche styfnecked Cytezens will not haue in their howses that lyuely worde of our soules, nor suffre their seruawtes to haue it" (p. 79).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The editor dates the *Complaynt* "about A.D. 1542"; the passage refers to the Parliament of 1541-2, before the 1543 Act. I read only these pages.
- The *Lamentacyon* complaint fits the Act's structure (householders read; servants may not) but does not cite it.

## Where the argument uses it
- A3 (contemporaries objected): **narrower**. Brinklow objects to clerical and householder custody of the English Bible around the time of the Act, not to the Act itself. Use with `furnivall1871four`, which names the law.
