## Summary
As Hamilton reports it, Sparks and Jayaram survey AI against several conceptions of freedom, including republican
liberty, and argue, against the grain of the republican-AI literature, that algorithmic government may protect
republican liberty better than human rule does. They give three reasons; the first two, as Hamilton summarises them,
are that algorithms lack wills or intentionality and so cannot be dominating agents, and that algorithms remove the
need for human discretion in applying rules. Hamilton presents this as the most thorough discussion of AI and
republican liberty alongside Susskind's *The Digital Republic* (p. 6).

## Key facts it relies on
- (Paraphrase of Hamilton, p. 6.) Sparks and Jayaram hold that algorithmic governance can decrease domination; the
  first reason is that algorithms have no will and so are not dominators; the second is the elimination of human
  discretion. The third reason was not captured in the passage read.

## Where the argument uses it
- A7, A8: **the strongest published objection to the draft's republican route that the draft does not name**. The
  refund agent of the draft's worked case (lines 402-407) acts inside a declared envelope, so on Sparks and
