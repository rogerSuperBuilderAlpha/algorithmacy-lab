## Summary
The binding code that all customers of Microsoft AI Services (Azure OpenAI, Foundry and others) "must adhere to in
good faith", replacing earlier codes and aligned, it says, with the EU AI Act. It puts on customers the duties to
disclose AI-generated outputs and actions, to test, to keep "appropriate human oversight", to give users a feedback
channel and to secure access. It bars consequential decisions "without appropriate human oversight". The human
oversight it requires serves the customer: it exists "so customers can find and mitigate undesirable behaviors". The
code gives the people acted on a disclosure and a feedback channel. Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- "This Microsoft Enterprise AI Services Code of Conduct (“Code of Conduct”) defines the requirements that all customers
  of Microsoft AI Services (as defined in the Product Terms) must adhere to in good faith" (opening).
- Customers must ensure their applications "Disclose when the output, decisions, or actions are generated by AI ...
  such that users are not likely to be deceived ... that any AI-generated decision or action is done by a human"
  (Responsible AI requirements).
- Customers must ensure applications "Are tested thoroughly, continuously, and are subject to appropriate human
  oversight so customers can find and mitigate undesirable behaviors" (Responsible AI requirements).
- "Establish feedback channels that allow users to report abuse or issues, and ensure reasonable responses to feedback
  that is received" (Responsible AI requirements).
- Barred: "To make decisions or take actions without appropriate human oversight as part of an application that may
  have a consequential impact on any individual’s legal position, financial position, life opportunities, employment
  opportunities, or human rights" (Usage restrictions).

## Critical notes from the literature
- "Appropriate" and "meaningful" human oversight are undefined in the code.
- The code binds customers to Microsoft. The people an application acts on cannot enforce it; they may report abuse
  through Microsoft's portal (Report abuse).

## Where the argument uses it
- A5: holds. Microsoft's contractual envelope for its AI platform is declared to and binds the customer; the oversight
  it requires is for the customer to "find and mitigate undesirable behaviors".
- A4 / M9 (notice without standing): holds for the people acted on, who receive disclosure that AI was involved and a
  feedback channel with "reasonable responses", not a remedy.
- Refutation sought: the disclosure duty for "decisions, or actions" reaches beyond chat to agent actions. It is notice,
  not an envelope; it narrows nothing in A5 but is the nearest thing to AI Act art. 50 in Microsoft's terms.
