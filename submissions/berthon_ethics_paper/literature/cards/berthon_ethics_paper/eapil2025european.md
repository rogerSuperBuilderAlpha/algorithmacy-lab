## Summary
A legal blog's record of the formal withdrawal of the proposed AI Liability Directive (COM(2022) 496), the EU
instrument that would have eased the burden of proof for people harmed by AI systems, which is the legislative form of
D06's "carry the risk". The Commission listed it for withdrawal in its 2025 Work Programme (11 February 2025) for lack
of a foreseeable agreement and published the withdrawal notice in the Official Journal on 6 October 2025. The EUR-Lex
procedure page (2022/0303(COD)) returned an empty 202 to scripted requests, so this secondary record is what was
opened; Bird & Bird's note (local copy, same folder) agrees on the dates and reason. Read in full.

## Key facts it relies on
- ¶2: "A notice of the withdrawals has been published in the Official Journal on 6 October 2025, following the
  Commission’s 2533rd meeting on 16 July 2025. Both proposals had previously been listed in Annex IV of the Commission
  2025 Work Programme of 11 February 2025".
- ¶3: "For the AI Liability Directive, the stated reason is also the lack of a foreseeable agreement; the Commission
  further indicated that it will evaluate whether to present a revised proposal or to pursue an alternative approach."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Bird & Bird (twobirds.com, 2025, local copy) adds that Parliament supporters "cited pressure from the tech industry
  for regulatory simplification as the main reason" and that the revised Product Liability Directive (2024/2853) still
  applies to software. The draft already cites the PLD (line 473).
- Secondary for the OJ notice; a Bentley user would confirm it on EUR-Lex (procedure 2022/0303/COD).

## Where the argument uses it
- **D06, "carry the risk" (draft lines 527-530), and O02 (lines 472-474).** The one legislative attempt to move the
  burden of proof toward the operator for AI harms was withdrawn without a vote. It is a failed precedent for the
  risk-carrying half of D06 as public law, and it sharpens O02 in the draft's favour: the law provides less than it
  did in 2022. Verdict: D06's second half **holds as a duty, unsupported as a working precedent in AI law**; the
  draft's precedents for it are the 1883 rule and Jones v. Meehan, and the PLD for software defects only.
