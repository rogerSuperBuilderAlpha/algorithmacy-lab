## Summary
The regulation behind AI Act Art 40. It defines a standard as a technical specification "with which compliance is not compulsory" (Art 2(1)); a harmonised standard is a European standard "adopted on the basis of a request made by the Commission" (Art 2(1)(c)). The Commission requests standards, sets their content requirements and deadline, and publishes their references in the OJ (Art 10(1), (6)); the standards themselves are "market-driven" and "based on consensus" (Art 10(1)). Participation is a duty to "encourage and facilitate", not a seat or a vote: European standardisation organisations "shall encourage and facilitate an appropriate representation and effective participation of all relevant stakeholders, including SMEs, consumer organisations and environmental and social stakeholders" (Art 5(1)), chiefly through four EU-funded stakeholder organisations defined in Annex III (SMEs, consumers, environment, social interests).

## Key facts it relies on
- Art 2(1): "'standard' means a technical specification, adopted by a recognised standardisation body, for repeated or continuous application, with which compliance is not compulsory".
- Art 2(1)(c): "'harmonised standard' means a European standard adopted on the basis of a request made by the Commission for the application of Union harmonisation legislation".
- Art 5(1): ESOs "shall encourage and facilitate an appropriate representation and effective participation of all relevant stakeholders, including SMEs, consumer organisations and environmental and social stakeholders", in particular "through the European stakeholder organisations receiving Union financing", at stages from new work items to revision.
- Art 7: Member States "shall, where appropriate, encourage participation of public authorities, including market surveillance authorities".
- Art 10(1): "European standards and European standardisation deliverables shall be market-driven, take into account the public interest as well as the policy objectives clearly stated in the Commission's request and based on consensus. The Commission shall determine the requirements as to the content to be met by the requested document and a deadline for its adoption."
- Art 10(6): where a harmonised standard satisfies the requirements, "the Commission shall publish a reference of such harmonised standard without delay in the Official Journal".
- Annex III(2): the consumer organisation must be "independent of industry, commercial and business or other conflicting interests" and mandated by national consumer organisations in "at least two thirds of the Member States".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Court of Justice, *Public.Resource.Org* (2024, para 74; `cjeu2024publicresource`): although Art 2(1) says compliance "is not compulsory", the presumption of conformity "is one of the essential characteristics of those standards", and recourse to other routes "may prove difficult, or even impossible" (para 75).
- Gornet and Maxwell (2024, p. 11; `gornet2024european`): "Even if Regulation 1025/2012 considers hENs to be voluntary in theory ... in practice it is difficult if not impossible for manufacturers to choose a different avenue."

## Where the argument uses it
- A4, M2 (P6): holds in form, complicated in effect. In law, a standard binds no implementer; in practice the presumption makes harmonised standards the normal route. The drafters are private bodies; affected persons have no seat, only representation through funded organisations whose participation the ESOs must "encourage and facilitate".
