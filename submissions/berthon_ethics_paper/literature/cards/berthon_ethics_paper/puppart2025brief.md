## Summary
A preprint, not peer-reviewed as far as I found (arXiv lists no journal reference). Found through a Warwick EduPort project summary. Twelfth-grade Estonian students were randomised to a brief introduction to ChatGPT (control) or the same plus a text on how LLMs work, their limitations and use strategies (intervention), then solved eight maths puzzles with ChatGPT advice that was wrong in half the trials. The analytic sample was 36 of 42 respondents. Students adopted incorrect advice in 52.1 per cent of trials; the intervention "did not significantly reduce over-reliance" and "led to an increase in under-reliance, as students were more likely to reject correct recommendations" (pdf p. 1). The sample is small and the result is one study.

## Key facts it relies on
- pdf p. 1: "Results indicated widespread over-reliance, with incorrect recommendations adopted in 52.1% of the trials. The intervention did not significantly reduce over-reliance. Instead, it led to an increase in under-reliance, as students were more likely to reject correct recommendations."
- pdf p. 1: "These findings provide preliminary evidence that brief text-based interventions may be ineffective in fostering appropriate reliance."
- pdf p. 4: "the analytic sample consisted of 36 participants, of whom 25 (69.4%) were female and 11 were male (30.6%)."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Consistent with Chiang and Yin (2022, p. 159; `chiang2022exploring`) on non-interactive tutorials; contrasted by Connell Pensky et al. (2026, abstract; `connellpensky2026can`), whose 90-minute modules with practice and feedback did improve checking skill.

## Where the argument uses it
- M11, E16: supports, weakly. A text about AI handed to people beside the task did not make them rely appropriately; it shifted the error. Use only as corroboration, given n = 36 and preprint status.
