## Summary
Colorado's replacement for SB 24-205. SECTION 1 repeals and reenacts Part 17 of Title 6, Article 1 as "AUTOMATED DECISION-MAKING TECHNOLOGY IN CONSEQUENTIAL DECISIONS" (p. 1). It keeps a slimmer set of second-party rights: a "clear and conspicuous notice" before a covered ADMT materially influences a consequential decision, which a deployer may satisfy by "maintaining a prominent public notice" near the point of interaction (§ 6-1-1704(1)-(2), p. 10); within thirty days of an adverse outcome, "a plain language description of the consequential decision and the role the covered ADMT played", a process to request the ADMT's name, version, developer and data sources, and an explanation of the consumer's rights (§ 6-1-1704(3), pp. 10-11); and, on request, correction of inaccurate data and "an opportunity for meaningful human review and reconsideration of the consequential decision, to the extent commercially reasonable" (§ 6-1-1705(1)(a), p. 14). "Meaningful human review" is review by a deployer-designated individual with authority to "approve, modify, or override" who "does not default to the system output" (§ 6-1-1701(15), p. 7). Enforcement is exclusive to the Attorney General and the Act creates no private right of action (§§ 6-1-1706(1)(b), 6-1-1709(1)). It applies to decisions made on or after 1 January 2027 (SECTION 5). Signed 14 May 2026 per the General Assembly's bill page (the PDF's approval block is not legible in extraction). Downloaded 2026-10-24 from the bill page's "Signed Act" link.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 1: "SECTION 1. In Colorado Revised Statutes, repeal and reenact, with amendments, part 17 of article 1 of title 6 as follows: PART 17 AUTOMATED DECISION-MAKING TECHNOLOGY IN CONSEQUENTIAL DECISIONS".
- § 6-1-1701(15), p. 7: "“MEANINGFUL HUMAN REVIEW” MEANS REVIEW BY A INDIVIDUAL DESIGNATED BY THE DEPLOYER WHO HAS AUTHORITY TO APPROVE, MODIFY, OR OVERRIDE A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION AND WHO: (a) CONSIDERS RELEVANT, AVAILABLE PRIMARY EVIDENCE; (b) IS TRAINED TO CONDUCT THE REVIEW; (c) DOES NOT DEFAULT TO THE SYSTEM OUTPUT; AND (d) HAS ACCESS TO SUFFICIENT INFORMATION TO UNDERSTAND" (the list continues).
- § 6-1-1704(1), p. 10: "PRIOR TO A DEPLOYER USING A COVERED ADMT TO MATERIALLY INFLUENCE A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION, THE DEPLOYER SHALL PROVIDE A CLEAR AND CONSPICUOUS NOTICE TO A CONSUMER THAT THE DEPLOYER USED OR WILL USE A COVERED ADMT IN A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION AFFECTING THE CONSUMER AND INSTRUCTIONS REGARDING HOW THE CONSUMER MAY OBTAIN THE ADDITIONAL INFORMATION DESCRIBED IN THIS SECTION."
- § 6-1-1704(2), p. 10: "A DEPLOYER COMPLIES WITH SUBSECTION (1) OF THIS SECTION BY MAINTAINING A PROMINENT PUBLIC NOTICE THAT IS REASONABLY ACCESSIBLE AT POINTS OF CONSUMER INTERACTION, INCLUDING THROUGH A LINK OR POSTING THAT IS REASONABLY PROXIMATE TO THE INTERACTION OR TRANSACTION IN WHICH A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION MAY OCCUR."
- § 6-1-1704(3), pp. 10-11: after an adverse outcome the deployer "SHALL PROVIDE WITHIN THIRTY DAYS AFTER MAKING THE DECISION: (a) A PLAIN LANGUAGE DESCRIPTION OF THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION AND THE ROLE THE COVERED ADMT PLAYED IN THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION; (b) INSTRUCTIONS AND A SIMPLE-TO-FOLLOW PROCESS TO REQUEST ADDITIONAL INFORMATION ABOUT THE COVERED ADMT AND THE INPUTS, INCLUDING THE NAME OF THE COVERED ADMT, THE COVERED ADMT VERSION NUMBER, IF APPLICABLE, THE COVERED ADMT DEVELOPER, AND THE TYPES, CATEGORIES, AND SOURCES OF PERSONAL DATA USED ... (c) AN EXPLANATION OF THE CONSUMER RIGHTS DESCRIBED IN SECTION 6-1-1705 AND HOW TO EXERCISE THEM."
- § 6-1-1705(1)(a), p. 14: the consumer "MAY REQUEST AND THE DEPLOYER SHALL PROVIDE IN RESPONSE TO THE REQUEST: (I) INSTRUCTIONS FOR REQUESTING PERSONAL DATA AND CORRECTING FACTUALLY INCORRECT OR MATERIALLY INACCURATE PERSONAL DATA ... AND (II) AN OPPORTUNITY FOR MEANINGFUL HUMAN REVIEW AND RECONSIDERATION OF THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION, TO THE EXTENT COMMERCIALLY REASONABLE."
- § 6-1-1706(1)(b), pp. 14-15: violations of §§ 6-1-1702 to 6-1-1705 "ARE ENFORCEABLE EXCLUSIVELY BY THE ATTORNEY GENERAL". § 6-1-1709(1): "NOTHING IN THIS PART 17 CREATES A NEW PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION."
- SECTION 5(1) and (3), pp. 22-23: "this act takes effect January 1, 2027"; "This act applies to consequential decisions made on or after January 1, 2027."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Law-firm summaries (Holland & Knight, fetched by WebFetch as a secondary check; Seyfarth, Finnegan surfaced by search but Finnegan returned 403): the Act drops SB 24-205's duty of reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination, mandatory risk-management programmes and annual impact assessments; a 60-day cure right sunsets in 2030.
- Two qualifiers weaken the rights: the pre-decision notice may be a public posting (§ 6-1-1704(2)), and human review is owed "to the extent commercially reasonable" (§ 6-1-1705(1)(a)(II)).

## Where the argument uses it
- A8 ("the law provides remedies after the harm, not voice or brake before it"): **narrower**. From 2027 a Colorado consumer gets notice before, a reasoned record and a human reconsideration after; she gets no brake before the decision, no independent decider, and no standing of her own (AG only).
- A5 (envelope declared to the organisation alone): the public notice is a declared fact of use, not a declared envelope of limits; A5 survives.
- CB duties 3 and 4: a present-day legislated precedent; the "does not default to the system output" definition (§ 6-1-1701(15)(c)) is a legal statement of the competence duty 4 asks the operator to show.
- Context for the paper's standards argument (A4, P6): the 2024 rights were narrowed before they applied; see `colorado2024consumer`.
