## Summary
Upgrade card. The course card read pp. 1027-1035, 1047-1050 and 1069-1072; this card adds the pages that carry
Calo's strongest defence of notice for objection O07: notice works through readers other than the person notified
(the discloser itself, regulators, NGOs, the press), and "visceral notice" changes the experience of the product
instead of adding text. Printed page numbers are confirmed by the running heads in the local PDF (PDF page + 1026).

## Key facts it relies on
- Notice's rationale: "Notice purports to respect the basic autonomy of the consumer or citizen by arming her with
  information and placing the ultimate decision in her hands." (p. 1049).
- Calo concedes: "Privacy notice as deployed in practice likely does not accomplish its avowed goal of enhancing
  consumer autonomy or policing the market." (p. 1050).
- Visceral notice: "Language is not the only means to convey information." (p. 1034); "You can add yet another
  traffic sign to say “road narrows,” or you can accentuate the roadway with rumble strips." (p. 1034).
- Notice as distinct from soft paternalism: "the law and scholarship around notice tracks not the consumer’s
  particular behavior, but rather the consumer’s understanding of the dangers and options" (p. 1046).
- Notice disciplines the discloser: GLB notices "work[ ] surprisingly well" (Swire), "not because consumers will
  actually read and act on them. Rather, they improve privacy because of the behavior the requirement to disclose
  tends to trigger." (p. 1060).
- Outsiders read: "the requirement to disclose can help motivated outsiders assess and criticize company
  practice" (FTC enforcement, NGOs, press) (p. 1061).
- On aggregate surveillance reporting: "Consumers may not read them but activists and the press likely will."
  (p. 1064).
- "an obvious remedy for this problem is not terms in a privacy policy but a requirement to notify the subject of
  the request" (p. 1064).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Ben-Shahar and Schneider (`berthon_ethics_course/benshahar2011failure`) is the skeptic position Calo answers.
- Calo's examples are privacy and product warnings; none involves an agent acting on the person.

## Where the argument uses it
- **O07 and M9 (P20), counter-case (e).** The strongest published form of "disclosure respects autonomy" is not
  that the person reads; it is that disclosure disciplines the discloser and arms outsiders (pp. 1060-1061, 1064).
  P20's "timely disclosure to those affected" plus "share learnings industrywide" fits that model. It **narrows**
  M3/M9: the failure survives only where disclosure is offered as the person's own safeguard and no outside reader
  has standing, which matches N3's narrowing (the grade card's inspector) and N4's (AI Act art. 73 reports to
  authorities).
- **D07 novelty.** Visceral notice (pp. 1033-1035) is a 2012 statement of "change the medium at the point of use";
  cite it as a predecessor.
