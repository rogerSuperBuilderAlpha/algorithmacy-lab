## Summary
Upgrade of the dxd card, which read only c. 72 of the *Admonitio generalis*. This card adds no. 30, Charlemagne's *Epistola generalis* (Boretius dates it 786-800), the circular that sent Paul the Deacon's homiliary to the churches. The king addresses "religiosis lectoribus nostrae ditioni subiectis" (the religious readers subject to our rule), says he has already corrected "universos veteris ac novi instrumenti libros, librariorum imperitia depravatos", and establishes the homiliary "nostra ... auctoritate" for reading in church. Together with c. 72 (correct the books, because men "per inemendatos libros male rogant") and no. 29 (circulate the letter "si gratiam nostram habere vis"), it shows the ruler holding the text and its correction, and naming as beneficiaries the readers in church and the people at prayer. Chapter 72 asks clergy to draw boys of servile as well as free birth ("non solum servilis conditionis infantes, sed etiam ingenuorum filios") into God's service and to keep schools for reading boys (p. 60).

## Key facts it relies on
- No. 30 address: "Karolus, Dei fretus auxilio rex Francorum et Langobardorum ac patricius Romanorum, religiosis lectoribus nostrae ditioni subiectis." (p. 80).
- No. 30, correction by the ruler: "Inter quae iam pridem universos veteris ac novi instrumenti libros, librariorum imperitia depravatos, Deo nos in omnibus adiuvante, examussim correximus." (p. 80; OCR "instrumentib" carries a footnote letter; "examussimcorreximus" divided). Boretius's note 1 cites Alcuin's statement that he emended the Old and New Testament "Karoli regis praecepto" (p. 80, n. 1).
- No. 30, the ground: the lections for the night office were full of faults, and "non sumus passi nostris in diebus in divinis lectionibus inter sacra officia inconsonantes perstrepere soloecismos" (p. 80).
- No. 30, authority: "Quarum omnium textum nostra sagacitate perpendentes, nostra eadem volumina auctoritate constabilimus vestraeque religioni in Christi ecclesiis tradimus ad legendum." (p. 81; OCR "constabiliraus" repaired).
- Boretius's dating: not 782 as Pertz held, because the king says he has already corrected the Bible, and Alcuin was at court only from 781 (p. 80, editorial note).
- *Admonitio generalis* c. 72: schools for boys, "non solum servilis conditionis infantes, sed etiam ingenuorum filios adgregent sibique socient. Et ut scolae legentium puerorum fiant. Psalmos, notas, cantus, compotum, grammaticam per singula monasteria vel episcopia et libros catholicos bene emendate; quia saepe, dum bene aliqui Deum rogare cupiunt, sed per inemendatos libros male rogant." (p. 60).
- No. 29 close: "Huius itaque epistolae exemplaria ad omnes suffragantes tuosque coepiscopos et per universa monasteria dirigi non negligas, si gratiam nostram habere vis." (p. 79).

## Critical notes from the literature
- None of these texts mentions word separation; they concern correct text and correct reading (facet O made the same point for c. 72 and no. 29).
- Steinová (2015, abstract only, facet O) warns that c. 72's grammar and "notas" are disputed; I build nothing on "notas".
- Westwell (2018, facet O) finds local initiative in liturgical correction. The royal letters state the king's custody; they do not show how far it was obeyed.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (first moment; custody of the page by ruler and church): **holds**. The king corrects the Bible, fixes the homiliary by his authority, and addresses readers "subject to our rule".
- A5 (whom the correction served): the texts name as beneficiaries the lector, the congregation and those who "male rogant" from faulty books; c. 72 asks clergy to draw boys of servile as well as free birth into God's service and to keep schools for reading boys (p. 60); it names no lay readership. The correction served the reader in church under the ruler's custody: both halves of A5's test, and the paper should not treat the Carolingian page as the reader's own.
