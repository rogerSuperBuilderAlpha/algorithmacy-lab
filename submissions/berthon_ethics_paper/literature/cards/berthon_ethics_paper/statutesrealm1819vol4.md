## Summary
Chapter 12 of Edward VI's first Parliament (1547), on treasons and felonies. Its repeal clause (p. 19) voids the heresy statutes, the Six Articles, and the Henrician Act touching books of the Old and New Testament in English and the reading, preaching, teaching or expounding of Scripture, which is 34 & 35 Hen. VIII c. 1 (the side-note to that Act in vol. 3, p. 893, points here: "See Stat. 1 Edw. VI. c.12. § 2."). The same Act's clergy clauses (p. 21) extend benefit of clergy and give the Lords of Parliament and peers the privilege even if they cannot read. The OCR of this scan is so damaged that I quote no sentence; the content below is paraphrase of the readable words, flagged as such. A clean copy is needed for any quotation.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The repeal ended the 1543 reading bar four years after it began. The 1543 bar was thus short-lived, which narrows its weight as a "print-stage institution".
- The peers' clause bears on H20 (benefit of clergy): the reading test that decided a commoner's life was waived by statute for the peerage. The legacy card `statute1827criminal` (pending) covers the abolition of 1827; this card is earlier evidence that the test was a gate by rank. I did not rewrite `statute1827criminal`.
