## Summary
Reusch's standard history of the Index, at its commentary on Rule IV. He translates the rule, then traces its history: Paul IV's Index (1559) reserved the licence to the Roman Inquisition; Trent's milder rule (1564) gave it to bishops and inquisitors; Sixtus V withdrew it; Clement VIII's "Observatio" (1596), while appearing to explain the rule, withdrew the bishops' power to license, restoring Paul IV's position until Benedict XIV modified the rule in 1758. Practice differed by country: Spain banned vernacular Bibles outright (Valdés 1551, 1559; Quiroga 1583) and had no Catholic Bible until 1790; Belgium enforced written licences by ordinance (1570) and synods; but in Germany and France strict Catholic writers record that the rule was not observed. I read this volume in the OCR of the Toronto scan; the title page reads "ERSTER BAND. BONN VERLAG VON MAX COHEN & SOHN (FR. COHEN) 1883."

## Key facts it relies on
- Clement VIII's observation, in Reusch's German: "Es ist zu bemerken, dass durch diese Regel den Bischöfen, Inquisitoren oder Ordensoberen nicht aufs neue die Vollmacht gegeben wird, die Erlaubniss zum Kaufen, Lesen oder Besitzen von Bibeln ... in der Volksprache zu ertheilen, nachdem ihnen diese Vollmacht durch das Mandat und den Usus der Rom. Inquisition entzogen worden." (pp. 333-334).
- Reusch's reading: "Damit ist die Bestimmung Pauls IV. einfach wiederhergestellt, und fortan konnte also ein Bischof nur etwa dann die Erlaubniss ertheilen, wenn er speciell durch den Papst oder die Rom. Inquisition ermächtigt worden war." (p. 334).
- Duration: the rule with Clement's addition was "nach der curialistischen Anschauung geltendes Recht" until the 1758 edition (p. 334).
- Spain: "In Spanien waren schon durch Valdes 1551 und 1559 alle Bibelübersetzungen unbedingt verboten" (p. 334); the first Spanish Catholic Bible apart from the Valencian of 1478 appeared in 1790 (p. 334).
- Not enforced north of the Alps: strict Catholic writers "constatiren doch ..., dass dieselbe in praxi diesseits der Alpen nicht überall als verbindlich angesehen wurde" (p. 335); Serarius (1612): reading Eck's or Dietenberger's Bible in Germany without licence is "nicht nur nicht getadelt und bestraft, sondern vielmehr gebilligt und sehr gelobt, als wenn die Erlaubniss allgemein ertheilt wäre" (p. 335).
- France: Véron (1646): "Da die 4. Regel nirgends in Frankreich bekannt gemacht worden, vielmehr durch den entgegengesetzten Gebrauch verworfen wird, so kann man in ganz Frankreich ohne vorherige Erlaubniss die Bibel lesen" (p. 336; OCR "Gre-' brauch" repaired).
- Belgium: an ordinance of 19 May 1570 forbade booksellers to sell vernacular Bibles to anyone without a written licence; a synod at Tournai in 1600 ordered the rule read from the pulpits twice a year (p. 335).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Reusch writes from outside the curial view (he calls it "der curialistischen Anschauung", p. 334); his reading of Clement's observation as a disguised change ("in Wirklichkeit aber dieselbe abändert", p. 333) is his judgement. The quoted texts of the observation and of Serarius and Véron are his translations into German.
- Agrees with Johns's claim that "in France ... the Index was effectively ignored" (`johns2002how`, p. 119) for Rule IV specifically.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (custody; gate held by the few): **holds and sharpens**: in 1596 the licence to read moved from the local bishop to Rome.
- Refutation sought ("the Index widely ignored"): **narrows** A2's reach. Rule IV was enforced in Italy and Belgium; Spain and Portugal banned vernacular Bibles outright under their own indices, a harsher national rule rather than Rule IV binding; north of the Alps it was often not observed (Reusch pp. 334-336). The paper should say where.
- A3: Reusch quotes Catholic writers who treat the rule as dead letter, not as a wrong; it is evidence of non-enforcement, not of objection.
