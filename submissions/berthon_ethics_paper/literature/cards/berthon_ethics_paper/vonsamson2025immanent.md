## Summary
Von Samson reconstructs Rachel Fraser's (2023) dilemma for immanent critique, a version of Enoch's shmagency
objection: norms constitutive of a practice lack normative authority unless there is independent reason to engage in
the practice. So either critique grants authority to "de facto governing norms", which "renders social critique
structurally conservative", or it denies it, and "immanent critique loses its normative bite" (abstract, p. 1308;
§3.1). She rejects two rescues and defends a third: immanent critique is "methodology, not metaethics" (p. 1318). It
clarifies contradictions, including the unmet or perverted "promises" by which social formations legitimise
themselves (Jaeggi), and leaves the norms' authority to be justified independently. The HU Berlin copy is the
published version (CC BY), deposited at HU edoc; it carries the published pagination and text (header
"Eur J Philos. 2025;33:1308-1325"). Printed page = PDF page plus 1307.

## Key facts it relies on
- Dilemma, from the abstract: "Either they grant normative authority to de facto governing norms, which renders social
  critique structurally conservative. Or they deny it, in which case immanent critique loses its normative bite."
  (p. 1308)
- Fraser's three premises as reconstructed: constitutive norms lack authority; critique needs authoritative norms;
  immanence requires constitutive norms (p. 1309; §3.3, p. 1313).
- Torture versus chess: constitutive aims carry no authority where the practice itself is morally laden (p. 1311).
- Jaeggi's "promises": capitalism's promises of freedom and equality "are not only systematically unrealizable under
  capitalist conditions. They are realized in a perverted way" (pp. 1316-1317).
- Authority may come from independent sources: "The form of life might be intrinsically valuable. We might have
  signed a contract. We might have made a promise. All that matters is that the normative authority of these norms is
  independently justifiable." (p. 1318)
- "immanent critique is methodology, not metaethics" (p. 1318).

## Where the argument uses it
- A7, refutation sought: **narrows the bridge, does not sink it**. A standard that binds only because it was embedded
  in a past practice (Tudor licensing, the jurat) has no authority over a present actor by that fact; and if it did,
  critique would be conservative. A7 holds when the standard's authority is independent: the present actor's own
  promise or professed standard (benchmarks 2 and 3 read in the present tense), or a value defended on its own
  terms (non-domination). The paper's three benchmarks show *that* the past practices failed; the authority of the
  standard over Microsoft must come from Microsoft's professions or from the external value.
- Benchmark 3 ("a promise went unmet") has its best philosophical support in the Jaeggi line reported here: critique
  of a formation by the promises it legitimises itself with (pp. 1316-1317). That fits the post's own promises
  ("trust architecture", P3).
