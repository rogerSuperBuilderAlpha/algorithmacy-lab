## Summary
The first US state statute to give the person on the receiving end of an automated "consequential decision" a package of rights: a statement of the principal reasons including "the degree to which, and manner in which" the AI system contributed, an opportunity to correct the data, and an opportunity to appeal with human review "if technically feasible" (§ 6-1-1703(4)(b), pp. 13-14). Its operative date, 1 February 2026 throughout the text, was postponed by a 2025 special-session bill and the Act was then repealed and reenacted with amendments by SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026 per the General Assembly's bill page; `colorado2026automated`) before any provision applied. Enforcement was exclusive to the Attorney General (§ 6-1-1706(1)) with no private right of action (§ 6-1-1706(6)). The card records what the law would have given the second party and that it never took effect; the signing date is not legible in the signed PDF's approval block and is not asserted here.

## Key facts it relies on
- § 6-1-1703(4)(b), pp. 13-14: "ON AND AFTER FEBRUARY 1, 2026, A DEPLOYER THAT HAS DEPLOYED A HIGH-RISK ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM TO MAKE, OR BE A SUBSTANTIAL FACTOR IN MAKING, A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION CONCERNING A CONSUMER SHALL, IF THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION IS ADVERSE TO THE CONSUMER, PROVIDE TO THE CONSUMER: (I) A STATEMENT DISCLOSING THE PRINCIPAL REASON OR REASONS FOR THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION, INCLUDING: (A) THE DEGREE TO WHICH, AND MANNER IN WHICH, THE HIGH-RISK ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM CONTRIBUTED TO THE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION; (B) THE TYPE OF DATA THAT WAS PROCESSED ... (II) AN OPPORTUNITY TO CORRECT ANY INCORRECT PERSONAL DATA ... (III) AN OPPORTUNITY TO APPEAL AN ADVERSE CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION CONCERNING THE CONSUMER ARISING FROM THE DEPLOYMENT OF A HIGH-RISK ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM, WHICH APPEAL MUST, IF TECHNICALLY FEASIBLE, ALLOW FOR HUMAN REVIEW UNLESS PROVIDING THE OPPORTUNITY FOR APPEAL IS NOT IN THE BEST INTEREST OF THE CONSUMER".
- § 6-1-1706(1), p. 22: "NOTWITHSTANDING SECTION 6-1-103, THE ATTORNEY GENERAL HAS EXCLUSIVE AUTHORITY TO ENFORCE THIS PART 17."
- § 6-1-1706(6), p. 23: "THIS PART 17 DOES NOT PROVIDE THE BASIS FOR, AND IS NOT SUBJECT TO, A PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION FOR VIOLATIONS OF THIS PART 17 OR ANY OTHER LAW."
- Operative date: every duty in the text opens "ON AND AFTER FEBRUARY 1, 2026".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Web search (law-firm summaries, not opened as sources): SB 25B-004 (August 2025) moved the date to 30 June 2026; SB 26-189 repealed and reenacted Part 17 on 14 May 2026 with effect for decisions on or after 1 January 2027, dropping the duty of reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination, risk-management programmes and impact assessments. The successor's consumer rights are on `colorado2026automated`.
- The appeal right was conditioned on technical feasibility and the consumer's "best interest"; the reviewer was the deployer's own.

## Where the argument uses it
- A8 ("the law provides remedies after the harm, not voice or brake before it"): **narrower**, as a legislated but never-operative counter-case: the Act would have given the second party a reasoned record, correction and a human appeal. Its repeal before effect, and the successor's "to the extent commercially reasonable" (SB 26-189 § 6-1-1705(1)(a)(II)), are evidence for the paper's claim that these protections are fragile where the deploying industry writes the rules.
- CB duties 3 and 4: a drafted precedent for the person's own record and for a human decider.
