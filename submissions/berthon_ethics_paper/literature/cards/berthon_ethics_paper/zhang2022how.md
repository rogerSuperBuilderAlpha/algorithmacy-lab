## Summary
Interview study (24 iPhone users) of Apple's privacy "nutrition labels", the privacy field's grade card: a short,
standardised, point-of-use disclosure that was meant to replace reading the policy. Zhang and colleagues find
misunderstanding of terms, confusing structure, disconnection from the controls, and low discoverability even among
the concerned. The paper is a failed-precedent case for D07 where the changed medium is still text to be read and is
authored by the party it describes. Printed page = PDF page + 203, confirmed by the header "2022 (4):204–228".

## Key facts it relies on
- Abstract (p. 204): "We uncovered misunderstandings of and dissatisfaction with the iOS privacy labels that hinder
  their effectiveness, including confusing structure, unfamiliar terms, and disconnection from permission settings and
  controls."
- p. 218: "However, the labels suffer from confusing terms and definitions (see Section 4.3.2), which led to a range of
  misunderstandings."; "These findings clearly demonstrate that Apple’s privacy labels still fail to fully support user
  comprehension of the disclosed app privacy practices."
- p. 218: "Our finding shows that the discoverability of privacy labels on each individual app’s page in the App Store
  is low, even for participants who said they were concerned about mobile app privacy."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Companion studies (Li et al. 2022, CHI EA, developers' difficulty producing accurate labels; Kollnig et al. 2022,
  FAccT, "Goodbye Tracking?") were located in OpenAlex but not opened.
- The labels are self-declared by the developer, like the model card (`liang2024whats`) and the San Diego 'A'
  (`ho2012fudging`): the same configuration recurs.

## Where the argument uses it
- **D07 (draft lines 530-532).** Shows the limit of "change the medium": a shorter text is still a text, and a label
  the person must find and decode leaves the reading with her. The grade card worked in Jin and Leslie's account
  because a single letter needs no decoding and an inspector, not the restaurant, wrote it. Verdict: D07 **narrower**:
  the duty holds for markers that require no reading and are issued by a party other than the operator; it does not
  hold for operator-authored summaries.
