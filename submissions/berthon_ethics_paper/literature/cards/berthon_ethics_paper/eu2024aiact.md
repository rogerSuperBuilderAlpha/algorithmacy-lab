## Summary
Upgrade card. The earlier card read Art 73(1)-(2) only and flagged that "the whole of Art 73 was not read line by line". I read all eleven paragraphs of Art 73 and the articles that feed it (3(49), 16, 17(1)(i), 20, 26(5), 55(1)(c)), the standards articles (40-42, recital 121) and the remedies (85-87, recitals 170-171). Copy: Publications Office CELLAR XHTML, fetched 2026-10-24, extracted to text; its sha256 equals the EUR-Lex extraction behind the superseded card, so the two copies are byte-identical.

Three findings. (1) The incident regime runs from provider to authority and from authority to authority. Every addressee in Art 73 is a market surveillance authority, a national public authority under Art 77(1), a notified body or the Commission; no paragraph names the affected person or requires notice to anyone harmed. Deployers who find a serious incident inform "first the provider" and then importers, distributors and authorities (Art 26(5)). When a system is non-conforming, providers inform distributors and "where applicable, the deployers" (Art 20(1)). (2) Harmonised standards confer a presumption of conformity "to the extent that those standards cover those requirements" (Art 40(1)); the Commission may substitute common specifications if standards are refused, late, non-compliant or "insufficiently address fundamental rights concerns" (Art 41(1)(a)(iii)). (3) The affected person's own rights are a complaint to the market surveillance authority, to be "taken into account" for surveillance (Art 85), and an explanation from the deployer for Annex III decisions with adverse legal or similarly significant effects (Art 86(1)), residual to other Union law (86(3)). The Act gives no right to compensation; recital 170 points to "Union and national law" for remedies.

## Key facts it relies on
- Art 3(49): "'serious incident' means an incident or malfunctioning of an AI system that directly or indirectly leads to any of the following: (a) the death of a person, or serious harm to a person's health; (b) a serious and irreversible disruption of the management or operation of critical infrastructure; (c) the infringement of obligations under Union law intended to protect fundamental rights; (d) serious harm to property or the environment".
- Art 73(1): "Providers of high-risk AI systems placed on the Union market shall report any serious incident to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where that incident occurred."
- Art 73(2)-(4): within 15 days of awareness; two days for a widespread infringement or a 3(49)(b) incident; 10 days on a death.
- Art 73(6): the provider "shall, without delay, perform the necessary investigations ... This shall include a risk assessment of the incident, and corrective action", and shall not alter the system in a way that may affect evaluation "prior to informing the competent authorities".
- Art 73(7): on a 3(49)(c) (fundamental-rights) incident the market surveillance authority "shall inform the national public authorities or bodies referred to in Article 77(1)"; "The Commission shall develop dedicated guidance ... That guidance shall be issued by 2 August 2025".
- Art 73(8): the authority takes measures under Art 19 of Reg 2019/1020 "within seven days". Art 73(11): national authorities "shall immediately notify the Commission".
- Art 73(9)-(10): where equivalent sectoral reporting exists (Annex III providers under other Union instruments; medical devices), notification under the Act is "limited to those referred to in Article 3, point (49)(c)".
- Art 26(5): "Where deployers have identified a serious incident, they shall also immediately inform first the provider, and then the importer or distributor and the relevant market surveillance authorities of that incident."
- Art 20(1): providers of non-conforming systems "shall inform the distributors of the high-risk AI system concerned and, where applicable, the deployers, the authorised representative and importers accordingly."
- Art 55(1)(c): GPAI providers with systemic risk "keep track of, document, and report, without undue delay, to the AI Office and, as appropriate, to national competent authorities, relevant information about serious incidents".
- Art 17(1)(i): the quality management system includes "procedures related to the reporting of a serious incident in accordance with Article 73". Art 73 is not itself listed in Art 99(4); the fine route runs through Art 16 (provider obligations, which include the Art 17 system).
- Art 40(1): systems in conformity with harmonised standards published in the OJ "shall be presumed to be in conformity with the requirements set out in Section 2 of this Chapter ... to the extent that those standards cover those requirements or obligations."
- Art 40(3): participants "shall seek to promote investment and innovation in AI ... and to enhance multi-stakeholder governance ensuring a balanced representation of interests".
- Art 41(1)(a)(iii): common specifications where "the relevant harmonised standards insufficiently address fundamental rights concerns".
- Recital 121: "A balanced representation of interests involving all relevant stakeholders in the development of standards, in particular SMEs, consumer organisations and environmental and social stakeholders ... should therefore be encouraged."
- Art 85: "any natural or legal person having grounds to consider that there has been an infringement ... may submit complaints to the relevant market surveillance authority"; complaints "shall be taken into account for the purpose of conducting market surveillance activities".
- Art 86(1): the affected person has "the right to obtain from the deployer clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken"; 86(3): "only to the extent that the right ... is not otherwise provided for under Union law".
- Recital 170: "Union and national law already provide effective remedies to natural and legal persons whose rights and freedoms are adversely affected by the use of AI systems."
- Recital 171: the explanation "should provide a basis on which the affected persons are able to exercise their rights."
- Art 4 (as enacted; since replaced, see `euomnibus2026`): literacy of "their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf ... and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used."
- Recital 20: for affected persons, literacy means "the knowledge necessary to understand how decisions taken with the assistance of AI will have an impact on them".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Veale and Zuiderveen Borgesius (2021, pp. 105, 111; `veale2021demystifying`) on the 2021 draft: "standardisation is arguably where the real rule-making in the Draft AI Act will occur"; "individuals affected by AI systems have no right to complain to an MSA". The enacted Act added Art 85 and Art 86, which answer the second point in part; the complaint right remains a surveillance input, not a determination of the complainant's case.
- Laux, Wachter and Mittelstadt (2024, §4; `laux2024three`): under the presumption of conformity "AI providers can be considered compliant while simultaneously setting subjective ethical trade-offs and thresholds without direct external stakeholder involvement."
- Latham & Watkins (client alert, 28 Oct 2025; secondary, facet search row 9) reports the Commission's draft Art 73 guidance of 26 September 2025 and a reporting template "for submissions to the competent market surveillance authority". I could not open the draft itself (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu returned HTTP 403).

## Where the argument uses it
- A4, M9 (P20 "timely disclosure to those affected"): holds, and the earlier card's reading now rests on the whole article. Art 73 contains no duty to notify affected persons. The post asks for more than the Act on this point (facet V1, contradiction 1 stands).
- O02 ("the law already provides"): narrower than the objection. The Act's own route for the affected is complaint (Art 85) and explanation (Art 86); compensation lies outside it (recital 170; see `eu2024productliability`, `eu2016gdpr`).
- A4, M2 (P6 industry standards): complicates "standards bind no implementer". Harmonised standards carry a presumption of conformity (Art 40(1)); the public authority keeps a fallback (Art 41). See `cjeu2024publicresource` for their legal status.
