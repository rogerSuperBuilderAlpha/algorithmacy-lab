## Summary
The earlier card rested on Encyclopedia Virginia's transcription and had not seen the printed page. This card reads
the act on the page images that Encyclopedia Virginia serves as "Original Images": two 600-dpi scans, p. 107
(`11750_e6e9bd51565c130.jpg`, above) and p. 108 (`11751_76f14bf889ef62b.jpg`, sha256 `c9197090eb2e…`). Each image
carries the HathiTrust stamp "uc1.a0001803188" and "Digitized by Google / Original from University of California".
The printed text matches the transcription. Sections 1-3 provide for the sale of free Black people who remain in the
state contrary to law; s. 4 makes meetings of free Black people "for teaching them reading or writing" an unlawful
assembly punishable by up to twenty lashes; s. 5 fines white people who assemble with them to teach them; s. 6 fines
white people who teach slaves to read or write "for pay or compensation", and anyone contracting with such a
teacher; s. 7 orders the act given in charge to grand juries; s. 8 sets 1 June 1831 as the start date. The page break
falls between ss. 5 and 6, as the earlier card said. The running heads read "Slaves, Free Negroes and Mulattoes."
(p. 107) and "Profane Swearing, &c.—Inspection.—Flour." (p. 108).

## Key facts it relies on
- Heading: "CHAP. XXXIX.—AN ACT to amend the act concerning slaves, free negroes and mulattoes. [Passed April 7,
  1831.]" (p. 107).
- s. 4: "all meetings of free negroes or mulattoes, at any school-house, church, meeting-house or other place for
  teaching them reading or writing, either in the day or night, under whatsoever pretext, shall be deemed and
  considered as an unlawful assembly"; corporal punishment "not exceeding twenty lashes" (p. 107). Margin note:
  "Meetings for teaching free negroes, &c. reading or writing, prohibited."
- s. 5: a white person who assembles with free Black people "for the purpose of instructing such free negroes or
  mulattoes to read or write" is fined "not exceeding fifty dollars" and may be imprisoned "not exceeding two
  months" (p. 107).
- s. 6: "if any white person, for pay or compensation, shall assemble with any slaves for the purpose of teaching,
  and shall teach any slave to read or write", that person and "any white person or persons contracting with such
  teacher so to act" are fined "not less than ten, nor exceeding one hundred dollars" (p. 108). Margin note:
  "Penalty on white persons for teaching slaves reading or writing for pay."
- s. 8: "This act shall be in force from the first day of June next" (p. 108).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The act states no ground for ss. 4-6. North Carolina's act of the same session does (see
  `berthon_ethics_paper/northcarolina1831acts.md`).
- It reaches assemblies of free Black people and paid teaching of slaves; it does not reach unpaid teaching of a
  slave by an owner or a parent (facet C's reading, confirmed on the page).
- The images are Google scans of a University of California copy served through HathiTrust; I did not open
  HathiTrust itself (it returned HTTP 403 to my request).

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (failure: competence withheld; H04): **holds, narrower**. The printed act withholds reading and writing from
  free Black people in assemblies and from slaves taught for pay; it is narrower than "criminalizing literacy
  instruction" in general.
- A3: the act alone shows the withholding, not a contemporary objection; the objection must come from other sources
  (Douglass, Margo on Williams; ledger H04).
