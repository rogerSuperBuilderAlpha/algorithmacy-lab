## Summary
A Science policy forum arguing that large models should be understood not as intelligent agents but as "cultural and
social technologies" in the line of writing, print, markets and bureaucracies. The paper is the most-cited (73 in
OpenAlex) recent statement of the print analogy for LLMs. It names the regulatory institutions that grew up around
print (editors, peer review, libel law) and says such countervailing institutions came from "concerted and sustained
efforts". It does not discuss vendor or enterprise governance of agents, the people an agent acts on, custody of the
record, or the burden of reading. Quotations are from the author's version; the version of record may differ in
wording. Verified: Crossref 10.1126/science.adt9819 (Science 387(6739): 1153-1156, 2025).

## Key facts it relies on
- Para. beginning "The new technology of large models": "Like pictures, writing, print, video, Internet search, and
  other such technologies, large models allow people to access information that other people have created."
- Para. beginning "The AI debate should focus": "We now have a technology that does for written and pictured culture,
  what large-scale markets do for the economy, what large-scale bureaucracy does for society, and perhaps even
  comparable to what print once did for language."
- Para. beginning "In the case of earlier social and cultural technologies": "a range of further institutions,
  including normative and regulatory institutions, emerged to temper their effects. These ranged from editors, peer
  review, and libel laws for print, to election law, deposit insurance and the Securities and Exchange Commission for
  markets, democracies, and bureaucracies."
- Same para.: "These countervailing forces did not emerge on their own, however, but resulted from concerted and
  sustained efforts by actors both within and outside the technologies themselves."

## Critical notes from the literature
- The analogy is to print as a cultural technology (access to others' information), not to print's governance; the
  Stationers, licensing and the reader bound by a text are absent.
- The paper rejects the agent frame that Nadella's "insider" metaphor presupposes; that is a disagreement the draft
  could note, since Nadella's post treats the model as an actor with privileges.

## Where the argument uses it
- **Opening, lines 63-70 (prior uses of the print analogy).** This is prior work the draft does not cite and should:
  the print analogy for LLMs is established in Science, in general and policy-oriented form. It does not pre-empt CA:
  it says nothing about who holds the record, the brake or the reading for the people acted on. Verdict for the
  novelty claim at lines 68-70: **narrower** (true for "turns the record on the people a deploying organization's
  agents act on"; not true for "reaches for the press").
- **A1 / A6.** Its "countervailing institutions" paragraph supports the paper's premise that remedies were
  institutional, not readers (O03 reply, draft lines 449-452).
