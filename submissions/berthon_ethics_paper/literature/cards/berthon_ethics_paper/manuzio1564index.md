## Summary
The Tridentine Index of 1564 with its ten rules. Rule IV makes the reading of Catholic vernacular Bibles depend on a written licence from the bishop or inquisitor, on the advice of parish priest or confessor, granted to those the licenser judges will gain "fidei, atque pietatis augmentum"; the unlicensed reader cannot be absolved until he surrenders the Bible, and booksellers who sell to the unlicensed lose the price and face the bishop's penalties. Rule III allows Old Testament versions by condemned authors only "viris tantum doctis & piis" by the bishop's judgement. Rule VI applies Rule IV to vernacular books of controversy. The OCR reads long s as "f" and u/v interchangeably; I normalise "f" to "s" and expand nothing else. Page numbers follow the running heads ("LIBRORVM PROHIB. 15", "16 REGVLAE INDICIS").

## Key facts it relies on
- Rule IV, ground and licence: "Cum experimento manifestum sit, si sacra Biblia uulgari lingua passim sine discrimine permittantur, plus inde, ob hominum temeritatem, detrimenti, quam utilitatis oriri; hac in parte iudicio Episcopi, aut Inquisitoris stetur; ut cum consilio Parochi, uel Confessarii Bibliorum, a Catholicis auctoribus uersorum, lectionem in uulgari lingua eis concedere possint, quos intellexerint ex huiusmodi lectione non damnum, sed fidei, atque pietatis augmentum capere posse: quam facultatem in scriptis habeant." (p. 15; OCR "fi-ne", "dc\",", "ConfelfariiBibliorum", "pofrcrquamfacultatem" repaired by word division only).
- Rule IV, sanction: "Qui autem absque tali facultate ea legere, seu habere praesumpserit; nisi prius Bibliis Ordinario redditis, peccatorum absolutionem percipere non possit." (p. 15).
- Rule IV, booksellers: those who sell vernacular Bibles to one "non habenti" the licence "librorum pretium, in usus pios ab Episcopo conuertendum, amittant; aliisque poenis pro delicti qualitate eiusdem Episcopi arbitrio subiaceant" (p. 15); regulars need their prelates' licence (p. 16).
- Rule III: Old Testament versions "uiris tantum doctis & piis iudicio Episcopi concedi poterunt", to be used as aids to understanding the Vulgate, not as sacred text (p. 14).
- Rule VI: vernacular books of controversy between Catholics and heretics "non passim permittantur; sed idem de iis seruetur, quod de Bibliis uulgari lingua scriptis statutum est" (p. 16).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Reusch (`reusch1883index`, Band 1 pp. 333-336) shows the rule was tightened in 1596, when Clement VIII's observation withdrew the bishops' power to license, and that north of the Alps it was often not observed in practice.
- Rule IV is a licence, so it reads as access for the approved as well as a bar for the rest. The criterion is the licenser's judgement of the reader's piety; the reader has no claim.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (custody; the gate held by the few): **holds**. The text puts vernacular Bible reading under a written licence issued by bishop or inquisitor on the cleric's advice.
- Refutation sought ("Rule IV's licence as access, not bar"): the text supports a weak form, since it permits licensed reading; the 1596 observation (Reusch) removed even the bishop's power to grant it.
