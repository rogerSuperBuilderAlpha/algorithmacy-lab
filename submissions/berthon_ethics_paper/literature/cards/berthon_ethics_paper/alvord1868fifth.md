## Summary
J. W. Alvord, general superintendent of schools for the Freedmen's Bureau, reports on the six months to
31 December 1867 and opens with a historical survey of the work since the war. The survey quotes his own report of
1 January 1866 at length in small type (p. 7): opposition to Black schools, a Louisiana petition for schools signed
by parents with their marks, and the need for Black teachers and normal schools. The consolidated table for the
half-year (pp. 12-13) and the summary on p. 14 count schools "sustained" wholly or partly by the freedmen and school
buildings they own. The archive.org legacy card `berthon_ethics_course/alvord1866report.md` holds only Fleming's
1905 reprint of the 1866 report's "native schools" passage; this card adds the Bureau's own later count. The OCR is
poor; I read the quoted passages on the page images (archive.org leaves n10, n17; `n10.jpg` sha256
`d78b54c8…`). The p. 12 image (leaf n15) is not saved in the card's folder, so nothing here rests on it; the
table on pp. 12-13 is described from the OCR only.

## Key facts it relies on
- Quoting the 1866 report (p. 7): a Louisiana petition "at least thirty feet in length, representing ten thousand
  negroes", in which Alvord noted "the names and marks (X) of such a long list of parents, ignorant themselves, but
  begging that their children might be educated; promising that from beneath their present burdens and out of their
  extreme poverty, they would pay for it".
- Also quoted from 1866 (p. 7): "A class of schools is called for in which colored teachers can be taught", and of
  the freed population, "A million, at least, of these four millions ... are to-day ready to engage in the study of
  books"; supplying them "we need ... 20,000 teachers".
- Summary for the half-year ending 31 December 1867 (p. 14): "Schools of all kinds, 3,084; teachers, 6,492; pupils,
  189,517. Of the whole number of schools 1,000 are sustained wholly or in part by the freedmen, and they own 364 of
  the buildings used for school purposes."
- Teachers (p. 14): "Two thousand nine hundred and forty-eight teachers are white and 3,544 colored."
- Fees (p. 14): "Twenty-six thousand one hundred and thirty-nine pupils have paid tuition, amounting in the
  aggregate to $65,319 75".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Alvord is the Bureau's own officer reporting on the Bureau's work; the counts include schools "within the
  knowledge of the superintendents, but not regularly reported" (p. 14).
- "Sustained wholly or in part" does not separate schools the freedmen ran from schools they helped fund.
- The petition passage shows people who could not sign petitioning for their children's schooling, in writing, by
  mark, through a literate scribe.

## Where the argument uses it
- B1 / H25 (capacity built from below): **holds**. By 1867 freedpeople sustained in whole or part about a third of
  the reported schools (1,000 of 3,084), owned 364 buildings, and over half the teachers were Black (p. 14).
- A2 (competence withheld; H04): **complicates** the picture of withheld competence by showing the bound party
  building it once the bar lifted, and paying for it (p. 7, p. 14).
