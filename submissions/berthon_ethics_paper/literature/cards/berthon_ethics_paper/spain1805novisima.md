## Summary
The Novísima Recopilación (1805) compiles the Pragmática that Isabel issued at Alcalá on 7 June 1503 on notaries
(escribanos) as libro X, título XXIII ("De las escrituras públicas, sus notas y registros"), leyes I-V, each marked
"Cap. [n] de la dicha pragmática". I read them on the page images of the archive.org copy BRes002064 (publisher line "Madrid : Julián
Viana Razola", 1805; holding library not stated in the metadata): p. 144 (above) and p. 145 (`novrec/p145.jpg`, sha256 `f51c6e5c2658…`); the OCR text
`novrec/BRes002064_djvu.txt` (sha256 `813b1131…`) was used only to find them. Ley I (cap. 1) requires each notary to
keep a bound protocol book, to write in it the full note of every instrument with parties, date, place and every
clause, to read the note in the presence of the parties and witnesses, and to have the parties sign, or, if a party
cannot write, a witness or another who can write signs for him and the notary records that he did so. No signed copy
may issue unless the parties and witnesses were present at the execution of the note; a copy issued otherwise is
void and the notary loses his office and pays the party's interest. Ley II (cap. 2) bars instruments for parties the
notary does not know unless two witnesses vouch for them. Ley III (cap. 3) obliges notaries to give the party its
signed copy within three days (eight for long instruments). Ley IV (cap. 4) obliges them to keep the registers and
to collate copies with the register in the parties' presence. Ley V (cap. 5) gives an instrument that belongs to both
parties to whichever party asks for it, "aunque la otra parte no la pida".

## Key facts it relies on
- Heading of ley I: "D.ª Isabel en Alcalá por pragmática de 7 de Junio de 1503 cap. 1." (p. 144).
- Reading and signing: "los dichos Escribanos las lean, presentes las partes y los testigos: y si las partes las
  otorgaren, las firmen de sus nombres, y si no supieren firmar, firmen por ellos qualquiera de los testigos, ó otro
  que sepa escribir; el qual dicho Escribano haga mencion como el testigo firmó por la parte que no sabia escribir"
  (p. 144).
- Sanction: a copy issued otherwise "sea en sí ninguna, y el Escribano que la hiciere pierda el oficio" (p. 144).
- The party's copy, ley III: "los Escribanos hayan de dar y den las escrituras á la parte, del dia que ge la pidiere
  y debiere de dar hasta tres dias primeros siguientes" (p. 145).
- Collation in the parties' presence, ley IV: copies are checked "con el registro en presencia de las partes, si
  fueren en el lugar, y quisieren estar á ello presentes" (p. 145).
- Either party's copy, ley V: the notary must give an instrument belonging to both parties "á la parte que se la
  pidiere, aunque la otra parte no la pida" (p. 145).

## Critical notes from the literature
- This is the 1805 compilation's text, with its spelling, not the 1503 print (*Libro de las Bulas y Pragmáticas*,
  1503), which I did not reach. Marchant (2015; `berthon_ethics_course/marchant2015expedicion.md`) reads the 1503
  text and places it in the shift from the notarial note to the protocol.
- The law requires reading aloud before parties and witnesses and a witness's signature for a party who cannot
  write. It does not require that anyone check what the party understood. Its protections are a public reading,
  a witness, a register the notary must keep and a copy the party is owed.
- Andrien's review of Burns (`berthon_ethics_course/andrien2012burns.md`) records notaries in colonial Cuzco who
  served the powerful despite these rules; the law states the professed standard, not practice.

## Where the argument uses it
- B1 (corrections share a property; H10, N07): **narrower**. The Pragmática moves the record into an office with
  duties to both parties and gives each party a copy on demand (leyes III, V). That fits "work moved to a party owed
  to the weaker side" and inverts the one-sided record. It does not check understanding; B1's "someone checked the
  person's understanding" does not hold for this precedent.
- A2 (record over voice; H07): **complicates**. The same law that makes writing outrank speech also gives the party
  a duty-bound reader and its own copy; the failure lies in record kept by one side, which this law forbids.
- Precedent for D04 (the person's own record): **holds** (leyes III, V).
