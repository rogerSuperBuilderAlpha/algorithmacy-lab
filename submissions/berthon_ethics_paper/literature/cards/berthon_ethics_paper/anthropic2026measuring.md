## Summary
Anthropic analyses Claude Code sessions and about one million public-API tool calls (late 2025 to early 2026) with
Claude-generated classifications. Full auto-approve rises from about 20% of sessions for new users to over 40% by 750
sessions, while per-turn interrupts rise from 5% to about 9%. On the API, human involvement falls from 87% of tool
calls on minimal-complexity tasks to 67% on high-complexity ones. The post recommends against mandating per-action
approval. Its classifications are the model's own, and Anthropic says it "cannot manually inspect the underlying
data". Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Newer users (<50 sessions) employ full auto-approve roughly 20% of the time; by 750 sessions, this increases to over
  40% of sessions" (section "Experienced users ... auto-approve more frequently").
- "New users (those with around 10 sessions) interrupt Claude in 5% of turns, while more experienced users interrupt in
  around 9% of turns" (same section).
- "87% of tool calls on minimal-complexity tasks ... have some form of human involvement, compared to only 67% of tool
  calls for high-complexity tasks"; "step-by-step approval becomes less practical as the number of steps grows" (same
  section; example parentheses omitted).
- "80% of tool calls come from agents that appear to have at least one kind of safeguard ... 73% appear to have a
  human in the loop in some way, and only 0.8% of actions appear to be irreversible" (section "Agents are used in
  risky domains"); footnote 14 calls 80% "an upper bound".
- "Oversight requirements that prescribe specific interaction patterns, such as requiring humans to approve every
  action, will create friction without necessarily producing safety benefits" (section "Looking ahead").
- "Our classifications are generated by Claude ... we cannot manually inspect the underlying data due to privacy
  constraints" (Limitations).
- Footnote 16: "In domains like law, medicine, or finance, verifying an agent’s output may require significant effort,
  which could slow the development of trust."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Anthropic-authored; corroborate. A vendor measuring its own product with its own model as classifier, and arguing a
  policy position (against mandated per-action approval) from the result.
- Auto-approve share is a configuration choice, not a measure of attention; the interrupt rise is read by the authors
  as "active monitoring" without a direct measure.

## Where the argument uses it
- M8 / A8: supports. Where the approval gate is offered, experienced users switch it off for a large share of
  sessions; per-action review thins as tasks lengthen. Partly corroborated outside Anthropic by yu2026habituation (dxd
  card; approval rose and scrutiny fell among 400 repeat reviewers of agent pull requests) and felt2012android (17% of
  users attended to permission screens; Felt concerns permission screens, not AI-action approvals).
- M2-adjacent (standards by industry): the post is a vendor arguing what oversight law should not require.
