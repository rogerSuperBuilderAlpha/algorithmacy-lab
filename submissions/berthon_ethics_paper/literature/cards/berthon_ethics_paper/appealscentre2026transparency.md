## Summary
The second annual report of the largest DSA Article 21 body, the closest working instance of "a dispute goes to a body
the operator does not control" for people acted on by a platform's automated decisions. In twelve months it received
over 24,000 disputes; where it could review the content it overturned the platform 59 percent of the time; but in the
larger share of eligible cases the platform never supplied the content, forcing "default decisions", and the Centre
"still" receives no consistent data on whether platforms implement its non-binding decisions. Page numbers are the
report's own (printed at the foot of each page; "29" confirmed). Existence verified by the Centre's site; no DOI.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 3: "While our decisions are non-binding, and it is up to platforms whether to implement them, they must engage in
  good faith. We are pushing platforms to consistently inform the user and us on whether they have implemented our
  decisions."
- p. 4: "we received more than 24,000 disputes from people and organisations across the EU."; "Where we were able to
  review the content in question, on average we disagreed with the platform’s decision 59% of the time."
- p. 5: "This is currently leading to a large number of default decisions, where a platform does not provide the
  content for an eligible case and we make a procedural decision in the user’s favour."
- p. 11: "In the vast majority of account suspension disputes, platforms failed to provide us with the content, causing
  considerable frustration among users."
- p. 11: "We are still not receiving consistent data about whether platforms have implemented our decisions."

## Critical notes from the literature
- The body is funded by a Meta Oversight Board grant and certified by Ireland's Coimisiún na Meán; its decisions bind
  no one (p. 3, p. 29), which is the ledger's own caveat on D04 ("DSA bodies cannot bind").
- Legal commentary ("The last duel?", Information & Communications Technology Law 2026, doi
  10.1080/13600834.2026.2728403) was not opened (T&F).

## Where the argument uses it
- **D04 (draft lines 524-526).** Feasibility: an independent reader for the person acted on exists at scale and
  overturns the operator in most reviewed cases. Failure: the operator controls the record (it withholds the content),
  and the remedy's effect is unknown because the operator reports nothing back. Verdict: D04 **holds, narrower**: a
  body the operator does not control is empty unless the person also holds the record (D04's first half) and the body's
  ruling binds or is reported. The two halves of D04 depend on each other, which the draft states but does not argue.
- **O02.** A present-tense instance of "the law already provides" that provides a reader without standing.
