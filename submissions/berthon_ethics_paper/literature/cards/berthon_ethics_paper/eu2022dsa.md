## Summary
Upgrade card limited to the two articles the earlier card did not read. Article 14 requires every intermediary service to publish, in its terms and conditions, "information on any policies, procedures, measures and tools used for the purpose of content moderation, including algorithmic decision-making and human review" (Art 14(1)). Article 17 requires hosting services to give "a clear and specific statement of reasons to any affected recipients" for removals, demotions, payment suspensions and account terminations (Art 17(1)), including "where applicable, information on the use made of automated means in taking the decision" (Art 17(3)(c)) and "clear and user-friendly information on the possibilities for redress available to the recipient of the service" (Art 17(3)(f)). Together with Arts 20-21 (internal complaints; certified out-of-court dispute settlement, on the superseded card), the DSA gives the platform user a declared envelope in the terms, a record with reasons, and a route to a body the platform does not control. The user of a platform is the second party of the platform's own moderation systems; this is the K02 one-party case, not the agent-for-an-organisation case, but the legal form is the one A5 says does not exist.

## Key facts it relies on
- Art 14(1): "Providers of intermediary services shall include information on any restrictions that they impose in relation to the use of their service in respect of information provided by the recipients of the service, in their terms and conditions. That information shall include information on any policies, procedures, measures and tools used for the purpose of content moderation, including algorithmic decision-making and human review, as well as the rules of procedure of their internal complaint handling system. It shall be set out in clear, plain, intelligible, user-friendly and unambiguous language, and shall be publicly available in an easily accessible and machine-readable format."
- Art 14(2): "Providers of intermediary services shall inform the recipients of the service of any significant change to the terms and conditions."
- Art 17(1): "Providers of hosting services shall provide a clear and specific statement of reasons to any affected recipients of the service for any of the following restrictions imposed on the ground that the information provided by the recipient of the service is illegal content or incompatible with their terms and conditions".
- Art 17(2): the duty applies "at the latest from the date that the restriction is imposed, regardless of why or how it was imposed."
- Art 17(3)(c): the statement shall contain "where applicable, information on the use made of automated means in taking the decision, including information on whether the decision was taken in respect of content detected or identified using automated means".
- Art 17(3)(f): "clear and user-friendly information on the possibilities for redress available to the recipient of the service in respect of the decision, in particular, where applicable through internal complaint-handling mechanisms, out-of-court dispute settlement and judicial redress."
- Art 17(4): the information "shall, in particular, be such as to reasonably allow the recipient of the service concerned to effectively exercise the possibilities for redress".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Ledger E06 (Kaminski and Urban; Lindblom, on the superseded cards): the DSA bodies cannot bind the platform (Art 21(2)), and first-instance review is the platform's own staff (Art 20(6)). The envelope is declared at the level of policy, not per case.
- Scope: content moderation by hosting services; the Regulation says nothing about agents acting for a deployer on third parties.

## Where the argument uses it
- A5 ("declares its envelope to the deploying organisation alone"; "No duty in the architecture makes the envelope known or binding to the person it constrains the agent toward", draft ll. 313-314): **holds for the agent architecture; narrower as a general claim about law.** EU law already requires one class of operator to declare its automated-decision tools and procedures to the people they act on (Art 14(1)) and to give each of them a reasoned record naming the automated means (Art 17(3)(c)).
- A8 and CB duties 2 and 3: present-day precedents for the declared envelope (Art 14) and the user's own record with a route to an outside body (Arts 17, 21), with the limit that the body cannot bind.
