## Summary
Microsoft's first product-side follow-through on Nadella's post, nine days after it: the executive vice president for
Security recasts deployed agents as "agentic insiders" and announces that Project Perception (July 2026) will supply
Guardian Agents, dynamic agent risk scores and containment for them. The post states that frontier models "regardless
of their origin, cannot be trusted", that "Model transparency will not replace containment", and that practitioners
provide judgment while agents provide speed. Its "we" is the enterprise and its security practitioners. The words
customer, user, applicant and affected do not occur. It names no availability date. Read in full from the local
capture; date confirmed on the page ("Oct 19, 2026").

## Key facts it relies on
- ¶3: "Unfortunately, frontier models, regardless of their origin, cannot be trusted. This is not because models are
  inherently malicious."
- ¶4-5: "These agents are frequently overprivileged, have access to data, tools and systems and can take actions and
  make decisions in critical processes. They have become active participants in the enterprise." "They are agentic
  insiders."
- ¶7: "To manage and control these insiders, we will need Guardian Agents — an established industry term — that watch
  and moderate every action of every agent, before it happens, 24/7."
- ¶14: "Agentic security provides speed. Practitioners provide judgment."
- ¶15: "Model transparency will not replace containment. It will make containment smarter and stronger. And it will
  allow us to better inform accountability and traceability."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Riley (`riley2026microsoft`) reports that the controls can "require verified human approval" and that "People keep
  control of policy and approval rules"; the post itself names practitioners, not any person acted on.
- The post treats the model as an actor to be contained, the frame Farrell et al. (`farrell2025large`) reject.
