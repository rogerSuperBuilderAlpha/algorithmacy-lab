## Summary
Empirical study of over 700,000 restaurant inspections in ten jurisdictions, concentrating on San Diego and New York.
Ho finds that the "targeted transparency" device the draft treats as a working precedent for D07 was, in practice,
inflated (San Diego: 99.9 percent of restaurants hold an 'A'), inconsistent (New York), without a detectable health
effect in New York's first year, and a drain on inspection resources diverted to grade disputes. Printed page numbers
are confirmed by the page footers in the local PDF (e.g. "611" and "the yale law journal 122:574 2012" at the Part
III.C.1 passage; "645" at the Part IV.B passage).

## Key facts it relies on
- Abstract (p. 574): "Despite grading’s great promise, we show that the regulatory design, implementation, and
  practice suffer from serious flaws: jurisdictions fudge more than nudge. In San Diego, grade inflation reigns.
  Nearly all restaurants receive ‘A’s."
- Abstract (p. 574): "Unsurprisingly, New York’s implementation of letter grading in 2010 has not discernably reduced
  manifestations of foodborne illness. Perhaps worse, the system perversely shifts inspection resources away from
  higher health hazards to resolve grade disputes."
- Part III.C.1 (p. 611): "Out of 8,941 graded restaurants, only eight received a ‘B,’ and none received a ‘C.’"; "Because
  99.9% of restaurants receive an ‘A,’ there is little variation from a consumer’s perspective in the sanitation level
  of restaurants."
- Part III.C.1 (p. 612): "county law permits restaurants to pay a fee ($142 per hour) to be reinspected, and possibly
  regraded, within thirty days."
- Part IV.B (p. 645): "In short, we find no evidence based on these indicators of positive health effects. Over the
  long run, such benefits may still materialize, but the evidence does not corroborate DOHMH’s own claims of the
  program’s benefits in the first year".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Ho's targets are San Diego and New York; Los Angeles is treated through the literature (he cites Simon et al. 2005 at
  n. 141 and the 20 percent claim at p. 587), and the LA replication is `ho2019new`.
- The paper is the standard counter to Sunstein-style "targeted disclosure"; it does not deny that a letter grade can
  carry an inspector's reading, only that the institution that issues the grade inflates and games it.

## Where the argument uses it
- **D07 (draft lines 530-532; CB).** The grade card is the draft's precedent for a safeguard at the point of use that
  "cost less to engage than the task". Ho shows the device fails where the grader is captured by the graded: the
  inspector's reading reaches the door, but the inspector's grade no longer discriminates (p. 611). Verdict for D07:
  **narrower**. The precedent holds only with a condition the draft does not state: the party that issues the marker
  must not be the party the marker constrains, and must not be able to redeclare it on request (the $142 regrade, p.
  612). That is the same condition the ledger puts on D03 (P2B's 15 days).
- **D03.** The regrade fee is a redeclarable envelope in miniature.
