## Summary
The method source behind Seele and Schultz. Analogies in organisation theory can be explanatory, not only
expressive, and must then be evaluated as reasoning. Placing analogies in Toulmin's model (grounds, warrants,
backings), the authors cross Gentner's three dimensions (relevance, structural soundness, factual validity) with
four uses to give twelve evaluation questions (Table 2), and illustrate with transaction cost economics. The copy is
the publisher's PDF in Erasmus University's repository (Dutch Copyright Act art. 25fa). Printed page = PDF page
plus 635.

## Key facts it relies on
- "The utility of the analogy then depends on whether 'the system of relations that holds in the [source] domain
  also holds in the target domain' (Gentner, 1999: 108)." (p. 638)
- "But validity cannot be treated as an auxiliary background assumption; it is an explicit knowledge claim that
  requires critical evaluation." (p. 640)
- "In a structurally sound analogy, the source domain consistently maps onto the target domain with links that are
  regarded as strong enough to suggest that similarity is not merely superficial or coincidental" (p. 647).
- Hasty generalisation: "upon identifying a few enticing similarities, we gloss over potentially relevant
  dissimilarities" (p. 647).
- "Structural soundness requires that a sufficient number of similarities be identified and no critical
  dissimilarities found." (p. 650)

## Critical notes from the literature
- Scope is explanatory theory (prediction, interpretation, explanation in organisation studies). The paper does not
  discuss normative or moral transfer.
- Counting similarities ("a sufficient number") is the criterion Bartha (SEP, §3.1, §3.5.2) calls an unreliable
  guide to plausibility; see `bartha2019analogy`.

## Where the argument uses it
- A6: **supports the procedural half**: name the mapped relations, then test for "critical dissimilarities". The
  paper's rule "a pairing that rests on resemblance alone is cut" matches the hasty-generalisation warning (p. 647).
- A6 as warrant for carrying a wrong from past to present: **does not reach**. Pair with Bartha's prior-association
  test.
