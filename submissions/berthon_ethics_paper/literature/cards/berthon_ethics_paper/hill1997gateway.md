## Summary
This upgrade closes the two gaps that kept the earlier card writer-grounded: pin cites and the certiorari denial.
I read the Caselaw Access Project text of 105 F.3d 1147, which carries star pages (*1148-*1151), and CAP's entry for
522 U.S. 808 ("No. 96-1760. Hill et ux. v. Gateway 2000, Inc., et al. C. A. 7th Cir. Certiorari denied.", decided
6 Oct. 1997; saved as `hill1997/us522_808.html`, sha256 `236b6148…`). The holding is unchanged from the earlier card:
terms shipped in the box bind a buyer who keeps the computer past the 30-day return period, the arbitration clause
need not be prominent, and the court rejects any duty to read terms aloud before sale. The pin cites below are new.

## Key facts it relies on
- "A contract need not be read to be effective; people who accept take the risk that the unread terms may in
  retrospect prove unwelcome" (105 F.3d at 1148).
- "Terms inside Gateway’s box stand or fall together" (at 1148).
- Quoting ProCD: "A vendor, as master of the offer, may invite acceptance by conduct" (at 1149).
- "Cashiers cannot be expected to read legal documents to customers before ringing up sales" (at 1149). Reading the
  four-page terms by phone "would anesthetize rather than enlighten many potential buyers" (at 1149).
- On the voice against the record: "oral recitation would not avoid customers’ assertions (whether true or feigned)
  that the clerk did not read term X to them, or that they did not remember or understand it. Writing provides
  benefits for both sides of commercial transactions" (at 1149).
- "Customers as a group are better off when vendors skip costly and ineffectual steps such as telephonic
  recitation, and use instead a simple approve-or-return device. Competent adults are bound by such documents, read
  or unread" (at 1149). The CAP text reads "approve-or-retum", an OCR error for "return"; the earlier card's
  law.resource.org copy reads "return".
- The open question it did not decide (return deterred by shipping cost) is "an interesting question, but one that
  need not detain us" (at 1150).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Specht v. Netscape (2d Cir. 2002; `berthon_ethics_course/specht2002netscape.md`) requires reasonable notice;
  Hill is one circuit's rule.
- The opinion treats reading aloud as both costly and useless; it offers no evidence on either point.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (failure: the burden of reading on the person bound; H19): **holds**, now with pin cites (1148, 1149).
- A2 (record over voice; H07): **holds**. The court prefers writing because oral recitation leaves disputes about
  what was said and understood (at 1149): the writing settles the record in the drafter's favour and the buyer's
  understanding drops out.
- A3 (standard broken): **does not hold** on this source alone; the opinion states the rule, not an objection to it.
