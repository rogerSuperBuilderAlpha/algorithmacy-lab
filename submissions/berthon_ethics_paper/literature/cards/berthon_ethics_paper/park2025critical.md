## Summary
An 800-participant study of error detection in chain-of-thought explanations from vision-language models on moral
scenarios. The authors insert three error types into reasoning chains (omissions, contradictions, hallucinations) and
vary the tone. Agreement and trust track agreement with the verdict, not the soundness of the reasoning; a confident
tone lowers detection; omissions are caught least often (about 40% of the time, p. 2) and are also the most frequent
error type found in real model reasoning.

## Key facts it relies on
- "we perturb reasoning chains generated by vision-language models (VLMs) with three error types (omissions,
  contradictions, hallucinations) and measure their effects on user trust and error detection through a study with
  800 participants" (abstract, p. 1).
- Findings: "users often equate trust with outcome agreement regardless of flaws in the model’s reasoning" and
  "confident tones suppress scrutiny while leaving agreement and self-reported trust largely unchanged" (abstract,
  p. 1).
- "agreement and trust are strongly correlated (r=+0.82), and both are inversely related to detection (agreement:
  r=−0.29; trust: r=−0.45)" (p. 5).
- Users catch contradictions and hallucinations "at moderate-to-high rates", omissions "only 40% of the time" (p. 2;
  the extracted text breaks the small-caps word "OMISSION").
