## Summary
Frahm, Doezema and Pfotenhauer study how two international organisations, the OECD (its Working Party on
Biotechnology, Nanotechnology and Converging Technologies, and the 2019 Council Recommendation on Responsible
Innovation in Neurotechnology) and the European Commission (the Horizon 2020 "RRI Practice" project), took up
"Responsible Innovation". Their method is two qualitative case studies built from policy documents, about fifty
interviews (2016-2019), two focus groups and the authors' own participant observation; they say plainly that they
helped write some of the documents they analyse (pp. 181-183). The argument: both organisations built a new
"deficit" (a "democratic deficit of innovation") in which a public's lack of engagement is cast as the barrier to
the uptake of technology, and that diagnosis licensed them to offer their own frameworks, principles and "keys" as
the fix and to claim authority over how nations should do democracy around technology. The paper extends Wynne's
Deficit Model of public understanding and Pfotenhauer, Juhl and Aarden's deficit model of innovation (pp. 176,
179). Page numbers are the printed ones in the SAGE PDF (the PDF carries the 2022 volume pagination).

## Key facts it relies on
- Thesis (abstract, p. 175): the turn to societal participation "relies on a new deficit logic—a democratic
  deficit of innovation—that frames a lack of societal engagement in innovation governance as a major barrier to
  the uptake and dissemination of new technologies". "These deficit politics enable global governance
  institutions to present “Responsible Innovation” frameworks as the solution" (p. 175).
- The older deficit model of innovation (citing Pfotenhauer, Juhl and Aarden 2019) "presents societies and
  institutions as lacking innovativeness and novel products such that interventions framed as innovation can be
  offered as the solution" (p. 176).
- Wynne's Deficit Model of public understanding, as the authors gloss it: "the construction of publics as lacking
  the necessary knowledge to accept or meaningfully engage with scientific and technological development"
  (p. 179).
- The EC's Rome Declaration valued RRI for its use in "reassure[ing] society about embracing innovative products
  and services" (p. 195, the authors' quotation of the Italian Presidency text).
- The RRI Practice project redrew the boundary of socially aligned innovation "by positing citizens as
  insufficiently equipped to appreciate the benefits of a wide range of new knowledge and technologies" (p. 200).
- "Deficit construction around the inclusion of society in technological governance goes hand in hand with the
  provision of Responsible Innovation as a solution" (p. 201).
- The mainstreaming of Responsible Innovation "locates both the power to correctly diagnose problems and enact
  solutions within international organizations" (p. 203).
- Method and position: the authors were participant observers and consultants in both projects and co-wrote
  deliverables (pp. 182-183).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The cases are innovation policy (neurotechnology, RRI), not literacy. The paper never mentions literacy
  campaigns; the bridge to Jomtien and Dakar is the facet's, not the authors'.
- The authors call their design "quasi-comparative" and "not designed as a rigorous comparative case study"
  (p. 182); the evidence is mostly the organisations' own documents read as discourse.
- Self-implication cuts both ways: the authors helped write the texts they critique (pp. 182-183), which gives
  access and a stake.
- Crossref dates the version of record 15 March 2021 (online); the issue is 2022. The lead's brief cites 2022.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (failure: competence withheld, then oversold; H13): **narrower**. It supplies the mechanism the literacy record
  lacks a name for: an institution defines what the public lacks so that its own framework becomes the fix and
  its authority grows. The paper must make the transfer to Jomtien's "basic learning needs" itself; Frahm et al.
  do not.
- A3 (standard: promise unmet): **does not hold** on its own; the paper describes legitimation, not an unmet
  promise.
- A4 (present case, M11 skilling): **holds as analogy** for reading AI-literacy and skilling claims as deficit
  construction, if the paper shows the vendor names the user's deficit and sells the fix.
