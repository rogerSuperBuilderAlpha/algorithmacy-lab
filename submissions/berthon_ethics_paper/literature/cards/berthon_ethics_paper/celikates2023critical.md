## Summary
The entry distinguishes three models of critique. Internal critique "refers to standards and standpoints that are
already recognized by those criticized"; external critique refers to standards "not (or not yet) recognized" and
derived independently; immanent critique, the Frankfurt model, draws its norms from "the (often implicit) normative
structures ... of the practices and self-understandings that are constitutive of the (type of) society in question"
(§2.1). Jaeggi's reconstructive critique of forms of life is "neither a mere description of what exists nor a
normative demand imposed on what exists from the outside" (§2.3). Genealogical critics object that immanent
critique idealises institutional "achievements" (§2.5). The bib key exists in `literature/references.bib`; this is
the first card. Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Immanent critique avoids the dichotomy between an internal critique that refers to standards and standpoints
  that are already recognized by those criticized and an external critique that refers to standards and standpoints
  that are not (or not yet) recognized" (§2.1, citing Jaeggi 2005, 2014; Celikates 2009; Stahl 2013a).
- Ideology critique "starts from the contradictions of a social and ideological constellation and the experience of
  those affected" (§2.1).
- Jaeggi: bracketing the evaluation of forms of life hinders "experimental learning processes" (§2.3, citing Jaeggi
  2014 [2018, 9-24, 318-319]).
- Genealogical challenge: commitment to liberal institutions "relies on an idealizing view of the history and present
  of this political formation" (§2.5).

## Where the argument uses it
