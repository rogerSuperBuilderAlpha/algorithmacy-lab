## Summary
The earlier card rested on NC ANCHOR's transcription and flagged that ANCHOR and the 1837 Revised Statutes disagree
on the penalty for a free person of colour and on books. I followed ANCHOR's credit link to North Carolina Digital
Collections (State Archives), which serves the printed 1830-31 session laws as page images
(`pubs_edp_lawsofnorthcarolina183031`) with an OCR transcript embedded in the page (`nc1831/ncdcr.html`, sha256
`55c757b9cad4…`). I read the act on the image. The printed act is chapter VI. It has a preamble and three
sections. **The printed text agrees with the 1837 Revised Statutes, not with ANCHOR**: s. I also punishes anyone who
"shall give or sell to such slave or slaves any books or pamphlets", and a free person of colour "shall be fined,
imprisoned, or whipped, at the discretion of the court". ANCHOR's transcription omits the books clause and gives
whipping alone. Section II punishes a slave who teaches another slave with lashes on conviction before a justice of
the peace; s. III orders the act given in charge to grand juries. The page number at the head of the scan is too
blurred to read even at 3,000 px; the chapter number is clear.

## Key facts it relies on
- Title: "An act to prevent all persons from teaching slaves to read or write, the use of figures excepted."
- Preamble: "Whereas the teaching of slaves to read and write, has a tendency to excite dissatisfaction in their
  minds and to produce insurrection and rebellion, to the manifest injury of the citizens of this State".
- s. I: "any free person, who shall hereafter teach, or attempt to teach, any slave within this State to read or
  write, the use of figures excepted, or shall give or sell to such slave or slaves any books or pamphlets, shall be
  liable to indictment"; a white offender is to "be fined not less than one hundred dollars, nor more than two
  hundred dollars, or imprisoned"; a free person of colour "shall be fined, imprisoned, or whipped, at the
  discretion of the court", within a range of lashes the printed text sets at twenty to thirty-nine.
- s. II: a slave who teaches "any other slave to read or write, the use of figures excepted" may be carried before a
  justice of the peace and on conviction sentenced to lashes on the bare back.
- s. III: "the judges of the Superior Courts and the justices of the County Courts shall give this act in charge to
  the grand jurors of their respective counties".

## Critical notes from the literature
- This corrects the earlier card's basis: ANCHOR's transcription is loose on two material points (books; the free
  person of colour's penalty). The 1837 codification did not change the law on those points.
- The "figures excepted" clause and the slave-only scope of the pupils still make the act narrower than a blanket
  ban (facet C; ledger H04).
- Chapter VII, on the same page, licenses free people of colour to peddle only within their county.

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (failure: competence withheld; H04): **holds**, now on the printed text, with the books-and-pamphlets clause
  adding a ban on supplying reading matter.
- A3 (standard): the preamble states the legislature's own ground (insurrection), which is the record's clearest
  statement that reading was withheld because it would serve the people denied it.
