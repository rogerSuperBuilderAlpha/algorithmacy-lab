## Summary
Lovett's survey of civic republicanism: freedom as non-domination, a structural relation, not a pattern of outcomes
(§1.2); arbitrariness as uncontrolled power on a procedural view (resilient common-knowledge rules) or a democratic
view (control by those subject) (§2.2); the instrumental "neo-Roman" reading of the tradition (§3.1); and the
contemporary programme, which keeps the tradition's core value while dropping its period prejudices (§4). Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- Structural freedom: liberty is "a sort of structural relationship that exists between persons or groups, rather
  than ... a contingent pattern of outcomes" (§1.2).
- Pettit's definition, as Lovett cites it: no other person or group has "the capacity to interfere in their affairs
  on an arbitrary basis" (1999, 165; cf. 1997) (§1.2).
- Benevolent master: the aim is "not to make our master a better person ... but to render him less of a master"
  (§2.1, Lovett and Ingham 2022).
- Procedural view: constraints "must be resilient over a wide range of possible changes or modifications in the
  relevant circumstances" (§2.2). "Just because one is better able to cope with arbitrary power, it does not follow
  that one's domination is any less." (§2.2)
- Carrying a past tradition forward (§4): classical republicans' elitism "might merely reflect the accidental
  prejudices of their day, in which case it can easily be dispensed with"; contemporary republicans "draw inspiration
  from the classical tradition, but they do not aim to anachronistically implement the republicanism of yore for its
  own sake."
- New dependency through reform: "It is always important from a civic republican point of view to be on guard
  against the introduction of new forms of dependency and arbitrary power through those very laws and policies
  designed to enhance individual freedom" (§4.1).
- Contestation (Pettit 1997, 186-7): reasons open to public debate; inclusiveness; impartial "courts of appeal"
  (§4.2).

## Where the argument uses it
- A7, refutation sought: **supports a bridge the internal benchmarks alone do not give**. Republicans apply a value
  first articulated in Roman law and the seventeenth century to present actors by separating the core value from the
  era's prejudices (§4). The paper can do the same: the literacy-era verdicts bind because they turned on a position
  of uncontrolled power, which non-domination condemns now, not because a Tudor statute's norms bind Microsoft.
- A5 and CB: the benevolent-master passage (§2.1) and the resilience condition (§2.2) state why a well-meant
  "trust architecture" declared and revisable by the deployer still leaves the deployer a master. §4.1's warning
  about new dependency introduced by protective policy fits P11-P12.
