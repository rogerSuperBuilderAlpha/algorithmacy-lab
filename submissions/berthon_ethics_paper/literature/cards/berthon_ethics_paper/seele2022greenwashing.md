## Summary
The authors (USI Lugano) transfer the greenwashing literature (source domain) to "machinewashing" (target) by
structural mapping across five dimensions (antecedents, underlying goals, practice, examples, outcomes), state
where machinewashing departs (seven "idiosyncrasies"), define it, and derive a research programme by theory level.
Open access (CC BY). Printed page = PDF page plus 1062.

## Key facts it relies on
- Method statement: "An analogy typically links a source and a target domain through structural mapping, thereby
  utilizing the knowledge of the source to draw implications in the target domain" (p. 1065, citing Ketokivi et al.
  2017).
- Breaks: they "use the problematization approach as an important means to stress the boundaries of the analogy"
  (p. 1065, Alvesson and Sandberg 2011), and ask "where the analogy does not fit" (p. 1064).
- Where it breaks (pp. 1067-1070): disruptive (not gradual) emergence; broad scope and scalability; lack of
  watchdogs; low tangibility; opacity and complexity; fluid algorithms that "change shape via software updates";
  automated decisions obscuring responsibility. "Machinewashing should not be considered a mere extension of
  greenwashing." (p. 1067)
- Mapping order: "By building on five major dimensions, we stress the analogy and indicate where new ways of
  thinking are required" (p. 1070).
- Validation claimed through links to existing theories and research questions: "we provide evidence of the
  structural soundness and factual validity of the analogy" (p. 1078).
- Limitations: "researchers and theorists should not simply accept the analogy for its own sake"; the boundaries
  must be mapped further (p. 1084).
- Table 4 lists "Misleading and lengthy data and privacy policies, terms of service, and informed consent" as
  machinewashing by jargon (p. 1073), and "Obscuring responsibility for unintended outcomes of semi-automated systems
  to human in the loop" (pp. 1074-1075).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The analogy's purpose is descriptive and explanatory (concept definition, a research programme), not normative.
  Its criteria of "structural soundness and factual validity" come from Ketokivi et al. (2017), who write about
  explanatory theory in organisation studies (card `ketokivi2017reasoning`). Neither source addresses whether a
  structural mapping licenses carrying a *wrong* from source to target.
- Typos in the list of assumptions (two items numbered "(3)", no "(5)", p. 1067).

## Where the argument uses it
- A6 ("map the source onto the target, then state where the mapping breaks"): **holds as method**. Seele and
  Schultz do both, in that order, and BEQ's sister journal published it.
- A6 as a warrant for *normative* transfer: **narrower**. The source is a knowledge-transfer analogy. For the
  paper's claim that a pairing carries the historical verdict, the stronger warrant is Bartha's articulation model
  (`bartha2019analogy`): the shared property must be the one the source verdict turned on, with no critical
  disanalogy. Cite Seele and Schultz for the procedure, not for the inference.
- The "fluid algorithms" break (p. 1070) is a ready-made candidate for A8's "something has changed": the deployer can
  change the envelope by update.
