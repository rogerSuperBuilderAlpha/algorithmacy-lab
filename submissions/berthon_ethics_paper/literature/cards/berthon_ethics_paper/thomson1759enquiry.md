## Summary
The anonymous *Enquiry* of 1759 is the Friendly Association's account of the Delaware grievances, written by Charles
Thomson, who was the clerk Teedyuscung chose at Easton in July 1757 (the Pennsylvania minutes name him,
`pennsylvania1851minutes` p. 665). Thomson gives the Delaware side's reason for the demand: before the public business
began, Teedyuscung asked leave "of appointing a Person to take down the Minutes of the Treaty for him with the
Secretary appointed by the Governor", because "He had seen the Secretary of the Province, at the last Easton Treaty,
throw down his Pen, and declare he would not take Minutes when Complaints were made against the Proprietors" (p. 110).
The Governor at first refused, citing an agreement with Croghan at Lancaster that only Croghan's secretary should take
minutes (p. 111); Teedyuscung "looking upon this as a Denial, was much dissatisfied", took it as "a Design to lead him
on blindfold, and in the Dark, or to take Advantage of his Ignorance", and resolved "not to treat, but to break up and
go home" if refused (pp. 111-112). The Governor yielded "against his Judgment" (p. 112). Thomson records that the
person nominated "was one Charles Thomson, who had, at the particular Request of Mr. Peters, taken Minutes at the last
Easton Treaty" (p. 112), that is, himself. Page numbers are from the printed folios in the OCR ("( 110 )", "( 111 )",
"( 112 )", the last two damaged); the long-s OCR has been normalised in the quotations below.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 110: "Before the public Business begun, Teedyuscung applied to the Governor to allow him the Liberty of
  appointing a Person to take down the Minutes of the Treaty for him with the Secretary appointed by the Governor."
- p. 110: "He had seen the Secretary of the Province, at the last Easton Treaty, throw down his Pen, and declare he
  would not take Minutes when Complaints were made against the Proprietors. He did not know but the same Thing might
  happen again, as the same Complaints would be repeated."
- p. 111: the Governor said that "by a particular Agreement between him and Mr. Croghan at the last Treaty at
  Lancaster, no one was to take Minutes of the Proceedings but the Secretary appointed by Mr. Croghan".
- pp. 111-112: the refusal "awakened his Suspicion, and induced him to believe that there was a Design to lead him on
  blindfold, and in the Dark, or to take Advantage of his Ignorance"; he now saw the demand "not only as reasonable but
  absolutely necessary to come at the Truth".
- p. 112: the Governor: "as no Indian Chief before him ever demanded to have a Clerk, and none had ever been appointed
  for Indians in former Treaties ... he could not help declaring it against his Judgment. However, to give you a fresh
  Proof of my Friendship and Regard, if you insist upon having a Clerk, I shall no longer oppose it."
- p. 112: "Four Days being spent in this Debate, the public Treaty began next Day, Teedyuscung having first nominated
  a Person to take Minutes of the Proceedings for him. The Person he nominated was one Charles Thomson, who had, at
  the particular Request of Mr. Peters, taken Minutes at the last Easton Treaty".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Thomson writes anonymously about himself and for the Quaker Friendly Association, which the provincial officers
  accused of prompting the demand (`pennsylvania1851minutes` pp. 657-658, Croghan and Weiser). The two accounts agree
  on the demand, the refusal, the "blindfold" phrase and the yielding; they differ on who stood behind it.
- Merrell, "'I desire all that I have said ... may be taken down aright': Revisiting Teedyuscung's 1756 Treaty Council
  Speeches", *William and Mary Quarterly* 63(4) (2006): 777-826 (`merrell2006desire`, record only; JSTOR, not read),
  compares the competing minutes of the 1756 council and is the modern study of how these records were made.

## Where the argument uses it
- B1 and D04 (the bound party's own record; draft ll. 270-274, 349-350, 526): **holds**, and adds the motive the
  minutes omit: the official clerk had refused to record complaints against the Proprietors in 1756.
- A3 (contemporaries objected to a record kept by one side): **holds**; the objection is stated here in the words of
  the objector's own clerk, and in the Governor's minutes.
- Draft l. 272 "demanded by the party himself": **contested**. Teedyuscung made the demand; his clerk was a Quaker
  schoolmaster who had minuted the previous treaty for the Province's secretary, and the officers thought Quakers had
  prompted it. The paper should not present the clerk as independent of every interested party.
