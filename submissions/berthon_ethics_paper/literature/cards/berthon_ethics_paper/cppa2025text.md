## Summary
The California Privacy Protection Agency's approved regulation text (the file the Agency's rulemaking page links as the approved text; fetched 2026-10-24) adds Article 11, "Automated Decisionmaking Technology", to the CCPA regulations. It applies to a business that "uses ADMT to make a significant decision concerning a consumer" (§ 7200(a), p. 111), where a significant decision is one that "results in the provision or denial of financial or lending services, housing, education enrollment or opportunities, employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation, or healthcare services" (§ 7001(ddd), p. 9). Three consumer-facing duties follow: a Pre-use Notice (§ 7220), a right to opt out of ADMT (§ 7221) unless the business offers an appeal to a human reviewer "who has the authority to overturn the decision" (§ 7221(b)(1)), and a right to access plain-language information about the ADMT's purpose, logic and outcome (§ 7222). Businesses already using ADMT must comply "no later than January 1, 2027" (§ 7200(b)). I did not open the Office of Administrative Law approval record; law-firm summaries (not opened as sources) date OAL approval to September 2025 and effect to 1 January 2026.

## Key facts it relies on
- § 7001(ddd), p. 9: "“Significant decision” means a decision that results in the provision or denial of financial or lending services, housing, education enrollment or opportunities, employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation, or healthcare services." § 7001(ddd)(6), p. 10: "Significant decision does not include advertising to a consumer."
- § 7200(b), p. 111: "A business that uses ADMT for a significant decision prior to January 1, 2027, must be in compliance with the requirements of this Article no later than January 1, 2027."
- § 7220(a), p. 111: "The Pre-use Notice must inform consumers about the business’s use of ADMT and consumers’ rights to opt-out of ADMT and to access ADMT". § 7220(b)(2): the notice must be "presented prominently and conspicuously to the consumer at or before the point when the business collects the consumer’s personal information that the business plans to process using ADMT."
- § 7221(a), p. 114: "A business must provide consumers with the ability to opt-out of the use of ADMT to make a significant decision concerning the consumer, except as set forth in subsection (b)."
- § 7221(b)(1), p. 115: no opt-out is required where "The business provides the consumer with a method to appeal the decision to a human reviewer who has the authority to overturn the decision." The reviewer "must know how to interpret and use the output of the ADMT that made the significant decision being appealed and must have the authority to change the decision based on their analysis" (§ 7221(b)(1)(A)).
- § 7221(b)(2)-(3), pp. 115-116: further exceptions for admission, hiring and work-allocation decisions where the ADMT "works for the business’s purpose and does not unlawfully discriminate based upon protected characteristics."
- § 7221(c)(1), p. 116: the online opt-out link "must state what the consumer is opting out of, such as “Opt-out of Automated Decisionmaking Technology.”"
- § 7222(b)(2), p. 119: on an access request the business must give "Information about the logic of the ADMT. Such information must enable a consumer to understand how the ADMT processed their personal information to generate an output with respect to them, which may include the parameters that generated the output as well as the specific output with respect to the consumer." § 7222(b)(3): "The outcome of the decisionmaking process for the consumer, including how the business used the output of the ADMT to make a significant decision with respect to the consumer."

## Critical notes from the literature
- None read this round. Law-firm summaries surfaced by web search (Foley; Willkie; Lowenstein; not opened as sources) report that the final text relaxed earlier drafts' opt-out rights. The text itself ties the opt-out to the absence of a human appeal.
- The regime is a privacy regulation (CCPA, Civ. Code § 1798.185); enforcement runs through the Agency and the Attorney General, with the CCPA's limited private action for data breaches only (not read here).

## Where the argument uses it
- A8 ("the law provides remedies after the harm, not voice or brake before it"): **does not hold as stated** for California from 1 January 2027 in the enumerated domains. The regulation gives the second party notice before the decision (§ 7220), a choice between opting out and a human appeal with authority to overturn (§ 7221), and a record of the logic and outcome (§ 7222).
- A4/A5 (the envelope and record declared to the organisation alone): **narrower**. The duty runs to the person acted on, by law, for significant decisions; it does not reach a refund, a service queue or a counterparty negotiation (§ 7001(ddd)).
- CB duties 1 (structural refusal), 3 (counter-delegation) and 4 (check understanding): the opt-out/appeal pair is a present-day precedent for duty 1 and a partial one for duty 3 (the reviewer is the business's own designate, not an independent body). The reviewer competence clause (§ 7221(b)(1)(A)) is the nearest legal analogue to Microsoft's A5.4.
