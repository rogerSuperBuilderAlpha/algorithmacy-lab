## Summary
Abstract card: paraphrase only. Cambridge's description presents the book as a revisionist history of Elizabethan
press censorship. The state wished to control the press but faced the competing interests of Crown, Church and the
printing trade; from the censored books themselves Clegg concludes that press control was not a routine or consistent
mechanism but a response to particular texts the state judged dangerous. Part I ("The Practice of Censorship") has
chapters on privilege, licence and authority; Elizabethan press controls; and censorship proclamations. Part II treats
censored texts (Catholic propagandists, Gascoigne, Stubbs, Holinshed, Marprelate, the 1599 bishops' ban). Crossref
resolves the DOI (monograph, 1997-08-07). OpenAlex gives no open copy.

## Key facts it relies on
- (Description, paraphrase) Press control in Elizabeth's reign was not a routine or consistent mechanism but an
  individual response to particular texts.
- (Chapter list) The Star Chamber decree of 1586 falls within the "Elizabethan press controls" chapter; the book is
  the standard account of how far those controls operated in practice.
