## Summary
Trade-press report on Gallot's post and the controls behind it, the only coverage found that ties the 10 October
essay to a dated Microsoft product move. It describes Dynamic Agent Risk (a continuously updated score per agent),
Dynamic Conditional Access (as the score rises the agent loses write access, then needs "verified human approval",
then is isolated or stopped), Dynamic Permissions (temporary, task-scoped, capped at the authority of "the person it
works for"), detection of 60 agent types including Claude Code and Codex, and chain-of-thought signals feeding the risk
score. No availability date or price; details promised at Ignite on 17 November. No one quoted criticises the essay;
"customers" appears only as Microsoft's audience. Read in full from the local capture.

## Key facts it relies on
- ¶2: "Microsoft Chairman and Chief Executive Satya Nadella laid out the thinking behind the controls in an Oct. 10
  essay that urged companies to treat models as insider risks, with safeguards kept outside the model itself."
- ¶8: "In her post, Gallot also wrote that future controls should explain why an agent acts, and getting there means
  seeing a model’s reasoning tokens. “Model transparency will not replace containment,” she added."
- ¶9: "Microsoft did not give availability dates or pricing for the new controls, which it is positioning as part of
  Project Perception and Microsoft 365 E7."
