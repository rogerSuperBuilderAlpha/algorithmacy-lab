## Summary
The US federal risk-management framework for AI, issued under the National AI Initiative Act of 2020. It is voluntary by its own terms, and so is its Playbook. It addresses "organizations designing, developing, deploying, or using AI systems" (p. 2). Affected communities appear as people to consult and as sources of reports to integrate, not as holders of rights. Crossref lists Elham Tabassi as author; the PDF credits NIST. Printed page numbers are five lower than PDF page numbers in this copy.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 2: "the goal of the AI RMF is to offer a resource to the organizations designing, developing, deploying, or using AI systems to help manage the many risks of AI"; "The Framework is intended to be voluntary, rights-preserving, non-sector-specific, and use-case agnostic".
- p. 21: "Like the AI RMF, the Playbook is voluntary and organizations can utilize the suggestions according to their needs and interests."
- p. 42 (Appendix D): the attributes include "Be risk-based, resource-efficient, pro-innovation, and voluntary."
- pp. 29-33 (search hits): the subcategories speak of "reports of errors and potential impacts on affected communities" and of affected communities being "consulted".

## Critical notes from the literature
- NIST's generative-AI profile (`nist2024artificial`, p. 1) repeats that the AI RMF "is intended for voluntary use".

## Where the argument uses it
- A4, M2 (P6): holds. A government-published standard that binds no one unless a contract, procurement rule or statute adopts it. California SB 53 is one such route: it makes a developer's own framework, which may incorporate "national standards", enforceable by the Attorney General (`california2025transparency`).
