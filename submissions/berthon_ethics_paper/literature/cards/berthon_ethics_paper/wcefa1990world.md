## Summary
I reread the ERIC microfiche OCR already held by the arm, this time the whole Declaration and most of the Framework,
for three things the earlier partial card did not carry: how the texts define what people lack, whom they make
responsible, and how firm the literacy target was. The Declaration defines "basic learning needs" (Art. 1.1), says
that meeting them "confers upon them a responsibility" (Art. 1.2), declares basic education for all "for the first
time in history — an attainable goal" (Preamble), and asks that education be judged by "actual learning acquisition
and outcome" (Art. 4). The Framework lets countries set their own targets and offers halving adult illiteracy as a
dimension they "may wish" to adopt, "say, one-half" (para. 8). Paragraph 31 treats learners as "a vital human
resource that needs to be mobilized" and names the costs they bear.

## Key facts it relies on
- Preamble: "More than 960 million adults, two-thirds of whom are women, are illiterate". The lack of basic education
  "prevents societies from addressing such problems with strength and purpose".
- Preamble, the promise: new forces "make the goal of basic education for all — for the first time in history — an
  attainable goal".
- Art. 1.1: basic learning needs comprise "essential learning tools (such as literacy, oral expression, numeracy, and
  problem solving)" and content needed "to make informed decisions, and to continue learning".
- Art. 1.2: "The satisfaction of these needs empowers individuals in any society and confers upon them a
  responsibility" to respect their heritage, promote others' education, further social justice, and more.
- Art. 4: the focus "must, therefore, be on actual learning acquisition and outcome, rather than exclusively upon
  enrolment".
- Closing: "The basic learning needs of all can and must be met."
- Framework para. 8: "Countries may wish to set their own targets for the 1990s in terms of the following proposed
  dimensions", including "Reduction of the adult illiteracy rate ... to, say, one-half its 1990 level by the year
  2000". (The OCR reads "tars"; the word is "targets".)
- Framework para. 9: setting performance levels "must be an autonomous task of individual countries".
- Framework para. 31: "The individual learners themselves constitute a vital human resource that needs to be
  mobilized"; "Potential learners need to see that the benefits of basic education activities exceed the costs the
  participants must bear, such as earnings foregone".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The literacy target is a proposed dimension for national plans, not a pledge with an owner (para. 8-9). "The
  promise went unmet" (A3) has to be stated against the Declaration's "can and must be met" and Dakar's later
  commitment, not against a binding Jomtien target.
- The OCR is a 1990 ERIC microfiche of the April 1990 printing; the official UNESDOC copy was not reachable
  (bot challenge, earlier rounds).

## Where the argument uses it
- A2 (failure: competence oversold; H13): **holds, narrower**. The Declaration promises attainability and frames the
  lack in the learner ("basic learning needs"), then places a "responsibility" on those whose needs are met
  (Art. 1.2) and asks learners to judge that benefits exceed their costs (para. 31). Read with Frahm et al. (2022),
  this is a deficit framed so the institution's programme is the fix; that reading is the facet's, not Frahm's.
- A3 (promise unmet): **narrower**. Jomtien's numeric literacy target was optional (para. 8); the unmet promise is
  carried better by Dakar's commitment and the 2015 GMR's finding (`berthon_ethics_paper/unesco2015education.md`).
