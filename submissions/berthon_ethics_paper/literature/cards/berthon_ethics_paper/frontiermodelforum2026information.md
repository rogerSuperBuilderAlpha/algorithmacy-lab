## Summary
The Frontier Model Forum is "an industry-supported non-profit 501(c)(6), established in 2023", "funded by fees from its member firms", with a board "made up of representatives from its member organizations" (About page, saved at `/Users/ludwitt/iit-playground/berthon_ethics_local/pdfs/nadella/N4/fmf/about.txt`, sha256 `98dd9d755a43b69f0b53258e2f993df363a40667e16a694ca2f9e6c936feb749`). Its members, as this brief lists them, are "Anthropic, Amazon, Google DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI". The brief separates three things: information sharing (ongoing, multi-directional, "Industry, researchers, government"), incident reporting ("Uni-directional, typically industry → authority", to "Regulators, oversight bodies"), and incident response ("Internal teams, affected parties") (Table 1). It reports that in March 2025 member firms "signed a first-of-its-kind voluntary agreement to share safety-relevant information about frontier AI risks", piloted among the six members only, covering vulnerabilities, threats and capabilities of concern. Affected persons appear once, as parties in incident *response*; no category gives them notice. The brief argues that sharing "erodes quickly if participants fear that sharing will expose them to regulatory penalty, competitive disadvantage, or legal liability", and warns against "overly broad mandatory reporting triggers". Accessed 2026-10-24.

## Key facts it relies on
- Table 1, "Direction": incident reporting is "Uni-directional, typically industry → authority"; "Actors": information sharing "Industry, researchers, government"; incident reporting "Regulators, oversight bodies"; incident response "Internal teams, affected parties".
- "In March 2025, FMF member firms signed a first-of-its-kind voluntary agreement to share safety-relevant information about frontier AI risks."
- "As a first step, sharing has been piloted among FMF's member companies: Anthropic, Amazon, Google DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI."
- "Further, its value erodes quickly if participants fear that sharing will expose them to regulatory penalty, competitive disadvantage, or legal liability."
- Incident reporting is "compliance-driven, meaning that the obligation to report arises from law, regulation, or order/agreement rather than voluntary choice".
- A well-designed ecosystem avoids "inadvertently discouraging voluntary sharing of early-warning signals through overly broad mandatory reporting triggers."
- About page: "The FMF is funded by fees from its member firms."

## Critical notes from the literature
Anthropic-authored; corroborate. Anthropic is a member firm of the publisher, which is funded by member fees and governed by member representatives; Microsoft is also a member. Treat it as the industry's own statement of how it shares "learnings".
