## Summary
Morris, late lieutenant-governor of Manitoba and the North-West Territories (title page) and Crown commissioner at Treaty 6, collected the treaty texts with the negotiation records and his reports, so that the Dominion could carry out "in their integrity, the obligations of these treaties" (preface). For Treaty 6 (Forts Carlton and Pitt, 1876) the record shows the Cree, anxious at the buffalo's disappearance and wishing to learn agriculture, putting a list of requests through the interpreter Peter Erasmus: livestock, implements, provisions for the poor, missionaries and school teachers, free medicines, and exemption from war service (p. 185). Morris answers in speech, promising among other things a medicine chest at each agent's house "in case of sickness amongst you" (p. 218). The written treaty, signed after it was "first read and explained" by three named interpreters (p. 357), puts the medicine chest "at the discretion of such Agent" (p. 355) and promises schools "as to her Government … may seem advisable, whenever the Indians of the reserve shall desire it" (p. 353).

## Key facts it relies on
- P. 185: "the interpreter, Peter Erasmus, would read what changes they desired in the terms of our offer. They asked for an ox and a cow each family … provisions for the poor, unfortunate, blind and lame; to be provided with missionaries and school teachers … a free supply of medicines".
- P. 185: "They were anxious to learn to support themselves by agriculture, but felt too ignorant to do so".
- P. 218 (Morris's speech): "A medicine chest will be kept at the house of each Indian agent, in case of sickness amongst you."
- P. 355 (treaty text): "That a medicine chest shall be kept at the house of each Indian Agent for the use and benefit of the Indians, at the discretion of such Agent".
- P. 353 (treaty text): "Her Majesty agrees to maintain schools for instruction in such reserves hereby made, as to her Government of the Dominion of Canada may seem advisable, whenever the Indians of the reserve shall desire it".
- P. 357: "Signed by the Chiefs within named in the presence of the following witnesses, the same having been first read and explained by Peter Erasmus, Peter Ballendine and the Rev. John McKay".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Morris is the Crown's commissioner reporting his own negotiation; the Cree record of Treaty 6 (elders' oral history) is not in this book.
- *Badger* (1996, ¶ 52) cites Morris pp. 338-42 for the point that written treaties did not always record the full oral agreement.
- The courts' readings of the medicine chest clause (*Dreaver* 1935; *Johnston* 1966) were not reached (facet V2, gaps).
- The schools promise connects to what Canada's schooling became; the arm's `trc2015honouring` card covers that record.
