## Summary
This is the pair of texts adopted at the World Conference on Education for All, held at Jomtien,
Thailand, on 5-9 March 1990 and sponsored by UNICEF, UNDP, UNESCO and the World Bank (ERIC record
header). The Declaration's Preamble opens on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and then counts what
"persist[s]": more than 100 million children with no primary schooling and "More than 960 million adults,
two-thirds of whom are women," who are illiterate. Article 1 defines "basic learning needs" as learning
tools such as literacy and numeracy, and the content people need "to survive, to develop their full
capacities, to live and work in dignity". The Framework for Action turns the Declaration into guidance.
Under "Goals and targets" it says that "Appropriate authorities at the national and subnational levels may
establish" intermediate goals (para. 4). Paragraph 8 then lists "proposed dimensions" that "Countries may
wish to set their own targets" in. One of them is halving the adult illiteracy rate by 2000. The Framework
leaves the setting of performance levels to "individual countries" (para. 9). Paragraph 15 makes girls and
women "a most urgent priority" because two-thirds of illiterate adults are female. I read the parts listed
under `pages_read`, not the whole 39 pages.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The halving was a proposal, not an adopted world target. AUDIT_02 (`literature/audit/AUDIT_02.md:78`, F10)
  marked an earlier facet line WRONG for calling it "a target of halving" that the conference adopted. Para. 8
  offers it as one of several "proposed dimensions", worded "to, say, one-half". Paras 4 and 9 leave target
  setting to countries. `T_adjudication.md:38` repeats the point.
- Dakar (2000, Expanded Commentary para. 4) says that "reality has fallen far short" of the Jomtien vision.
  AUDIT_02 (F13, `AUDIT_02.md:81`) notes that the sentence refers to the vision in general. It does not judge the
  adult-literacy dimension, so it cannot serve as the official verdict on the halving.
- Facet F (`literature/facets/F_promise_and_campaigns.md:359`) says that the only world-scale documents on
  these campaigns are UNESCO's own, so UNESCO is both promoter and evaluator. Facet F (:330) adds that the
  conference's "more than 960 million" for 1990 has no counterpart in the current UIS series, which begins
  in 1993.
- The passages I read do not define "illiterate" (facet F:192 says the same).
- Provenance: I read an ERIC microfiche OCR. The official UNESCO PDF was not reached (facet F:349). The OCR
  garbles some paragraph numbers, for example "3." where 5 should be.
