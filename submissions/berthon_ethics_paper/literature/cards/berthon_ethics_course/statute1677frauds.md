## Summary
The Act, in force from 24 June 1677, requires writing for a set of transactions "for prevention of many fraudulent Practices which are commonly endeavoured to be upheld by Perjury and Subornation of Perjury". Section 1 reduces parol leases and freehold interests created by livery and seisin alone to estates at will, s. 2 excepts leases of three years or less at a rent of two-thirds of the improved value, and s. 3 requires assignments and surrenders to be in writing, excepting copyhold and customary interests. Section 4 bars actions on five classes of promise unless the agreement or a memorandum is in writing and signed by the party to be charged or his authorised agent. Section 5 requires devises of land to be in writing and attested by three or four witnesses. Section 17 (s. XVI in *Statutes of the Realm*) makes contracts for goods of £10 or more good only on part acceptance, earnest or part payment, or a signed note. Section 19 (s. XVIII there) bars oral (nuncupative) wills above £30 unless proved by three witnesses present at the making. Most of s. 4 was repealed in England in 1954 (`statute1954lawreform`, in facet V2's bib).

## Key facts it relies on
- Preamble (p. 839): "For prevention of many fraudulent Practices which are commonly endeavoured to be upheld by Perjury and Subornation of Perjury".
- S. 4 (p. 840): no action "upon any speciall promise to answere for the debt default or miscarriages of another person or to charge any person upon any agreement made upon consideration of Marriage or upon any Contract or Sale of Lands … or upon any Agreement that is not to be performed within the space of one yeare from the makeing thereof unlesse the Agreement … or some Memorandum or Note thereof shall be in Writeing and signed by the partie to be charged therewith or some other person thereunto by him lawfully authorized." (The OCR prints "Contraét or Sale"; the BHO transcription in facet A was not re-checked for this word.)
- S. 3 (p. 840): the writing rule for assignments applies to interests "not being Copyhold or Customary Interest".
- S. 17 (margin "XVI." in *Statutes of the Realm*, p. 841): goods "for the price of ten pounds Sterling or upwards" — good only if "the Buyer shall accept part of the Goods soe sold and actually receive the same or give some thing in earnest to bind the bargaine or in part of payment, or that some Note or Memorandum in writeing of the said bargaine be made and signed".
- S. 19 (margin "XVIII." in *Statutes of the Realm*, OCR "XVI.", p. 841): "for prevention of fraudulent Practices in setting up Nuncupative Wills", none good above "thirty pounds that is not proved by the Oathes of three Wittnesses (at the least) that were present at the makeing thereof".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Stollery (1976, `stollery1976statute`, p. 222): the ground made sense when parties could not testify; that ended in 1844-1854. The 1937 Law Revision Committee (via Stollery p. 254): it "promotes more frauds than it prevents".
- Facet V2 (Narrows 3): not a literacy test; agents may sign and oral routes survive in ss. 17 and 19. Facet A: I found no contemporary objector.
