## Summary
Two Hungarian linguists sketch a stratification of human communication in which speech is primary, writing
secondary, twentieth-century media produce "secondary orality and secondary literacy", and AI now produces
"tertiary orality and tertiary literacy" (p. 609). Following Ong, they describe secondary orality as dialogue
through technical devices (pp. 604-605). "Tertiary verbality" covers talking machines, synthetic voices and live
translators (pp. 606-607). "Tertiary literacy" covers machine-generated text: "Computer-generated text is a new
form of literacy, tertiary literacy", with ChatGPT as the 2023 instance (p. 607). Section 6 surveys AI in the
Hungarian judiciary, and the paper closes by asking what tertiary communication means for "planned evolution"
(abstract). It is descriptive and programmatic; it makes no argument about disclosure, burden or design duty.

## Key facts it relies on
- Abstract: changes "from primary forms through secondary forms to tertiary forms in the present under the influence of AI" (p. 601).
- Recorded and transmitted speech is "secondary verbalism" after Ong (p. 604).
- "Computer-generated text is a new form of literacy, tertiary literacy. In 2023, chatGPT ... bursts into the public consciousness" (p. 607).
- Text generators "write and speak (tertiary literacy and verbalism in one)" (p. 607).
- "in the 21st century, today, artificial intelligence is leading to the emergence of tertiary orality and tertiary literacy" (p. 609, with a stratification table).

## Critical notes from the literature
- A short programmatic paper in a national linguistics journal; no data. Its Ong page reference (Ong 1982: 136) differs from the New Accents pagination (pp. 133-134) used in facet V6.
- Heyd (2021) uses "tertiary orality" in a narrower, medium-based sense; the two uses are not the same.
