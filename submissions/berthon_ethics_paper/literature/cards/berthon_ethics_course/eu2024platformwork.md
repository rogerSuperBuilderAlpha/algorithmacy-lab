## Summary
The Directive aims to improve working conditions and personal-data protection in platform work by helping
determine correct employment status, by "promoting transparency, fairness, human oversight, safety and
accountability in algorithmic management", and by improving transparency (art. 1(1)). Its algorithmic-management
rules cover all "persons performing platform work", including those without an employment contract (art. 1(2)).
Chapter III bars certain data processing (art. 7), requires a data-protection impact assessment (art. 8), and
obliges platforms to inform workers about automated monitoring and decision-making systems, including "the
categories of data and the main parameters" and their "relative importance" (art. 9(1)(b)(iii)). Platforms must
oversee and regularly evaluate the impact of automated decisions, and any decision to restrict, suspend or
terminate a worker's account "shall be taken by a human being" (art. 10(5)). Workers may obtain an explanation,
must receive written reasons for listed automated decisions, and may request review by the platform, which must
reply within two weeks (art. 11). Chapter on remedies requires "timely, effective and impartial dispute
resolution and a right to redress" (art. 18), representative standing (art. 19), court-ordered disclosure (art.
21) and protection from adverse treatment and dismissal (arts. 22-23). Member States must transpose by 2 December
2026 (art. 29(1)).

## Key facts it relies on
- Purpose includes "transparency, fairness, human oversight, safety and accountability in algorithmic
  management" (art. 1(1)(b)); algorithmic-management rules reach non-employees (art. 1(2), second subpara.).
- Information duty: "the categories of data and the main parameters that such systems take into account and the
  relative importance of those main parameters in the automated decision-making, including the way in which the
  personal data or behaviour of the person performing platform work influence the decisions" (art. 9(1)(b)(iii)).
- Grounds for decisions to restrict, suspend or terminate an account must be disclosed in advance (art.
  9(1)(b)(iv)).
- "Any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate the contractual relationship or the account of a person
  performing platform work or any other decision of equivalent detriment shall be taken by a human being"
  (art. 10(5)).
- Written statement of reasons for decisions "taken or supported by an automated decision-making system" to
  restrict, suspend or terminate an account, refuse payment and the like (art. 11(1), second subpara.).
- Right to request review by the platform; written reply "within two weeks" (art. 11(2)); art. 11 does not apply
  to business users under Regulation 2019/1150 (art. 11(5)).
- "timely, effective and impartial dispute resolution and a right to redress" (art. 18); courts may order
  disclosure of evidence (art. 21).
- Transposition "by 2 December 2026" (art. 29(1)).

## Where the manuscript uses it
- v2 l. 167: the Directive "requires that a decision to restrict, suspend, or terminate a platform worker's
  account be taken by a human being". Source art. 10(5). **holds**; the Directive's subject is the wider "person
  performing platform work", and it adds "any other decision of equivalent detriment". The manuscript cites
  "arts. 10-11"; art. 10(5) is the precise locator.
- v2 l. 167: "and gives the worker a right to written reasons and to a review". Source art. 11(1) second subpara.,
  art. 11(2). **narrower**: written reasons are owed for decisions "taken or supported by an automated
  decision-making system"; the review is a request to the platform, answered within two weeks; business users
  are excluded (art. 11(5)).
- v2 l. 167: "member states must transpose it by 2 December 2026". Source art. 29(1). **holds**.
- v2 l. 215, quotation: "the categories of data and the main parameters that such systems take into account and
  the relative importance of those main parameters in the automated decision-making" (cited art. 9(1)(b)).
  Exact-string check against the OJ text: **exact match**, as the opening of art. 9(1)(b)(iii). **holds**; the
  locator should be art. 9(1)(b)(iii), and the clause continues "including the way in which the personal data or
  behaviour ... influence the decisions".
- v2 l. 215: "That is a duty to inform about the method." Source art. 9(1). **holds**; formally the Directive
  obliges Member States to "require digital labour platforms to inform".
- v2 l. 237: "The Platform Work Directive's human decision on termination is a fragment of structural refusal; its
  duty to inform workers about automated systems is a fragment of the envelope." Source arts. 10(5), 9. **holds**
  as a description of the provisions.
- v2 l. 237: "each places the first review inside the firm, and none supplies the binding independent party".
  Source arts. 10(1)-(2), 11(2) (oversight and review by the platform) and art. 18. **narrower**: the first

  dispute resolution and a right to redress", and art. 21 lets courts order disclosure. These are independent
  and binding, though after the decision; "none" holds only if the sentence means a party at the point of
  decision, as v2 l. 21 frames it.

  platform work. Official Journal of the European Union, L, 11.11.2024." Matches the OJ header and the "Done at
