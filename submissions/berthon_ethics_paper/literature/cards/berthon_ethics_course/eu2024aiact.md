## Summary
The AI Act sets harmonised rules for AI systems in the Union. Its duties toward the people a system acts on are notices and explanations. Providers must design systems that interact with people so that they "are informed that they are interacting with an AI system", unless that is obvious to a person "reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect" (Art 50(1)); the information must be "clear and distinguishable" and given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" (Art 50(5)). Deployers of Annex III high-risk systems must inform the people "subject to the use" of the system (Art 26(11)), and an affected person may obtain "clear and meaningful explanations" of the system's role in a decision (Art 86(1)). Transparency about how to interpret outputs runs to deployers (Art 13(1)). Oversight belongs to natural persons the deployer assigns, who need "competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support" (Art 26(2)) and must stay aware of "automation bias" (Art 14(4)(b)). The one duty about competence, Article 4, binds providers and deployers for their own staff and agents; affected persons appear in the definition of AI literacy (Art 3(56)) and in recital 20, not in the operative duty. Serious incidents go to market surveillance authorities (Art 73(1)).

## Key facts it relies on
- Art 3(56): AI literacy is "skills, knowledge and understanding that allow providers, deployers and affected persons ... to make an informed deployment of AI systems".
- Art 4 (as enacted): providers and deployers "shall take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf ... and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used." Applied from 2 February 2025 (Art 113). Replaced in July 2026 (`euomnibus2026`).
- Recital 20: for affected persons, literacy means "the knowledge necessary to understand how decisions taken with the assistance of AI will have an impact on them"; codes of conduct to advance literacy are "voluntary".
- Art 13(1): high-risk systems "sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately"; 13(2): instructions "comprehensible to deployers".
- Art 14(4)(b), (e): overseers aware of "automation bias"; able to "interrupt the system through a 'stop' button".
- Art 26(2): oversight assigned "to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support"; Art 26(11): deployers "shall inform the natural persons that they are subject to the use of the high-risk AI system".
- Art 50(1), (5): notice of AI interaction; "clear and distinguishable", "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", accessible.
- Art 73(1): providers report serious incidents "to the market surveillance authorities"; Art 85: anyone may complain to a market surveillance authority.
- Art 86(1): right "to obtain from the deployer clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken" (Annex III systems, adverse legal or similarly significant effects); residual (86(3)).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet N2 (`facets/N2_platform_law.md`): Art 13 transparency "flows to the party that buys the reading, not to the party read"; the human at the point of decision is the deployer's (Arts 14, 26(2)); Chapter III obligations for Annex III systems are postponed to 2 December 2027 by the Omnibus; whether Art 86 bites before then is unclear.
- Facet Z (`facets/Z_gap_search.md` R1): Art 4 is not among the Art 99(4) fine categories; national penalties unread.
- DXD card `wt-dxd-foundation/.../library/eu2024regulation.md` (Art 14 from a web edition, S2-confirmed): 14(4)(e) is the statutory counterpart of Nadella's P19 brake.
- Microsoft's 2025 Transparency Report (PDF p. 26) attributes recital 20's words to a duty on providers and deployers; the words are a recital about what literacy "should" do (facet V1).
