## Summary
Article 25 of Spain's Notarial Law of 1862 governs how notarial deeds are written and executed. Deeds are drafted in Castilian, in clear script, without abbreviations, blanks or numerals for dates and sums. The notary must attest that he read the whole deed to the parties and the instrumental witnesses, "or let them read it, at their choice", before they sign. He must also attest that he warned them of their right to read it themselves. The 1862 text is unchanged in the current law. In 2021, Ley 8/2021, a law reforming the legal capacity of persons with disabilities, added a final paragraph. Persons with disabilities appearing before a notary may use the supports and reasonable adjustments they need, "including ... braille, easy reading, pictograms, easily accessible multimedia devices, interpreters, oral-communication supports, sign language" and others.

## Key facts it relies on
- Para. 1 (1862 and current): "Los instrumentos públicos se redactarán en lengua castellana, y se escribirán con letra clara, sin abreviaturas y sin blancos."
- Para. 3 (1862 and current): "Los Notarios darán fe de haber leído a las partes y a los testigos instrumentales la escritura íntegra, o de haberles permitido que la lean, a su elección, antes de que la firmen, y a los de conocimiento lo que a ellos se refiera, y de haber advertido a unos y a otros que tienen el derecho de leerla por sí." (The notary attests to having read the whole deed to the parties, or let them read it, at their choice, and to having warned them of their right to read it themselves.)
- Para. 4 (from 3 September 2021): persons with disabilities "podrán utilizar los apoyos, instrumentos y ajustes razonables que resulten precisos, incluyendo sistemas aumentativos y alternativos, braille, lectura fácil, pictogramas, dispositivos multimedia de fácil acceso, intérpretes, sistemas de apoyos a la comunicación oral, lengua de signos, lenguaje dactilológico, sistemas de comunicación táctil y otros dispositivos".
- Version note: "Se añade un párrafo al final, con efectos de 3 de septiembre de 2021, por el art. 1.2 de la Ley 8/2021, de 2 de junio."

## Critical notes from the literature
- The reading duty continues the Castilian practice of the Pragmática of 1503 (`almeida2019firmo`, p. 24). That card records that the 1503 text says the notary reads "presentes las partes", and does not say "aloud". The 1862 text says "haber leído a las partes", reading to them.
- The 2021 paragraph is limited to persons with disabilities. It does not extend to people who simply cannot read, unless they fall within that category. I did not read Ley 8/2021 itself.
- Norm, not practice: I read no evidence on how notaries apply art. 25.
