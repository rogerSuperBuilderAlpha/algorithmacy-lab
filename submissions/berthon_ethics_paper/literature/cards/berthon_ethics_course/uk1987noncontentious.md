## Summary
Rule 13 governs proving in the probate registry a will that appears to have been signed by a blind or illiterate testator, or by another person at the testator's direction. It also covers any will that "for any other reason raises doubt as to the testator having had knowledge of the contents". Before admitting such a will to proof, the district judge or registrar "shall satisfy himself that the testator had such knowledge." The words "district judge or" were inserted in 1991. The rule does not prescribe a form of attestation or a reading over. It puts on a judicial officer the duty to reach a positive finding of knowledge. (Verifier: a sentence here said that "in practice" the risk falls on the person who propounds the will. The rule does not say so, and the card did not read the case law, so I removed it.)

## Key facts it relies on
- Heading: "Execution of will of blind or illiterate testator".
- Text (latest): "Before admitting to proof a will which appears to have been signed by a blind or illiterate testator or by another person by direction of the testator, or which for any other reason raises doubt as to the testator having had knowledge of the contents of the will at the time of its execution, the [district judge or] registrar shall satisfy himself that the testator had such knowledge."
- As made (1987), the duty falls on "the registrar" alone.
- Amendment: "Words in rule 13 inserted (14.10.1991) by The Non-Contentious Probate (Amendment) Rules 1991 (S.I. 1991/1876), rules 1(1), 7(1)."
- Commencement: "Rule 13 in force at 1.1.1988".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The rule protects the integrity of the estate, not a living reader. The testator is dead and cannot be bound or harmed by the will in the way a contracting party can.
- I did not read the "knowledge and approval" case law (e.g. *Barry v Butlin*) that stands behind the rule. This card claims only what the rule says.
- It is the clearest instance in facet V3 of an institution checking knowledge instead of presuming it from a signature or mark.
