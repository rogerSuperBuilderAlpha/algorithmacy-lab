## Summary
The Hills ordered a Gateway computer by phone. The box arrived holding a list of terms that would govern unless the computer was returned within 30 days, and one of those terms required arbitration. The Hills kept the computer, then sued under RICO, and the district court refused to compel arbitration. Judge Easterbrook, writing for the Seventh Circuit, reversed. Under the Federal Arbitration Act an arbitration clause need not be prominent. "A contract need not be read to be effective; people who accept take the risk that the unread terms may in retrospect prove unwelcome." Following *ProCD v. Zeidenberg*, a vendor as "master of the offer" may invite acceptance by conduct, and keeping the product past the return period is acceptance. The opinion rejects any requirement that terms be read aloud before sale: "Cashiers cannot be expected to read legal documents to customers before ringing up sales". Telephonic recitation "would anesthetize rather than enlighten". "Competent adults are bound by such documents, read or unread." It refuses to limit *ProCD* to software, to executory contracts or to merchants. Shoppers can ask for terms in advance, consult public sources or inspect them after delivery. The case was remanded with instructions to compel arbitration.

## Key facts it relies on
- Facts: a phone order, terms in the box, an accept-or-return period of 30 days, and an arbitration clause. The Hills kept the computer beyond 30 days.
- FAA § 2 and *Doctor's Associates v. Casarotto* (1996): an arbitration clause need not be prominent.
- "A contract need not be read to be effective; people who accept take the risk that the unread terms may in retrospect prove unwelcome."
- *ProCD*: "A vendor, as master of the offer, may invite acceptance by conduct". Terms "stand or fall together".
- "Cashiers cannot be expected to read legal documents to customers before ringing up sales." Oral recitation "would anesthetize rather than enlighten many potential buyers".
- "Customers as a group are better off when vendors skip costly and ineffectual steps such as telephonic recitation". "Competent adults are bound by such documents, read or unread."
- *ProCD* applies "for merchants and consumers alike". It is not limited to software or to executory contracts.
- Three ways to discover terms: ask the vendor (the Magnuson-Moss Act requires warranty terms on request), consult public sources, or inspect after delivery.
- Disposition: vacated and remanded "with instructions to compel the Hills to submit their dispute to arbitration."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet M3 (:113, :126-129) and ledger P4-056: *Specht v. Netscape* (2d Cir. 2002) finds no assent without reasonable notice. Assent to standard terms therefore depends on adequate notice, and *Hill* is one circuit's rule, not settled law everywhere.
- Ledger P5-025: the saved copies have no reporter pagination, and the pin cite 105 F.3d at 1148-49 is unconfirmed. This session's law.resource.org copy also lacks star pages.
- The opinion itself flags an open question it did not decide: the remedy where return is deterred by shipping cost ("an interesting question, but one that need not detain us").
