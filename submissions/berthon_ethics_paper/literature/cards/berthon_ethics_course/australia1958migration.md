## Summary

The Migration Act 1958 is "An Act relating to Immigration, Deportation and Emigration" and replaced Australia's immigration statutes from the 1901 Act onward. Section 4(1) repeals the Acts listed in its Schedule, which runs from the "Immigration Restriction Act 1901" through every amending Act to the "Immigration Act 1949", and adds the Pacific Island Labourers Acts of 1901 and 1906 and the Aliens Deportation Act 1948. In place of the old machinery Division 1 of Part II creates the entry permit: an immigrant who enters without a permit in force "thereupon becomes a prohibited immigrant" (s. 6(1)), and "An officer may … grant to the immigrant an entry permit" (s. 6(2)). The Act does not contain a dictation or education test. Section 2 left commencement to proclamation. The Federal Register's legislation history records that ss. 59–64 commenced on 10 November 1958 and the remainder, including the repeal in s. 4, on 1 June 1959. The Act therefore ended the 1901 regime in law on 1 June 1959, eight months after assent.

## Key facts it relies on

- Long title and assent: "An Act relating to Immigration, Deportation and Emigration. [Assented to 8th October, 1958.]" (as made, p. 1).
- s. 1: "This Act may be cited as the Migration Act 1958."
- s. 2: "The several Parts of this Act shall come into operation on such dates as are respectively fixed by Proclamation."
- s. 4(1): "The Acts specified in the Schedule to this Act are repealed."
- The Schedule ("Section 4"), "Acts relating to immigration and deportation repealed": "Immigration Restriction Act 1901", the Amendment Act 1905, Acts of 1908 and 1910, and "Immigration Act" 1912 through 1949, with the Pacific Island Labourers Acts and the Aliens Deportation Act 1948.
- s. 6(1)–(2): entry without a permit makes the immigrant a "prohibited immigrant"; an officer "may" grant an entry permit. The marginal note reads "Cf. I.A., s. 3 (1.) (a)", where "I.A." means the Immigration Act 1901-1949 (as made, p. 1, footnote to the marginal notes).
- Compilation No. 1 (C2025C00529), Endnote 3: "Migration Act 1958 | 62, 1958 | 8 Oct 1958 | s 59-64: 10 Nov 1958 (s 2 and gaz 1958, p 3857) Remainder: 1 June 1959 (s 2 and gaz 1959 p 1831)".

## Critical notes from the literature

- The Act shows when the 1901 regime ended. It cannot show that the dictation test was still in that regime at the end. The ira1901immigration card (l. 38–40) records, from the Museum of Australian Democracy page, a 1905 amendment to "any prescribed language" and the Museum's line that the Act "remained in force until 1958"; Lake (2006, p. 228) prints "The dictation test remained in immigration legislation until 1958" (ira1901immigration card, l. 56). Those sources date the end by enactment; this Act dates it by commencement, 1 June 1959.
- The marginal note to s. 6 ties the new entry-permit rule to s. 3(1)(a) of the old Act, which the 1949 consolidation had numbered as the prohibited-immigrant clause. I did not open the 1949 consolidation, so I cannot say what that paragraph then contained.
- The entry permit keeps an officer's discretion ("may … grant"). The manuscript makes no claim about the 1958 regime, so this is context, not a fault.
- `review/v3/CLOSED_LEDGER.md` (l. 213) and `review/v3/resolutions/C1_historical.md` (l. 50–54) rated the v2 sentence ("until 1958") narrower; v3 adopts the commencement date.
