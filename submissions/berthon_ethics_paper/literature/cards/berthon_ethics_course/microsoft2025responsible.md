## Summary
Microsoft's second annual report covers governance, AI Act preparation, tooling for customers and research. On the pages read it treats AI literacy as an AI Act compliance matter met by training. The "AI literacy requirements" were among the first AI Act provisions to apply (February 2025), and Microsoft "has undertaken various initiatives to promote AI literacy ... empowering our employees, customers, and others" (PDF p. 7). Its "AI literacy efforts" section (PDF p. 26) lists role-specific learning paths and Trust Code training for employees, public "AI courses", an "AI Literacy Starting Guide" for vendors and customers, an "AI Skills Navigator", and an AARP/OATS programme for adults over 50, and tells customers they "need to assess how literacy requirements in the EU AI Act ... apply to them". The same section misattributes recital 20 of the AI Act as a requirement on providers and deployers. The page also reports more visibility for users and admins into Copilot's web queries, with admin audit and eDiscovery. Research pages name overreliance mitigations "such as uncertainty highlighting, cognitive forcing functions, contrastive explanations, and AI critiques" (PDF p. 28). Self-reported.

## Key facts it relies on
- PDF p. 7 (printed 11): "The first set of provisions that came into effect in February 2025 were the AI literacy requirements and the prohibited practices provisions".
- PDF p. 26 (printed 49): "Promoting AI literacy helps ensure that individuals can make informed decisions regarding AI systems."
- PDF p. 26: "The EU AI Act requires that deployers and providers of AI systems 'provide all relevant actors in the AI value chain with the insights required to ensure the appropriate compliance and its correct enforcement.'" The quoted words are recital 20's, whose subject is "AI literacy", not an operative duty (`eu2024aiact`, recital 20); the operative duty is Art 4.
- PDF p. 26: "We offer a variety of AI literacy programs for our employees, with role-specific online learning paths"; "an AI Literacy Starting Guide with training materials for vendors and customers"; "AI Skills Navigator"; OATS programme for "American adults over the age of 50".
- PDF p. 26: web-query visibility for "both users and admins"; admins can "perform search, audit, and eDiscovery".
- PDF p. 28 (printed 53): overreliance mitigations "such as uncertainty highlighting, cognitive forcing functions, contrastive explanations, and AI critiques" (research, not product requirements).
