## Summary
Nussbaum sets out the capabilities approach for a general reader. This card rests only on one-line search
snippets from the lending scan, so it records phrases, not arguments in context, and gives no page numbers. A
decent political order, she argues, must secure for all citizens "at least a threshold level" of ten Central
Capabilities. The fourth, "Senses, imagination, and thought", is to be exercised in a way "informed and cultivated by
an adequate education, including, but by no means limited to, literacy and basic mathematical and scientific
training". She distinguishes internal capabilities — trained, "fluid and dynamic" traits of a person — from combined
capabilities, which are internal capabilities "plus the social/political/economic conditions" in which they can be
exercised. "A society might do quite well at producing internal capabilities but might cut off the avenues through
which people actually have the opportunity" to function. Following Wolff and De-Shalit, a fertile functioning "tends
to promote other related capabilities", and "corrosive disadvantage" is its flip side.
