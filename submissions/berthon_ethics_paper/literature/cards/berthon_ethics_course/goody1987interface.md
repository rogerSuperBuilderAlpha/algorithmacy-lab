## Summary
I could not open the book. The archive.org copy (`interfacebetween00good`) is lending-only, and I read only the
highlights its public full-text search API returns, a line or two around each query term, without page numbers.
This card claims nothing beyond those lines. In them Goody returns to the reception of his and Watt's 1963 essay:
"the Greek achievement has been interpreted as a Great Divide theory (Finnegan 1973) separating cultures with and
without writing. On the contrary, we were attempting to avoid the binary divisions current in much scholarship".
He proposes instead "that we should not think of a simple binary shift between oral and literate, but a whole
sequence of changes that have to be defined in terms of the means" (sentence cut off by the search window). In a
numbered list he writes: "Fourthly, and this is the important point for our present purpose, the division between
'literacy' and 'orality' is never a question of crossing a single frontier, a simple binary shift. If it were only a
matter of the way poetry is presented, in writing or in recitation, there…". The context around that last sentence
(poetry presented in writing or recitation) suggests a chapter on oral and written verse, but I have not seen it.
The book's argument as a whole is not on this card.
