## Summary
Twenty authors from the Bielefeld-Paderborn explainability programme argue that explainable AI treats the
explainee as passive and explanation as a complete description of a system's internals (pp. 717-718). They name
three limits: the "passive explainee", a narrow view of the social process, and an undifferentiated measure of
understanding (p. 717). The XAI concepts of interpretability and completeness assume that "the mere availability
of information will suffice to ... warrant an understanding that ... enables the explainee to act further"
(p. 718). In place of this they propose explanation as a "social practice in which explainer and explainee
co-construct understanding", governed by monitoring and scaffolding (p. 717). Borrowing scaffolding from
developmental psychology (Wood et al.), they say "The goal in scaffolding is to allow a less competent partner to
participate in an interaction and to contribute to a task" and that scaffolds "are temporary" (p. 723).
Transparency that aims at autonomy should be conceived from the side of the individuals demanding it, but "We
must be careful, however, to not add the empowerment of persons to a number of already existing burdens"
(p. 726).

## Key facts it relies on
- Limitations: "passive explainee, narrow view on the social process, and undifferentiated assessment of explainee's understanding" (abstract, p. 717).
- Implicit assumption that "the mere availability of information will suffice to 3) warrant an understanding that 4) enables the explainee to act further" (p. 718).
- The explainee is "conceptualized as an independent actor, who makes autonomous decisions on the basis of information made available to them through transparency" (p. 718, quoting Felzmann et al. 2019).
- Framework "relies on the key concepts of monitoring and scaffolding" (abstract, p. 717).
- §IV-B "Explaining Is Scaffolding": scaffolds are "temporary"; learning is "co-constructed by both partners" (p. 723).
- "understanding means clearly more than an access to information, because it connects with further actions" (p. 726).
- Burden caution, citing Matzner et al. 2016, "Do-It-yourself data protection—Empowerment or burden?" (p. 726).

## Critical notes from the literature
- A conceptual framework from a research programme; no evaluation of a deployed system in the pages read.
- Its "explainee" is any user of an AI system; it does not distinguish the deploying organisation's staff from the people decided about (facet V6, O4).
- Facet V6 (Q1a) treats it as the nearest prior statement of the information-suffices critique and of scaffolding as the remedy; it does not frame the assumption as a literacy assumption or argue from history.
