## Summary
This card rests on the abstract only. Lund and colleagues write a conceptual paper that responds to a gap in AI
literacy among users of large language models. They note that explainable AI has been framed "as a mechanism for
transparency and accountability" and argue that its use "as a developmental learning support for end users remains
underexplored". From an integrative reading of explainable-AI, AI-literacy, educational-scaffolding and human–AI
teaming literatures, they conceptualise explanations as scaffolds supporting "the gradual, in situ development of AI
literacy skills through explanation-driven interaction". Three principles organise the framework: "contingent
support calibrated to the learner's Zone of Proximal Development, progressive fading of explanatory support as
competence grows, and the reframing of explanations as entry points for inquiry rather than disclosure endpoints."
They claim these principles support "calibrated trust" rather than "uncritical acceptance or unwarranted
skepticism", and propose methods for identifying scaffolding behaviour in user interactions with explainable systems.
It is a conceptual paper with no data.
