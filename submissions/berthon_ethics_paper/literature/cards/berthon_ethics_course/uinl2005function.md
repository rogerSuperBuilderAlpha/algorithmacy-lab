## Summary
The UINL's *Principles of the notarial function* is a short statement by the international professional
body of civil-law notaries. It presents itself as "the essence of the notarial institution and the model which
all Notariats should aspire to" (preamble). It is a professional body's statement, not a statute. Part I
defines notaries as "professional lawyers and public officials appointed by the State to confer authenticity"
on deeds they draft and "to advise persons who call upon their services," and requires them to act "both
impartially and independently, outside any State hierarchy." Part II sets out the notarised deed: the notary
interprets the parties' wishes within the law, verifies identity and capacity, ensures intentions "are freely
expressed," keeps the original, and the deed enjoys a "dual presumption of legality and accuracy of content,"
contestable only in court. Part III leaves competence, numbers, location and entry qualifications to national
law and requires membership in a single collegiate body. Part IV, on ethics, places notaries under public and
collegiate supervision, imposes good faith and secrecy, and states that "a Notary's impartiality can also take
the form of lending adequate assistance to whichever of the parties might be in a position of inferiority."
The page closes "Approved by the General Meeting of member Chambers of Notaries of the UINL / Rome, Italy - 8th
November 2005."

## Key facts it relies on
- Notaries are "public officials appointed by the State to confer authenticity on judicial deeds and
  contracts contained in documents drafted by them and to advise persons who call upon their services"
  (Part I, para. 1).
- "Although Notaries have official powers they are required to act both impartially and independently,
  outside any State hierarchy." (Part I, para. 2.)
- Notaries ensure "that the intentions of the parties uttered in their presence are freely expressed"
  (Part II, para. 2).
- The notary keeps the original; parties are entitled to copies (Part II, paras. 1, 4).
- Notarised deeds carry a "dual presumption of legality and accuracy of content" and "may be contested only
  through judicial channels" (Part II, para. 5).
- Notaries are "under the constant supervision of their public authorities and collegiate bodies" (Part IV,
  para. 1).
- "To achieve the balance needed in order to conclude a contract on an equal footing a Notary's impartiality
  can also take the form of lending adequate assistance to whichever of the parties might be in a position of
  inferiority." (Part IV, para. 4.)
- Approved at Rome, 8 November 2005 (closing line).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet M3 (`literature/facets/M3_intermediaries_records.md:190-194`): the page is "the union's own
  description"; Facet G found reviews reporting that Cuzco notaries acted "for both litigants and judges" and
  could be moved by clients (Andrien on Burns), so norm and practice differ. The norm is for civil-law
  notaries, and the English notarial tradition differs. National statutes behind the text were not searched.
- AUDIT_05 M3-13 (`literature/audit/AUDIT_05.md:403`) confirmed the three passages exact and the Rome date.
- Ledger P5-032 (`review/v3/ledger_parts/P5.md:38`) places the "position of inferiority" sentence in "function
  Part I"; on the page it sits in **Part IV** (Notarial ethics). The bib URL (`uinl.org/en_GB/principios-de-la-funcion`)
  differs from the canonical English URL in the saved page's links (given above).
- Ledgers P5-053 and P6-022: the manuscript's past-tense claims about what the notary's duty "was" and that
  his force "came from an office with authority over both sides" rest on this modern self-description, which
  does not document historical practice.

## Where the manuscript uses it
- **Line 175**, "The civil-law notary's professed duty runs to both parties and to the weaker of them (Union
  Internationale du Notariat, 2004, 2005)." Takes, from the 2005 text: impartiality between parties and
  assistance to the party "in a position of inferiority." Source: Part I para. 2; Part IV para. 4. **holds.**
  "Professed" fits a professional body's statement. "Both parties" is stated outright only in the 2004 ethics
  principles (principle 9, per M3:192 and AUDIT_05 M3-14); the 2005 text gives impartiality, which implies it.
- **Line 199** (no citation; ledger P5-053), "The notary's duty was never loyalty. It was impartiality toward two
  parties and care for the weaker, enforced by an office the parties did not control." Source: Part I para. 2
  (impartial, independent, "appointed by the State"), Part IV paras. 1, 4. **narrower.** The text supports the
  duty as professed in 2005, not as a historical fact ("was").
- **Line 225** (no citation; ledger P6-022), "The notary's force came from an office with authority over both
  sides." Source: Part I (public official, official powers); Part II para. 5 (presumption of legality).
  **narrower.** The 2005 text describes official powers and conclusive deeds today; it does not document the
  historical office.
- **Line 387, reference entry**: "Union Internationale du Notariat. (2005). *Principles of the notarial
  function*." Title and year match the page (Rome, 8 November 2005). **holds.**
