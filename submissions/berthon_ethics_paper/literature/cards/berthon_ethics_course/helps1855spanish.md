## Summary
Helps, a Victorian historian of Spanish colonial policy, gives an English translation of the Requerimiento ("the Requisition") in full in his account of Pedrarias Dávila's expedition (pp. 379-382), credits it to Palacios Rubios, and comments on it (pp. 382-383). He judges the document's "logic, the history, even the grammatical construction" all "alike in error" and notes that its key terms "require a knowledge of Christianity and of the peculiar civilization of Europe for any one to understand", so that even when read it could convey little "to a number of Indians sitting in a circle, and listening to European voices for the first time" (p. 383). He notes that the instructions allowed Caribs, called cannibals, to be taken "without even the reading of the Requisition" (p. 383), and translates Oviedo's account of being handed the text at Santa Marta (p. 387). The card is used for an English text of the Requerimiento's clauses, checked against the Spanish in Las Casas (`lascasas1876historia`).

## Key facts it relies on
- P. 380: the donation is "contained in certain writings which passed upon the subject as aforesaid, which you can see if you wish."
- P. 381: "we ask and require you that you consider what we have said to you, and that you take the time that shall be necessary to understand and deliberate upon it" (Spanish: "el tiempo que fuere justo", Las Casas p. 156; "just" is closer than "necessary").
- P. 382: "we protest that the deaths and losses which shall accrue from this are your fault, and not that of their Highnesses, or ours, nor of these cavaliers who come with us. And that we have said this to you and made this Requisition, we request the notary here present to give us his testimony in writing, and we ask the rest who are present that they should be witnesses of this Requisition."
- P. 383: "the very terms 'Church,' 'privileges,' 'vassalage,' 'exemptions,' are such as require a knowledge of Christianity and of the peculiar civilization of Europe for any one to understand."
- P. 383: "There were Indians who might be taken without even the reading of the Requisition. These were the Caribs, or cannibals."
- P. 387 (Oviedo, translated): the Governor "gave it to me from his own hand, as if I understood the Indians, or as if we should find any one there who would make them understand it, even if they were willing to listen."

## Critical notes from the literature
- A nineteenth-century translation with editorial asides inside the quoted text (for example "(imagine what Tiberius or Nero would have said to this assertion!)", p. 380); quote only the translated clauses, not the asides.
- Helps renders "fuere justo" as "shall be necessary"; give the Spanish beside it when the time clause matters.
