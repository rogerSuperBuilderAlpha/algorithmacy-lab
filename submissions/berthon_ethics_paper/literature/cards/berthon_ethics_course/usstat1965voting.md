## Summary
The Act's title is "To enforce the fifteenth amendment to the Constitution of the United States" (p. 437).
Section 2 bars any voting qualification or practice that denies or abridges the vote "on account of race or
color". Section 3 lets courts in Attorney General suits appoint federal examiners and suspend tests and
devices. Section 4 is the core. In any state or subdivision covered by the formula in s. 4(b), "no citizen
shall be denied the right to vote … because of his failure to comply with any test or device" (p. 438). The
formula covers areas that kept a test or device on 1 November 1964 where under half the voting-age population
was registered or voted in November 1964. Section 4(c) defines "test or device" to include any requirement to
"demonstrate the ability to read, write, understand, or interpret any matter" (pp. 438-439). A covered state
can escape only by a declaratory judgment from the District of Columbia court that it has used no
discriminatory test for five years. Section 4(e) protects voters schooled in American-flag schools taught in a
language other than English (p. 439). Section 5 requires covered jurisdictions to clear new voting rules with
that court or the Attorney General (p. 439). Section 6 provides federal examiners (pp. 439-440).

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 437: Public Law 89-110, "AN ACT To enforce the fifteenth amendment to the Constitution of the United States, and for other purposes"; s. 1, short title "Voting Rights Act of 1965".
- p. 437, s. 2: no qualification or procedure "shall be imposed or applied … to deny or abridge the right of any citizen of the United States to vote on account of race or color."
- p. 437, s. 3(b): where a court finds a test or device used to discriminate, "it shall suspend the use of tests and devices" there.
- p. 438, s. 4(a): "no citizen shall be denied the right to vote in any Federal, State, or local election because of his failure to comply with any test or device in any State with respect to which the determinations have been made under subsection (b)", unless the D.C. district court finds no discriminatory use "during the five years preceding the filing of the action".
- p. 438, s. 4(b): the formula is a test or device maintained on 1 November 1964, together with under 50% of voting-age persons registered on that date or voting in the presidential election of November 1964.
- pp. 438-439, s. 4(c): "test or device" includes any requirement to "(1) demonstrate the ability to read, write, understand, or interpret any matter, (2) demonstrate any educational achievement …, (3) possess good moral character, or (4) prove his qualifications by the voucher of registered voters".
- p. 439, s. 4(e): Congress declares it "necessary to prohibit the States from conditioning the right to vote" of persons educated in American-flag schools "on ability to read, write, understand, or interpret any matter in the English language".
- p. 439, s. 5: covered jurisdictions may not enforce a new voting qualification unless the D.C. court approves it or the Attorney General does not object within sixty days.

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet D (entry 7; "What ended it", l. 464): the tests ended in stages. *Guinn* (1915) struck the grandfather
  clause but left the test valid. *Lassiter* (1959) upheld a literacy test applied to all voters. This Act
  suspended tests only where the formula applied. The 1970 amendments extended the suspension nationwide
  (`usstat1970voting`), and the 1975 amendments made the ban permanent (89 Stat. 400, title, per facet D entry 9;
  the arm has not opened the 1975 Act's operative text).
- FIX_CHECK_C: the entry "Pub. L. No. 89-110, 79 Stat. 437" matches the running head. Optional: the in-text form
  "(79 Stat. 437, s. 4)" omits the Act's short title, which the reference entry leads with.
- The "suspension" ran until a covered state won a bail-out judgment. It was not a fixed-term suspension,
  although s. 4(a) set five-year periods for bail-out eligibility.
