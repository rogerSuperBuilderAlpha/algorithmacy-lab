## Summary
Israeli traces the colonial Requerimiento back to an ordinary procedure of late medieval Castilian law. Drawing on fourteenth- and fifteenth-century notarial records (*testimonios de requerimiento*), he treats its "ritual core" as "the presentation of a demand or warning to an addressee and its official recording" (p. 39). The procedure was performative: once conducted, "the addressee could not pretend to be ignorant of the demand, and could be considered as having been given a fair opportunity to make amends" (p. 46). Its force depended on the notary's record, without which the procedure could be "meaningless" (p. 46). In Castile it also had "an important dialogical component": addressees were asked whether they would comply, often answered, and the notary had to record their answer (p. 47). He reads the sixteenth-century critics (Las Casas, via Faudree) as arguing that these conditions "never existed in the New World" because the *indios* could not understand the text or respond (p. 47). He reports, and qualifies, Seed's hypothesis of an Islamic origin (p. 39).

## Key facts it relies on
- P. 39: the ritual core is "the presentation of a demand or warning to an addressee and its official recording".
- P. 46: "Once a requerimiento had been conducted, the addressee could not pretend to be ignorant of the demand, and could be considered as having been given a fair opportunity to make amends."
- P. 46: "the lack of a testimonio could render the entire procedure meaningless, preventing the claimant from demonstrating that a requerimiento had indeed been conducted."
- P. 47: critics argued "that the conditions needed for the requerimiento to function as a performative speech act in fact never existed in the New World, both because in many cases the indios could not understand the text recited to them and because they did not receive the proper opportunity to respond."
- P. 47: in fifteenth-century Castile "requerimientos did have an important dialogical component … the notary was obliged to document the addressee's response and incorporate it into his testimony."
- P. 39: Seed is "the main proponent" of an origin in "Islamic rituals of war".
