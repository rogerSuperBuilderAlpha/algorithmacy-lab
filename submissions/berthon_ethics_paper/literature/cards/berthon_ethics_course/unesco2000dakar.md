## Summary
The Dakar Framework for Action is the commitment that the World Education Forum adopted in April 2000, ten
years after Jomtien. The participants "commit ourselves to the achievement of education for all (EFA) goals
and targets for every citizen and for every society" (para. 1). Paragraph 2 says that "Governments have an
obligation to ensure that EFA goals and targets are reached and sustained". The Framework reaffirms the Jomtien
vision (para. 3). Drawing on the EFA 2000 Assessment, it calls it "unacceptable" that "880 million adults are
illiterate" (para. 5). Paragraph 7 then lists six goals to which the participants "collectively commit
ourselves". Goal (iv) is "achieving a 50 per cent improvement in levels of adult literacy by 2015, especially
for women". The Expanded Commentary judges that "reality has fallen far short" of the Jomtien vision and
blames weak political will and insufficient resources, among other causes (para. 4). Under goal 4 it repeats
the 880 million figure, says two-thirds are women, and places adult education "at the periphery of national
education systems and budgets" (para. 37). Unlike Jomtien, the 2000 text is a collective commitment to stated
goals, not a menu of proposed dimensions.

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet F (`literature/facets/F_promise_and_campaigns.md:330`): Dakar's 880 million for 2000 does not match the
  current UIS series (801,852,096 for 2000). The population base and method have been revised, and the official
  reports' counts should not be set beside current UIS counts as if they measured the same thing.
- AUDIT_02 (`AUDIT_02.md:80-81`, F12-F13): the quotations are confirmed in both mirror copies. The "far short"
  sentence in Expanded Commentary para. 4 is about the Jomtien vision in general and does not test the
  adult-literacy halving.
- Provenance: I read the University of Minnesota Human Rights Library mirror (archived 2023-02-01) and a
  five-page PDF at bice.org. The official UNESCO copy (unesdoc pf0000121147) was walled (facet F:197, F:349).
- My own note, not in the record: goal (iv) is worded as "a 50 per cent improvement in levels of adult
  literacy", not as halving illiteracy. The 2015 monitoring report reads it as "halving illiteracy" (facet
  F:211). At world level only that reading is feasible: 1.5 times the 2000 literacy rate of 81.12% (UIS,
  facet F:250) is 121.68%, which I computed this session. Still, the manuscript's line 109 quotes Dakar's
  wording and then the report's gloss, and a careful reader may notice that the two differ.
- The passages I read do not define literacy (facet F:206).
