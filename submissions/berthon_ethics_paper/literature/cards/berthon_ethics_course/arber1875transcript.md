## Summary
Arber's edition of the Stationers' Company registers, volume 1, with a long introduction. I read the charter of incorporation, printed in Latin with Arber's English translation (Introduction, pp. xxviii–xxxii), Arber's statements of its date, and the 1643 petition in the Addendum. The charter, granted by Philip and Mary, names the ground as "seditious and heretical books rhymes and treatises" daily printed (p. xxviii), incorporates the named freemen under a Master and two Wardens (pp. xxviii–xxix), forbids anyone to print "any book or any thing for sale or traffic" in the realm unless a member of the Company or licensed "by the letters patent" of the Crown (pp. xxx–xxxi), and lets the Master and Wardens search any printer's, binder's or bookseller's premises and "seize, take, hold, burn" books printed against any statute, act or proclamation, with three months' imprisonment and a 100-shilling forfeit for offenders (p. xxxi). In the Addendum, the Company petitions the House of Commons against eleven of its own members who sought the sole printing of a new annotated Bible, calling them "Pattentees, and Monopolizers" (p. 583). The text is archive.org OCR, with damage throughout.

## Key facts it relies on
- Ground of the grant: "certain seditious and heretical books rhymes and treatises are daily published and printed by divers scandalous malicious schismatical and heretical persons" (p. xxviii).
- Monopoly clause: no one shall "practise or exercise ... the art or mistery of printing any book or any thing for sale or traffic within this our realm of England or the dominions of the same, unless the same person ... is or shall be one of the community of the foresaid mistery or art of Stationery ... or has therefore licence of us ... by the letters patent" (pp. xxx–xxxi).
- Search and seizure: the Master and Keepers or Wardens may "make search whenever it shall please them in any place, shop, house, chamber, or building of any printer, binder or bookseller" and "seize, take, hold, burn" books printed contrary to law (p. xxxi).
- Penalties: imprisonment "without bail for the space of three months" and a forfeit of "a hundred shillings", half to the Crown and half to the Company (p. xxxi).
- Date: "the fourth day of May", enrolled "Pat. 3 & 4 Phil. & Mar." (p. xxxii). Arber dates the incorporation "4th May 1556" in his introduction (e.g., arber1.txt l. 1707, 1837) and in his contents ("4 May 1666" in the OCR, l. 560).
- Arber's own brackets date "the iiijth and 5th yeares" of the reign to 1557 (l. 3180, 3217), which puts a 4 May in the 3rd and 4th years in 1557.
- 1643 petition, "[January 1643]", "To the honourable House of Commons": "a very few Persons of the said Company, but Eleven in number ... labouring to acquier the Printing and profit thereof to themselves" (p. 583).
- The same petition: "these Eleven men ... are now, and have bin, (many of them) Pattentees, and Monopolizers of Printing the most vendable Bookes of worth and quantity now in use" (p. 583).
- Page markers in the OCR ("I. NNN") are page footers: the contents table puts the charter at pp. xxviii–xxxii and the 1557 enrollment at p. 496, and both texts fall after the preceding page's marker. So the petition text between "I. 582" and "I. 583" is on p. 583.

## Critical notes from the literature
- Date: Arber says 4 May 1556; the regnal formula puts 4 May in 1557 (AUDIT_01.md B2; review/R4_history.md:84; ledger P4-002). The manuscript gives 1557 "by the regnal year" without noting Arber's 1556.
- The charter admits royal patentees as well as members (R4_history.md:85; ledger P4-003); the 1643 dispute is about patentees.
- The seizure power is over books, not presses (R4_history.md:86).
- facets/B_print_1450_1800.md:59 assigns "Eleven in number" to p. 582; by the footer reading above it is on p. 583. Arber's "[January 1643]" may be Old Style (ledger P4-010).
- MS l. 161 reverses the petition's direction (ledger P5-008, "wrong"); that line does not cite Arber and is outside this card.
