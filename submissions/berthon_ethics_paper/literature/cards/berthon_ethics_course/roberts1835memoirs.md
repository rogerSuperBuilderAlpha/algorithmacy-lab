## Summary
William Roberts's *Memoirs* collect Hannah More's letters with a connecting narrative. I read the title page and
More's letter of 1801 to the Bishop of Bath and Wells, Dr. Beadon (pp. 67-73), in the Harper two-volume edition,
vol. II, 1835. The letter answers the Blagdon controversy: the curate Mr. Bere and pamphleteers had charged her
schools with "sedition, disaffection" and fanaticism (p. 67), and she defends the schoolmaster Young, her
churchmanship and her politics (pp. 68-71). She then describes her schools. When she settled in the district
"thirteen years ago" there were almost no Sunday schools; she founded none without "the hearty concurrence of the
clergyman of the parish" (p. 72). Her plan "is extremely simple and limited": week-day work fit for servants, no
writing, and Scripture-based teaching aimed at "habits of industry and piety" (p. 72). She and her sisters teach
every Sunday; the books are the Mendip questions, the Church Catechism, spelling-books, Psalter, Common Prayer,
Testament and Bible, and parents are invited to a Sunday-evening reading (pp. 72-73). The rest of the volume is not
on this card.

## Key facts it relies on
- Title page: "Memoirs of the Life and Correspondence of Mrs. Hannah More. By William Roberts, Esq. … In two volumes.
  Vol. II. New-York: Published by Harper & Brothers … 1835."
- Letter heading: "From Mrs. H. More to the Bishop of Bath and Wells (Dr. Beadon). 1801." (p. 67).
- The charge she answers: "sedition, disaffection, and a general aim to corrupt the principles of the community"
  (p. 67).
- "Not one school here did I ever attempt to establish without the hearty concurrence of the clergyman of the
  parish" (p. 72).
- "My plan of instruction is extremely simple and limited. They learn, on week days, such coarse works as may fit
  them for servants. I allow of no writing for the poor. My object is not to make fanatics, but to train up the lower
  classes in habits of industry and piety" (p. 72).
- "My sisters and I always teach them ourselves every Sunday, except during our absence in winter" (p. 72).
- Books used: "Questions for the Mendip Schools", "The Church Catechism", "Spelling-books, Psalter, Common Prayer,
  Testament, Bible" (pp. 72-73).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet C (`literature/facets/C_literacy_withheld.md`, entry 14) read the same passage and notes that she states an
  aim (servants, "industry and piety", not "fanatics") but gives no reason for excluding writing in particular, and
  that the letter is a defence against charges of sedition and Methodism, not a general statement of educational
  philosophy.
- AUDIT_01 row C15 confirmed the quotation but had not checked the page; this card confirms p. 72.
- The arm's bib URL (`memoirslifeandc02robegoog`) is catalogued on archive.org as vol. 1, 1834, but its title page
  reads vol. II, Harper, 1835, and its text also contains the passage. I used `memoirslifeandc01robegoog`, a second
  scan of the same edition, also titled vol. II, 1835. Page 72 is the same in both scans.
