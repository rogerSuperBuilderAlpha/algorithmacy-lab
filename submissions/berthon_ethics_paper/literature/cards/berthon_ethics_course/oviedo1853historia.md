## Summary
Oviedo, royal chronicler and the *veedor* (overseer of gold smelting) on Pedrarias Dávila's 1514 expedition, describes the first landing at Santa Marta. Pedrarias handed him the requirimiento in writing to read to any Indians found, "como si yo entendiera á los indios" or had someone to make them understand (p. 27); he prints the text in full with its signatories, Palacios Rubios among them (pp. 28-29). On Wednesday 14 June 1514 "mas de tresçientos" (more than three hundred) men landed with it; the friars the text names stayed on the ships (p. 29). After a skirmish and the capture of ten women, Oviedo told Pedrarias before the captains that the Indians would not listen to "la teología deste requirimiento", nor had he anyone to explain it, and proposed keeping it until they had an Indian "en una jaula" to learn it slowly; Pedrarias took it "con mucha risa", as did all who heard (p. 31). Oviedo wished it had been made understood first, but it was treated as superfluous (p. 31). In 1516 he asked Palacios Rubios whether Christian conscience was satisfied by it; the author said yes, if done as the text says, and laughed often at Oviedo's stories (pp. 31-32). Oviedo wishes the text had said how long the "tiempo que fuere justo" was to be, says he will not decide whether it was kept, and promises to tell later "el tiempo que los capitanes les daban": reading the text to Indians already seized and tied (p. 32).

## Key facts it relies on
- Pp. 27-28: "mandó el gobernador que yo llevasse el requirimiento in scriptis que se avia de hacer á los indios, é me lo dió de su mano, como si yo entendiera á los indios, para se lo leer, ó tuviéramos allí quien se lo diera á entender, queriéndolo ellos oyr: pues mostrarles el papel, en que estaba escripto, poco hacia al caso".
- P. 29: the reading party landed "el miércoles siguiente catorçe de junio de mill é quinientos y catorçe"; "los religiosos predicadores, que el requirimiento diçe, se quedaron en los navios hasta ver cómo subçedian las cosas".
- P. 31: "Señor: parésçeme que estos indios no quieren escuchar la teología deste requirimiento, ni vos tenes quien se la dé á entender: mande vuestra merçed guardalle, hasta que tengamos algún indio destos en una jaula, para que despaçio lo aprenda é el señor obispo se lo dé á entender." Pedrarias "lo tomó con mucha risa".
- P. 31: "Quisiera yo que aquel requirimiento se les hiçiera entender primero; pero como cosa excusada ó por demás, no se tractó dello".
- Pp. 31-32: Palacios Rubios "díxome que sí, si se hiçiesse como el requirimiento lo diçe. Mas parésçeme que se reía muchas veçes"; Oviedo could laugh at him "si pensaba que lo que diçe aquel requirimiento lo avían de entender los indios, sin discurso de años é tiempo".
- P. 32: "Adelante se dirá el tiempo que los capitanes les daban, atando los indios después de salteados, y en tanto leyéndoles toda aquella capitulaçion del requirimiento."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Oviedo was an officer of the expedition he describes, so his account is that of a participant; Las Casas (`lascasas1876historia`, pp. 167, 198-199) corroborates non-comprehension from outside it. I did not read the wider literature on Oviedo's reliability.
- Helps (1855, `helps1855spanish`, p. 387) translates the pp. 27-28 sentence; the OpenAlex abstract field for Faudree 2015 (doi:10.1080/10609164.2016.1150029) begins with the Palacios Rubios exchange in English.
