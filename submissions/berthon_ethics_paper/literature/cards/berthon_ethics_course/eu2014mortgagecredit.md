## Summary
The Mortgage Credit Directive sets a common framework for consumer credit secured by a mortgage or
otherwise relating to residential immovable property, "including an obligation to carry out a
creditworthiness assessment before granting a credit" (art. 1). Chapter 4 governs "information and
practices preliminary to the conclusion of the credit agreement". Art. 14 requires personalised
pre-contractual information "in good time before the consumer is bound by any credit agreement or offer".
Art. 16 requires creditors, credit intermediaries or appointed representatives to "provide adequate
explanations" of proposed agreements and ancillary services "in order to place the consumer in a position
enabling him to assess" whether they suit his needs and financial situation, and lets Member States adapt
the manner, the extent and "by whom it is given". Art. 6 asks Member States to promote financial education
on responsible borrowing. Art. 18 requires a thorough creditworthiness assessment before conclusion and,
on rejection, notice "where applicable, that the decision is based on automated processing of data"
(art. 18(5)(c)). The recitals touch notaries only at the margins: parties may choose appraisers or notaries
by agreement, notarial costs fall outside the cost of credit, and notaries are excluded from the definition
of credit intermediary.

## Key facts it relies on
- Art. 1: the framework covers mortgage-related consumer credit, "including an obligation to carry out a creditworthiness assessment before granting a credit".
- Art. 6(1): Member States "shall promote measures that support the education of consumers in relation to responsible borrowing and debt management".
- Art. 14(1)(b): personalised information must be given "in good time before the consumer is bound by any credit agreement or offer".
- Art. 16(1): creditors, credit intermediaries or appointed representatives must "provide adequate explanations to the consumer on the proposed credit agreements and any ancillary services, in order to place the consumer in a position enabling him to assess whether the proposed credit agreements and ancillary services are adapted to his needs and financial situation".
- Art. 16(1)(a)-(d): explanations include the pre-contractual information, the essential characteristics, the specific effects including "the consequences of default in payment", and bundling.
- Art. 16(2): Member States "may adapt the manner by which and the extent to which the explanations ... is given, as well as by whom it is given".
- Art. 18(1): a thorough creditworthiness assessment "before concluding a credit agreement"; art. 18(5)(c): on rejection, the creditor says, "where applicable, that the decision is based on automated processing of data".
- Recital passage (searched): the Directive provides "that the consumer receives adequate explanations before concluding the credit agreement".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The C2 resolution (`review/v3/resolutions/C2_law.md`, P5-036) used art. 16(1)-(2) with the Consumer Credit Directive to narrow the paper's claim that no rule requires anyone to "read, explain, and certify" understanding: the law requires explanation, not reading or certification. It flagged the change as one for the author.
- FIX_CHECK_B (P5-036, `review/v3/FIX_CHECK_B.md`) passed the v3 sentence and noted that "2014/17 art. 16 itself states no timing (recital: 'before concluding the credit agreement')" and that the manuscript says "before signing".
- From my reading: art. 16(2) lets Member States decide "by whom" the explanation is given, which leaves room for a third party, though the Directive names none.
