## Summary
The decree, sanctioned by the Emperor on 9 January 1881 on the minister Barão Homem de Mello's
countersignature, reformed Brazil's electoral law. Art. 1 made elections for senators, deputies,
provincial assemblies and other elective offices "directas", with all citizens enrolled as electors under
the law taking part. Art. 2 set the elector's qualification by reference to the Constitution of the Empire
and a net annual income of at least 200$ from land, industry, commerce or employment. Art. 6 set up the
enrolment procedure under municipal and district judges: no citizen could be enrolled without a written
request signed by himself "ou de especial procurador" (§ 4), and each voter's title was to record "a
circumstancia de saber ou não ler e escrever" (§ 14). Art. 8 provided for an annual revision of the rolls
from the first working day of September 1882, at which new citizens would be included only if they proved
the qualifications "e souberem ler e escrever" (art. 8, II), proof of literacy being the citizen's own
handwriting and signature, recognised by a tabellião (notary) (§ 1). The decree thus left literate and
illiterate electors on the first roll and closed later entry to the illiterate.

## Key facts it relies on
- Art. 1: direct elections, in which "tomarão parte todos os cidadãos alistados eleitores de conformidade com esta lei".
- Art. 2: an elector must have "renda liquida annual não inferior a 200$ por bens de raiz, industria, commercio ou emprego".
- Art. 6 § 4: "Nenhum cidadão será incluido no alistamento dos eleitores sem o ter requerido por escripto e com assignatura sua ou de especial procurador".
- Art. 6 § 14: the voter's title records, among other particulars, "a circumstancia de saber ou não ler e escrever".
- Art. 6 § 15: on receiving the title, an elector "que não souber ou não puder escrever" may have another sign for him.
- Art. 8 (opening): revision of the general roll "No primeiro dia util do mez de Setembro de 1882, e de então em diante todos os annos em igual dia".
- Art. 8, II: inclusion of citizens who prove the qualifications "e souberem ler e escrever".
- Art. 8 § 1: proof of literacy "pela lettra e assignatura do cidadão que requerer a sua inclusão no alistamento, uma vez que a lettra e firma estejam reconhecidas por tabellião".
- Closing formula: "Palacio do Rio de Janeiro em 9 de Janeiro de 1881"; published at the Secretaria de Estado dos Negocios do Imperio on 10 January 1881.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The C1 resolution (`review/v3/resolutions/C1_historical.md`, P3-019) judged the v2 sentence "made reading and writing a condition of enrolment" narrower than the decree: literacy applied at the revisions from September 1882, not at the first enrolment, where a proxy could sign (art. 6 § 4). The v3 text adds "new" and "from 1882".
- Engerman and Sokoloff (2001) date Brazil's literacy qualification to 1891, the Republic's constitution (ledger P3-019; facet M5:260 reads 1891 as a re-enactment). Ledger row P3-N07 records that a reader who checks Engerman and Sokoloff will find 1891, not 1881; v3 l. 71 now states the difference.
- Aleixo and Kramer (2010, p. 69) read art. 8, II the same way (C1 resolution, P3-019, from `pdfs/D/br_unb.txt`). I did not open that file this session.
- From my reading: the decree also kept an income qualification (art. 2), so literacy was one gate among several, and its proof ran through a notary's recognition of the applicant's handwriting (art. 8 § 1).
