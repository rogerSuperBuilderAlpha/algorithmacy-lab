## Summary
Promulgated on 15 May 1985 by the presiding boards of the Chamber of Deputies and the Federal Senate under
art. 49 of the Constitution, the amendment rewrote a set of constitutional provisions on elections and
parties during the return to civilian rule. Art. 1 gave new text to, among others, art. 74 (direct,
secret, universal election of the President and Vice-President), art. 147 (electors) and art. 150
(ineligibility), and opened art. 152 to the free creation of political parties. The new art. 147 § 3
lists those who "Não poderão alistar-se eleitores": persons who cannot express themselves in the national
language, and persons deprived of political rights. Illiterates are not on that list, and the new § 4
provides that "A Lei disporá sobre a forma pela qual possam os analfabetos alistar-se eleitores e exercer
o direito de voto." The new art. 150 keeps illiterates out of office: "São inelegíveis os inalistáveis e os
analfabetos." Arts. 2-7 are transitional rules for the municipal elections of 15 November 1985 and the
Federal District's first representation in 1986; art. 8 revokes several provisions.

## Key facts it relies on
- Preamble: the Mesas of the Câmara and Senado "nos termos do art. 49 da Constituição Federal, promulgam a seguinte Emenda".
- Art. 1, new art. 74: President and Vice-President elected "por sufrágio universal e voto direto e secreto".
- Art. 1, new art. 147 caput: "São eleitores os brasileiros que, à data da eleição, contém dezoito anos ou mais, alistados na forma da Lei."
- Art. 1, new art. 147 § 3: "Não poderão alistar-se eleitores: a) os que no saibam exprimir-se na língua nacional; e b) os que estiverem privados, temporária ou definitivamente, dos direitos políticos." (The Planalto text reads "no saibam".)
- Art. 1, new art. 147 § 4: "A Lei disporá sobre a forma pela qual possam os analfabetos alistar-se eleitores e exercer o direito de voto."
- Art. 1, new art. 150: "São inelegíveis os inalistáveis e os analfabetos."
- Art. 1, new art. 152: "É livre a criação de Partidos Políticos."
- Closing: "Brasília, em 15 de maio de 1985", signed for the Câmara by Ulysses Guimarães and for the Senado by José Fragelli.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The C1 resolution (`review/v3/resolutions/C1_historical.md`, P3-020) rests "until 1985" on this amendment and on Aleixo and Kramer (2010, p. 68), who say that EC 25 "e, em seguida, a Constituição Federal de 5 de outubro de 1988 repararam uma secular injustiça". I did not open Aleixo and Kramer this session.
- Engerman and Sokoloff (2001) say the restriction "endured until 1988" (ledger P3-020, from `pdfs/audit5/M5/w8512.txt`). Ledger P3-N07 and the C1 resolution treat 1988 as the Constitution that confirmed the change; v3 l. 71 now says so.
- Ledger P3-020 notes, from facet D:495, that the 1985 change made enrolment optional for illiterates and left them ineligible for office. The ineligibility is in the new art. 150. The "optional" point is not in the amendment's text: § 4 leaves the form of enrolment to ordinary law, which I did not open.
- From my reading: the amendment lifts the bar by omission and delegation. It does not itself state that illiterates were barred before, so the "until 1985" claim needs the prior text or a secondary source for its first half.
