## Summary
An elderly gentleman, Mackinnon, signed the back of a bill of exchange for £3,000. Callow, the bill's acceptor, told him it was a guarantee like one he had signed before. Callow showed him only the back of the paper. The bill passed through later indorsers to Foster, a holder for value without notice of fraud, who sued Mackinnon as indorser. The trial judge directed the jury that Mackinnon was entitled to the verdict if he signed without knowing it was a bill, believing it a guarantee, and without negligence. The jury found for him. On a rule for a new trial, the full Court of Common Pleas (Byles J.) held the direction right. Where a signer is "deceived, not merely as to the legal effect, but as to the actual contents of the instrument", his mind does not go with his signature, and the signature is "of no force". The Court states the principle through the old rule for a blind or illiterate man who has a contract "falsely read over to him", and *Thoroughgood's Case*. It extends the principle from deeds to other written contracts, qualified for negotiable instruments signed in blank. On the second ground, that the verdict was against the evidence, the Court ordered a new trial without giving reasons (pp. 713-714).

## Key facts it relies on
- Headnote (p. 704): the defendant "was induced to put his name upon the back of a bill of exchange by the fraudulent representation of the acceptor that he was signing a guarantee". The action was "by indorsee against indorser" at the suit of "a bona fide holder for value".
- Facts (pp. 704-705): the bill was drawn by Cooper and "accepted by one Callow", indorsed by Cooper, the defendant, Parker, Pooley & Co. and A. G. Pooley "to the plaintiff". Callow "only shewed the defendant the back of the paper".
- Judgment, Byles J. (p. 710): the judgment of the Court (Bovill C.J., Byles, Keating, Montague Smith JJ.) was delivered on 5 July.
- p. 711: "if a blind man, or a man who cannot read, or who for some reason (not implying negligence) forbears to read, has a written contract falsely read over to him, the reader misreading to such a degree that the written contract is of a nature altogether different from the contract pretended to be read ... then, at least if there be no negligence, the signature so obtained is of no force."
- p. 711: it is invalid "not merely on the ground of fraud ... but on the ground that the mind of the signer did not accompany the signature".
- p. 711: "In Thoroughgood's Case it was held that, if an illiterate man have a deed falsely read over to him, and he then seals and delivers the parchment, that parchment is nevertheless not his deed." Of a case in Keilwey: "the grantee himself was the defrauding party".
- p. 712: "These cases apply to deeds; but the principle is equally applicable to other written contracts". For negotiable instruments it is limited to protect innocent transferees, as where a man signs a blank bill-stamp.
- pp. 713-714: "the direction of the Lord Chief Justice was right". On the second branch, "the case should undergo further investigation". The rule was made "absolute for a new trial".

## Critical notes from the literature
- The Latin name of the plea, *non est factum*, does not appear in the judgment I read. The Court decides the point "on the traverse of the indorsement" and through the deed cases. The plea's name comes from the later doctrine (see Connell 2016 via facet G; `audit/AUDIT_03.md`:74).
- The misreader in Foster was the acceptor, Callow, a party to the bill, and not a neutral third-party reader. Facet G and the adjudication table use this to conclude that the English cases do not show an independent reader (`facets/G_third_parties.md`:216, :230; `facets/T_adjudication.md`:127; `audit/AUDIT_03.md`:73, G3 CONFIRMED).
- Facet G also notes that the law cut both ways. Foster protected the blind or illiterate signer, but the House of Lords in *Saunders v Anglia* (via Connell) refused the plea to a widow who relied on her nephew's associate (`facets/G_third_parties.md`:252). *Thoroughgood's Case* itself was not opened, and it is known only through Byles J.'s summary (`facets/G_third_parties.md`:63-64; `audit/AUDIT_03.md`:109).
- Pagination: the OCR loses the "711" running head, but the passage sits between heads 710 and 712. AUDIT_03 G1 and ledger P3-066 place the quotation at 711. The report ends at p. 714, not p. 712 as in the bib.
- Outcome: the holding approves the jury direction, but the case went to a new trial. It is not a final judgment for Mackinnon.

"Was a party to the bill" or naming Callow as acceptor would be exact (ledger P3-067).
