## Summary
This card rests on the abstract only; the repository returned 403 to my requests. Willis, a consumer-law scholar,
argues that the dominant US model for regulating consumer credit, insurance and investment products is "disclosure
and unfettered choice", and that policymakers have embraced financial-literacy education "as a necessary corollary
to the disclosure model". The belief that education makes consumers "responsible" and "empowered" market players
"lacks empirical support" and is "implausible, given the velocity of change in the financial marketplace, the gulf
between current consumer skills and those needed ..., the persistence of biases ..., and the disparity between
educators and financial services firms in resources". She says the pursuit has costs that "almost certainly swamp
any benefits": education can "increase confidence without improving ability", and "the regulation through education
model blames them for their plight, shaming them and deflecting calls for effective market regulation". Consumers
"generally do not serve as their own doctors and lawyers"; the search for effective education "should be replaced
by a search for policies more conducive to good consumer financial outcomes" (all abstract).

## Key facts it relies on
- Education is "a necessary corollary to the disclosure model of regulation" (abstract).
- The belief in its effectiveness "lacks empirical support" (abstract).
- Implausible given "the velocity of change in the financial marketplace" and "the disparity between educators and
  financial services firms in resources" (abstract).
- "For some consumers, financial education appears to increase confidence without improving ability, leading to
  worse decisions" (abstract).
- "the regulation through education model blames them for their plight, shaming them and deflecting calls for
  effective market regulation" (abstract).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Ben-Shahar and Schneider cite her (94 Iowa L. Rev. 197, 208-09) for studies finding "no effect or small
  paradoxical results" (sister arm `L_disclosure_and_choice.md`, entry 6).

  support" claim for financial education (`V5_scaffolding.md`, Q5).
