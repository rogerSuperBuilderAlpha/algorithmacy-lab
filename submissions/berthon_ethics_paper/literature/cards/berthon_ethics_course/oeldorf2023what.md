## Summary
A review of 50 articles on users' understanding of social-media algorithms (preprint p. 6). The authors report
two working definitions of algorithmic literacy: DeVito's "capacity and opportunity to be aware of both the
presence and impact of algorithmically-driven systems ... and the capacity and opportunity to crystalize this
understanding into a strategic use of these systems", and Dogruel et al.'s "being aware of the use of algorithms
..., knowing how algorithms work, being able to critically evaluate algorithmic decision-making as well as having
the skills to cope with or even influence algorithmic operations" (p. 6). They trace a short history from
awareness to knowledge, critique and skill, and propose four research directions, the first being "balancing
users' expectations of algorithmic literacy with developers' responsibility for algorithmic transparency"
(p. 2). Users who knew posts were filtered "did not understand how such filtering worked, or why it was done,
which meant they had little power to influence or leverage it" (p. 10). They concede that "the current reality is
that individuals must endure most of effort in improving literacy" (p. 23), and name "the new algorithmic divide".
