## Summary
Phule's statement to the Indian Education (Hunter) Commission, printed in the Bombay evidence volume between
pp. 140 and 145 and signed "Poona; JOTEERAO GOVINDRAO PHOOLEY, [19]th October 1882. Merchant and Cultivator,
and Municipal Commissioner" (p. 145). He opens with his experience: a girls' school he founded with his wife
about 1854 and an "indigenous mixed school for the lower classes, especially the Mahars and Mangs" (pp. 140–141).
He reproduces the English preface of an earlier Marathi book (pp. 141–142) charging that Government funds higher
education, which the Brahmins monopolize, while the ryots who pay the revenue get almost nothing. On primary
education he argues that nine-tenths of villages lack schools, that the cess is misspent, that primary
education should be compulsory to about age 12, and that Mahars, Mangs and other lower classes are "practically
excluded from all schools owing to caste prejudices", with Government special schools "only in large towns"
(p. 142). He invokes "the promise of the Queen's Proclamation" for separate schools (p. 142), asks for teachers
drawn from the cultivating classes rather than Brahmins (pp. 142–143), and closes on higher education,
scholarships and female education (pp. 144–145).

## Key facts it relies on
- Heading in the volume: "A Statement for the information of the Education Commission" (p. 140); the volume's
  section is "Memorials addressed to the Education Commission" (title page).
- Signature and date: "Poona; JOTEERAO GOVINDRAO PHOOLEY, Idth October 1882" (OCR, p. 145); the Round Table
  India reproduction reads "19th October 1882".
- He founded a girls' school about 1854 and a mixed school for "the Mahars and Mangs" (pp. 140–141).
- "The Ma[h]ars, Mangs, and other lower classes are practically excluded from all schools owing to caste
  prejudices, as they are not allowed to sit by the children of higher castes" (p. 142).
- "Consequently special schools for these have been opened by Government. But these exist only in large towns";
  in Poona one school with under 30 boys (p. 142).
- "Under the promise of the Queen's Proclamation I beg to urge that Mahars, Mangs, and other lower classes,
  where their number is large enough, should have separate schools for them" (p. 142).
- Primary education "should be made compulsory up to a certain age, say at least 12 years" (p. 142; OCR
  "1£", reproduction "12").
- "The teachers now employed in the primary schools are almost all Brahmins" (p. 142).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet M5 (`literature/facets/M5_wider_cases.md:128-133`) read the statement only in the Round Table India
  reproduction (from *Selected Writings of Jotirao Phule*, 2002) and uses it as a contemporary objector invoking
  the ruler's own standard. It flags that Phule's *Gulamgiri*, Ambedkar and the Adam reports were not opened, so
  the wider claim that Phule named the withholding of learning a wrong rests on this one text.
- Dating: the reproduction's header says 1881; AUDIT_05 M5-34 (`literature/audit/AUDIT_05.md:722`) found the
  signature "19th October 1882". The 1884 volume confirms October 1882 (OCR "Idth", p. 145).
- The statement is one witness's evidence to a commission, from Poona. It describes Bombay Presidency practice,
  not British India as a whole.
- Phule's own wording is that Government *opened* special schools, but too few. "Tolerated" fits the general
  schools' exclusion; it understates that Government made partial provision (ledger P3-046).
- OCR noise: the 1884 OCR reads "Makars" for "Mahars" in the quoted sentence and "Idth" for "19th"; the page
  images were not checked.
