## Summary
A historical-linguistics study of how Castilian notarial documents of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries record whether participants could sign. Almeida Cabrejas analyses 932 documents of those centuries in the CODEA corpus (the abstract says "unos 1000"), sorted as probatory, dispositive and petitionary, and by whether a professional or a non-professional wrote them (pp. 21–23). Private notarial documents begin to carry the signature of the party granting the act (the otorgante) from 1504, and she ties the change to the Pragmática of Alcalá of 7 June 1503, which reorganised the notariat, required a register of full deeds, and ordered notaries to read their notes before the parties and witnesses, with a witness or another literate person signing for a party who could not (pp. 23–24). She quotes the law at second hand, through Marchant Rivera (2015) and the 1777 *Recopilación*. The paper then catalogues the formulas for inability to sign ("dijo no saber firmar", "a su ruego"; pp. 28–29), problems with women's non-signatures (p. 30), and occupational and dowry patterns, finding no clear link between dowry size and signing (pp. 38–39). Petitions show unofficial and even "false" signatures (pp. 27–28).

## Key facts it relies on
- Corpus: 932 CODEA documents of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries were analysed (p. 22); the abstract gives "unos 1000" (p. 21).
- The first CODEA document with the grantor's signature is from Ávila, 1504 (CODEA 2273) (p. 23).
- The Pragmática of Alcalá de Henares, 7 June 1503, required notaries to keep a protocol "que recibe el nombre de registro" with the full deeds and the grantors' signatures (pp. 23–24).
- The law, as quoted from Marchant Rivera (2015, p. 335): "los dichos escrivanos las lean presentes las partes e los testigos ... E sy no supieren firmar, fyrme por ellos qual quiera de los testygos, o otro que sepa escrivir" (p. 24).
- The notary must record that a witness signed for a party "que no sabìa escrevir" (*Recopilación*, Madrid 1777, tít. 25, ley XIII, p. 877, quoted p. 24).
- Witnesses in private documents sign only when a grantor cannot (pp. 24–25).
- Inability to sign, "con la consiguiente firma de uno de los testigos, como ordenaba la pragmática", is "muy frecuente también desde el inicio, dada la alta tasa de analfabetismo especialmente entre las mujeres" (p. 29).
- Petitions often carry "falsas firmas" written by the scribe, not meant to deceive (p. 27).
- Dowry size and the husband's ability to sign show no clear relation; data are "aún insuficientes" (pp. 38–39).
