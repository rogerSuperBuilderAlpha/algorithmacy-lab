## Summary
The UINL's *Principles of notarial ethics* set out ten rules of conduct that member notariats undertake to apply
through national systems of sanctions, overseen by an ad hoc compliance committee in each country (final
considerations). The principles cover competence and continuing preparation (1), office facilities and
availability (2), collegial relations (3), fair competition without fee-cutting or canvassing (4), a ban on
personal advertising (5) and free choice of notary (6). Principle 7 requires a personal relationship of trust,
verification of the parties' identities and capacity, and that notaries "give expression to their wishes".
Principle 8 imposes professional confidentiality. Principle 9, impartiality and independence, requires notaries to
"balance the respective interests of the parties concerned" with "the sole objective of safeguarding both
parties". Principle 10, diligence, requires them to inform and advise the parties of the consequences of their
instructions and to ensure the parties' "awareness of the legal force of the deed". The General Meeting of Member
Chambers approved the text in Mexico City on 17 October 2004.

## Key facts it relies on
- Principle 6: "In the pursuance of their duty of impartiality notaries must refrain from any action that might
  directly or indirectly influence the free choice of a notary enjoyed by all."
- Principle 7: "Notaries must always verify the identities of parties and the capacity in which they are acting.
  They must also give expression to their wishes."
- Principle 9: notaries must act "with impartiality and independence, avoiding all personal influence over their
  activities and any form of discrimination against clients."
- Principle 9: "When acting in their official capacity notaries must balance the respective interests of the
  parties concerned and seek a solution with the sole objective of safeguarding both parties."
- Principle 10: notaries "must inform and advise the parties as to the possible consequences of their
  instructions ... [and] provide the parties with any clarification requested or necessary to ensure conformity
  with decisions taken and awareness of the legal force of the deed."
- Final considerations: enforcement "by means of an appropriate system of sanctions" and an "ad hoc" compliance
  committee in every country.
- Footer: "Approved by the General Meeting of Member Chambers of Notaries of the UINL Mexico City, Mexico – 17th
  October 2004".

## Critical notes from the literature
- It is the union's own statement of its norm. Facet G found reviews reporting that colonial Cuzco notaries acted
  "for both litigants and judges" and could be moved by clients (Andrien on Burns); norm and practice differ
  (`literature/facets/M3_intermediaries_records.md`:194).
- The norm belongs to the civil-law (Latin) notariat; the English notary tradition differs, and facet M3 did not
  search the national statutes behind the UINL text (M3:194).
- The weaker-party duty is not in this document. It is in the companion *Principles of the notarial function*
  (Rome, 8 November 2005, Part I): impartiality "can also take the form of lending adequate assistance to
  whichever of the parties might be in a position of inferiority" (`pdfs/M3/uinl_905bff.html`; AUDIT_05, M3-13).
