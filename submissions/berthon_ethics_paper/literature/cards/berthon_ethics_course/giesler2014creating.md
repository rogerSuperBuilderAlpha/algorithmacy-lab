## Summary
Giesler and Veresiu argue against the usual view that responsible consumption stems from consumers' growing awareness. They hold that it "requires the active creation and management of consumers as moral subjects" (abstract, p. 840). From the sociology of governmentality, in particular Shamir's "responsibilization", they build the P.A.C.T. routine: personalization, authorization, capabilization and transformation. These are four processes "through which responsibility is shifted away from the state and corporations and the responsible consumer subject is shaped" (p. 841). Personalization moves a social problem "from the systemic to the level of individual decision making" (p. 843). Their data are all World Economic Forum materials from 2004 to 2013 (about 490 GB) and longitudinal in-depth interviews with 15 corporate participants, transcribed to 764 pages (p. 844). From these they trace how the WEF created four responsible-consumer subjects: the bottom-of-the-pyramid consumer, the green consumer, the health-conscious consumer and the financially literate consumer. Each carries "responsibility for the solution of a particular historical problem" (p. 845). In the financial case, WEF chairman Klaus Schwab shifted the problem of debt "away from the level of better financial regulation to a need for more conscious individual financial decision makers" (p. 851). Delegates cast regulation as a "bureaucratic straightjacket". The authors limit their findings to "the creation of responsibility as a consumption system" (p. 854).

## Key facts it relies on
- Abstract (p. 840): "responsible consumption requires the active creation and management of consumers as moral subjects". The four subjects include "the financially literate consumer".
- p. 841: responsibilization occurs through "four distinct but interrelated processes through which responsibility is shifted away from the state and corporations and the responsible consumer subject is shaped".
- p. 843: personalization requires "a particular social, ecological, or financial problem to be shifted from the systemic to the level of individual decision making". In the neoliberal mythology, responsibility is "reassigned onto the individual agent".
- p. 844: data from WEF materials 2004-2013 and interviews "with 15 corporate participants between 2004 and 2012 ... up to four interviews per informant".
- p. 845: "Each consumer subject was created to carry responsibility for the solution of a particular historical problem".
- p. 851: "A fourth responsible-consumer type cultivated at the WEF is the financially literate consumer." The setting is the crises after 2008.
- p. 851: Schwab and the WEF "shifted the problem of financial debt away from the level of better financial regulation to a need for more conscious individual financial decision makers".
- p. 852: delegates cast legalistic solutions as a "bureaucratic straightjacket of incomprehensible rules" and promoted an "ethos of financial literacy" and school "financial literacy program[s]".
