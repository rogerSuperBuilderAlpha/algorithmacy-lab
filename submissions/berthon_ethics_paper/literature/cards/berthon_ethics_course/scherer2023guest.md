## Summary
The guest editors of BEQ's special issue define organized immaturity as "the reduction of individual capacities
for public use of reason constrained by sociotechnological systems" (p. 409) and trace it from Kant through
Foucault's pastoral power to data capitalism. Organized immaturity need not be planned; it "can emerge simply as
an effect of more or less subtle versions of paternalism", which "remains, in a political sense, a form of
interference with one's liberty" (p. 422). Remedies fall into "disorganizing immaturity" (resistance,
disconnection, education and self-help tools against surveillance; pp. 425-426) and "organizing maturity"
(strengthening positive freedoms; p. 426). They assign roles: the state should strengthen education first, then
regulation with consent provisions and "effective appeal processes", data property rights, watchdog NGOs and
public forums (p. 427); media and social movements inform and educate (pp. 427-428); business has "a responsibility to contribute to organizing individual and collective maturity" (p. 428), which, "Extending Matten and Crane's ideas", "could mean" among other things
"providing social rights by informing and training citizens in the appropriate use of social media and the pitfalls of
manipulative algorithms" (pp. 428-429; offered as "only a few illustrations", p. 429). They summarise the five articles, including Schade's finding that GDPR
transparency leads to "intransparencies and an infantilization of users" (p. 430), and close by inviting studies
of "the capacities of state and nonstate actors to educate individuals and collectives" (p. 432).

## Key facts it relies on
- Definition of organized immaturity (p. 409).
- Paternalism as a source of immaturity; "technological paternalism specific to data capitalism" (p. 422).
- Disorganizing immaturity: Harcourt's "combination of education, awareness building, self-help, and technical devices" (p. 426).
- State remedies, education listed first (p. 427).
- Business: "a responsibility to contribute to organizing individual and collective maturity" (p. 428); "informing and training citizens" and "training in argumentation processes and collective will formation" (p. 429).
- Schade on GDPR: transparency leads "paradoxically, to intransparencies and an infantilization of users (e.g., when one has to click through myriad detailed GDPR settings ...)" (p. 430).
- Ruehle: workplace nudging "in its paternalistic impetus, runs the risk of fostering tendencies of organized immaturity" (p. 430).
- Research call on educating capacities, "digital literacy or data literacy" (p. 432).

## Critical notes from the literature
- N5 reads it as BEQ's own programme, against which the paper's "educating the user addresses the wrong party" is a position (N5 §3a). V6 adds that the issue already asks business to train people (p. 429), so a duty to scaffold is not new in BEQ.
- Its frame is Kantian maturity of the individual and collective; it does not use literacy history or the triad.
