## Summary
An experimental survey (N = 500) of adults aged 50 and over who were invited to join a fictitious social network, NameDrop, as part of a "pre-launch evaluation" (p. 4786; design in abstract, p. 4779). The design repeats the authors' 2020 study of undergraduates (Obar and Oeldorf-Hirsch 2020, `obar2018biggest`), which this article summarises with its numbers (p. 4784). Most older adults took the clickwrap: 77.6% agreed to the privacy policy without opening it; those who opened the policies spent about 70 seconds on the privacy policy and 81.4 seconds on the terms (p. 4779). Most accepted policies containing planted "gotcha" clauses, including one requiring a kidney or other "redundant organ" as payment (p. 4779). The authors frame the result as a burden that transparency puts on the individual, "both perpetual data subject and overwhelmed privacy savior" (p. 4780), and they place the remedy on providers and policymakers, who "must do more to address the burden placed on individuals through deceptive interface designs like the clickwrap" and long, complicated policies (p. 4796). The methods and results sections (pp. 4785-4794) were not read line by line. The 2022 article's restatement is the only source this facet holds for the 2020 numbers.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 4779: N = 500 adults 50+; "77.6% chose the clickwrap"; reading time about 70 s (PP) and 81.4 s (TOS).
- p. 4779: "91.4% of participants accepted the NameDrop PP, which included data collection/sharing 'gotcha' clauses, while 83.4% accepted the TOS, including an extreme clause requiring users to provide a kidney or other 'redundant organ' in exchange for service."
- p. 4780: "a primary flaw of the data privacy transparency efforts of digital service providers is the impossible burden placed on the individual (McDonald & Cranor, 2008; Solove, 2012)".
- p. 4784 (2020 study, N = 543 undergraduates): 74% took the clickwrap; the PP "should have taken about 30 minutes to read", average 76 s, median 14 s; TOS needing 16 minutes, average 51 s, median 14 s.
- p. 4784: "Ninety-seven percent of participants agreed to the PP and 93% to the TOS, with 98% missing the gotcha clauses" (NSA data sharing; a first-born child as payment).
- p. 4784: quotes the 2020 article, participants aimed to "pursue the ends of digital production, without being inhibited by the means" (2020, p. 128).
- p. 4795: "417 of 500 participants would agree to donate a kidney or an eyeball for access"; the individual as "overburdened privacy savior".
- p. 4796: providers and policymakers "must do more to address the burden placed on individuals through deceptive interface designs like the clickwrap, and via TOS and privacy policies that are long and complicated."

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors state the limits (p. 4795): "participants knew that they were taking part in an academic study", so trust in the university may have raised acceptance; the sample skews younger, female and white relative to US adults 50+; and the fictitious employment site may have mattered little to this group.
- Sister facet L (`wt-dxd-foundation/.../facets/L_disclosure_and_choice.md`, entry 9) could not read the 2020 article and warned against citing its numbers from a search summary; this card supplies them from the authors' own restatement, which should still be checked against the 2020 article.
- "Solove, 2012" in the authors' citation is the SSRN-dated version of Solove (2013), `solove2013introduction`.
