Its credit line reads: North Carolina General Assembly, "An Act to Prevent all Persons from Teaching Slaves to Read or Write, the use of Figures Excepted." In *Acts Passed by the General Assembly of the State of North Carolina [1830-1831]*. Lawrence & Lemay, 1831. NC Digital Collections. The transcription gives no chapter number, no page, no ratification date and no signatures. I did not see the 1831 volume.

## Summary
ANCHOR's transcription gives a preamble and two enacting clauses. The preamble states the legislature's ground: teaching slaves to read and write "has a tendency to excite dissatisfaction in their minds and to produce insurrection and rebellion to the manifest injury of the citizens of this state". The first clause makes any free person who teaches or attempts to teach "any slave within this State to read or write, the use of figures excepted" liable to indictment. On conviction a white man or woman is fined one hundred to two hundred dollars or imprisoned. A free person of colour is "whipped at the discretion of the court not exceeding thirty nine lashes nor less than twenty lashes". The second clause punishes a slave who teaches another slave to read or write, figures again excepted, with thirty-nine lashes on conviction before a justice of the peace. The act reaches only slaves as pupils. Free people of colour may still be taught, and arithmetic is exempt. ANCHOR's headnote says the Assembly "first prohibited anyone from teaching slaves to read or write in 1818" and "strengthened the law in 1830". It also says a bill the next year barred enslaved and free people of colour from preaching where slaves of different families gathered. Those claims are ANCHOR's, not the act's.

## Key facts it relies on
- Preamble (transcription): "the teaching of slaves to read and write has a tendency to excite dissatisfaction in their minds and to produce insurrection and rebellion to the manifest injury of the citizens of this state".
- First clause: "any free person who shall hereafter teach or attempt to teach any slave within this State to read or write, the use of figures excepted, Shall be liable to indictment".
- Penalty, white offender: "be fined not less than one hundred dollars nor more than two hundred dollars or imprisoned".
- Penalty, free person of colour: "whipped at the discretion of the court not exceeding thirty nine lashes nor less than twenty lashes".
- Second clause: a slave who teaches "any other slave to read or write the use of figures excepted … shall be sentenced to receive thirty nine lashes on his or her bare back".
- Credit line: the volume is *Acts Passed by the General Assembly of the State of North Carolina [1830-1831]*, Lawrence & Lemay, 1831 (ANCHOR, "Credit text").
- No chapter, page or date appears in the transcription. Facet C (entry 5) reports that the *Revised Statutes* of 1837 cite the source acts in the margin as "1830, c. 6, s. 1" and "1830, c. 6, s. 2" (vol. 1, pp. 209, 578).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Resolution C1 (P3-071, "blocked" (b)) and FIX_CHECK_A ("REF P3-071 North Carolina", fail-unread): the volume the reference entry names was never opened. The page image is needed to confirm the chapter number (the ledger's "c. 6" is unconfirmed) and the page. FIX_CHECK_C (l. 95) flags that no locator is possible until then.
- The transcription and the 1837 consolidation differ on the penalty for a free person of colour. ANCHOR has "shall be whipped". The *Revised Statutes* (ch. XXXIV s. 74, p. 209) have "shall be fined, imprisoned or whipped, at the discretion of the court" (facet C, entry 5). The 1837 text also adds the giving or selling of "books or pamphlets" to slaves. Either ANCHOR's transcription is loose or the 1837 text changed the law; only the 1831 page image can settle which.
- ANCHOR calls the text "A Bill" in its heading and "An Act" in its citation. Facet C (entry 5) notes that NCpedia also prints it as a bill. Whether the transcribed text is the enrolled act is unconfirmed.
- Facet C ("Claims the sources contradict" 2) and ledger P3-029: North Carolina's exemption of "figures" makes the act narrower than a blanket ban on Black literacy.
