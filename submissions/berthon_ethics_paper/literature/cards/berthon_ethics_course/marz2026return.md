## Summary
Marz answers a 2025-2026 run of commentary claiming that screens and AI are returning culture to orality. She
names Derek Thompson's "handoff from a culture of literacy to a culture of orality", Joe Weisenthal's warning that
"the return to orality" will "fundamentally rewire the logic engine of the human brain", and James Marriott's
newsletter. These writers use Ong's primary orality "to describe the conversational nature of online
communication". Her reply is that this "is to ignore the main thrust of his work": Ong held that "literacy and
orality have been inextricably linked since writing began". She reports Ong's 1996 interview remark that online
writing "comes not in oral-aural format but through the eye, and thus is not directly oral at all", and his term
"secondary literacy". On LLMs she writes that they "entail a new type of writing that comes in the form of the
prompt"; calling them oral because "the chatbots simulate conversations with us" would be, in Brandt's phrase,
"a holdover from the social arrangements of a disintegrating print culture". Literacy "hasn't been killed but
taken hostage".

## Key facts it relies on
- Names Thompson, Weisenthal and Marriott as the "return to orality" writers (opening paragraphs).
