## Summary
The study describes a modified research-consent process used with 204 ethnically diverse adults aged 50 and over who were consenting to a trial of advance-directive forms. The consent form was written at a sixth-grade level and **read to participants**. Seven comprehension questions and targeted education followed, repeated until the participant understood ("teach-to-goal"). Forty per cent had limited literacy. Only 28% answered every comprehension question correctly on the first pass, and 80% after the second. With teach-to-goal, 98% of participants who engaged in the process reached complete comprehension. After adjustment, lower literacy and being black were associated with needing more passes. Not speaking English as a primary language was associated with more passes only in bivariate analysis. The authors conclude that literacy and minority status shape understanding of consent information, and that a modified process can reach comprehension "regardless of literacy or language barriers".

## Key facts it relies on (all from the abstract)

## Critical notes from the literature
- Descriptive study with no control arm. The 28% first-pass figure is for a form **already simplified and read aloud**, so it measures read-aloud disclosure at its best, not a standard form.
- "Who engaged in the consent process" may exclude people who dropped out. The abstract does not say how many.
- Tamariz et al. (2013, abstract) cite teach-back and teach-to-goal studies as achieving "the highest level of comprehension" among low-literacy interventions, from a base of only six studies.

## Second-pass notes (2026-10-15)
- Read the whole article in PMC's HTML rendering (PMC1831581; saved locally, text extracted to `sudore_pmc.txt`). The scanned PDF on PMC (`pdf/jgi0021-0867.pdf`) sits behind a reCAPTCHA page and Springer's copy is paywalled ("Buy article"), so **no printed page numbers below p. 867 can be given**. Springer's metadata gives pp. 867-873. Locators below are section names.
- The page carries "This article has been corrected. See J Gen Intern Med. 2006 Sep;21(9):1009." I did not read the erratum.
- **Procedure (Methods, "The Consent Form").** "participants were given a copy of the consent form (in English or Spanish) and were asked to read along while the form was slowly read to them verbatim over approximately 10 minutes." So the first pass is read-aloud *plus* the participant's own copy, not read-aloud alone.
- **What "full understanding" means (Methods, "Consent Comprehension Assessment").** "7 true/false comprehension statements concerning study procedures, risk, and confidentiality"; "I don't know" counted as incorrect. Complete comprehension = all seven correct.
- **What the "teaching" was (Methods, "Teach-To-Goal Strategy").** "those sections of the consent form corresponding to each missed statement were re-read. Missed statements were then repeated." Up to 3 passes, then a dementia screen (Mini-Cog), then up to 3 more; beyond 6 passes, ineligible. The targeted education is re-reading the relevant sections and asking again; the paper describes no other instruction.
- **Population (Methods).** General Medicine Clinic, San Francisco General Hospital, Aug-Dec 2004; aged 50+, English or Spanish spoken "well" or "very well"; dementia, deafness, delirium and visual acuity <20/100 excluded. Mean age 61. The title calls them "vulnerable patients".
- **Denominators (Results).** "Study staff approached 329 potential participants ... leaving 208 participants." "Only 4 participants who engaged in the consent process (2%) could not achieve complete comprehension of consent information within 6 passes." "The remaining sample (N =204)". So 98% = 204/208.
- **28% (Results).** "Only 57 (28%) participants responded correctly to all of the comprehension statements, thereby requiring only 1 pass through the consent process (Fig. 1), 106 (52%) required a second pass, and 41 (20%) required 3 or more passes." The 28% is of the 204 who finished; the 4 who never reached comprehension are outside that denominator (57/208 = 27%).
- **98% (Abstract, p. 867; Discussion).** Abstract: "With a teach-to-goal strategy, 98% of participants who engaged in the consent process achieved complete comprehension." Discussion: "Despite significant consent modifications (improving readability of the consent form, having bilingual research assistants read the consent form to participants, and allowing time for discussion), few participants (28%) had complete comprehension and required only 1 pass through the consent process. However, further use of a teach-to-goal strategy was successful in achieving complete comprehension in 98% of all participants who engaged in the consent process".
- **Authors' own conclusion (Abstract).** "Using a modified consent process, little additional education was required to achieve complete comprehension, regardless of literacy or language barriers."
- **Limitations (Discussion).** The seven statements did not cover every required element of consent; "some participants may have had better test-taking strategies (i.e., guessing cor[rectly])".
- v4 line 105: **narrower** (see `v4/V4_THIN_SOURCES.md`). "Older adults" stretches "aged ≥50"; the first pass included reading along; "targeted teaching" was targeted re-reading; the authors read their result as showing *little* additional education was needed. v4 line 165: **holds** (72% = 147/204 at first pass; 2% = 4/208 never reached it).
