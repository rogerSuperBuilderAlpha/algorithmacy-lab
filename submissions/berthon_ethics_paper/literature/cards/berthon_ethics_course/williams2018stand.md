## Summary
Williams, a former Google strategist writing as an Oxford philosopher, argues that the digital "attention
economy" threatens human freedom: technologies built to capture attention privilege impulses over intentions
and so erode the will at the individual and collective level (back-cover description; contents, pp. ix-x). The
book runs in three parts, "Distraction by Design", "Clicks Against Humanity" and "Freedom of Attention", and
ends with proposals for reform (ch. 12) (contents). I read the contents and chapter 11, "The Monster and the
Bank" (pp. 97-102), whose stated job is to sidestep "pitfalls" and clear "misconceptions." There Williams
refuses a cost-benefit framing on the ground that adversarial design should not be tolerated at all (p. 97),
holds that the problem is new in power, pace and centralization (p. 98), and says his argument needs neither
"manipulation" nor "addiction" (pp. 99-100). He then lists things "we must not do" in response (p. 100):
tell users to unplug or detox, moralize with drug metaphors, ask users to "just adapt," rely on education or
media literacy alone, focus only on children, expect companies to self-regulate, or put stock in mindfulness
among tech employees (pp. 100-102). He closes by declining to blame designers, locating fault in "the emergent
dynamics of complex multiagent systems" (p. 102). I did not read chapter 12's reforms.

## Key facts it relies on
- Ch. 11 frames a list of "pitfalls we should avoid, namely things we must not do in response to the
  challenges of the attention economy" (p. 100).
- "we must not reply that if someone doesn't like the choices on technology's menu, their only option is to
  'unplug' or 'detox'" (p. 100).
- Users should not be asked to "just adapt" and "bear the burdens of impossible self-regulation" against "the
  armies of industrialized persuasion" (p. 101).
- "Additionally, education is necessary – but not sufficient – for transcending this problem. Nor will 'media
  literacy' alone lead us out of this forest." (p. 101; printed folio confirmed from a rendered page image of
  PDF p. 119, running head "THE MONSTER AND THE BANK 101".)
- His own anecdote: a printed list of cognitive biases on his Google desk "didn't help at all" (p. 101).
- "We also can't expect companies to self-regulate" (p. 101).
- "there is no one to blame"; fault lies in "the emergent dynamics of complex multiagent systems" (p. 102).
- Book is open access under CC BY-NC 4.0 (copyright page).
