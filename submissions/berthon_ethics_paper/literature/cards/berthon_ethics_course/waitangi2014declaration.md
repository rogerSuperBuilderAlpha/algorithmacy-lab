## Summary
The Waitangi Tribunal's Stage 1 report on the Te Paparahi o Te Raki (Northland) inquiry asks what the
rangatira who signed he Whakaputanga (the 1835 Declaration of Independence) and te Tiriti o Waitangi (1840)
understood and agreed to. Its central finding, stated in the chairperson's letter, is that "in February 1840
the rangatira who signed te Tiriti did not cede their sovereignty"; they agreed to share power with the
Governor, who would control Pākehā, and "Though Britain intended to obtain the sole right to make and enforce
law over Māori as well as Pākehā, Hobson did not explain this" (p. xxii). Chapter 7 narrates the proceedings at
Waitangi on 5-6 February 1840: Hobson's address, interpreted by the CMS missionary Henry Williams "sentence by
sentence" (p. 355, quoting Hobson's dispatch); the signing on 6 February (section 7.6.5, from p. 369),
including Colenso's question whether the chiefs understood the articles (p. 373); and the later signings, by
more than 500 rangatira, only 39 of them on the English text (p. 386). Chapter 8 reviews historians on
Williams's translation, Ward against the charge of deceit (p. 414) and Belich for it (p. 415), and claimants'
arguments on his conflict of interest (p. 417). The conclusions find that Williams "may have been honest" in
using "kawanatanga" but "must, however, have known" that tino rangatiratanga conveyed more than the English
text (p. 514). I read the pages listed, not the whole 600-page report.

## Key facts it relies on
- Finding: "in February 1840 the rangatira who signed te Tiriti did not cede their sovereignty"; and "Hobson
  did not explain" Britain's intention to obtain sole law-making authority (p. xxii).
- Hobson's dispatch: "Mr H Williams, of the Church Missionary Society, did me the favour to interpret, and
  repeated in the native tongue, sentence by sentence, all I said" (p. 355).
- Colenso "asked Hobson whether 'these Natives understand the articles of the treaty which they are now called
  on to sign'" (p. 352, photo caption; full exchange at p. 373); the intervention came "on the morning of
  6 February" (p. 421), within section 7.6.5, "Waitangi, 6 February - the signing of te Tiriti" (p. 369).
- "signed at about 50 meetings around the coast of both islands by more than 500 rangatira. Only 39 rangatira
  signed the English text" (p. 386).
- Ward (1988) did "not think any of this is true"; the missionaries were "genuine, not deceitful" (p. 414).
- Belich: the use of "rangatiratanga" was likely "a deliberate or semi-deliberate act of deceit" by Williams
  and his son Edward (p. 415).
- Claimants argued that Williams's "significant land holdings motivated him to serve the Crown well" (p. 417).
- On 10 January [1840] Bishop Broughton wrote to Williams "instructing him to support Hobson's efforts to get
  the chiefs to cede their sovereignty" (p. 506).
- "While Williams may have been honest in his choice of 'kawanatanga' to translate 'sovereignty', he must,
  however, have known that tino rangatiratanga conveyed more than what was set out in the English text."
  (p. 514.)

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet A (`literature/facets/A_documents_and_standing.md:236`) records that the report (pp. 257-259, not
  re-read by me) finds substantial and growing Māori literacy by 1839 while siding with oral primacy. Ledger
  P4-054 accordingly judges "a party who could not make its own [text]" a poor fit; most signatories signed the
  Māori text.
- Ledger P6-040: the Tribunal is a commission of inquiry with mostly recommendatory findings, not a court.
- Ledger P3-059: Belich's phrase concerns the use of "rangatiratanga" in article 2, not "the choice of terms"
  in general (confirmed at p. 415).
- AUDIT_03 (`literature/audit/AUDIT_03.md:7`) established printed page = PDF page minus 26; I confirmed this on
  every page cited here from the printed folios.
- Facet A (`:253`) lists unread parts: Orange's history, Colenso's 1890 account, and most of chapters 7-10.
  The evidence on Williams's land and conflict of interest is reported by the Tribunal from claimants and
  historians (Moon, Fenton), not found by it (p. 417).
