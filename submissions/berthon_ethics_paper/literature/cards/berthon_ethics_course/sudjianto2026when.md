## Summary
The first substantive commentary on Nadella's post that this round found. Sudjianto accepts the insider-risk frame and "his insistence that we separate the intelligence supplied by a model from the authority to act on its recommendations." His objection is that "information security and decision correctness are different problems": a fully logged, authorised agent can still make a bad decision, and "An audit trail tells us what the agent did, not whether it should have done it." Verification should be engineered into the decision process (calculations, authoritative data, validated models, fixed acceptance criteria), not delegated to another LLM. But all verification depends on people who understand what is verified, and companies are "rushing to automate the very work through which people develop the expertise needed to independently verify AI". Expertise grows by doing: "An experienced model validator does not become experienced by reading validation reports." He recommends engineering verification "rather than relying on human review as the primary safeguard", redesigning junior roles "around developing judgment, not merely supervising AI outputs", keeping internal technical capability, and measuring institutional competence with planted-error exercises. His subject is the organisation's staff; end users and those affected do not appear.

## Key facts it relies on
- "Companies are rushing to automate the very work through which people develop the expertise needed to independently verify AI."
- "An audit trail tells us what the agent did, not whether it should have done it. And an emergency stop is useful only when something recognizes that intervention is necessary."
- "An experienced model validator does not become experienced by reading validation reports."
- "There is a considerable difference between using AI to help someone understand a problem and using AI to eliminate the need for that person to understand it."
- Recommendation 1: "engineer independent verification into the AI system rather than relying on human review as the primary safeguard."
- Recommendation 2: "redesign junior roles around developing judgment, not merely supervising AI outputs"; "That requires deliberate training and mentoring, not simply providing access to a chatbot."
- Recommendation 4: measure "whether AI adoption is improving or weakening institutional competence", e.g. teams detecting planted errors.
