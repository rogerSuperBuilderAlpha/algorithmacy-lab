## Summary
Bhola's UNESCO study argues for the mass literacy campaign and presents eight national cases (USSR, Viet Nam,
China, Cuba, Burma, Brazil, Tanzania, Somalia). I read the Cuba chapter, the Brazil chapter's funding and evaluation
sections, and a passage on the uses of literacy. Cuba's 1961 campaign closed schools for eight months and sent
everyone of 13 and over to teach peasants, living in their homes and teaching two or three adults at a time
(p. 92). About 100,000 instructors trained at Varadero; the full force was 268,420 (p. 97). Each brigadista got a
kit and ten pesos a month (p. 98). Learners took initial, intermediate and final tests (p. 102). The campaign
"made 707,212 literate during 1961", bringing illiteracy from 23.6 to 3.9 per cent at about a first-grade level
(p. 100). Follow-up courses brought 586,000 back toward sixth grade (p. 103). The primer's lessons included "Fidel
is Our Leader" (p. 100), and Bhola says campaigns need "ideological justifications" (p. 104). Brazil's MOBRAL took
Freire's technique with different themes (p. 130); external evaluators found brief instructor training and weak
follow-up (p. 134).

## Key facts it relies on
- "Fidel Castro closed all schools for eight months in order to send everyone of 13 years and older, including
  teachers, to teach the peasants"; tutors lodged "in the homes of illiterates, teaching small groups of two to
  three adults" (p. 92).
- Final force: popular instructors 120,632; workers' brigades 13,016; Conrado Benítez brigades 100,000; teachers
  34,772; total 268,420 (p. 97).
- "Each brigadista was given ten pesos a month for incidental expenses"; ratio "1:3 and sometimes 1:2" (p. 98).
- "made 707,212 literate during 1961, resulting in an illiteracy rate of only 3.9 per cent" from 23.6 per cent
  (p. 100); literate meant roughly end of first grade (p. 100).
- Primer *Venceremos* lessons included "The Revolution; Fidel is Our Leader; The Land is Ours" (p. 100).
- Follow-up: "some 586,000 people returned to school" toward sixth grade; about 65,500 graduated by 1966
  (p. 103).
- Municipal files "merely provided numerical data on the number of persons declared literate" (p. 104).
- "Literacy campaigns cannot be justified in purely economic terms. Ideological justifications are necessary"
  (p. 104).
- MOBRAL's method has the technical structure of Freire's, but its generative themes "set the two methods apart"
  (p. 130); external evaluations found training "lasting only a few days" and "failure ... in follow-up" (p. 134).
- "literacy has not always been used exclusively for conscientization but also for mystification and for creating
  and reinforcing dependencies" (pp. 30-31).

## Critical notes from the literature
- A UNESCO advocacy volume ("advocates the literacy campaign", Preface); Cuban figures are official, drawn from
  Fagen and Kozol. Facet M5 of this arm gives the 3.9 per cent figure and lists Leiner (1987) and Arnove and Graff
  (1987) as unread standard works (`M5_wider_cases.md`, entries on Cuba).
- Bhola himself notes critics doubt official statistics for MOBRAL (p. 136); no equivalent caveat is given for
  Cuba.
- The OCR of the microfiche scan has errors ("1$u1" for 1961); figures above were checked against the page text.
