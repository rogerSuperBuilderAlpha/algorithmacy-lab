## Summary
A mobile telephone operator refused CK a contract on the strength of a credit assessment by Dun & Bradstreet Austria (formerly Bisnode). CK sought "meaningful information about the logic involved" under GDPR art. 15(1)(h), and the Vienna Administrative Court asked what that information must contain and how it squares with trade secrets. The First Chamber read the provision by wording, context and purpose. Because the right of access serves the art. 22(3) rights to express a view on and contest a solely automated decision (para. 55), and recital 71 speaks of an explanation, the Court held that art. 15(1)(h) "affords the data subject a genuine right to an explanation as to the functioning of the mechanism involved in automated decision-making of which that person was the subject and of the result of that decision" (para. 57). The explanation must cover "the procedure and principles actually applied" in a concise, intelligible form (paras 58, 61); neither a complex formula nor a full step-by-step account will do (para. 59). Where the controller claims trade secrets or third-party data, it must give the material to "the competent supervisory authority or court", which balances the interests case by case (paras 74, 76). I read the CELEX text: header, paras 39–77 and the ruling; the ECLI comes from the Publications Office notice.

## Key facts it relies on
- Caption: "CK v Magistrat der Stadt Wien, other party: Dun & Bradstreet Austria GmbH"; First Chamber; 27 February 2025 (header).
- Facts: D & B profiled CK "on behalf of the mobile telephone undertaking which, on that basis, refused to conclude or renew a contract with her" (para. 64).
- Purpose of the access right in automated decisions: to enable the data subject "effectively to exercise the rights conferred on him or her by Article 22(3)", to express a view and contest (para. 55).
- "Article 15(1)(h) of the GDPR affords the data subject a genuine right to an explanation as to the functioning of the mechanism involved in automated decision-making of which that person was the subject and of the result of that decision" (para. 57).
- The explanation covers "the procedure and principles actually applied" in "a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form" (para. 58); "mere communication of a complex mathematical formula, such as an algorithm" does not suffice (para. 59).
- A court could find it enough to tell the data subject "the extent to which a variation in the personal data taken into account would have led to a different result" (para. 62).
- Trade secrets or third-party data go to "the competent supervisory authority or court" for balancing (para. 74); a national rule excluding access to protect trade secrets as a rule is precluded (para. 75).
- Ruling 1 limits the right to automated decision-making "within the meaning of Article 22(1)" (ruling; para. 66).
