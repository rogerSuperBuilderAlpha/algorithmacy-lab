## Summary
Through document analysis of Meta's free educational resources (Facebook's Get Digital and Instagram's Community
Programs and guides), the authors show how a platform's "social media corporate curriculum invites learners to
interpret, discuss, and act upon the problems of social media as if they were an individual issue" (abstract,
p. 1). They frame these programmes as "a performative practice of corporate social responsibility" (p. 2) that
individualises the harms of platforms and keeps users present and datafied (pp. 2, 17). The Get Digital lessons
teach that whether social media harms well-being "boils down to individual decisions, thought processes, and
habits"; "this burdening of the user to live well on social media perpetuates existing neoliberal logics of
responsibilization" (p. 11). Drawing on Elias, they read the curricula as a "civilizing" discourse that disciplines
users into governable subjects (pp. 16-17). They propose Freirean critical digital literacy as an alternative but
warn that a literacy "devoid of the collective could serve simply to redistribute, rather than challenge, the
primary responsibility of the individual" (p. 18).

- Critical literacy "devoid of the collective" may "redistribute, rather than challenge, the primary responsibility of the individual" (p. 18).
