## Summary
This is Microsoft's current transparency document for Copilot in organisations, the kind of document the Responsible AI Standard requires (T2). It describes the system, its models, its limitations and Microsoft's mitigations, then gives "best practices for deploying and adopting". It frames responsible AI as "a shared commitment between Microsoft and its customers". Under "Limitations" it lists "User training and adoption": effective use "requires users to understand its capabilities and limitations", and "users need to be trained". Its mitigations against ungrounded output are source references "for users to verify", "explicit notice that they're interacting with an AI system", and advice "to check the source materials". Under "End-users should:" it asks users to write good prompts and "always review and verify AI-generated responses", to "exercise human oversight" by reviewing responses, and to be aware of overreliance, stating that Microsoft mitigates that risk "by adding disclaimers to our products but users should still make sure to review the accuracy of the answers." User feedback goes through thumbs up/down and "Customer admins can review feedback".

## Key facts it relies on
- "Limitations": "User training and adoption: Effective use of Microsoft Copilot requires users to understand its capabilities and limitations. There might be a learning curve, and users need to be trained to effectively interact with and benefit from the service."
- Mitigations, grounding: "Users should always take caution and use their best judgment"; references "for users to verify the response"; "explicit notice that they're interacting with an AI system and advised to check the source materials".
- "Best practices": "Responsible AI is a shared commitment between Microsoft and its customers."
- "End-users should": "always review and verify AI-generated responses"; "users should review the responses generated by Microsoft Copilot and verify that they match their expectations and requirements."
- "End-users should", overreliance: "we help mitigate this risk by adding disclaimers to our products but users should still make sure to review the accuracy of the answers."
- Feedback: "Customer admins can review feedback submitted in the Microsoft 365 admin center."

## Critical notes from the literature
- The DXD arm's findings (facets L, G) caution that the critique cannot rest on "people review badly" alone; the evidence on review is mixed and mostly about alerts and approvals (DXD G C4). This card shows where Microsoft places the review, not how well users do it.
- This is Microsoft's product documentation, not Nadella's post.
