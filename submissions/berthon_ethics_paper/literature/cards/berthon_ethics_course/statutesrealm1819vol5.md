## Summary
The Licensing Act of 1662 as printed in the Record Commission's *Statutes of the Realm*. The preamble names the danger: "evil disposed persons" printing "heretical schismatical blasphemous seditious and treasonable Bookes", for which "no surer meanes can be advised then by reducing and limiting the number of Printing Presses" (p. 428). The Act bars such books (§ I), requires every book to be entered in the Stationers' Company register and licensed before printing, and assigns licensers by subject: law books to the Lord Chancellor and chief justices, state history to the Secretaries of State, heraldry to the Earl Marshal, everything else to the Archbishop of Canterbury, the Bishop of London or the university chancellors (§ II, p. 429). Later sections bar unlicensed sellers, imports of English books printed abroad and presses set up without notice to the Stationers (§§ VII–IX, p. 431). § X admits no new master printer until the masters are reduced to twenty, beside the King's and the universities' printers, with four master letter-founders (p. 431). Penalties, deposit copies, provisos and a two-year term follow (pp. 432–433). I read the OCR text of the whole Act and checked the chapter number, page range and § X against archive.org page images.

## Key facts it relies on
- Heading on the page image: "CHAPTER XXXIII." with the margin note "Rot. Parl. 14 C. II. p. 3. nu. 17"; running heads "14° Car. II. c. 32, 33" (p. 428) and "14° Car. II. c. 33" (pp. 431, 433). The OCR reads the heading as "CHAPTER XXXII." and also heads the preceding Woollen Cloth Act "XXXII"; the image settles it as c. 33.
- The Act begins on p. 428 (lower half, after the end of c. 32) and ends on p. 433 with § XXIV. P. 434 opens the list of private acts ("ITEM quedam Petic̃ões privatas personas concernentes").
- Ground (p. 428): "no surer meanes can be advised then by reducing and limiting the number of Printing Presses and by ordering and setling the said Art or Mystery of Printing by Act of Parliament".
- Register and licence (§ II, p. 429): no book may be printed "unlesse the same Booke and Pamphlet ... be first entred in the Booke of the Register of the Company of Stationers of London" and "first lawfully licensed".
- The cap (§ X, p. 431, page image): "no man shall be admitted to be a Master Printer untill they who are now actually Master Printers shall be by death or otherwise reduced to the number of Twenty and from thence forth the number of Twenty Master Printers shall be continued and no more besides the Kings Printers and the Printers allowed for the Universities ... and but four Master Founders of Letters".
- Vacancies are filled by the Archbishop of Canterbury and the Bishop of London; a printer gives security of £300 (§ X, p. 431).
- Presses (§ XI, OCR, between page markers 432 and 433): no master printer "shall keep above Two Printing Presses at once" unless he has been Master or Upper Warden of the Company, who may keep three.
- Deposit (§ XVI, p. 433): three copies of every new book, for the King's Library and the two universities.
- Term (§ XXIV, p. 433): "this Act shall continue and be in force for two yeares to commence from the Tenth of June One thousand six hundred sixty and two and no longer"; the bracket's footnote reads "annexed to the Original Act in a separate Schedule".
