## Summary
The volume prints the acts of the Alabama General Assembly's thirteenth annual session, held at Tuscaloosa
from November 1831 under Governor John Gayle. The act cited, approved 16 January 1832 and titled "To
prevent the Introduction of Slaves into Alabama, and for other Purposes", restricts who may bring slaves
into the State (s. 1), sets forfeitures and prosecution fees (ss. 6-8), and then tightens control over free
people of colour and slaves. From 1 January following, free people of colour may not settle in Alabama, on
pain of whipping, sale into one year's servitude and, in the end, loss of freedom (s. 9). Section 10 fines
"any person or persons who shall endeavor or attempt to teach any free person of color, or slave, to spell,
read, or write" between $250 and $500 on indictment. Sections 11-12 punish writing a pass or free paper for
a slave. Section 13 makes the circulation of "seditious papers, pamphlets or writing" tending to
insurrection a capital crime. Further sections limit trade and association without written permission,
assemblies of more than five male slaves, and preaching by Black people without five slaveholders present
(ss. 14-18, 24). Section 20 requires slaveholders to feed and clothe their slaves.

## Key facts it relies on
- Title page (p. 1): "Acts passed at the Thirteenth Annual Session of the General Assembly of the State of Alabama, begun and held in the town of Tuscaloosa, on the third Monday in November, one thousand eight hundred and thirty-one ... Tuscaloosa: printed by Wiley, M'Guire & Henry, State Printers. 1832."
- p. 12 (verified on the page image): act heading "To prevent the Introduction of Slaves into Alabama, and for other Purposes."
- s. 9 (pp. 15-16): from "the first day of January next" no free person of colour may "settle within the limits of this State".
- s. 10 (p. 16, verified on the image): "any person or persons who shall endeavor or attempt to teach any free person of color, or slave, to spell, read, or write, shall, upon conviction thereof by indictment, be fined in a sum not less than two hundred and fifty dollars nor more than five hundred dollars."
- s. 11 (p. 16): a free person of colour who writes "a pass or free paper" for a slave receives "thirty-nine lashes on the bare back" and must leave the State.
- s. 12 (pp. 16-17): a slave who writes a pass for another slave receives fifty lashes for a first offence and one hundred thereafter.
- s. 13 (p. 17): distributing "any seditious papers, pamphlets or writing, tending to produce conspiracy or insurrection or rebellion" is punished by death.
- s. 17 (p. 17): more than five male slaves may not assemble off their plantation.
- p. 18: "Approved, January 16, 1832."
