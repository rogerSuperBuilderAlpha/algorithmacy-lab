## Summary
Public Law 91-285 is titled "To extend the Voting Rights Act of 1965 with respect to the discriminatory use of
tests, and for other purposes" (p. 314). Section 3 lengthens the s. 4(a) bail-out period of the 1965 Act from
"five years" to "ten years" (p. 315). Section 4 adds a second coverage trigger keyed to 1 November 1968 and the
November 1968 election (p. 315), and s. 5 conforms preclearance to it. Section 6 adds new titles to the 1965
Act. Title II, s. 201(a), suspends literacy and similar tests in every state and subdivision not already
covered: "Prior to August 6, 1975, no citizen shall be denied, because of his failure to comply with any test
or device, the right to vote in any Federal, State, or local election" (p. 315). Section 201(b) repeats the
1965 definition of "test or device", including the ability to "read, write, understand, or interpret any
matter" (p. 315). Section 202 addresses durational residence requirements (p. 316), s. 203 lets the
Attorney General sue to enforce, and s. 204 sets fines and up to five years' imprisonment (p. 317). Title III
(from p. 318) lowers the voting age to eighteen in federal, state and local elections.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 314: "Public Law 91-285 … June 22, 1970 … AN ACT To extend the Voting Rights Act of 1965 with respect to the discriminatory use of tests, and for other purposes"; s. 1, short title "Voting Rights Act Amendments of 1970".
- p. 315, s. 3: s. 4(a) of the 1965 Act is amended "by striking out the words 'five years' … and inserting in lieu thereof the words 'ten years'".
- p. 315, s. 4: a new s. 4(b) trigger covers areas that maintained a test or device on 1 November 1968 with under 50% registration on that date or turnout in November 1968.
- p. 315, s. 6: adds "TITLE II—SUPPLEMENTAL PROVISIONS", headed "APPLICATION OF PROHIBITION TO OTHER STATES".
- p. 315, s. 201(a): "Prior to August 6, 1975, no citizen shall be denied, because of his failure to comply with any test or device, the right to vote in any Federal, State, or local election conducted in any State or political subdivision of a State as to which the provisions of section 4(a) of this Act are not in effect by reason of determinations made under section 4(b) of this Act."
- p. 315, s. 201(b): "test or device" means any requirement to "(1) demonstrate the ability to read, write, understand, or interpret any matter, (2) demonstrate any educational achievement …, (3) possess good moral character, or (4) prove his qualifications by the voucher of registered voters".
- p. 317, s. 204: anyone who deprives a person of a right under s. 201 or 202 "shall be fined not more than $5,000, or imprisoned not more than five years, or both" (verifier: the OCR reads "$6,000"; the page image reads $5,000).
- p. 318, Title III, s. 301 onward: "REDUCING VOTING AGE TO EIGHTEEN IN FEDERAL, STATE, AND LOCAL ELECTIONS".

## Critical notes from the literature
- Ledger P3-026 and closed ledger: the v2 sentence said the 1970 amendments "barred" the tests nationwide. The Act
  is a five-year suspension, to 6 August 1975, and the 1975 amendments made the ban permanent (facet D entry 9:
  89 Stat. 400, title, "to make permanent the ban against certain prerequisites to voting"). v3 adopted
  "suspended … until 1975".
- Facet D entry 6: the Supreme Court upheld the nationwide literacy-test suspension in *Oregon v. Mitchell*, 400
  U.S. 112 (1970). Black J. wrote that "all of my Brethren join me in this judgment" on that point (AUDIT_01 D7).
