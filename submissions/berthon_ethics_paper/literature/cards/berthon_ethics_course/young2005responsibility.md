## Summary
Young argues that obligations of justice arise from social structural processes, not from shared political institutions. Because some of those processes reach across the globe, so do some obligations of justice (p. 709). She illustrates the argument with the transnational apparel supply chain and its sweatshops (pp. 713-716). Structural injustice, on her account, occurs "as a consequence of many individuals and institutions acting in pursuit of their particular goals and interests, within given institutional rules and accepted norms" (p. 715). Against the "liability model", which isolates a culpable agent, she sets a "social connection model". Under it, "all agents who contribute by their actions to the structural processes that produce injustice have responsibilities to work to remedy these injustices" (p. 709). She does not reject liability. She keeps it for factory owners, managers and states (pp. 717-719) and treats a corporation under it as a single agent (p. 717). The social connection model has five features (pp. 720-724): it does not isolate; it questions background conditions; it is "more forward looking than backward looking"; it is shared; and it is discharged only through collective action, so it is "ultimately political responsibility" (p. 723). Its rhetoric avoids blame (p. 725). Power sets the degree of responsibility (p. 725). The parameters of reasoning are deferred to "another time" (p. 726).

## Key facts it relies on
- Definition: "The social connection model of responsibility says that all agents who contribute by their actions to the structural processes that produce injustice have responsibilities to work to remedy these injustices" (p. 709).
- Status of this text. Footnote 1 reads: "January 2005. Prepared for the International Congress on Law and Philosophy, May 2005. This is a shortened version of a paper prepared for presentation at a conference on Global Justice, Bowling Green State University ... and forthcoming, Philosophy and Social Policy" (p. 709 n. 1). Young misnames the journal there; the 2006 venue is *Social Philosophy and Policy*.
- Under the liability model, the responsible agent "can be a collective entity, such as a corporation, but when it is, that entity can be treated as a single agent for the purposes of assigning responsibility" (p. 717, citing French 1984).
- "By proposing a social connection model of responsibility, I do not aim to replace or reject the liability model of responsibility" (p. 719).
- The model's source is role responsibility. "people have certain responsibilities by virtue of their social roles or positions" (p. 719), and the model "draws more on the latter usage of the term 'responsibility' than on the liability usage" (p. 720).
- Firms are not absolved. Convicting the thugs and managers "does not absolve the multinational corporations from responsibility for the widespread nature of poor working conditions" (p. 720).
- On time: "(3) More forward looking than backward looking". "The point is not to blame, punish, or seek redress from those who did it, but rather to enjoin those who participate by their actions in the process of collective action to change it" (p. 722). In the same section Young says that every assignment "always has both backward looking and forward looking aspects" (p. 722).
- On sharing and discharge: "(4) Shared responsibility ... the responsibility is essentially shared". The responsibility "can be discharged only by joining with others in collective action ... No one of us can do this on our own". "Responsibility from social connection, then, is ultimately political responsibility" (p. 723).
- Victims share it too. They "can be called to a responsibility they share with others in the structures" (p. 724).
- Degree. "This does not necessarily mean that all who share responsibility have an equal responsibility. The power to influence the processes that produce unjust outcomes is an important factor distinguishing degrees of responsibility" (p. 725). The model works "without blaming anyone in particular for the structures" (p. 725).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Facet N4 (`literature/facets/N4_responsibility_intermediaries.md:28-37, 125-133`) concludes that the model cannot by itself ground a duty on the operator alone, because discharge is collective (p. 723). It also says that the manuscript leaves three things in Young unused: liability runs alongside her model and reaches a corporation as a single agent (pp. 717, 719), roles carry responsibilities (p. 719), and power sets degree (p. 725). N4's recommendation 3 (l. 145) asks the paper to cite p. 719 so that readers do not take Young to rule out an operator's own duty. V3 l. 197 does not yet cite p. 719.
- In *BEQ*, Tempels, Blok and Verweij (2020, p. 401, via N4 l. 51) say that Young "does not clearly substantiate the claim that a connection to structural injustice also implies a responsibility to take action". Young herself defers the parameters (p. 726).
- Wolff (2024, p. 15, via N4 l. 59) records critics' view that Young's split between liability and structural injustice "is too stark" where corporate actors deliberately create structures.
- Lin and Chen (2022, pp. 14, 17, via N4 l. 63) apply the model to AI. They spread responsibility to "nearly all participants" and warn against deferring to companies. This is the reading the manuscript's "who else must act" use invites.
- Resolution C3 (`review/v3/resolutions/C3_scholarly.md:211-229`) cites this 2005 text in place of the closed *SPP* 2006 article. AUDIT_06 (`literature/audit/AUDIT_06_changed.md:95`) agrees with the P5-047 fix. The *SPP* body page of the quotation is still unread.
- This version lacks the 2006 essay's section on parameters of reasoning (N4 l. 28; p. 726).

- **L197 (P5-046, application)**: "...the employers who adopt a screening vendor, the lenders who buy a score, and the legislators who could require any of the three duties are such agents." This applies the definition (p. 709) to new agents. Young's text names states (pp. 718-719, 724) and corporations (p. 720) as agents. **holds** as the author's application, because the definition covers any agent who contributes by action. Young's p. 725 point that power sets degree would let the sentence rank these agents, but the manuscript does not use it.
- **L197 (P5-047)**: "Young's model is forward-looking and shared". This takes two of the five features. Page 722 has "(3) More forward looking than backward looking", and p. 723 has "(4) Shared responsibility" and "the responsibility is essentially shared". **holds**, with one narrowing: Young's claim is comparative ("more forward looking than backward looking"), and she says that every assignment has both aspects (p. 722). The hyphenated "forward-looking" does not occur in the text; Young writes "forward looking".
- **L197 (P5-047, quotation)**: "its point 'is not to blame' (Young, 2005, p. 722)". Exact-string check: "is not to blame" occurs once in the text, on p. 722, in "The point is not to blame, punish, or seek redress from those who did it, but rather to enjoin those who participate by their actions in the process of collective action to change it." The match is exact, and the page is correct (PDF p. 14, running head 722). **holds**. The cut drops "punish, or seek redress" and the positive half ("to enjoin ... collective action"). That does not distort Young's meaning, and p. 725 ("without blaming anyone in particular") supports the gist.
- **L197 (P5-047, scope)**: "...and I use it only to say who else must act. The operator's duty rests on the simpler ground..." This takes nothing further from Young. It is consistent with her text, because the model's responsibility is shared and political and is discharged collectively (p. 723). The implied contrast is **narrower** than Young: she keeps the liability model (p. 719), applies it to a corporation as a single agent (p. 717), grounds her own model in role responsibility (pp. 719-720) and grades responsibility by power (p. 725). On her terms, an operator's own duty is not outside her account. The manuscript does not claim that it is. Flag for the author: cite p. 719 here, as N4 recommendation 3 proposes.
