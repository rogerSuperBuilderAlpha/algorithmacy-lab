## Summary
Yeung argues that Big Data analytics are a mode of "design-based" regulation. She treats regulation as a
cybernetic cycle of information gathering, standard setting and behaviour modification (AM p. 3). She
separates automated decision-making from "digital decision guidance" and sets the first aside: "this paper
focuses on Big Data driven decision guidance techniques" (AM p. 5). These techniques work as nudges, in Thaler
and Sunstein's sense. Unlike static nudges such as a speed hump, they run as "self-contained cybernetic
systems". The whole regulatory cycle runs "via a recursive feedback loop" that keeps reconfiguring each
person's choice environment. That loop makes them "nimble, unobtrusive and highly potent", and Yeung calls
them "hypernudge" (AM p. 6). She then asks whether hypernudging is legitimate. Within liberal theory, the
manipulation, deception and opacity critiques apply with extra force (AM pp. 7-8). Notice and consent cannot
cure them, because people do not read privacy policies and Big Data purposes cannot be specified in advance
(AM pp. 8-13). STS and surveillance studies show a deeper problem: hypernudging configures the self, as
"surveillance capitalism" (AM pp. 13-16). She concludes that "practically enforceable constraints" and
"meaningful accountability over the algorithms" are needed (AM p. 18).

## Key facts it relies on
- Abstract: Big Data analytics nudges, "unlike the static Nudges", "are extremely powerful and potent due to their
  networked, continuously updated, dynamic and pervasive nature (hence 'hypernudge')" (AM p. 1; PDF p. 2).
- Scope: Yeung sets automated decision-making aside ("raises serious concerns") and writes "this paper focuses on
  Big Data driven decision guidance techniques" (AM pp. 4-5; PDF pp. 5-6).
- Mechanism: "Networked, Big Data driven digital guidance technologies thus operate as self-contained cybernetic
  systems, with the entire tripartite regulatory cycle continuously implemented via a recursive feedback loop".
  This includes "refinement of the individual's choice environment in response to changes in the target's
  behaviour" and "data feedback to the choice architect" (AM p. 6; PDF p. 7).
- Scale: "Big Data hypernudging operates on a one-to-many basis ... a single algorithmic hypernudge initiated by
  Facebook can directly affect millions of users simultaneously" (AM p. 7; PDF p. 8).
- Opacity: the mechanisms "are embedded into the design of complex, machine-learning algorithms, which are highly
  opaque (and typically protected by trade secrets: Rauhofer 2015; Pasquale 2006)" (AM p. 8; PDF p. 9).
- Notice fails: reading privacy policies "would consume 244 hours per year" (citing McDonald and Cranor 2008),
  and simplified notices are "also inadequate" (AM pp. 9-10; PDF pp. 10-11).
- Change rate: Facebook's News Feed algorithm "adapts to users' behavior", and Facebook typically makes "two to
  three changes to the algorithm weekly" (reporting Luckerson 2015; AM p. 15; PDF p. 16).
- Conclusion: "the selective omission of relevant information can be deceptive, yet is virtually impossible for
  affected users to detect" (AM p. 16; PDF p. 17).
