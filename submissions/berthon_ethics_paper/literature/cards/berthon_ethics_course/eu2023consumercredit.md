## Summary
The Consumer Credit Directive replaces Directive 2008/48/EC and harmonises rules on consumer credit
agreements. Recital 56 links it to the AI Act proposal, which classes AI systems that evaluate credit
scores or creditworthiness as high-risk "since they determine those persons' access to financial resources
or essential services", and says that where the creditworthiness assessment involves automated
processing the consumer should be able to obtain human intervention and a "meaningful, comprehensible
explanation". In the operative text, art. 12 requires creditors and credit intermediaries to give
"adequate explanations" of proposed agreements and ancillary services, free of charge and "before
concluding the credit agreement", so that the consumer can assess whether they suit her needs and
financial situation; Member States may adapt manner and extent. Art. 13 requires notice when an offer is
personalised "based on automated processing of personal data". Art. 18(8) gives a right to human
intervention by the creditor, comprising an explanation, the right to express a view, and a review of an
automated creditworthiness assessment and decision. Art. 34 requires Member States to promote financial
education. Art. 48 sets transposition by 20 November 2025 and application from 20 November 2026.

## Key facts it relies on
- Recital 56: AI systems evaluating creditworthiness "should be classified as high-risk AI systems, since they determine those persons' access to financial resources or essential services such as housing, electricity, and telecommunication services" (reporting the Commission's AI Act proposal).
- Art. 12(1): creditors and, where applicable, credit intermediaries must "provide adequate explanations to the consumer on the proposed credit agreements and any ancillary services"; "Such explanations shall be provided free of charge and before concluding the credit agreement."
- Art. 12(2): Member States "may, in justified cases, adapt the requirement" as to manner and extent, by circumstances, person and type of credit.
- Art. 13: consumers must be told "in a clear and comprehensible manner" when a personalised offer "is based on automated processing of personal data".
- Art. 18(8): where the assessment "involves the use of automated processing of personal data", the consumer may "request and obtain from the creditor human intervention": (a) an explanation "including on the logic and risks involved", (b) to "express the consumer's own point of view", (c) "request a review of the assessment of the creditworthiness and the decision".
- Art. 18(9): on rejection, the creditor must say, where applicable, that the assessment "is based on automated processing of data" and give the procedure for contesting it.
- Art. 26(8): withdrawal rules may be disapplied for agreements concluded through a notary, "provided that the notary confirms that the consumer is guaranteed the rights provided for under Articles 10, 11, 20 and 21".
- Art. 34(1): Member States "shall promote measures that support the education of consumers in relation to responsible borrowing and debt management".
- Art. 48(1): transpose "by 20 November 2025"; "apply those measures from 20 November 2026".

It flagged this as a change to the argument for the author. FIX_CHECK_B (P5-036) passed the v3 text and noted that the Directive says "before concluding", the manuscript "before signing".
- The C2 resolution notes art. 18(8) and art. 13 as instruments relevant to l. 167; they are not cited there.
- From my reading: art. 26(8) is a rule in which a notary "confirms" that the consumer is guaranteed certain information rights. It certifies rights, not understanding, but it is the nearest certifying role in this Directive and bears on l. 177.
- From my reading: art. 12 binds the creditor, a party to the contract, so the explanation comes from an interested party. The Directive does not apply until 20 November 2026 (art. 48), after the date of this card.
