## Summary
Chapter 28 of the 1827 session is one of Peel's criminal-law statutes. Its printed title is "An Act for further
improving the Administration of Justice in Criminal Cases in England", dated "21st June 1827" (p. 85). The preamble
says trials in England "are attended with some Forms which frequently impede the due Administration of Justice" and
that it is "expedient to abolish such Forms, and also to abolish the Benefit of Clergy" (p. 85). The Act deems a plea
of "Not guilty" to put the accused on the country without further form (s. I), lets the court enter that plea for a
prisoner who stands mute (s. II), voids excess peremptory challenges (s. III), and limits pleas of attainder (s. IV).
Section VI abolishes benefit of clergy for persons convicted of felony. Section VII stops the abolition from making
any felony capital that was clergyable before the session; s. VIII supplies a default punishment (transportation
for seven years, or up to two years' imprisonment, with whipping for males). Later sections deal with repeat
offenders, Admiralty offences, pardons, and a gender-and-number interpretation rule (ss. XI-XIV). The Act took effect
on 1 July 1827 (s. XV) and "shall [not] extend to Scotland or Ireland" (s. XVI, p. 87).

## Key facts it relies on
- Title and date: "An Act for further improving the Administration of Justice in Criminal Cases in England. [21st
  June 1827.]" (p. 85). The short title "Criminal Law Act 1827" does not appear in the printed text.
- Preamble: it is "expedient to abolish such Forms, and also to abolish the Benefit of Clergy, and to make better
  Provision for the Punishment of Offenders in certain Cases" (p. 85).
- s. VI: "That Benefit of Clergy, with respect to Persons convicted of Felony, shall be abolished; but that nothing
  herein contained shall prevent the Joinder in any Indictment of any Counts which might have been joined before the
  passing of this Act" (p. 86; OCR reads "shall he abolished ; hut").
- s. VII: no person convicted of felony "shall suffer Death, unless it be for some Felony which was excluded from the
  Benefit of Clergy before or on the First Day of the present Session of Parliament, or which hath been or shall be
  made punishable with Death by some Statute passed after that Day" (p. 86).
- s. VIII: a felony with no special punishment is punishable by transportation for seven years or imprisonment up to
  two years, and, "if a Male", whipping (p. 86).
- s. XV: commencement "on the First Day of July One thousand eight hundred and twenty seven" (p. 87).
- s. XVI: "nothing herein contained shall extend to Scotland or Ireland" (p. 87).
