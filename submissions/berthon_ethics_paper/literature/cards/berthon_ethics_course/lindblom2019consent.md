## Summary

Lindblom gives a normative justification for unions from premises that are usually unfriendly to them: the economic theory of incomplete contracts and a Lockean ethics of consent and the separation of powers. He makes three arguments. The argument from consent justifies unions only where the employer is willing to bargain with them. The argument from contestability starts from the fact that employment contracts are incomplete, so that authority stands in for contractual terms; consent to authority is therefore given in ignorance, and specific acts of authority cannot be justified by consent (pp. 189, 191, 199). Pettit's contestability fills the gap. It requires a basis for contestation (transparency and reasons), an opportunity for voice, and a forum in which contested decisions are heard and settled (pp. 199–201). Lindblom borrows the mechanism but not the premise of non-domination (p. 200). The argument from the separation of powers then decides where the forum must sit. Company unions, ombuds and other firm-internal channels leave the check under the power of the party to be checked, so "we should choose to design the checks on employer power outside the domain of the firm" (p. 206). Courts are rejected for everyday contestation, and federal or employee-controlled firms combine powers that should be balanced (pp. 206–207).

## Key facts it relies on

- Abstract (p. 189): incomplete contracts imply "that consent to authority has been given under ignorance, and, therefore, that authority cannot be justified by consent"; contestability "demands transparency, channels for voice, and a forum where contestations can be evaluated"; unions "are organized outside of the employer's domain of authority".
- p. 191: "we have reason to prefer that unions are in place, even if internal grievance mechanisms have been implemented."
- p. 199: quotes Pettit (1997, 185), "what matters is not the historical origin of decisions in some form of consent but their modal or counterfactual responsiveness to the possibility of contestation."
- p. 199: quotes Pettit (1997, 62), "Whether a relationship sprang originally from a contract or not, whether or not it was consensual in origin, the fact that it gives one party the effective capacity to interfere more or less arbitrarily in some other's choices means that one person dominates or subjugates the other."
- p. 200: "my argument does not use the ideal of non-domination as a premise."
- pp. 200–201: the forum must guarantee "a proper hearing of complaints"; authorities should not be able to "ignore certain challenges" and "should be required to answer for how they behave" (quoting Pettit 1997, 195–196).
- p. 205: "Unless employer power is checked, the problem of coercion is not fully resolved."
- p. 206: "Therefore, we should choose to design the checks on employer power outside the domain of the firm."
- p. 206: an individual ombuds route "runs into the same kind of problems as company unions did"; settling contestations in court "would make companies difficult to run and hamper their decision-making abilities."

## Critical notes from the literature

- Lindblom's independent forum is a countervailing organisation (the union, through collective bargaining), not an adjudicator. He explicitly sets aside courts for everyday contestation (p. 206). A reader who meets him beside "a body that can bind the operator" (v3 l. 225) should not take him to endorse a binding third-party arbiter.
- He uses Pettit's contestability without the republican premise (p. 200). The manuscript's l. 235 uses him as the conduit for Pettit's (1997, p. 62) domination claim. The quotation is accurate, but Lindblom quotes it to mark the point at which his own argument departs from republicanism.
- Facet N1 (`literature/facets/N1_republican_domination.md`, l. 75–77, 141) rates Lindblom the closest prior statement of counter-delegation's independent party: "made in BEQ seven years earlier for employers." The paper's novelty claim at §5.4 has to concede that much.
- Facet N1 (l. 81) also records Frye (2024), who places workplace domination in managerial discretion and disputes ownership-based remedies; that critique bears on the wider workplace-republican literature Lindblom sits in. I did not read Frye this session.
- The pagination is the journal's. The issue year is 2019, though the PDF's copyright line prints "©2018".
