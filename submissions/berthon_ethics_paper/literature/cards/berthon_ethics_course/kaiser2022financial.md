## Summary
Kaiser, Lusardi, Menkhoff and Urban meta-analyse 76 randomised experiments of financial education, with more than
160,000 participants (abstract). They find positive average causal effects on financial knowledge and on
downstream behaviour, of a size "similar to those realized by educational interventions in other domains" and "at
least three times as large as the average effect documented in earlier work" (abstract). Fernandes et al. (2014)
had 13 RCTs (p. 3). Re-running Fernandes et al.'s own common-effect method on the larger set gives 0.065 SD, about
3.6 times their 0.018; allowing heterogeneous true effects gives about 0.10 SD, "approximately 5.5 times larger"
(pp. 16-17). "Thus, one of the main findings of Fernandes et al. (2014) is not confirmed in this larger sample of
RCTs" (p. 16). Average effect across all outcomes is 0.123 SD (median 0.098), from a mean of 11.7 hours of
instruction measured about seven months after treatment (p. 13). They find no difference for low-income people and
"no strong evidence to support a rapid decay", but also no support for long-run effects (p. 3). Results survive
restriction to top journals, adequate power and publication-bias correction (abstract).

## Key facts it relies on
- "a meta-analysis of 76 randomized experiments with a total sample size of over 160,000 individuals" (abstract).
- Effects "similar to those realized by educational interventions in other domains, and are at least three times as
  large as the average effect documented in earlier work" (abstract).
- Fernandes et al. (2014) "examined 13 Randomized Controlled Trials" (p. 3).
- Common-effect estimate 0.065 SD (CI95 0.043 to 0.089) against Fernandes et al.'s 0.018 (p. 16).
- "one of the main findings of Fernandes et al. (2014) is not confirmed in this larger sample of RCTs" (p. 16).
- Heterogeneous-effects mean 0.1003 SD (CI95 0.071 to 0.129), "approximately 5.5 times larger" (pp. 16-17).
- Average effect 0.123 SD, median 0.098; mean 11.71 hours of instruction; measured 30.4 weeks after treatment on
  average (p. 13).
- No difference for low-income individuals; no strong evidence of rapid decay, no support for long-run effects
  either (p. 3).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The published JFE version (2022) was not read; numbers may have changed between the working paper and the
  article. Check before quoting.
- The outcomes are financial knowledge and behaviour after courses; nothing concerns adaptive systems, platforms
  or AI (`V5_scaffolding.md`, Q5).
- 0.10 SD is a small effect in absolute terms; the paper should not present it as a vindication of training.
