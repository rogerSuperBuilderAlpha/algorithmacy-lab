## Summary
Calo defends mandatory notice against "extreme skepticism" (p. 1030) by proposing a new kind: "visceral" notice that "leverage[s] a consumer's very experience of a product or service to warn or inform" (p. 1027), such as a phone camera required to make a shutter sound. He accepts much of the skeptics' case. Few read privacy policies, those who do find them unclear, and "Studies show only marginal improvement in consumer understanding where privacy policies get expressed as tables, icons, or labels, assuming the consumer even reads them" (pp. 1032-1033). He argues notice should not be abandoned: regulators choose it because it is cheap, preserves innovation and avoids setting arbitrary privacy floors (pp. 1047-1049); it "purports to respect the basic autonomy of the consumer or citizen by arming her with information and placing the ultimate decision in her hands" (p. 1049); and "notice is the worst regulatory mechanism, except for all of the alternatives" (p. 1047). He keeps visceral notice distinct from a nudge: its purpose is "to ensure consumers have the correct mental model of the product or service and can make decisions accordingly" (p. 1070). The copy read is the law review's production file with journal pagination; the middle of Part I (examples) and most of Part II.B were not read.

## Key facts it relies on
- p. 1027: thesis, "experience as a form of privacy disclosure is worthy of further study before we give in to calls to abandon notice as a regulatory strategy in privacy and elsewhere."
- p. 1028: notice "underpins law's basic legitimacy" (Fuller's "internal morality"; Hayek).
- p. 1033: "Studies show only marginal improvement in consumer understanding where privacy policies get expressed as tables, icons, or labels, assuming the consumer even reads them."
- p. 1033: visceral notice "does not rely exclusively on language or its symbolic equivalent".
- p. 1047: "notice is the worst regulatory mechanism, except for all of the alternatives"; substitutes "are perceived to be invasive, ineffective, expensive, or arbitrary."
- p. 1049: "Notice purports to respect the basic autonomy of the consumer or citizen by arming her with information and placing the ultimate decision in her hands."
- p. 1070: the goal of visceral notice is "to ensure consumers have the correct mental model of the product or service and can make decisions accordingly."
- pp. 1071-1072: strategies "that do not necessarily rely on words or symbols ... may not be susceptible to the same withering critiques as more traditional notice."

## Critical notes from the literature
- Solove (2013, p. 1885) answers that privacy consequences are too abstract to make visceral in the way graphic cigarette warnings are. See `solove2013introduction`.
- Calo reports no test of visceral notice; the article calls for study (p. 1072).
