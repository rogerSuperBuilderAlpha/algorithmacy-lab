## Summary
Fernandes, Lynch and Netemeyer meta-analyse the relation of financial literacy and financial education to
financial behaviour and add three empirical studies. The published abstract reports 168 papers covering 201 prior
studies (the June 2013 working paper I read says 155 papers and 188 studies, p. 2). Interventions to improve
financial literacy "explain only 0.1% of the variance in financial behaviors studied, with weaker effects in
low-income samples" (published abstract). Effects decay: "at delays of 20 months or greater, there is no
significant effect of even 24 hours of instruction" (WP p. 14). Correlational studies show larger associations, but
these shrink when omitted psychological traits are controlled or instruments used (WP pp. 2, 15). The authors do
not conclude for defaults instead of education. Defaults "work best when almost all consumers have similar needs";
where needs differ "one needs to know something to decide for oneself", and "just-in-time" education, alone or
inside decision-support systems, "may have promise" (WP p. 22). The published abstract envisages "a real but
narrower role" for such education.

## Key facts it relies on
- Published abstract: "168 papers covering 201 prior studies"; "only 0.1% of the variance in financial behaviors
  studied, with weaker effects in low-income samples".
- Working paper: "155 papers covering 188 prior studies" (p. 2).
- "at delays of 20 months or greater, there is no significant effect of even 24 hours of instruction" (WP p. 14).
- Omitted traits and instrumental variables shrink correlational effects (WP pp. 2, 15).
- "defaults work best when almost all consumers have similar needs. When consumers' needs are heterogeneous, one
  needs to know something to decide for oneself" (WP p. 22).
- "'just-in-time' financial education may have promise, alone and embedded in decision support systems that help
  select tailored options" (WP p. 22).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Kaiser, Lusardi, Menkhoff and Urban (NBER w27057, 2020; JFE 2022) re-estimate on 76 RCTs: the effect is "at least
  three times as large" (p. 3), about 0.10 SD with heterogeneous effects, and "one of the main findings of
  Fernandes et al. (2014) is not confirmed" (p. 16). Fernandes et al.'s RCT base was 13 trials (Kaiser et al.,
  p. 3) (`V5_scaffolding.md`, Q5).
- The sister arm read the same working paper in part (`dxd_foundation/literature/facets/L_disclosure_and_choice.md`,
  entry 5); its quotations match this reading.
- Version caution: quotations from the body come from the working paper, not the journal.

## Where the manuscript uses it
- v4 (not yet drafted): proposed use — the strongest evidence against decontextualised training as the answer to
  wrong (c) (published abstract; WP p. 14), cited with Kaiser et al.'s correction. holds.
- v4 (not yet drafted): proposed use — the authors' own alternative is in-task support where needs differ (WP
  p. 22), which matches scaffolding in Belland's (2017) sense only in part. narrower: "just-in-time" education
  "tied to a particular decision", and coaching, fit scaffolding; but "decision support systems that help select
  tailored options" and recommender systems (WP p. 22) are closer to what Belland (2017, p. 19) calls a job aid,
  which is not scaffolding.
