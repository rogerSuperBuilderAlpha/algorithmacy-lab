## Summary
From the abstract: the essay theorizes "the responsibilities moral agents may be said to have in relation to
global structural social processes that have unjust consequences". It asks how agents, "whether individual or
institutional", should conceive their responsibilities for global injustice. Young proposes "a model of
responsibility from social connection" and illustrates it with "transnational processes of production,
distribution and marketing of clothing". The model "says that all agents who contribute by their actions to
the structural processes that produce injustice have responsibilities to work to remedy these injustices".
She sets it against a "more standard model of responsibility, which I call a liability model". Five features
distinguish it. It "does not isolate perpetrators", it "judges background conditions of action", it "is more
forward looking than backward looking", "its responsibility is essentially shared", and "it can be discharged
only through collective action". A final section "begins to articulate parameters of reasoning that agents can
use for thinking about their own action in relation to structural injustice". The body of the 2006 text was
not opened, so this summary claims nothing beyond the abstract.

## Critical notes from the literature
- **The 2005 shortened version** (a separate text, read in this session at the lines given). Young writes "By
  proposing a social connection model of responsibility, I do not aim to replace or reject the liability model"
  (p. 719). She calls it "usually inappropriate to blame" connected but removed agents (p. 719), and she writes
  "The point is not to blame, punish, or seek redress from those who did it" (p. 722). Facet N4
  (`literature/facets/N4_responsibility_intermediaries.md:30-35`) adds that she treats a corporation as a single
  agent under the liability model (p. 717), that discharge is collective and "ultimately political" (p. 723), and
  that power sets the degree of responsibility (p. 725). Footnote 1 calls the 2005 text "a shortened version" of
  the paper "forthcoming" in this journal. Its pages are not the 2006 pages.
- **2006-only passages, via Ferguson (2024)** (facet N4:39): external pressure on the powerful "is often
  necessary" (Young 2006, p. 128, via Ferguson p. 5). The corporation-as-single-agent passage recurs at Young
  (2006, p. 116) (via Ferguson p. 5, n. 5). Both are secondary quotations, not checked against the 2006 text.
- **Critics:** Tempels et al. (2020) say that Young "does not clearly substantiate the claim that a connection to
  structural injustice also implies a responsibility to take action" (facet N4:125). Ferguson reports Goodin and
  Barry's "free pass" objection and Barry and Ferracioli's charge of "an unduly narrow conception of liability"
  (facet N4:45). Gädeke (2021) finds it unclear "what exactly a 'social connection' to injustice amounts to"
  (facet N1:85).
- **Glosses:** McKeown (2018, p. 2) calls the responsibility "nonblameworthy" (facet M4:55). The ledger traces
  the manuscript's "assigns no blame" to this gloss (P5-047).
