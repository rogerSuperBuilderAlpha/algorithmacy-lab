## Summary
A short article in the Brazilian Senate's journal that recounts how Brazil's illiterate citizens lost and regained the right to enrol as voters, then argues that they should also be eligible for office. The authors open on the turning point: Constitutional Amendment no. 25 of 15 May 1985, followed by the Constitution of 5 October 1988, "repararam uma secular injustiça" by guaranteeing illiterates the right to enrol (p. 68). The history runs from the 1824 Imperial Constitution, which did not bar illiterates, through the Saraiva Law (Decree 3.029 of 1881), whose art. 8 required proof of reading and writing for inclusion on the roll (p. 69), to the Republic, which confirmed the exclusion in Decree 6 of 1889 and the 1891 Constitution (pp. 70–71). The bar recurs in the constitutions of 1934, 1937, 1946 and 1967 until the 1985 amendment (p. 71). Parliamentary objectors are quoted at length: José Bonifácio o Moço, Joaquim Nabuco, Saldanha Marinho and Lauro Sodré (pp. 69–71). From p. 71 the authors argue for eligibility, noting that illiterates may opt out of enrolment and remain barred from candidacy. I read pp. 68–73 of the UnB repository PDF (13 PDF pages; the article runs pp. 68–79).

## Key facts it relies on
- EC 25 of 15 May 1985, passed by 423 votes in the Chamber and 61 in the Senate, and the 1988 Constitution "repararam uma secular injustiça, garantindo aos analfabetos o direito de se alistarem como eleitores" (p. 68).
- Illiterates were 5.9% (8,007,074) of 135,804,433 voters in the TSE's then-recent data (p. 68).
- "Mas, apesar da conquista da alistabilidade, mantém-se, até hoje, a inelegibilidade dos analfabetos" (p. 68).
- The 1824 Constitution did not restrict illiterates' right to vote and be voted for (p. 68).
- The Saraiva Law (Decreto Imperial 3.029, 9 Jan. 1881) marks "o efetivo início de um processo de marginalização cívico-política dos analfabetos que se completaria depois da proclamação da República"; art. 8 II required proof of "ler e escrever", by handwriting and signature recognised "por tabelião" (p. 69).
- The 31 October 1881 election was the last at which an illiterate elector could name a proxy under art. 6 § 15 (p. 69).
- The 1887 roll of 200,000 electors was 1.5% of the population (p. 69, citing Buarque de Holanda).
- Decree 6 of 19 Nov. 1889 and the 1891 Constitution kept illiterates off the rolls; the constitutions of 1934 (art. 108), 1937 (art. 117), 1946 (art. 132) and 1967 (art. 142) repeat the bar, which EC 25/1985 and the 1988 "Carta-cidadã" ended (pp. 70–71).
- After the change, illiterates may choose not to enrol, unlike most citizens, for whom voting is a duty (p. 71).

## Critical notes from the literature
- Engerman and Sokoloff (2001, p. 23) date Brazil's literacy qualification to 1891 and its end to 1988 (FIX_CHECK_A.md, P3-017/019/020/N07 row; ledger P3-020).
