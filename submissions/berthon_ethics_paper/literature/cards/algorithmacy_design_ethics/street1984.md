## What it argues

Nothing verified from the text. The book's distinction, as restated by a tester of it (Armer 1992, abstract): "According to the 'autonomous' model, literacy in any language produces uniform consequences for individuals such as interest in current affairs, personal efficacy, status aspiration, and logical thinking. This view is countered by the 'ideological' model which holds that consequences vary across languages depending upon the processes through which literacy is acquired and the purposes for which it is intended." The sibling arm's card (from the publisher description) reports the structure: the autonomous model traced through Goody, Olson and Hildyard; the ideological model proposed against it; fieldwork in an Iranian village in the 1970s where maktab (Qur'anic-school) literacy, a "commercial" literacy developed by fruit traders in a boom, and state-school literacy coexisted with different uses and statuses; and a critique of UNESCO-style adult literacy campaigns for importing the autonomous model. Wagner's review references (visible on Cambridge Core) cite Goody, Graff, Heath, and Scribner and Cole, which confirms the book's position in that debate.

## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. Street is the citation for the concession the paper must make: the "autonomous" reading of literacy — uniform cognitive and social consequences read off the technology — is contested, and the paper does not need it. His "ideological model," on every report of it, is a claim that literacy's consequences depend on the institutions that confer and use it, which is the paper's own register. The paper can therefore cite Street twice: once conceding the great divide, once as support for "there is no single literacy, only literacies embedded in institutions," and hence no single algorithmacy. Both uses depend on secondary knowledge until the book is read.

The scaffolding essay's use. The Iranian case (three literacies, one village, different statuses) is the ethnographic counterpart to Clanchy's and Goody 1968's objection: literacy is not one competence that print made universal but several, each conferred by an institution. It contradicts "converted literacy from a specialized guild privilege into an existential requirement" as a description of a single event.
