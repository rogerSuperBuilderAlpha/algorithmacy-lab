## What it argues

The title answers Lévi-Strauss's *La pensée sauvage*: the "savage mind" is not a different mentality but an oral one, and writing "domesticates" it. What Ong extracts, with Goody's pages: oral narrators "introduce new elements into old stories" and "vigorous leaders — the 'intellectuals' in oral society, Goody styles them (1977, p. 30) — invent new shrines and with these new conceptual universes" (Ong p. 41, citing pp. 29–30, 30); an entirely oral language "may have no ready term for a 'word' as an isolated item" (p. 115; Ong p. 59); among the LoDagaa the Invocation to the Bagre, "something everyone 'knows'," varies from recitation to recitation even for the same speaker (pp. 118–19; Ong p. 60); "backward scanning" — the reader's ability to look back over a text — "makes it possible in writing to eliminate inconsistencies" (pp. 49–50, 128; Ong pp. 96–97, 102); and, the book's central chapters, "the poetic significance of tables and lists" (pp. 52–111; Ong p. 96), including the Ugaritic and early-script lists (pp. 74–111; Ong p. 121) in which "the information in the lists is abstracted from the social situation in which it had been embedded ('fattened kids', 'pastured ewes', etc.)" and "lists as such have 'no oral equivalent'" (pp. 86–8, 86–7; Ong p. 121). Ong also relays Goody's warning that when anthropologists display items from oral myths as written lists "they actually deform the mental world in which the myths have their own existence" (Ong p. 97).

## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. The list-and-table argument is the part of Goody 1977 that supports the competence/institution register without the cognitive baggage: a list is an administrative instrument, "abstracted from the social situation," and the earliest writing "served mostly workaday economic and administrative purposes" (Ong p. 84, following Goody). That is a claim about what an order can do with writing, and it is the bridge to Goody 1986's bureau and file. The "domestication" framing itself is cognitive and is what Street's "autonomous model" names; the paper should cite the lists, not the domestication.

The scaffolding essay's use. Nothing on print. Goody's evidence is Mesopotamian, Egyptian and West African. The book cannot support the clerical-monopoly-to-print sequence.
