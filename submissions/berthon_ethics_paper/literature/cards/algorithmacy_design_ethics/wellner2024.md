# Wellner, G. (2024). A postphenomenological guide to AI regulation. *Journal of Human-Technology Relations*, 2(1): 1–18.

**Identifier:** doi:10.59490/jhtr.2024.2.7397 · **Read depth:** full_text (the CC-BY PDF from TU Delft OPEN, journals.open.tudelft.nl/jhtr/article/view/7397, read whole; page numbers are the journal's) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed (double-blind, the PDF's header says), new open-access journal (ISSN 2773-2266, vol. 2), in a topical collection "Postphenomenology in the Age of AI" · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Parties modeled:** human–algorithm–dataset (one experiencing "I"; a "we" is deferred to a future article, p. 5 n. 4 and p. 12) · **Remedy locus:** adoption (regulation) and artifact (disclosure of "the underlying logic governing the algorithmic system's operations," p. 13) · **Adaptivity:** adaptive · **Relation last checked:** 2026-09-18

## What it argues

Wellner rewrites Ihde's formula for AI: "the 'world' element is replaced by 'dataset,' and 'technology' by the narrower term 'algorithm,'" giving "I – algorithm – dataset" (p. 5), and her "main hypothesis is that intentionality is no longer solely human, and we are witnessing a stronger technological intentionality" (p. 5). Each of the four relations is then re-drawn with the arrow reversed toward the human. Algorithmic bodily relations, "( I ← algorithm ) → dataset": a face-recognition system where, unlike Ihde's embodiment that "must be learned," "it is the algorithm that needs to learn ('machine learning') to identify the face," and "the picture and the results of the algorithmic analysis are fed back into the dataset to improve the next rounds of operation" (p. 6); also a typing-speed mood detector used by investment banks to curb brokers (p. 6). Maximum opacity, "I ← ( algorithm – dataset )": the hermeneutic relation with two opacities, "a planned opacity, in which the algorithm is considered a trade secret," and a second "even less controllable" one that "stems from the process of machine learning," so that "even the developers of a system do not always understand how it reaches a certain decision" (p. 6); Facebook's sexist photo search fed by "a feedback loop mechanism" (p. 7); Ali et al.'s job ads delivered to men (over 90%) and white users (over 70%) for lumber, to women (over 65%) and Black users (over 75%) for janitors (p. 7). "Her," "I ← algorithm ( – dataset )": chatbots whose "dialogue is directed towards achieving certain goals, such as making the human user reach a purchase decision" (p. 8); Gmail Smart Compose, where "these emails are fed back into the system and reinforce the biased answer" (p. 8). Background collection, "( I ← ) algorithm → dataset": the user "becomes the least important element in the transaction" (p. 8); on Uber and Airbnb "service providers who earn their living from the platform ... are less important for the companies who own the platform," and "because they operate in the background, the average user cannot know whether she is discriminated against by the AI system" (p. 9). She names the new balance "relegation," "a kind of delegation that is imposed on the end user. The end user cannot resist" (p. 9).

The normative half reads Verbeek (2011), Rosenberger (2017) and Verbeek (2020). Against Rosenberger's benches: "AI systems are dramatically different from street benches mainly because they exhibit intense technological intentionality ... Whereas street benches come in various designs, AI systems exceed that multiplicity because they show different responses not only to different users, but even to the same user at the same time and place" (p. 11). Reporting Verbeek's 2020 second dimension: platforms' "filter bubbles and echo chambers that show people certain posts and block others (p. 149). When these platforms show and hide news items, they de facto manage the public space," and "they enact the rules, execute them, judge the users, and even define the exceptions, sometimes with no explanations and sometimes retroactively (Shadmy, 2019)" (p. 12). The recommendations ask regulators to turn "from the algorithm itself or the training datasets towards elucidating the underlying logic governing the algorithmic system's operations" (p. 13), and close: "AI regulation should assist users in fighting against relegation, first by revealing its existence and then by building mechanisms that would allow them to regain their intentionality" (p. 14).

## Relation to the argument

RQ1, adaptivity axis: this is the adaptivity axis stated inside postphenomenology, by one of its own. Page 11 draws exactly the line the working hypothesis predicted the school lacked: a bench is multistable across users, an AI system varies "even to the same user at the same time and place." Pages 6 and 8 supply the mechanism, results "fed back into the dataset to improve the next rounds," and p. 6 gives the consequence, an opacity that "even the developers" cannot read. The reversed arrow is her notation for what the construct calls a mediator: the technology interprets the person (p. 6), commits determinations the person cannot resist (p. 9, "relegation"), and in the chatbot case pursues an objective the person did not set (p. 8). So the critique cannot be "postphenomenology has no adaptivity axis." It has one as of 2024, in a variant one author calls her own, built after Verbeek's canonical texts, and Verbeek's schema (verbeek2015, verbeek2014) does not contain it.

What Wellner still lacks is the second human. The formula has one "I"; the "we" is explicitly deferred (p. 5 n. 4; p. 12, "yet to be pursued"). The gig-platform passage (p. 9) puts driver and passenger on the same platform and reads it only as the platform ranking the driver below the customer, which is the objective axis without the structural one. Her remedy is regulatory and disclosive: reveal relegation, restore intentionality (p. 14), locate the logic (p. 13). It goes to the adoption gate and the artifact, not to the pair. The paper's claim that all six accounts misplace the remedy therefore holds for this seventh, with the qualification that Wellner names the adaptivity the others miss.

RQ2: "relegation" is a usable term for what a seam discloses, delegation that was never offered. The two-opacity distinction (planned vs. learned, p. 6) maps onto the paper's split between contesting what a mediator commits and inferring what it is doing.

## Caution

A programmatic piece in a new journal, with policy recommendations rather than analysis of any system; its cases are press reports (Wired, Reuters, AlgorithmWatch) plus one CSCW study (Ali et al. 2019). The claim that AI responds differently "to the same user at the same time and place" is asserted, not evidenced. The Verbeek 2020 quotations are Wellner's report of pp. 144–152 of "Politicizing Postphenomenology" (see verbeek2020.md, partial). The author discloses ChatGPT and Paperpal for editing (p. 15). Her earlier statements of the I–algorithm–dataset formula (Wellner 2020c, 2021; Wellner & Rothman 2019) were not read.
