## What it argues

The abstract (verbatim): "The desire for meaning is recognized as a central human motive. Yet, knowing that people want meaning does not explain its function. What adaptive problem does this experience solve? Drawing on the feelings-as-information hypothesis, we propose that the feeling of meaning provides information about the presence of reliable patterns and coherence in the environment, information that is not provided by affect. We review research demonstrating that manipulations of stimulus coherence influence subjective reports of meaning in life but not affect. We demonstrate that manipulations that foster an associative mindset enhance meaning. The meaning-as-information perspective embeds meaning in a network of foundational functions including associative learning, perception, cognition, and neural processing. This approach challenges assumptions about meaning, including its motivational appeal, the roles of expectancies and novelty in this experience, and the notion that meaning is inherently constructed. Implications for constructed meaning and existential meanings are discussed."

Martela and Steger quote two sentences from p. 154. Coherence is "the feeling that one's experiences or life itself makes sense" (H&K 2014, p. 154, quoted at M&S p. 533); and "although these three aspects of meaning are often treated as synonymous (with each other and with meaning in life), they are potentially distinct" (p. 154, quoted at M&S p. 532). Martela and Steger also report H&K's sorting: "purpose and significance are motivational components, while coherence is a cognitive component" (M&S p. 532), and that H&K "argue that human beings have an adaptive trait that motivates us to aim to detect reliable patterns and connections in the environment, and rewards us when we are able to find such coherence that we can rely on in our lives" (M&S p. 533). The reference list on SAGE shows the paper's lineage: Antonovsky (1993), Heine, Proulx & Vohs's meaning-maintenance model (2006), Rescorla and Kamin on predictability in conditioning, Turk-Browne on statistical learning without awareness, and Schwarz and Clore's feelings-as-information theory.

## Relation to the argument

This is the immediate source behind the "coherence" facet in Martela and Steger, and it settles the direction of the RQ4 hypothesis at the level of the abstract alone. The feeling of meaning is here an *information signal* about "the presence of reliable patterns and coherence in the environment," and it moves under "manipulations of stimulus coherence" — the cited experiments expose people to ordered versus random stimuli. Regularity produces the feeling; explanation is not required, and the reference list (Rescorla, Kamin, Turk-Browne) places the account in the tradition of associative learning and statistical learning "without awareness." That is a predictability-shaped construct at its root, which is why Martela and Steger's coherence inherits that shape. For Principle III, this is the stronger ally of the two: it says that a bounded, regular environment feeds the sense of meaning directly, and that an unpredictable one starves it, without any appeal to seeing mechanism.

The source has no party structure and no remedy; it is not a critique target. What the arm cannot get from it is any claim about environments that are regular for one party and not for another, which is where a mediator's two-sidedness would bite.

## Caution

The body of the paper has not been read; the p. 154 quotations are taken on Martela and Steger's authority and should be checked against the article before use in the draft. The abstract's "reliable patterns" is about stimulus-level coherence in laboratory tasks (word triads, ordered photographs); extrapolating to a working life mediated by an AI system is this arm's inference. The abstract also says the approach "challenges … the notion that meaning is inherently constructed," which sits uneasily with any draft that treats meaning at work as something a person builds; the arm should not cite H&K for a constructivist reading. The 2014 paper is a review of the authors' own experimental program, so it is a partisan synthesis rather than a neutral one.
