## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. The task's key question — whether Graff's finding that literacy did *not* deliver the promised mobility is compatible with the narrower claim that institutions came to *require* literacy without rewarding the literate — cannot be settled from this card, and the honest answer is that it can only be settled by reading the 1991 introduction (pp. i–xliv) and chapter 7. On the description alone the two claims are logically compatible and even complementary: "institutions required it" is a statement about entry conditions, "it did not reward those who met them" a statement about returns; the "myth" Graff attacks is the second, and his social-control reading of schooling (if accurately reported) implies that institutions did impose the competence. But the paper must not claim Graff's support for the requirement thesis until someone has read him; it may only claim that his work blocks the reward thesis, and even that on secondary authority for now.

The scaffolding essay's use. Graff is the direct counter to "stripped of agency, cast out from the self-governing citizenry": his argument is precisely that illiteracy in the nineteenth century did not uniformly exclude or disadvantage, and that the promise attached to literacy was ideological. The essay's sentence is the literacy myth in its purest form. That judgement rests on the book's well-known thesis, not on pages read here.
