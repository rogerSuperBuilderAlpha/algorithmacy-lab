## What it argues

Obstfeld introduces the *tertius iungens*, "the third who joins," as a strategic orientation toward one's network in which a person connects disconnected others or fosters new coordination among people already connected, and sets it against structural-holes theory's *tertius gaudens*, whose advantage comes from keeping parties apart and playing them off. A multimethod study in the engineering division of an automotive manufacturer — survey measures of network structure and orientation plus qualitative material on innovation projects — finds that a *iungens* orientation, dense networks, and diverse social knowledge each predict greater involvement in innovation. The wider claim is about agency: the same bridging position can be worked divisively or conjunctively, and the choice matters for what the organization gets from its brokers.

## Relation to the argument

Obstfeld supplies the missing pole of the brokerage vocabulary the paper needs for RQ8: the *iungens* is the broker who introduces the parties and then steps aside, which is Simmel's mediator who "seeks to eliminate himself" and, in the construct's terms, the *channel* — after the introduction the parties reach each other and the coordination factors. The *gaudens*/*iungens* contrast is therefore the algorithmacy/literacy contrast stated as a broker's *orientation*: the same position, two dispositions toward the direct tie. That is useful because it shows the structural axis is not fixed by position alone; a platform that let matched parties contract directly thereafter would be *iungens* and would cease to be a party. The construct's claim that platforms are *gaudens* is thus a claim about a design choice (suppressing the direct channel), not about brokerage as such — and Obstfeld's finding that *iungens* brokers are the innovative ones is a quiet argument that the *gaudens* design is not the only viable one. CUTS AGAINST: Obstfeld's orientation is a person's disposition measured by survey; nothing here attaches to a mechanism, and the paper should not imply that he theorizes platforms.
