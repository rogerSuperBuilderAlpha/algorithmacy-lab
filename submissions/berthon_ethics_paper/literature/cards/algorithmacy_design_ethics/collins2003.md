## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. On its structure this is the one source in the set that does the paper's two jobs at once: chapter 2 reviews the literacy thesis (Goody, Ong) and its "vexed questions of rationality," chapter 3 the situated critique (Street, Scribner and Cole, Finnegan), and chapter 4 the institutional question the paper actually needs — "literacies and power in modern nation states." If the paper wants a single citation that both concedes the contested great divide and carries the claim that modern states came to require literacy of their members, chapters 2–4 are where to look; but that is a promise from chapter titles, not a verified finding.

The scaffolding essay's use. Chapter 4's "Euro-American lessons" on nation states is the closest thing in this set to a source for "an existential requirement for civic, religious, and economic life," and it is also, by its framing ("power," "official versus popular literacies"), the source most likely to say that the requirement was imposed rather than that print "converted" literacy into it. Unread; do not cite for content.
