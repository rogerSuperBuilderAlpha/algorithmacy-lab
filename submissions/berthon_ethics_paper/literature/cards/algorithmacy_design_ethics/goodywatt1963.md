## What it argues

Verbatim opening (p. 304): "The accepted tripartite divisions of the formal study both of mankind's past and present are to a considerable extent based on man's development first of language and later of writing. Looked at in the perspective of time, man's biological evolution shades into prehistory when he becomes a language-using animal; add writing, and history proper begins. Looked at in a temporal perspective, man as animal is studied primarily by the zoologist, man as talking animal primarily by the anthropologist, and man as talking and writing animal primarily by the sociologist." That sentence already contains the "great divide": the line between anthropology's and sociology's objects is drawn at writing.

What Ong takes from it (with the 1968 reprint's pages): the "homeostatic" character of oral societies, which "live very much in a present which keeps itself in equilibrium or homeostasis by sloughing off memories which no longer have present relevance" (Goody and Watt 1968, pp. 31–4; Ong p. 46); "direct semantic ratification," meaning of words controlled "by the real-life situations in which the word is used here and now" (p. 29; Ong p. 46); the Tiv genealogies used in court that diverged from those the British had recorded forty years earlier, and the Gonja "structural amnesia" in which Ndewura Jakpa's seven sons became five when two divisions vanished (pp. 31–3, 33; Ong pp. 47–48); genealogy as "an account describing personal relations" rather than a neutral list (p. 32; Ong p. 42); and societies "of limited literacy" that regard writing as dangerous "demanding a guru-like figure to mediate between reader and text" (Ong p. 91, citing "Goody and Watt 1968, p. 13" — which is in fact within Goody's Introduction to the volume, so the attribution may be Ong's slip). The article's best-known further claims — that the Greek alphabet, being easy to learn, underwrote a critical, sceptical and democratic public and that writing makes contradiction visible and so gives rise to logic and history — are not verified here because the pages were not read; they are reported by the sibling card and by every secondary account, and Ong's chapter 4 restates them with Havelock (Ong pp. 88–89).

## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. The article is the origin of the cognitive register and should be cited as such, i.e. as the claim under contest. The Tiv/Gonja material is, however, an institutional finding dressed in cognitive language: it shows a coordination order (courts, succession) whose ground of record was living memory adjusted to present relations, which is exactly the contrast to Clanchy's documentary order the paper wants. Citing Goody and Watt for that contrast is defensible at the competence/institution register; citing them for "alphabetic literacy produced logic and democracy" is not, and if the paper does the latter it must concede that Street, Finnegan, Halverson and Scribner and Cole reject it.

The scaffolding essay's use. The article is about ancient Greece and West Africa, not print. It gives the essay nothing on Gutenberg, the clergy, or "stripped of agency." Its opening paragraph, if anything, undercuts the essay's rhetoric: for Goody and Watt the decisive line is the advent of writing itself, not of print.
