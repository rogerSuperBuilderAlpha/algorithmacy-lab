# Flags: CEPE 2027

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Cautions kept out of the prose.]

1. **Bovens (2007) is quoted in paraphrase only.** The card gives the definition without a page. Find the page in the PDF before quoting.
2. **Peirce's reduction thesis is contested.** Herzberger's card (snippets only) reports that it holds only under stated conditions. The abstract says so in a clause. Burch (1991) is metadata only.
3. **The CP 1.363 date:** the Peirce Edition Project dates "A guess at the riddle" 1887–88. The abstract says "c. 1890" with CP.
4. **Simmel's arbitrator is chosen** by the parties. The abstract does not say platforms are chosen.
5. **Alfrink and Lyons pages:** Alfrink's are journal pages; Lyons's and Kroll's are arXiv pages. Convert them before print.
6. **The "near-existing institutions" sentence** (two-sided review disputes, online dispute resolution, labour tribunals) is a list for the paper to examine. No card supports any of them yet. The Katsh & Rifkin "fourth party" cards exist but need their wording re-checked.
7. **Double-blind:** the abstract does not mention the author's other work.
8. **Overlap with `../facct2027_triad_accountability/`:** both argue three-place accountability. FAccT is computational and audit-facing; CEPE carries the logical and philosophical argument (Peirce, Simmel). If both are accepted, the second presented must cite the first. Decide which to pursue, or keep both distinct as above.
