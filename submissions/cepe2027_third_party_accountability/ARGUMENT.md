# Argument: accountability needs three places

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. For the author's ruling.]

**P1.** Accountability, as a mechanism, is a two-place relation: an actor and a forum. The actor explains and justifies its conduct, and the forum questions it, judges it and imposes consequences. Algorithmic-accountability proposals keep this form. A decision subject contests the system's decision before a human controller or an appeal process.

**P2.** An AI system that evaluates two interdependent people and binds both stands in a genuinely three-place relation, because its verdict is about their joint conduct. On Peirce's reduction thesis, such a relation cannot be built from two-place relations without loss. Simmel's sociology shows what is lost: a third who decides can also profit from the parties' division and keep them apart.

**C.** Two-place accountability, applied to a three-place relation, misplaces responsibility. Each party contests alone, the joint conduct is never heard as joint, and responsibility falls on whichever party is nearest, the moral crumple zone. Accountability for AI-mediated systems therefore needs a three-place form: a forum before which both parties and the system's operator answer together.
- *Source for the crumple zone:* `dxd_foundation/literature/library/elish2019moral.md` (pp. 40, 49).
