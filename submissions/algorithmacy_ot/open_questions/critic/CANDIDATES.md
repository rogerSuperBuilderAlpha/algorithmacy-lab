# Candidates for the critics (stage S11)

> **[Assembled by Claude, the run's lead, 22 Nov 2026.]** Six candidate entry points, in random order.
> Each gives the asker's words (verbatim, located) and the proposed answer as the run states it. Judge
> each on its merits.

## C-A. Partner or medium: technology between people

- **Asked by:** Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024): "This new sociality—when the partner with which we are
  communicating is an algorithm—will be a critical component of further research on algorithmic
  management." (OQ-stark2024-07, p. 16, "Out of Bounds"). Whyte et al. (2023): scholars should "examine
  such forms of online organizing that involve (as Uhlin (2022) also notes) interacting with represented
  people as well as other co-located people" (OQ-whyte2023-09, p. 14, "Concluding Remarks").
- **Proposed answer:** With a third position. The system is neither the person's counterpart (a direct
  dyad) nor a channel between two people (a moderated dyad), but an intermediary that evaluates and binds
  both. The two failure modes in algorithmacy are the two pictures each taken as complete. Algorithmacy
  names what a person must be able to do once neither picture holds.

## C-B. Legitimacy tests co-produced by humans and technology

- **Asked by:** Patriotta et al. (2025): "suggesting the need to examine how legitimacy tests are
  co-produced by both human and technological actors in the contemporary digital landscape." (OQ-patriotta2025-10, p. 19,
  "Future research directions: Legitimacy in a changing world").
- **Proposed answer:** A platform stands between an organization and its audience, and it is opaque and
  adaptive. The organization must interpret outcomes it cannot see into, specify its justifications so
  they survive the platform's curation, and track the platform's changing rules. Algorithmacy is the
  ability that "competent agency" (p. 17) needs under these conditions, so it explains how humans and
  algorithms co-produce the test.

## C-C. Blind following and emergent interdependence

- **Asked by:** Hinds & von Krogh (2024): "With the introduction of GenAI, might emergent interdependence be
  disrupted because team members blindly follow guidance from the AI?" (OQ-hinds2024-04, p. 7, "Team
  coordination").
- **Proposed answer:** Blind following is algorithmacy's first failure mode: the member treats the system
  as the counterpart, and the colleague drops from view. The three parts describe the member who does not
  collapse the triad. That member works out whether a prompt reflects the system or the colleague,
  specifies intent so it reaches the colleague, and keeps track of guidance that changes as the system
  learns. Emergent interdependence is disrupted where members lack the ability and kept where they hold
  it.

## C-D. Expertise and the division of labour

- **Asked by:** Hsu & Bechky (2024): "finding a way to retain the expertise that will be needed to
  critically analyse, question and channel this powerful technology is a formidable challenge."
  (OQ-hsu2024-08, p. 12). Hinds & von Krogh (2024): "as progressive encapsulation evolves across levels and
  functions in organizations, how does the process shape the division of labor?" (OQ-hinds2024-16, p. 10,
  "Discussion").
- **Proposed answer:** Task deskilling and rising relational demand go together. As a system takes over
  tasks, what a person must be able to do shifts toward working with a counterpart through that system.
  The arrangement calls for an ability none of the askers names.

## C-E. Organization and communication as one field

- **Asked by:** Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024): "Also like them, further research should study the
  corresponding field of communication. But whereas Merton and Lazarsfeld analyzed mass communication as
  the related, yet separate, field corresponding to bureaucratic organization, in our case, organizational
  form and communication field are conjoined." (OQ-stark2024-06, p. 15, "Out of Bounds").
- **Proposed answer:** Classify communicative capacities by the form of the relation: oracy belongs to
  direct dyads, literacy to dyads moderated by a text, and algorithmacy to triads. This supplies the
  participant's part of the communication field that goes with the platform form: what a co-opted person
  must be able to do with a rating, namely interpret it, specify intent so it survives the intermediary,
  and keep track of a rule that changes.

## C-F. The co-opted person's side

- **Asked by:** Hinds & von Krogh (2024): "what are individuals' psychological responses to the new
  relational fabric in which they are embedded?" (OQ-hinds2024-21, p. 11, "Discussion"). Justesen &
  Plesner (2024): "Finally, we could account more fully for the emotional costs, mental loads and
  unintended tasks associated with digitalization." (OQ-justesen2024-13, p. 22).
- **Proposed answer:** Algorithmacy says what the person inside the arrangement must be able to do, which
  comes before the question of how the person responds. The three parts are a specified case of the
  "unintended tasks" and mental loads. They form a cycle, and people with more turns at it hold more of
  the ability than others in the same formal position. This gives the co-opted person's side of
  co-optation.
