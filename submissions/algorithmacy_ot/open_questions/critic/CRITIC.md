### C-E. Organization and communication as one field: holds with reframing

**The reframing.**

- *Which question.* OQ-stark2024-06, read with the sentence that continues it, an agenda of "genre forms, for example, the genre forms of ratings and rankings" (p. 16; OQ-stark2024-09). The paper answers the genre half from the participant's side and leaves the field to its askers.
- *Which asker.* Stark and Vanden Broeck alone, entered through their Yates paragraph (pp. 15–16) and their account of ratings that "circulate among them and the platform operator in peculiarly shaped feedback loops" (p. 14).
- *Which level.* The person. The askers "operate at the organizational level" (p. 2), and their field is made of genres, technologies and interfaces: "organizational design is inseparable from design of the digital interface" (p. 15). The paper states the step down and claims one component of that field.
- *Which part of algorithmacy.* The single criterion, "what stands between coordinating actors", and of the three parts interpreting first: reading a rating to tell the platform's doing from the counterpart's. The sequence of oracy, literacy and algorithmacy enters as a classification by what stands between two people, with Yates's written report as the literacy case. It is not a history and it does not lie over the columns of Table 1.
- *The disagreement it must state.* The askers people the field with technologies that "are themselves our communication partners" (p. 16). The paper answers their call and disputes that sentence, which is C-A.

**Objections I sustain.**

1. *Level and object* (Seat 1 obj. 1 and 4; Seat 2 obj. 1). The text is as quoted above (pp. 2, 15, 16). A typology of capacities is one input to the field.
2. *The partner premise* (Seat 1 obj. 3), at p. 16.
3. *Table 1* (Seat 1 obj. 2), in part. The table runs factory, project, platform (p. 14), and the askers add that "none of the three styles of management is confined to its initial period" (p. 7). The three capacities do not map onto those columns, so the paper cannot present its sequence beside the table as a parallel history.
4. *Person or profile* (Seat 1 obj. 5). For the askers "the user—as nothing more than an ensemble of data points—has no personhood from the algorithm’s perspective" (p. 9). An ability that grows with turns needs a holder, and the paper must say which of the two it is.
5. *The CCO school* (Seat 3 obj. 1 and 2), as a duty of positioning. Dion et al. hold that organizationality "is a property of communication" (p. 11) and that "binary relations are meaningless without a third term that provides interpretive grounding for the situation" (p. 15). Their third is a term of meaning, as the shortlist ruled; the paper concedes the field-level conjoining to that school and separates a third of meaning from a party that evaluates. Hall and Kuhn (2025), a review from that school by its title, cites the asking article and is unread (card held).
6. *Precedents for the sequence* (Seat 3 obj. 4). Emigh (2024) and Heyd (2021) sequence by medium, and Gloy (2025) is unread (`GAP_CHECK.md`). The criterion is the claim.

**Objections I do not sustain.**

- *Seat 2 obj. 2, that the literacy row fails against the askers' lineage.* Yates's technology is "a medium for communicating orders from top to bottom or for reports issued by subordinates to superiors" (p. 16). An order or a report is a text between a superior and a subordinate, which is the brief's dyad moderated by a text, repeated along a chain. The askers' own precedent supports the literacy row. The objection holds for mass communication alone (p. 15), which is not a relation between two people and which the classification does not place.
- *Seat 2 obj. 5, that "technology as organization" leaves no separate third* (p. 16). The point bears on Simmel's figure, panel objection 3. The triangle on p. 14 keeps the operator at the apex whether or not technology and structure are synonyms.

**Panel objections.** The entry worsens 1: the criterion is the form of the relation, so the charge that the typology sorts positions strikes the classification itself. The askers give partial cover, since their ratings are "of and by providers and users" (p. 4); nothing in their text shows both corners bound. It worsens 3: the operator "benefits from her agency without her acting as its agent" and "leverages rather than delegates" (p. 14), which is the profiting third of the panel's objection. It worsens 4, because a communication-field entry draws the readers who own audience design and the algorithmic audience. It worsens 6 unless the author settles it: the literacy analogy becomes the centre of the paper, and under a relation-form criterion the third member is defined by a triad, which needs no algorithm, while the name points at the medium. It defuses 2: the criterion needs no Okhuysen and Bechky, at the price of GDOC's grounding for the three parts. It leaves 5 where it was.

**Pick-up.** The least taken up of the ten: three flagged rows, one weak and unframed take-up, no answer, and one likely engagement unread.

### C-C. Blind following and emergent interdependence: holds with reframing

**The reframing.**

- *Which question.* The first horn of OQ-hinds2024-04 only, restated as a condition (when does following disrupt), and joined to the call that follows it: "We need theorizing that captures what shapes team participants’ dynamic relations with AI to coordinate their work." (p. 8; OQ-hinds2024-25). The second horn, that emergent interdependence might be "less necessary as the AI reliably identifies more nuanced requirements for coordination" (pp. 7–8), lies outside a construct that presupposes interdependent work, and the paper says so. The askers' "stance toward GenAI is neutral" (p. 2), so the paper also says when following the system is the right call.
- *Which asker.* Hinds and von Krogh.
- *Which level.* The member, as a condition of a team property. Emergent interdependence holds "between a core team and a shifting group at the periphery" (p. 7). The paper either states the step from members' abilities to the team's property or claims the member-level mechanism alone. The askers "call for process theories" (p. 11); the cycle of repeated turns is the process element to bring forward.
- *Which part of algorithmacy.* The first failure mode. Interpreting, applied to the passage's own object: statements the system synthesizes from "specialists’ needs" that "prompt each participant to reflect on their work as it relates to the activities of other participants" (p. 7), where a member has to tell the colleague's need from the system's inference. Keeping track, tied to progressive encapsulation (pp. 5–6).
- *The cost.* Scope. In the question's passage the system guides and returns no verdict. The paper can pay in one of two ways. It can widen "binding" to guidance that members come to follow, arguing the continuity from the askers' text, which sets "responses to AI-generated guidance" beside "resistance to algorithmic management" (p. 9). Or it can keep the definition and anchor in the askers' evaluating and allocating cases: GenAI "determining job assignments or pay increases" (p. 4), a hiring algorithm "inaccessible to the manager" (p. 6), brokerage in which members' "actions are always recorded and under surveillance" (p. 9). The second way concedes that the question is asked elsewhere. The brief's rule caps either at fit 3.

**Objections I sustain.**

1. *Two horns* (Seat 1 obj. 1; Seat 2 obj. 4), at pp. 7–8.
2. *Team level and process* (Seat 1 obj. 2 and 4; Seat 3 obj. 2), at pp. 7, 8 and 11.
3. *Guidance, not a verdict* (Seat 1 obj. 6; Seat 2 obj. 2; Seat 3 obj. 3), at p. 7. This is the scope cost above.
4. *Circular as worded* (Seat 2 obj. 1). The candidate's sentence, "disrupted where members lack the ability and kept where they hold it", defines the ability by its outcome. The cure is to specify the ability by what a member does and to state the outcome as a prediction.
5. *Older names* (Seat 2 obj. 5; Seat 3 obj. 5), including one in the journal: "infantilization", the "subordination of individuals’ judgement to external judgement by systems, algorithms and/or machines that ‘know better’" (Scherer and Neesham, p. 6).
6. *Team-level rivals in the pick-up* (Seat 3 obj. 4): Steiger et al.'s delegation calibration (p. 15), Hubert's evidence artefacts (p. 25), and outside the citing set Yuh et al.'s team competence (`GAP_CHECK.md`; abstract and excerpts).

**Objections I do not sustain.**

- *Seat 2 obj. 3, that the askers' mechanism denies the third part.* The askers say the process turns opaque while "we can study relations and functions as input and as output" (p. 6), and they "recommend focusing on inputs and outputs" (p. 11). Keeping track of a rule from its outputs is what adaptivity calls for in the brief, and opacity is the first feature of the brief's intermediary. Seat 1 reads the same pages as the entry's best fit (its obj. 7). The passage does set a ceiling the paper should state: the askers expect visibility to keep shrinking.
- *Seat 1 obj. 3, the reversed causal arrow*, in part. Encapsulation as cause and members' ability as a condition are compatible. The paper must present the ability as what matters under encapsulation, and must not present members' deficits as the cause.
- *Seat 3 obj. 1, that Scherer and Neesham already supply the capacity.* Their "infantilization" names the failure and belongs in the paper. Their remedy does not rival the triadic content: "reversibility of standpoints" is "the potential for ‘changing one’s mind’ and adopting a different position" (p. 14), a principle of public deliberation among citizens, with no colleague, no joint task and no system between two people.

Seat 2's verdict of shoehorned rests on its objections 1, 2 and 3. The first is curable, the third does not survive the text, and the second is the scope cost the reframing pays in the open. I do not follow that verdict.

**Panel objections.** The entry worsens 1: the question's passage does not make the system the colleagues' only route to each other, the system at p. 7 evaluates none of them, and in the p. 4 case it evaluates the employee for the manager. It worsens 4. It worsens 5, for a reason other than Seat 2's: the askers themselves point to "resistance to algorithmic management" (p. 9) and to drivers who "play games with the algorithm" (p. 4), so the intermediary is not beyond answer. It defuses 2 in part, on Seat 2's reading (its obj. 6): the askers describe members who "anticipated and adapted their own work to the needs of those specialists" (p. 7), so common understanding has a referent in the asker's text. I did not test that reading against Okhuysen and Bechky (2009), which the run's store does not hold. It defuses 6 in part: the conditions the parts answer are properties of the technology here, "GenAI’s malleable features of learning, adaptation, and, especially, encapsulation" (p. 9). It leaves 3 where it was.

**Pick-up.** Eight flagged rows and four cards, each a take-up and none an answer; the nearest rival is a team process.

### C-A. Partner or medium: holds with reframing, as a contest

**The reframing.**

- *Which question.* The candidate offered a choice between partner and medium as if OT authors posed it. The shortlist says the split is its own reading, and with Whyte et al. gone no OT asker holds the medium side. What the askers' text does hold is both pictures in one sentence: "A line is crossed, however, when technologies are no longer merely the means of communication among parties but themselves become communication partners" (p. 9). The paper enters at that line and at the call that follows from it (OQ-stark2024-07, p. 16). It amends the premise: between the means and the partner stands the position the askers draw themselves.
- *Which asker.* Stark and Vanden Broeck alone. Whyte et al. leave the entry. Their video-conference meeting is "a pervasive medium of organizing" (p. 14) and judges no one, so it can serve only as a contrast case, the moderated dyad.
- *Which level.* The person inside a triangle that belongs to the askers: "a triangular structure (Vallas & Schor, 2020) with the platform operator at the apex and producers and users at the other corners" (p. 14).
- *Which part of algorithmacy.* The two failure modes, as the two pictures each taken whole, and then the three parts. The paper claims the ability and does not claim the position.
- *The restriction.* Only settings with a person on the far side. Most of the askers' examples at p. 16 have none (Duolingo, Happify, textwith.me, purchases, travel bookings). Their example at p. 9 has: specialists who coordinate with one another, in a setting where "the summaries, scheduling, and agendas of meetings are determined by algorithms using generative AI" (p. 9). The paper enters there.

**Objections I sustain.**

1. *The answer denies the premise* (Seat 1 obj. 1; Seat 2 obj. 1), at pp. 9 and 16. Toward OQ-stark2024-07 the paper contests; it does not answer.
2. *Partial coverage* (Seat 1 obj. 2), at p. 16.
3. *The triad is the askers'* (Seat 1 obj. 3; Seat 3 obj. 1), at pp. 4, 9 and 14.
4. *Level* (Seat 1 obj. 4), at p. 2.
5. *Whyte et al.* (Seat 1 obj. 5 and 6; Seat 2 obj. 2; Seat 3 obj. 6), at p. 14.
6. *The third who profits* (Seat 2 obj. 4), at p. 14.
7. *A first version of the skill exists* (Seat 3 obj. 2): Curchod et al.'s sellers "exploit their practical knowledge of the algorithm" (`GAP_CHECK.md`, abstract only; I did not re-read it). The paper names them.

**Updated by the pick-up.** Seat 3 read Bujold et al. (2026) as an abstract. Read in full, they hold a third position in the worker–organization relation and reject both pictures, with an intermediary that acts "on behalf of one party" (p. 3), and they name two of the three parts as conditions (pp. 11, 15; `PICKUP.md`, candidate 3). The position is therefore taken twice, by the askers and by the closest citing work. The ground left to the paper is an intermediary between two people that evaluates both, and the ability as an ability.

**Objection I sustain only in part.** *Seat 2 obj. 3, the lopsided triangle.* The eBay sentence does run one way (p. 14). The same page has ratings "by users (e.g., clients on Talkspace) and by providers (e.g., sellers on eBay)" circulating among them and the operator, and p. 4 has ratings "of and by providers and users". Evaluation of both corners is in the askers' text. Binding of both is not, and Zelles et al. show the asymmetry in one case: "We can rate the riders, and they can rate the drivers", and the driver alone is suspended (p. 16).

**Panel objections.** The entry worsens 1, on binding: the closest rival's intermediary is unilateral by definition. It worsens 3 (p. 14). It worsens 4 a little: the citing literature already holds names for parts of the skill. It worsens 6: for Bujold et al. algorithmic management "erodes the buffering role of human managers between the organization’s practices or decisions and the employees" (p. 2), so a person held the middle position before an algorithm did. On 5 the evidence splits. The askers call accountability "distributed, deflected, and denied" (p. 14), and Zelles et al.'s couriers "had no formal avenue to contest “system decisions”" (p. 16); Bujold et al. make "Correctability: Perceived opportunity to appeal and correct erroneous AM procedures" a dimension that varies (Table 2, p. 10). It avoids 2 if the parts keep ASM's grounding.

**Pick-up.** Twenty-nine flagged rows, seven cards, one answer in its own scope and no contest.

### C-F. The co-opted person's side: holds with reframing, on the Justesen leg

**The reframing.**

- *Which question.* The "unintended tasks" clause of OQ-justesen2024-13 (p. 22), with the gap the same paragraph states: invisible digi-work is "neither systematically analysed nor theorized in the organizational literature on digitalization" (p. 22). The paper theorizes one family of such tasks. It does not account for costs or loads, which the askers ask of "more empirical studies" (p. 22).
- *Which asker.* Justesen and Plesner. The Hinds leg is shoehorned: OQ-hinds2024-21 asks for "psychological responses" and for outcomes "such as stress, creativity, sense of control, psychological safety, turnover intentions" (p. 11). The candidate says algorithmacy comes before that question. An antecedent does not answer it, and the brief "measures no one".
- *Which level.* The individual, which fits.
- *Which part of algorithmacy.* The three parts as tasks the arrangement imposes without acknowledging them, a specification for settings with a counterpart of compensating work, "activities that compensate for errors, limitations, or other shortcomings related to digital technologies that promise more than they can deliver" (p. 12); algorithmacy is the ability to carry them. The claim that people with more turns hold more of the ability stays, set beside the askers' point that such work is "devalued economically, socially, or morally" (p. 9).
- *Two changes the entry forces.* A new claim, that exercising the ability is unrecognized work; the hand-check on shortlist candidate 6 called this "a new claim for the paper to argue", and under the brief's rule it changes the genus. And a changed treatment of "pacifying the algorithm", which the askers file as invisible emotional labour (p. 15) and the brief files as a failure mode.

**Objections I sustain.**

1. *The Hinds leg* (Seat 1 obj. 1; Seat 2 obj. 1; Seat 3 obj. 6), at p. 11.
2. *Pacifying* (Seat 1 obj. 3; Seat 2 obj. 2; Seat 3 obj. 1), at p. 15.
3. *Devalued labour recast as an asset* (Seat 1 obj. 2; Seat 2 obj. 3), at pp. 3, 8, 9 and 20, with a limit the askers supply: naming the work "may bestow recognition on the workers and the work" (p. 20), and their note 2 grants that "a certain degree of invisibility is the precondition for professional autonomy and learning" (p. 22).
4. *People inside the intermediary* (Seat 1 obj. 4). A rider's and a driver's meeting "hinges on another woman, two oceans away" (p. 10, quoting Gray and Suri). The brief's "system, counterpart, or both" has a fourth possibility.
5. *The asker's categories already fit the parts* (Seat 1 obj. 6; Seat 2 obj. 4; Seat 3 obj. 2), at pp. 10 and 12. The paper specifies a category and cannot call the parts unnamed.
6. *The ability is supplied by others* (Seat 2 obj. 6): case workers act as "supporters, teachers and IT problem solvers" (p. 13).

- *Seat 3 obj. 5, that Rikala et al. measure differential capacity in one formal position.* Their subgroups differ in technostress; they do not measure an ability.

**Panel objections.** The entry defuses 1 in part, since both askers pose their questions from one seat, if the paper accepts that its unit is a position. It worsens 3, on Seat 2's inference (its obj. 7): the operator profits most from the most able user. It worsens 4: the askers have compensating work (p. 12) and connecting work, "the maintenance and repair of digitally afforded connections" (p. 10), and the pick-up has users positioned as "interpreters" (Choroszewicz, p. 9). It worsens 5, since repair is in the askers' definition, though their microworkers "often struggle to identify who to contact" (p. 11). It leaves 2 and 6 where they were.

### C-D. Expertise and the division of labour: shoehorned

All three seats rule so, and the texts agree.

- Hsu and Bechky name the ability the candidate says no asker names: "the expertise that will be needed to critically analyse, question and channel this powerful technology" (p. 12), expertise "rooted in their knowledge and experiential learning" (p. 9) and reproduced through writers' rooms. Their problem is to retain it across a career system, with change "at the level of individual work tasks, skills and specializations, career ladders, organizational decision-making and broader market structures" (p. 11).
- Hinds and von Krogh ask how encapsulation "evolves across levels and functions in organizations" (p. 10) and offer hyper-specialization, hyper-generalization or an amalgam (pp. 10–11). Algorithmacy chooses none of them.
- Neither passage holds a system between two people that evaluates them.
- The askers' forecasts run against rising relational demand: "the number of creatives needed throughout the production process will substantially decrease" (Hsu and Bechky, p. 9), and GenAI "might absorb entirely the function of informal coordination and minimize the relations between team members" (Hinds and von Krogh, p. 7). Seat 2 overstates the second as a prediction; it is a conjecture under a neutral stance, and the hyper-generalization branch leaves people "supplier negotiations, strategy, and leadership" (p. 11). The paper would still have to argue against it.
- The pick-up already names the new demand: "fonctions d'orchestration" (Hubert, p. 7) and "argumentation labor" (S.G. and Lyytinen, abstract only).

The brief's claim that task deskilling and rising relational demand go together can stand in a discussion, argued against these forecasts. It cannot carry the entry. The entry worsens panel objections 4 and 6 and defuses none.

## What algorithmacy can claim that no rival claims

**What rivals already hold.**

- *The position.* Stark and Vanden Broeck draw the triangle (p. 14). Bujold et al. define a technological intermediary that stands "between two parties in a relationship" and acts "on behalf of one party" (pp. 2–3). Zelles et al. observe a platform that "stands formally independent of both parties" (p. 17). Outside the citing set, Pfadenhauer and Kirschner (2017) move from dyad to triad (`GAP_CHECK.md`).
- *A skill, a labour or a trust directed at the system.* Bujold et al.'s moderator, by which knowledgeable workers "better distinguish between system inherent flaws and organizational choices" (p. 15). Choroszewicz's users as "interpreters" (p. 9). S.G. and Lyytinen's "argumentation labor" (abstract only). Hong et al.'s "algorithmic skills" and Curchod et al.'s "practical knowledge of the algorithm" (`GAP_CHECK.md`, abstracts only).
- *The colleague dropping from view, and a remedy at team level.* Johnsson and Nikolausson (p. 45); Steiger et al. (p. 15); Yuh et al. (`GAP_CHECK.md`).
- *A third that grounds a relation.* Dion et al. (p. 15), where the third is a term of meaning.
- *A sequence from orality through literacy to a digital stage.* Emigh (2024) and Heyd (2021), by medium (`GAP_CHECK.md`).
- *A competence in the asker's own text.* Hsu and Bechky's expertise (p. 12); Patriotta et al.'s competent agency, held by organizations (p. 17).

**What is left.**

1. *An ability whose object is another person.* In every rival the object of the skill is the system or its output. Algorithmacy's object is a counterpart, reached through a system that evaluates the pair. Two of its parts have neighbours stated as conditions or as labour: interpreting (Bujold et al., p. 15; Choroszewicz, p. 9) and keeping track (Bujold et al., p. 11). The second part, specifying intent so that it survives the intermediary and reaches the counterpart, has no neighbour in the cards, and the seats found none in the corpus; the panel's objection 4 gives it an older name outside both, audience design. The nearest in the cards are a system feature, Bujold et al.'s "human influence" (p. 8), and a report in Decius of people who "strategically present themselves as more analytical" to an AI evaluator (p. 15), with no counterpart.
2. *The criterion.* Communicative capacities classified by what stands between coordinating actors, which is the form of the relation and not the medium.
3. *The two together.* The capacity placed by that criterion, as the third member after oracy and literacy.

I found no work that names an individual's ability to do interdependent work with another person through an intermediary that evaluates both, or that classifies communicative capacities by the form of the relation, in these sources: the 66-item corpus as its records, the hand-check and the shortlist report it, which I did not re-read at this stage; the six asking articles at the pages listed above; the 19 carded citing works, at the depths `PICKUP.md` states; and the gap check's searches of the OpenAlex search API and Consensus on 22 November 2026, for shortlist candidates 1 to 3, with Scholar Gateway unavailable.

**What the paper cannot claim on this evidence.** The triad. The third position between partner and channel. That the parts are unnamed. That the intermediary binds both parties, which no asker's text and no card shows. The communication field itself.

**Reads owed before any of this enters a manuscript.** Hall and Kuhn (2025); Gloy (2025); Curchod et al. (2020) in full; S.G. and Lyytinen (2026) in full; Snyder (2025).

## Flags for the author

These concern the author's text as the brief reports it. I report them and change nothing.

1. **"Pacifying the algorithm."** The brief pairs the phrase with "ignoring the counterpart", as the first failure mode (GDOC §4.3, per the brief). In Justesen and Plesner's account of Bucher et al. (2021), workers "swallow their negative emotions both during interactions with clients, but also in replying to negative reviews", and that is why they spend the effort "aimed through direct and indirect practices at pacifying the algorithm" (p. 15). The client is in it. I have read neither Bucher et al. nor GDOC §4.3. If the manuscript takes the phrase from Bucher et al., the pairing needs a check against that source.
2. **"Evaluates and binds" both.** Evaluation of both parties has support in the asking article (Stark and Vanden Broeck, pp. 4, 14) and in one citing work (Zelles et al., p. 16). Binding of both has none in the texts I read, and the closest rival defines its intermediary as acting for one party (Bujold et al., pp. 2–3).
3. **Answerability varies.** Zelles et al. report "no internal appeal" on Upwork (p. 17) and contestation after the fact in traditional firms (p. 20); Bujold et al. treat the opportunity to appeal as a dimension (Table 2, p. 10).
