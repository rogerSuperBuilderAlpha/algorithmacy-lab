## How each version frames the paper

### Problem

- **GDOC: coordination.** "how do two people work together when an opaque, adaptive system sits
  between them to judge and control their actions?" (Introduction ¶3). The frame is Okhuysen and Bechky's
  (2009) integrating conditions: algorithmacy "restore[s] the essential organizational conditions of
  accountability, shared understanding, and predictability" (Introduction ¶2).
- **ASM: a dilemma between two pictures.** "how to treat the system as something that decides without
  losing sight of the other person" (Abstract). One picture treats the system as the counterpart (a
  direct dyad), the other as a channel (a moderated dyad), and the paper names this "the dyad dilemma"
  (Introduction ¶3–4).
- **PAPER: displacement and co-optation.** Generative AI quietly displaces institutional values, and the
  debate "still leaves open how someone coordinates through a cooptive, opaque, adaptive intermediary"
  (Introduction ¶1). Organizations are "giving books to people who lack literacy" (¶3).

### Gap

- **GDOC:** neighbouring fields stay "trapped in a dyadic framework", and organization theory has "not
  yet explained what practical sensibility an individual needs to coordinate with another person inside
  that arrangement" (§2 ¶3).
- **ASM:** the triadic view (Cameron, Curchod et al., Stark & Vanden Broeck) "has established what the
  arrangement is and how it controls the people in it, [but] has not said what a person inside the
  arrangement must be able to do" (Introduction ¶4). Stelmaszak et al. (2026) place the capability in
  the organization, "which leaves the person's own part without a name".
- **PAPER:** OT "has successfully mapped this macro-level shift in structural control", while
  micro-foundational models stay "trapped in ... a persistent, set-theoretic dyadic ontology" (§2 ¶1).

### Contribution

- **GDOC:** two contributions to platform and algorithmic-management research: connecting "macro-level
  accounts of platform co-optation to everyday workplace practice", and moving the opacity debate "from
  technical transparency to practical, situated competence" (Discussion ¶2). The three competencies
  restore accountability, common understanding and predictability.
- **ASM:** two proposals. First, the form of the relation is the single criterion that separates
  oracy, literacy and algorithmacy. Second, algorithmacy has three parts, each answering one feature of
  the intermediary (opacity → interpreting, bindingness → specifying intent, adaptivity → keeping
  track). The paper offers "the co-opted person's side" of Stark and Vanden Broeck's form, and three
  courses of action for organizations.
- **PAPER:** unwritten beyond the introduction, which names three competencies and calls algorithmacy
  "a situated engagement with the unseen" (Porsfelt et al., 2026).

### The paper's own research agenda (GDOC and ASM)

Both end with the same four questions: (1) whether the three parts are distinct, and whether
separating a rule change from a counterpart's change is a fourth part (Rahman, 2021; DeVito, 2021);
(2) whether algorithmacy explains outcomes once platform dependence and past score drops are held
constant; (3) whether algorithmacy differs from general digital skill and ordinary communication skill;
(4) GDOC asks about asymmetric triads and generative AI in professional work; ASM asks which of three
courses organizations take.

## Where the three agree and where they differ

**Agree:** the irreducible triad of participant, counterpart and an intermediary that evaluates and
binds both; oracy → literacy → algorithmacy as a sequence; three parts (interpreting, specifying intent,
keeping track); Simmel's third party; Stark and Vanden Broeck's co-optation; the two dyadic failure
modes; generative AI carrying the triad into professional work.

**Differ:**

| Point | GDOC | ASM | PAPER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genus of the construct | "sensibility" in headings, "skill" and "competencies" in text | "ability" | "sensibility" operating through "competencies and capacities" |
| Main theoretical anchor | Okhuysen & Bechky (coordination) | Form of the relation (Simmel, Powell) | Displacement, co-optation, organizationality (Orlikowski & Scott; Stark; Schoeneborn et al.) |
| What the three parts are grounded in | The integrating conditions they restore | The intermediary's three features | Not yet grounded |
| Generative AI | "printing press" for the dynamic | Named in passing | The opening problem |

These differences matter for the run: a question about coordination favours GDOC's framing, a question
about the form of organizing or about platforms favours ASM's, and a question about AI's quiet
displacement of institutions favours PAPER's.
