## Candidate 1. Organization and communication as one field (OQ-stark2024-06)

- **Takes up:** gulbrandsen2025 (weak; the authors do not frame it as Stark and Vanden Broeck's call).
- **Passing:** alishani2025 (does not cite stark2024).
- **Not touched:** zelles2026. The triage tag came from the title; the paper never discusses communication, genres or a field.
- **Not classifiable:** hall2025, which I could not open.
- **Answers or contests:** none.

**Convergence.** The one work that takes the question up, Gulbrandsen and Just, finds organization and communication joined in the platform: "people's ability to participate is constrained by the platforms' ability to organise publics" (p. 162). That is Stark and Vanden Broeck's conjoining, seen from the public sphere rather than from an organization. Alishani et al. add, in passing, that a chatbot gives citizens "a much more direct and uninterrupted form of communication" with government (p. 41). No work names a genre, a rating as a genre, or a communication field that belongs to the platform form.

**Does any work give algorithmacy's answer?** No. None describes the participant's part of the field: interpreting a rating, specifying intent so that it survives the intermediary, and keeping track of a rule that changes. Gulbrandsen and Just come nearest in one sentence: "when users become aware that they are interacting with technologies rather than directly with each other, this awareness shapes their social and their technical interactions" (p. 161). They state it and move on. The candidate stays as open as the triage found it ("nearly absent"), with one gap: Hall and Kuhn's review, the likeliest engagement by its title, is unread.

## Candidate 3. Partner or medium: technology between people

- **Takes up:** bujold2026 (abstract), alishani2025.
- **Passing:** gulbrandsen2025, zelles2026, decius2026 (abstract), avnoon2026 (abstract).
- **Answers or contests:** none.

**Convergence.** Three works place the system between parties rather than at either pole. Bujold et al. call it "a technological intermediary between workers and organization" and trace how trust in the system becomes trust in the organization (abstract). Alishani et al. show a chatbot that seems to be the citizen's counterpart while backstage professionals "apply discretion in making technological artifacts fit with real-world conversations" (p. 41). Zelles et al. describe the delivery platform as standing "formally independent of both parties", courier and customer, while its rating algorithm sanctions one on the other's word (p. 17). Decius goes the other way: he treats the AI as a second agent whose layer couples to the person's, which is the party premise.

**Does any work give algorithmacy's answer?** Partly in structure, not in substance. Zelles et al. describe the triad the brief names, two people and an intermediary that evaluates one on the other's verdict, but only as a finding about accountability. Bujold et al. use the word "intermediary" for a worker–organization relation and ask about justice judgments and trust. Neither work says what a person must be able to do once neither the partner picture nor the channel picture holds. The third position is visible in this literature; the competence that goes with it is not.

## Candidate 7. Artificial persons, technology as structure

- **Takes up:** decius2026 (abstract; party side), phillips2026 (abstract; structure side).
- **Not touched:** chao2026 (the agentic AI is a delegate, not a member).
- **Answers or contests:** none.

**Convergence.** The two works split along the two questions of the candidate. Decius extends social cognitive theory to "conjoined human–AI agency" (abstract), so the machine is a co-agent; his "second triad" is person, behaviour and environment doubled into a human and an AI layer, not a mediating third between people. Phillips, who asked the artificial-persons question in 2024, argues in 2026 that organization theory has missed "a world of organizations constituted by" intelligent technologies, and lists as the first cause "early framings of technology as exogenous to organizations" (abstract). That is the structure question, OQ-stark2024-08, from the asker of OQ-phillips2024-09.

**Does any work give algorithmacy's answer?** No. Algorithmacy contests the artificial-persons framing and adopts technology as a structure of the relation. Phillips 2026 moves toward the second question (on the abstract) but does not reject the first, and he puts what changes in theory, not in what members must be able to do. Decius strengthens the framing that algorithmacy contests. The full Phillips text would settle whether he drops "artificial persons".

## Candidate 8. Accountability when an algorithm decides (OQ-stark2024-04)

- **Answers:** zelles2026.
- **Takes up:** chao2026, zientara2026 (abstract).
- **Passing:** alishani2025.
- **Contests:** none.

**Convergence.** The works answer "who is accountable" with "the nearest human, unless an organization re-anchors it". Zelles et al. find three variants in Hungary: on delivery platforms "responsibility is systematically displaced from the platform … to the courier … and the customer" (p. 17); on Upwork it is displaced to the client; in traditional firms "formal managerial accountability is preserved rather than displaced" (p. 20). Alishani et al. place discretion in "opaque backstage practices" and call for accountability forums (p. 41). Chao and Ma convert the question into design: the "accountability architecture is a design variable" (p. 2) that sets how much autonomy is safe. Zientara restates the question as the first of three ethical tests, "who bears responsibility for a system's consequences" (abstract).

**Does any work give algorithmacy's answer?** No, and this is expected: the shortlist says algorithmacy answers how a participant assigns an outcome to a source so that two people can go on working together, and "does not say who is liable". All four works answer, or ask, the liability and governance question. Zelles et al. supply evidence for the participant's problem without theorizing it: their coding of a Wolt courier's complaint records "opacity of the system; denial of the rating's existence; no addressable counterpart" (Table, p. 10). That is the interpreting task left undone, and a source the paper could cite for it.
