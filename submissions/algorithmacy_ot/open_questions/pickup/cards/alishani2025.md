## What the work says

Alishani, Homburg and Velsberg treat the government chatbot as a conversational party that "mimic[s] face-to-face interactions" (abstract, p. 35) while showing that backstage professionals keep shaping what it says, so the encounter is between a citizen and a public body through a system that seems to be the counterpart. They set three research directions — discretion of developers, citizens' emotions and acceptance, and accountability forums — and they do not theorize what the citizen must be able to do.
