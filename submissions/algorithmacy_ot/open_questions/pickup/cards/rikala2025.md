## What the work says

Rikala et al. survey Finnish employees and, from open answers, find the five forms of meta-work the literature names (adaptation, configuration, contact and information management, troubleshooting, administrative reporting); by cluster analysis they sort respondents into four technostress subgroups that differ by skill, age and organization. Meta-work, above all troubleshooting and constant learning, raises technostress, and the authors read this as support for Justesen and Plesner's "hidden costs of digital transformation" (p. 17).

## Caution

Meta-work here is work on the technology (setting it up, fixing it, learning it), not work through it with a counterpart; none of the five forms is interpreting a colleague via a system. The evidence is a self-report survey with a small share of meta-work responses (14%). It is useful as measured support for candidate 4's "mental loads and unintended tasks", not as a precedent for algorithmacy's three parts.
