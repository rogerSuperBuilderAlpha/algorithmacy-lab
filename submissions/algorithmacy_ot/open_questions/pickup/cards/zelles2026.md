## What the work says

Zelles, Pap and Makó answer "who is accountable" with three empirical variants: twisted accountability on delivery and ride-hailing platforms, where responsibility moves to courier and customer; client-displaced accountability on Upwork, where the platform's rating algorithm enforces the client's judgment; and "hybrid, partially re-anchored" accountability in traditional firms, where a line manager or partner keeps formal responsibility. They propose a "partial Möbius effect" for the bounded spread of platform-like governance into hierarchies, and present it as a hypothesis.

## Caution

The answer is about where liability and responsibility land, the legal and managerial question that the shortlist says algorithmacy does not address. It gives no account of how a participant traces an outcome to its source. The Hungarian sample is small and the authors flag the Möbius effect as untested beyond two cases (abstract, p. 1).
