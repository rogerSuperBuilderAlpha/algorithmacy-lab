## What the work says

Chao and Ma reconcile a "delegation tradition", in which they place Stark and Vanden Broeck, with an "accountability tradition" by modelling the value of AI autonomy as efficiency minus error and oversight costs, with four accountability architectures (none, ex-post traceability, ex-ante boundary setting, hybrid) shifting the optimal autonomy level; a stylized run moves it from 3.7 to 4.3 under hybrid accountability (p. 2). They turn "who or what is accountable" into the question of which accountability architecture makes a given autonomy level safe [S10e: this paraphrase stood in quotation marks; the words are not the authors'], a design answer at the organization's level.

## Caution

The paper reads Stark and Vanden Broeck as a pro-delegation design theory, which ignores their own accountability principle; treat its framing of the source with care. The computational results are labelled illustrative by the authors (p. 2), and the incident panel is a public proxy (p. 27). It is a preprint.
