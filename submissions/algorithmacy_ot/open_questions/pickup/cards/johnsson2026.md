## What the work says

From twelve interviews in two Swedish audit and accounting firms, Johnsson and Nikolausson find that employees turn to AI first when uncertain, to prepare before asking a colleague or to avoid using a colleague's time, so AI becomes "an additional layer" between the individual and the team. This improves the questions people bring to colleagues, but some learning stays private ("A lot stays between me and the chat", p. 45), and teams may lose the informal exchanges through which members know each other's expertise.

## Caution

A master's thesis with twelve interviews. It counts as a citing work only through stark2024, cited for a definition; it does not engage Hinds and von Krogh. It documents the colleague dropping from view as a matter of fewer exchanges, not as members following AI guidance blindly, and it treats AI as a resource beside the team, not as an intermediary that evaluates members.
