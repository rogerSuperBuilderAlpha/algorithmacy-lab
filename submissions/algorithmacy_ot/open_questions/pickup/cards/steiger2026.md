## What the work says

Studying 28 student teams (119 participants) in a 48-hour innovation sprint, Steiger et al. find GenAI taking four temporary roles (servant, counselor, navigator, oracle) and theorize "delegation calibration": teams set GenAI's permitted influence, expected output and intended reliance for each task episode, and retain authority to accept or reject its output. Reliance rises most in the oracle role, when teams are exhausted and let GenAI propose a direction they may lack capacity to evaluate (p. 13). In the navigator role, GenAI's synthesis can erode the team's own voice.

## Caution

The finding implies a conditional "no" to the blind-following question: teams calibrated rather than followed blindly, and the risk of over-reliance appears where the team's own capacity is exhausted. But the paper never poses the question, the unit is the team's use of GenAI as a resource, and members' relations to each other through the system are not analysed. The sample is students in a sprint, and the data are retrospective focus-group accounts, not interaction logs (p. 16).
