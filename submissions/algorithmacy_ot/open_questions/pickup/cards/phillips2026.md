## Candidates touched

**7. Artificial persons, technology as structure — takes_up (both sides).** Class unchanged; the reading of it changes. The phrase "artificial persons" does not occur in the essay, and Phillips neither repeats nor withdraws it. He holds two pictures at once. On the structure side he names the assumption to be dropped:
> "The overriding assumption causing this problem is the belief among many institutional theorists that technology is something an organization has rather than something an organization is." (p. 250)

On the agent side he keeps generative AI as a participant: it can act as "institutional entrepreneurs" (p. 242), is among the "active participants in the process of sensemaking" (p. 244), and
> "The fact that we are no longer alone in the processes of social construction that produce organizations and society has largely gone unnoticed." (p. 248)

His remedy is a "digital turn" and a retheorizing of institutional theory, identity and sensemaking (pp. 249–251), cast as "symbolic machines" (p. 239) rather than persons.

## What the work says

Phillips argues that institutional theory, organizational identity and sensemaking have not absorbed intelligent technologies, traces the neglect to four causes (technology framed as exogenous, anti-materialism, the ubiquity and invisibility of the technologies, and OT scholars' lack of interest), and proposes three steps: recognize "symbolic machines" in social construction, change OT's institutional logic, and retheorize the founding theories. On candidate 7 he presses the structure question — technology as what an organization "is" (p. 250) — while keeping AI as a co-participant in social construction, so he answers neither question and drops neither premise.

## Caution

The earlier abstract-based guess that Phillips "leans to" technology-as-constitutive holds, but he does not trade the agent picture for it; both stand side by side, and he never asks what organization members must be able to do. Candidate 3 is touched only by one example about advice-seeking; the essay has no account of technology between two parties.
