## Candidate 2. Blind following and emergent interdependence

**Take it up:** steiger2026, johnsson2026, hubert2025, choroszewicz2026. None answers or contests it. None poses Hinds and von Krogh's question in their words. Only Steiger et al. and Hubert cite hinds2024, and Johnsson and Nikolausson cite only stark2024.

**Convergence.** The four works agree that following AI blindly is a risk that depends on conditions, not a default. Steiger et al. find student sprint teams calibrating "what GenAI may influence, what it should contribute, and how strongly they intend to rely on its output" (p. 15). In their data, reliance on GenAI grows when the team is exhausted (the oracle role), and GenAI's synthesis can erode "the team's own voice" (p. 12). Hubert locates the switch in evidence artefacts: without them, responses swing "entre contournement et dépendance aveugle" (p. 25). Choroszewicz reports users' worry that fluency "could lead to tacit acceptance or overreliance" (p. 9). Johnsson and Nikolausson come closest to the disruption of interdependence itself. Employees ask AI first, so "the number of occasions where knowledge is articulated and circulated within the team decreases" (p. 45).

**Algorithmacy's answer?** No. The shortlist's answer is that blind following collapses the triad: the member treats the system as the counterpart and the colleague drops from view. Johnsson and Nikolausson document the colleague dropping from view, but as a matter of fewer exchanges, with AI as a resource beside the team rather than an intermediary between members. Steiger et al.'s delegation calibration is the closest rival answer, and it is a team process of setting reliance. It is not an individual's ability to tell whether guidance reflects the system or the colleague. No work names the three parts, and none ties the ability to repeated turns.

## Candidate 4. The co-opted person's side

**Take it up:** choroszewicz2026, rikala2025, aleksic2026. None answers or contests it.

**Convergence.** All three answer in the askers' genus, which is the experience and costs to the person. Choroszewicz shows users positioned as "testers, AI outputs' quality verifiers and interpreters" (p. 9), carrying a burden of vigilance that is cognitive and emotional labour. That is close to Justesen and Plesner's "emotional costs, mental loads and unintended tasks". Rikala et al. measure the load: "Meta-work was found to increase burdens and worsen technostress" (p. 1). Aleksić reports the favourable side, where chatbot use goes with "higher levels of autonomy, competence, and relatedness" (p. 236).

**Algorithmacy's answer?** No. The shortlist's answer concerns what the person must be able to do, which comes before how the person responds. All three works describe responses or burdens, so the Risk bullet's warning holds: the genus of the answer differs. Choroszewicz comes nearest, because she names interpreting AI output as a role imposed on users. But the object of that interpretation is the tool's output, not a colleague reached through the tool.

## Candidate 5. Expertise and the division of labour

**Take it up:** hubert2025, dowsett2026, akhil2026 (abstract). Dowsett also partly contests a finding of Hsu and Bechky (experts gain less from GenAI than novices) but not the question. Choroszewicz mentions deskilling only in passing. Avnon, the triage's possible_answer for this candidate, is in batch P1.

**Convergence.** The three works agree that expertise is not simply lost. It moves, as it does in the brief's account. Hubert argues that value moves from "une expertise substitutive" to "fonctions d'orchestration" (p. 7), while juniors risk losing command of synthesis (p. 25). Dowsett finds GenAI displacing expertise in routine stages of the creative process and letting novices "enter expert domains" (p. 168). In S.G. and Lyytinen's abstract, experts repair the limits of machine reasoning and "absorb asymmetric liability".

**Algorithmacy's answer?** No. The works share the brief's pattern, in which tasks pass to the system and new demands fall on the person. But the new demand they name is orchestrating evidence, adjudicating machine claims, or guarding the scope of one's work. None says the demand is relational, that is, working with a counterpart through the system, and none treats GenAI as evaluating professional work between two parties.

## Candidate 6. Invisible work and opacity

**Answers:** akhil2026 (abstract only). **Take it up:** ivarsson2026 (abstract only), choroszewicz2026, rikala2025. **Passing:** johnsson2026, hubert2025.

**Convergence.** These works extend Justesen and Plesner's invisible digi-work to AI and to data. S.G. and Lyytinen name "argumentation labor", the work of adjudicating machine-generated reasoning, as "a layered, additive form of invisible labor". Ivarsson and Islind give a taxonomy of five forms, one of them "interpretative" work. Choroszewicz cites Justesen and Plesner directly: prolonged engagement "can function as users' invisible work to compensate for the limitations of AI tools" (p. 6). She also adds "interpretative" and "operational" opacity to epistemic opacity (p. 9). Rikala et al. find meta-work hard to recognize: only 14% of responses reported it (p. 17).

**Algorithmacy's answer?** Partly, and this is the candidate where the field comes closest. The shortlist says the paper would argue that interpreting, specifying intent and keeping track are unrecognized work that keeps automated relations running. S.G. and Lyytinen, and Choroszewicz, already make that argument for **interpreting**, framed as interpreting the AI's output or reasoning. Neither covers specifying intent or keeping track, and neither puts a colleague on the far side of the system. The new claim the hand-check flagged therefore has precedent for its first part. The paper should cite S.G. and Lyytinen and Choroszewicz as neighbours and draw the line at the triad. The S.G. and Lyytinen paper needs a full read before that sentence is written, because only its abstract has been seen.

## Across the batch

Interpretation of AI output, recast as invisible labour or as an imposed role, is the one idea several works converge on. It runs through candidates 6, 4 and 2. In every work, what gets interpreted is the machine's claim. No work in this batch treats the system as an intermediary that stands between two people and evaluates both.
