## How the list is ranked

I rank on three things and report them separately: the final fit of the lead question; what the answer costs the brief's definition (nothing, a change of level, a change of scope, a change of genus, or a dispute with the asker's premise); and the support the surrounding conversation gives the entry. No candidate scores 4. Two score 3, and the other eight lead with a question at 2, which the scale defines as adjacent and citable.

Three facts about the whole list bear on every entry.

1. **One volume.** All ten lead questions come from six pieces of volume 5: Stark and Vanden Broeck (three), Hinds and von Krogh (three), Justesen and Plesner, Phillips, Kalvapalle and Kennedy, Hsu and Bechky, and Räcker, Geiger and Seidl, all published in 2024. No question asked in volume 6 or 7 reaches fit 2. Each lead question is between 24 and 32 months old at this run.
2. **Level decides.** The brief defines an individual's capacity exercised in an interaction. The two questions at 3 are the ones the brief can answer at the person's level, for one horn of the question (candidate 2) or for the participant's part of a call (candidate 1). Questions about organizations, professions or fields stop at 2.
3. **What `BASELINES.md` records.** It gives each version's problem, gap, contribution and anchor, and an abstract for ASM only. It records no title. Where I name a part it does not quote (a title, GDOC's or PAPER's abstract), I describe the move and leave the wording of the present text to the author.

## 1. Organization and communication as one field

- **Question.** OQ-stark2024-06 (future_research_call; level: organization; published 2024-06-18): "Also like them, further research should study the corresponding field of communication. But whereas Merton and Lazarsfeld analyzed mass communication as the related, yet separate, field corresponding to bureaucratic organization, in our case, organizational form and communication field are conjoined." (p. 15, "Out of Bounds")
- **Who else asks.** The same article continues the call in OQ-stark2024-09, "our agenda considers genre forms, for example, the genre forms of ratings and rankings" (p. 16, "Out of Bounds"; fit 2). Phillips et al. cite the article and name it among "examples of work beginning to answer these questions" (phillips2024, p. 11; quoted in the stark2024 record, Hand-check), and they ask "how engaging with generative AI affects human interaction" (OQ-phillips2024-08, p. 11; fit 2). The call sits in two conversations. R22, technology between people: theory/review articles 5.5; commentary or exchange pieces 1; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 3 (4, 5, 7); citation edges among askers 1; most recent ask 2026-03-31. R23, openness to other fields: theory/review articles 4; commentary or exchange pieces 2; editorial signal y; distinct volumes 4; citation edges among askers 2; most recent ask 2026-07-26. The editorial signal in R23 is the 2026 editors' welcome for proposals that "bridge disciplinary boundaries, introduce new conceptual vocabularies" (OQ-hollerer2026-01, p. 5; fit 1).
- **How algorithmacy answers.** Stark and Vanden Broeck ask for the communication field that goes with the platform form, and their precedent is Yates, who studied bureaucratic organization through its written genres. The brief makes the same pairing from the person's side: "oracy belongs to direct dyads and chains of them, literacy to dyads moderated by a text, and algorithmacy to triads" (ASM, Abstract), with one criterion, "what stands between coordinating actors" (GDOC, Discussion ¶1; ASM). It supplies the participant's part of the field: what a co-opted person must be able to do with a rating, namely interpret it, specify intent so that it survives the intermediary, and keep track of a rule that changes.
- **What would move.**
  - *ASM.* Least movement. The abstract already carries the sequence, and the gap sentence already names Stark and Vanden Broeck. The introduction would open on their closing call, so that the sequence of oracy, literacy and algorithmacy arrives as the answer to a question an OT article poses. In the contributions, "the co-opted person's side" would move to the front.
  - *GDOC.* The opening problem (two people working together through a system, Introduction ¶3) and the frame of Okhuysen and Bechky's integrating conditions (Introduction ¶2) would leave the introduction and go to the section that grounds the three parts. The gap sentence (§2 ¶3) would be set against this call and no longer against neighbouring fields. The first contribution, linking accounts of platform co-optation to everyday practice, already fits and would lead.
  - *PAPER.* The opening on displacement (Introduction ¶1) would give way to the platform form and its communication field. The gap (§2 ¶1: OT has mapped the macro shift) has this candidate's shape and stays. The image of "giving books to people who lack literacy" (¶3) would serve the parallel with Yates. The unwritten sections could be built to the call.
- **Risk.** A change of level: the authors state "we operate at the organizational level" (p. 2, "Introduction"; stark2024 record, Caution), and their field is made of interfaces and genres, so the brief fills the participant's part and no more. A conflict of premise: they people the field with technologies that are "our communication partners" (p. 16; stark2024 record, Fit), the picture the brief rejects, so the paper answers the call while disputing the authors' first step. It is a call and not a question, from one article, 29 months old. Rival answers in the corpus: the authors' own programme of genre forms; Phillips et al.'s account of AI as participant; Hinds and von Krogh's progressive encapsulation.
- **Final fit.** 3 (A 3, B 3). The hand-check holds the 3 "for a partial answer at the person's level" and says it "would not hold for a claim to have described the field".

## 2. Blind following and emergent interdependence

- **Question.** OQ-hinds2024-04 (explicit_question; level: interaction; published 2024-10-29): "With the introduction of GenAI, might emergent interdependence be disrupted because team members blindly follow guidance from the AI?" (p. 7, "Team coordination"). A follow-on question on p. 8 asks whether it might instead become less necessary (reader's paraphrase).
- **Who else asks.** The same article, in the same section: "We need theorizing that captures what shapes team participants' dynamic relations with AI to coordinate their work." (OQ-hinds2024-25, p. 8; fit 2), and three more questions at 2 on cohesion, on teams' capacity to evolve practices, and on collective mental models (OQ-hinds2024-03, -07, -08). Phillips et al. ask "how an AI system as a teammate affects team dynamics and performance" (OQ-phillips2024-07, p. 11; fit 2). Friedrich (2026) cites the article. The question sits in R49, the coordination cluster (theory/review articles 2; commentary or exchange pieces 1; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 1 (5); citation edges among askers 0; most recent ask 2024-10-29), and in R22 (support line under candidate 1).
- **How algorithmacy answers.** Blind following is the brief's first failure mode: the member treats the system as the counterpart and the colleague drops from view. The three parts describe the member who does not collapse the triad: this member works out whether a prompt reflects the system or the colleague, specifies intent so that it reaches the colleague, and keeps track of guidance that changes as the system learns. Emergent interdependence is then disrupted where members lack the ability and kept where they hold it, and the brief adds that people with more turns at the cycle hold more of it.
- **What would move.**
  - *GDOC.* Closest in vocabulary. The opening problem (Introduction ¶3) stays almost as it is, and its setting moves from platform work to teams inside organizations. The gap sentence (§2 ¶3) would be set against Hinds and von Krogh's open question. The literature positioned against would narrow from coordination theory at large to the relational view of technology and to team coordination. The fourth agenda question, on generative AI in professional work, would move from the agenda into the argument. The phrase "to judge and control" in the opening problem marks the feature that has to widen.
  - *ASM.* Generative AI, now named in passing, would become a main setting. The dyad dilemma serves as the opening, since blind following is the system-as-counterpart picture. The gap sentence would need a second leg beside the platform sources. Bindingness, the second feature of the intermediary, is where the scope change lands. The three courses of action for organizations fit teams that deploy such systems.
  - *PAPER.* The opening is already on generative AI. It would move from the displacement of institutional values to the absorption of coordination among colleagues. The gap (§2 ¶1, the "dyadic ontology" of micro-foundations) fits the reading of blind following as a collapse into a dyad.
- **Risk.** A change of scope in the definition. The brief's intermediary "evaluates and binds" both parties; in this passage the system issues guidance, and "nothing in the passage says it evaluates the members or binds them" (hinds2024 record, Fit). The answer covers the first horn only, because algorithmacy presupposes interdependent work and cannot show that it stays needed. A hostile reader says the answer renames the mechanism the question already proposes. Emergent interdependence is Mayo's construct for a team and its periphery, and the brief speaks of single members. The article is a thought experiment by one pair of authors. Rival answer in the corpus: the authors' own, in which the system absorbs relations and may "minimize the relations between team members" (p. 7; hinds2024 record, Hand-check); they do not theorize it as a party between parties.
- **Final fit.** 3 (A 3, B 3). Change needed: scope, "from an intermediary that evaluates and binds to one that guides coordination" (hinds2024 record, Fit).

## 3. Partner or medium: technology between people (R22)

- **Question, as its askers phrase it.** The party side: "This new sociality—when the partner with which we are communicating is an algorithm— will be a critical component of further research on algorithmic management." (OQ-stark2024-07, p. 16, "Out of Bounds"; fit 2). The medium side: scholars should "examine such forms of online organizing that involve (as Uhlin (2022) also notes) interacting with represented people as well as other co-located people" (OQ-whyte2023-09, p. 14, "Concluding Remarks"; fit 1). No asker poses the choice between the two; the split is my reading of their premises.
- **Who else asks.** R22: Whyte et al. (2023), Stark and Vanden Broeck (2024), Hinds and von Krogh (2024), Justesen and Plesner (2024), Mena et al. (2026), with Phillips et al. (2024) and a boundary condition from Räcker et al. (2024). Support: theory/review articles 5.5; commentary or exchange pieces 1; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 3 (4, 5, 7); citation edges among askers 1; most recent ask 2026-03-31. The party premise appears at full strength in R14, the generative-AI exchange (theory/review articles 5; commentary or exchange pieces 3; distinct volumes 4; citation edges among askers 10; most recent ask 2026-04-01; without Friedrich's practitioner-addressed question, 4 theory articles, 3 volumes and a most recent ask of 2025-11).
- **How algorithmacy answers.** With a third position. The brief holds that the system is neither the person's counterpart ("a direct dyad") nor a channel between two people ("a moderated dyad") (ASM, Introduction ¶1–2), but an intermediary that evaluates and binds both. Its two failure modes are the two pictures taken as complete: collapsing the triad into a person-and-machine dyad, and treating the platform as a neutral channel. Algorithmacy names what a person must be able to do once neither picture holds.
- **What would move.**
  - *ASM.* It carries this frame already, as "the dyad dilemma" (Introduction ¶3–4). One move: source the two pictures in the journal's own pages, the counterpart picture in Stark and Vanden Broeck and in Phillips et al., the channel picture in Whyte et al., so that the dilemma appears as a division among OT authors and not as the paper's construction.
  - *GDOC.* The gap sentence (§2 ¶3, "trapped in a dyadic framework") would point at this division inside OT and no longer at neighbouring fields. The two failure modes (§4.3) would move forward to state the problem. The coordination frame would drop to the grounding of the three parts.
  - *PAPER.* The gap (§2 ¶1, "a persistent, set-theoretic dyadic ontology") would be specified as these two pictures with their OT sources. The displacement opening would need a bridge down to the interaction.
- **Risk.** The paper would answer a question it assembles, and an asker may not recognize it as their own. It disputes a premise: for Stark and Vanden Broeck technologies "themselves become communication partners" (p. 9; stark2024 record, Fit), and the hand-check calls this passage "the paper's best foil in the article", not a question the brief answers. The medium side is weak ground: Whyte et al.'s video conference evaluates no one, so the brief's triad is absent from their case and the paper can say only that their case differs. Rival answers in the corpus: Grodal et al. (2024), for whom the partner is a perception produced when a technology seems to cross the human–machine boundary; Hsu and Bechky (2024), for whom technology is one actor in a relational network; Hinds and von Krogh (2024), for whom the system absorbs relations.
- **Final fit.** The lead question scores 2 (A 3, B 2). The conversation's members score 3 × 2; 2 × 5; 1 × 5; 0 × 10.

## 4. The co-opted person's side (R48)

- **Question, as its askers phrase it.** "what are individuals' psychological responses to the new relational fabric in which they are embedded?" (OQ-hinds2024-21, p. 11, "Discussion"; fit 2). "Finally, we could account more fully for the emotional costs, mental loads and unintended tasks associated with digitalization." (OQ-justesen2024-13, p. 22, "Future research: Some suggestions"; fit 2).
- **Who else asks.** Justesen and Plesner also ask for "the various lived experiences of people engaged with invisible digi-work in practice" (OQ-justesen2024-11, p. 22; fit 1). Stark and Vanden Broeck ask of the category "user": "What does this mean? How could it become so taken-for-granted? And how does that familiarity indicate some fundamental similarities across dissimilar contexts while masking other important differences?" (OQ-stark2024-01, p. 8, "Object of management"; fit 2). Jaser and Tourish (2024) ask why workers consent. R48 support: theory/review articles 3; commentary or exchange pieces 0; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 1 (5); citation edges among askers 0; most recent ask 2024-10-29.
- **How algorithmacy answers.** It says what the person inside the arrangement must be able to do, which is the prior question to how the person responds. The three parts are a specified case of Justesen and Plesner's "unintended tasks" and mental loads, and they form a cycle in which people with more turns hold more of the ability than others in the same formal position. Stark and Vanden Broeck answer the question of the user from the organization's side, by co-optation; algorithmacy gives "the co-opted person's side".
- **What would move.**
  - *ASM.* The gap sentence already states this gap: the triadic view "has not said what a person inside the arrangement must be able to do" (Introduction ¶4). One move: attach the gap to these askers' words, so that it is a gap OT authors state and not only one the paper infers. Nothing else moves.
  - *GDOC.* The gap (§2 ¶3) is the same gap in the vocabulary of sensibility. The askers would be cited at it, and the first contribution would lead. The coordination frame could stay as the grounding of the three parts.
  - *PAPER.* The gap (§2 ¶1) matches. The opening would move from institutions to the person, and the phrase "a situated engagement with the unseen" would be tied to Justesen and Plesner's invisible work.
- **Risk.** The genus of the answer differs from the genus of the question. These askers ask for empirical accounts and psychological responses (the scope of OQ-hinds2024-21 lists stress, sense of control and turnover intentions), and the brief offers a capacity and "measures no one". The hand-check on OQ-stark2024-01 notes that the section answers its first question itself and that the label "user" covers people with no counterpart. One practice is read two ways: Justesen and Plesner file work "aimed through direct and indirect practices at pacifying the algorithm" (p. 15, quoting Bucher et al., 2021; justesen2024 record, Hand-check) as labour the organization depends on, and the brief uses the same phrase for a failure mode. Support is three articles in one volume with no citation among them. Rival answers in the corpus: Jaser and Tourish's consent; Scherer and Neesham's organized immaturity, whose remedy lies outside the systems; Justesen and Plesner's typology of connecting, compensating and cleaning work.
- **Final fit.** 2 for each lead (OQ-hinds2024-21: A 2, B 2; OQ-justesen2024-13: A 2, B 1; OQ-stark2024-01: A 3, B 3, lowered at hand-check against both scorers).

## 5. Expertise and the division of labour (R18, R44)

- **Question, as its askers phrase it.** "finding a way to retain the expertise that will be needed to critically analyse, question and channel this powerful technology is a formidable challenge." (OQ-hsu2024-08, p. 12, "The Deeper Role that Technology Plays in Social Categorization"; fit 2). "as progressive encapsulation evolves across levels and functions in organizations, how does the process shape the division of labor?" (OQ-hinds2024-16, p. 10, "Discussion"; fit 2).
- **Who else asks.** Stark and Vanden Broeck: "Under what conditions can we expect resistance by professionals to incursions by algorithmic practices? When are compromises likely?" (OQ-stark2024-02, p. 9; fit 2). Hinds and von Krogh on "hyper-generalization" (OQ-hinds2024-17, p. 11; fit 2). Corver and Filler on the capacities of a good "tool user" (OQ-corver2026-09, p. 17; fit 1). Guercini and Lechner (2023) on competence attrition. R18, competence: theory/review articles 6; commentary or exchange pieces 1; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 3 (4, 5, 7); citation edges among askers 0; most recent ask 2026-07-28. R44, division of labour: theory/review articles 3; commentary or exchange pieces 1; distinct volumes 1 (5); citation edges 0; most recent ask 2024-10-29.
- **How algorithmacy answers.** The brief gives an account of why task deskilling and rising relational demand go together. As a system takes over tasks, what a person must be able to do shifts toward working with a counterpart through that system. The askers ask whether expertise is lost, kept or rebuilt; the brief adds that the arrangement calls for an ability none of them names, and it extends the setting to generative AI that evaluates professional work.
- **What would move.**
  - *PAPER.* Nearest. "giving books to people who lack literacy" (¶3) is already a claim about competence. The opening would move from displaced institutional values to the competence the new arrangement calls for, and the literature positioned against would become the generative-AI exchange and Hinds and von Krogh.
  - *GDOC.* The section on deskilling and relational demand (§5.2) would move from late in the paper to the opening problem. The coordination frame would recede. The agenda question on professional work would become part of the argument.
  - *ASM.* The sequence in the abstract presents algorithmacy as one competence in a line of competences, which suits this entry, and "ability" is the genus that fits. The gap sentence would need a second leg on expertise under AI. The brief records the genus as unsettled, and this entry would force the choice.
- **Risk.** The askers mean something else by competence. Hsu and Bechky mean domain expertise that apprenticeship builds, and in their cases generative AI "is a production tool, not an evaluator standing between two people" (hsu2024 record, Fit), so the triad is absent. The questions sit at the level of organizations, professions and fields; on OQ-hinds2024-17 the hand-check concludes that the answer would need two changes, of level and of scope, and "still would not say which tasks go where". On OQ-stark2024-02 algorithmacy "predicts neither outcome". Rival answers in the corpus: Guercini and Lechner's attrition by interference between competences; Hinds and von Krogh's hyper-specialization and hyper-generalization; Corver and Filler's tool user; Patriotta et al.'s "competent agency".
- **Final fit.** 2 for each lead (OQ-hsu2024-08: A 2, B 2; OQ-hinds2024-16: A 2, B 2; OQ-hinds2024-17: A 3, B 3, lowered at hand-check; OQ-stark2024-02: A 2, B 2).

## 6. Invisible work and opacity (R21)

- **Question.** OQ-justesen2024-16 (invitation; level: organization; published 2024-03-15): "there is great potential in further developing our understanding of the invisible in the digitalized organization in a particular sense, namely as a type of work that is distributed, practical and embodied." (p. 21, "Invisible Digi-Work: New topographies of digitalized organizations")
- **Who else asks.** The same article: "we still need solid and systematically produced knowledge about the invisible work required to uphold, maintain and repair the connected and automated organization." (OQ-justesen2024-01, p. 9; fit 2). It answers Orlikowski and Scott's call: "there is little attention focused on the undertow, and thus limited knowledge of the corollary effects that are being generated by digitalization or their consequences." (OQ-orlikowski2023-02, p. 5, "The Digital Undertow"; fit 1). R21 support: theory/review articles 5; commentary or exchange pieces 2; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 2 (4, 5); citation edges among askers 6; most recent ask 2024-10-29.
- **How algorithmacy answers.** The brief claims a shift in the opacity debate "from technical transparency to practical, situated competence" (GDOC, Discussion ¶2). On this entry the paper would argue that interpreting, specifying intent and keeping track are themselves unrecognized work that keeps automated, connected relations running, nearest to what Justesen and Plesner call compensating work. The hand-check marks this as "a new claim for the paper to argue, not an answer the brief already holds".
- **What would move.**
  - *GDOC.* The second contribution (the opacity debate, Discussion ¶2) would become the first and would be addressed to the invisible-work and undertow conversation. The gap would be set against Justesen and Plesner and against Orlikowski and Scott.
  - *PAPER.* Its opening on quiet displacement already sits in this conversation. It would move from displaced institutional values, a field-level matter, down to the unseen work a person does, and "the unseen" would take the defined sense these authors give "invisible".
  - *ASM.* Least prepared. Opacity is one of three features of the intermediary there and carries no frame, so this entry would need a new positioning paragraph.
- **Risk.** A change of genus, from ability to work, which the brief does not make. Justesen and Plesner separate their invisible from opacity: they grant that "visibility is connected to opacity" (p. 21) and then turn to the invisible "in a particular sense", and their note 1 places "the hidden operations of big tech companies" (p. 22) outside the concept. Orlikowski and Scott work at the level of institutions and industries. The conversation has had no ask since October 2024 and none in volumes 6 or 7. Rival answer in the corpus: Justesen and Plesner's own typology already names this work, "pacifying the algorithm" included.
- **Final fit.** 2 (OQ-justesen2024-16: A 3, B 2, settled at the lower score; OQ-justesen2024-01: A 2, B 2).

## 7. Artificial persons, technology as structure (R14, R09)

"What if, instead of thinking about technology as organizational infrastructure, we considered technology as synonymous with organizational structure?" (OQ-stark2024-08, p. 16, "Out of Bounds"; fit 2).
- **Who else asks.** Grodal et al. (2024) and Hsu and Bechky (2024) reply to Phillips et al. inside the exchange; Hinds and von Krogh ask "what it means to be a team in the world of GenAI" (OQ-hinds2024-09, p. 9; fit 1); Patriotta et al. R14 has more citation edges than any other conversation in the map, and R09 was last asked two months before this run.
- **How algorithmacy answers.** By contesting the first question and adopting the second. The "artificial persons" of the first question, like the AI teammate, are the system taken as a counterpart, which is the brief's direct dyad. The brief holds that the system which matters for organizing is the one that stands between two people and evaluates both, which is technology as a structure of the relation. What changes in organizations, on this reading, is what their members must be able to do.
- **What would move.**
  - *PAPER.* Nearest, through its generative-AI opening and its organizationality anchor. The opening problem would be stated as a disagreement with the exchange's premise, and the literature positioned against would become the 5(3) exchange. The paper would need a claim about organizations that the brief does not make.
  - *ASM.* The dyad dilemma would take "artificial persons" as its example of the counterpart picture. The gap and the contributions would stay.
  - *GDOC.* Little in it carries this entry; the failure modes (§4.3) are the one foothold.
- **Risk.** The paper would enter by telling the askers their question rests on a mistaken picture. The level is the organization; on OQ-phillips2024-09 the hand-check finds "a premise and a level the brief does not share", and on OQ-stark2024-08 that algorithmacy "adopts the premise and adds nothing about structure at the organizational level". The exchange is about category construction, which the brief does not reach. Rival answers in the corpus: Phillips et al. (AI as participant in category work), Grodal et al. (a perceived crossing of the human–machine boundary), Hsu and Bechky (technology as an actor), and, for what organizations are, Schoeneborn et al.'s (2026) collective actorhood held in degrees.
- **Final fit.** 2 (OQ-phillips2024-09: A 1, B 2; OQ-stark2024-08: A 2, B 2).

## 8. Accountability when an algorithm decides (R17)

- **Question.** OQ-stark2024-04 (explicit_question; level: multi-level; published 2024-06-18): "But who or what is accountable when an algorithm exercises decisions that occur faster and with a vastly larger number of inputs than a human can physiologically comprehend and react to (Vesa & Tienari, 2022, p. 1137)?" (p. 14, "Accountability")
- **Who else asks.** Schoeneborn et al.: "What does the recurrent charging or discharging of a collective social address with actorhood imply for individual as opposed to collective responsibility?" (OQ-schoeneborn2026-06, p. 14; fit 1). Mena et al. (2026), Pavlisa et al. (2026), Jaser and Tourish (2024), Scherer and Neesham (2023) and Friedrich (2026) each ask a version. R17 support: theory/review articles 7; commentary or exchange pieces 0; editorial signal n; distinct volumes 3 (4, 5, 7); citation edges among askers 0; most recent ask 2026-09-19.
- **How algorithmacy answers.** With its first part. Interpreting an outcome to find its cause, in the system, the counterpart or both, is what opacity calls for, and GDOC presents it as restoring accountability in Okhuysen and Bechky's sense. The answer concerns how a participant assigns an outcome to a source so that two people can go on working together. It does not say who is liable.
- **What would move.**

## Not included: the anti-dyadic thread

The lead asked for this thread as a candidate if, and only if, the text supports it. The text does not support it as an entry question, for three reasons.

1. **Neither passage is an open question.** Patriotta, Augustine and Voronov fault crisis-communication research for "focusing primarily on cognitive and dyadic relational processes" (p. 16, "Legitimacy tests as the mobilization of higher-order principles"; patriotta2025 record, Topic flags) and say such explanations "do not bring broader society and shared cultural norms, values and expectations into the core of theoretical explanations" (p. 16). That sentence positions their own model, and the model answers it. None of their eleven open OQs concerns dyads, and all score 0 or 1. Dion, Cooren and Dupuis's Thirdness is their proposal; the one open question that turns on it invites scholars to ask "when and how they participate or are affected by an emergent Thirdness" (OQ-dion2025-11, p. 15; fit 1).
2. **The third is not a party.** What Patriotta et al. add to the dyad is a cultural grammar, the orders of worth. For Dion et al. "binary relations are meaningless without a third term that provides interpretive grounding for the situation" (p. 15), and the third is a rule, a habit or a meaning; the hand-check read the whole text for a social third party and found none. The same third of meaning appears in two more records: Winkler and Schoeneborn's "abstract authoritative “third” in the form of a shared goal or value" (winkler2025, p. 14) and Fotaki's "thirdness", "in which subject and other are intertwined" (fotaki2023, p. 7). The brief's third is a party that evaluates and binds two people. On OQ-dion2025-11 the hand-check concludes that "the contact with the brief's triad stops at the word and at the claim that a relation needs three".
3. **It is not a conversation under the rule.** One asker holds an open question (Dion et al.); the other holds a positioning sentence.

What the text does support is narrower. A paper may cite both passages as evidence that OT authors find dyadic accounts insufficient, in a sentence that says what each puts in the third place. Presenting either as an ally for a triad of parties would misread it. The corpus's three-party passages with a social third are elsewhere: Stark and Vanden Broeck's "triangular structure (Vallas & Schor, 2020) with the platform operator at the apex and producers and users at the other corners" (p. 14, "Accountability"), which their record notes is not modelled further (reader's inference); Justesen and Plesner's hidden worker on whom a rider's and a driver's meeting "hinges" (p. 10, quoting Gray & Suri, 2019); and Hinds and von Krogh's broker, which is candidate 9 and the only one of the three posed as an open question.

Two further items at fit 2 are also left off the list. OQ-scherer2023-13 asks how action can be organized for mature citizenship, and its authors place the needed skills outside the systems. OQ-suddaby2024-06 is a closing line about resisting data-driven science, with no counterpart and no question.

**The superficial case a careless scorer would make.** A platform stands between an organization and its audience, it is opaque and adaptive, the organization must interpret outcomes it cannot see into, and "competent agency" is the ability to do so; algorithmacy therefore answers how humans and algorithms co-produce the test.

**Why it is a 1.** The platforms in the call curate what audiences see; "they bind no one, and the asker's level is the organization and its field" (patriotta2025 record, Hand-check). The evaluation is an audience's judgment of an organization. The critique of dyads is answered with orders of worth, as the section above shows. The contact is shared vocabulary, which is what fit 1 means.
