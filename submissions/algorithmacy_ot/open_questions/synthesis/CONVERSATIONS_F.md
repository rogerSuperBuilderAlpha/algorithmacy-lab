## Counting rules

- **Recurring.** A conversation has askers with at least two different first authors, or one article plus an editorial. Every entry below meets the first test.
- **Families.** A conversation is a family of questions. An article's narrower questions belong to the family when they put the family's question to that article's case. Questions about an article's own construct that no other author asks are singletons.
- **Two memberships at most.** No OQ sits in more than two entries, cross-cutting entries included. Where an OQ fitted three, I kept the two closest, so the cross-cutting lists are lower bounds.
- **Boundary conditions.** They are marked (bc). A piece that enters a conversation through boundary conditions alone counts ½.
- **Types.** "Theory/review articles" includes the two pieces printed as Theory Articles and typed `essay` (`powell2023`, `petriglieri2023`). "Commentary or exchange pieces" are the nine typed `controversies_conversations`. Two theory articles carry an exchange id (`orlikowski2023`, `dion2025`); I count them as theory articles.
- **Editorial signal.** It is "y" only where an OQ from one of the two editorials (`cornelissen2024`, `hollerer2026`) is a member. The scope note of OQ-hollerer2026-04 names new technologies, artificial intelligence, sustainability, ecological crises, geopolitics and changing forms of work as emerging developments. I report that as a domain mention in the paragraphs concerned and do not count it as a signal.
- **Citation edges.** They come from `EDGES.tsv`, which appeared during the run and holds 55 links between corpus pieces. 3 of them are text-body citations inside the generative-AI exchange (method `text_body`). I count every row.
- **Coding taken as given.** Some rows are coded `explicit_question` although the paraphrase says the article goes on to answer them (for example OQ-georgiou2023-01). I treat them as open because the table does. The hand-check reclassifies such rows record by record, as it did in `giovannoni2025`, `gumusay2024`, `werner2026`, `winkler2025` and `wright2023`. It had reached 34 of the 66 records when I built this file, and records it has not reached may lose further rows.
- **Order.** Entries are ordered by the weighted number of askers (theory/review plus commentary, plus one for an editorial), then by distinct volumes, then by the most recent ask. This measures breadth and not cohesion: the first entry has fifteen askers and no citation between any two of them.

Fifteen pieces ask this, more than ask any other question in the corpus, and no citation links any two of them. Arnold and Costas (2023) ask which forms of organizing make a shift in who counts as Other last, and how people denied standing as speakers come to hold a speaking position at all. Thirteen pieces ask what place normative claims should have in theory. Kalkman (2026) invites rival normative purposes for crisis management, or an ethical defence of the functionalist one. Seidemann (2024) asks by what criteria a response to paradox counts as good. Mena, Mair and Bruder (2026) ask deliberation research to move from ideals back to description. Langenmayr et al. (2024) leave open whether a societal orientation can be reconciled with the business case. The test remains unanswered: who decides that a normative claim is sound, and by what procedure. Kalkman concedes that causal assessment of crisis interventions is hard and offers moral scrutiny of the process as a partial substitute.

### C03. How can scholars theorize what does not yet exist, or cannot be known, and still be rigorous?

- **Members** (19 OQs from 10 pieces): Laine 2023: OQ-laine2023-07, -09; Jaser 2024: OQ-jaser2024-08; Cornelissen 2024: OQ-cornelissen2024-05 (bc); Gümüsay 2024: OQ-gumusay2024-01, -06, -09, -11, -12; Lantela 2024: OQ-lantela2024-08; Phillips 2024: OQ-phillips2024-14; Hsu 2024: OQ-hsu2024-02; Hinds 2024: OQ-hinds2024-24; Giovannoni 2025: OQ-giovannoni2025-03, -06, -07, -09, -10; Friedrich 2026: OQ-friedrich2026-02.
- **Representative quotation:** OQ-hinds2024-24: “we need 'prospective theorizing' with speculative vigor (e.g., Gümüsay & Reinecke, 2024)” (p. 12, "Discussion")
- **Support:** theory/review articles 7; commentary or exchange pieces 2; editorial signal y (through a boundary condition only); distinct volumes 4 (4, 5, 6, 7); citation edges among askers 9; most recent ask 2026-04-01 (friedrich2026).
- **Level:** Field: the discipline's standards for theory about futures, moving targets and unknowables.

Gümüsay and Reinecke (2024) pose the question of speculative rigour and answer it in part, with four criteria that, by their own account, no single contribution need meet in full. They leave open how the framework should be refined, what methods and infrastructure such theorizing needs, and how the academic system must change to admit imagination and values. Others take up the call without settling the standard. Hinds and von Krogh (2024) invite speculative theorizing on generative AI, against scholars who would wait for the technology to stabilize. Lantela (2024) suggests microhistory as a way to theorize what does not exist. Giovannoni and Quattrone (2025) call for theory that takes the unknowable as its foundation, and they ask what methods can study an absence without filling it. The commentaries on generative AI mark their own claims as speculative and their target as moving (Phillips et al., 2024; Hsu and Bechky, 2024), and the 2024 editorial bounds its conclusions to current models (bc). Two questions remain. What separates disciplined speculation from assertion, once validation against data is unavailable? And will the field's institutions accept it? Giovannoni and Quattrone say that editors and reviewers would have to accept open-ended work. The askers also differ on the aim: desirable futures (Gümüsay and Reinecke), readiness for a technology (Hinds and von Krogh), or inquiry with no knowable end (Giovannoni and Quattrone).

Powell and DiMaggio (2023) call the entry, persistence and resistance of new practices and institutions perhaps the most vexing questions in organization studies, and they say their 1983 paper evaded what diffuses, through which channels and with what effect. The other askers each hold a piece. Ocasio (2023) asks for the functional origins of institutions and their imprint. Orlikowski and Scott (2023) ask which further industry-structuring mechanisms digitalization displaces.
