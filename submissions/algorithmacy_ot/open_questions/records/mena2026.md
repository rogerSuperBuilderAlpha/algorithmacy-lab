# Mena, Mair & Bruder (2026). What Do We Mean by Democracy in Management and Organization Studies?

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) The authors say management and organization studies (MOS) uses "democracy" widely but seldom theorizes it. They sort MOS work on democracy into three foci: democracy inside the organization, democracy in the environment that shapes the organization (outside-in), and organizations' influence on democracy (inside-out). They cross these foci with four political-theory perspectives (liberal, republican, deliberative, agonistic) to build a twelve-cell framework (Table 2). The grid places existing literatures (democratic organizing, corporate political activity, international business, CSR), marks thinly studied cells, and attaches future research questions to each of the twelve cells (hand-check: the reader had "most cells"; every cell on pp. 10–17 closes with them). The discussion adds three implications: keep one perspective consistent across foci, study spillovers between foci, and treat organizations as sites of democratic experimentation that can speak back to political theory.

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "democracy remains often undertheorized in MOS, as researchers use the term in different ways, thereby limiting the accumulation of knowledge" (p. 2, opening section). Also: "research tends to treat liberal democracy as a taken-for-granted environment in which organizations are supposed to thrive" (p. 2, opening section).
- Named interlocutors: Battilana, Yen, Ferreras & Ramarajan (2022) and Battilana, Beckman & Yen (2025) on the organization-democracy nexus; Held (2006) and Cunningham (2002) on models of democracy; Mair & Seelos (2021), Stern & Barley (1996) and Parsons (1956) for the three mandates; Scherer & Palazzo (2007, 2011) on political CSR; Mouffe (2013) and Habermas (1996) for the agonistic and deliberative perspectives; Scudder & White (2023) on compatibility of the two.
- OT articles it cites: Battilana, Yen, Ferreras & Ramarajan (2022), "Democratizing work" (OT 3(1)); Mair & Seelos (2021), "Organizations, social problems, and system change" (OT 2(4)); Nyberg (2021), "Corporations, politics, and democracy" (OT 2(1)); Suddaby (2024), "What theory is, What theory was" (OT 5(4)).
