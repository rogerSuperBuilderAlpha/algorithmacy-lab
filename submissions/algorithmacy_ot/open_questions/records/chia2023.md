# Chia, R., & Holt, R. (2023). Strategy, Intentionality and Success: Four Logics for Explaining Strategic Action.

## What it argues
Strategy theory explains success by prior, conscious intention and a means-ends logic. The authors say this ignores where the "urge to act" is located. They set out four logics, each with its own account of intentionality: means-ends rationality (cognitivism), a logic of practices (Bourdieu's habitus, Dreyfus's absorbed coping), a logic of situation (Jullien's propensity of things, Gibson's affordances, Bergson's elan vital), and a logic of potential (Nietzsche's will to power). In the fourth, intention is an experimental, self-overcoming force that keeps the organization "open". Each logic has a place in practice, but strategy theorists tied to the first should admit the other three. The paper is conceptual and carries no empirical case. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "the field is becoming split between deliberate, individual decision making and non-deliberate, situational views of strategy, with intention being aligned to the former" (p. 2, "Introduction"); it argues "the split in the field is not configured by the presence or absence of intention, but by the location of intentional force" (p. 2, "Introduction"). It also treats emergent-intentionality accounts (Dittrich & Seidl, 2018; MacLean et al., 2015) as still cognitivist (p. 5, "Cognitivism and the Means-Ends Logic of Rationality") (reader's paraphrase).

## Implicit openings
- The authors call the proposed logics "plausible" explanatory bases but present no empirical test; how the four logics would be told apart in observed strategy work is left open (reader's inference; p. 1, Abstract: "demonstrate the plausibility").
- They note that "strategic automation de- and then re-materializes human ‘will’ in architectures of code" and that intentionality is "suffused by technologies of calculation" (p. 19, "Discussion"); what this does to the four logics is not worked out (reader's inference).
- They write that the "resonance" of situation as constitutive of action "remains underexplored, not least because it imperils the centrality of human agency" (p. 11, "A Logic of Situation") (reader's inference: invitation to study situation as an actor).
