# Langenmayr, T., Splitter, V., Tienari, J., & Vesa, M. (2024). Moving from the Business Case Towards an Equity-Based Approach: Theorizing Diversity and Inclusion in Open Strategy.

## What it argues
Open strategy research opens strategy making to a broader set of actors, yet it uses "diversity" and "inclusion" without drawing on the diversity and inclusion literature. The authors show that open strategy mostly follows a business case approach, in which inclusion and diversity matter for their effect on strategy outcomes. They offer an equity-based alternative from critical diversity scholarship, in which inclusion is all strategy actors' contribution and diversity is their self-identified heterogeneity. For each concept they propose research themes: traditional strategy actors' control, new actors' compliance with norms and imposed categories, and contextual understandings, with suggested lenses (critical discourse analysis, Butler on recognition, Foucault, identity work, post-colonial, decolonial and intersectional theory). They argue that diversity and inclusion should be separated conceptually and that the equity-based approach would widen open strategy's societal relevance. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "open strategy scholars have not engaged with the literature on diversity and inclusion" (p. 2, "Introduction"); the paper adds that the open strategy literature "has thus far not engaged with the concept of diversity as such" (p. 3, "Introduction") and answers a call "to advance open strategy theorizing (Vaara et al., 2019)" (p. 3).
- Named interlocutors: Whittington et al. (2011) and Seidl et al. (2019) on open strategy; Hautz et al. (2017, 2019); Vaara et al. (2019); Splitter et al. (2021, 2024); Dobusch et al. (2019); Zanoni et al. (2010) and Adamson et al. (2021) on equity-based diversity and inclusion; Ahonen & Tienari; Butler; Foucault.
- OT articles it cites: Whittington & Yakis-Douglas (2020), "The grand challenge of corporate control: Opening strategy to the normative pressures of networked professionals"; Benschop (2021), "Grand challenges, feminist answers"; Friesl, Brielmaier & Dobusch (2023), "Taking individual choices seriously: A process perspective of self-selection in strategy work"; Gümüsay & Reinecke (2024), "Imagining desirable futures: A call for prospective theorizing with speculative rigour".
- Move: problematization of a literature; new conceptual contrast (business case versus equity-based approach); agenda-setting.

## Implicit openings
- The authors note that open strategy studies largely examine inclusion "from a Western point of view" (p. 4, "The Dominant Business Case Approach") and draw on studies "conducted in Europe and North America" (p. 3; see also p. 7, "Contextual understandings of inclusion"); empirical work on non-Western open strategy is not done by the authors here (reader's inference).
- The paper states that open strategy can be "opened to an unrestricted crowd" yet traditional and new actors stay distinct (p. 10, "Reproducing power relations through managing diversity"); how digital intake channels shape that distinction is not examined (reader's inference).
