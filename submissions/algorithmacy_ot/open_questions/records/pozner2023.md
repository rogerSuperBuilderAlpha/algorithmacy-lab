# Pozner, J.-E., & Hannigan, T. R. (2023). How Relational Publics Become Scandal Audiences: Values and the construction of scandal.

## What it argues
Organizational scandal research, the authors argue, studies scandal's consequences or its mediated construction and treats audiences as passive. Borrowing the sociological concept of relational publics (open-ended networks of strangers attending to the same media), they propose an audience-centered, three-stage model. In stage 1, scandal entrepreneurs alert the media, which frame an organizational act as transgressive. In stage 2 (proto-scandal), relational publics evaluate the act against their values; through recursive feedback with the media they may adjudicate it "deplorable," a new construct. In stage 3 (activated scandal), activated publics spread their judgment to adjacent groups and assemble into a scandal audience, which realizes the scandal. Social control agents are not necessary to scandal construction; the audience is its sine qua non. Theranos and R. Kelly illustrate the model, and the authors offer it as a way to open "the black box of audiences" in organization theory. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- The model says that "If a threshold of targeted engagement is surpassed, the audience deems the transgressor deplorable" (p. 13, "Discussion"); what sets that threshold, and how it would be measured, is not specified. (reader's inference)
- Social control agents and scandal audiences "are only loosely coupled" (p. 12, "The role of social control agents"); when an audience forces regulators to act, as in Theranos, and when it does not, is left open. (reader's inference)
- "Organizations interested in avoiding scandal might find inspiration for impression management directed at activating certain values—and diminishing others" (p. 15, "Future directions"); the transgressing organization's own role in shaping audience formation is not modeled. (reader's inference)
