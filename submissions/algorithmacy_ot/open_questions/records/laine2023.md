# Laine, L., & Kibler, E. (2023). Towards an Object-Oriented Organization Theory: The role of entrepreneurial objects in organizational emergence.

## What it argues
The authors argue that processual entrepreneurship studies, built on a strong process ontology, treat organizations as epiphenomena of process and so cannot explain how genuinely new organizations emerge. Drawing on Graham Harman's object-oriented ontology (OOO), they name two reductive techniques, undermining (reducing organizations to components) and overmining (reducing them to relations or effects), and find both in process ontology, CCO and ANT. They propose a tentative object-oriented organization theory (OOOT): organizations are emergent, autonomy-seeking objects that withhold something from their relations. "The entrepreneurial" is cast as a real (not sensual) quality that any object, human or not, may carry, and that is "loaned out" to other objects through commitment, producing symbiotic combinations from which organizations emerge. Sun Ra and the Arkestra serve as a proto-theoretical illustration of an organization that persists through drastic change in personnel, name and activity. (reader's paraphrase)
