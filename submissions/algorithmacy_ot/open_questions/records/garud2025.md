# Garud, Turunen & Karunakaran (2025). Revisiting Exploration and Exploitation: Temporal structuring for innovation at work.

## What it argues
The authors hold that exploration and exploitation can be intertwined inside everyday work, so that innovation is ongoing and distributed rather than balanced through separation. They draw on design theory (Simon, Schön) and narrative theory (Ricœur) to add two notions of time to the chronological one (Chronos) around which organizations are usually structured. Kairos is the opportune moment in which an employee reflects in action. Aion is a circular time of attention, memory and anticipation in which employees reflect on action and keep ideas alive. Using 3M's Post-it Notes and Google's Gmail as illustrations, they say actors can explore while exploiting when all three times are structured. They then read March's classical themes against four counter-themes and close with implications and a section on digital technologies and generative AI. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "Classical views on organizing for innovation suggest that exploration and exploitation can be balanced in ambidextrous organizations by first separating and then integrating the two. In this paper, we argue that exploration and exploitation can be intertwined" (p. 1, "Abstract"). Also: "scholars have conceptualized exploration and exploitation as opposites because they adopted a "representational" perspective" (p. 4, "Classical view on organizing for innovation").
- Named interlocutors: March (1991, 1999, 2010); Gibson & Birkinshaw (2004, 2006) on contextual ambidexterity; Simon (1996); Schön (1983); Ricœur (1984); Pickering (1993); Boland & Collopy (2004); Otto et al. (2024); Blagoev & Schreyögg (2025); Pentland et al. (2025).
