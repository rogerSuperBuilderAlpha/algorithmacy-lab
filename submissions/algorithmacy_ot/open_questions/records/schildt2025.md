# Schildt & Kodeih (2025). The Reproduction and Evolution of Institutional Logics: A practice-centric perspective.

## What it argues
The authors argue that an institutional logic can be understood as a bundle of interconnected practices, not only as a shared belief system. Three kinds of connection hold practices together: shared constitutive parts (meanings, materials, competencies), shared practitioners, and referential relationships between practices (discursive, sequential, evaluative). From these they derive processes that reproduce a logic and a model of how new practices lead to maintenance or expansion, blurring, renewal through retheorization, contraction, or cascading change. They use academia as the running example and treat artificial intelligence in universities as a hypothetical case. They suggest that this view suits late modernity, where meta-narratives are contested, and that practices are an arena of political contestation over logics. (reader's paraphrase)
