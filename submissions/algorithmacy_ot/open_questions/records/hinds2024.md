# Hinds, P., & von Krogh, G. (2024). Generative AI, Emerging Technology, and Organizing: Towards a theory of progressive encapsulation.

## What it argues
The authors extend Bailey et al.'s (2022) relational view of technology, which studies the evolving relations and functions around a technology rather than the technology as an entity. GenAI, they argue, learns and absorbs functions and relations in ways users cannot see. They name this "progressive encapsulation": as the system learns, it widens the black box around the relations it relies on and the functions it performs, so researchers can observe inputs and outputs but not the process between them. Using team coordination as a thought experiment, they speculate that GenAI may absorb informal coordination, rearrange team boundaries and broker resources, with effects on cohesion, psychological safety, identity and power. The Discussion lists five domains of research questions (power, division of labor, creativity, multi-team knowledge sharing, individual responses). It calls for process theories to complement variance theories, and for "prospective theorizing". The authors declare their stance toward GenAI neutral. (reader's paraphrase)
