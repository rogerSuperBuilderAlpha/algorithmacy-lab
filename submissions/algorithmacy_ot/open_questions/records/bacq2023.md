# Bacq, Toubiana, Ruebottom, Ormiston & Ajunwa (2023). Entrepreneurship Out of Shame: Entrepreneurial Pathways at the Intersection of Necessity, Emancipation, and Social Change.

## What it argues
The article asks how stigmatized people start ventures despite the paralysing effect that the literature attributes to shame. It builds a process model. Direct shaming produces felt shame (a "first-person experience"). Hearing the stories of similarly shamed others produces a "third-person experience" and identification with a collective. The collective's narrative then decides the path. A victimization narrative lowers entrepreneurial self-efficacy and, with labor-market exclusion, leads to necessity entrepreneurship. A resilience narrative raises self-efficacy and leads to emancipatory entrepreneurship, meaning ventures that act as shame-free spaces and push for wider change. Three vignettes illustrate the model; they are not data. The authors also list breakdowns along the pathways. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "existing theorizing tends to see these entrepreneurs as exceptions—as individual heroes who defy expectations to do the impossible, or as necessity entrepreneurs who provide for their basic needs but are not able to create any broader social change." (p. 3, "Introduction"). Also challenges the self-/other-oriented split: "We challenge this dichotomy by theorizing an entrepreneurial pathway that is driven by and connected to both self and others." (p. 18, "Transcending the self/other dichotomy in entrepreneurial motives")
