# Petriglieri & Ashford (2023). Theorizing Gets Personal: Management Academia in the Mirror of Independent Work.

## What it argues
The essay reads management academia through a study of independent workers (freelancers, writers, artists, consultants). Both groups live with freedom, loneliness, precariousness and work that is personal, and both answer with an obsession with productivity that covers existential worries. The authors offer an interpretive "third way" between instrumental advice and critical, political resistance: cultivate a "holding environment" through four connections (place, routine, people, purpose). They tie this to calls for a more pluralist, personal and potent organization theory, and argue that sustaining personal investment in work is needed for better theory. The essay mixes the authors' own experience with informant quotations from an earlier study. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- The advice rests on interviews with independent workers and on the authors' own experience; whether the four connections work for academics is asserted, not tested. (reader's inference; p. 7, "Why We Write" and p. 14, "Holding Our (Working) Selves Together")
- The essay asks whether personalized theorizing yields "more plural and potent" theories, but the link between holding and theory quality is argued, not traced. (reader's inference; p. 20, "Making Organization Theory Personal, Plural, and Potent")
