# Wright, A. L., Kent, D., Hällgren, M., & Rouleau, L. (2023). Theorizing as Mode of Engagement in and through Extreme Contexts Research.

## What it argues
The authors ask how management and organization scholars theorize when they study extreme contexts, meaning settings of risk, emergency or disruption. Existing accounts of theorizing treat it as a craft and ignore the researcher's personhood. The authors propose that theorizing runs through "modes of engagement", in which a researcher connects a personal, professional, moral or vulnerable self to the context. Four ideal types follow: adventuresome inquiry (phenomenon-driven problematization), instrumental scholarship (theory elaboration), ideological improvement (change-driven abstraction) and reflexive labor (dialectical interrogation). A two-by-two (complementing or differentiating existing theory; specialized or generalized practice contribution) places the modes. Researchers can shift modes within a project, combine them in teams, and sequence them across a career. The paper is conceptual and draws on published studies and the authors' own experience. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: strategies for theorizing "remain centred on the process of writing papers and theorizing according to generic categories of social science (Cunliffe, 2022) and promote a view of theorizing as craft (Rivard, 2021)" (p. 3, "Introduction"); the authors say that the extreme-context literature, since the REDS review of Hällgren et al. (2018), still leaves "how theorizing happens in these contexts" "largely opaque" (p. 4, "What are Extreme Contexts?").
- Named interlocutors: Hällgren, Rouleau & de Rond (2018) and the REDS framework; Hannah et al. (2009); Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl (2021); Sandberg & Alvesson (2020); Cutcher et al. (2020); Cunliffe (2022); Weick (1989, 1993); Hibbert (2021); Whiteman & Cooper (2011); Boxenbaum & Rouleau (2011).
- OT articles it cites: Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl (2021), "What theory is and can be: Forms of theorizing in organizational scholarship"; Cunliffe (2022), "Must I grow a pair of balls to theorize about theory in organization and management studies?"; Cutcher, Hardy, Riach & Thomas (2020), "Reflections on reflexive theorizing: The need for a little more conversation".
- Move: new framework (typology of ideal-typical modes of engagement), reorientation of theorizing research toward the researcher's selves; synthesis.

## Implicit openings
- The framework is built from published studies and the authors' experience; whether researchers' own accounts of their mode match the typology is not tested (reader's inference; p. 3, "Based on the literature and our own experiences researching extreme contexts, we propose").
- The authors expect reflexive labor to be hidden in write-ups given "the difficulty of convincing audiences that reflexivity requires organizational researchers to authentically report their own painful experiences" (p. 16, "Shifting modes of engagement within a research project"); how journals' norms shape which modes become visible is not studied (reader's inference).
