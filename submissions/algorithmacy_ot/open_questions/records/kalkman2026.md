# Kalkman (2026). The Purpose of Crisis Management: Normative Directions for a Care-Based Approach.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) Crisis management research in organization and management studies has tacitly adopted a functionalist purpose: contain the disruption, restore order and the status quo. The author calls this purpose unexamined and ethically underdeveloped. Drawing on the feminist ethics of care, he proposes a different purpose built on three normative directions: compassionate caring, responsible intervening, and meaningful restructuring. For each direction a table (Table 1) sets out a mission, vision, value, aspirational outcome and actionable pathways, borrowing building blocks from compassion organizing, responsible management and grand-challenges research. The article claims three contributions: space for ethical theorizing on crisis management, a relational ontology for crisis organizing, and a process view that gives crisis processes ethical meaning. It frames itself as a theoretical provocation meant to open a debate, not to close it.

## The conversation it joins

(p. 2, "Introduction", quoting Alpaslan & Mitroff, 2021, p. 1)

(reader's inference) "Resistance to reorganizing crisis management around a care-based purpose is to be anticipated, though, because conservative actors will try to preserve the status quo" (p. 13, "Toward a New Purpose for Crisis Management"). 2. (reader's inference) "In practice, various stakeholders will have to engage in ongoing, open deliberation on how to pursue and implement a care-based approach." (p. 13). How such deliberation is organized and who takes part is left open.

(reader's inference) Coordination studies "offer valuable insights into crisis management processes, but there is no ethical deliberation on the ultimate purpose of these crisis management attempts."
