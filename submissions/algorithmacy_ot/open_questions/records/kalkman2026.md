# Kalkman (2026). The Purpose of Crisis Management: Normative Directions for a Care-Based Approach.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) Crisis management research in organization and management studies has tacitly adopted a functionalist purpose: contain the disruption, restore order and the status quo. The author calls this purpose unexamined and ethically underdeveloped. Drawing on the feminist ethics of care, he proposes a different purpose built on three normative directions: compassionate caring, responsible intervening, and meaningful restructuring. For each direction a table (Table 1) sets out a mission, vision, value, aspirational outcome and actionable pathways, borrowing building blocks from compassion organizing, responsible management and grand-challenges research. The article claims three contributions: space for ethical theorizing on crisis management, a relational ontology for crisis organizing, and a process view that gives crisis processes ethical meaning. It frames itself as a theoretical provocation meant to open a debate, not to close it.

## Open questions
### OQ-kalkman2026-01
- Quote: "Scholars can propose other normative approaches to continue the debate or provide an ethical defense of current approaches." (p. 14, "Theoretical Implications")
- Form: invitation
- Scope: Normative purposes for organizational crisis management. The author says "My aim with this paper is not to close the conversation with proposing an alternative purpose" (p. 14) and hopes the ideas "will inspire crisis scholars and practitioners to think anew about the purpose of crisis management" (p. 13, "Discussion").
- Addressee: crisis management and organization theory scholars
- Level of analysis: field
- Paraphrase: Other scholars should put forward rival normative purposes or defend the functionalist one. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. (reader's inference) "Resistance to reorganizing crisis management around a care-based purpose is to be anticipated, though, because conservative actors will try to preserve the status quo" (p. 13, "Toward a New Purpose for Crisis Management"). How that resistance plays out against care-oriented change agents is left open.
2. (reader's inference) "In practice, various stakeholders will have to engage in ongoing, open deliberation on how to pursue and implement a care-based approach." (p. 13). How such deliberation is organized and who takes part is left open.
3. (reader's inference) Coordination studies "offer valuable insights into crisis management processes, but there is no ethical deliberation on the ultimate purpose of these crisis management attempts." (p. 4, "Purpose in the Crisis Management Literature"). What research on coordination in crisis would look like with an explicit purpose is left open.
