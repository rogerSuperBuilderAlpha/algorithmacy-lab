# Brennecke, J., Carnabuci, G., & Ertug, G. (2025). Relational History: Correcting temporal myopia in social network research.

## What it argues
The authors argue that organizational network research, in both its networks-as-pipes and networks-as-prisms views, commits Granovetter's "temporal reductionism" by treating a tie's past as background. They introduce relational history, the biography of a dyadic tie, made of three parameters: founding conditions, trajectories and critical events. They distinguish it from relational embeddedness and from tie age. They then show how the concept reorients four debates: network cognition (historically informed accuracy; historically inflected activation), network agency (historically constituted agency and agency-shaped history), brokerage (including tertius iungens, gaudens and separans), and multiplexity (sequence of becoming multiplex; relational contraction/expansion). They propose data collection and analysis tools (interviews, longitudinal surveys, digital traces, relational event models, sequence analysis, latent growth curves) and close with four future research directions. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- The authors suggest that historically informed accuracy might help actors "counterbalance the influences of the founding conditions or trajectory of a tie" (p. 7, "Relational history and network cognition"); whether perceiving history loosens path dependence is stated as possible but not tested. (reader's inference)
- The brokerage section notes that tie "histories are not always visible to third parties" (p. 12, "Relational history and brokerage"), which leaves open how observers' differential knowledge of history shapes reputations in triads. (reader's inference)
- The methods section notes that the stochastic actor-oriented model "explicitly builds on Markov assumptions" (p. 15, "What Relational History Means for Data and Methods"); whether history-dependent extensions of such models are feasible is left open. (reader's inference)
