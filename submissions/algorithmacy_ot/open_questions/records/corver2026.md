# Corver & Filler (2026). Modeling Corporate Sustainability Change: A Deweyan Pragmatist Alternative.

## What it argues
The authors argue that corporate sustainability maturity models (CSMMs) rest on a representationalist and prescriptive view of models. They name two problems that follow: a representational-adequacy problem (external sustainability problems are treated as fixed and detached from internal processes) and a knowledge-transfer problem (a one-way supply chain from scholars to practitioners). In reply they draw on Dewey's account of inquiry, in which problem description and problem solving are co-constituted, and describe models as tools for ongoing inquiry. They develop a Deweyan approach to corporate sustainability modeling (DACSM) with three dimensions (activity, actions, consequences) and six modeling actions, three of model construction and three of model use. Adequacy is judged by consequences, retrospectively. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "this approach reflects a conception of knowledge production that overemphasizes the role of models as finished products and struggles to account for the lived practice of corporate sustainability change."
