# Scherer, A. G., & Neesham, C. (2023). Organized Immaturity in a Post-Kantian Perspective: Toward a critical theory of surveillance capitalism.

## What it argues
The authors argue that the loss of autonomy documented in critiques of surveillance capitalism, Zuboff's above all, has a deeper cause: "organized immaturity," the erosion of people's capacity for the public use of reason under socio-technological systems. From Zuboff's evidence they derive three maturity-eroding patterns: infantilization, reductionism and totalization. They trace the concept of (im)maturity from Kant's mentalist maxims (unprejudiced, enlarged, consecutive thought: introspective maturity) through Hegel's historicism to Habermas's communicative conditions (reversibility of standpoints, inclusion, reciprocity: socialized maturity). Combined, the Habermas–Kant framework grounds countermeasures: professional oaths for designers of digital infrastructure, democratic control of algorithm design, regulation of large language models, education, datafication-free public spaces, and roles for the state, businesses, NGOs and social movements. Without cultivated maturity, they argue, Zuboff's call for institutional revolution remains utopian. (reader's paraphrase)
