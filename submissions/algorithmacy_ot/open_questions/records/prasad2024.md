# Prasad, M. (2024). Disagreeing to Agree: Problem-Solving and Knowledge Production.

## What it argues
Prasad responds to Abend's complaint that unresolved disagreement over words blocks knowledge. She agrees that unchecked disagreement often yields only more disagreement, but treats definitional disputes as a small part of scholarly disagreement and asks how Abend's proposed definitional conferences would ever happen or bind anyone. Her answer draws on two routes to closure: psychology's reform (pre-registration), which grew out of disagreeing with disagreement but suits only some fields, and communities formed around solving a practical problem, such as climate change, whose members need agreement to act. Following pragmatist fallibilism, inaction is also a decision; acting in community with humility yields provisional knowledge. On this view a conference of the kind Abend proposes is a symptom of a knowledge-seeking community, not its cause; she closes by inviting readers to the problem-solving sociology infrastructure. (reader's paraphrase)
