# Phillips, N., Kalvapalle, S., & Kennedy, M. (2024). Beyond the Turing Test: Exploring the implications of generative AI for category construction.

## What it argues
Phillips, Kalvapalle and Kennedy open a Controversies and Conversations set by claiming that generative AI now does category work, that is, it uses categories with cultural savvy and takes part in constructing them. They test the claim against the "participation game" (Kennedy & Phillips, 2023), which asks an AI to pass in interaction, produce novel texts, and persuade humans to accept its categorizations; current systems, they argue, meet all three, and the LaMDA episode serves as an example. A typology of four modes (digital assistant, co-pilot, artificial team member, digital influencer) orders the likely impact on categories by the AI's agency and goals. Against both AI optimists and pessimists, the authors propose a cultural-evolution reading: generative AI alters variation, transmission and selection, and is new in that it produces cultural variation itself. They close by calling for a symbolic turn in organization theory and for taking technology seriously. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. The typology ranks the co-pilot mode as having "less potential to impact the social construction of categories" because it conforms to existing categories (p. 9, "Modes of implementation of generative AI in organizations"). The essay does not test whether expert co-pilot use reproduces categories, which would itself shape them over time. (reader's inference)
2. In the influencer mode, "Whoever develops this system will then be able to provide the generative AI system with specific goals" (p. 10, "Modes of implementation of generative AI in organizations"). The developer or goal-setter stands as a third party behind the AI's category work; the essay does not theorize that party's role. (reader's inference)
3. The essay treats persuasion as the test of participation, but the evidence it cites, personalized ChatGPT messages (Matz et al., 2024, p. 8), concerns single messages. Whether persuasion scales into collective acceptance of a category is not shown. (reader's inference)
