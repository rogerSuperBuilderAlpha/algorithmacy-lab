# Jaser, Z., & Tourish, D. (2024). Hegemonic surveillance at work: Fabricating the cyberized, totalized and thespianized employee.

## What it argues
Opening with the Royal Mail "Outdoor Actuals" PDA tracking system, agreed with the postal workers' union, the authors ask why workers, managers and unions increasingly consent to workplace surveillance rather than resist it. They propose a four-level model of "hegemonic surveillance": neoliberal ideology, which treats workers as units of productive capacity (socio/political); surveillance made inescapable (continuous performance monitoring, algorithmic management) and pervasive (bodily, cognitive and emotional, health and lifestyle) (institutional); performative consent, marked by an appearance of voluntariness and performative display, extending Gramsci's hegemony and Burawoy's manufactured consent (relational); and a totally surveilled worker who is cyberized, totalized and thespianized, with the back stage eliminated (individual). The theory is explicitly future-looking and dystopian, acknowledges that resistance persists and that the trajectory is not inevitable, and closes by calling for democratic oversight and an ideological challenge to neoliberalism. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "In this paper, therefore, we are less concerned with resistance, which is an already well-theorized phenomenon in studies of surveillance ... ## Implicit openings
- (reader's inference) The authors "conjecture that managers are likely to conclude that what they need is more surveillance. Paradoxically, this may unleash ever more ingenious forms of resistance" (p. 16, "Resistance"). The conjecture names an escalation dynamic that the paper does not test.
- (reader's inference) The paper reports an agreement between Royal Mail and the union (Table 1, p. 3) and treats the union's consent as performative consent (p. 11). How a third party such as a union mediates consent between management and workers is described in the example but not theorized as a distinct mechanism.
- (reader's inference) On mitigation proposals, the authors "remain unconvinced that this is possible" (p. 16, "Discussion"). Whether design or governance alternatives (e.g., developmental rather than deterrent use) can change the trajectory is left open.
