# Godart & Wittman (2024). Staying True to Ourselves: Organizational purity at the crossroads of institutional logics and identity work.

## What it argues
The authors introduce "organizational purity", an organization's steadfast adherence in action to a single institutional logic. They say it is driven by identity work and self-categorization, not by survival. Purity runs on a continuum and can be compartmentalized, as when Chanel keeps haute couture apart from its mass-market lines. Pure organizations tend to be predictable, may carry a "purity discount" in resources, and can either spark institutional change in new fields or block it where their logic dominates. Audiences respond by granting authenticity and status, compliance-based legitimacy, or contempt, depending on how closely each audience's logic aligns with the organization's. The authors say purity helps explain non-rational action and extends the institutional logics perspective. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- The paper puts hybridization on the table without settling it: "Whether purity is antithetical to hybridization is a thorny issue since hybrids can themselves become new logics." (pp. 6–7, "Purity as continuum"). Its case 4 gives one reading, but what makes a hybrid a new "pure" logic is left open. (reader's inference)
- The authors place their work beside, not against, pluralism: "Neither do we challenge the complex micro underpinnings of pluralism (McPherson & Sauder, 2013)." (p. 3, "Introduction"). How micro-level actors inside a pure organization hold the single logic is not addressed. (reader's inference)
- The paper predicts different audience responses (Figure 2, p. 14) and some outcomes with cases, but no test; "We predict that a given audience will award a pure organization status, legitimacy, or contempt" (p. 12, "Audience Responses to Organizational Purity") stays a proposition. (reader's inference)
