# Ernst, Lassalle, Kerr & Robinson (2026). The Construction of the Organizational Object in Organization Studies: An Invitation to Epistemic Reflexivity with Bourdieu.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) The authors argue that organization studies (OS) scholars should treat the construction of the research object as the place where reflexivity does its work. Using Bourdieu's theory and career as an exemplar, they set out three overlapping principles of "epistemic reflexivity": breaking with pre-constructed categories, objectifying the researcher's own position in the academic field, and positioning the study against other studies and traditions. They say these principles grow across a career into an embodied "reflexive researcher habitus". The paper reviews reflexivity debates in OS, retraces Bourdieu's research practice, and applies the principles to OS research objects (sector and industry categories, diversity management, the Covid-19 field disruption). It claims the approach counters scholastic bias, theoretical reductionism and a journal monoculture. The authors frame it as an invitation, not a prescription.

## Implicit openings
- (reader's inference) The three principles are illustrated with examples from published studies (Bourdieu's housing-market study, Özbilgin & Tatli 2011, Daly & Larsen 2026), and the paper gives no worked application of the full set to a new organizational object; how one would do so is left to readers (p. 16, "Conclusions", "an invitation").
- (reader's inference) The paper says reflexivity is "a collective rather than an individual endeavour" (p. 14, "Theory generation in OS") but the principles are described at the level of the researcher's career and habitus; what collective routines of a lab, journal or review process would carry them is not specified.
