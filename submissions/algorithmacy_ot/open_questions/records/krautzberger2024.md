# Krautzberger & Tuckermann (2024). Navigating Both/And and Either/Or Approaches in Response to Paradoxical Demands: A meta-both/and approach.

## What it argues
The authors start from the point that paradox theory now treats either/or responses as serious alternatives to both/and, yet cognitive and organizational structures bias members toward one or the other. Using Luhmann's "decision paradox", they argue that choosing between the two approaches is itself a choice that routine structures make invisible. Their process model adds "reflective episodes": organizations decouple from daily work when members feel "meta-undecidability", apply a "principle of otherness" to the approach they are not using, and then recouple by deciding. Repeated episodes build a portfolio of both/and and either/or approaches, which they call a meta-both/and approach. They place the contribution in the paradoxical relation between the two approaches and in a new concept, reflective episodes. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "previous research indicates that both/and and either/or approaches are based on contradictory beliefs and organizational structures, making it difficult to leverage them effectively" (p. 1, "Abstract"). On Berti and Cunha: "Berti and Cunha neither elaborate on how organizations can establish a rational choice nor expand on how to maintain either/or and both/and approaches as viable alternatives." (p. 5, "Organizational Paradox Theory and the Decision Paradox").
- Named interlocutors: Berti & Cunha (2023); Luhmann (2018); Smith & Lewis (2011); Smith & Besharov (2019); Smith (2014); Hendry & Seidl (2003); Lüscher & Lewis (2008); Jay (2013); Jarzabkowski & Lê (2017); Seidl, Lê & Jarzabkowski (2021); Tuckermann (2019); Cunha & Putnam (2019).
- OT articles it cites: Weiser & Laamanen (2022), "Extending the dynamic equilibrium model of paradox" (Organization Theory 3(3)).
- Move: process model with a new concept (reflective episodes), bringing systems theory into paradox theory (reader's paraphrase).

## Implicit openings
- The model rests on members meeting "meta-undecidability" after a shock or discussion (p. 14, "Initiating reflective episodes"). The paper does not say how an organization without such a trigger would start an episode other than "purposively constructing" one (p. 15). (reader's inference)
- The authors say reflective episodes "do not negate the importance of cognitive and organizational both/and structures" (p. 19, "Cognitive and organizational both/and structures"); how an organization sets the balance between episodes and daily routines is not specified. (reader's inference)
- The empirical anchor is Covid-19 hospital resource allocation (p. 11); whether the model holds in other settings is untested in the text. (reader's inference)
