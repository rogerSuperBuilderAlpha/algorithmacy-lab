# Schoeneborn, D., Dobusch, L., & Seidl, D. (2026). Toward a Theory of Organizationality: A Gradual View on Collective Actorhood.

## What it argues
Organization theory faces an "openness-specificity dilemma": a narrow, entity-based definition of organization misses new forms such as platforms, networks and communities, while a broad processual definition blurs organization into sociality in general. The authors take the third way of "organizationality", which treats organization as a quality held in degrees. They sort earlier proposals into configurational, stage-based and axial logics and choose an axial one, with collective actorhood as the single defining criterion. Collective actorhood arises when communication attributes agency to a collective social address. Recurrent attribution practices "charge" the collective with actorhood (anthropomorphizing metaphors, legal personhood, accusations of corporate irresponsibility); others "discharge" it by attributing actions to individuals (scapegoating, heroizing a founder or CEO). Identity claims and interlinked decision-making become secondary concepts that contribute to actorhood. The paper closes with a four-question research agenda. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: against narrow, entitative definitions and overly broad processual ones: "an overly narrow approach risks rendering organization studies irrelevant by failing to account for new organizational phenomena, an excessively broad view risks blurring the distinction between organization and sociality in general" (p. 1, "Abstract"). Extends Wilhoit and Kisselburgh's axial view, which does "not clarify the conceptual grounds on which such variations can be accounted for" (p. 4, "Organizationality as a 'Third-Way' Approach"), and responds to the critique that the approach is underdeveloped (Dion et al., 2025; p. 2, "Introduction").
- Named interlocutors: Dobusch & Schoeneborn (2015); Wilhoit & Kisselburgh (2015); Grothe-Hammer (2019); Ahrne & Brunsson (2011) and Ahrne, Brunsson & Seidl (2016) on partial organization; King, Felin & Whetten (2010) on the organization as social actor; Dion, Cooren & Dupuis (2025); du Gay (2020) and du Gay & Vikkelsø (2016) on formal organization; Luhmann (2018) on initial conceptual distinctions; List & Pettit (2011) on group agency; Bromley & Sharkey (2017) on expanding actorhood.
- OT articles it cites: Dion, Cooren & Dupuis (2025), "Organization theory and ontological claims: Working out tensions in CCO scholarship and organization studies"; Roulet & Pichler (2020), "Blame game theory: Scapegoating, whistleblowing and discursive struggles following accusations of organizational misconduct"; Winkler & Schoeneborn (2025), "The escalating dis/organization of post-truth communication".
- Move: synthesis (typology of configurational, stage-based and axial logics) plus new conceptual framework (gradual collective actorhood; charging/discharging) and agenda-setting.

## Implicit openings
- The authors call their choice of criterion "obviously a contingent choice in that other criteria could have been chosen instead" (p. 5, "Collective Actorhood as a Defining Criterion of Organizationality"). What organizationality would look like on a different axis is left open. (reader's inference)
- The framework claims that "empirical cases can be compared more readily because they are assessed along one and the same underlying dimension" (p. 12, "Conceptualizing Organizationality as a Continuum Between Individual and Collective Actorhood"), but the text gives no procedure for placing a case on that axis. How degree is observed or measured remains open. (reader's inference)
- Discharging can run toward "individual (human or other-than-human) social addresses" (p. 11, "Discussion"), and digital platforms may "strategically downplay their collective actorhood status to avoid accountability or to preempt regulation" (p. 13, "Dynamizing Organizationality"). Attribution to non-human addresses, such as technologies, is named but not theorized. (reader's inference)
