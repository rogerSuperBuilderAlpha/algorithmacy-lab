# Pavlisa, Harvey & Doh (2026). Stakeholder Governance and Inequality: A Theory of Reciprocal Exchange.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) Stakeholder governance research looks at fairness of exchange and value allocation but not at how firm-stakeholder exchanges produce advantage or disadvantage that adds up to societal inequality. The authors bring in Bourdieu's capital and field concepts. A firm and its stakeholders sit in different but homologous economic fields and exchange several forms of capital (economic, social, cultural, symbolic). Whether an exchange helps a stakeholder depends on the weighting and convertibility of what is given in the stakeholder's own field. Inadequate provision, with low weighting or low convertibility, weakens stakeholder power, and repeated localised disadvantage aggregates into broader inequality. The paper states a principle of fair reciprocal exchange, gives an analytic framework (Figure 1), and illustrates it with Rio Tinto, ending with managerial implications.

### OQ-pavlisa2026-04
- Quote: "These considerations reveal the boundary conditions of this theory while simultaneously paving the way for further developments of a capitals lens in stakeholder governance." (p. 19, "Discussion")
- Form: boundary_condition
- Scope: The theory assumes the rationality and morality of economic actors. The authors name two ways that assumption fails: reliance on generalised morality ("business norms and networks may vary in moral intent") and the role of individual cognition ("Misunderstandings may lead to the risks of spiralling retaliatory reciprocity"), including claims of positive change that carry no real value, such as "greenwashing".
- Addressee: stakeholder governance scholars, behavioural stakeholder theorists
- Level of analysis: multi-level
- Paraphrase: Moral intent and individual cognition limit the theory and mark where it can be developed. (reader's paraphrase)
