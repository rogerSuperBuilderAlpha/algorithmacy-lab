# Grodal, S., Ha, J., Hood, E., & Rajunov, M. (2024). Between Humans and Machines: The social construction of the generative AI category.

## What it argues
The authors reply to Phillips, Kalvapalle and Kennedy (2024). They accept that humans and machines co-construct categories, but ask why generative AI draws attention for doing so while other technologies that also shape categories, such as quantitative trading and technology standards, go unnoticed. Their answer is a model (Figure 1): a technology is perceived as a participant in the social construction of categories when people believe it challenges the human–machine boundary and so anthropomorphize it; this perception is moderated by the technology's affordances (accessible chat interfaces versus hidden trading algorithms) and by powerful stakeholders with vested interests in promoting or hiding it. How far generative AI is allowed to construct categories is therefore itself socially negotiated. They close with a paragraph of future-research directions and open questions about where the human–machine boundary will move. (reader's paraphrase)
