# Nigam, A., Amis, J., & Logue, D. (2025). People as Institutions or Why Taylor Swift Rules the World.

## What it argues
The authors propose that an individual person, not an office-holder, can become an institution. Using Taylor Swift as the case, they argue that a focal individual can become the point around which an enduring set of roles, role relations and practices forms, embodying values (authenticity, inclusiveness, community) and offering a "blueprint" for living. They adapt Ocasio's (2023) cross-level process model into three stages, typification, objectivation and sedimentation, and illustrate each with Swiftie practices (friendship bracelets, Easter-egg hunting, theory crafting) and with Swift's effects on other institutions (music industry, ticketing, politics, sport). They set reciprocity and resonance as boundary conditions that separate person-institutions from celebrities (Oprah Winfrey as the contrast) and close with questions about emergence, transmission, intersections with formal roles and the death of the focal person. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: extends Ocasio's process model and Selznick's organizations-as-institutions to persons: "We echo this thinking to propose that individual people can become institutions that are similarly imbued with values and meaning, and co-constituted by a set of relations." (p. 3, opening section, untitled). Against an office-based view (Searle): "Our theorizing differs in that we see Taylor Swift as an institution in and of herself." (p. 4, "Typification"). Against the field's focus: "We argue that, by neglecting this idea, organizational theorists have missed a powerful opportunity for developing insight into the emergence and spread of institutions and their attendant values, norms and understandings." (p. 2, opening section, untitled)
- Named interlocutors: Ocasio (2023); Selznick (1949); Searle (2005); Bitektine et al. (2020); Berger & Luckmann (1967); Zucker (1977); Tolbert & Zucker (1996); Meyer et al. (2018); Zietsma & Toubiana (2018); Douglas (1986); Schwartz (1996).

## Implicit openings
1. (reader's inference) The model places reciprocity at its centre while also crediting a management team that steers fans and sidelines norm-violators: "Taylor Swift’s management team – part of the set of committed role practitioners – actively nurture fan loyalty, steer behaviours (discouraging hate, promoting kindness) and protect narratives through regulative and legal means" (p. 6, "Sedimentation"). How deliberate organizing and emergent fan practice combine in a person-institution is left open.
2. (reader's inference) The piece notes but does not examine variation within the community: "While there is clearly heterogeneity, the shared and recognizable experience is apparent." (p. 6, "Objectivation"). How heterogeneous role practitioners sustain one blueprint is unaddressed.
3. (reader's inference) Digital connection is named as the channel for typification across geographies ("These communities are connected digitally and located around the world", p. 3, opening section) and as a condition in OQ-10, but the role of digital media in the three stages is not specified.
