# Winkler, P., & Schoeneborn, D. (2025). The Escalating Dis/Organization of Post-Truth Communication.

## What it argues
The authors ask how post-truth communication (PTC) manages to organize itself and keep its "anti-establishment" character while attacking, simulating and partly displacing established organizations. Using the communicative-constitution-of-organization (CCO) view of organizationality, they combine Cooper's "dis/organization" with Serres' "parasite" to propose "parasitic disorganizationality": gradual organization achieved through disorder. Their model (Figure 1) pairs three ordering properties of established organization (interconnected decision-making, identity from boundary drawing, actorhood from authority) with three suppressed parasitic properties (undecidability, hypocrisy, fictionality) and three PTC properties that escalate them: disruption of decision-making, surface simulation of identity, and exploitation of authority with blame reversal (the "Joker"). Escalation is two-sided. Expelling PTC by asserting order feeds it; the authors recommend that established organizations acknowledge their own parasitic properties in order to de-escalate. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. The model is said to apply to "external attack", "internal infiltration" and "partial displacement" (pp. 9–10, "Unfolding the Escalating Dis/Organization Dynamics of Post-Truth Communication"), but it is illustrated with secondary examples only. How to observe the three dynamics empirically, and how to tell the scenarios apart, is left open. (reader's inference)
2. Digital media and "platform providers" appear among parasitic agents (p. 14, "How PTC accomplishes actorhood through exploitation of authority") and "largely unregulated and attention-driven digital media environments" facilitate parasitic cascades (p. 11). The role of platforms and their sorting mechanisms as intermediaries in the host–parasite relation is not modelled. (reader's inference)
3. The recommended de-escalation (embracing parasitic properties, "tolerance of ambiguity", p. 17, "Practical and societal implications") is stated as a proposition, without conditions under which it would or would not work. (reader's inference)
