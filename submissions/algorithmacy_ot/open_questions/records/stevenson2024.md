# Stevenson, Honingh & Brandsen (2024). Putting Centrality Central in the Study of Institutional Complexity: On the Relative and Relational Aspects of the Centrality of Institutional Logics.

## What it argues
The authors argue that research on institutional complexity has focused on the compatibility of institutional logics and neglected their centrality, the meaning a logic has to actors relative to other logics. Centrality has two aspects: relational (a logic is central only through its relation to actors) and relative (central only against other logics). They compare five assumptions each of the institutional logics approach (IL) and inhabited institutionalism (II). IL suits the relative aspect but not the relational one; II suits the relational aspect but, at first sight, not the relative one. They propose three considerations that would let II study the relative aspect, and note that the mutually constitutive view of IL probably suits both. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "scholars have focused on the compatibility among different institutional logics, while paying little attention to the centrality of institutional logics" (p. 1, abstract); "Most scholars studying institutional complexity only discuss how different logics of interest are conflicting to the actors under study" (p. 2, "Introduction"); they distinguish "relational" from the notion of "adherence" (p. 5, "The Relational and Relative Aspects of Centrality").

## Implicit openings
- (reader's inference) The authors state that II scholars "should actively take three points into consideration" and that doing so "would open the door for the empirical study of both aspects of centrality using II" (pp. 11-12, "Inhabited Institutionalism" and "Discussion and Conclusion"); no empirical study applying the considerations is offered.
- (reader's inference) The paper says "it is unlikely for actors to be stably and tightly coupled to multiple logics at the same time and in the same place" (p. 11, "Inhabited Institutionalism"); how long such multi-logic coupling lasts is stated as a likelihood, not tested.
