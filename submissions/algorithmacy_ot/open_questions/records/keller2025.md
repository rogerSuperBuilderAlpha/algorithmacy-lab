# Keller, Berti & Smith (2025). Paradoxical Theorizing: A multimodal approach to generate new theory.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) The authors return to Poole and Van de Ven's 1989 proposal that scholars use paradox to build theory. They add a four-stage process model of what they call paradoxical theorizing: surface tensions, differentiate the opposing poles, integrate across them, and manage the issues that follow. They then argue that theorizing is more than cognitive reasoning and sort it into four modalities (embrained, embodied, social, material), each with its own embedded tensions. A table sets out how the four stages play out in each modality. The three stated contributions are: more creative and impactful theory, more diverse voices in theorizing, and less risk of field hegemony. The article asks scholars to treat the tensions in their own scholarly work, including co-author conflict, emotion, language, time, space and technology, as a source of ideas.

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "Over three decades ago, Marshall Scott Poole and Andrew Van de Ven proposed using paradoxes to develop more rigorous and robust organizational theories. We reignite their efforts while offering two important advances." (p. 1, Abstract). Against a cognitive-only view: "Often theorizing is conceptualized as an individual endeavor based on formal analysis and reasoning (Shapira, 2011)." (p. 2, introduction)

## Open questions
### OQ-keller2025-01
- Quote: "We invite future scholarship to continue to explore how paradox theory applies to our own work as scholars and theorists." (p. 16, "Discussion")
- Form: invitation
- Scope: Paradox theory applied reflexively to scholarly work, for example to data collection and analysis, to framing papers and articulating contributions, and to rigor versus relevance. The Discussion names these three examples (Langley & Klag, 2019; Locke & Golden-Biddle, 1997; Sharma et al., 2022). The Conclusion repeats the call: "Future scholarship can continue to expand upon how we can navigate the tensions in our work as scholars." (p. 16, "Conclusion")
- Addressee: paradox scholars and organizational theorists generally
- Level of analysis: multi-level
- Paraphrase: Other scholars should extend paradox theory to the tensions in how scholars themselves research, write and theorize. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-keller2025-02
- Quote: "requires an understanding of fixed constraints—both material and institutional." (p. 14, "Material modality")
- Form: boundary_condition
- Scope: Limits on a theorist's freedom to change rhythm, space or technology, and on the incentives to produce alternative outputs of theorizing. The preceding text names "Disciplinary conventions and reward systems" as the institutional constraint.
- Addressee: theorists attempting paradoxical theorizing; no one is directly asked to research it
- Level of analysis: field
- Paraphrase: The approach assumes that fixed material and institutional constraints can be identified, and the article does not say how they should be studied. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-keller2025-03
- Quote: "What is an opposing theory, insight, or ontological assumption to my current theory? What might be the opposite understanding of this theory?" (p. 4, "A process model of paradoxical theorizing")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: Questions that a theorist asks while surfacing tensions. They are method prompts offered to the reader, not questions the authors leave unanswered for the field.
- Addressee: individual theorists
- Level of analysis: individual
- Paraphrase: The authors pose the questions as a working technique for the first stage of the model. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. (reader's inference) "paradoxical theorizing will require active recognition of alternative uses of time, space, and technology" (p. 13, "Material modality"), said of a time when "generative AI becomes taken for granted". How a technology that theorists now "reinterpret ... as an active agent instead of simply a tool" enters the tensions of theorizing is left open.
2. (reader's inference) "This includes recognizing when to make “both/and” or “either/or” decisions about which contradictory yet interrelated theories to build on" (p. 5, "A process model of paradoxical theorizing"). The text gives no criteria for the choice, and p. 8 adds that "not all theories and concepts can be integrated".
3. (reader's inference) "Organizational theory should constantly expand to address new societal challenges (Barley, 2016), while preserving robust knowledge accumulation." (p. 16, "Discussion"). How the field keeps both is left open.
