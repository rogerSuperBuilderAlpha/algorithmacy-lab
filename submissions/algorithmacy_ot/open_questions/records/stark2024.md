# Stark, D., & Vanden Broeck, P. (2024). Principles of Algorithmic Management.

## What it argues
The authors reject the view that algorithmic management is an amplified Taylorism, a workplace device for supervising labor, or the automation of managerial decisions. They tie it instead to a change in the topology of organization: the most valuable actors, assets and activities now sit on platforms rather than inside firms, so the managerial problem becomes how to manage what one does not contain. Where hierarchies command, markets contract and networks collaborate, platforms co-opt. The paper compares scientific, collaborative and algorithmic management on five dimensions (organizational form, object of management, ideology, modality, accountability) and summarises them in Table 1: platform, co-opting many types of user, immediacy/totality, synthetic modality, and "twisted" accountability. A closing section sets a research agenda that joins the study of organization with the study of communication, treating algorithms as communication partners and technology as organization. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. Note 5 expects hybrid structures: "we should expect to find mixtures of bureaucratic structure and algorithmic structure (Christin et al., 2023)" (p. 17, note 5). The paper does not say how such mixtures are composed or studied; that is left open. (reader's inference)
2. The authors claim one firm can house all three management styles: "At Amazon, for example, we can find scientific management in the operation of its distribution centers, collaborative management in the projects of its research units, and algorithmic management on its platforms" (p. 7, "Organizational form"). How the three styles interact within one entity is not examined. (reader's inference)
3. The triangular rating structure, in which "The platform operator leverages rather than delegates." (p. 14, "Accountability"), is stated as the shape of platform power but not modelled further as a three-party relation. (reader's inference)
