# Whyte, J., Comi, A., & Mosca, L. (2023). Simultaneously Here and There: Situating online organizing in our embodied material practices.

## What it argues
Research on online and offline practices has asked how they co-evolve over time; the authors ask how they co-exist in time. Drawing on practice theory (embodiment, materiality, and connections between practices), they argue that an online practice such as a video-conference meeting is always grounded in multiple, mostly unshared offline practices in which participants are bodily present: homes, offices, time zones, children, devices and infrastructures. Participants' experience of presence, proximity and participation online is constituted in relation to these offline practices. Being simultaneously "here" and "there" has three organizational consequences: boundaries between practices blur (work and home, morning and evening), purposes get diverted, and glitches and breakdowns expose the material infrastructure. Illustrations come from the authors' own hybrid paper-development workshop. They claim two contributions: recasting online organizing as embedded in unshared offline practices, and theorizing the implications of that co-existence for interacting and organizing. (reader's paraphrase)
