# Whyte, J., Comi, A., & Mosca, L. (2023). Simultaneously Here and There: Situating online organizing in our embodied material practices.

## What it argues
Research on online and offline practices has asked how they co-evolve over time; the authors ask how they co-exist in time. Drawing on practice theory (embodiment, materiality, and connections between practices), they argue that an online practice such as a video-conference meeting is always grounded in multiple, mostly unshared offline practices in which participants are bodily present: homes, offices, time zones, children, devices and infrastructures. Participants' experience of presence, proximity and participation online is constituted in relation to these offline practices. Being simultaneously "here" and "there" has three organizational consequences: boundaries between practices blur (work and home, morning and evening), purposes get diverted, and glitches and breakdowns expose the material infrastructure. Illustrations come from the authors' own hybrid paper-development workshop. They claim two contributions: recasting online organizing as embedded in unshared offline practices, and theorizing the implications of that co-existence for interacting and organizing. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: prior work on online–offline interrelations studied co-evolution over time rather than co-existence in time (p. 2, "Introduction") (reader's paraphrase); and against Uhlin's multiple-practice view (p. 14, "Concluding Remarks").
- Named interlocutors: Shove, Pantzar & Watson (2012); Karunakaran, Orlikowski & Scott (2021); Rauch & Ansari (2022); Nicolini (2007, 2012); Hafermalz & Riemer (2020); Cooren & Fairhurst (2009); Uhlin (2022); Oh et al. (2018).
- OT articles it cites: Whyte, Comi & Mosca (2022), "Making futures that matter".
- Move: new conceptual framing (simultaneity of online and offline practice) with an agenda for research.

## Implicit openings
- "As new proximities are formed, there is a danger that the real-world consequences of online organizing are seen as distant and thus are under-recognized." (p. 8, "Proximity"); how organizations counter this moral detachment is raised, not studied. (reader's inference)
- "Hidden infrastructure, may, for example, connect those in an online practice with other unrelated online practices, as images, audio and other data become accessible to large software providers" (p. 14, "Concluding Remarks"); the role of software providers as unseen third parties in meetings is opened but not theorized. (reader's inference)
- "As questions arise about where the jurisdiction of the corporation legitimately starts and ends" (p. 10, "Blurring boundaries"); the employer's jurisdiction over home spaces is named and left open. (reader's inference)
