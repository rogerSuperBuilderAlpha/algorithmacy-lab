# Ocasio (2023). Institutions and Their Social Construction: A Cross-Level Perspective.

## What it argues
Ocasio defines an institution as a taken-for-granted, organized system of roles and interactions. He modifies Berger and Luckmann's micro-phenomenological account (typification, objectivation, sedimentation) by adding two things: sedimentation requires a committed network of role practitioners, and it occurs at several levels (communities, fields, societies, world system) that are culturally embedded in higher-level institutional orders. He introduces "micro-institutions" as functionally dependent components of larger institutions, and treats institutions as both external and internal to organizations, supplying blueprints for organizing. He adopts a critical realist ontology and claims partial autonomy of levels. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- (reader's inference) The author notes that sedimentation and objectivation "are overlapping and need to be clearly demarcated, particularly not when the processes are still occurring" (p. 7, "Sedimentation"); the point at which a practice counts as sedimented in a live case is left unsettled.
- (reader's inference) The claim that "the locus of sedimentation ... occurs at multiple levels" is illustrated by single cases (mutual funds, hospital medicine, the Catholic Church) (pp. 8-9, "The Macro Structure of Institutions"); no comparative test of locus is proposed.
