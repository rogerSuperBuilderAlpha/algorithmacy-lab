# Gümüsay, A. A., & Reinecke, J. (2024). Imagining Desirable Futures: A call for prospective theorizing with speculative rigour.

## What it argues
The authors argue that organization theory is bound to the empirical past, which leaves scholars as followers of reality rather than thought leaders, and that the planetary emergency makes the future unlike the past. They call for "prospective theorizing": theorizing oriented to imagining desirable futures, which they describe as pragmatist, preparatory and prefigurative. It requires a double leap, from projection to imagination (onto-epistemological axis) and from values-neutral to values-led claims (axiological axis), giving four modes of theorizing futures (Figure 1). Table 1 lists practices for each leap at the input, throughput and output stages of theorizing (imaginatory and values-led data, thought experiments and values extrapolation, metaphors and purposive abstractions). Because such theory cannot be validated against data, they propose "speculative rigour" with four criteria: generative potency, process transparency, speculative plausibility and plausible desirability (Table 2). (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. The authors observe that language models' "predictive outputs are likewise backward-oriented, as these language models are trained on data inputs sourced from the empirical past." (p. 2, introduction). Whether and how generative AI could serve prospective (imaginative, values-led) theorizing is not taken up. (reader's inference)
2. The criteria are ranked only loosely: "speculative rigour is enhanced when multiple criteria are satisfied" (p. 18, "Plausible desirability"). How reviewers should weigh the four criteria against one another, or against conventional criteria, is left open. (reader's inference)
3. "Whether an actualization of desirable futures is pursued is not for academics to decide alone" (p. 18, "Plausible desirability"): the deliberative process that would decide, and the scholar's role in it, is not specified. (reader's inference)
