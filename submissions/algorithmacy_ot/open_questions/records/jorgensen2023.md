# Jørgensen & Beyes (2023). Organizing Half-Things: Knowing, Theorizing and Studying Atmospheres.

## What it argues
Jørgensen and Beyes take stock of the rising notion of atmosphere in organization studies. They sketch a genealogy of atmospheric thought (Schmitz, Böhme, Sloterdijk, Anderson, McCormack, feminist new materialism) and read atmospheres as "half-things": in-between forces that condition organized life and are themselves organized. From the genealogy they draw an onto-epistemology (situation, embodiment, envelopment), a methodology (attunement) and a politics (rupture). They argue that organizational atmospherics has leaned toward affirmative readings and has not yet taken up the dark side and the politics of atmospheres. (reader's paraphrase)

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "this forming has led to concerns about a paucity of shared assumptions and the lack of a common vocabulary" (p. 2, "How to Give Form to the Atmospheric?"); Julmi's suggestion to relate atmosphere to organizational culture: "half-things can hardly be reduced to (themselves perhaps outdated) assumptions of organization culture in formal organizations" (p. 3, "How to Give Form to the Atmospheric?"); "there seems to be a largely shared belief in, and practice of, finding and performing the notion of (affective) atmospheres optimistically" (p. 11, "Half-Thing Critique: Rupture").
- Named interlocutors: Schmitz (2014a, 2014b); Böhme; Sloterdijk (2009); McCormack (2018); Anderson (2009); Stewart (2007, 2011); Ahmed (2010, 2014); Gherardi (2019); Michels & Steyaert (2016); Ash & Gallacher (2015); Philippopoulos-Mihalopoulos (2016); Julmi (2017a, 2017b); Berlant (2011).
- OT articles it cites: Beyes & Holt (2020), "The topographical imagination: Space and organization theory"; Cooren (2020), "Beyond entanglement: (Socio-)materiality and organization studies"; Parker (2022), "Weeds: Classification, organization, and wilding".
- Move: synthesis and genealogy; onto-epistemological, methodological and political vocabulary for a nascent field; critique of the field's affirmative tilt.
