# Giovannoni, E., & Quattrone, P. (2025). Nothing is Right in Individual and Collective Action: Placing unknowability at the heart of theorizing through rhetoric.

## What it argues
The authors argue that theories of organizational tensions and paradoxes assume the poles of a tension (profit, welfare, values, ends) can be known, either positively or through social construction. They propose instead to place unknowability, a "no-thing", at the heart of theorizing. Organization studies has treated rhetoric as persuasion, legitimation and reality construction; the authors recover from medieval and ancient rhetoric a different use, rhetoric as a ritualized practice of inquiry into what cannot be known. They derive three features (Figure 1): tensions and "di-visions", imagination and invention (the rhetorical grid), and moral scrutiny and rituals of "de-finition". They illustrate these with anecdotal examples from the UN Sustainable Development Goals (trade-off matrices, the UK IPA roadmap, annual SDG summits), and argue that academics and practitioners acting as "medieval rhetors" would produce open-ended, revisable theory. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. The SDG illustrations are described as "anecdotal examples inspired by the Sustainable Development Goals" (p. 1, Abstract). How the three rhetorical features would be observed or traced in empirical fieldwork is not specified; OQ-10 names the methodological gap without a design. (reader's inference)
2. The SDG trade-off matrix is read as a grid "where the SDGs serve as coordinates" and as "a rhetorical device" (p. 11, "Tensions and di-visions"). Whether other organizational devices of this kind (matrices, dashboards, accounting grids) can be used as inquiry devices rather than as knowledge devices is left open. (reader's inference)
3. The annual UN summit is cast as a ritual that kept a "group making it, as an acting ‘collective’" (p. 12, "Moral scrutiny and rituals of de-finition"). How such rituals bind collectives of differently situated actors, and with what effects on collective action, is asserted but not examined. (reader's inference)
