# Dion, F., Cooren, F., & Dupuis, J. (2025). Organization Theory and Ontological Claims: Working out tensions in CCO scholarship and organization studies.

## What it argues
The authors challenge the claim, made by Schoeneborn and colleagues (2019, 2022), that "organizationality" can serve as an ecumenical umbrella between entitative and processual views of organization in CCO scholarship. They argue that the "theory of organizationality", which merges Luhmannian decided-order theory (Ahrne & Brunsson) with CCO's focus on communication through three criteria (interconnected decisions, collective actorhood, identity claims), is a renewed entitative view: its "adjective" tension is really a verb–adjective–noun tension. Drawing on the Montreal School (Taylor & Van Every's organization "as Thirdness"), Bencherki & Iliadis, Simondon and Latour, they propose a fourth, "verb–adverb" tension in which organizationality is a property of communication, so that no ontological cut separates organizations from other collectives. They outline ontological, epistemological and methodological corollaries and urge scholars to state their ontological premises. (reader's paraphrase)
