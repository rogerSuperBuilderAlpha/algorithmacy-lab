# Paca & Rozuel (2024). The Emergence of Mythologies in Organisations: A process model.

## What it argues
The authors ask how and why some organisational stories become "mythologised", that is, acquire sacred qualities, while others do not. Drawing on Jung, they propose a recursive process model. Archetypes in the collective unconscious attach to suitable organisational images; members receive this "numinosity", interpret it through stories, and, when many members read the same image alike (synchronicity), the stories become shared. Leaders' psychological maturity, the organisation's stage of development and analogy to traditional myths (amplification) decide whether the story hardens into a mythology or lapses back into the unconscious. They argue that organisations driven by efficiency push other archetypal qualities into a "shadow", and that a conscious grasp of the process can support change and development. Future research is to test the model empirically. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- The model gives "Readiness" and "maturity" a decisive role (p. 10, "Readiness of the images"; p. 14, "Leadership development"), yet the article offers no way to assess an organisation's collective conscious maturity beyond studying "an organisation’s structures, practices and processes" (p. 13, "Engagement with numinosity at the organisational level"). How such maturity would be observed is left open. (reader's inference)
- The authors say myths cannot be instrumentally created: "it seems harder to argue that stories can be instrumentally mythologised in organisations" (p. 5, "Situating Myths in Organisations"). Yet the Discussion hopes to turn "failing change attempts into relatively more successful ones" (pp. 18-19). The relation between unconscious emergence and deliberate practice is not worked out. (reader's inference)
- The article treats the shadow and counter-narratives as signs of unmet archetypal qualities (p. 17, "Discussion") but does not say how a counter-narrative becomes mythologised in its own right. (reader's inference)
