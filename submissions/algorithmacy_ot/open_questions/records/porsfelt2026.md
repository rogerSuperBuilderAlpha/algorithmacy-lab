# Porsfelt, Vestergaard & Hjorth (2026). Image, Imagination, Imaginaries: A Genealogy of Imago-Concepts in Organisational Research.

## What it argues
(reader's paraphrase) "Imaginaries" has become a popular but diffuse term in organisation studies. The authors trace a genealogy from early philosophy of the mental image, through Kant and the Romantics, to the social-theory notion of imaginaries (Taylor, Castoriadis). They then inventory how organisation research has used image, imagination and imaginaries, and diagnose three inherited habits: images reduced to a single function, imagination narrowed to creativity, and imaginaries stretched across different theoretical jobs. Three interventions follow: images as layered palimpsests that shift across modes, imagination as a situated engagement with the non-present, and a split of imaginaries into a Taylorian lineage (inherited horizons) and a Castoriadian lineage (instituting forces). The last section adds "inter-subjunctive" imagination for shared, situated departures from the present, and a processual, new materialist reading in which imagination is a relational capacity ("intra-subjunctive").

## The conversation it joins
- Positions against / extends: "we show how inherited conceptual habits continue to constrain the field by reducing images to single discrete functions, by narrowing imagination to creativity, and by stretching imaginaries across distinct theoretical tasks in ways that blur differences which matter for analysis" (p. 1, Abstract). Also: "this enthusiastic uptake of the term has not been accompanied by greater clarity either about what imagination actually is or – by the same token – what imaginaries are and how they work" (p. 2, "Introduction"). It extends Komporozos-Athanasiou and Fotaki (2015) and the processual entrepreneurship stream (Hjorth, 2014; Hjorth et al., 2018) against the creativity-centred line (Thompson, 2018; Kier & McMullen, 2018).
- Named interlocutors: Ricoeur (2024); Taylor (2004); Castoriadis (1987); Augustine et al. (2019); Komporozos-Athanasiou & Fotaki (2015); Morgan (1986); Alvesson (1990); Shackle (1979); Beckert (2016, 2021); Weick (1989, 2005); Thompson (2018); Zittoun & Gillespie (2016); Kier & McMullen (2018); Alacovska & Kärreman (2023); Levy & Spicer (2013); Bell, Dacin & Toraldo (2021).
- OT articles it cites: Beckert (2021), "The firm as an engine of imagination"; Bell, Dacin & Toraldo (2021), "Craft imaginaries"; Chiles, Crawford & Elias (2021), "Mind, body, and soul: A spiritual perspective on the entrepreneurial imagination"; Gümüsay & Reinecke (2024), "Imagining desirable futures: A call for prospective theorizing with speculative rigour"; Hernes & Schultz (2020), "Translating the distant into the present"; Jørgensen & Beyes (2023), "Organizing half-things"; Kornberger & Mantere (2020), "Thought experiments and philosophy in organizational research"; Whyte, Comi & Mosca (2022), "Making futures that matter". Acknowledgments thank Joep Cornelissen and Markus Höllerer (p. 26).
- Move: genealogy and taxonomy (conceptual clarification), plus new concepts (palimpsestic and transmodal image, inter-subjunctive imagination) and a processual, new materialist redirection.

## Key constructs
| Term | Definition (verbatim, with locator) | Coined or borrowed |
|---|---|---|
| Imaginaries | "collectively held meaning-making assemblages of representations" (p. 2, "Introduction", citing Salazar, 2020) | Borrowed |
| Imago-concepts | "a genealogy of imago-concepts, in the Foucauldian sense of a critical history of the present" (p. 3, "Introduction"); the term covers image, imagination and imaginaries | Coined (umbrella label) |
| Imagination (the authors' sense) | "a situated engagement with the non-present" (p. 1, Abstract); also "a situated capacity to enter distal spheres of thought and return" (p. 4, "Introduction") | Extension of Schütz (1945) and Zittoun & Gillespie (2016) |
| Palimpsest and transmodality | "the notions of palimpsest and transmodality to better capture how images accumulate meaning over time, shifting between experiential, symbolic and material modes" (p. 4, "Introduction") | Borrowed terms (Foucault, Deleuze & Guattari, Murphy), applied to images |
| Taylorian vs Castoriadian imaginaries | "(i) inherited imaginaries that provide continuity and (ii) instituting imaginaries that generate novelty" (p. 23, "Imaginaries") | Distinction drawn by the authors from Taylor and Castoriadis |
| Inter-subjunctive collective imagination | "moments when imagining otherwise is done jointly and in situ, as when a group collectively entertains a provisional elsewhere" (p. 4, "Introduction") | Coined (from the subjunctive mood, Murphy, 2005) |
| Intra-subjunctive | "the intra-subjunctive traces how the sensing of the virtual (i.e. the potential that cannot yet be clearly thought but that guides action) as an encounter or event entangles participants through relational dynamics and produces the subjects needed for actualisation to happen" (p. 25, "Moving Forward") | Coined, following Barad's inter-/intra-action |

## Open questions
### OQ-porsfelt2026-01
- Quote: "As such, it remains open to question whether this concept genuinely enriches our theoretical vocabulary to the extent implied by its popularity and cross-disciplinary flexibility." (p. 2, "Introduction")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: The analytical value of "imaginaries" in organisation studies. This is the paper's framing question; the genealogy and inventory are offered as the means of assessing it.
- Addressee: organisation scholars using "imaginaries"
- Level of analysis: field
- Paraphrase: Whether the concept of imaginaries earns its popularity is left as the open question that motivates the paper. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-02
- Quote: "if organisations rely more frequently and to a higher degree on other forms of imagination in their operations than they do on imagination as a source of creativity, should not this greater ‘mass’ of imaginative practices warrant all the greater interest?" (p. 18, "Imagination")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: Non-creative, "mundane" imagination in organisations. The question is rhetorical and prepares the paper's broader definition of imagination.
- Addressee: organisation scholars
- Level of analysis: organization
- Paraphrase: The authors ask whether ordinary, non-creative imagination deserves more research attention than creative imagination. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-03
- Quote: "What does it mean in practice to function as an ‘unarticulated backdrop’?" (p. 20, "Imaginaries")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: How imaginaries work as tacit cultural structures, taken from Augustine et al.'s (2019) definition. The paper then answers in part through the declarative/non-declarative distinction (p. 20-21).
- Addressee: scholars of imaginaries
- Level of analysis: field
- Paraphrase: The mechanism by which an imaginary acts as an unarticulated backdrop is asked and only partly specified. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-04
- Quote: "opens up the concept to a wider set of empirical and theoretical questions." (p. 24, "Moving Forward")
- Form: future_research_call
- Scope: Imagination as "a general capacity to decouple from the present rather than a synonym for creativity". The text gives an example of daydreaming strategies in relation to boredom and managerial "technologies of attention" (p. 24).
- Addressee: empirical and theoretical researchers in organisation studies
- Level of analysis: individual
- Paraphrase: Treating imagination as a general capacity opens many empirical and theoretical questions, such as daydreaming and managerial attention. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-05
- Quote: "it becomes important to extract it from its current conflation with creative outcomes and to examine and explore it as distinct in its own right without being defined by productive ends." (p. 24, "Moving Forward")
- Form: future_research_call
- Scope: Imagination as a phenomenon "sui generis in organisations", separated from creativity.
- Addressee: organisation theorists
- Level of analysis: individual
- Paraphrase: Researchers should study imagination in its own right and stop defining it by its creative output. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-06
- Quote: "Less examined are those moments when imagination, in the broader phenomenological sense developed in the inventory, is enacted and coordinated in situ as a shared and situated practice of temporarily stepping outside the here-and-now." (p. 24, "Moving Forward")
- Form: future_research_call
- Scope: Collective imagination that is ephemeral and situated, as against durable imaginaries; the authors propose "inter-subjunctive" for it. The brainstorming session is the "emblematic organisational example" (p. 24).
- Addressee: organisation scholars of collective imagination and meetings
- Level of analysis: interaction
- Paraphrase: Short-lived, in-the-moment collective imagining is called under-examined in comparison with imaginaries. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-07
- Quote: "For organisation studies, this raises questions about how such moments are triggered, how they draw on and reconfigure existing images and cultural resources, and how they can be consequential without forming durable meaning structures." (p. 24, "Moving Forward")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: Inter-subjunctive moments: triggers, use of existing images and cultural resources, and consequences without durable structures.
- Addressee: empirical researchers
- Level of analysis: interaction
- Paraphrase: Three open questions on shared imaginative departures: what triggers them, what they draw on, and how they matter without becoming structures. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-08
- Quote: "the processual stream traced in our inventory of ‘imagination’ has already raised the question of whether the imaginative encounter is best understood as something that happens between pre-formed subjects at all." (p. 25, "Moving Forward")
- Form: explicit_question
- Scope: Whether imagination is a relational event that co-constitutes subjects (the "intra-subjunctive"), in processual and new materialist terms, including "the images and scenarios generated by AI systems" as participants in the encounter (p. 25).
- Addressee: processual and new materialist organisation scholars
- Level of analysis: interaction
- Paraphrase: The paper leaves open whether imagination is an exchange between ready-made subjects or an event that produces its subjects. (reader's paraphrase)

### OQ-porsfelt2026-09
- Quote: "These promising avenues for future inquiry include exploration of (i) the layered pragmatics of images as they travel and acquire meaning, (ii) everyday forms of imaginative detachment and return, and (iii) enacted practices of collective imagination" (p. 26, "Moving Forward")
- Form: future_research_call
- Scope: Three agendas that match the three interventions. The Image section states the gap for (i): "a key dynamic overlooked in our field relates to the pragmatic ways in which images move between and transform across these positions" (p. 14, "Image"). The text adds that these "suggest new empirical agendas for organisation studies" (p. 26).
- Addressee: organisation scholars, empirical researchers
- Level of analysis: multi-level
- Paraphrase: The closing list names three research directions: how images travel and gain meaning, everyday imaginative detachment and return, and enacted collective imagination. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
1. (reader's inference) The Image section defers a topic: "conclusion for a fuller reflection on a potential future analytics of this kind of dispersed nonhuman imagination" (p. 14, "Image"). The conclusion mentions AI systems only in one clause (p. 25), so a fuller analytics of nonhuman imagination, including AI-generated images, is left open.
2. (reader's inference) "the approach rests on ontological claims that render it less easily commensurable with typical sociological and cultural approaches to organisations" (p. 23, "Imaginaries"). How the Castoriadian lineage can be combined with mainstream cultural analysis is left open.
3. (reader's inference) The paper presents inter-subjunctive and intra-subjunctive as two orientations ("this further orientation could be described as the intra-subjunctive", p. 25) and does not say how to choose between them or test them against each other.
