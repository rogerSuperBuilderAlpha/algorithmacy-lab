# Wedlin & Weinryb (2025). Pathways to Institutional Disruption? Translation, hope, and the dynamics of hype.

## What it argues
The authors treat hype as a process of translation that can alter or disrupt institutional orders. Hype ties materialized ideas to a master-idea of progress, to hope (goals, pathway thinking, agency thinking) and to expectations. They set out a three-phase model: materialization (the energizing phase), mobilization of institutional support (the exploratory phase), and immunization (the integrative phase), in which a new pathway escapes the accountability and evaluative mechanisms of the field it enters. They name three reasons for immunity: shifting rationalities, images of a distant future, and dispersed responsibility. Hype need not end when one pathway is institutionalized; attention can move to another pathway under the same hope. AI examples recur as illustrations. (reader's paraphrase)

## Implicit openings
- (reader's inference) The model's third phase turns on "a lack of competence to evaluate the newly rationalized pathway" (p. 16, "The integrative phase: Immunization"); who holds or lacks that competence, and how it could be built, is not examined.
- (reader's inference) The "institutional distance" between fields is said to "determine the extent to which the control mechanisms and the rationalized arguments used in one field will be of relevance in another" (p. 15), but how to measure that distance for hype is left open.
- (reader's inference) Different pathways under one hope (AI in health care, schools, public sector, p. 13) are used as illustrations; the paper offers no comparison of pathways that gain immunity with ones that do not.
