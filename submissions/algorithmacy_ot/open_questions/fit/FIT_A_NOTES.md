## Coordination OQs

The coordination fit is mixed. Algorithmacy claims to restore Okhuysen and Bechky's integrating
conditions (accountability, predictability, shared understanding). Two coordination articles share
that frame:

- **Hinds & von Krogh (2024)**: the strongest coordination fit (one 3 on interdependence and several 2s on encapsulated coordination, digital twins, mental models and brokerage). The fit holds only if a GenAI coordinator counts as an intermediary that "evaluates and binds" both parties. The brief's GenAI extension covers evaluation of professional work, not coordination, so every Hinds score carries that scope caveat.
- **Räcker, Geiger & Seidl (2024)**: they use the same three integrating conditions as outcomes of spatial production. This makes them a natural citation partner, but their questions are about physical space, and they leave technology-mediated collaboration aside (racker2024-05, -10 at 2; the rest at 1).
- **Friesl et al. (2023)** (self-selection as a coordinating force), **du Gay & Lopdrup Hjorth (2024)** (formal coordination of offices) and **Winkler & Schoeneborn (2025)** (coordinated exploitation in post-truth communication) are flagged for coordination but have no intermediary. They score 0.

In short, algorithmacy answers coordination questions only where an algorithm stands between the
coordinating actors. In this corpus that means Hinds and, through accountability and the
communication field, Stark. Elsewhere it adds a mechanism to the repertoire but answers no stated
question.
