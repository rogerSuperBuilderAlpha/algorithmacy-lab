## Coordination OQs

Algorithmacy fares moderately against the coordination OQs, and only where an intermediary stands
between the coordinating people.

- **Hinds (GenAI and team coordination).** These are the best coordination fits (hinds2024-03, -04,
  -05, -07, -08, -09). The three parts map onto Okhuysen and Bechky's integrating conditions, which
  is what these OQs put at risk. The brief's intermediary evaluates and binds, while Hinds' GenAI
  coordinates and guides, so every answer above 2 needs a scope change.
- **Räcker et al. (spatial coordination).** These OQs share the Okhuysen and Bechky frame
  (accountability, predictability, common understanding), but they concern physical space.
  Algorithmacy only adds the algorithmic intermediary as a further mechanism (racker2024-10 = 2);
  the rest score 0 or 1.
- **Whyte et al. (online/offline meetings).** The video call is a channel, which is the brief's
  "moderated dyad", not a triad. These score 0 or 1.
- **Overall.** Algorithmacy has no general theory of coordinating mechanisms. Its contribution is
  narrow: it describes what a participant must do to restore the integrating conditions when the
  mechanism is an opaque, binding, adaptive system. It answers coordination OQs only when the asker
  already places an algorithm or AI between the people.
