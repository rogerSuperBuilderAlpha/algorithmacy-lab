# Model papers — how five *Organization Theory* theory articles are built

Compiled 2026-10-07 from the version-of-record PDFs of four articles, each read in full that day, plus
the Räcker, Geiger & Seidl (2024) analysis already in
[`../../lima_pdw/manuscript/MODEL_PAPER_COMPARISON.md`](../../lima_pdw/manuscript/MODEL_PAPER_COMPARISON.md).
Headings are verbatim. Word counts are approximate, from the extracted text, body only. All five carry
the journal's "Theory Article" label.

## What the five have in common

1. **The construct's workings take the largest share of the body** — a third in Schoeneborn et al., about
   three-fifths in Stark and Vanden Broeck. The literature each paper argues against gets 12–20 per cent.
   The submitted Lima paper ran the other way: 5,900 words on extant constructs, about 800 on the three
   operations.
2. **None states propositions.** OT's guidelines allow "proposition development, process theorizing, or
   … a theoretical critique and synthesis"; these five all theorize in prose.
3. **Each carries its mechanism on a vehicle** — worked cases from the authors' or others' published
   studies, or an explicit thought experiment — and never on new data.
4. **Each condenses the mechanism into one figure or one table.** Orlikowski and Scott have a process
   model; Schoeneborn et al. a sliding bar; Stark and Vanden Broeck a comparison table; Porsfelt et al.
   a diagram and an inventory table. Hinds and von Krogh have neither, and theirs is the shortest.
5. **The headings name the paper's own content**, not generic functions. Only two of five have a
   section called "Discussion"; only two have "Conclusion" in a heading.

## The six-phase table from the AI session, checked

The table in `process/2026-10-07_ai_session_gdoc.md` attributes a heading to each paper for each of six
phases. Of the twenty-four cells, **three name a heading the paper has** ("Introduction" aside):
Schoeneborn's "Discussion", and near-matches for Stark's "Principles of Algorithmic Management" and
Orlikowski and Scott's "Sociomaterial Approach". The rest are invented:

| Paper | Headings the table gives that the paper does not have |
| --- | --- |
| Orlikowski & Scott | "The Limits of Current Frameworks"; "Mechanisms of Institutional Displacement"; "Implications for Theory" (it is "Theoretical and Methodological Implications") |
| Porsfelt et al. | "A New Materialist Account"; "Situated Engagement"; "Discussion"; "Conclusion" |
| Schoeneborn et al. | "The Entity vs. Process Divide"; "The Concept of Organizationality"; "Attributing Collective Actorhood" |
| Stark & Vanden Broeck | "Beyond Scientific Management"; "The Platform as a New Form"; "Theoretical Implications"; "Conclusion" |

At least one cell summary is wrong on substance: Orlikowski and Scott's undertow does not replace
rules "with algorithmic authority" — their book-publishing case involves no algorithm. I did not check
the other cell summaries line by line.

**What survives.** The broad order holds for three of the four: position against the literature, state
the construct, work the mechanism at length, draw implications. The advice that the mechanism is the
core is right and is the most useful thing in the table. Two parts of it do not hold: the claim that
all four "move through the exact same six theoretical phases", and the implied even weighting. The
proposed section plan is sound as a starting order; its headings should be the author's own.

## What this means for the algorithmacy paper

- **Give the mechanics the largest share**, and give them a vehicle. The Lima paper has one ready: the
  pull request, the test suite and the peer reviewer, which already appears in each of the three
  operations' definitions. Hinds and von Krogh's single sustained illustration is the pattern.
- **Build one figure or one table that is the argument.** The Lima paper's Figure 1 placeholder
  ("the diagram did not survive the document merge") is the obvious candidate: properties → what each
  withholds → the operation it demands → how the three loop.
- **A paired or dimensional structure helps.** Stark and Vanden Broeck hold one comparison across five
  dimensions; oracy, literacy and algorithmacy across a fixed set of dimensions would do the same work
  and would put the introduction's sequence to use.
- **Keep the literature critique to a fifth or less.** Section 2 as drafted is 490 words; it can grow
  to take the three missing rivals without passing that share.
