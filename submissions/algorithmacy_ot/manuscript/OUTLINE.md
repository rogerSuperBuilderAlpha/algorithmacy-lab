## What changes from the Lima paper, in one paragraph

The Lima paper heard eight rival constructs one at a time and then stated its own in a quarter of the
space. The OT version inverts that. The rivals are grouped under one charge — each reduces the
arrangement to two parties — and the space saved goes to the three operations, which get a worked
illustration, a figure, and an account of how they depend on one another. The empirical design and
the three appendices leave the paper. The entry point moves from communication competence to
organization theory's own debate about algorithmic management and generative AI.

## 1. Introduction — 800 words

**Job.** Name the problem in organization theory's terms, state the construct in one sentence, say
which approach to theory the paper takes, and give the itinerary once.

**Has.** The debate (¶1), the books-without-literacy image (¶3), the oracy–literacy–algorithmacy
sequence and the three operations (¶4).

**Still needs.**
- A sentence signalling the approach — the guidelines require a submission to "explicitly signal its
  theoretical aims and position". The five models all theorize in prose without propositions.
- A contribution statement "in relation to the existing literature".
- One itinerary sentence.
- A ruling on ¶2, the parenthetical about refactoring the *why*. If it stays, it is the paper's
  largest promise and section 5 must keep it.

**Open before drafting.** Flags A1, B1–B6, and the overlap with section 2 (flag E).

## 2. The Triadic Blind Spot — 1,700 words

**Job.** Show that the accounts a reader would reach for each see two of the three parties, and say
exactly what is left over.

**Has.** Four paragraphs: OT's macro picture; the dyadic reduction in two families (machine as
partner — HMC, AI literacy; machine as channel or delegate — CMC, AI-mediated communication); Simmel's
triad; the turn to sensibility.

**Still needs.**
- **A third family: the worker facing the system.** Algorithmic competency (Zhou et al., 2025, *APJHR*),
  reactivity (Rahman, 2021) and gig literacies (Sutherland et al., 2020) are the nearest rivals, and
  section 2 does not mention them. Each sees the worker and the algorithm and loses the counterpart.
  Never cite "Zhou et al. 2025" bare — there are two (`../../lima_pdw/literature/TRAPS.md`).
- **The accounts that already have the triad.** Curchod et al. (2020) and Cameron (2024) drew it in
  *ASQ*, from the system's side. The claim has to be the narrowed one the Lima hearings left standing
  (flag A5).
- Citations for every construct named (flag C4).
- A corrected reading of Stelmaszak et al. (flag A6). They include the riders and reject
  "counterpart"; what they leave untheorized is the tie between the humans, and the capacity of the
  driver who "learn[s] how to change" — the narrower charge, and a better one.

**Packet.** The seven hearings and `FINDINGS.md` in `../../lima_pdw/literature/steelmans/`; Table 1 of
the Lima paper (`CARRYOVER.md`), which already sorts eight constructs by where each puts the human
counterpart, and could become this section's one table.

## 3. Algorithmacy and Coordinative Co-optation — 1,600 words

**Job.** Define the arrangement and the construct, and draw the construct's boundary.

**Must settle.**
1. **The arrangement.** Three properties of the intermediary — opacity, adaptivity, bindingness — and the
   scope conditions. The compound "coordinative co-optation" is introduced here as the author's term,
   built on Stark and Vanden Broeck's "on platforms they are co-opted" (flag A2).
2. **The genus.** Sensibility, or competency? The introduction says algorithmacy is a sensibility that
   "operates through the competencies and capacities to…"; the Lima paper has a subsection titled
   "Competency, Not Skill or Capability". One sentence has to relate the two, and Suddaby's (2010)
   construct-clarity criteria, which the Lima paper already applied, test whichever is chosen.
3. **What "irreducible" means.** The introduction calls the triad irreducible. That word carries the
   dissertation's larger claim and a reviewer will ask what it rules out.
4. **Whether the platform is a collective actor.** Schoeneborn et al. can support a platform holding
   actorhood by degree, and note that platforms work to lower it (flag A1). That may be a better
   point than the one the draft makes: the third party in this triad disclaims being a party.

**Packet.** Carry-over: the Lima construct head, the competency subsection, and Appendix A's
comparison of coordination forms. Cards: `starkvandenbroeck2024`, `starkpais2020`, `schoeneborn2026`,
`simmel1902`, `simmel1950`, `suddaby2010`. Research line 4 in [`../RESEARCH_PLAN.md`](../RESEARCH_PLAN.md).

## 4. The Mechanics of Algorithmacy — 2,900 words

**Job.** Show how the three operations work, why each is demanded by one property of the
intermediary, and why none works without the others. This is the section the workshop said was
missing.

**Shape, from the models.**
- **One vehicle carried through.** The Lima paper's definitions already use one: the pull request, the
  test suite and the peer reviewer. Hinds and von Krogh do the same with team coordination, as "an
  illustration as well as a thought experiment".
- **One subsection per operation**, each answering the same questions in the same order — what the
  property withholds, what the person does, what it looks like in the vehicle, how it fails.
- **A subsection on the loop.** The introduction and the Lima paper both say the operations are
  recursive. This is where the theory is: what goes wrong when one runs without the others.
- **One figure.** Property → deficit → operation → loop. The Lima paper's Figure 1 was lost in a
  document merge and has to be redrawn.

**Must settle.**
- What the first operation interprets: the counterpart's intentions (introduction ¶4), or the
  intermediary's rule and the counterpart's intention from one composite signal (Lima paper)? (flag E)
- Whether a contrast table — oracy, literacy, algorithmacy across fixed dimensions — belongs here or
  in section 3. Stark and Vanden Broeck's Table 1 is the pattern.
- How much hedging. Hinds and von Krogh hedge every mechanism; Stark and Vanden Broeck assert.

**Packet.** Carry-over: "Constitutive Operations: The Tripartite Model" — the lead-in sentence, the
three definitions, the "requires both at once" argument and the loop. Cards: `porsfelt2026` for the
situated engagement with the non-present (flag B4), `hindsvonkrogh2024` for the manager–AI–employee
cascades. Research lines 1 and 2.

## 5. Discussion — 1,500 words

**Job.** Say what changes for organization theory if the construct holds.

**Candidates, each needing the author's choice.**
- **For algorithmic-management research.** Stark and Vanden Broeck describe the form from the
  operator's side; the construct supplies the co-opted party's side.
- **For the relational view of AI.** Stelmaszak et al. and Hinds and von Krogh put capability in
  relations; the construct asks which relations, and who cannot see them.
- **The paradox.** The Lima paper's "A Paradox the Construct Raises Rather Than Resolves" carries over.
- **Refactoring the *why*.** Whatever introduction ¶2 promises is paid here.
- **Future research.** The empirical design reduces to a paragraph, anonymized.

**Packet.** Carry-over: Theoretical Implications, the paradox, Acquisition and Distribution, Semantic
Relations. Research line 5.

## 6. Conclusion — 400 words

**Job.** Restate the construct and what it lets a researcher see. Two of the five models end on a
section named for its content ("Out of Bounds", "Moving Forward").
