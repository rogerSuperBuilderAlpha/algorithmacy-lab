⟦How⟧ organizations adopt and understand the new platforms and services enabled by generative AI ⟦has attracted growing interest among organizational scholars in recent years⟧. While global leaders promote these technologies, quieter displacements of institutional values, norms, and rules are what actually reconfigure how organizations perform (Orlikowski & Scott, 2023). Focusing on those quieter shifts allows us to recognize how generative AI sits inside the work and reshapes it, functioning more as—but not quite—a "counterpart" in a system ⟦of work that includes its design,⟧ implementation, and use (Anthony et al., 2023). Hoping to install a plug-and-play intelligence ignores that organizing now depends on relations among humans and algorithmic black boxes (Stelmaszak et al., 2026), which ⟦may⟧ reduce visibility and control (Hinds & von Krogh, 2024) while paradoxically manufacturing a false sense of epistemological security (Leonardi & Leavell, 2026).

While the academic debate has clearly moved past the surface of "tooling," it still leaves open how someone coordinates through a cooptive, opaque, adaptive intermediary. Building on the OT/OS discussion and exploring other popular models including Human-Machine Communication and AI Literacy, I argue that we are trying to preserve a "literacy"-based understanding. We do this by either setting aside the third actor, treating the mediator as a passive channel, or relying on a dyadic ontology that is blind to the emergent, binding power of the triad.

⟦This leaves⟧ organizations doing the equivalent of giving books to people who lack literacy. We heap algorithmic management systems on people who lack algorithmacy, or the sensibility to navigate opaque triadic coordinative cooptation. As traditional firm boundaries dissolve, these algorithmic intermediaries co-opt (Stark & Vanden Broeck, 2024), i.e., evaluate and bind ⟦, while downplaying their own collective actorhood⟧ (Schoeneborn et al., 2026). ⟦In this paper, I therefore aim to specify how someone coordinates through such an intermediary;⟧ the how I propose also requires that we refactor the why. ⟦To this end, I make two proposals.⟧

⟦First, I propose to distinguish three sensibilities by the form each one navigates.⟧ Oracy is the sensibility to navigate direct dyadic forms through speaking and hearing (or feeling and tapping). Literacy is the sensibility necessary for the competency to read and write texts that moderate chained or linked dyads. Algorithmacy is the sensibility to navigate irreducible triads.

⟦Second, I propose that,⟧ as a sensibility like literacy and oracy, ⟦algorithmacy⟧ operates through the competencies and capacities to: 1) interpret a variable counterpart's intentions from an otherwise innocuous output; 2) specify intent so meaning survives the opaque, adaptive mediator; and 3) keep track as governance radically, repeatedly, and suddenly shifts. Drawing on recent views of imagination as a relational capacity enacted in the encounter between bodies, materials, and circumstances (Porsfelt et al., 2026), algorithmacy can be seen as a situated engagement with the unseen.

⟦In sum, this paper sets out an account of algorithmacy as the sensibility that triadic coordination requires.⟧ We cannot read our way out of or into this, just as we cannot—or should not—listen to a book.

## Facts

Changed in the text:

- **Schoeneborn et al. (2026)** attribute collective actorhood to collectives and say platforms "rather
  strategically downplay their collective actorhood status to avoid accountability" (p. 13). The author's
  sentence had the intermediaries "operat[ing] with gradual organizationality… with collective actorhood",
  which is the reverse.
- **Anthony et al. (2023)** write "counterpart in a system of work that includes its design,
  implementation, and use" (abstract). The author's text read "in a system, implementation, and use".
- **Hinds & von Krogh (2024)** hedge: the system "may ultimately obscure" visibility (p. 4). "may" added.
- "Stark, 2024" → Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024); "Stelmaszak et al., 2025" → 2026 (author's earlier
  ruling); "though" → "through".

- **"coordinative cooptation"** is the author's coinage. Stark & Vanden Broeck say "co-opted" and never
  use the compound. As placed, the sentence does not attribute it to them.
