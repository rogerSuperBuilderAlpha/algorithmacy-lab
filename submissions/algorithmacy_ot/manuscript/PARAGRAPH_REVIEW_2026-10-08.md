# Paragraph review — the plain draft against four *Organization Theory* articles

8 October 2026. The draft reviewed is [`DRAFT_2026-10-07_plain.md`](DRAFT_2026-10-07_plain.md). The four
models are Orlikowski & Scott (2023), Hinds & von Krogh (2024), Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024) and
Schoeneborn, Dobusch & Seidl (2026). Each was read in full and mapped paragraph by paragraph; the maps
are in `process/model_paragraphs_*.md`. Porsfelt et al. (2026) is left out because it is a genealogy, not
a construct-building article.

"Does the job" below means: a paragraph in the same position in at least one model does the same thing
for its argument.

- **Total length is right.** 7,000 words sits inside the 6,600–9,100 range. Our outline budget of 8,900
  was set from the journal's ceiling; three of the four models are well under it.
- **Paragraphs were too short and too even.** Three models run 135–190 words; ours ran 97. Only Stark
  and Vanden Broeck write paragraphs our length, and they do it on purpose: 23 of their 89 paragraphs
  are one or two sentences, placed at a turn in the argument, and the rest are longer.
- **Sentences are half the venue's length.** 14.5 words against 25–29. In Orlikowski and Scott, 4 of 237
  sentences have twelve words or fewer. This is the largest gap, and it comes from the instruction the
  draft was written to. See section 4.
- **Citation spread is right.** Half our paragraphs carry no citation. The models run from a third
  (Hinds & von Krogh, Stark & Vanden Broeck) to a half (Orlikowski & Scott). All four keep citations out
  of the paragraphs that state their own claims, and ours does too.

## 4. What this adds up to

**Fifty-two of 64 paragraphs do the job a model paragraph does.** The structure matches. The gaps are few and
specific.

### Fixed today

- Eight paragraph breaks removed where two or three short paragraphs shared one job (the three groups
  of existing accounts, the skill-and-capability paragraph, the cycle, the first Discussion paragraph,
  the stray "Three propositions follow."). No wording changed. Paragraphs went from 72 to 64 and the
  mean from 97 to 109 words.
- One sentence added to "Interpreting" ("It is needed because the intermediary is opaque.") so the three
  subsections open in the same order.

### Needs writing, in order of weight

1. **The paragraph on "sensibility" (27) needs sources.** It is the construct's central term and it
   cites no one. Research line 4.
2. **The workplace cases need their own paragraphs (10), and "Specifying intent" needs one (after 42).**
   In the models the real cases carry the paper. Ours is carried by an invented classroom. Giving the
   driver and the freelancer a paragraph each in "The situation", and returning to both in each of the
   three subsections, would also add most of the words "How it works" is short of.
3. **Turn "Limits and open questions" (59–61) into research questions.** Same content, the venue's frame.
4. **Decide on the propositions (56–58).** Cut them, or give each a paragraph.
5. **Say the thesis in paragraph 2**, and add a one-sentence statement of what the paper finds beside the
   roadmap (5).
6. **Open the Discussion by restating the framework** in three or four sentences.
7. **Split the two contributions in paragraph 50** and announce them as two.

### A decision for the author: sentence length

The draft was written to the instruction to explain plainly and not sound clever, and its sentences
average 14.5 words. The four models average 25–29, and their paragraphs open on sentences of 23–30 words
that pick up the previous paragraph's point before adding the new one. Ours open on sentences of 13.

These are not the same thing as jargon. A sentence can be long and plain: "The student has the result,
the comments and the votes, and what she lacks is a way to decide among several explanations that all
fit." But a reviewer at this journal will notice a paper written in sentences half the usual length.
Two ways to go:

- **Keep it as it is.** Clear, and visibly unlike the venue.
- **Join, do not rewrite.** Where a short sentence only explains the one before it, make them one
  sentence with "because", "so" or "which". Open each paragraph by naming what the last one ended on.
  The words stay ordinary and the average rises toward 20.

I have not done this. It changes every paragraph, and it is the author's call.

### Terms to hold fixed

The models repeat their terms and never vary them. Ours mostly does. Three sets drift:

- "ability", "capacity", "sensibility", "competence" for algorithmacy. Pick one for general use and
  keep "sensibility" for the genus paragraph.
- "the system", "the program", "the gate", "the algorithm", "the rule", "the intermediary". "Intermediary"
  is the defined term; "the gate" is its name in the example. The others should go.
- "the other person", "the counterpart", "her reviewer". "Counterpart" is the defined term.
