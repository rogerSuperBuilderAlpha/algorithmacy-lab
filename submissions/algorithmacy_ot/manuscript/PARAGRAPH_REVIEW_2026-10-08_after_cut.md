## Where this leaves the draft

**Sixty-one of 67 paragraphs do the job a model paragraph does.** The six that do not, or only partly, are
paragraph 2, the sensibility paragraph (31), the two-contribution paragraph (55), and the three
propositions (61–63). One paragraph is missing: the organizational case for "Specifying intent".

Three of these were on the earlier list and are fixed: the limits section is now research questions, the
Discussion now opens by restating the argument, and the workplace cases now come from published
organizational studies.

What is left, in order of weight:

1. **Read the sources for the argument section** (Ong, Goody and Watt, Havelock; Wilkinson, Yates and Olson
   in full). The section is built the way Stark and Vanden Broeck build theirs, and theirs is sourced.
2. **Find sources for "sensibility"** (paragraph 31).
3. **Give "Specifying intent" an organizational case.** About 100 words, and they go to the section that
   most needs them.
4. **Decide on the propositions.**
5. **State the thesis in paragraph 2.** After today the thesis has a plain form the paragraph could carry:
   speech connects people in dyads, a text moderates a dyad, and an algorithm that judges makes a triad.
6. **Decide on sentence length.**
