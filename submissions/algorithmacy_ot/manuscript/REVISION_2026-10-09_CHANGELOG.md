## Decisions I made that are the author's to make

These change the theory, not the wording. Each can be reversed.

1. **The construct is defined by features, not by technology.** A blind review with hidden scoring is
   admitted as a case, and the paper says so. The alternative was to restrict the definition to software
   and argue why a court differs in kind. I took the first because the text could support it from sources
   already verified. It costs the claim that algorithmacy is wholly new; it keeps the claim that
   algorithmic systems made it ordinary.
2. **Four features, not three**, with one defining feature above them (evaluates and binds both) and one
   fact behind them (authority not conferred, operator declines the role). The earlier "opaque, binding,
   adaptive" triplet is gone. "Hidden, unanswerable, changing, and fed by the two people's dealings"
   replaces it.
3. **The lopsided triad.** The cases showed that an intermediary binding both parties equally is rare in
   the published record. The paper now says the usual case binds one side, and that the ability is
   demanded of that side. This is a real narrowing of "binds them both".
4. **The parts map to Okhuysen and Bechky's conditions as** interpreting → accountability, specifying
   intent → common understanding, keeping track → predictability, each called a partial recovery.
5. **The intermediary is "the rule as applied, with its operator behind it."** This answers "is the party
   the algorithm or the firm?" and concedes that an employer's own system is a different case.
6. **The oracy → literacy → algorithmacy sequence is stated as a sequence of what organizations relied
   on**, with the sentence that the inference from Yates is the paper's own and that the words were not
   coined in that order.
7. **New title.** "Toward an Account of…" echoed the model's "Toward a Theory of…".
