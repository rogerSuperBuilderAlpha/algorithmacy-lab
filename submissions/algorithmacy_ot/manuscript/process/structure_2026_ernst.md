- **Roadmap paragraph:** yes, P7, last paragraph of the introduction.
- **Contribution statement:** yes, P6, opening "Our contribution to…"; one sentence, 63 words. It comes after the core claim and before the roadmap.
- **Statement of the approach to theorizing:** partial. No paragraph explains how the authors theorize. P4 says they take Bourdieu's career as an exemplar, and P7 says the account rests on a reading of Bourdieu's work and of collaborators' accounts. That is all.
- **Central term first defined:** the introduction names "epistemic reflexivity" in P2 (p. 2) and never defines it in a sentence. P5 defines it operationally through the three principles. The first definitional sentence comes in P17 (p. 5), which says what epistemic reflexivity concerns for Bourdieu; P37 (p. 10) says why this reflexivity counts as epistemic. The paper defines illusio in the introduction (end of P3) and "reflexive researcher habitus" not until P15 (p. 5).
- **Order of moves:** field-level motivation and gap → concept and what the paper does → theory background (two paragraphs) → core claim → contribution → roadmap.

## 5. How the core argument is built

The argument runs in four steps, one per level-1 section after the introduction.

1. **Literature to gap** ("Research Reflexivity in the OS field", 1,979 words, 21.4%). The authors sort the OS reflexivity literature into three foci (self-reflexivity; foregrounding participants; researchers' relations with the whole object and with scholastic bias), single out the one prior paper that already invokes Bourdieu's epistemic reflexivity, and fault it for not saying how to practise it. The single subsection then turns to Bourdieu and restates the three principles, each paired with the bias it answers and a note on how far the literature covers it.
2. **Exposition through biography** ("The Core Tenets…", 2,515 words, 27.2% — the longest section). Four subsections derive Bourdieu's concepts from episodes in his career: relational thinking and fields; the academic field; habitus; epistemological rupture. The last subsection maps the three principles onto Bourdieu's vocabulary.
3. **Application** ("Epistemic Reflexivity and the Construction of the Organizational Object", 1,498 words, 16.2%). This section carries the paper's title, and it is shorter than the exposition before it.
4. **Discussion and Conclusions** (2,264 words, 24.5%).

**How the main section is organized.** Two subsections, and they do not map onto the three principles.
- "Thinking reflexively and relationally about the object under construction" (6 paragraphs, 829 words) stays with Bourdieu: the photography work, the field concept as a construction device (with a block quotation), the relational principle, the housing-market study, attention to history and crisis.
- "Object construction challenges for OS researchers" (6 paragraphs, 669 words) turns to OS: a one-sentence orienting paragraph, principle one explained, the sector/industry illustration, principles two and three in one paragraph, two published OS examples, and a 29-word bridge to the Discussion.

The section has no repeated internal order and no subsection per element. The authors weave the three principles through continuous prose and mark them with parenthetical tags. No table or figure summarizes the framework anywhere in the article.

## 6. How the paper ends

**Discussion** (level 1, p. 13; 11 paragraphs, 1,598 words, 17.3%), no lead-in paragraph, two subsections:
- "The importance of reflexivity for generating epistemologically robust OS objects" (3 paragraphs, 509 words): ties the principles back to the OS literature, notes that a review of reflexive traditions in this journal omits Bourdieu, sketches the business-school history of the field, and uses substantialism as the worked example.
- "Theory generation in OS" (8 paragraphs, 1,089 words): what the principles do for theory development; who shapes the choice of object; reflexivity as collective; journal-genre and template constraints; the academic field's power structure; the room for heterodoxy; pressures to publish; and a closing paragraph on what reflexivity means for organization theory.

- **Limitations:** yes, but two sentences inside P65 with no heading. The limitation named is the breadth of traditions in OS: sub-fields will lean toward different reflexive approaches.
- **Future research:** no section, no paragraph, and the phrase never appears. The Discussion says twice that it offers ways forward.
- **Practical implications:** no section. The implications address research practice — researchers, editors, reviewers — and sit in the last two paragraphs.
- **Final paragraph:** opens "In conclusion,", agrees with a cited paper from this journal that reflexivity is essential to the field's future, asks a rhetorical question about divorcing inquiry from political and economic crisis, calls the question urgent given attacks on higher education, and ends on a sentence addressed to the reader in the second person about what one owes one's field. The paper closes on a civic appeal, not on a summary.

**Back matter, in order:** ORCID iD; Funding; Declaration of conflicting interests; References; Author biographies (one short paragraph per author). No acknowledgements, no notes, no appendix.

## 7. References and voice

- **References:** 94 entries (pp. 17–20), APA style, counted two ways (hanging-indent entries; year-in-parentheses pattern).
- **Voice:** first person plural throughout — 82 uses of we/our/us in the body, 8.9 per 1,000 words. "We" marks the paper's own moves ("we argue", "we suggest", "we propose", "we now discuss") and also stands for the community of researchers ("as researchers, we"). The literature and Bourdieu appear in the third person with named agents. The two instances of "I" sit inside a Bourdieu quotation.
- **Other measured habits:** 10 uses of "for example"/"for instance"; a handful of spaced en dashes used as parenthetical dashes, no em dashes; British spelling with -ize; single quotation marks.
