## 2. Section by section

### Introduction (p. 2) — 4 paragraphs, 535 words, mean 134

Each paragraph does one job and the jobs run in a fixed order: subject and gap, outcome construct, approach, roadmap. The first three open on the authors' own stance or on the received view they will qualify, and none opens on a citation; the ten citations sit at sentence ends as support. Paragraph 2 ends on the definition and paragraph 3 ends on a promise of what the approach will provide, so each close sets up the next opening. The digital undertow does not appear until the fourth sentence of the roadmap; the introduction defines the outcome (institutional displacement) and holds the titular metaphor back.

### Waves of Digitalization (pp. 2–4) — 7 paragraphs, 883 words, mean 126

- *Section opening* — 1 paragraph, 144 words. The paragraph carries nine of the section's fourteen citations. It reads the wave metaphor back to the field, uses the paper's shortest sentences to do it ("Resistance is futile"), and closes by naming the two industries.
- *Book publishing* (p. 3) — 2 paragraphs, 343 words, mean 172. Paragraph 6 runs chronology, then the latest wave, then the pivot to the indirect change, then the institutional object. Paragraph 7 gives that object's history and standing before stating the threat, and it ends on a promissory note ("as we will theorize below").
- *Hospitality* (pp. 3–4) — 4 paragraphs, 396 words, mean 99. The same sequence is spread over three shorter paragraphs: waves (8), the tension and the institutional object (9), the finding with one dated event (10). Paragraph 11 then closes both cases at once.

The two cases are told in parallel and in the same words: each has a sentence saying the wave was not aimed at the institution, and each names the institution only after the visible change is on the table. Neither case paragraph uses a theoretical term. Topic sentences are long here (26 to 44 words) because each carries a time span and a list of examples.

### The Digital Undertow (pp. 4–5) — 5 paragraphs, 601 words, mean 120

The section defines the construct in five steps: physical analogy, naming, what the literature omits, difference from the nearest concept, why it is hard to study. Paragraphs 12 and 13 are built as a pair, the first describing the ocean and the second carrying the description over to digitalization with the same verb for what waves do to an undertow. Citations concentrate in paragraph 15, the only place in the section where the authors argue with named sources, and Panayiotou et al. get a narrative citation and a direct quotation there. The last sentence of paragraph 16 is an explicit hand-off to the next section.

### Sociomaterial Research Approach (pp. 5–8) — 10 paragraphs, 1,664 words, mean 166

- *Section opening* (p. 5) — 2 paragraphs, 212 words, mean 106. Paragraph 17 sorts the literature into streams; paragraph 18 states which version the authors adopt and names the two concepts that become the subsection headings.
- *Sociomaterial entanglement* (pp. 5–6) — 3 paragraphs, 596 words, mean 199. A short paragraph on what others do, one very long paragraph that defines the concept, and a short paragraph on what follows for analysis. Paragraph 20 is the longest in the paper (392 words, 13 sentences, two block quotes); it stays one paragraph because it is one argument, quotation, objection, answer and consequence in a row.
- *Apparatus* (pp. 6–8) — 5 paragraphs, 856 words, mean 171. Three paragraphs each introduce one term (apparatus, constitutive exclusions, relational performativity), each new term arriving at the end of the paragraph before it is used as a subject in the next. Paragraph 24 opens on the last words of the quotation that ends paragraph 23 (questions of accountability), the clearest tail-head link in the paper. Two summary paragraphs close the subsection, the first ending on two questions and the second on a transition.

This section holds 29 of the paper's 69 citations and all three block quotes. Barad is always cited in the narrative, as the agent of the idea; other sources sit in parentheses. The two cases appear once in ten paragraphs, in a single sentence of paragraph 22 that points back to them as examples of institutional apparatuses. Topic sentences are the paper's shortest here (15 to 33 words; subsection means 23 and 24).

### Theorizing the Digital Undertow and Institutional Displacement (pp. 8–11) — 13 paragraphs, 1,393 words, mean 107

The section goes from cases to theory term to general model to figure. Paragraph 27 states the finding; 28 to 30 re-read the two cases; 31 states what they share; 32 and 33 rename the case objects as institutional apparatuses; 34 to 36 walk the model as condition, branch and counter-branch; 37 and 38 restate the model against Figure 1; 39 claims that other approaches cannot do this. Paragraphs are the shortest in the paper and four of them (27, 31, 32, 39) have two or three sentences; these are the hinge paragraphs between blocks. The model paragraphs open on a subordinate clause that states the condition ("When…", "In response…", "However, when…"), so the opening words locate the reader in the sequence. Only three citations appear in thirteen paragraphs and all three are to the authors' own empirical papers: the theory is stated in the authors' voice, on the vocabulary already built.

### Theoretical and Methodological Implications (pp. 11–13) — 10 paragraphs, 1,532 words, mean 153

One paragraph states the theoretical contribution; six paragraphs (41 to 46) give a first-person account of how the book study was done; three paragraphs extend outward (other sectors, newer technologies, ethics). The methodological block is narrative and chronological: what the authors heard, what they then found, how they turned the question around, what they traced, what the concept made visible. Its paragraphs open on participles or on the first person ("During our research fieldwork…", "Alerted to…", "Drawing on…", "We sought…") and each opens where the previous one stopped. Paragraph 47 tests the model on a third case by repeating the chain of paragraph 28 with new nouns. Five citations in the section, three of them in the genealogy paragraph.

### Conclusion (pp. 13–14) — 2 paragraphs, 253 words, mean 126

Two paragraphs and no citations. The first restates what was done in the order prior work, approach, result, contribution. The second states the stakes in an evaluative register the paper has not used before, then closes on the opening metaphor (waves and undertow, above and below the water line). The conclusion introduces nothing new.

## 3. Whole-paper patterns

**Paragraph length.** Mean 134.5 words, median 120, range 65 to 392. Thirty-one of the 51 paragraphs fall between 100 and 160 words, ten fall below 100 and ten above 160. Paragraphs run 2 to 13 sentences with a median of 4. The short paragraphs are hinges (synthesis, redescription, closing claim); the long ones are definitions that work through a quotation. Sentences are long and even: mean 28.9 words, median 28, and only 4 of 237 sentences have twelve words or fewer, against 32 with forty or more.

**How paragraphs open.** I sorted first sentences into four kinds. The first three are the kinds asked for; the fourth is one this paper needs.

| Opens with | Paragraphs | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| The authors' own claim or position | 24 | 47% |
| Case or research narration (a fact about the industry or the study) | 11 | 22% |
| A transition, back-reference or signpost | 9 | 18% |
| A statement about prior literature | 7 | 14% |

Only four first sentences contain a citation (paragraphs 2, 5, 17 and 20), and only one paragraph opens with a scholar as the agent (paragraph 20, on Barad). Topic sentences average 29.6 words, range 15 to 48. They are long because most carry two things: a back-reference to the prior paragraph or section and the new claim. Many open on a subordinate or participial clause that does the linking before the main clause arrives.

**Citations.** Sixty-nine citations fall in 25 paragraphs; 26 paragraphs have none. Fifty-five of the 69 sit in the first 21 paragraphs, where the paper positions itself and builds its approach. The theorizing section has three, all self-citations, and the conclusion none. Narrative citation is kept for sources the authors lean on or argue with (Barad, Cooren, Panayiotou et al., Schultze et al.); everything else trails a sentence in parentheses.

**How the introduction is built.** Four paragraphs of similar length (104 to 159 words): (1) the subject, the misdirected attention and a claim of consequence from the authors' own research; (2) the known kind of displacement conceded, then the new kind named and defined with a plain formula ("We use this term to refer to"); (3) why existing tools fail and which approach the paper adopts; (4) roadmap. There is no opening anecdote, no case detail and no literature review. The introduction is 8% of the body.

**How the two cases are introduced and then returned to.** The cases appear three times, and each appearance does a different job.

1. *Set-up, before any theory* (paragraphs 6–11). Each case is told in ordinary industry language in the order: visible waves; the latest wave; a less visible change; the long-standing institution it touches and that institution's standing; a statement that the wave was not aimed at it. One synthesis paragraph follows both.
2. *Re-read, after the vocabulary is built* (paragraphs 28–33). The same facts are retold in a fixed chain of terms: what the institution is premised on; how the core phenomenon now materializes; constitutive exclusion; lack of correspondence; lost capacity to coordinate and regulate; displacement. The book paragraph and the hospitality paragraph end on the same clause with only the nouns changed. A synthesis paragraph follows, then two paragraphs that rename the case objects in theory terms.
3. *Methodological account* (paragraphs 42–46). The book case alone returns as a story of how the authors found the problem.

The book case always comes first and hospitality second. Where both appear in one paragraph, each gets one sentence or one clause in parallel syntax (paragraphs 11 and 33). A third example, cryptocurrency, is held back for the implications section and run through the same chain in a single paragraph.

**How the constructs are defined.** The paper spreads definition over several paragraphs and gives each paragraph one move.

- *Institutional displacement* gets one defining sentence in paragraph 2 of the introduction and is not elaborated until the theorizing section, where paragraph 31 names it as the outcome and paragraph 36 states the conditions that produce it.
- *Digital undertow* takes five paragraphs (12–16): the physical image; the naming; what existing studies omit, with a supporting term coined; the difference from the nearest existing concept; why it is hard to see and why that matters. The authors give the image and a relation (the undertow exists in relation to the waves) and no one-sentence formal definition.
- *The theoretical vocabulary* (entanglement, agential cut, apparatus, institutional apparatus, constitutive exclusion, relational performativity) takes ten paragraphs. Each term is glossed at first use by an em-dash appositive or a parenthesis, anchored in a Barad quotation, and then used as a subject. The body has 25 em-dashes, about 3.6 per 1,000 words, and most of them weld a gloss to a term.

Once a term is fixed the authors repeat it and do not vary it. "Digital undertow" occurs 27 times, "institutional displacement" 16, "corollary effect(s)" 16, forms of "constitutive exclusion" 15, "correspondence" 10, "industry-structuring" 17, and forms of "materializ-" 39.

**How the theorizing section walks the process model.** The model is given three times at rising generality: through each case (28–30), in general prose with one paragraph per stage or branch (34–36), and as a restatement keyed to Figure 1 (37–38). The figure arrives after the prose has already walked the model, and the paragraphs that follow it restate the model and do not explain the figure box by box. The two branches get one paragraph each and the same construction: a conditional opening, the mechanism, the outcome. A named kind of tension marks each stage (tactical, then existential).

**How implications and conclusion are built.** The implications section spends one paragraph on theory, six on method and three on extensions. Its methodological part is a narrative of the authors' own study, in the past tense and the first person, which shows the approach at work in place of prescribing steps. The extensions each take one paragraph: another sector, newer technologies, ethics. The conclusion is two paragraphs, a summary and a statement of stakes, and it closes on the metaphor the paper opened with.

**Other habits.**

- First person is heavy and carries claims as well as moves: 129 instances of we/our/us, about 19 per 1,000 words, including the verbs of arguing, suggesting and believing. This differs from the rule in this repo's `CLAUDE.md` that first person marks authorial labor only.
- Back-pointers and forward-pointers are frequent and explicit: I counted ten phrases that point the reader back to an earlier passage or ahead to a later one.
- Paragraphs that close a section or subsection end on a hook to the next (paragraphs 5, 7, 11, 16, 26), and paragraph 25 ends on two questions. Paragraphs inside a section more often end on the consequence of the paragraph's own claim.
- Evaluative closers are rare. The nearest thing to an epigram is the last sentence of paragraph 24 ("On this view, performativity is ontological, not only empirical"), and it restates a distinction the paragraph has just drawn.

**Quotations used above** beyond the table's opening words: four, from paragraphs 2, 5, 7 and 24. The openers quoted for paragraphs 34–36 and 42–45 repeat words already in the table.
