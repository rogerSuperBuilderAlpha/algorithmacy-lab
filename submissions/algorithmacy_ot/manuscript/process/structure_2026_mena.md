**Shape.** Five headed level-1 sections after an unheaded introduction; 13 level-2 and 19 level-3 headings. Every level-1 section except the Conclusion opens with a lead-in paragraph before its first subheading (one or two paragraphs, 85–244 words) that says what the section does. Level-3 headings appear in three places only, and each set is a parallel list: the three foci, the four perspectives, and the twelve focus-by-perspective cells. The three "X focus" labels serve as level-3 run-ins on p. 6 and return as level-2 headings in the main section.

## 5. How the core argument is built

The authors build a two-dimensional typology and then fill it. Each section constructs one part and its last paragraph hands off to the next.

1. **Rows, by induction from the field** ("A Brief Overview of Democracy in MOS", 2,213 words, 21.7%). Four subsections review four literatures (336, 198, 259 and 546 words). A fifth derives three foci from them, anchored in the classic three mandates of organization theory, with one run-in subsection per focus.
2. **Columns, imported from another discipline** ("Broadening the Perspective(s)…", 1,279 words, 12.5%). Two paragraphs justify the choice of four perspectives and list those omitted; Table 1; then one run-in paragraph per perspective (150–208 words) in a fixed order of content: core emphasis, intellectual lineage, how the people are conceived, key constructs, source of legitimacy. A closing paragraph says the four are commensurable.
3. **The matrix** ("Mobilizing Perspectives on Democracy in MOS", 4,103 words, 40.3%).
4. **What the matrix shows as a whole** ("Discussion and Implications", 1,593 words, 15.6%), with Table 2.

**How the main section is organized.** Strictly, and the same way throughout.
- A two-paragraph lead-in (244 words): how far each perspective has already entered the field, then a signpost.
- Three level-2 subsections, one per focus, in the order internal, outside-in, inside-out (1,510, 1,143 and 1,206 words). None has a lead-in paragraph.
- Inside each, four level-3 run-in subsections, one per perspective, always in the order liberal, republican, deliberative, agonistic. Each heading is the perspective plus the focus ("Liberal internal focus." and so on).
- **Twelve cells, 3,859 words; mean 322 words per cell (range 268–437); two to four paragraphs each (eight cells have exactly two).**
- **Every cell follows the same internal order:** (a) a first sentence that says what this lens examines; (b) the mechanisms or structures it attends to, with one or two published studies as instances; (c) a paragraph of future research, opening "Future research…" in nine cells and reaching it by the second or third sentence in the other three, often ending in direct questions. Future-research paragraphs total 1,518 words — 39% of the cells.
- Seven of the eight outside-in and inside-out cells split evenly: one paragraph on what exists, one on what is missing.

The section contains no synthesis paragraph of its own. Synthesis waits for the Discussion and Table 2.

## 6. How the paper ends

**Discussion and Implications** (level 1, p. 17; 13 paragraphs, 1,593 words, 15.6%). A lead-in paragraph (166 words) restates the framework, calls out Table 2, lists what being explicit about one's theory of democracy buys, and announces the subsections. Four level-2 subsections follow, each one implication of the framework taken as a whole:
- "Theoretical consistency and coherence" (2 paragraphs, 307 words) — hold one perspective across foci.
- "Cross-focal dynamics and spillover effects" (2 paragraphs, 355 words) — effects run both ways between foci.
- "Comparative analysis across perspectives" (4 paragraphs, 457 words) — compare perspectives within one focus; tensions and hybrids.
- "Advancing epistemic dialogue between MOS and other disciplines" (4 paragraphs, 308 words) — what organization research can give back to political theory.

Each subsection states its point in the first sentence, concedes where the point is not new, gives one example and ends on a direction or a question for research.

**Conclusion** (level 1, p. 21; **one paragraph, 115 words, four sentences**, 1.1%). No subsections.

- **Limitations:** no section and no paragraph. Scope limits appear where the choices are made (P8: the overview is not comprehensive; P26: four perspectives are a selection, others named).
- **Future research:** no separate section. It is distributed: one paragraph or more in each of the twelve cells ("future research" appears 28 times in the body), and each Discussion subsection adds cross-cutting directions.
- **Practical implications:** none. The implications named in the section title are for research. The nearest thing is the last Discussion paragraph (P75), on organizational experiments informing institutional reform.
- **Final paragraph:** a plain recapitulation in four sentences — the objectives and the problem; the first step (three foci); the second step (four perspectives applied to each); and a closing statement of belief about what the framework will enable. It adds no new claim, question or appeal.

**Back matter, in order:** Acknowledgements (thanks the handling editors by name and a copyeditor); ORCID iDs; Ethical considerations; Informed consent; Funding; Declaration of conflicting interests; Data availability statement; References; Author biographies. No notes, no appendix.

## 7. References and voice

- **References:** 109 entries (pp. 21–25), APA style, counted two ways (hanging-indent entries; year-in-parentheses pattern).
- **Voice:** first person plural for the paper's own moves — 78 uses of we/our/us in the body, 7.7 per 1,000 words ("we propose", "we build", "we define", "we distinguish", "our framework"). The introduction, section lead-ins, Discussion and Conclusion carry most of it. Inside the twelve cells the grammatical subject is usually the lens or the research ("This lens examines…", "Research from this perspective…", "Future research could…"), with cited authors as named agents; there the register is impersonal-descriptive and first person is rare.
- **Other measured habits:** 39 uses of "for instance"/"for example"; spaced en dashes for parenthetical asides, no em dashes; British spelling with -ize; single quotation marks; lettered inline lists, (a)–(c), for the three foci.
