## 1. Aims, scope and article types

### Takeaway
OT is EGOS's dedicated theory journal (OS is the empirical flagship). It publishes three standing types — Theory Article, Review Article, Controversies & Conversations — and since July 2026 a fourth guest-edited format, "Catalysing & Crystallizing" special themed sections. Empirical, data-driven theorizing and summative reviews are out of scope by the journal's own words.

### Cited Findings
- **Scope (EGOS mirror, [VERIFIED] 2026-10-09).** OT aims to advance understanding of "organizations, organizing, and the organized in and between societies" through double-blind, peer-reviewed theoretical papers; it publishes "work in all areas of organization theory", including papers that develop new theories or extend existing ones, historical and philosophical work on management and organization theory, meta-theoretical analyses of core assumptions, critiques of theories, and theory-driven reviews. Out of scope: OT "does not publish papers that draw on empirical data to make data-driven theoretical claims"; empirical work belongs in *Organization Studies*, "the other journal under the auspices of the European Group for Organizational Studies (EGOS)". — [EGOS: OT Aims and Scope](https://egos.org/organization_theory/aims-and-scope)
- **Hallmark of a contribution (EGOS manuscript-submission page, [VERIFIED]).** The page states the journal's hallmark: "a theoretical argument is coherently developed and laid out"; "Meaningful new implications or insights for theory must be present in all articles published in the journal"; submissions must "clearly signal and communicate the nature of their theoretical contribution in relation to the existing literature". Theory articles cover organizations, organizing and the organized "including strategy, organizational behavior, and entrepreneurship", and "Each submission should therefore explicitly signal its theoretical aims and position". Review articles must be "strongly theory-led" and "The submission and review processes for these reviews are identical to those for regular submissions." Controversies & Conversations are shorter, essay-style contributions to a debate on a theoretical topic; "Proposals for this section are received on a rolling basis" through the manuscript central system. For word limits, formatting, APC and AI policy, the page defers to the SAGE author instructions. — [EGOS: Manuscript submission](https://egos.org/organization_theory/manuscript-submission)
- **Same hallmark on the SAGE guidelines ([CAPTURE 2026-10-07]).** "(a) a theoretical argument is coherently developed and laid out; and in a way that (b) significantly contributes to our understanding of organizations and management." And: "Submissions to OT may feature different approaches to theory development, including approaches centred on proposition development, process theorizing, or on a theoretical critique and synthesis of the existing literature. Each submission should therefore explicitly signal its theoretical aims and position and maintain congruity with this approach throughout the paper." Submissions "should be written in such a way that our general readers are able to access and understand the ideas and arguments presented." Theory Article is "the regular full-length" submission; Controversies and Conversations is by proposal to the editors. — [SAGE author instructions, as captured](https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/OTT) (live page blocked 2026-10-09)
- **Founding editorial on the three sections ([VERIFIED], full text).** "Submissions to the journal can be targeted at three standard features ... (a) regular full-length submissions (what we have labelled a regular 'theory article') which will be the bulk of submissions; (b) review papers that provide a comprehensive and theory-driven review of a particular research field (labelled 'review article'); and (c) shorter, essay-style invited contributions to a debate or conversation regarding a theoretical problem or issue (labelled 'controversies and conversations'). All regular theory and review submissions will be double-blind peer-reviewed". For C&C, "authors can submit an informal proposal to a member of the editorial team"; if the team sees potential it asks for "a more formal proposal which describes the relevance, scope and contribution of the essay, including the different positions of the respective authors on the issue or topic as well as the proposed outcome of the conversation or debate." (pp. 3). — Cornelissen & Höllerer (2019/2020), "An Open and Inclusive Space for Theorizing: Introducing Organization Theory", *Organization Theory* 1: 1–5, DOI 10.1177/2631787719887980, [repository PDF](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/127903/Repub_127903_O-A.pdf)
- **Founding editorial on what is out of scope ([VERIFIED]).** "Review papers that only present a summative review of prior work without any form of theoretical synthesis, critique or development do not match with the journal's mission. Similarly, papers that directly draw on empirical data or empirical material to form theoretical arguments fall outside the scope." Also: "There will also be papers across different genres that, while they fall within the scope of OT's mission, fall short of our expectations towards a strong and significant theoretical contribution. Papers that are published in the first couple of issues of the journal already give a good sense of the magnitude of the contribution that we are looking for." (p. 3). — same source
- **Fourth format, "Catalysing & Crystallizing" (2026) ([ABSTRACT ONLY] via Crossref).** Höllerer, Quack & Delbridge, "'Catalysing & Crystallizing' and 'Conversations & Controversies': Facilitating Debate in Organization Theory", *Organization Theory* 7(2), published online 26 July 2026, DOI 10.1177/26317877261466261, licence CC BY-NC 4.0. The abstract (per the Crossref record and Cardiff ORCA record) says the editorial introduces the new "Catalysing & Crystallizing" section and explains how it complements "Conversations & Controversies"; C&C is meant to capture and encourage debate on significant topics in organization and management studies, while the new section "seeks to stimulate engagement with emerging topics and conceptual ideas originating at the margins of, or even beyond, our traditional domain of scholarly inquiry"; both reinforce the journal's "commitment to theoretical plurality, intellectual openness, and innovative forms of organizational theorizing"; since 2020 the journal has aimed to be "an open and inclusive space for theorizing that embraces diversity and paradigmatic plurality". — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/26317877261466261); [ORCA record (403 on fetch; metadata via search snippet)](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/188705/). Note the repository title spells "Catalysing", the abstract "Catalyzing"; the EGOS CfP uses "Catalysing".
- **How C&C sections work (configurational CfP, [VERIFIED] full text).** The format runs through guest editors who package a set of papers "in a special themed section alongside a short introductory essay"; per the AOM listserv summary of the CfP, the first such sections publish in 2027 and will recur "once or twice per calendar year" [that sentence is from the search summary of the AOM CTO/OMT posts, not from the PDF I read — treat as [UNVERIFIED] wording]. The PDF itself says: "In line with Organization Theory's editorial policy, we therefore also welcome a variety of forms of contribution, including theory papers, generative scholarly essays, agenda-setting provocations, and cross-disciplinary syntheses. We are looking for theoretical work that engages deeply with the configurational character of organizational phenomena. ... Purely methodological contributions, empirical applications, and data-driven theorizing are outside the scope of this section. Limited empirical illustrations are welcome where they advance theory." Submission: "with the appropriate Special Themed Section selected at the 'Manuscript Type' option, by January 31, 2027. Authors should follow the journal's submission guidelines". Managing editor contact: Sophia Tzagaraki, orgtheoryjournal@gmail.com. Guest editors: Joanna T. Campbell, Peer C. Fiss, Santi Furnari, Markus A. Höllerer. — [EGOS CfP PDF](https://www.egos.org/jart/prj3/egos/releases/de/upload/Uploads/CfP/CfP_Configurational%20Nature.pdf); AOM posts: [CTO](https://cto.aom.org/discussion/organization-theory-special-themed-section-catalysing-crystallizing-theorizing-the-configurational-nature-of-organizational-phenomena), [OMT](https://omt.aom.org/discussion/reminder-organization-theory-call-for-proposals-special-themed-section-catalyzing-and-crystallizing)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The phrase "Limited empirical illustrations are welcome where they advance theory" in a 2026 OT-sanctioned call is the clearest current statement I found of how far a theory paper may lean on cases: illustration yes, data-driven claims no. A manuscript carrying an empirical cohort as evidence (as the Lima version did) sits on the wrong side of that line for OT.
- [INFERENCE] The journal's repeated test is two-part — coherence of argument plus magnitude of contribution — and the founding editorial explicitly says in-scope papers are rejected for falling "short of our expectations towards a strong and significant theoretical contribution".

## 2. Formal requirements for a Theory Article (and 2025–2026 changes)

### Takeaway
Per the 7 October 2026 capture: 11,000 words including references; unstructured abstract ≤300 words; 3–10 keywords; unnumbered three-level headings; APA references; double-anonymized with a separate title page carrying a six-item Statements and Declarations block; cover letter; Word or LaTeX; figures at 300 dpi. I could not confirm any 2025–2026 change at source.

### Cited Findings
- **Length.** "Submissions should generally not exceed 11,000 words (including references), although in some cases longer versions may be accepted." The guideline does not say whether abstract, tables and figure text count. — [CAPTURE 2026-10-07] of https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/OTT
- **Abstract:** unstructured, "300 words maximum", between title and body. **Keywords:** 3–10, "as specific as possible". **Headings:** "primary, secondary, and tertiary headings … rather than numbered headings". **Typeface:** double-spaced 12-pt Times New Roman; tables may be single-spaced and smaller. **Footnotes:** "Use footnotes sparingly". **File:** Word preferred; LaTeX accepted. **References:** APA; every in-text citation in the list and vice versa, "identical spelling and year". **Preprints:** posting accepted; citing preprints "discouraged". **Figures:** numbered in order of appearance, 300 dpi. — [CAPTURE 2026-10-07]
- **Anonymization.** The manuscript file must be "fully anonymized": no author names in text, file name, headers or footers. Identifying material goes on a separate Title Page reviewers do not see. The Title Page carries title; authors and affiliations; corresponding author's contact details; acknowledgments; and a "Statements and Declarations" section with each of these sub-headings answered or marked "Not applicable": Ethical considerations; Consent to participate; Consent for publication; Declaration of conflicting interest; Funding statement; Data availability. — [CAPTURE 2026-10-07]
- **Third-party writing help.** The guidelines require that "any writing or editing assistance from a third-party" be stated in the Acknowledgements and the cover letter. — [CAPTURE 2026-10-07]
- **Submission mechanics.** Via Sage Track (ScholarOne); files: cover letter ("why you think the manuscript suitable"), Title Page, anonymized manuscript, figures, any supplemental material; submitting author needs an ORCID iD; the form asks for number of figures, tables and words; work must be original and not under consideration elsewhere. — [CAPTURE 2026-10-07]
- **Founding-editorial expectation on length and form ([VERIFIED]).** "Given the inclusive and pluralistic ethos of the journal, papers that are written on a subject can take a variety of forms and lengths." (p. 3) — Cornelissen & Höllerer 2019/2020, [PDF](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/127903/Repub_127903_O-A.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The 11,000-word all-in cap is the binding constraint on a theory paper with a long reference list; the local JOURNAL_SPEC budget (body 8,900 / refs 1,620 / abstract 250 / tables 200) is a reasonable safe reading, since the guideline is silent on whether abstract and tables count.

## 3. What the editors say makes a contribution; current editorial team

### Takeaway
The editors define OT against the "formal" AMR-style contribution (constructs plus testable propositions) and welcome propositional, process, configurational, perspectival, meta-theoretical and critical forms — provided each paper signals which form it takes, holds to it, and delivers "meaningful new implications or insights for organization theory". The team in 2026: Co-Editors-in-Chief Rick Delbridge and Sigrid Quack; Consulting Editor Markus Höllerer; nine Associate Editors; a search is open for a new Co-EiC from September 2028.

### Cited Findings
- **Founding statement of the problem the journal answers ([VERIFIED], p. 1).** "Most journals that currently publish theory papers limit themselves to 'scientific articles' that build, or elaborate, theory. This is the case as, in many instances, the mission and review processes of those journals inevitably push authors to limit their papers to 'formal' contributions such as defining constructs or formulating a set of 'testable' propositions. While these are perfectly legitimate theoretical contributions in themselves, it is important to recognize that they are far from the only type of theorizing that is possible and valuable." — Cornelissen & Höllerer, [PDF](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/127903/Repub_127903_O-A.pdf)
- **Mission and forms welcomed ([VERIFIED], p. 2).** "What counts as theory is not restricted to a set of propositions or construct definitions alone, as important though those elements may be for some papers." "the mission of OT is to be a truly open and inclusive space for theory development across the entire breadth of our field – crossing paradigms, subject areas, disciplines and geographical communities." Breadth "will come from the journal's openness to papers based on different styles of theorizing, including more philosophically informed essays, process theorizing, perspective pieces and critical essays, among various others." Openness to forms "will also imply an openness to different genres of writing (Cornelissen, 2017). Beyond scientific articles that build on an existing literature and elaborate novel theoretical claims around a model or a set of propositions, we are also open to various genres of essays – provided they make a solid theoretical contribution". — same
- **Problem-driven essays and technology topics ([VERIFIED], p. 2).** "The essay format in particular offers the potential for more problem-driven theoretical work around contemporary problems and societal challenges, such as the automatization of labour, new forms of work, or novel organizational forms." — same
- **Meta-theorizing and critique ([VERIFIED], p. 2).** "Problematizing and reflecting on the roots of our theories, or what is sometimes called meta-theorizing, has the potential to change conversations in our field and kickstart new lines of research or redirect existing ones." "Our aim is thus to encourage and support bold theoretical thought experiments through in-depth critique and reflection. This does not mean that every paper needs to have such an orientation, but simply that we offer the space for such contributions alongside publishing high-quality papers on topics that build off an established theoretical lens." — same
- **The test every paper must pass ([VERIFIED], p. 3).** "Whatever the format and style in which papers are written, the expectation for publication in the journal is that papers offer a theoretical contribution with clear implications for how we understand or study the world of organizations. In other words, meaningful new implications or insights for organization theory must be present in all articles published in the journal, regardless of whether such implications or insights are derived from the development of novel ideas into new theory, from a critique of received theory, or from a conceptual synthesis of recent advances. This in turn implies that submissions should clearly signal and communicate the nature of their theoretical contribution in relation to the existing literature and our current understanding of a topic." Also: "We seek to publish articles in all disciplinary areas of organizational research, covering both 'micro' and 'macro' theoretical approaches and topics." — same
- **Review stance ([VERIFIED], pp. 3–4).** The editors "will work hard to make the review process a developmental one, giving authors constructive and actionable feedback on their manuscript, and one as well that, given the journal's pluralistic ethos, ultimately supports the integrity of a manuscript's theoretical position and the voice of its authors." — same
- **2021 editorial on forms of theorizing ([ABSTRACT ONLY]).** Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl, "What Theory Is and Can Be: Forms of Theorizing in Organizational Scholarship", *Organization Theory* 2(3), 1–19, July 2021, DOI 10.1177/26317877211020328; 217 citations per Semantic Scholar. Abstract: "Theory is at the very heart of organizational scholarship"; the essay focuses on conceptual rather than empirical work; "Beyond the propositional approach to theory building, which has dominated the literature so far," it discusses process, configurational, perspectival and meta-theorizing and several forms of critique; it compares them "in terms of their aims, reasoning styles, contributions, and the ways in which they are structured as papers", and calls for "a pluralistic stance when it comes to advancing theory in organization studies." — [WU record](https://research.wu.ac.at/en/publications/what-theory-is-and-can-be-forms-of-theorizing-in-organizational-s/); [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1177/26317877211020328?fields=title,abstract,openAccessPdf,year,venue,citationCount); [UZH record](https://www.merlin.uzh.ch/publication/show/22044). Full text blocked at SAGE; the founding editorial announced it as "one on 'what theory is', which in a comprehensive manner will describe the different styles and genres of writing theory papers in our field" (p. 3).

Convenors: "Rick Delbridge & Sigrid Quack, Co-Editors-in-Chief of Organization Theory; Markus Höllerer, Consulting Editor of Organization Theory". ## 5. Generative AI policy (journal and SAGE)

### Takeaway
The journal guidelines say only that AI chatbots may not be authors and point to SAGE's policy. SAGE's policy distinguishes assistive AI (no disclosure) from generative AI (disclose "upon submission" in methods or acknowledgements), lists prohibited uses, and reserves the right to reject "at any time" for undisclosed inappropriate use, while not rejecting "solely because of the disclosed use".

### Cited Findings
- **Journal-level wording ([CAPTURE 2026-10-07]).** "AI chatbots, for example ChatGPT, should not be listed as authors. For more information see the policy on Use of ChatGPT and generative AI tools." The page opening says SAGE's ethics policies cover "authors' appropriate disclosure of AI use as well as a list of prohibited uses of AI." Separately, "any writing or editing assistance from a third-party" must be stated in the Acknowledgements and cover letter. — https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/OTT (blocked live)
- **SAGE policy, exact wording ([VERIFIED] 2026-10-09; page undated).** Heading "AI assistance (no disclosure required)": "AI tools that make suggestions to improve or enhance your own work" ... "are considered assistive AI tools and do not require disclosure by authors or reviewers." Heading "AI generated (disclosure required)": generative uses "must be disclosed upon submission so the editorial team can evaluate the content generated", with disclosure "within the methods or acknowledgements". Examples the page lists as requiring disclosure: literature review assistance, translation, AI-generated research code, AI-assisted data visualization, representative illustrations or infographics, AI-checked code, reference compilation. Authors should cite original sources rather than the GenAI tool and "must take steps to verify the accuracy of all outputs"; "authors are responsible for ensuring their submission is accurate". Heading "Prohibited use of AI": "Generation of incorrect text or content"; "Generation of data or submission using a series of prompts"; "Conducting interviews with GenAI tools in lieu of participants for qualitative research"; "Analysis of experiences and themes"; "Plagiarism or inappropriate attribution to prior sources"; "Generated images that are presented as unique or novel research images"; "Fabricated references or falsified claims"; "Use of GenAI tools for editorial work or to conduct peer-review"; "Use of GenAI to generate peer review reports" including "uploading or copy-pasting the manuscript or parts of it to a GenAI tool". Consequences: "While submissions will not be rejected solely because of the disclosed use of GenAI tools" ... if undisclosed inappropriate use is found "the editor reserves the right to reject the submission at any time during the publishing process"; published articles go to a joint SAGE/editor investigation under COPE guidance and SAGE's corrections and retractions policy. On authorship the page links COPE's "position statement on Authorship and AI tools" rather than stating a rule itself. The page does not mention the cover letter. — [SAGE: ChatGPT and Generative AI](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/nam/chatgpt-and-generative-ai) (the `us.sagepub.com` URL 301-redirects here)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For a manuscript drafted with heavy AI assistance, the policy requires a disclosure statement at submission — in the acknowledgements for a theory paper, which has no methods section — naming the generative uses (drafting text, literature-review assistance, reference compilation), plus the journal's own requirement to state writing assistance in the acknowledgements and cover letter. "Generation of ... submission using a series of prompts" is a prohibited use; a paper whose argument was produced by prompting, rather than assisted, falls under it. The author's verification of every AI-produced reference and claim is a stated condition, not a courtesy.
- [INFERENCE] Whether a given manuscript is "assisted" or "generated" is a judgement SAGE leaves to editors; the safe course is full disclosure of scope.

### Gaps
- Whether OT's editors apply any stricter journal-specific AI rule beyond SAGE's: nothing found. [UNVERIFIED]
- The policy page carries no version date; whether it changed in 2025–2026 cannot be established from the page.

## 7. Positioning against AMR, OS, Organization Science, JMS; attitude to pluralism, philosophy, technology

### Takeaway
OT positions itself explicitly against the construct-plus-propositions template of "most journals that currently publish theory" (AMR is the implied referent, unnamed), and against OS as the empirical outlet. It welcomes philosophically informed essays, process, perspectival and critical work, meta-theory, and problem-driven essays on technology ("automatization of labour, new forms of work"). The journal never names AMR, Organization Science or JMS in any document I read.

### Cited Findings
- **Versus propositional outlets ([VERIFIED]).** See §3: "the mission and review processes of those journals inevitably push authors to limit their papers to 'formal' contributions such as defining constructs or formulating a set of 'testable' propositions" (p. 1); the pluralistic stance "will stretch theory development beyond existing journals that publish theory" (p. 2). — [founding editorial](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/127903/Repub_127903_O-A.pdf)
- **Versus OS ([VERIFIED], p. 4).** "As a more specialized and dedicated theory journal, OT sits alongside OS and complements what OS offers. OS will continue to be the flagship generalist journal of EGOS, with a dedicated focus on empirical studies of all sorts and styles, and will also publish conceptual articles for the foreseeable future as well. OT, however, will be fully devoted to theory, with the aspiration of becoming a premier, globally recognized and inclusive outlet for theoretical work in our field." — same
- **Global and non-Western inclusion ([VERIFIED], p. 2).** The editorial praises a critical essay that "problematizes our existing ways of treating non-Western contexts" and says openness "will ... ensure the journal's global relevance, allowing it as a theory journal to span across intellectual traditions and geographies." The Lima panel "Researching (and Publishing) using non-Northern Contexts" continues this. — same; [Lima programme](https://www.udep.edu.pe/cceeee/wp-content/uploads/sites/99/2026/10/PDW_Lima_2026_Programme.pdf)
- **Technology and AI as topics.** The 2026 configurational CfP lists "platforms, algorithms and human-AI assemblages (Hsieh & Vergne, 2023; Glaser, Sloan & Gehman, 2024)" among fields with configurational intuitions, and invites "cross-disciplinary explorations that venture outside the domain of organization theory" (Elias, Bourdieu, Benedict, critical realism, complexity science). — [CfP PDF](https://www.egos.org/jart/prj3/egos/releases/de/upload/Uploads/CfP/CfP_Configurational%20Nature.pdf). Roughly a quarter of the Lima PDW papers were on AI, algorithms or platforms. — [Lima programme](https://www.udep.edu.pe/cceeee/wp-content/uploads/sites/99/2026/10/PDW_Lima_2026_Programme.pdf)
- **The current pluralism debate the editors cite.** The CfP cites "renewed debates about pluralism in theorizing (Cornelissen, 2025; Cornelissen, Höllerer, & Seidl, 2021)", with Cornelissen (2025) given as "The problem with propositions: Theoretical triangulation to better explain phenomena in management research. Academy of Management Review, 50(2): 342-365." — CfP PDF. I did not retrieve that AMR paper (a guessed DOI returned 404); [UNVERIFIED] beyond the citation.
- **Cloutier & Langley (2020), "What makes a process theoretical contribution?", Organization Theory 1(1)** is cited in the CfP as the journal's own statement on process contributions. — CfP PDF; not read this session.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] A construct-defining paper is not disfavoured at OT ("perfectly legitimate"), but it competes on the journal's two-part test rather than on AMR's proposition template; the paper must say which form it is and hold to it ("maintain congruity with this approach throughout the paper").

### Gaps
- No OT document names AMR, Organization Science or JMS; the contrast is inferential.
- No editorial statement on AI/technology papers as a category beyond the 2019 "automatization of labour" sentence and the 2026 CfP's topic list.

## 8. New constructs, construct clarity, single-authored and early-career submissions

### Takeaway
No guideline text on neologisms or construct clarity was found; the only direct evidence is that "construct clarity" is a plenary topic at the editors' own workshop. The journal says nothing about author count; it actively recruits early-career authors through PDWs and the Winter Workshop and encourages "researchers in earlier stages of their careers".

### Cited Findings
- "construct clarity" named as a workshop plenary topic alongside "developing a theory contribution" and "genres of theory writing"; "We encourage both seasoned scholars as well as researchers in earlier stages of their careers to submit conceptual papers". — [7th OT Winter Workshop CfP](https://www.egos.org/jart/prj3/egos/releases/de/upload/Uploads/CfP/7th%20OT%20Workshop%20CfP.pdf)
- The founding editorial names "defining constructs" as a legitimate but not exclusive contribution type (p. 1), and requires that any paper "clearly signal and communicate the nature of their theoretical contribution in relation to the existing literature" (p. 3). — [PDF](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/127903/Repub_127903_O-A.pdf)
- PDWs for "Advanced PhD Students, Early Career Researchers (ECRs), and Junior Faculty" (Lima, Ottawa) are co-sponsored by OT. — [Lima programme](https://www.udep.edu.pe/cceeee/wp-content/uploads/sites/99/2026/10/PDW_Lima_2026_Programme.pdf); [EGOS announcements](https://www.egos.org/CPs_and_Announcements_Conferences_and_Workshops)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] A coined construct ("algorithmacy") is admissible, but the journal's "in relation to the existing literature" test means the paper must show what existing constructs fail to do, not only define the new one; the 2021 editorial's typology (unread) is the editors' reference frame for which kind of theorizing a construct paper is.

### Gaps
- No OT statement on neologisms, single authorship, or early-career desk treatment exists in any document reached.
