## 1. Has anyone already named or described the ability? (claim 4, and the word "algorithmacy")

### Takeaway

The strict claim survives: I found no published work that defines a single capability as doing interdependent work with another person through an algorithm that is opaque, binding on both and adaptive, in Consensus, OpenAlex, Crossref, the four journals' tables of contents, or the open web. The loose claim does not: the phenomenon is described in at least six organization-science papers the manuscript does not cite, two of the three parts have near-namesakes elsewhere, and "algorithmic competency" was coined in 2019 for the same population and the same asymmetry. The word "algorithmacy" traces only to the author's own posts, site and conference, plus one unverified 1995 full-text hit.

## 2. The oracy–literacy lineage (claim 1, the sequence oracy → literacy → algorithmacy)

### Takeaway

The standard orality–literacy sources do not describe speech as dyadic or writing as a dyad moderated by a text; Ong 1975 makes the reader a fiction the writer constructs, Goffman 1981 makes even face-to-face talk multi-party, and Thompson 1995 already sorts modes of interaction by relational form and separates the letter (dyadic, mediated) from the book (one-to-many). The claim that "a text judges no one, decides nothing" collides with the CCO literature in the target journal's own neighbourhood (Cooren 2004). Wilkinson's oracy is a general curriculum ability, not a dyadic one, and the historical order of coinages is literacy → numeracy → oracy (and graphicacy), not oracy → literacy.

### Inferences

- "Oracy belongs to the direct dyad" and "literacy belongs to the moderated dyad" are the manuscript's own constructions; no source found supports either, and Ong 1975, Goffman 1981 and Thompson 1995 contradict them. A literacy-studies reader will also object that (i) the field has moved to plural, ideological *literacies* (Street), (ii) the great divide between orality and literacy is itself contested (Scribner & Cole; Finnegan; Halverson), and (iii) the manuscript's sequence reverses the history of the "-acy" coinages and drops numeracy.
- "A text judges no one, decides nothing, and does not change once it is written" is the sentence most likely to draw an OT reviewer's pen: CCO holds that texts act, and Lehtinen & Pälli show an appraisal form doing evaluative work between two people. The moderator/third-party cut cannot rest on texts being inert.
- The move that survives is Thompson's: distinguish modes by the form of the relation. The manuscript should cite Thompson 1995 as the antecedent and say how a triad with a binding, adaptive third differs from mediated quasi-interaction. Olson 1994 survives as the warrant for "fixes the writer's words and leaves out illocutionary force," with the page (p. 93) and wording corrected.

## 3. Simmel and the triad; organizational triads; technology as a party (claims 1 and 4)

### Takeaway

## 7. Verdict

### Takeaway

Four of the manuscript's load-bearing sentences are defeated by sources a reviewer at this journal will know; the central construct survives in a narrowed form; and the reference list is missing the Org Sci 33(1) cluster, the hotel-valuation papers, Thompson, Cooren, Latour and Jarrahi & Sutherland.

### (a) Claims that survive as stated

- No published work *names* a capability and *defines it* as doing interdependent work with another person through an algorithm that is opaque, binding on both and adaptive. I found no such definition in Consensus, OpenAlex, Crossref, the 2022–2026 tables of contents of ASQ, Org Sci, OS and OT, or the open web.
- The word "algorithmacy" has no independent prior use in print that I could verify (one unverified 1995 full-text hit).
- Simmel's arbitrator holds power the two parties conferred (Wolff 1950, p. 151); the algorithmic third does not.
- The multi-sided-platform (economics/strategy) literature and the co-optation essays do not state what a participant must be able to do.
- "Opaque" and "adaptive" hold for platform scoring systems (Rahman 2021; Curchod et al. 2020; Heiland 2023; Glaser et al. 2021).
- The quotations from Stelmaszak et al. 2026, Hinds & von Krogh 2024, Orlikowski & Scott 2023 and Sundar & Kim 2019 that I checked are accurate.

### (b) Claims that survive only if narrowed, with the narrowing

- Claim (4): narrow "no existing account describes the ability" to "no existing account names it or defines it over the triad with a third that binds both"; cite Cameron & Rahman 2022, Cameron 2022, Chan 2022, Curchod et al. 2020, Wang et al. 2024 as accounts of the structure, and Viduchinsky 2026, Meng 2024, DeVito 2021 as prior names for single parts.
- Claim (2): narrow "existing accounts reduce the triad to a dyad" to "... reduce it to a dyad *or* keep three parties and make the intermediary a one-sided or non-binding assistant" (Hancock et al. 2020; Kim et al. 2026; Daryanto et al. 2026). Use Jarrahi & Sutherland 2019, Fig. 1, as the exhibit.
- Claim (3) ("the triadic view has not said what a person must be able to do"): narrow to "has not stated a general, cross-setting capability requirement"; the triadic view also predates Cameron/Curchod/Stark & Vanden Broeck (Fuller & Smith 1991; Lopez 2010; Havard et al. 2009).
- Simmel: "in each case their relation passes through the third, who has aims of its own" → "in the second and third cases"; "determines" → "conditions" or an explicit note that "determining" is Small's rendering.
- Claim (1), algorithm as a Simmelian third: say whose purposes it carries (the firm's, by delegation), and engage Pyyhtinen 2018.
- "Binding on both": weaken to "the result counts for both, even where the consequence falls on one," or change cases.
- Oracy/literacy: keep Thompson's move (distinguish by relational form) and Olson's "fixes the words, leaves illocutionary force underspecified" (p. 93); drop "belongs to the direct dyad / moderated dyad."
- Unevenness and learning by taking part: keep, but as inherited (Jarrahi & Sutherland 2019; Rahman 2021; Jiang et al. 2025; Lebovitz et al. 2022), and answer Rahman's no-experience-advantage finding.
- Terminology: "intermediary"/"moderator" survives only with a sentence on Latour's reverse usage, or by switching to Latour's pair or to "conduit."

### (c) Claims that prior work defeats

- "A text judges no one, decides nothing, and does not change once it is written" — Cooren 2004; Taylor et al. 1996; Vásquez et al. 2016; Lehtinen & Pälli 2020; and, on meaning, Olson 1990 himself.
- "Oracy ... belongs to the direct dyad" — Goffman 1981 (via Dynel 2011; Heath 1986); Wilkinson's own general framing.
- "Literacy belongs to the moderated dyad, in which a text stands between two people" — Ong 1975; Thompson 1995; Eisenstein 1979 (record).
- "In each case ... the third, who has aims of its own" — Wolff 1950, pp. 149–150.
- The four cases as instances of an intermediary that is opaque, binding on both and adaptive: Hinds & von Krogh 2024 is a thought experiment; Curchod's rule binds sellers only; Orlikowski & Scott 2023's guests are anonymous and unbound; Leonardi & Leavell's simulation neither evaluates nor binds the planners.
- The Substack claim, if carried into the manuscript, that the term "has never appeared in a published academic text" — contradicted by the Oberschelp 1995 full-text index hit until it is opened and read.
