# Journal specification — *Organization Theory*

Extracted 2026-10-07 from the journal's submission guidelines,
https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/OTT, read in full that day with every section
expanded. This file is the authority for format. Where it disagrees with anything else in the arm, it
wins. Quotations are the guidelines' own words. Re-read the page before submitting; SAGE revises it.

## Article type

**Theory Article** — the "regular full-length" submission. The other two types are Review Article and
Controversies and Conversations (the latter by proposal to the editors).

> "Submissions to OT may feature different approaches to theory development, including approaches
> centred on proposition development, process theorizing, or on a theoretical critique and synthesis
> of the existing literature. Each submission should therefore explicitly signal its theoretical aims
> and position and maintain congruity with this approach throughout the paper."

Two requirements follow for this paper. The introduction must say which approach it takes. And the
paper must be readable outside its specialty: submissions "should be written in such a way that our
general readers are able to access and understand the ideas and arguments presented."

The hallmark the editors apply: "(a) a theoretical argument is coherently developed and laid out; and
in a way that (b) significantly contributes to our understanding of organizations and management."
Submissions "should therefore also clearly signal and communicate the nature of their theoretical
contribution in relation to the existing literature."

## Length — 11,000 words, references included

> "Submissions should generally not exceed 11,000 words (including references), although in some cases
> longer versions may be accepted."

The submitted Lima paper was 16,531 words all-in: a body of about 10,790, references of about 2,200,
and three appendices of about 3,200. It does not fit. The guideline does not say whether the abstract,
tables and figure text count; this budget counts them, which is the safe reading.

| Component | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Body, introduction through conclusion | 8,900 |
| References (about 60 entries at ~27 words) | 1,620 |
| Abstract | 250 |
| Table and figure text | 200 |
| **Total** | **10,970** |

Every reference added costs about 27 words of body. The body budget is split by section in
[`manuscript/OUTLINE.md`](manuscript/OUTLINE.md). Published theory articles in the journal run
6,700–9,300 words of body (see [`manuscript/MODEL_PAPERS.md`](manuscript/MODEL_PAPERS.md)), so 8,900
sits at the top of the observed range.

## Required components

| Component | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Abstract | Unstructured, **300 words maximum**, between title and body |
| Keywords | 3–10, after the abstract, "as specific as possible" |
| Headings | "primary, secondary, and tertiary headings … rather than numbered headings" |
| Typeface | Double-spaced, 12-point Times New Roman; tables may be single-spaced and smaller |
| Footnotes | "Use footnotes sparingly" |
| File | Word preferred; LaTeX accepted |
| References | APA; every in-text citation in the list and every entry cited, "identical spelling and year" |
| Preprints | Accepted. Citations *to* preprints "discouraged"; update to the published version |
| Figures | Numbered in order of appearance, 300 dpi |

## Anonymity — double-anonymized

The manuscript file must be "fully anonymized": no author names in the text, the file name, headers or
footers. Identifying material goes on a separate **Title Page**, which reviewers do not see.

This bites in three places for this paper:

- **Self-citation.** The dissertation's Paper 1, the Lima workshop paper and the lab's own work must be
  cited in the third person or held back.
- **The site.** If any empirical design survives, GauntleTT and Hult identify the author.
- **The repo.** `algorithmacy-lab` is public and carries the author's name; "algorithmacy" is a
  searchable coinage. Nothing can be done about that except not to point at the repo from the
  manuscript.

The Title Page carries: title; authors and affiliations; corresponding author's contact details;
acknowledgments; and a **Statements and Declarations** section with every one of these sub-headings,
each answered or marked "Not applicable": Ethical considerations; Consent to participate; Consent for
publication; Declaration of conflicting interest; Funding statement; Data availability.

## Generative AI

The guidelines say only: "AI chatbots, for example ChatGPT, should not be listed as authors. For more
information see the policy on Use of ChatGPT and generative AI tools." The opening of the page adds
that SAGE's ethics policies cover "authors' appropriate disclosure of AI use as well as a list of
prohibited uses of AI."

**Not yet read: the SAGE policy page itself.** The disclosure this paper needs — what was assisted,
where the statement goes, what uses are prohibited — depends on it. Section 2 of the present draft was
first drafted by an AI assistant, and the research support is AI-assisted throughout, so this is not a
formality. It is an author-only item in [`AGENDA.md`](AGENDA.md). The guidelines also require that
"any writing or editing assistance from a third-party" be stated in the Acknowledgements and the cover
letter.

## Submission

- Through Sage Track (ScholarOne). Check for an existing account first.
- Files: cover letter ("why you think the manuscript suitable"); Title Page; anonymized manuscript;
  figures; any supplemental material.
- The submitting author needs an ORCID iD. The form asks for the number of figures, tables and words.
- The work must be original, not under consideration elsewhere, for first publication here.

## Cost

Open access, online only, Creative Commons licence. **The APC is US$1,000.** Discounts "up to 100%
might apply depending on the affiliation of the lead author and the respective institutional
arrangements"; where there are no institutional funds, "the European Group for Organization Studies
(EGOS) might be able to offer financial support." Production does not start until the APC is paid.

`submissions/proposals/` records a July 2026 discount to $750 for the special-section paper; the
guidelines page did not show that figure on 2026-10-07.
