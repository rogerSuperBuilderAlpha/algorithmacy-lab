## Summary

The primary legal text. Article 86 gives a person affected by a decision a deployer takes on the output of an Annex III high-risk system a right to explanation. Locators below are PDF page numbers of the 144-page Official Journal file (not OJ page numbers). New numbering (rr-2026-11): section 9, jurisdiction boundary condition.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Article 86, Right to explanation of individual decision-making** (PDF p. 110/144):
- "1. Any affected person subject to a decision which is taken by the deployer on the basis of the output from a high-risk AI system listed in Annex III, with the exception of systems listed under point 2 thereof, and which produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects that person in a way that they consider to have an adverse impact on their health, safety or fundamental rights shall have the right to obtain from the deployer clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken."
- "3. This Article shall apply only to the extent that the right referred to in paragraph 1 is not otherwise provided for under Union law."

**Article 113, Entry into force and application** (PDF p. 123/144): "It shall apply from 2 August 2026." Exceptions: Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025; Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII and Article 78 from 2 August 2025 (not Article 101); "Article 6(1) and the corresponding obligations ... shall apply from 2 August 2027". Article 86 sits in Chapter IX and is in none of the listed exceptions, so on the text of Art. 113 it applies from 2 August 2026 (a reading of the list, not a quotation; amendments unchecked).

**Annex III, areas relevant to a hotel** (PDF p. 127/144), "High-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 6(2) are the AI systems listed in any of the following areas": no area names accommodation, hospitality, hotels or tourism. The two areas a hotel's decisions could be tested against by their wording:
- Point 4(b): "AI systems intended to be used to make decisions affecting terms of work-related relationships, the promotion or termination of work-related contractual relationships, to allocate tasks based on individual behaviour or personal traits or characteristics or to monitor and evaluate the performance and behaviour of persons in such relationships."
- Point 5 (essential private services): the listed uses are public-assistance eligibility (5(a)), "creditworthiness ... or ... credit score" (5(b)), life and health insurance risk and pricing (5(c)), emergency calls and triage (5(d)). Guest-facing hotel decisions (admission, pricing, ejection, ratings) do not appear in the text of 5(a)-(d).
- Point 1(c) "AI systems intended to be used for emotion recognition" (biometrics, "in so far as their use is permitted under relevant Union or national law").

**Article 6(3)** (PDF p. 54): an Annex III system "shall not be considered to be high-risk where it does not pose a significant risk"; but "shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons."

## Critical notes & threats

- Text-only: no interpretation of whether a given hotel system is high-risk beyond what Annex III's words say. Staff-facing systems (4(b)) are the textual hook; guest-facing ones are not named.
- Art. 86(1) excludes Annex III point 2 and requires a decision "by the deployer" with legal or similarly significant effect, so it does not on its face reach every guest-facing refusal.
- Application dates may have been amended after 12.7.2024 (flag `check-amendments`); not checked in this session. A verifier should look at EUR-Lex consolidated text before the sentence is printed.
