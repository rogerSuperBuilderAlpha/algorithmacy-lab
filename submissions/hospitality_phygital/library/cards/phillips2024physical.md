## Summary

Thirty interviews around a simulated just-walk-out retail store. Finds that effort is transferable between physical, cognitive and interpersonal components and that removing interpersonal effort makes the experience more forgettable.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Method: "Interviews were conducted with 30 customers using a simulated frictionless retail experience" (author manuscript, PDF p. 2, abstract; PDF p. 1 is the QUT cover sheet) [full-text]
- Finding: "the reduction of customer effort is facilitated by removing high interpersonal effort which is associated with a more forgettable, or slippery, experience" (PDF p. 2) [full-text]
- Positive effortless experiences "involved interaction with staff as knowledge experts" who could "intercept, absorb, and alleviate customer effort" (PDF p. 36) [full-text]
- Conclusion: reducing effort "can facilitate a slippery slope for memorability within a utilitarian customer experience" (PDF p. 50) [full-text]
- Individual differences found: desire for effortful human interaction, shopping value type, attitude to technology, age (PDF p. 2) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "So the aim of phygital hospitality should not be to wipe out friction. It should be to remove the pointless kind while keeping the moments where recognition, negotiation, and exception stay possible (Padigar et al. 2025; Phillips et al. 2024)." SUPPORT: PARTIAL. Phillips supports the first sentence: removing effort, especially interpersonal effort, lowers memorability. It does not discuss recognition, negotiation or exception, and it separates "positive" from "negative" effort without defining a "pointless kind" of friction in the paper's terms. The second sentence is the authors' synthesis; Padigar presumably carries the "keep valuable friction" half (not checked here).
- Setting is retail (a convenience store), a utilitarian service, not hospitality. The paper itself limits the finding to "a utilitarian customer experience" (PDF p. 50). Memorability, not welcome or standing, is the outcome.
- The paper reports individual differences, so some customers want less human interaction; the v4 claim should not read as a universal preference for friction.
- Bib vs Crossref: all fields match. Crossref online 2022, print issue 2024; the bib uses 2024.
