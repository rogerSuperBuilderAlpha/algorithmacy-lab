## Summary

Three-phase qualitative lab study (November 2013 to April 2014) with 40 Facebook users. Before, during and after a "seamful" probe (FeedVis) that showed the News Feed algorithm's outputs, participants were interviewed about their folk theories. The study reports ten folk theories. Seams let users quickly form and extend theories, yet users acted only on the theories in which they themselves had agency.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Sample and design: "a diverse, non-probability sample of 40 Facebook users" (p. 2371); "Fewer than half of our participants (n=15) were aware of the algorithm's existence" (p. 2375) [full-text]
- Theories from use: "the Aware participants' theories discovered in the first phase developed naturally from their regular Facebook usage" (p. 2373); "Users made plans that depended on their theories" (p. 2371) [full-text]
- Seamlessness causes opacity: opaqueness "stems in part from the merits of 'seamless' design, where designers hide details from users to make interactions effortless" (p. 2371) [full-text]
- Theories do not confer agency: "in most of their proposed theories, they did not see themselves as possessing any agency ... many users see even these simple tasks as being outside of their abilities" (p. 2380); "If they told me what to do, I would do it!" (P35, p. 2379) [full-text]
- Gaming felt inauthentic: "they were forced to deceive the algorithm by changing their behavior" (p. 2379) [full-text]
- Limitation: "we almost certainly produced some folk theories in the minds of the participants when they may have held no theory at all before participating" (p. 2379) [full-text]
- Seams as empowerment: "Seamful interfaces may then be legitimately seen as a tool for the user to be empowered against a system when their interests might differ" (p. 2380) [full-text]

## Support verdicts (rr-2026-11)

- v4 §6: "Through repeated encounters they build working theories of how a system behaves (Eslami et al. 2016; ...)". SUPPORTED: Aware participants' theories "developed naturally from their regular Facebook usage" (p. 2373). Caveat: the paper's main finding is that a seamful probe let unaware users form similar theories quickly (p. 2378). Repetition is one route, not the only one.
- Old card claim "Folk theories form and drive behaviour with no livelihood at stake": PARTLY. Participants were ordinary users paid $10/hour for the session (p. 2373). The paper never frames the point as economic stakes, so "nothing economic at stake" is the card's inference.
- v4 §6 "But there is a ceiling on what skill can do ... Skill cannot conjure a counterparty": not cited to Eslami, but the source supports it directly. Users holding theories still saw themselves without agency (p. 2380).
- Revision plan §7 (separating interruptibility from seamful design): this paper is a primary seamful-design source and should be engaged there. It ties "seamless" design to opacity (p. 2371) and seams to user empowerment (p. 2380).

## Critical notes & threats

- Laboratory, non-representative sample; the authors decline prevalence claims (p. 2379).
- The p. 2380 agency finding is better evidence for "competence is not standing" than the current v4 citations for that sentence. It also comes from a source the paper already cites.
