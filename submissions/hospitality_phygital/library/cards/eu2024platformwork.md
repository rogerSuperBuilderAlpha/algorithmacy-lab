## Summary

The Directive sets minimum rights for people who work through digital labour platforms in the Union. Chapter III, on algorithmic management, applies to every person performing platform work, employed or not. It requires platforms to oversee their automated monitoring and decision-making systems and to evaluate their effects at least every two years, through persons who have "the competence, training and authority" to do so, "including for overriding automated decisions" (art. 10(1)-(2)). Decisions to restrict, suspend or terminate a person's contract or account "shall be taken by a human being" (art. 10(5)). A person affected by an automated decision is entitled to an explanation without undue delay, to a designated contact person with whom to discuss it, to a written statement of reasons for the gravest decisions "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect", to a review answered within two weeks, and to rectification of a decision that infringed their rights or, where rectification is not possible, adequate compensation (art. 11). Data protection authorities enforce these articles, with the ceiling on fines set by Article 83(5) of the GDPR, and the persons affected have a right to redress (arts 18, 24). Member States must transpose by 2 December 2026.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Who is bound and who is protected**

- Art. 1(3): "This Directive applies to digital labour platforms organising platform work performed in the Union, irrespective of their place of establishment or of the law otherwise applicable." [full-text]
- Art. 2(1)(a) defines a digital labour platform as a person providing a service that is provided at least in part at a distance by electronic means (i), "at the request of a recipient of the service" (ii), that "involves, as a necessary and essential component, the organisation of work performed by individuals in return for payment, irrespective of whether that work is performed online or in a certain location" (iii), and that "involves the use of automated monitoring systems or automated decision-making systems" (iv). [full-text]
- Accommodation platforms are outside. Art. 2(2): the definition "does not include providers of a service whose primary purpose is to exploit or share assets or by means of which individuals who are not professionals can resell goods." Recital 20 gives the example: "such as short-term rental of accommodation". [full-text]
- Art. 1(2), second subparagraph: the Directive "lays down rules to improve the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of their personal data by providing measures on algorithmic management applicable to persons performing platform work in the Union, including those who do not have an employment contract or employment relationship." [full-text]
- Art. 2(1)(c): "‘person performing platform work’ means an individual performing platform work, irrespective of the nature of the contractual relationship or the designation of that relationship by the parties involved". The recipient of the platform's service, its customer, is given no rights by Chapter III. [full-text]
- Art. 2(1)(i): "‘automated decision-making systems’ means systems which are used to take or support, by electronic means, decisions that significantly affect persons performing platform work", including decisions on "their access to and the organisation of work assignments, their earnings", and "their contractual status including the restriction, suspension or termination of their account." The definition covers systems that support a decision as well as those that take it. [full-text]
- A vendor's system changes nothing. Recital 55: the platforms' obligations "apply irrespective of whether such systems are managed by the digital labour platform itself or by an external service provider which carries out data processing on behalf of the digital labour platform." [full-text]
- Intermediaries, art. 3: where a platform uses intermediaries, Member States must ensure that persons contracted through them "enjoy the same level of protection pursuant to this Directive as those who have a direct contractual relationship with a digital labour platform", by mechanisms "which shall include, where appropriate, joint and several liability systems." [full-text]

**What the legislature said algorithmic management removes**

- Recital 8: "Automated monitoring systems and automated decision-making systems powered by algorithms increasingly replace functions that managers usually perform in businesses", and persons subject to them "often do not know the reasons for decisions taken or supported by automated systems and are not able to obtain an explanation for those decisions, to discuss those decisions with a human contact person, to contest those decisions or to seek rectification or, where relevant, redress." [full-text]
- Recital 47: such decisions "directly affect the persons performing platform work, who might not have a direct contact with a human manager or supervisor." [full-text]

**Article 10: human oversight**

- Art. 10(1): "Member States shall ensure that digital labour platforms oversee and, with the involvement of workers’ representatives, regularly and in any event every two years, carry out an evaluation of the impact of individual decisions taken or supported by automated monitoring systems and automated decision-making systems on persons performing platform work, including, where applicable, on their working conditions and equal treatment at work." [full-text]
- Art. 10(2): "Member States shall require digital labour platforms to ensure sufficient human resources for the effective oversight and evaluation of the impact of individual decisions taken or supported by automated monitoring systems or automated decision-making systems. The persons charged by the digital labour platform with the function of oversight and evaluation shall have the competence, training and authority necessary to exercise that function, including for overriding automated decisions. Those persons shall enjoy protection from dismissal or its equivalent, disciplinary measures and other adverse treatment where they exercise their functions." [full-text]
- Art. 10(3): where the oversight or evaluation finds "a high risk of discrimination at work" or that individual decisions "have infringed the rights of a person performing platform work, the digital labour platform shall take the steps necessary, including, if appropriate, the modification of the automated monitoring system or the automated decision-making system or the discontinuation of its use, in order to avoid such decisions in the future." [full-text]
- Art. 10(5): "Any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate the contractual relationship or the account of a person performing platform work or any other decision of equivalent detriment shall be taken by a human being." [full-text]
- The reason given, recital 48: the GDPR requires "as a minimum, the data subject’s right to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller, to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision. In addition to the requirements laid down in Regulation (EU) 2016/679, in the context of algorithmic management and considering the serious impact on persons performing platform work of decisions of restricting, suspending or terminating their contractual relationship or their account, or any decision of equivalent detriment, such decisions should always be taken by a human being." [full-text]

**Article 11: human review**

- Explanation and a person to talk to, art. 11(1), first subparagraph: "Member States shall ensure that persons performing platform work have the right to obtain an oral or written explanation from the digital labour platform for any decision taken or supported by an automated decision-making system without undue delay." And: "Member States shall ensure that digital labour platforms provide persons performing platform work with access to a contact person designated by the digital labour platform to discuss and to clarify the facts, circumstances and reasons having led to the decision. Digital labour platforms shall ensure that such contact persons have the competence, training and authority necessary to exercise that function." [full-text]
- Reasons no later than the effect, art. 11(1), second subparagraph: "Digital labour platforms shall provide the person performing platform work with a written statement of the reasons for any decision taken or supported by an automated decision-making system to restrict, suspend or terminate the account of the person performing platform work, any decision to refuse the payment for work performed by the person performing platform work, any decision on the contractual status of the person performing platform work, any decision with similar effects or any other decision affecting the essential aspects of the employment or other contractual relationships, without undue delay and at the latest on the date on which it takes effect." [full-text]
- Review within a fixed time, art. 11(2): the person, or a representative, "shall have the right to request the digital labour platform to review the decisions referred to in paragraph 1. The digital labour platform shall respond to such request by providing the person performing platform work with a sufficiently precise and adequately substantiated reply in the form of a written document, which may be in electronic form, without undue delay and in any event within two weeks of receipt of the request." [full-text]
- Rectify first, compensate second, art. 11(3): "Where the decision referred to in paragraph 1 infringes the rights of a person performing platform work, the digital labour platform shall rectify that decision without delay and in any case within two weeks of the adoption of the decision. Where such rectification is not possible, the digital labour platform shall offer adequate compensation for the damage sustained. In any event, the digital labour platform shall take the steps necessary, including, if appropriate, the modification of the automated decision-making system or the discontinuation of its use, in order to avoid such decisions in the future." [full-text]
- Art. 11(4): "This Article does not affect disciplinary and dismissal procedures laid down in national law, collective agreements and practice." [full-text]
- Art. 11(5): "This Article shall not apply to persons performing platform work who are also business users as defined in Article 2, point (1), of Regulation (EU) 2019/1150." Recital 49 adds: "With regard to human review of decisions, the specific provisions of Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 should prevail in respect of business users." [full-text]
- The word 'human' appears in the title of art. 11 ("Human review") and in recital 49 ("a human contact person"), not in art. 11(2): the text says the platform must reply to a review request and does not say who conducts the review. [full-text]

**Related rights, remedies and dates**

- Information before the event, art. 9(3): platforms must inform persons performing platform work about the systems "at the latest on the first working day", "prior to the introduction of changes affecting working conditions, the organisation of work or monitoring work performance", and "at any time upon their request." [full-text]
- Art. 18: Member States must ensure that persons performing platform work "have access to timely, effective and impartial dispute resolution and a right to redress, including adequate compensation for the damage sustained, in the case of infringements of their rights arising from this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 22 protects them "from any adverse treatment by the digital labour platform and from any adverse consequences resulting from a complaint lodged with the digital labour platform". [full-text]
- Art. 24(1): the GDPR supervisory authorities "shall also be responsible for monitoring and enforcing the application of Articles 7 to 11 of this Directive as far as data-protection matters are concerned", and "The upper limit for administrative fines referred to in Article 83(5) of that Regulation shall be applicable to infringements of Articles 7 to 11 of this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 29(1): "Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive by 2 December 2026." Art. 32: "This Directive is addressed to the Member States." [full-text]
- Art. 26(2): Member States may keep or introduce provisions "which are more favourable to platform workers". The Directive sets minimum requirements (recital 68). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Workers, not customers.** Every right in arts 10 and 11 belongs to a "person performing platform work". The guest of a hotel, or the user of a platform, is the "recipient of the service" (art. 2(1)(a)(ii)) and receives nothing. In hospitality the Directive would reach, at most, people who work through a labour platform (cleaners or couriers, for example), and only where the service meets the four-part definition.
- **Accommodation platforms are excluded by name.** A service "whose primary purpose is to exploit or share assets", "such as short-term rental of accommodation", is not a digital labour platform (art. 2(2); recital 20).
- **Three different people, three different powers.** The overseers of art. 10(2) can override automated decisions, and nothing gives the affected person access to them. The "human being" of art. 10(5) must take the gravest decisions, which puts a person inside the decision without making that person reachable. The contact person of art. 11(1) is reachable, and has the authority "necessary to exercise that function", the function being "to discuss and to clarify" the facts and reasons. The text does not say the contact person can change the decision. The power to rectify lies with "the digital labour platform" (art. 11(3)).
- **Timing is stated, and it is mixed.** Before effect: a human must take the decision to restrict, suspend or terminate (art. 10(5)); reasons must arrive "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect" (art. 11(1)); information about the systems comes before changes (art. 9(3)). After effect: explanation "without undue delay", review within two weeks of the request, rectification within two weeks, compensation where rectification is not possible (art. 11(1)-(3)). No provision suspends a decision while it is reviewed.
- **"within two weeks of the adoption of the decision" (art. 11(3)) is unclear.** The decision may be the original one or the platform's decision on review. I record the words and do not resolve them.
- **A directive.** It binds Member States and takes effect for platforms through national law. The transposition deadline was three weeks away when I read it; I read no national measure. The paper can describe what the Directive requires Member States to ensure, and should not write as if these rights were yet in force everywhere.
- **Business users.** A self-employed person who is also a business user under Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 is outside art. 11 (art. 11(5)); card not written for that Regulation in this list.
- Later treatment not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports by analogy, and is the nearest statutory statement of its timing line.** K7's 'confirms' list asks for "statutes that make one party answerable for performance by others and require a contact point or a human reviewer". For platform workers the Directive requires both a contact point that is a person (art. 11(1)) and persons with authority to override the system (art. 10(2)), puts a human inside the gravest decisions before they take effect (art. 10(5)), and orders the remedies as K7 orders them: correct the decision, and pay only "Where such rectification is not possible" (art. 11(3)). It also enacts a version of P3: oversight includes regular evaluation and a duty to act on what it finds (art. 10(1), (3)). It narrows K7 in three ways. The protected party is a worker. The reachable person is not said to hold the power to change the outcome. And the affected person's report triggers a review after the event, with nothing held in suspense meanwhile.

**K9 (mediation): supports the premise, for another relationship.** The recitals state as a legislative finding that automated systems "replace functions that managers usually perform" and leave people unable "to discuss those decisions with a human contact person" (recitals 8, 47). That is K9's claim about what mediation takes away, made about workers. It is a legislature's premise and no evidence about guests.

**K8 (holder under delegation), in passing.** The platform answers whether it or "an external service provider" runs the system (recital 55), and Member States must reach intermediaries, where appropriate by joint and several liability (art. 3). I have not added the K8 claim to the frontmatter because the point rests on a recital and on an article I report only for context.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- The EU's platform work directive requires that the persons a platform charges with overseeing its automated systems have "the competence, training and authority necessary to exercise that function, including for overriding automated decisions" (art. 10(2)).
- It requires that any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate a platform worker's contract or account "shall be taken by a human being" (art. 10(5)).
- It gives a person performing platform work access to a designated contact person to discuss and clarify a decision taken or supported by an automated system (art. 11(1)).
- For the gravest decisions the platform must give written reasons "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect" (art. 11(1)).
- A requested review must be answered within two weeks, and a decision that infringed the person's rights must be rectified, with compensation "Where such rectification is not possible" (art. 11(2)-(3)).
- The recitals record that algorithmic management can leave people unable to discuss decisions with a human contact person (recital 8).
- Member States were to transpose the Directive by 2 December 2026 (art. 29(1)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the Directive gives guests, customers or consumers any right. Its rights belong to persons performing platform work (arts 1(2), 2(1)(c)).
- That it applies to accommodation platforms. Services whose primary purpose is to exploit or share assets, such as short-term rental of accommodation, are excluded (art. 2(2); recital 20).
- That the contact person must be able to reverse the decision. The contact person's function is to discuss and clarify; rectification is the platform's duty (art. 11(1), (3)).
- That a decision is suspended while it is reviewed. The text contains no such rule.
- That every automated decision must be taken by a human. Art. 10(5) covers restriction, suspension, termination and decisions "of equivalent detriment".
- That these rights are in force as national law. The Directive is addressed to the Member States and I read no transposing measure.
- That art. 11(2) requires a human reviewer in terms. The word appears in the article's title and in recital 49.
