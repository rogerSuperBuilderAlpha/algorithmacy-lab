## Summary

From the publisher abstract: Cornell rejects the common view that "to be wronged ... is to have one’s rights violated". Wrongs and rights "often correspond and overlap" but "diverge systematically". Potential wrongs "do not determine how we ought to conduct ourselves", while rights "do not tell us what corrective action is appropriate after a violation". Bradley's critical notice (abstract) restates the thesis as: "accountability practices are rooted in wrongs, ... obligations are rooted in rights, and ... these are two distinct relations", against what Bradley names the "Obligation-Accountability Nexus (OAN)".

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Book abstract: "wrongs and rights are not flip sides of the same coin but instead represent fundamentally distinct moral phenomena." (publisher abstract via OpenAlex) [abstract]
- Book abstract: "rights—unlike wrongs—do not tell us what corrective action is appropriate after a violation." (publisher abstract) [abstract]
- 2015 article abstract: "having a right and standing to be wronged are distinct and separable moral phenomena." (papa.12054 abstract) [abstract]
- Bradley 2026 abstract: Cornell "convincingly pushes back against a widely held family of views ... according to which there is a necessary conceptual relation between, on the one hand, rights and obligations, and, on the other, wrongs and accountability practices. This is the Obligation-Accountability Nexus (OAN)." Bradley argues for "a coherent rights-based view that rejects the OAN" (cjlj.2026.10074 abstract). [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Why it matters.** R2.3's circle is an instance of the OAN: the paper defines standing through answering and answering through standing. Cornell and Bradley both reject the OAN, from different directions. That gives the paper published cover for separating (i) the directed duty and the guest's claim from (ii) accountability for breach.
- **Caution on Cornell's terms.** Cornell ties "standing to complain" to *wrongs*, not to rights (Liau's reading, liau2023standing pdf p. 4). If the paper defines standing as the correlate of the host's duty (rights side), it is closer to Bradley's "rights-based view that rejects the OAN" than to Cornell. Read both before choosing.
- Everything here is abstract-level. Do not attribute arguments beyond the abstracts.
