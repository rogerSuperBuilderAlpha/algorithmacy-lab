## Summary

Section 1 of the Act deems a "hotel", and only a hotel, to be an inn, and moves the common-law duties, liabilities and rights of an innkeeper to the hotel's proprietor and to no other person. It defines a hotel by a holding-out: food, drink and, if required, a bed offered without special contract to any traveller who presents, appears able and willing to pay, and is fit to be received.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Holder: "the duties, liabilities and rights which immediately before the commencement of this Act by law attached to an innkeeper as such shall, subject to the provisions of this Act, attach to the proprietor of such an hotel and shall not attach to any other person." (s. 1(1)) [full-text]
- Boundary: "An hotel within the meaning of this Act shall, and any other establishment shall not, be deemed to be an inn" (s. 1(1)) [full-text]
- Activation: "In this Act, the expression “ hotel ” means an establishment held out by the proprietor as offering food, drink and, if so required, sleeping accommodation, without special contract, to any traveller presenting himself who appears able and willing to pay a reasonable sum for the services and facilities provided and who is in a fit state to be received." (s. 1(3); spacing inside the quotation marks as the legislation.gov.uk HTML renders it) [full-text]
- Goods: the proprietor "shall, as an innkeeper, be under the like liability, if any, to make good to any guest of his any damage to property brought to the hotel as he would be under to make good the loss thereof." (s. 1(2)) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- The Act does not restate the content of the duties; it carries over whatever the common law attached to an innkeeper "as such". Content (receive, custody of goods) has to come from the cases and from scholarship (calye1584case, bogen1996innkeeper, sandovalstrausz2005travelers).
- Activation has four conditions, three on the guest's side (traveller presenting, able and willing to pay, fit state). It is conditional hospitality in statutory form, which suits the paper and cuts against "simply because they walked in the door" (pitch l. 21).
- Holder is "the proprietor", a single legal person. It answers R2.1c in law but restates R2.1e: in an owner/brand/operator structure, who is "the proprietor" is a question the Act does not settle. I found no case on that question in this run (not searched; H3's remit).
- England, Wales and Scotland only. Restaurants without beds and short-term rentals that do not hold out to "any traveller" fall outside.
