## Summary

The Regulation governs the relation between an online intermediation service and the businesses that use it to reach consumers. A hotel or a professional host that offers rooms to consumers through a booking platform under the platform's terms is a 'business user' on the definitions; a guest is a 'consumer' and receives no rights under the instrument. The provider must write its terms plainly and state in them the grounds on which it may suspend, terminate or restrict a business user (art. 3). When it restricts or suspends, it must give a statement of reasons on a durable medium before or at the time the measure takes effect; when it terminates the whole service, thirty days before (art. 4). It must give the business user a chance to clarify the facts through its internal complaint system (art. 4(3)). Art. 11 requires that system: easily accessible, free of charge, handling complaints within a reasonable time, on principles of transparency and equal treatment, with an individualised outcome in plain language and published figures on its working. Small enterprises are exempt. Art. 12 adds mediation. The provider must also keep the identity of the business user clearly visible on the platform (art. 3(5)). The Regulation applies from 12 July 2020 (art. 19(2)).

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Who is bound, and to whom the duties are owed**

- Art. 1(1): the purpose is "to ensure that business users of online intermediation services and corporate website users in relation to online search engines are granted appropriate transparency, fairness and effective redress possibilities". [full-text]
- Art. 2(1): "‘business user’ means any private individual acting in a commercial or professional capacity who, or any legal person which, through online intermediation services offers goods or services to consumers for purposes relating to its trade, business, craft or profession". [full-text]
- Art. 2(2): online intermediation services are information society services that "allow business users to offer goods or services to consumers, with a view to facilitating the initiating of direct transactions between those business users and consumers, irrespective of where those transactions are ultimately concluded" and that "are provided to business users on the basis of contractual relationships between the provider of those services and business users which offer goods or services to consumers". [full-text]
- Art. 2(4): "‘consumer’ means any natural person who is acting for purposes which are outside this person’s trade, business, craft or profession". [full-text]
- The guest is outside it. Recital 3: "Direct impacts of the development of the online platform economy on consumers are, however, addressed by other Union law, especially the consumer acquis." [full-text]
- Peer hosts are outside it. Recital 11: "this Regulation should not apply to peer-to-peer online intermediation services without the presence of business users". The same recital excludes "Technological functionalities and interfaces that merely connect hardware and applications" and online payment services. [full-text]
- The mischief. Recital 2: "the providers of those services often have superior bargaining power, which enables them to, in effect, behave unilaterally in a way that can be unfair and that can be harmful to the legitimate interests of their businesses users and, indirectly, also of consumers in the Union". [full-text]
- Art. 1(4): the Regulation "shall not affect national civil law, in particular contract law". [full-text]

**Art. 11: internal complaint handling**

- Art. 11(1), first subparagraph: "Providers of online intermediation services shall provide for an internal system for handling the complaints of business users." [full-text]
- Art. 11(1), second subparagraph: "That internal complaint-handling system shall be easily accessible and free of charge for business users and shall ensure handling within a reasonable time frame. It shall be based on the principles of transparency and equal treatment applied to equivalent situations, and treating complaints in a manner which is proportionate to their importance and complexity." [full-text]
- What may be complained of, art. 11(1)(a)-(c): "alleged non-compliance by that provider with any obligations laid down in this Regulation which affects the business user lodging the complaint"; "technological issues which relate directly to the provision of online intermediation services, and which affect the complainant"; "measures taken by, or behaviour of, that provider which relate directly to the provision of the online intermediation services, and which affect the complainant". [full-text]
- What the provider must do, art. 11(2): "duly consider complaints lodged and the follow-up which they may need to give to the complaint in order to adequately address the issue raised"; "process complaints swiftly and effectively, taking into account the importance and complexity of the issue raised"; "communicate to the complainant the outcome of the internal complaint-handling process, in an individualised manner and drafted in plain and intelligible language". [full-text]
- Art. 11(3): the terms and conditions must give "all relevant information relating to the access to and functioning of their internal complaint-handling system". [full-text]
- Art. 11(4): providers must publish "information on the functioning and effectiveness of their internal complaint-handling system", including "the total number of complaints lodged, the main types of complaints, the average time period needed to process the complaints and aggregated information regarding the outcome of the complaints". [full-text]
- Art. 11(5): "The provisions of this Article shall not apply to providers of online intermediation services that are small enterprises within the meaning of the Annex to Recommendation 2003/361/EC." [full-text]
- Recital 37, the aim: "to have access to immediate, suitable and effective possibilities of redress", so that "a significant proportion of complaints can be solved bilaterally by the provider of online intermediation services and the relevant business user in a reasonable period of time". [full-text]
- Recital 37, the decision stands meanwhile: "The providers of online intermediation services might maintain in force during the duration of the complaint the decision that they have taken." [full-text]
- Recital 38: the requirements "aim to allow providers of online intermediation services a reasonable degree of flexibility when operating those systems and addressing individual complaints, so as to minimise any administrative burden". [full-text]
- Recital 39, whole, on handing the system to someone else: "The use of the word ‘internal’ should not be understood as preventing the delegation of an internal complaint-handling system to an external service provider or other corporate structure, as long as such a provider or other corporate structure has full authority and the ability to ensure compliance of the internal complaint-handling system with the requirements in this Regulation." [full-text]
- Recital 5, why the article exists: "In many cases, those providers do not offer accessible and effective internal complaint-handling systems." [full-text]

**Art. 4: reasons before or at the moment of effect**

- Art. 4(1): where the provider "decides to restrict or suspend the provision of its online intermediation services to a given business user in relation to individual goods or services offered by that business user, it shall provide the business user concerned, prior to or at the time of the restriction or suspension taking effect, with a statement of reasons for that decision on a durable medium". [full-text]
- Art. 4(2): for termination of the whole service the statement of reasons is due "at least 30 days prior to the termination taking effect". [full-text]
- Art. 4(3): "In the case of restriction, suspension or termination, the provider of online intermediation services shall give the business user the opportunity to clarify the facts and circumstances in the framework of the internal complaint-handling process referred to in Article 11." If the measure is revoked the provider "shall reinstate the business user without undue delay". [full-text]
- Art. 4(4): the thirty days do not apply where the provider acts under a legal obligation, exercises "a right of termination under an imperative reason pursuant to national law", or "can demonstrate that the business user concerned has repeatedly infringed the applicable terms and conditions". [full-text]
- Art. 4(5): the statement must refer to "the specific facts or circumstances, including contents of third party notifications, that led to the decision" and to the ground in the terms and conditions. [full-text]
- Recital 22, what the statement is for: it "should allow business users to ascertain whether there is scope to challenge the decision". [full-text]

**Who is shown as the provider**

- Art. 3(5), whole: "Providers of online intermediation services shall ensure that the identity of the business user providing the goods or services on the online intermediation services is clearly visible." [full-text]
- Recital 21: providers "should not completely prevent their business users from featuring their trading identity as part of their offering or presence on the relevant online intermediation services", though that is not "a right for business users to unilaterally determine the presentation of their offering". [full-text]
- Art. 3(1)(c): the terms must "set out the grounds for decisions to suspend or terminate or impose any other kind of restriction upon, in whole or in part, the provision of their online intermediation services to business users". Art. 3(3): terms that do not comply "shall be null and void". [full-text]

**Other recourse, enforcement, dates**

- Art. 12(1): providers "shall identify in their terms and conditions two or more mediators with which they are willing to engage"; art. 12(3): both sides "shall engage in good faith throughout any mediation attempts"; art. 12(5): mediation "shall not affect the rights of the providers of online intermediation services and of the business users concerned to initiate judicial proceedings at any time before, during or after the mediation process". [full-text]
- Art. 14(1): representative organisations and public bodies may sue "to stop or prohibit any non-compliance". Recital 44 gives the reason: "limited financial means, a fear of retaliation and exclusive choice of law and forum provisions in terms and conditions, can limit the effectiveness of existing judicial redress possibilities". [full-text]
- Art. 15: "Each Member State shall ensure adequate and effective enforcement of this Regulation." Recital 46: the Regulation "should not oblige Member States to provide for ex officio enforcement or to impose fines". [full-text]
- Art. 5(1): providers must set out "the main parameters determining ranking"; art. 5(6): they are not "required to disclose algorithms". [full-text]
- Art. 2(9): "‘control’ means ownership of, or the ability to exercise decisive influence over, an undertaking, within the meaning of Article 3(2) of Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004". [full-text]
- Art. 19(2): "It shall apply from 12 July 2020." [full-text]

**What the text does not contain** (whole-text searches of the saved file, 11 Nov 2026)

- The words 'hotel' and 'accommodation' do not occur. That a hotel listed on a booking platform is a business user is this reader's application of art. 2(1) and (2), not a statement in the text. [full-text]
- The word 'human' does not occur. 'automat' occurs twice, both about waiver of a notice period (recital 19; art. 3(2)). Art. 11 says nothing on whether a person must handle a complaint. [full-text]
- No provision gives a consumer a complaint, a contact point or a remedy. [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Possibly repealed or about to be.** The Publications Office metadata links a 2025 Commission proposal (CELEX 52025PC0837, 'Digital Omnibus') whose title includes repealing Regulation (EU) 2019/1150. I read only that title, from the metadata, and did not establish the proposal's fate. Before the paper cites art. 11 as law in force, someone must check the Official Journal. If the instrument has been repealed, the card remains good as evidence of what a legislature required between 2020 and the repeal.
- **The wrong party for K7's main line.** K7 concerns a channel from the guest. This Regulation gives the channel to the hotel or host against the platform. It bears on the paper in two ways only. It shows the host's own position in an assembled arrangement: a hotel on a platform is itself subject to unilateral restriction and has a statutory route to contest it. And it shows a legislature tying recourse to the moment of effect: reasons "prior to or at the time of" a restriction (art. 4(1)), thirty days before termination (art. 4(2)).
- **Reasons before effect; the complaint does not suspend.** Art. 4(1) and (2) put the statement of reasons before or at the moment the measure bites. Recital 37 says the provider may keep the decision in force while the complaint runs. The recourse is timely notice with later review, and K7's 'while performance is still possible' is met only by the thirty-day notice of termination.
- **Recital 39 is the useful sentence for delegation, and it is a recital.** A complaint system may be handed to an external provider only if that provider "has full authority and the ability to ensure compliance". That is the legislature saying, of a delegated channel, that whoever runs it must have the power to deliver what the channel is for. It is not an operative article, it speaks of compliance with this Regulation's requirements and not of authority over the outcome of a single case, and it is owed to business users.
- **Standards are soft.** "reasonable time frame", "swiftly and effectively", "duly consider" (art. 11(1), (2)). No deadline, no duty to reverse, no person. Recital 38 stresses flexibility for the provider.
- **Art. 3(5) is a disclosure duty owed to the business user.** It keeps the hotel's or host's identity visible on the platform. It reads with art. 30(7) and recital 24 of Regulation 2022/2065, which make the same visibility matter to the consumer.
- **The phrase "decisive influence" here is merger-control language** (art. 2(9)) about control of an undertaking. It is not the Court of Justice's test in Uber.
- National enforcement, the Commission's guidelines on ranking, and any case law were not read. Later treatment not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports by analogy only; narrows on who holds the channel.** The claim file asks for "Law that enacts the conclusion: statutes that make one party answerable for performance by others and require a contact point or a human reviewer". This statute requires a complaint system and timely reasons, for the business user. It is evidence that EU law answers a platform's unilateral power with a duty to hear the affected party, and that the legislature thought about what a delegated handler must have (recital 39). It is no authority for a guest's channel.

**K8 (holder under delegation): a note for the table.** Among the firms of an assembled host, the Regulation treats the hotel or host as the party that "offers goods or services to consumers" (art. 2(1)) and the platform as a provider of a service to that business. It requires the platform to keep the business user's identity "clearly visible" (art. 3(5)). The instrument therefore presupposes that the business user, and not the platform, is the consumer's counterparty, which agrees with Airbnb Ireland.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- EU law has required online intermediation services, since 12 July 2020, to run an internal system for handling the complaints of the businesses that sell through them (arts 11(1), 19(2)).
- The system must be easily accessible and free of charge, handle complaints within a reasonable time, and communicate the outcome to the complainant in an individualised manner and in plain language (art. 11(1), 11(2)(c)).
- A platform that restricts or suspends a business user must give a statement of reasons before or at the time the measure takes effect, and thirty days before terminating the whole service (art. 4(1), (2)).
- The recitals allow the complaint system to be delegated to an external provider only if that provider has full authority and the ability to ensure compliance with the Regulation's requirements (recital 39).
- The platform must ensure that the identity of the business providing the goods or services is clearly visible (art. 3(5)).
- The Regulation leaves the platform's effects on consumers to other Union law (recital 3).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the Regulation gives guests or consumers a complaint system, a contact point or any right.
- That it names hotels or accommodation. It does not; a hotel on a booking platform falls within the definition of business user.
- That a complaint under art. 11 suspends the platform's decision. Recital 37 says the provider may maintain the decision while the complaint runs.
- That art. 11 requires a human being to handle complaints, or sets a deadline.
- That recital 39 is a binding rule, or that it requires authority to change the outcome of a particular case.
- That small platforms are bound by art. 11. They are exempt (art. 11(5)).
- That the Regulation is in force at the date of the paper, without checking the Official Journal for the outcome of the 2025 proposal to repeal it.
