## Summary

A review article that separates phygital from multichannel, crosschannel and omnichannel logic and proposes the PH-CX framework. Its parts are driving forces (consumer value), four connectors (media, digital, physical, human) and six pillars (practicality, sociability, immersivity, technicality, sensoriality, affectivity). Two processes run through it: digitalization and physicalization.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Abstract: PH-CX helps managers design experiences, "This is done by fluidifying the journeys of customers from online to offline and inversely" (p. 1220). The v4 quotation is verbatim and correctly paged [full-text]
- Definition: phygital is "a holistic and integrative ecosystem that adopts a consumer standpoint as a starting point and then integrates a combination of physical, human, digital and media content elements ... the goal of phygital is to offer unique and compelling customer experiences that should guarantee a coherent continuum in the delivery process of consumer value" (p. 1229) [full-text]
- "Phygital allows companies to provide compelling experiences in hybrid settings by fluidifying how customers navigate through digital spaces and physical places." (p. 1230); experiences "should also focus on fluidifying the shift of the shopping experience from physical to digital settings and vice versa" (p. 1235) [full-text]
- The two processes are "shifting from physical to digital ('digitalization') and from digital to physical ('physicalization')" (pp. 1230-1231; elaborated p. 1236) [full-text]
- Seamlessness named as the goal of physical connectors: merchandising and website should fit "to ensure a seamless customer experience is delivered" (p. 1233) [full-text]
- Human connectors cannot be compensated: "the negative effects generated by the three connectors, including media, digital, and the physical, can be fixed by positive human interactions. However, if the human connectors generate negative effects, this cannot be fixed or improved by improving other connectors" (p. 1234) [full-text]
- Negative experience is something to prevent, not a value: technology should be "easy to use, intuitive, and reliable ... and thus prevent irritation and negative feelings" (p. 1235); negative emotions should be "prevented" (p. 1236) [full-text]
- Closing caution: the framework "should not consider a positive phygital experience to be the aim of the phygital framework. Instead, its aim should be to identify the dynamics of consumer value in the shifting process" (p. 1238) [full-text]
- No discussion of AI. Technologies named are wearables, AR, 3D printing, apps and digital concierge services (p. 1221, p. 1227) [full-text]

## Support verdicts (rr-2026-11)

- v4 §7 quotation "'fluidifying the journeys of customers from online to offline and inversely' (Batat 2024: 1220)". SUPPORTED: verbatim, p. 1220 (abstract).
- v4 §7 "the founding framework of phygital customer experience aims at ..." SUPPORTED, with a nuance (verifier_4, accepted). In the abstract fluidifying is the means, not the aim: the framework helps managers "design compelling customer experiences. This is done by fluidifying the journeys ..." (p. 1220). The aim-language is on p. 1235 ("should also focus on fluidifying"). "Aims at" is fair to the paper as a whole, not to the sentence quoted. "Founding" rests on the paper's own claim that "no attempts have been made to conceptualize phygital" before it (p. 1237).
- v4 §7 "smoothness itself has stayed a goal, never a suspect": SUPPORTED. Fluidity and seamlessness are goals (pp. 1230, 1233, 1235), and friction appears only as irritation to prevent (p. 1235). Caution: p. 1238 says a positive experience should not be the framework's aim. A reviewer close to the editor may cite it.
- v4 §4 "The frameworks call this movement fluidity (Batat 2024)": SUPPORTED. The source's word is "fluidifying" (pp. 1220, 1230, 1235).
- v4 §1 "It presents artificial intelligence, apps, and automated interfaces as tools for a smoother, more personal, more guest-centred experience (Batat 2024, 2026; ...)": PARTLY for Batat 2024. Apps and smoothness are there and the framing is consumer-centric (p. 1229), but PH-CX does not discuss AI. Any AI support must come from Batat 2026 or Brochado et al. 2026, which this card does not cover.
