## Summary

An exploratory qualitative case study of one small, privately owned, full-service hotel in the lower price bracket in central Helsinki. Schumacher and Komppula interviewed the seven reception staff, their frontline manager and the hotel manager (about an hour each) and analysed the transcripts by content and thematic analysis. The hotel learned of service failures mainly from guests' own feedback; staff did not routinely ask guests how their stay was going. Reception staff resolved most failures themselves, with discretion over discounts and room changes but without vouchers or alcohol, and passed large failures and large compensation to the hotel manager. The staff did not know where the limits of their authority lay and wanted written guidance. Only staff and managers were interviewed; no guest data.

## Critical notes & threats

- **One hotel, nine people, staff side only.** The authors claim transferability only "for hotels of a similar type" (p. 125). Nothing here comes from guests.
- **Reliance on the guest is a practice.** The hotel "relies mainly" on guest feedback because staff do not ask (p. 121), and the authors allow that staff can spot failures themselves (p. 120). The study does not show that no other signal exists.
- **Authority is graded and informal.** Frontline discretion covered discounts and room changes; the manager took large cases (pp. 121-122). That matches the abstract of Hewagama et al. (2019), whose 'staircased' empowerment this run could not read in full.
- **Numbers.** The 10–20% figure is the interviewees' reported reluctance, not a rule; the hotel set no amount (p. 122).
- **Journal.** A small open-access hospitality journal; the article is a short exploratory case.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7, P4, first half: supports, narrowed.** In this hotel the guest's own feedback was the main route by which failures reached staff, because staff did not ask (p. 121). The authors' frame admits a second route, staff noticing (p. 120). The case shows dependence on the guest's report as the hotel's practice; it does not show that the report is the only signal.

**K7, P4, second half: supports, narrowed.** Correction ran through people with graded power over the single case: reception staff with discretion over discounts and room changes, the manager for large failures and large compensation (pp. 121-122). Some remedies lay outside the desk's power (p. 122), structural failures outside anyone's at the desk (p. 123), and the staff did not know where the line fell (p. 123).

**K9, empirical side: supports the first half only.** In a staffed hotel, guests' problems were resolved through people with authority (pp. 121-122). The study says nothing about automated or self-service arrangements.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- In a small Helsinki hotel, Schumacher and Komppula (2016: 121) found that the hotel learned of service failures mainly from guests' own feedback; its staff did not routinely ask guests how their stay was going.
- Reception staff there corrected most failures themselves, with discretion over discounts and room changes, and referred large failures and large compensation to the hotel manager (Schumacher and Komppula 2016: 121-122).
- The staff did not know how far their authority to resolve failures reached (Schumacher and Komppula 2016: 123).

**What it may not say**

- That hotels in general, or large and chain hotels, rely on guests' reports. One hotel; the authors limit transfer to similar hotels (p. 125).
- That the guest's report is the only signal of failure (p. 120).
- That a 10–20% discount is the frontline limit; it is what interviewees said they were reluctant to exceed (p. 122).
- Anything about what guests think or do; only staff and managers were interviewed (p. 121).
