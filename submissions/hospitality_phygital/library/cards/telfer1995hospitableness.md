## Summary

Not read. Metadata only: a philosophical paper on hospitableness, with a 2000/2001 chapter version in *In Search of Hospitality*.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Philosophical Papers 24(3), pp. 183-196, 1995 [metadata]
- Chapter "The philosophy of hospitableness", In Search of Hospitality, pp. 38-55 (Crossref year 2001) [metadata]
- Secondary report, not Telfer's text (blain2014hospitableness p. 2): Blain and Lashley write that Telfer "makes a distinction between hospitality and hospitableness". They paraphrase her definition of hospitality, without quotation marks or a Telfer page, as involving the provision of food, drink, and accommodation to those who are not members of a household; these are Blain and Lashley's words, not a quotation of Telfer. The only words they quote from her here are that even in commercial settings "the central ides [sic] of the concept remains that of sharing one's own home and provisions with others" (Blain and Lashley give Telfer p. 39) [metadata]
- Secondary report (blain2014hospitableness p. 2) of Telfer's list of motives, with a duty among them: "These include the desire to please others, stemming from general friendliness and benevolence or from affection for particular people; concern or compassion, the desire to meet other's needs; and allegiance to what one sees as duties to be hospitable, a duty help one's friends or a duty to help those in trouble" (Blain and Lashley give Telfer p. 42; wording as printed) [metadata]
- A second, unquoted report of the list (lashley2015hospitality p. 5): Lashley introduces a bulleted paraphrase ("the qualities of hospitableness include the following points"), without quotation marks or a Telfer page, which gives "A desire to entertain one's friends or to help those in trouble", omits the duty clause, and adds two items absent from Blain and Lashley's quotation ("A desire to have company or to make friends"; "the pleasures of entertaining"). The two are not like-for-like: the only quoted report (Blain and Lashley p. 2) includes "duties to be hospitable"; Lashley 2015's unquoted paraphrase (p. 5, no Telfer page) omits it. Neither is checked against Telfer; check p. 42 before using either as her words [metadata]
- Secondary report on commercial settings: Telfer "reminds us that this is a somewhat simplistic view because it may be that hospitable people are drawn to work in bars, hotels and restaurants, and offer hospitableness beyond, and in spite of, the commercial transaction and materialistic instructions from owners" (lashley2015hospitality p. 5; blain2014hospitableness p. 3 has nearly the same sentence with "managers") [metadata]
- Secondary report: Telfer's "biggest assertion is that hospitable people may not be good hosts", since hospitableness "cannot be undone by a lack of skill" (Blain and Lashley's paraphrase, p. 3); and the quoted formula "what good hosts are good at is making their guests happy" (Blain and Lashley give Telfer p. 40) [metadata]
