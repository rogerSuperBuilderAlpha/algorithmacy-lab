## Summary

Three-day site visit (May 2023) with 60 semi-structured interviews across roles in one unionized, mid-sized West Coast hotel ("Hotel WCM") on how an algorithmic management platform (UpKeep) mediates housekeeping work. The hotel's union contract secured self-sequencing of room assignments and manual dispatch; even so, managerial visibility, documentation labour, receiver-privileged communication and digital task assignment between peers strain communication and coordination.

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence (first): "It splits: staff keep some freedom to act, but more and more of their day goes to reading decisions made elsewhere and working around categories set elsewhere (Pedersen and Pors 2023; Spektor et al. 2025)". SUPPORT: PARTIAL at full text (verifier_2's reading confirmed by Fable). "Staff keep some freedom to act" fits §5.1.1 (self-sequencing by contract). "Working around categories set elsewhere" fits §5.3.2 (GRAs call managers or fetch supplies themselves "instead of sorting through all of the categories in UpKeep") and §5.5.1 (a request with no menu item). "More and more of their day goes to reading decisions made elsewhere" is not the paper's framing: its time cost is documentation labour (§5.3), updating progress and typing in English, not reading decisions. Pedersen and Pors must carry the reading-decisions half.
- v4 sentence (second): housekeeping systems "lock task order and, in their most limited configurations, withhold the day's full task list ... (Spektor et al. 2023, 2025)". SUPPORT: YES as a general description (§5.1.1 and §3 describe the one-at-a-time list and enforced order as configurations), NOT as a finding at the study site, where the union contract secured self-sequencing and the authors call the hotel's implementation "relatively careful" (§3) and "better" (§6). The pair holds because 2023 carries the observation; cite 2025 for the configuration space and the counter-case, not for the restriction.
- Single hotel, three days, 60 interviews, 14 transcripts plus field notes; the authors say findings "may not be generalizable to the hotel industry as a whole" (§4.2).
