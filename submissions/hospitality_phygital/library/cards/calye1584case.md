## Summary

The King's Bench resolved that an innkeeper is not liable for a horse stolen from pasture where the guest asked for it to be put out, and used the case to set out, from the words of the original writ, when the innkeeper answers for a guest's loss: only a common inn, only travellers, only goods within the inn, only by default of the innkeeper or his servants, and only goods, not the guest's person.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Only the common innkeeper is bound; a private host is not: "if a man be lodged with another (who is not an innholder) upon request, if he be robbed in his house by the servants of him who lodged him, or any other, he shall not answer for it" (fol. 32a) [full-text]
- Only travellers: "common inns are instituted for passengers and wayfaring men" (fol. 32b) [full-text]
- Strict custody of goods: "for the innkeeper is bound in law to keep them safe without any stealing or purloining" (fol. 33a) [full-text]
- Answers for his servants: "the innholder shall not be charged, [unless] there be a default in him or his servants, in the well and safe keeping and custody of their guest's goods and chattels within his common inn" (fol. 33a; OCR "un'eſs") [full-text]
- Not the person: "the innholder ought to keep the goods and chattels of his guest, and not his person" (fol. 33b) [full-text]
- The guest's own fault limits it: where the guest ignores the innkeeper's request to lock goods away, "the innkeeper shall not be charged, for the fault is in the guest" (fol. 33a) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Corrects the Fable lead (FABLE_REVIEW §4(d)) that the innkeeper's duty "contains ... a duty to take reasonable care for the guest's safety". In 1584 it did not: the duty was custody of goods, "and not his person" (fol. 33b). A protective duty to the person is a later development (in the UK the general occupier's duty, uk1957occupiers; in US law an affirmative duty described in maese2015rethinking, p. 503).
- Liability runs to "default in him or his servants" (fol. 33a): the holder answers for the servants who perform. This is a located statement of performance delegated, liability retained, which H2 should weigh.
- The duty to receive is not in this report; it comes from Year Book dicta and later cases (wyman1904law, pp. 158-159; bogen1996innkeeper, pp. 77-78).
- Read in an eighteenth-century translation, from OCR; a verifier should check each quotation against the page images.
