## Summary

A comparative ethnography of first encounters in three Danish public services: cancer pathways
(surgeons), IT-supported self-reporting in maternity care (midwives) and digital citizen
self-service (citizen-service bureaucrats). Technologies meant to standardize decisions bring
rough binary categories and scripts that call for new discretionary responses and workarounds,
which the authors type as adaptive handling, attentive compensation and affective encouragement.
How far practitioners can bend the standard depends on their training and status.
