## Summary

The narrow, relational definition of accountability: a relationship between an actor and a
forum in which the actor is obliged to explain and justify conduct, the forum can question and
judge, and the actor may face consequences. Bovens separates it from transparency,
responsiveness, participation and control, classifies accountability by forum, actor, conduct
and obligation (including the "problem of many hands" and four strategies for it, one being
corporate accountability of the organisation as actor), and offers democratic, constitutional
and learning perspectives for judging deficits. The setting is public authority and EU
governance.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Definition: "Accountability is a relationship between an actor and a forum, in which the actor has an obligation to explain and to justify his or her conduct, the forum can pose questions and pass judgement, and the actor may face consequences." (p. 450) [full-text]
- Who is who: "This implies a relationship between an actor, the accountor, and a forum, the accountholder or accountee." (p. 450) [full-text]
- Answerability: "there needs to be a possibility for the forum to interrogate the actor and to question the adequacy of the information or the legitimacy of the conduct—hence, the close semantic connection between 'accountability' and 'answerability'." (p. 451) [full-text]
- Seven elements (Box 1): actor–forum relation; actor obliged; to explain and justify; conduct; forum can question; pass judgement; actor may face consequences (p. 452) [full-text]
- Consequences can include veto: the forum "can block or amend decisions made by the actor." (p. 452) [full-text]
- Transparency is not accountability: "transparency as such is not enough to qualify as a genuine form of accountability, because transparency does not necessarily involve scrutiny by a specific forum." (p. 453) [full-text]
- Retrospective: "accountability, as defined here, is in nature retrospective. Actors are to account to a forum after the fact." Responsiveness and participation "do not constitute accountability" (p. 453) [full-text]
- Many hands: "it is often particularly difficult to unravel who has contributed in what way to the conduct of an agency ... and who, and to what degree, can be brought to account for it. This is the problem of many hands." (p. 457) [full-text]
- Corporate accountability: organisations "can operate as unitary actors and can be held accountable accordingly"; forums "can turn directly to the organisation and hold it to account for the collective outcome, without having to worry too much about which official has met what criteria for responsibility." (p. 458) [full-text]
- Clients as forum: social accountability toward "customers or clients as relevant 'stakeholders'", but "It remains an empirical question to what extent these groups and panels already are full accountability mechanisms, because the possibility of judgement and sanctioning often are lacking." (p. 457) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

The definition the manuscript borrows is exactly as v4 states it. Two uses go beyond it, and one gets the roles backwards.

**The forum is the questioner, not the answerer.** In Bovens the *actor* is obliged to explain and justify and the *forum* questions and judges (pp. 450–452). v4 §7 (line 73) asks a property to constitute "a forum that is required to answer challenges and making sure someone in that forum has real authority to act". In Bovens's terms that is the actor's role. The guest (or a body acting for guests) would be the forum. The paper can keep its idea, an institutional place where a claim must be received and answered by someone with authority, but should not call that place the forum while citing Bovens, or should say it uses the word differently. That is why `claim-contradicted` is set.

**No alternative or exit in Bovens.** The roadmap's last stage, "preserving a meaningful alternative for those who decline the automated route (Bovens 2007)", has no support in the article. Wilson-Nash et al. (pp. 978–981) or Hirschman would carry it.

**Useful and not yet used.** Corporate accountability of "the organisation as actor" (p. 458) supports the plan's *Holder* move (the institution, not the employee, answers) and answers the many-hands problem without naming an official. Bovens's note that client-forums often lack "judgement and sanctioning" (p. 457) fits the guest's position.

Support verdicts (v4):

- §2 (line 16): "Accountability, or answerability, is the duty to explain and justify what was done to someone entitled to ask (Bovens 2007)." **SUPPORTED** (pp. 450–451). Bovens adds the possibility of consequences (p. 452), which v4 omits.
- §4 (line 34): "the arrangement separates the power to make or shape decisions from the duty to answer for them (Bovens 2007)." **PARTLY.** Bovens's many-hands problem describes the difficulty of finding who can be held to account (p. 457), not a separation of decision power from answering duty. The separation is the paper's own formulation.
- §5 (line 39): "some identifiable party has to be obliged to receive and answer a challenge, because standing is empty if no one must respond. Call this accountability (Bovens 2007)." **SUPPORTED** for "obliged ... to answer" (actor's obligation, pp. 450–452). "To receive ... a challenge" fits the forum's power to question (p. 451).
- §7 (line 73): "constituting a forum that is required to answer challenges and making sure someone in that forum has real authority to act, and preserving a meaningful alternative for those who decline the automated route (Bovens 2007)." **NOT SUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED.** Forum and actor are inverted (pp. 450, 452); the alternative is not in Bovens.
- §8 (line 87): the three conditions and six questions "(Okhuysen and Bechky 2009; Bovens 2007)". **SUPPORTED** for the accountability condition only.
- Revision plan §2 *Distinctness*: "Bovens (2007) supplies the mechanism of answering". **SUPPORTED** (pp. 450–452). The plan's added contrast, that Bovens does not carry hosting's content, is fair: his conduct-to-be-justified is public authority's, and accountability is "retrospective" (p. 453), not an obligation owed on arrival.
- Revision plan §5: "Accountability cannot be bought back with the other two, which is why transparency alone does not make a welcome." **SUPPORTED in part** (transparency "not enough", p. 453).

Scope: public organisations and EU governance throughout (pp. 447–448); applying the framework to a hotel and its guests is the authors' extension and should be stated once.
