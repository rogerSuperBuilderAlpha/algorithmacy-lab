## Summary

Section 80 rewrites the United Kingdom's law on automated decisions. It removes Article 22 of the UK GDPR and puts four articles in its place. Article 22A defines a decision "based solely on automated processing" as one with "no meaningful human involvement", and a "significant decision" as one with a legal or similarly significant effect. Article 22B forbids such a decision in two cases only: where it rests on special-category personal data, unless there is explicit consent or a contract or legal ground together with a substantial-public-interest condition, and where the processing relies on Article 6(1)(ea). Article 22C governs the rest: where a significant decision is taken solely by automated processing of personal data, the controller "must ensure that safeguards" are in place, which must include telling the data subject about such decisions and enabling the data subject to make representations, to obtain human intervention and to contest them. Article 22D lets the Secretary of State define by regulations what counts as meaningful human involvement and as a significant effect, and add to the safeguards, including "how and when" they must operate. The section came fully into force on 5 February 2026.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**What is replaced**

- s. 80(1): "For Article 22 of the UK GDPR (automated individual decision-making, including profiling) substitute—" a new "Section 4A" headed "Automated individual decision-making", containing Articles 22A to 22D. The former Article 22 goes. I did not read the former UK text; the EU text of Article 22 is on card `gdpr2016art22`. [full-text]

**Article 22A: the two definitions**

- Art. 22A(1)(a): "a decision is based solely on automated processing if there is no meaningful human involvement in the taking of the decision". [full-text]
- Art. 22A(1)(b): "a decision is a significant decision, in relation to a data subject, if— (i) it produces a legal effect for the data subject, or (ii) it has a similarly significant effect for the data subject." [full-text]
- Art. 22A(2): "When considering whether there is meaningful human involvement in the taking of a decision, a person must consider, among other things, the extent to which the decision is reached by means of profiling." [full-text]

**Article 22B: what remains forbidden**

- Art. 22B(1): "A significant decision based entirely or partly on processing described in Article 9(1) (processing of special categories of personal data) may not be taken based solely on automated processing, unless one of the following conditions is met." [full-text]
- Art. 22B(2): "The first condition is that the decision is based entirely on processing of personal data to which the data subject has given explicit consent." [full-text]
- Art. 22B(3): the second condition is that the decision is "necessary for entering into, or performing, a contract between the data subject and a controller" or "required or authorised by law", and that "point (g) of Article 9(2) applies." [full-text]
- Art. 22B(4): "A significant decision may not be taken based solely on automated processing if the processing of personal data carried out by, or on behalf of, the decision-maker for the purposes of the decision is carried out entirely or partly in reliance on Article 6(1)(ea)." I did not read Article 6(1)(ea). [full-text]
- No other provision of the new articles forbids a solely automated significant decision. A decision that uses no special-category data and does not rely on Article 6(1)(ea) may be taken with no human involved, subject to Article 22C. That is a reading of the structure of Articles 22B and 22C, not a quotation. [full-text]

**Article 22C: the safeguards**

- Art. 22C(1): "Where a significant decision taken by or on behalf of a controller in relation to a data subject is— (a) based entirely or partly on personal data, and (b) based solely on automated processing, the controller must ensure that safeguards for the data subject’s rights, freedoms and legitimate interests are in place which comply with paragraph 2 and any regulations under Article 22D(3)." [full-text]
- Art. 22C(2): "The safeguards must consist of or include measures which— (a) provide the data subject with information about decisions described in paragraph 1 taken in relation to the data subject; (b) enable the data subject to make representations about such decisions; (c) enable the data subject to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller in relation to such decisions; (d) enable the data subject to contest such decisions." [full-text]
- The duty falls on the controller for decisions taken "by or on behalf of a controller" (art. 22C(1)), so a decision taken for the controller by another party is covered. [full-text]
- The text states no time for any safeguard. Paragraph 2(a) speaks of "decisions described in paragraph 1 taken in relation to the data subject", and (b) to (d) of "such decisions". [full-text]

**Article 22D: what ministers may add**

- Art. 22D(1): "The Secretary of State may by regulations provide that, for the purposes of Article 22A(1)(a), there is, or is not, to be taken to be meaningful human involvement in the taking of a decision in cases described in the regulations." [full-text]
- Art. 22D(2): regulations may provide that "a description of decision is, or is not, to be taken to have a similarly significant effect for the data subject." [full-text]
- Art. 22D(3): regulations may require further safeguards (a), may impose "requirements which supplement what Article 22C(2) requires the safeguards to consist of or include (including, for example, provision about how and when things described in Article 22C(2) must be done or be capable of being done)" (b), and may provide for "measures which are not to be taken to satisfy one or more of points (a) to (d) of Article 22C(2)" (c). [full-text]
- Art. 22D(4): "Regulations under paragraph 3 may not amend Article 22C." Art. 22D(5): "Regulations under this Article are subject to the affirmative resolution procedure." [full-text]

**Law enforcement and intelligence processing** (s. 80(3)-(5))

- s. 80(3) substitutes ss. 50A-50D of "the 2018 Act" on the same pattern. New s. 50C(3) allows the safeguards to be disapplied for listed reasons if "the controller reconsiders the decision as soon as reasonably practicable" and "there is meaningful human involvement in the reconsideration of the decision." [full-text]
- s. 80(4) inserts s. 96(4): for intelligence services processing, "a decision is based on entirely automated processing if the decision-making process does not include an opportunity for a human being to accept, reject or influence the decision." [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **From a rule against, to a rule about how.** On the EU text, and on the Court of Justice's reading of it (card `schufa2023cjeu`, para 52), a solely automated significant decision is forbidden unless an exception applies. The new UK articles forbid it only in the two cases of Article 22B and otherwise require safeguards. I have read the new UK text and the EU text; I have not read the former UK Article 22, so the comparison assumes what s. 80(1) implies, that the article replaced was the UK's counterpart of the EU provision.
- **After the decision, on the grammar; unfixed, on the power.** The safeguards concern decisions "taken" (art. 22C(2)(a)). Nothing requires human intervention before a decision takes effect. Nothing forbids it either: Article 22D(3)(b) lets regulations say "how and when" each safeguard must be available. I did not check whether any regulations under Article 22D exist.
- **The person's power is not stated.** Article 22C(2)(c) requires that the data subject can "obtain human intervention on the part of the controller". It does not say the human must be able to change the decision.
- **The hinge is "meaningful human involvement".** A decision with meaningful human involvement in its taking is outside Articles 22B and 22C altogether (art. 22A(1)(a)). A hotel that puts a member of staff with real discretion into a decision owes none of these safeguards under this section. What counts as meaningful may be fixed by regulations (art. 22D(1)).
- **Data protection, not consumer or hospitality law.** The duties attach to a controller's processing of personal data and are owed to a data subject. Enforcement and remedies sit elsewhere in the UK GDPR and the 2018 Act, which I did not read.
- **Revised text from an archive capture.** The page is the 'Latest available (Revised)' version and lists alternative versions dated 19/06/2025 and 05/02/2026. Quotation in print should be checked against the live site.
- **Punctuation.** The saved text prints the inserted articles with opening and closing quotation marks and em-dashes after 'if' and 'which'; quotations above keep the wording and run list items on.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): collides with it as a statement of UK law; leaves P3 alone.** K7's 'refutes or narrows' list expects that "the legal analogues all point to later repair". This one does. In the United Kingdom a controller may now take a significant decision about a person with no human involved, provided the person can afterwards be told, make representations, obtain human intervention and contest (art. 22C). The statute keeps a route to a person, which is half of K7's conclusion. It does not place that person before the effect, does not give that person stated power, and no longer starts from a rule that such decisions should not be taken. K7 therefore cannot lean on UK data protection law for its timing line, and the draft's passage on Article 22 must be confined to the EU or rewritten for the UK.

**K9 (mediation, before reception): narrows.** K9 names the case where a system decides admission. Under the new articles an automated refusal to contract is lawful in the UK unless it rests on special-category data or on Article 6(1)(ea) (art. 22B), and is followed by safeguards. The EU position on the same decision is on card `schufa2023cjeu`.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- Since 5 February 2026 the United Kingdom has replaced Article 22 of the UK GDPR with Articles 22A to 22D (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, s. 80(1); S.I. 2026/82, reg. 2(j)).
- The new articles forbid a solely automated significant decision only where it is based on special-category data without one of two stated conditions, or where the processing relies on Article 6(1)(ea) (art. 22B).
- For other solely automated significant decisions the controller must have safeguards in place that tell the data subject about the decision and enable the data subject to make representations, obtain human intervention and contest it (art. 22C).
- A decision is solely automated only if there is "no meaningful human involvement in the taking of the decision" (art. 22A(1)(a)).
- The Act sets no time for human intervention and empowers the Secretary of State to make regulations about "how and when" the safeguards must operate (art. 22D(3)(b)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That UK law gives a person the right not to be subject to a solely automated decision, without qualification. Section 80 substitutes new articles for the article that carried that right in the EU text.
- That UK law requires a human before an automated decision takes effect. The safeguards concern decisions "taken" (art. 22C(2)).
- That the human who intervenes must be able to reverse the decision. The text does not say so.
- That the UK position is identical to the EU's. The EU text and the Court of Justice's reading of it are on cards `gdpr2016art22` and `schufa2023cjeu`.
- That the former UK Article 22 said any particular thing, on this card's authority. I did not read it.
- That regulations under Article 22D have or have not been made. I did not check.
