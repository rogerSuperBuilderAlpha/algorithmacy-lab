## Summary

Asks whether an Airbnb host and guest stand as landlord and tenant or as innkeeper and guest, compares the two bodies of law, and argues that the relationship should be classified and regulated under the law of innkeepers.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Activation by admission: "If the guest thus “presents himself” and is accepted, the relationship “is instantly established between them” and the rights and duties of both parties are at once fixed by law rather than by contract." (p. 501, citing Beale) [full-text]
- "once a guest is admitted to an inn, the innkeeper’s obligation to care for him is imposed by law and not by the will of the parties." (p. 501) [full-text]
- The common-law duties as Maese restates them from Beale (1906), undated: "the innkeeper was obligated to provide reasonably safe premises; furnish accommodations; exercise reasonable care in the protection of the guest; protect the guest from injury by third parties; and supply as much food as the guest reasonably required." (p. 499; footnote numbers 164-167 removed) [full-text]
- "To be sure, the innkeeper was not the insurer of his guest’s safety; nonetheless, the innkeeper had the duty to protect his guests, to the best of his ability, through reasonable means." (p. 499 n. 166, citing Beale at 123) [full-text]
- Goods: the innkeeper's responsibility for the guest's goods "could not be limited by contract" (p. 499) [full-text]
- Protective duty in modern US law: "the innkeeper must not only provide reasonably safe accommodations but has an affirmative duty to exercise reasonable care in the protection of the guest and to protect the guest against third parties." (p. 503) [full-text]
- Reciprocal powers: the innkeeper "may resort to self-help to eject a guest who is “unruly and disorderly,”" and "receives a lien over the guest’s property as security for payment" (p. 503) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Student work; its doctrinal statements rest on Beale (1906) and state cases I did not read.
- Two-step activation in US law: the guest presents, the innkeeper receives. The duty to receive governs the first step; the status and its duties begin at the second. This is the closest located statement of "admission" as the trigger.
