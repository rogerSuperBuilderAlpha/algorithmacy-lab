## Summary

From the abstract: the responsibility-gap thesis for deployed AI agents rests on a "delegation
illusion", an inference from the principal's causal and epistemic remoteness to reduced
answerability. Separating attributability, answerability and accountability, it defends a
"principle of responsibility conservation under delegation" grounded in the deployer's control over
the prior act of delegating.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Names the error: "the inference from a principal’s causal and epistemic remoteness from an outcome to a diminution of her answerability for it." [abstract]
- Autonomy, opacity and adaptivity "bear on attributability while leaving intact an answerability grounded, through a standard tracing structure, in the deployer’s guidance control over the prior act of delegation." [abstract]
- Defends "a principle of responsibility conservation under delegation", operationalised through foreseeability of harmful dispositions "at the level of types" [abstract]
- Article 518, vol. 6, issue 5 [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- Close to the paper's *Transfer* conclusion (delegating to a system does not shed the deployer's responsibility) by a different route: Kong grounds it in the deployer's control over the act of delegation and foreseeability of harms; the paper grounds it in the role constituted by the arrival. A referee who knows Kong will ask why the paper needs hospitality at all. The answer is the *Distinctness* move (content: an obligation to this arrival including the negotiated exception), and that answer should name Kong.
- Harm-centred and agent-centred, like the responsibility-gap literature it engages; nothing in the abstract concerns obligations owed before harm.
- Very recent (Sep 2026) and thinly cited; abstract only.
