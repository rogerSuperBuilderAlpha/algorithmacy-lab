## Summary

The Directive regulates contracts for packages: combinations of at least two different types of travel service (carriage, accommodation, vehicle rental, other tourist services) sold for one trip. It binds traders in three capacities, organiser, retailer and travel service provider, and puts the liability on the organiser, the trader who combines and sells the package. The organiser answers to the traveller for every service in the package whoever performs it (art. 13(1)), must remedy a failure during the trip unless that is impossible or disproportionately costly (art. 13(3)), must help a traveller in difficulty without undue delay (art. 16), and must put in the contract a contact point through which the traveller can reach it quickly during the trip (art. 7(2)(d)). The traveller for their part must report a failure without undue delay (art. 13(2)), and may fix it and claim the cost if the organiser does not act within a reasonable period the traveller sets (art. 13(4)). Price reduction and damages follow where remedy fails (art. 14). None of this can be waived or avoided by the organiser calling itself an intermediary (art. 23). The Directive says nothing about who or what must answer at the contact point, lays no duty on the organiser to supervise its suppliers, and does not reach a single travel service bought alone.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Scope and definitions**

- Art. 2(1): "This Directive applies to packages offered for sale or sold by traders to travellers and to linked travel arrangements facilitated by traders for travellers." [full-text]
- Art. 3(2): "‘package’ means a combination of at least two different types of travel services for the purpose of the same trip or holiday", if combined by one trader before a single contract is concluded (point (a)) or, whatever the number of contracts, if the services are, among other cases, "purchased from a single point of sale and those services have been selected before the traveller agrees to pay" (point (b)(i)), "offered, sold or charged at an inclusive or total price" (point (b)(ii)), or "advertised or sold under the term ‘package’ or under a similar term" (point (b)(iii)). [full-text]
- Art. 3(1): a travel service is carriage of passengers; "accommodation which is not intrinsically part of carriage of passengers and is not for residential purposes" (point (b)); rental of cars and certain other vehicles; and "any other tourist service not intrinsically part of a travel service within the meaning of points (a), (b) or (c)" (point (d)). [full-text]
- A stay sold alone is outside. Recital 15: "The purchase of a travel service on a stand-alone basis as a single travel service should constitute neither a package nor a linked travel arrangement." [full-text]
- What a hotel ordinarily supplies counts as one service. Recital 17: services "which are intrinsically part of another travel service should not be considered as travel services in their own right", and the examples include "meals, drinks and cleaning provided as part of accommodation, or access to on-site facilities such as a swimming pool, sauna, spa or gym included for hotel guests". [full-text]
- Extras bought on site do not make a package. Recital 18: "where other tourist services are added, for instance, to hotel accommodation, booked as a stand-alone service, after the traveller's arrival at the hotel, this should not constitute a package." Art. 3(2), second subparagraph, point (b), excludes tourist services that "are selected and purchased only after the performance of a travel service as referred to in point (a), (b) or (c) of point 1 has started". [full-text]
- Accommodation plus one other tourist service bought in advance can be a package. Recital 18: that combination creates a package "only if they account for a significant proportion of the value of the package or linked travel arrangement, or are advertised as or otherwise represent an essential feature of the trip or holiday", and "If other tourist services account for 25 % or more of the value of the combination, those services should be considered as representing a significant proportion". [full-text]
- Member States may go further for single services. Recital 21: "Member States may maintain or introduce corresponding provisions for certain stand-alone contracts regarding single travel services (such as the rental of holiday homes)". [full-text]
- Exclusions, art. 2(2): packages "covering a period of less than 24 hours unless overnight accommodation is included" (a); those offered "occasionally and on a not-for-profit basis and only to a limited group of travellers" (b); and those "purchased on the basis of a general agreement for the arrangement of business travel between a trader and another natural or legal person who is acting for purposes relating to his trade, business, craft or profession" (c). [full-text]
- To whom the duties are owed. Art. 3(6): "‘traveller’ means any person who is seeking to conclude a contract, or is entitled to travel on the basis of a contract concluded, within the scope of this Directive". The traveller need not be the person who paid. [full-text]
- Who is bound. Art. 3(8): "‘organiser’ means a trader who combines and sells or offers for sale packages, either directly or through another trader or together with another trader, or the trader who transmits the traveller's data to another trader in accordance with point (b)(v) of point 2". Art. 3(9): "‘retailer’ means a trader other than the organiser who sells or offers for sale packages combined by an organiser". Art. 3(7) defines "trader" to cover anyone acting "in the capacity of organiser, retailer, trader facilitating a linked travel arrangement or as a travel service provider". [full-text]
- Art. 3(13): "‘lack of conformity’ means a failure to perform or improper performance of the travel services included in a package". [full-text]
- Art. 4 (full harmonisation): "Unless otherwise provided for in this Directive, Member States shall not maintain or introduce, in their national law, provisions diverging from those laid down in this Directive, including more or less stringent provisions which would ensure a different level of traveller protection." [full-text]

**Who answers for services performed by others, and why**

- Art. 13(1), first subparagraph: "Member States shall ensure that the organiser is responsible for the performance of the travel services included in the package travel contract, irrespective of whether those services are to be performed by the organiser or by other travel service providers." [full-text]
- Art. 13(1), second subparagraph: "Member States may maintain or introduce in their national law provisions under which the retailer is also responsible for the performance of the package." [full-text]
- The stated rationale, recital 22: "The main characteristic of a package is that there is one trader responsible as an organiser for the proper performance of the package as a whole." [full-text]
- Recital 22 fixes the role by what the trader does: "Whether a trader is acting as an organiser for a given package should depend on that trader's involvement in the creation of the package, and not on how the trader describes his business." [full-text]
- Recital 23 gives the mischief: the earlier Directive's flexibility "has led to ambiguity in some Member States as to which trader is liable for the performance of the relevant travel services. Therefore, it should be clarified in this Directive that organisers are responsible for the performance of the travel services included in the package travel contract, unless national law provides that both the organiser and the retailer are liable." [full-text]
- Recital 8 ties the definition to the assumption of responsibility: packages are combinations "that display features which travellers typically associate with packages, in particular where separate travel services are combined into a single travel product for which the organiser assumes responsibility for proper performance." [full-text]
- Recital 10 ties it to what the traveller may expect: the further criteria "predominantly relate to the way in which the travel services are presented or purchased and where travellers may reasonably expect to be protected by this Directive." [full-text]
- The suppliers answer to the organiser. Art. 22: where the organiser pays compensation, grants a price reduction or meets its other obligations, "Member States shall ensure that the organiser or retailer has the right to seek redress from any third parties which contributed to the event triggering compensation, price reduction or other obligations." Recital 36: "The organiser's liability should be without prejudice to the right to seek redress from third parties, including service providers." [full-text]
- The contrast case, where no one party answers. For linked travel arrangements the facilitating trader must tell the traveller that he "will not benefit from any of the rights applying exclusively to packages under this Directive and that each service provider will be solely responsible for the proper contractual performance of his service" (art. 19(2)(a)); the standard form reads "our company/XY will not be responsible for the proper performance of those additional travel services. In case of problems please contact the relevant service provider." (Annex II, Part A). [full-text]

**What the traveller is given as a means of reaching the organiser**

- Before the contract, art. 5(1)(b): "the trading name and geographical address of the organiser and, where applicable, of the retailer, as well as their telephone number and, where applicable, e-mail address". Art. 5(1) lays this duty on "the organiser and, where the package is sold through a retailer, also the retailer". [full-text]
- In the contract, art. 7(2)(b): information that the organiser is "responsible for the proper performance of all travel services included in the contract in accordance with Article 13" and "obliged to provide assistance if the traveller is in difficulty in accordance with Article 16". [full-text]
- The contact point, art. 7(2)(d): the contract must give "the name, address, telephone number, e-mail address and, where applicable, the fax number of the organiser's local representative, of a contact point or of another service which enables the traveller to contact the organiser quickly and communicate with him efficiently, to request assistance when the traveller is in difficulty or to complain about any lack of conformity perceived during the performance of the package". [full-text]
- Art. 7(2)(e): the contract must carry "information that the traveller is required to communicate any lack of conformity which he perceives during the performance of the package in accordance with Article 13(2)". [full-text]
- Art. 7(2)(f): where unaccompanied minors travel on a package with accommodation, "information enabling direct contact with the minor or the person responsible for the minor at the minor's place of stay". [full-text]
- Art. 7(2)(g): "information on available in-house complaint handling procedures and on alternative dispute resolution (‘ADR’) mechanisms". [full-text]
- Art. 8: "As regards compliance with the information requirements laid down in this Chapter, the burden of proof shall be on the trader." [full-text]
- The standard form the traveller receives before contracting says: "There is always at least one trader who is liable for the proper performance of all the travel services included in the contract." and "Travellers are given an emergency telephone number or details of a contact point where they can get in touch with the organiser or the travel agent." (Annex I, Part A; the same two sentences are in Parts B and C). The word "emergency" occurs only in the Annex forms, not in the articles. [full-text]
- A second channel through the seller, art. 15: "Member States shall ensure that the traveller may address messages, requests or complaints in relation to the performance of the package directly to the retailer through which it was purchased. The retailer shall forward those messages, requests or complaints to the organiser without undue delay." And: "For the purpose of compliance with time-limits or limitation periods, receipt of the messages, requests or complaints referred to in the first subparagraph by the retailer shall be considered as receipt by the organiser." Recital 24 gives the reason: "In order to facilitate communication, in particular in cross-border cases, travellers should be able to contact the organiser also via the retailer through which they purchased the package." [full-text]

**Remedies while the trip is under way**

- The traveller's report is the trigger, art. 13(2): "The traveller shall inform the organiser without undue delay, taking into account the circumstances of the case, of any lack of conformity which he perceives during the performance of a travel service included in the package travel contract." [full-text]
- The duty to remedy, art. 13(3): "If any of the travel services are not performed in accordance with the package travel contract, the organiser shall remedy the lack of conformity, unless that: (a) is impossible; or (b) entails disproportionate costs, taking into account the extent of the lack of conformity and the value of the travel services affected." Where the organiser does not remedy for either reason, "Article 14 shall apply." [full-text]
- The traveller's right to set a period, art. 13(4): "Without prejudice to the exceptions laid down in paragraph 3, if the organiser does not remedy the lack of conformity within a reasonable period set by the traveller, the traveller may do so himself and request reimbursement of the necessary expenses. It shall not be necessary for the traveller to specify a time-limit if the organiser refuses to remedy the lack of conformity or if immediate remedy is required." [full-text]
- Art. 13(5): "Where a significant proportion of the travel services cannot be provided as agreed in the package travel contract, the organiser shall offer, at no extra cost to the traveller, suitable alternative arrangements". [full-text]
- Art. 13(6): "Where a lack of conformity substantially affects the performance of the package and the organiser has failed to remedy it within a reasonable period set by the traveller, the traveller may terminate the package travel contract without paying a termination fee". [full-text]
- Assistance, art. 16: "Member States shall ensure that the organiser gives appropriate assistance without undue delay to the traveller in difficulty, including in the circumstances referred to in Article 13(7), in particular by: (a) providing appropriate information on health services, local authorities and consular assistance; and (b) assisting the traveller to make distance communications and helping the traveller to find alternative travel arrangements." The organiser may charge "a reasonable fee for such assistance if the difficulty is caused intentionally by the traveller or through the traveller's negligence." [full-text]
- The stated rationale, recital 34: "The traveller should be entitled to have problems resolved"; "If the organiser does not remedy the lack of conformity within a reasonable period set by the traveller, the traveller should be able to do so himself and request reimbursement of the necessary expenses."; and the example for immediate remedy, "when, due to the delay of a bus provided by the organiser, the traveller has to take a taxi to catch his flight on time." [full-text]
- Recital 34 also states what follows if the traveller stays silent: "The traveller should be required to inform the organiser without undue delay, taking into account the circumstances of the case, of any lack of conformity he perceives during the performance of a travel service included in the package travel contract. Failure to do so may be taken into account when determining the appropriate price reduction or compensation for damages where such notice would have avoided or reduced the damage." No article repeats that consequence. [full-text]
- Recital 37: "If the traveller is in difficulty during the trip or holiday, the organiser should be obliged to give appropriate assistance without undue delay." [full-text]

**Remedies afterwards, and the defences**

- Art. 14(1): "the traveller is entitled to an appropriate price reduction for any period during which there was lack of conformity, unless the organiser proves that the lack of conformity is attributable to the traveller." [full-text]
- Art. 14(2): "The traveller shall be entitled to receive appropriate compensation from the organiser for any damage which the traveller sustains as a result of any lack of conformity. Compensation shall be made without undue delay." [full-text]
- Art. 14(3): "The traveller shall not be entitled to compensation for damages if the organiser proves that the lack of conformity is: (a) attributable to the traveller; (b) attributable to a third party unconnected with the provision of the travel services included in the package travel contract and is unforeseeable or unavoidable; or (c) due to unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances." Art. 3(12) defines the last as "a situation beyond the control of the party who invokes such a situation and the consequences of which could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken". [full-text]
- Art. 14(4): outside the international conventions, "the package travel contract may limit compensation to be paid by the organiser as long as that limitation does not apply to personal injury or damage caused intentionally or with negligence and does not amount to less than three times the total price of the package." [full-text]
- Art. 14(6): "The limitation period for introducing claims under this Article shall not be less than two years." [full-text]

**Whether the provisions can be excluded by contract**

- Art. 23(1): "A declaration by an organiser of a package or a trader facilitating a linked travel arrangement that he is acting exclusively as a travel service provider, as an intermediary or in any other capacity, or that a package or a linked travel arrangement does not constitute a package or a linked travel arrangement, shall not absolve that organiser or trader from the obligations imposed on them under this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 23(2): "Travellers may not waive the rights conferred on them by the national measures transposing this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 23(3): "Any contractual arrangement or any statement by the traveller which directly or indirectly waives or restricts the rights conferred on travellers pursuant to this Directive or aims to circumvent the application of this Directive shall not be binding on the traveller." [full-text]
- Recital 46: organisers "may not escape from their obligations by claiming that they are simply acting as a travel service provider, an intermediary, or in any other capacity." [full-text]

**Enforcement and dates**

- Art. 24: "Member States shall ensure that adequate and effective means exist to ensure compliance with this Directive." Art. 25 leaves penalties to the Member States, which must be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive". The Directive attaches no specific remedy to a missing or unanswered contact point. [full-text]
- Art. 28: Member States were to adopt measures "by 1 January 2018" and "apply those measures from 1 July 2018". Art. 29: "Directive 90/314/EEC is repealed with effect from 1 July 2018." [full-text]

**What the text does not contain** (whole-text searches of the saved file, 11 Nov 2026)

- No provision says who or what must answer at the contact point. "natural person" occurs twice, in recital 7 (who counts as a traveller) and in the definition of trader (art. 3(7)); "human" occurs once, in "human health" (recital 31); "automat" does not occur. [full-text]
- No provision obliges the organiser to supervise or monitor its suppliers. "monitor" does not occur; "supervision" occurs only for the supervision of organisers by Member States (recital 42, art. 18(2)). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Packages only.** The whole of Chapter IV attaches to a "package travel contract". A hotel room booked directly, with its meals, cleaning, pool and spa, is one travel service (recitals 15, 17). The organiser's duties are owed by whoever combined and sold the package, which may be a tour operator, an online agent or the hotel itself where it sells the room together with, say, a ski pass that is an essential feature (recital 18). The hotel that merely supplies the room to an organiser is a "travel service provider"; the Directive gives the traveller nothing against it and leaves it to the organiser's redress (art. 22).
- **The 2015 text requires a contact point to be named, not answered.** Art. 7(2)(d) is an information duty about the content of the contract. The duties to act are art. 13(3) (remedy) and art. 16 (assistance). The duty to be "easily contactable" comes only with Directive (EU) 2026/1024 (card eu2026package).
- **Nothing requires a person, or a person with power to decide.** The contact may be a "local representative", "a contact point" or "another service". K7's conclusion asks for a channel "to such a person", one with power over the output in the single case. The Directive stops at speed and efficiency of communication.
- **The duty to remedy gives way to cost.** Art. 13(3)(b) releases the organiser where remedy "entails disproportionate costs"; the traveller then has price reduction and damages (art. 14). K7's timing line turns only on whether performance is still possible. The statute adds a second exit.
- **The scheme works by liability, not by oversight.** The organiser answers for the result whoever performs (art. 13(1)), and learns of failures from the traveller (art. 13(2)). The Directive states no duty to watch suppliers and no principle like K7's P3. It enacts the conclusion for one sector without the premise.
- **Defences and caps remain** (art. 14(3), (4)): the traveller's own fault, an unconnected third party, unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances; and a contractual cap of not less than three times the price for loss other than personal injury or damage caused intentionally or negligently.
- **Business travel under a framework agreement is excluded** (art. 2(2)(c)), which matters for guests placed by an employer.
- **Not a consolidated text.** I read the 2015 Official Journal version. Directive (EU) 2025/2647 is named in the 2026 directive (footnote 8) as amending this Directive after the end of the online dispute resolution platform; I did not read it.
- **Comparison with the Directive it replaced** (Directive 90/314, art. 5(2), read only as quoted in kuoni2021cjeu, para 7): the old defence of "an event which the organiser and/or retailer or the supplier of services, even with all due care, could not foresee or forestall" has no counterpart in art. 14(3). This is my comparison of the two wordings, not a statement any source makes. CV-C (11 Nov) leaves this OPEN and leaning against: art. 14(3)(c), read with the definition of unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances in art. 3(12) (reg. 16(4)(c) with reg. 2(1) in the UK), is the nearest analogue. Do not say the old defence was abolished.
- Later treatment not checked, apart from Directive (EU) 2026/1024.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K8 (holder under delegation): supports the contract limb, for packages.** A statute makes the party that sold the combined trip answerable for its performance by other firms (art. 13(1)), gives the reason (one responsible trader; recitals 22, 23), forbids the label "intermediary" as a way out (art. 23(1)), and sends the suppliers' share to redress between firms (art. 22). That matches K8's allocation: the seller answers to the traveller, and suppliers who deal only with the seller answer to the seller. It narrows K8 in two ways. The rule is statutory and sectoral, and where the Directive does not apply (a stay sold alone; a linked travel arrangement, art. 19(2)(a)) each provider answers only for its own service. And the organiser keeps three defences and a cap (art. 14(3), (4)).

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports the conclusion as enacted law, for packages; narrows it on the person and on cost.** The Directive joins three things K7 joins: one answerable party (art. 13(1)), a channel from the traveller to that party for use during the trip (art. 7(2)(d); art. 15), and a duty to correct while the trip is still running (art. 13(3)-(6); art. 16), with money as the fallback (art. 14). It treats the traveller's own report as the signal (art. 13(2); recital 34), which is P4's assumption written into law, though it is no evidence that P4 is true. It does not state P3, does not require a person with authority at the end of the channel, and lets the organiser decline a remedy that costs too much.

**K3 (grounds), in passing.** The recitals rest the organiser's liability on assumed responsibility (recital 8) and on what the traveller may reasonably expect from how the services are presented (recital 10), and art. 23(1) removes disclaimer by notice. That is support for K3's first two grounds in one statute.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- EU package-travel law makes the organiser of a package responsible for the travel services in it whether the organiser or another provider performs them (art. 13(1)).
- The Directive gives as its reason that a package has one trader responsible for the whole, and that the earlier law left it unclear which trader was liable (recitals 22, 23).
- The organiser cannot avoid these obligations by declaring itself an intermediary, and the traveller cannot waive them (art. 23).
- The package contract must name a local representative, contact point or other service through which the traveller can reach the organiser quickly, to ask for help or to complain of a failure perceived during the trip (art. 7(2)(d)).
- The traveller must report a failure without undue delay; the organiser must then remedy it unless that is impossible or disproportionately costly; and if the organiser does not act within a reasonable period the traveller sets, the traveller may put it right and recover the cost, at once where immediate remedy is needed (art. 13(2)-(4)).
- The organiser must give appropriate assistance without undue delay to a traveller in difficulty (art. 16).
- The traveller may send messages and complaints to the retailer that sold the package, and receipt by the retailer counts as receipt by the organiser (art. 15).
- The traveller is entitled to a price reduction for any period of non-conformity unless it is attributable to the traveller, whether or not the failure was remedied (art. 14(1)), and to compensation, subject to three defences (art. 14(2)-(3)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That this law applies to a hotel booked directly, or to a guest's dealings with the hotel as such. It applies to packages, and a stand-alone stay is not one (art. 3(2); recitals 15, 17, 18).
- That the Directive gives the traveller a right to reach a person, or a person with authority to decide. It requires a contact point or service that allows quick and efficient communication (art. 7(2)(d)).
- That the 2015 Directive obliges the organiser to be reachable. It obliges the contract to name a contact point. The duty to be "easily contactable" is in the 2026 amendment, which applies from 29 March 2029 (card eu2026package).
- That the organiser must remedy every failure during the trip. The duty yields to impossibility and to disproportionate cost (art. 13(3)).
- That the Directive imposes a duty to supervise or monitor suppliers. It imposes liability for the result.
- That the traveller has a claim against the hotel under this Directive.
- That the consequence of the traveller's silence is in the articles. It is stated only in recital 34.
