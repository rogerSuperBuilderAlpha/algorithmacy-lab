## Summary

The Directive governs automated decision systems used by Canadian federal institutions to make administrative decisions about clients. Appendix B grades a decision's impact in four levels by how reversible and lasting it is; Appendix C sets requirements by level, including human involvement.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Scope: 'This directive applies to any automated decision system in production used to make an administrative decision or a related assessment about a client.' (5.1) [full-text]
- Impact levels (Appendix B): Level I, 'little to no, easily reversible, and brief impacts'; Level II, 'moderate, likely reversible and short-term impacts'; Level III, 'high, difficult to reverse and potentially ongoing impacts'; Level IV, 'very high, irreversible and perpetual impacts'. [full-text]
- Human involvement (Appendix C, section 6.3.13): at Levels I and II, 'The system may make decisions and assessments without direct human involvement'; at Levels III and IV, 'The final decision must be made by a human.' [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Binds federal institutions only, for administrative decisions; says nothing of private firms or hospitality.
- Ties a human's final decision to the impact level, which an algorithmic impact assessment sets (6.1.2; Appendix B) and whose descriptors turn on how reversible and lasting the impacts are. It also requires recourse that is 'timely, effective, and easy to access' (6.4.1.1) and the use of 'documented feedback ... to identify issues and take corrective actions' (6.3.4.1). It binds the institutions subject to the Policy on Service and Digital (8.1): it is not soft law.

## Where it bears on the argument

- **Timing: in print for public administration.** The paper's boundary (a route while correction can still help) has an official counterpart that indexes human decision to how reversible the impact is.

**What the paper may say**

- Canada's Directive on Automated Decision-Making requires that the final decision be made by a human only where a decision's impacts are 'high, difficult to reverse' or 'very high, irreversible' (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat 2025: Appendices B, C).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the Directive gives the person affected no route: it requires recourse (6.4.1, 6.4.1.1).
- That it is soft law, or that reversibility alone sets the level.

- That it applies to hosts, to private firms, or outside Canadian federal administration.
