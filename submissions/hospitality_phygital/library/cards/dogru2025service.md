## Summary

Not read beyond the abstract. On the abstract, a conceptual paper proposing that organizations of any kind, 'from corporations and nonprofits to governmental institutions', can practise hospitality as a virtue and so drive social change. It applies an 'E3 framework' of hospitality virtue (empathy, execution, embrace) to five organizations, frames their impact against the United Nations sustainable development goals, and ends with a research agenda. Its stated gap is that hospitality as a virtue is understood for persons and not for organizations.

## Critical notes & threats

- **What the abstract says about a host's duty.** Nothing. It does not use the words host, guest, duty, obligation or responsibility. Its subject is what organizations can achieve for society by adopting hospitality as a value, not what a hospitality business owes the people it receives.
- **Register.** Virtue, used instrumentally: organizations 'leverage' and 'operationalize' it in the abstract and 'harness' it in the title. The abstract makes no claim that organizations are bound.
- **Holder.** Organizations, explicitly, as bearers of a virtue. The five named cases include a United Nations agency and, by its name, one tour company; none is named as a hotel, restaurant or lodging business. Whether the article discusses hotels at all cannot be told from the abstract.
- **Nearness to the paper.** It shares the paper's level (the organization) and reverses its move. v6 argues that an account built on motive cannot follow the obligation under delegation because a motive belongs to a person. This abstract proposes that the virtue can belong to an organization. If the article shows how, v6's sentence needs an answer; if it only asserts it, the sentence stands. The abstract does not show how.
- **Same school.** The scout found the same authors' group publishing on hospitality as virtue (Line et al. 2025; Mody 2023; both closed, abstracts only in the scout's report). A reviewer from that group is a plausible reader of this paper.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K2 (rival at abstract depth).** K2's second refuter is 'hospitableness scholars who already assign the host's duty to an organization. One such source defeats the gap statement'. This abstract assigns a virtue, and no duty, to organizations, so on the abstract it does not defeat the gap statement. It does show that 'hospitality at the organizational level' is a named topic with a stated gap of its own, and K2's wording should not suggest that the organizational level is unoccupied.

**K2's premise (collides).** K2 treats hospitableness as the manner in which a role is performed and holds that the duty attaches to the role. This abstract treats hospitality itself as a virtue that scales from persons to organizations. The two framings compete for the same ground.

**Verdict for the dossier.** Open on whether the article assigns any duty to an organizational host: the deciding source is closed.
