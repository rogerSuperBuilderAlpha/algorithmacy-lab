## Summary

Twelve ethnographic interviews (September to November 2022) with de-platformed Instagram and TikTok users: sex workers, sex educators, LGBTQIA+ creators, activists and a journalist. Participants experience appeals as opaque, glitching and "dysfunctional". Recovery depends on contacts and clout, not on the formal process. Restored accounts carry a lingering "criminal record" in the form of shadowbanning.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Design: "12 virtual, ethnographic interviews with Instagram and TikTok users" (p. 2002); interviews "between September and November 2022" (p. 2003) [full-text]
- Appealing into a void: participants "lamented being stuck in 'algorithmic loops' of trying to explain the situation over and over, without any success – they felt they were appealing into a void" (p. 2005); "I don't bother doing conventional appeals anymore 'cause I never hear back" (Reed, p. 2005) [full-text]
- Recovery depended on networks, not process: "When participants did manage to recover their account, it was therefore often thanks to friends in high places" (p. 2006); "It shouldn't be dependent on clout" (Rob*, p. 2006) [full-text]
- Persistence sometimes worked: "So I think it's just the utter persistence" (Malli, p. 2004) [full-text]
- Metaphor, not a structural finding: "an algorithmic cop seizes their content ... Subsequently, an invisible algorithmic judge and jury of one or more moderators" (p. 2010) [full-text]
- Appeals exist but fail: the system "is a case study in the failure of due process" (p. 2011) [full-text]

## Support verdicts (rr-2026-11)

- v4 §6: "Knowing how to file an appeal is not the same as having a right to an answer (Vaccaro et al. 2020; Are 2025)." SUPPORTED: users who used every option heard nothing back (p. 2005), and recovery tracked clout and contacts (p. 2006). Caveat: one participant credits "utter persistence" (p. 2004), so the evidence is not that skill never works. It shows that working depends on someone at the platform choosing to act.
- v4 §8: "individual skill cannot create institutional rights, authority, or responsibility (Vaccaro et al. 2020; Are 2025)". SUPPORTED on the same evidence (pp. 2005-2006).
- Old card claim "The same actor investigates, judges and decides the appeal": PARTLY. "cop, judge and jury" is the author's metaphor. The exact string "an algorithmic cop, judge and jury" is on p. 2011; p. 2010 reads "an algorithmic cop, algorithmic judge and jury", and the abstract has "'algorithmic cop, jury and judge'" (p. 1997). In the p. 2010 account the algorithm is the cop and moderators the judge and jury, so it does not literally describe a single actor.

## Critical notes & threats

- Sample of 12, recruited partly through the author's own following of over 400,000 (p. 2003); the author flags positionality and recruitment bias (p. 2003).
- Content moderation, not commercial service; the guest-side version of this study does not exist, which is a gap the discussion can claim.
