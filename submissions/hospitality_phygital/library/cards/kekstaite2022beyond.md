## Summary

Kekstaite interviewed eighteen Brussels residents who sheltered 'illegalised' migrants in their own homes through the Citizens' Platform in 2020. She grants Derrida's account of hospitality as a vertical relation in which the host's sovereignty brings hostility with it, says the account 'falls short' of guidance for everyday relations, and adds feminist care ethics (Hamington, Rosello, Young, Chamberlain) as a complement. Her analysis has three parts. Hosting becomes a personal, sometimes reciprocal relation in which the roles of host and guest shift; hosts use conscious strategies to soften the verticality of the relation, while dependency and gender keep reproducing it; and the hosting network forms an 'affirmative politics' against the Belgian state's inhospitality, even as hosts suspect that they do the state's work for it. Only hosts were interviewed. The migrants' own view of the relation is absent by design.

## Critical notes & threats

- **Non-commercial, and the guests are not heard.** The hosts are private residents sheltering people the state refuses; nobody pays, and the migrants were not interviewed (AM p. 7). The article is evidence for how hosts describe their power and their efforts to soften it. It is not evidence for what guests could say or do.
- **Derrida at second hand, with loose 'ibid.' chains.** The p. 77 sentence is a paraphrase. The quoted phrase "ethics itself" is attributed by 'ibid.' to *Of Hospitality* (AM p. 3), which may not be its source; "giving something determinate" is "Derrida 2003: 129, as quoted in Bulley 2017" (AM p. 4). Use the article for what Kekstaite says Derrida holds, never for Derrida's words.
- **'Contestations and conflicts' is cited to 'Lynch 2011'** (AM p. 9), whose reference is the founding editorial (`lynch2011theorizing`). That editorial in turn reports Dikeç (2002), which this run could not read. Kekstaite's sentence treats contestation as a risk that comes with hospitality, not as a route the guest is given.
- **Bibliography error.** `literature/references.bib` gives the author as 'Kekstaite, Jurgita'; Crossref gives 'Julija'. Its note also records read depth as abstract. Flagged for the authors in `WS_P1_R9.md`; I did not edit the bib.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K1 and Step 1, warrant 5 (Derrida's conditional laws): narrows.** Kekstaite reads p. 77 as saying that the plural laws "set limits, mark powers, rights, and duties" (AM pp. 3-4). Westmoreland quotes the sentence she paraphrases: the laws, "in marking limits, power, rights, and duties", consist "in challenging and transgressing the law of hospitality" (`westmoreland2008interruptions`, p. 8). Two independent carriers therefore attach 'power' to the same list that contains 'rights and duties'. The dossier may say that Derrida places rights and duties in the conditional laws; it may not gloss those laws as 'not against hospitality' without the second half. Kekstaite adds, with no page, that the laws "would cease to exist" without the guidance of the unconditional law (AM p. 4).

**K7 (the channel): gives the problem and a footing for the guest's side, not the host's duty.** The article treats the guest's negotiation of the host's power as part of hospitality (Rosello as reported, AM p. 6) and treats contestation in "daily mundane interaction" as an opening hospitality provides (AM p. 9). That is voice as a counter to power, which is closer to the critical scholar's S1 reading than to the dossier's derivation of the channel from the host's need to perform. It says nothing of a route kept by the host, of correction, or of commercial hosts.

**K8 (several hosts): an image, not an allocation.** A host says the network does "the work of the state" (AM p. 12). The state's withdrawal makes private households hosts. The article does not ask which of them answers to the guest.

**What the paper may say**

- Kekstaite, in *Hospitality & Society*, reads Derrida's conditional laws as laws that "set limits, mark powers, rights, and duties" (AM pp. 3-4), and the paper may cite her, with Westmoreland, for the point that the laws mark power as well as duty.
- She treats hospitality as a relation in which hosts "impose conditions and demands upon their guests" (AM p. 1) and which "can also create circumstances for contestations and conflicts" (AM p. 9).
- Following Rosello, she holds that the guest "has agency to appropriate hospitality language and negotiate hospitality relationships" (AM p. 6).

**What the paper may not say**

- That Kekstaite quotes Derrida on p. 77 or p. 25. She paraphrases.
- That the article reports guests' experience of complaint, contestation or exit. Only hosts were interviewed.
- That it supports a host's duty to keep a channel, or says anything about commercial hosts.
