## Summary

The convention, signed at Paris on 17 December 1962, obliges each contracting state to make its national law conform, within twelve months of entry into force for that state, to rules on hotel-keepers' liability set out in an annex. Under the annex a hotel-keeper is liable for damage to, destruction of or loss of property brought to the hotel by a guest who stays there and has sleeping accommodation at his disposal. Liability is capped at 3,000 gold francs and is unlimited where the property was deposited with the hotel-keeper, where he refused property he was bound to receive, or where he or a person for whom he is responsible was at fault. Notices and agreements made beforehand to exclude or reduce the liability are void. The guest loses the benefit if he does not inform the hotel-keeper without undue delay. States may impose greater liabilities and may take five listed options. The instrument deals with property only.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- **Form.** A Council of Europe treaty: "Each Contracting Party undertakes that, within twelve months of the date of entry into force of the present Convention in respect of that Party, its national law shall conform with the rules on the liability of hotel-keepers concerning the property of their guests set out in the annex to this Convention." (Convention, art. 1(1)) [full-text]
- **A floor.** "Each Contracting Party shall nevertheless remain free to impose greater liabilities on hotel-keepers." (Convention, art. 1(2)) [full-text]
- **Stated purpose.** The preamble gives harmonisation as the reason: "Believing it expedient to harmonise certain rules relating to the liability of hotel-keepers concerning the property of their guests". No other ground for the liability is stated. [full-text]
- **Date and languages.** "Done at Paris, this 17th day of December 1962, in English and in French, both texts being equally authoritative" (testimonium). It comes into force "three months after the date of the deposit of the third instrument of ratification or acceptance" (Convention, art. 4(2)). [full-text]
- **The liability.** "A hotel-keeper shall be liable for any damage to or destruction or loss of property brought to the hotel by any guest who stays at the hotel and has sleeping accommodation put at his disposal." (Annex, art. 1(1)) [full-text]
- **What counts as brought to the hotel.** Property "which is at the hotel during the time when the guest has the accommodation at his disposal"; property "of which the hotel-keeper or a person for whom he is responsible takes charge outside the hotel" during that period; and property of which they take charge "during a reasonable period preceding or following the time when the guest has the accommodation at his disposal" (Annex, art. 1(2)(a)–(c)). [full-text]
- **The cap.** "The liability shall be limited to the equivalent of 3 000 gold francs." (Annex, art. 1(3)); the gold franc is defined in art. 1(4). [full-text]
- **When liability is unlimited.** "The liability of a hotel-keeper shall be unlimited: a where the property has been deposited with him; b where he has refused to receive property which he is bound to receive for safe custody." (Annex, art. 2(1)) [full-text]
- **A duty to receive valuables.** "A hotel-keeper shall be bound to receive securities, money and valuable articles; he may only refuse to receive such property if it is dangerous or if, having regard to the size or standing of the hotel, it is of excessive value or cumbersome." (Annex, art. 2(2)) [full-text]
- **Exclusions.** No liability so far as the loss is due "to the guest or any person accompanying him or in his employment or any person visiting him", "to an unforeseeable and irresistible act of nature or an act of war", or "to the nature of the article" (Annex, art. 3). Vehicles, property left with a vehicle and live animals are outside (Annex, art. 7). [full-text]
- **Fault lifts the cap, and the hotel-keeper answers for others.** "The hotel-keeper shall be liable and shall not have the benefit of the limitation on his liability laid down in paragraph 3 of Article 1 of this annex where the damage, destruction or loss is caused by a wilful act or omission or negligence, on his part or on the part of any person for whose actions he is responsible." (Annex, art. 4) [full-text]
- **What the guest must do.** "Except in any case to which Article 4 of this Annex applies, the guest shall cease to be entitled to the benefit of these provisions if after discovering the damage, destruction or loss he does not inform the hotel-keeper without undue delay." (Annex, art. 5) [full-text]
- **Notices are void.** "Any notice or agreement purporting to exclude or diminish the hotel-keeper's liability given or made before the damage, destruction or loss has occurred shall be null and void." (Annex, art. 6) [full-text]
- **Options left to states.** A state may limit liability "to at least 100 times the daily charge for the room" (Convention, art. 2(a)); set a limit per article (art. 2(b)); confine annex art. 1(2) to "property which is at the hotel" (art. 2(c)); permit a reduction of liability, in deposit and fault cases short of intent, "by an agreement with the guest signed by him and containing no other terms" (art. 2(d)); and deal with vehicles and animals as it sees fit (art. 2(e)). [full-text]
- **The French text names the guest a traveller.** The French title, printed in the Dutch treaty bulletin, is "Convention sur la responsabilité des hôteliers quant aux objets apportés par les voyageurs", and annex art. 1(1) speaks of "le voyageur qui y descend et y dispose d'un logement" (Tractatenblad 1963 no. 44). [full-text]
- **Persons and admission.** Neither the convention nor the annex contains any rule on the guest's person or any duty to admit a guest. "Hotel", "hotel-keeper" and "a person for whom he is responsible" are not defined. [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **It binds states.** A guest takes nothing from the convention itself; the guest's claim arises under the national law that implements it. The paper can cite it for what the contracting states agreed the rule should be.
- **Parties and status unverified.** I could not establish which states ratified or whether the convention is in force for any state named in the paper. The date 15 February 1967 comes from a university library's copy and from no official record.
- **The guest is defined by lodging.** Annex art. 1(1) asks only that the guest "stays at the hotel and has sleeping accommodation put at his disposal". It mentions neither payment nor a contract. A day visitor to the restaurant is outside; a person lodged at another's expense is, on the words, inside. That is my reading; no commentary was read.
- **Liability without fault is a reading of the structure.** Art. 1(1) imposes liability without mentioning fault and art. 4 makes fault the condition for losing the cap.
- **"A person for whom he is responsible" is left to national law.** The annex does not say whether a contractor's staff are such persons.
- **The gold franc.** The cap is stated in a unit of gold; its present value was not worked out.
- **Close to BGB §§ 701–703.** The German sections follow the same pattern (card bgb701). Whether they implement this convention was not checked.
- **Later treatment not checked.** No explanatory report, national implementing law or case applying the annex was read.

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K4 (two tiers): supports the goods limb of reception across the legal traditions of the Council of Europe's members; nothing on admission or on the person.** Read with the Digest and the BGB, it shows that the civil-law support for K4 reaches goods only.
- **K5 (directedness): supports in form.** The rules run between hotel-keeper and "the guest", who is "entitled to the benefit of these provisions" (Annex, art. 5). The entitlement exists in national law.
- **K3 (grounds): narrows voluntary assumption.** A liability that no notice or prior agreement can exclude (Annex, art. 6) is laid on the hotel-keeper by law. The role fixes holder, trigger and content here; the hotel-keeper's consent does not make it bind.
- **K7 (channel): two narrow analogues for goods, as in the BGB.** The guest's prompt report to the hotel-keeper is the condition of the guest's protection (Annex, art. 5). The hotel-keeper must respond to one kind of request, to take valuables, and loses the cap if he refuses (Annex, art. 2(1)(b), 2(2)). The annex places the burden of reporting on the guest and says nothing of how the hotel-keeper is to be reached.
- **K8 (holder): supports.** The hotel-keeper answers for "any person for whose actions he is responsible" (Annex, art. 4) and for property that "a person for whom he is responsible takes charge" of (Annex, art. 1(2)).

**What the paper may say** (citing article):
- A 1962 Council of Europe convention set common rules under which a hotel-keeper is liable for loss of or damage to the property of a guest who has sleeping accommodation at the hotel, up to a cap, and without the cap where he or a person for whom he is responsible was at fault (Annex, arts 1(1), 1(3), 4).
- Under those rules, subject to the state option in Convention art. 2(d), a notice or prior agreement excluding the liability is void (Annex, art. 6).
- The hotel-keeper is bound to receive a guest's valuables for safe custody, unless they are dangerous or, given the hotel's size or standing, of excessive value or cumbersome (Annex, art. 2(2)).
- Except in any case to which Article 4 applies, the guest loses the protection unless he informs the hotel-keeper without undue delay after discovering the loss (Annex, art. 5).
- The convention is confined to property; it has no rule on the guest's person or on admission (title; Annex, arts 1–7).

**What the paper may not say:**
- That the convention gives guests rights directly, or that it is in force in a named state, without a check of the Treaty Office record.
- That any state named in the paper ratified it.
- That it shows a European duty to receive guests or to protect them.
- That it requires hotels to be reachable or to provide a contact point.
- That contractors' staff are persons "for whom he is responsible".
