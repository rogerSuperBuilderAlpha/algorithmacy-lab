## Summary

Reviews coordination research across organizational settings and identifies three integrating
conditions that any coordinating mechanism must produce: accountability, which establishes who
answers for what; predictability, which lets participants anticipate what will happen; and common
understanding, which gives participants a shared picture of the situation and of one another. This
is the published anchor from which the paper derives both its augmentative/substitutive condition
and its five affordances.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Names accountability, predictability and common understanding as the integrating conditions of any coordinating mechanism [abstract]
- Published in *Academy of Management Annals* 3:1, pp. 463–502 [metadata]
- Derives the conditions inductively from the literature: "We identified three integrating conditions for coordination: accountability, predictability, and common understanding." (p. 483) [full-text]
- Accountability: "Accountability addresses the question of who is responsible for specific elements of the task." (p. 483); restated as "the integrating condition that establishes who is responsible for particular aspects of the task" (p. 486) [full-text]
- Accountability need not be hierarchical: it "is not only attained through hierarchical authority, as in the traditional coordination literature, but can also be achieved in other ways, such as lateral interactions in meetings or through public status reports" (p. 483) [full-text]
- Predictability: "Predictability, the second integrating condition, enables interdependent parties to anticipate subsequent task related activity by knowing what the elements of the task are and when they happen." (p. 486) [full-text]
- Common understanding: "Common understanding is high when participants in an interdependent activity share knowledge of the work that is to be done, how it is to take place, and the goals and objectives of the work." (p. 488) [full-text]
- Relations among the conditions: "we found that accountability supports predictability" and accountability and common understanding have "a seemingly additive effect"; the one substitution they report is between the other two — "common understandings can replace predictability and vice versa" (p. 490) [full-text]
- Hierarchy as the accountability device: "By establishing authority, reporting lines, and unity of command, the design of hierarchies ensures that interdependencies and conflicts between different areas of the organization can be managed and given resolution" (p. 491) [full-text]
- "The integrating conditions of coordination are necessary, but are not always sufficient for coordination to occur." (p. 493) [full-text]
- The conditions erode and must be re-created; status differences are one eroding force, because "high-status groups may feel no need to recognize the task contributions of members of low-status groups" (p. 494) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Load-bearing in the strict sense: if this framing is wrong or misattributed, section 4's condition and section 6's derivation both collapse.
- It replaced an unpublished derivation that could not be cited. That substitution is what makes the affordances defensible to a referee, so the citation must be accurate.
- Full text should be read before submission — the paper leans on the three-condition scheme for two sections and currently cites it at abstract depth.
- (rr-2026-11) **The non-compensability claim is the authors', not O&B's.** The draft's "Accountability cannot be bought back with the other two" is consistent with p. 490 — the only substitution O&B report runs between predictability and common understanding, while accountability only *supports* the others — but O&B never say accountability is non-substitutable. Write it as the paper's inference from p. 490, not as a finding.
- (rr-2026-11) O&B's parties are interdependent workers coordinating a task (p. 483), and their accountability is task responsibility, including lateral and informal forms (p. 483). The draft already says the term "changes sense on the way in" (v4 §5 ¶2); the full text confirms that caveat is needed. The guest is not one of O&B's interdependent parties; the extension to host-guest relations is the authors'.
- (rr-2026-11) The "necessary but not always sufficient" line (p. 493) is the natural hook for the test: a touchpoint can have all three conditions and still fail as a welcome.
