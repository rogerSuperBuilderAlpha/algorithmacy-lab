## Summary

An industry analyst's attack on the "three-domain" approach of Lashley and Morrison (2000) and
Brotherton (1999). Slattery argues that commercial hospitality is a seller–buyer relation, that the
host–guest frame imported from the social and private domains degrades the industry, and that the
industrial, corporate and venue contexts render the approach redundant. It is the plainest
statement in the hospitality literature of the position that hospitality is a service business and
nothing more.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The denial the paper must answer: 'it is inaccurate and misleading to assume that in the hospitality business the critical relationship is between host and guest.' (p. 21) [full-text]
- 'The buyers are not guests, they are customers. The relationship is not philanthropic it is economic.' (p. 21) [full-text]
- Quotes Lashley's counter-claim, the one the paper sides with: 'on receiving genuine hospitality, the individual feels genuinely wanted and welcome. This is not the same thing as being welcomed as a valued customer' (p. 22, quoting Lashley and Morrison 2000: 13) [full-text]
- Quotes Brotherton and Wood's definition of hospitality as 'a contemporaneous human exchange, which is voluntarily entered into and designed to enhance the mutual well being of the parties concerned' (p. 23, quoting Lashley and Morrison 2000: 142) [full-text]
- Locates the distinctive work of the industry in managing consumption on site: 'Hospitality managers are centrally involved in the creation of the context of consumption and the management of consumption' (pp. 25-26) [full-text]
- Conclusion: 'By focusing only on the process of exchange in hospitality the three-domainers exclude the industrial and corporate contexts of hospitality and the diversity of venue contexts.' (p. 27) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Useful precisely because it is hostile. The editor wants the hospitality lens shown to differ from service management; Slattery is the field's own on-record claim that it does not, so the paper answers a named opponent rather than a straw one.
- The corporate context Slattery insists on (chains, brands, portfolio management, pp. 24-25) is where the paper locates the holder of the obligation. That turns his argument: the institution he says the three-domainers ignore is the party the paper says cannot hand the obligation to a system.
- Tone is polemical (the Taliban example, p. 20; "blind leading the blind", p. 27). Cite the argument, not the rhetoric.
- The Brotherton replies were not read. Do not characterise the exchange beyond Slattery's side.
