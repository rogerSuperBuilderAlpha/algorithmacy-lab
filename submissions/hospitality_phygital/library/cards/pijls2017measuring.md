## Summary

Scale-development paper for customers' experience of hospitality (Dutch samples, six service sectors), built across two qualitative and two quantitative studies into a 13-item scale with three factors: inviting, care, comfort. The exploratory phase found nine dimensions, one of them autonomy ("the level of control over what happens", p. 128), which the authors call new to the literature (p. 129). Of the autonomy items, "feeling independent" and "having choice" were dropped at the CFA; "experiencing freedom" survived inside the inviting factor.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- "The Experience of Hospitality Scale (EH-Scale) takes a broader perspective compared to existing scales, which predominantly measure hospitable behavior of service employees" (abstract) [abstract]
- Three factors: "the experience of inviting (open, inviting, freedom), the experience of care (servitude, empathy and acknowledgement), and the experience of comfort (feeling at ease, relaxed and comfortable)" (abstract) [abstract]
- "two qualitative and two quantitative studies resulted in the thirteen-item EH-Scale" (abstract) [abstract]
- Autonomy as a dimension: "The sixth experiential dimension of hospitality is autonomy: the level of control over what happens seems to be part of the experience of hospitality." (p. 128, Study 1 results); "the autonomy dimension suggested in the present study seems to be new" (p. 129, §4.3) [full-text]
- Autonomy attributes from the two qualitative studies: being in control, having influence, having choice, independence, freedom (Table 3, p. 129) [full-text]
- EFA (n = 434): "Experiencing freedom" loads .62 on inviting; "Feeling independent" and "Having choice" load .76 on comfort (Table 4, p. 130); "Variables of the welcome dimension and one variable of the autonomy dimension make up the inviting experiential factor" (p. 130) [full-text]
- CFA (n = 255): "To improve the fit of the model, two items with low loadings were removed one by one (the items of 'feeling independent' and 'having choice') resulting in a model that resulted in adequate model fit." (§5.2, pp. 130-131) [full-text]
- Final scale, inviting factor, item 3: "During my visit I experience freedom." (Appendix A, p. 132); CFA loading 0.57 (Table 5, p. 131) [full-text]
- Positions itself against employee-behaviour instruments: the scale "takes a broader perspective by focusing not only on the characteristics of the interaction with staff, but instead looks for aspects on a more abstract level of the organization" (p. 132, §7.1) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "When the validated scale of experienced hospitality was built, it kept inviting, care, and comfort. It also tried two questions about agency, whether the guest had a choice and whether they felt independent, and dropped both when the scale was tested (Pijls et al. 2017)." SUPPORT: YES at full text for the two sentences quoted (verifier_2's reading confirmed by Fable): "having choice" and "feeling independent" were separate items of the autonomy dimension and were the two items removed at the CFA (§5.2, pp. 130-131). Two cautions. (1) The source's word is "autonomy", not "agency". (2) The v4 follow-on, "It tried to measure something next to it, and the questions did not survive", overstates: a third autonomy item, "experiencing freedom", survived into the final scale inside the inviting factor (Table 5; Appendix A item 3), and the authors say autonomy is "new" to the hospitality literature (p. 129). The honest reading is that the scale absorbed autonomy into inviting rather than discarding it.
- Dutch samples only; the authors say the scale "cannot just be generalized to other nationalities" (p. 132, §7.2).
- The bib note says the instrument locates hospitality in guest-side acknowledgment, explicitly against employee-behaviour measures. The abstract confirms the second half.
