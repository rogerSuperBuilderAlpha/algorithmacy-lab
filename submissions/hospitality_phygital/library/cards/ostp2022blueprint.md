## Summary

A non-binding statement of five principles for automated systems. The fifth, 'Human Alternatives, Consideration, and Fallback', asks that people be able to opt out where appropriate and reach a person who can quickly consider and remedy problems, through a fallback and escalation process, and that in time-critical systems this be available before harm occurs.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The principle: 'You should be able to opt out, where appropriate, and have access to a person who can quickly consider and remedy problems you encounter.' (p. 46; also p. 7) [full-text]
- 'You should have access to timely human consideration and remedy by a fallback and escalation process if an automated system fails, it produces an error, or you would like to appeal or contest its impacts on you.' (p. 46) [full-text]
- Timing: 'In time-critical systems, this mechanism should be immediately available or, where possible, available before the harm occurs. Time-critical systems include, but are not limited to, voting-related systems, automated building access and other access systems, systems that form a critical component of healthcare ...' (p. 50) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Status.** A white paper from a previous US administration, archived; non-binding; no legal force.
- **Ground.** Rights of the person affected, not the duty of a party that performs through systems.

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K7 (the further duty): the nearest statement in print, in soft law.** It names a person able to 'consider and remedy', a fallback when the system 'fails', and availability 'before the harm occurs' for access systems. The paper's contribution is the derivation from the host's own duty, the host-guest relation, and the route usable without the host's goodwill.

**What the paper may say**

- A US government white paper of 2022 states that people should have 'access to a person who can quickly consider and remedy problems' with automated systems (OSTP 2022: 46), and that in time-critical systems, among them 'automated building access and other access systems', the mechanism should be available 'before the harm occurs' where possible (p. 50).

**What the paper may not say**

- That it is law, or that it binds anyone.
- That it derives the person's availability from a duty of the operator.
