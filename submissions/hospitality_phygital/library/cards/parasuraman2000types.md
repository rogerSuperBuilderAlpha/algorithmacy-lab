## Summary

Model of four types of automation (information acquisition, analysis, decision selection, action implementation), each at levels from fully manual to fully automatic, with human-performance consequences as the primary criterion for choosing.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- "automation can be applied to four broad classes of functions: 1) information acquisition; 2) information analysis; 3) decision and action selection; and 4) action implementation" (abstract) [abstract]
- "Within each of these types, automation can be applied across a continuum of levels from low to high, i.e., from fully manual to fully automatic." (abstract) [abstract]
- "automation does not merely supplant but changes human activity and can impose new coordination demands on the human operator" (abstract) [abstract]
- "The human performance consequences of particular types and levels of automation constitute primary evaluative criteria for automation design" (abstract) [abstract]
