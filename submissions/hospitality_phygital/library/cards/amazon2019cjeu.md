## Summary

A German federation of consumer organisations sought an injunction against Amazon EU because its website offered consumers email, a callback and a chat, showed telephone numbers only behind several clicks, and gave no fax number. The Bundesgerichtshof asked what art. 6(1)(c) of the Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU) requires when it tells a distance seller to give its telephone number, fax number and email address "where available" so that the consumer can "contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". The Court of Justice held two things. The provision "necessarily requires" a trader to put at every consumer's disposal a means of communication that allows quick contact and efficient communication. It does not require a telephone line, a fax line or a new email address, and national law may not demand a telephone number in all circumstances; a web form, a chat or a callback will do if it meets the standard of direct and effective communication. The Court reached that balance by weighing consumer protection against the competitiveness of undertakings and the freedom to conduct a business.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**The case**

- The proceedings: "an application for an injunction brought by the Federal Union, relating to Amazon EU practices for the display of information allowing consumers to contact that company." (para 2). The claimant is a federation of consumer organisations, not a consumer. [full-text]
- The provision as it then stood, quoted at para 10: before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, the trader shall provide "the geographical address at which the trader is established and the trader’s telephone number, fax number and e-mail address, where available, to enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". [full-text]
- What Amazon offered: consumers "had the choice between three options: namely, to send an email, to make contact by telephone or to start an online conversation by way of chat." Choosing the telephone opened a page on which the consumer "had the possibility to provide his telephone number and to be called back." (para 18). [full-text]
- The complaint: "Amazon EU did not respect its legal obligation to provide consumers with effective means to enter into contact with it", and "the callback service did not fulfil the information requirements, since consumers have to undertake a number of steps to make contact with that company." (para 20). [full-text]
- The referring court's worry: on the wider reading traders "would be required to change their business organisation and to employ new employees, which would be likely to undermine their freedom to conduct a business, enshrined in Article 16 and Article 17(1) of the Charter" (para 29). [full-text]

**What the Court required**

- Why contact matters: "the possibility, for consumers, to contact traders quickly and to communicate with them efficiently, as provided for in Article 6(1)(c) of Directive 2011/83, is of fundamental importance for ensuring and effectively implementing consumer rights and, in particular, the right of withdrawal" (para 41). [full-text]
- The information serves performance as well as formation: art. 6(1) seeks to ensure the communication of "information necessary for proper performance of that contract and, in particular, for the exercise of their rights, in particular the right of withdrawal" (para 43). [full-text]
- The requirement: "although Article 6(1)(c) of Directive 2011/83 does not determine the precise nature of the means of communication which must be established by traders, that provision necessarily requires traders to put at the disposal of all consumers a means of communication which allows the latter to contact them quickly and to communicate with them efficiently." (para 46). [full-text]
- The test is for the national court, "in the light of all the circumstances in which consumers make contact with traders through a website and in particular of the presentation and functionality of that site" (para 47). [full-text]
- Vulnerable consumers: traders "should take account of the specific needs of consumers who are particularly vulnerable because of their mental, physical or psychological infirmity, age or credulity in a way which the trader could reasonably be expected to foresee." (para 45, restating recital 34). [full-text]
- Operative part, second indent: "although that provision requires traders to make available to consumers a means of communication capable of satisfying the criteria of direct and effective communication, it does not preclude those traders from providing other means of communication than those listed in that provision in order to satisfy those criteria." (para 53 and ruling). [full-text]

**What the Court refused to require**

- The balance: "it is necessary to ensure the right balance between a high level of consumer protection and the competitiveness of undertakings, as is stated in recital 4 of that directive, while respecting the undertaking’s freedom to conduct a business, as set out in Article 16 of the Charter" (para 44). [full-text]
- No unconditional telephone duty: "an unconditional obligation to provide consumers, in all circumstances, with a telephone number, or even to put in place a telephone or fax line, or to create a new email address in order to allow consumers to contact traders seems to be disproportionate, in particular in the economic context of the functioning of certain undertakings, in particular small undertakings, which might seek to reduce their operating costs by organising sales or the provision of services at a distance or off-premises." (para 48). [full-text]
- "where available" covers "cases where traders have a telephone or fax number and do not use them solely for purposes other than contacting consumers. In the absence thereof, that provision does not impose on traders the obligation to inform consumers of that telephone number, to provide a telephone or fax line, or to create a new email address to allow consumers to contact them." (para 51). [full-text]
- Other means suffice: the provision "does not preclude traders from providing other means of communication than by telephone, fax or email in order to satisfy the criteria of direct and effective communication, such as, in particular, an electronic enquiry template, by means of which consumers can contact traders by means of a website and receive a written response or can be quickly called back." (para 52). A trader may encourage "instant messaging or a telephone callback system" (para 52). [full-text]
- Clicks are not fatal: "the fact that the telephone number is available only following a series of clicks does not, in itself, mean that the manner used is not clear and comprehensible" (para 52). [full-text]
- Operative part, first indent: art. 6(1)(c) precludes "national legislation, such as that at issue in the main proceedings, which imposes on traders, before concluding a distance or off-premises contract referred to in Article 2(7) and (8) of that directive, to provide, in all circumstances, their telephone number." It "does not imply an obligation for traders to establish a telephone or fax line, or to create a new email address to allow consumers to contact them" (para 53 and ruling). [full-text]

**What the judgment does not contain** (whole-text searches of the saved file, 11 Nov 2026)

- It does not say that a person must answer. "human", "natural person", "automat" and "staff" do not occur. The only reference to employees is the referring court's concern at para 29. It says nothing about who replies to the form, the chat or the callback, or with what authority. [full-text]
- It sets no time within which a trader must answer, beyond "quickly". [full-text]

**The Directive around it** (read in the texts saved at sources/eu2011consumer/)

- Scope, art. 3(3) of Directive 2011/83 as published in 2011: the Directive "shall not apply to contracts" which, among others, "fall within the scope of Council Directive 90/314/EEC" on package travel (point (g)) or are "concluded by means of automatic vending machines or automated commercial premises" (point (l)). The two amending directives in the folder (2019/2161, art. 4(2); 2023/2673, art. 1(1)) change other points of art. 3 and leave point (l) alone. I read no consolidated text and no ruling on what "automated commercial premises" covers. [full-text]
- The provision has since been rewritten. Directive (EU) 2019/2161, art. 4(4)(a)(i), replaces art. 6(1)(c) with: "the geographical address at which the trader is established as well as the trader’s telephone number and email address; in addition, where the trader provides other means of online communication which guarantee that the consumer can keep any written correspondence, including the date and time of such correspondence, with the trader on a durable medium, the information shall also include details of those other means; all those means of communication provided by the trader shall enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". The words "where available" are gone. Member States were to apply the change "from 28 May 2022" (art. 7(1)). [full-text]
- The only recital I found on the change, recital 46 of Directive 2019/2161: "it is necessary to remove the reference to fax number from the list of the means of communication in point (c) of Article 6(1) of Directive 2011/83/EU since fax is rarely used now and largely obsolete." [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **A pre-contract information duty.** Art. 6(1)(c) tells a distance or off-premises seller what to disclose "Before the consumer is bound" (para 10). The Court links it to performance and to the exercise of rights (paras 41, 43), above all withdrawal. It is not a duty to be reachable while a service is being performed, and the judgment concerns an online retailer of goods.
- **The Court does not require a person.** The standard is "direct and effective communication" (para 52). A written reply to a web form satisfies it. K7's conclusion asks for a channel to a person with power over the single case; this judgment stops two steps short, at a channel that works.
- **The Court weighs the channel against the trader's costs.** Para 48 treats cutting operating costs by selling at a distance as a legitimate interest, and para 44 puts the freedom to conduct a business in the balance. A reader could cite this judgment against K7 as readily as for it.
- **Enforcement is collective.** A consumer federation sued for an injunction (para 2). The case shows no individual claim to be answered.
- **The holding on the telephone number rests on wording that has changed.** The legislature rewrote art. 6(1)(c) in 2019 without "where available". Whether a telephone number is now required in all cases is a question for the amended text, which I read, and for later case law, which I did not look for. The general statement at para 46 does not depend on the deleted words.
- **Automated premises and packages are outside the Directive** (art. 3(3)(l), (g)). On its words a contract concluded at an unstaffed, automated hotel may fall outside the Consumer Rights Directive altogether. I read nothing that decides this; it is a caution for the paper, not a finding.
- The Opinion of Advocate General Pitruzzella, cited at para 50, was not read. Later case law not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports the first half of the conclusion and narrows the second.** The Court states as a requirement of EU consumer law that a trader must give all consumers a means to contact it quickly and communicate efficiently, and calls that possibility "of fundamental importance for ensuring and effectively implementing consumer rights" (paras 41, 46). That is law for the proposition that rights against a trader presuppose a way to reach it, which is the second route in K7 (a claim needs an addressee able to respond) more than the first. The Court then declines to say what the means must be, accepts forms, chat and callback, and counts the trader's costs (paras 44, 48, 52). Nothing in it supports a channel to a person, or to a person with authority.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- The Court of Justice has held that the Consumer Rights Directive "necessarily requires traders to put at the disposal of all consumers a means of communication which allows the latter to contact them quickly and to communicate with them efficiently" (para 46).
- The Court called the possibility of quick contact and efficient communication "of fundamental importance for ensuring and effectively implementing consumer rights" (para 41).
- The same judgment held that a trader need not maintain a telephone line and may meet the requirement by other means, such as a web form, a chat or a callback, provided they allow direct and effective communication (paras 48, 51, 52).
- The Court reached that result by balancing consumer protection against the competitiveness of undertakings and the freedom to conduct a business (para 44).

**What the paper may not say**

- That EU consumer law gives a consumer a right to speak to a person. The judgment does not say so and accepts written and automated-looking channels without asking who answers.
- That the judgment concerns hotels, services in progress, or complaints during performance. It concerns information given before a distance or off-premises contract.
- That the Court required a telephone number. It held the opposite on the wording then in force (para 53), and the wording has since changed.
- That the judgment gives an individual consumer a claim for an unanswered message.
- That the Consumer Rights Directive applies to a package holiday, or, without further authority, to a contract concluded at automated premises (art. 3(3)(g), (l)).
