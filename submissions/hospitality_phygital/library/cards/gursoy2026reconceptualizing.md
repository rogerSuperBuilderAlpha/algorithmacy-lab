## Summary

Conceptual paper that reframes hospitality co-creation from a dyad to a triad of customers,
employees and AI systems ("hybrid hospitality"), drawing on service-dominant logic,
sociomateriality and emotional labour. It reviews tensions (competence vs warmth; algorithmic
power and governance; emotional labour; phenomenology; service recovery; culture;
sustainability; ethics), recommends allocating competence to AI and warmth to staff with
human-led recovery, and sets an agenda on distributed agency, guest autonomy, employee
identity, experiential equity and the ethical governance of algorithmic actors.

## Critical notes & threats

**Settled: does Gursoy ask who owes the guest a welcome?** No, but he comes closer than the abstract suggested. He says AI "shifts responsibility" (pp. 155–156) and moves "authority from interpersonal judgment to computational logics, raising questions about accountability when harm, bias, or exclusion arise" (p. 160). That is the authority half of the paper's split, stated in this field. What he does not do: ask to whom accountability is owed or whether the guest can reach it, or treat the welcome as anything other than an experience ("the embodied sense of being welcomed", p. 162). His accountability is harm-indexed and governance-side (audits, transparency, consent, pp. 155, 159). The paper's differentiator holds, but §4 should cite p. 160 as the nearest statement and say what it leaves out. `must-engage` stays.

He also treats friction removal as a gain (p. 156). That fits v4 §7's claim that phygital frameworks keep smoothness as a goal.
