## Summary

The 'modernisation' Directive amends four consumer directives. Three of its changes matter here. First, art. 4(5) inserts art. 6a into the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU: before a consumer is bound by a distance contract on an online marketplace, the marketplace provider must tell the consumer the main ranking parameters, whether the third party offering the service is a trader, that consumer rights from Union law do not apply if it is not, and, where applicable, how obligations under the contract are shared between the third party and the marketplace, "without prejudice to any responsibility" either has under other Union or national law. Second, art. 4(4)(a)(i) replaces CRD art. 6(1)(c): the trader's pre-contract information must include its address, telephone number and email address, and "all those means of communication provided by the trader shall enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". Third, art. 3(5) inserts art. 11a into the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, giving consumers harmed by unfair practices access to remedies including compensation (card eu2005ucpd). Member States were to apply the measures from 28 May 2022 (art. 7(1)).

## Critical notes & threats

- **Information, not allocation.** Article 6a requires the marketplace to say who the supplier is and how obligations are shared. It does not allocate liability; point (d) is expressly "without prejudice to any responsibility" either party has under other law. T10's phrase that EU law "allocates between platform and supplier by disclosure" overstates: it requires disclosure of an allocation made by contract (recital 27) and leaves liability where other law puts it.
- **Non-trader hosts.** Where the host on a platform is not a trader, the guest must be told that "the consumer rights stemming from Union consumer protection law do not apply to the contract" (art. 6a(1)(c)). That confirms T7's point that consumer law reaches only trader hosts. The status is "on the basis of the declaration of that third party" (art. 6a(1)(b)) and the marketplace need not verify it (recital 28).
- **Pre-contract, distance contracts on marketplaces only.** Article 6a applies "Before a consumer is bound by a distance contract ... on an online marketplace" (art. 6a(1)). It says nothing about contact during the stay or about bookings made directly with a hotel.
- **The contact-means rule is the nearest CRD text to a channel, and it is pre-contract information.** CRD art. 6(1)(c) requires that the means the trader lists "enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". It is an information requirement for distance and off-premises contracts. Whether it implies a duty to answer during performance is not stated *(my reading; the Court of Justice's reading of the earlier version is on card amazon2019cjeu, which I did not re-read)*.
- **Package contracts.** The consolidated CRD excludes contracts on packages from most of its provisions (CRD art. 3(3)(g), read in the 27.9.2026 consolidation only for this point).
- Articles 1-2, the rest of arts 3-4 and most recitals not read.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K8 (holder under delegation), several firms: narrows T10.** EU consumer law asks the platform to tell the guest who the supplier is and how contractual obligations are shared (art. 6a(1)(b), (d)); it does not pick a holder, and it preserves "any responsibility" under other law (art. 6a(1)(d)). The dossier may say EU law requires the platform to disclose the allocation, not that it makes one.

**K4 (legal tiers): supports T7's narrowing.** Where the host is not a trader, the guest is to be told that Union consumer rights "do not apply to the contract" (art. 6a(1)(c)). The services tier built on consumer law covers trader hosts only.

**K7 (channel from the guest): narrows the legal gap.** The trader's listed means of communication must "enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently" (CRD art. 6(1)(c), as replaced). That is a channel requirement of a kind, attached to pre-contract information in distance and off-premises consumer contracts; it requires no person with power over the single case and says nothing of the stay.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- Since 28 May 2022 an online marketplace must tell a consumer, before the consumer is bound, whether the third party offering the service is a trader and, if not, "that the consumer rights stemming from Union consumer protection law do not apply to the contract" (Directive 2011/83/EU, art. 6a(1)(b)-(c), inserted by Directive (EU) 2019/2161, art. 4(5); applied from 28 May 2022, art. 7(1)).
- The marketplace must also say, where applicable, how obligations under the contract are shared between it and the supplier, "without prejudice to any responsibility" either has under other Union or national law (art. 6a(1)(d)).
- A trader's listed means of communication must "enable the consumer to contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently" (Directive 2011/83/EU, art. 6(1)(c), as replaced by Directive (EU) 2019/2161, art. 4(4)). (Narrowed after CV-G: this is pre-contract information only.)

**What the paper may not say**

- That EU law allocates responsibility for a stay between a platform and a host. Article 6a requires information about an allocation and preserves other law.
- That art. 6a requires any contact during the stay, or reaches a booking made directly with the host.
- That CRD art. 6(1)(c) requires a person with power to correct performance, or a response within any time.
