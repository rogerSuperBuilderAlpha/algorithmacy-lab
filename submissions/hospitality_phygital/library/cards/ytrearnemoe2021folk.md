## Summary

A qualitative thematic analysis of open-ended answers in a March 2019 representative survey of
Norwegians aged 16+ (N = 1363). Only the 59% (N = 804) who said they understood algorithms were
asked whether media companies' use of algorithms had positive or negative consequences; 553
replied. The authors identify five folk theories (algorithms are confining, practical,
reductive, intangible, exploitative) and propose "digital irritation" in place of "digital
resignation" for the dominant affect: people find algorithms annoying but inescapable.

## Critical notes & threats

- (rr-2026-11) The representativeness claim the card leaned on is weaker than stated. The survey is representative; the folk-theory material comes from the self-selected 59% who claimed understanding, and 553 of them answered (p. 812). Theories differ by age, gender, education and digital skill (pp. 812, 815, 819). The paper does not show folk theorizing is general across the population, and so cannot carry "it belongs to the form of mediation rather than to a particular population."
- (rr-2026-11) What the theories are: perceptions of the consequences of algorithms, elicited by one survey prompt. They are not theories of how to work a system, and the data do not show them being built "through repeated encounters."
