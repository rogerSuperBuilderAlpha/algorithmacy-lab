## Summary

Survey of UK hospitality workers using Hirschman's exit-voice-loyalty framework to ask what predicts intention to join a union (voice) and intention to quit (exit).

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Frame: "Drawing on the Hirschman's exit-voice-loyalty framework" (abstract) [abstract]
- "job dissatisfaction with intention to quit" were positively associated, while "there was no significant positive relationship between job dissatisfaction and intention to join a union" (abstract) [abstract]
- Design is a questionnaire survey of UK hospitality workers measuring intentions (abstract) [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "hospitality's own voice research finds that workers in the sector often prefer to leave quietly rather than speak up (Zientara et al. 2023)." SUPPORT: PARTIAL at abstract level. Dissatisfaction predicts intention to quit and does not predict intention to join a union, which is the exit-not-voice pattern. "Often prefer to leave quietly" overstates it: the study measures intentions, not preferences or behaviour, and says nothing about doing it quietly. Union membership is one narrow form of voice.
- Bib note says "dissatisfaction routes to exit and does not route to collective voice"; that wording fits the abstract better than the v4 sentence.
