## Summary

International-relations account of hospitality that argues host power does not end at the threshold: the distinction between inside and outside has to be policed and managed after entry. Illustrated with refugee camps, where the guest population is categorised and governed once inside.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Thesis: the inside/outside distinctions of hospitality "also need to be policed, managed and controlled, even after the threshold of the home is crossed" (accepted manuscript, PDF p. 3, introduction) [full-text]
- Poses the question v4 leans on: "Once inside, both host and guest are destabilized" and "how is the power of hospitality exercised to manage and control the home?" (PDF p. 11) [full-text]
- Camp example: assessment of guests is used "to enable their better categorisation and thus the management of their behaviour once inside" (PDF p. 15) [full-text]
- Crossref: Hospitality & Society 5(2), pp. 185-201; the bib entry and the paper's own cover page give 5(2-3), which is the printed issue [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "The host's power over the guest runs through the whole stay, and so does what the host owes (Bulley 2015)." SUPPORT: PARTIAL. The first half is directly supported: power is exercised after the threshold is crossed (PDF p. 3, p. 11). The second half, "what the host owes", is not what Bulley argues. I searched the text for owe, duty and obligation: the only hits are about international moral duties (PDF p. 4) and the camp setting, and Bulley analyses power, space and control, not a continuing host obligation. The "owes" clause rests on the authors' own inference. Fable (rr-2026-11): one qualification. Bulley does treat hospitality as ethics, 'ethical responsibility-taking for others' (PDF p. 7), and the abstract calls it 'a practice that continues once the guest crosses the threshold', so an ethical responsibility does run past the threshold in Bulley. A continuing host *obligation* in the commercial sense does not. The 'owes' half is weakly grounded, not absent.
- Setting is international hospitality (borders, asylum, camps), not commercial hospitality. The transfer to a hotel stay is the authors' move.
- Bib vs Crossref: Crossref lists issue "2" where the bib and the paper give "2-3". No other discrepancy (authors, title, year, volume, pages match).
