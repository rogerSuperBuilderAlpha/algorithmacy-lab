## Summary

The plaintiff, Mr Constantine, described in the report as "the well-known West Indian cricketer", sued the proprietors of the Imperial Hotel, London, for refusing to receive and lodge him on 30 July 1943. He was lodged the same night at the Bedford Hotel, which the same company owned. The defendants conceded that they were innkeepers, that the Imperial was a common inn, that he came as a traveller, that they had room, that he was ready to pay, and that they had no ground to refuse him. He had a contract for rooms but did not sue on it, and he neither pleaded nor proved special damage. The defendants argued that an action on the case needs proof of damage and that lodging him at their other hotel discharged their duty. Birkett J rejected both arguments. The traveller's right "is founded on the common law", its violation "imports damage", and the principle of *Ashby v White* applies. He refused exemplary or substantial damages and awarded nominal damages of five guineas.

[full-text]
- And that there was no ground of refusal: "that the plaintiff was a man of high character and that, although he was a man of colour, no ground existed on which the defendants were entitled to refuse to receive and lodge him at the hotel" (p. 694). [full-text]

- Headnote, first holding: "An action by a traveller against an innkeeper for wrongfully refusing to receive and lodge him is for the violation of a right at common law so that the action, although sounding in case, can be sustained without proof of special damage." (p. 693). [full-text]
- Headnote, second holding: where an innkeeper owning two inns refused a traveller at one and lodged him at the other, "Held, that the innkeeper was in breach of his duty at common law." (p. 694). [full-text]
- The second holding in the judge's words: "The claim before me is that the defendants refused to receive and lodge Mr. Constantine at the Imperial Hotel, and I hold it to be no answer that Mr. Constantine went to the Bedford Hotel under protest." (p. 697). [full-text]
- Common ownership did not help the defendants: "Apart from the fact that the two hotels were in the same ownership, no further evidence was given as to the relationship between them" (p. 697). [full-text]
- A Scottish judge's words, quoted with approval: "A traveller is, in my opinion, entitled to choose the hotel at which he desires to be a guest" (p. 697, Birkett J quoting Lord Anderson in *Rothfield v North British Railway Co.* 1920 SC 805, 812). [full-text]
- Source of the action: "This action was based on the common law and on what was termed the custom of the realm." (p. 698). [full-text]
- The public route noted in passing: "and, incidentally, an indictment may be brought, as in Reg. v. Sprague, which was brought so recently as 1891 at Surrey quarter sessions and shows that an innkeeper could be indicted, and, indeed, in that case was indicted, for not receiving a guest who was attired in what in those days was called rational dress" (p. 698). [full-text]
- A new point: "I find that I have yet to decide the express point in this case without any express authority to guide me." (p. 708). [full-text]
- The holding: "I hold this action by Mr. Constantine to be maintainable without proof of special damage. His right, I think, is founded on the common law. That right I found was violated. ## Critical notes & threats

- **What was decided and what was conceded.** The existence of the innkeeper's duty to receive, and every condition of it on these facts, was conceded (p. 694). The court decided three things: the defendants did refuse; lodging at their other hotel was no answer (p. 697); and the action lies without special damage because it vindicates a right at common law (p. 708). It is authority on the nature of the traveller's position, a right, and on who may sue. It is not a contested ruling on when the duty to receive arises.
- **First instance, new point.** A single judge, deciding "without any express authority" (p. 708). The reasoning is by analogy with *Ashby v White* (the right to vote), which I know here only as Birkett J quotes it.
- **The remedy is thin.** Five guineas, with exemplary damages refused although humiliation and distress were found (p. 708). The law vindicated the right after the event and did nothing at the desk.
- **The ground of refusal was race, and the case is not a discrimination ruling.** The defendants conceded they had no ground to refuse. The judgment turns on the innkeeper's duty to any traveller, not on a rule against racial discrimination.
- **Private right and public offence together.** Birkett J notes that an innkeeper may also be indicted (p. 698). The report gives two dates for *R v Sprague*: 1891 in the text and "(1899) 63 J. P. 233" in the footnote (p. 698). The discrepancy is in the printed page, not the OCR.
- **Scope.** Inns and travellers only. Nothing here extends the right to restaurants or bars; the refreshment bar in *R v Rymer* is the contrast (rymer1877queen).
- **OCR.** The saved text has many small misreadings in the argument and the long quotations (pp. 695-707). Quotations above come from pages checked against the images; line-break hyphens are mended, footnote markers inside quoted sentences are dropped, the printed space before semicolons is closed up, and "Reg.v." is given with its space restored.
- **Later treatment.** The scout reports citations in *Watkins v Home Office* (CA) and *Uzbekov v Revolut*; I read neither. Later treatment not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K5 (directedness): supports, strongly, for the duty to receive in England.** A refused traveller sued the innkeeper in his own name and won: "His right, I think, is founded on the common law. That right I found was violated." (p. 708; the headnote at p. 693 puts it as "a right at common law"). Birkett J places the action among actions on the case brought "certainly from very early times" (pp. 697-698), so the private action is much older than 1944; what was new was that it lay without special damage. This collides with the reading that the innkeeper's duty was public only (the Sandoval-Strausz lead in K5), at least for England in 1944. The same page records the indictment route (p. 698), so the duty was both.
- **K4 (admission tier): supports the conditions as conceded.** Common inn, traveller, room available, ready and willing to pay, no ground of refusal (p. 694).
- **K7 (channel): narrows.** The remedy was damages afterwards, and nominal (p. 708). This is an instance of the defeater K7 names: remedies doctrine satisfied by later damages.
- **K8 (holder): a small point for several-firm cases.** Performance at another establishment of the same owner did not discharge the duty owed at the inn the traveller chose (p. 697). The holder was "the proprietors" of the inn (p. 694).

**What the paper may say**

- A traveller wrongfully refused by an inn could sue the innkeeper in his own name without proof of special damage: Birkett J held the action "maintainable without proof of special damage" because "His right, I think, is founded on the common law" (p. 708). The words "a right at common law" are the headnote's (p. 693).
- The court said of the traveller: "His right, I think, is founded on the common law. That right I found was violated." (p. 708).
- Where the traveller went to the innkeeper's other hotel "under protest", and the only evidence of the relation between the hotels was common ownership, lodging him there was "no answer" to the refusal (p. 697).
- The same judgment notes that an innkeeper could also be indicted for refusing a guest (p. 698).
- The remedy was nominal damages of five guineas; the judge refused more although he found "much unjustifiable humiliation and distress" (p. 708).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the court awarded substantial or exemplary damages, or ordered the hotel to admit him.
- That the case decided the conditions of the duty to receive. They were conceded.
- That the case is a ruling against racial discrimination by hotels.
- That it shows a general right to be served by any hospitality business. It concerns a common inn and a traveller.
- Anything about *Ashby v White*, *Rothfield* or *R v Sprague* beyond what this report says of them.
