## Summary

The bill adds a chapter on customer service chatbots to California's Business and Professions Code. It binds a "large private business", one with more than 500 million dollars in gross annual revenue nationally, in its dealings with customers who are natural persons residing in California. Such a business may not present a chatbot as a human (s. 22626). During its regular business hours it must offer, on every platform through which it gives customer service, a simple feature by which a customer can ask to be connected to a customer service agent, who is a natural person (ss. 22625(c), 22627(a)). It must then make a good-faith effort to connect the customer within 15 minutes or to give an appointment within one business day (s. 22627(b)). A public prosecutor enforces the chapter by civil penalty, and the chapter "does not establish a private right of action" (s. 22628(a), (b), (d)). I read the enrolled bill. The Governor's office reports it signed on 28 September 2026; I could not reach the chaptered text.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Who is bound, and for whom**

- s. 22625(g): "“Large private business” means a business with more than five hundred million dollars ($500,000,000) in gross annual revenue nationally that provides goods and services to customers." [full-text]
- s. 22625(b): "“Customer” means a natural person residing in California." and "For purposes of paragraph (1), “residence” refers to the customer’s account address." The protected class is fixed by where the customer lives, not by where the service is performed. [full-text]
- s. 22625(j): "“Services” means services for which a customer will provide or has provided monetary consideration, whether through a one-time payment, subscription, fee, or recurring charge." Free tiers are excluded by the next sentence of the same subdivision. [full-text]
- s. 22625(c): "“Customer service agent” means a natural person who, as an employee, contractor, or representative of the large private business provides either of the following:" direct customer service "relating to the sale and delivery of goods and services" (1), or direct customer support, "including technical assistance functions" (2). The agent may be a contractor. The definition states a function and no authority. [full-text]
- s. 22625(d) defines a customer service chatbot as "an artificial intelligence system with a natural language interface that provides adaptive, human-like responses to user inputs" used for customer service or support. [full-text]
- s. 22625(i): "“Regular business hours” means the hours during which the business customarily maintains customer-service functions, excluding on days that the business is ordinarily closed or state or federal holidays. “Regular business hours” includes a 10-hour period per day over a five-day week or 8 hours per day for a full seven-day week." [full-text]

**What the business must do**

- s. 22626(a): "A large private business shall not represent that a customer service chatbot is a human." Subdivision (b) requires "a clear and conspicuous disclosure that the customer service chatbot is artificially generated and not human if a reasonable person interacting with the customer service chatbot is likely to be misled to believe that the person is interacting with a human." [full-text]
- The route to a person, s. 22627(a): "During at least a large private business’s regular business hours, a large private business shall provide customers who require customer assistance with goods or services offered by or provided from the large private business with a simple customer service feature on all platforms through which the large private business provides customer service, including online chatbot customer service and telephonic customer service platforms, that allows customers to request to connect to a customer service agent." The business complies if a customer can make the request "through commonly understood commands, prompts, menu selections, or verbal requests during the customer interaction." [full-text]
- When, s. 22627(b)(1): "A large private business shall make a good faith effort to provide a customer who requests to connect to a customer service agent through the feature described in subdivision (a) with one of the following: (A) Connection to a customer service agent within 15 minutes of the request. (B) A specific appointment time within one business day of the request at which a customer service agent will call or connect with the customer." [full-text]
- Hold times, s. 22627(c): "A large private business shall make a good faith effort to ensure a customer is not placed on hold or made to wait for more than 15 minutes at any time after connection with a customer service agent, and that cumulative hold or wait times or escalation queues do not exceed more than one hour total." [full-text]
- s. 22627(d): businesses serving California customers through online platforms that have a telephone service "shall post their telephonic customer service telephone number clearly and conspicuously on their internet website." [full-text]
- Limits written into the duty, s. 22627(e): the section does not require a business "To connect or respond within 15 minutes if the request or connection is made via electronic mail, web-based contact form, or voicemail message." (1), nor "To offer customer service through a communication platform that the business has not otherwise made available to customers as of January 1, 2027." (2). [full-text]

**Enforcement, defences and exemptions**

- s. 22628(a): "An action to enforce this chapter may be brought by a public prosecutor." [full-text]
- s. 22628(b): "A large private business that violates this chapter shall be liable for a civil penalty not exceeding five thousand dollars ($5,000) for an initial violation, and ten thousand dollars ($10,000) for each subsequent violation." [full-text]
- s. 22628(c): "For purposes of this chapter, the use of commercially reasonable and practicable standards that are designed and intended to achieve compliance shall satisfy the “good faith effort” requirements of Section 22627." [full-text]
- s. 22628(d): "This chapter does not establish a private right of action." [full-text]
- s. 22628(e): the chapter does not require a business that on 1 January 2027 uses no telephone customer service to provide one, "provided that the large private business offers customer service methods by which the customer may promptly connect with a customer service agent consistent with this section." [full-text]
- s. 22628(f): inability to comply is no violation if due to "Unforeseen circumstances beyond its reasonable control." or to "Extraordinary or emergency situations", including emergencies and "information technology or telecommunications outages." [full-text]
- s. 22628(g) exempts services under two named general orders of the Public Utilities Commission, "Exclusive business lines.", a hospital's communications about health care services, and "A consumer reporting agency, as defined in the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act". [full-text]
- s. 22629: "The duties, remedies, and obligations imposed by this chapter are cumulative to the duties, remedies, or obligations imposed under other law and shall not be construed to relieve a large private business from any duties, remedies, or obligations imposed under any other law." [full-text]
- The Legislative Counsel's digest summarises the duty as "to provide a customer service feature allowing customers to contact a customer service agent during its regular business hours" and the enforcement as by "a public prosecutor". [full-text]
- The bill is headed "Enrolled September 04, 2026", "Passed IN Senate August 30, 2026", "Passed IN Assembly August 31, 2026", introduced "January 20, 2026". The saved status page of 28 Sep 2026 marks it "Non-Urgency". [full-text]

**What the text does not contain** (whole-text searches of the saved extract, 11 Nov 2026)

- 'hotel' and 'lodging' do not occur. No sector is named except those exempted. [full-text]
- 'authority' and 'resolve' do not occur. The bill says who the agent is and how fast the customer must reach one, and nothing about what the agent can decide. [full-text]
- No section states an operative date. The only date in the text is "January 1, 2027", used as a reference point in ss. 22627(e)(2) and 22628(e). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Enrolled, not chaptered.** Everything here comes from the bill as passed by both houses. A chaptered statute normally reproduces the enrolled text, but I did not confirm that, and I have no chapter number. The signature rests on a press notice. Check the chaptered text before the paper cites a section number.
- **Operative date not verified.** The text gives none. I have not read the California rule on when a non-urgency statute takes effect and do not state one.
- **A duty of effort.** The business must "make a good faith effort", and "commercially reasonable and practicable standards" satisfy that (s. 22628(c)). No customer is promised a person within 15 minutes.
- **Owed to no one who can sue.** Section 22628(d) is the clearest sentence in the eight texts on this list on K5's question. The duty protects customers and belongs, for enforcement, to the public prosecutor.
- **A person without stated power.** The agent is a natural person who provides service or support (s. 22625(c)). Nothing requires that the agent can change an outcome. The reference to "escalation queues" (s. 22627(c)) assumes that some matters go higher and regulates only the waiting.
- **Residents, not guests.** The duty runs to natural persons whose account address is in California (s. 22625(b)). On the words, a large hotel company owes it to a Californian customer wherever the hotel is, and does not owe it to a visitor from elsewhere staying in California. That reading is mine; I found no guidance and looked for none.
- **Size.** A business below 500 million dollars in national gross revenue is outside (s. 22625(g)). Independent hotels are outside.
- **Customer service, not performance.** The chapter regulates how a customer reaches help "with goods or services". It does not tie the route to any duty of the business to perform with care, and it applies only "During at least" regular business hours (s. 22627(a)), which may be ten hours on five days (s. 22625(i)). A guest locked out at night gets nothing from it unless the business customarily staffs customer service then.
- Later treatment not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports the conclusion as legislative policy; narrows it on duty, power, time and holder of the right.** K7's 'confirms' list names "duties on traders to be reachable". California has enacted one with a person in it and a clock on it (s. 22627(a)-(b)). The statute has none of K7's premises: it does not speak of oversight, of a duty still capable of performance, or of the customer's report as a signal. Its timing rule is the same for every request and is met by an appointment the next business day.

**K5 and K6 (directedness; the guest's claim): narrows.** The legislature that required the channel refused the customer a claim to it (s. 22628(d)). If the paper says that law has begun to require a route to a person, it must also say that this law keeps the route out of the customer's hands. The paper's own position, that reachability follows from the holder's duty and is not a free-standing right of the guest, is consistent with that.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- A California bill enrolled in September 2026, and reported signed by the Governor, requires businesses with more than 500 million dollars in national revenue to give California customers a simple way to ask for a human customer service agent on every customer-service platform, during regular business hours (ss. 22625(b), (c), (g), 22627(a)).
- It requires a good-faith effort to connect the customer to a natural person within 15 minutes or to give an appointment within one business day (s. 22627(b)).
- It forbids representing a customer service chatbot as a human (s. 22626(a)).
- It is enforced by public prosecutors through civil penalties of up to 5,000 dollars for a first violation and 10,000 dollars for each later one, and it "does not establish a private right of action" (s. 22628(a), (b), (d)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the statute's section numbers, chapter number or operative date are confirmed. I read the enrolled bill only.
- That California gives customers a right to a human. The text creates a duty on large businesses, enforced by prosecutors, and denies a private right of action (s. 22628(d)).
- That a customer must be connected within 15 minutes. The duty is one of good-faith effort, with an appointment as an alternative (ss. 22627(b), 22628(c)).
- That the agent must have authority to decide the customer's problem. The text is silent on the agent's powers.
- That the statute covers hotels as such, or guests as such. It covers large businesses of any kind and customers resident in California (s. 22625(b), (g)).
- That the statute applies at all hours. It applies during regular business hours as the business customarily keeps them (ss. 22625(i), 22627(a)).
