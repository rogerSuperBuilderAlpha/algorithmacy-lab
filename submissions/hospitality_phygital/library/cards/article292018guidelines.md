## Summary

The EU data-protection regulators' interpretation of GDPR Article 22: what counts as solely automated,
what counts as a legal or similarly significant effect, and what the human-intervention safeguard requires.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Token review does not count: "the controller must ensure that any oversight of the decision is meaningful, rather than just a token gesture. It should be carried out by someone who has the authority and competence to change the decision." (p. 21) [full-text]
- Legal effects include "cancellation of a contract" (p. 21) [full-text]
- Significance test: the decision must have the potential to "significantly affect the circumstances, behaviour or choices of the individuals concerned; have a prolonged or permanent impact on the data subject; or at its most extreme, lead to the exclusion or discrimination of individuals." (p. 21) [full-text]
- Pricing: "Automated decision-making that results in differential pricing based on personal data or personal characteristics could also have a significant effect if, for example, prohibitively high prices effectively bar someone from certain goods or services." (p. 22) [full-text]
- Vulnerability shifts the threshold: "Processing that might have little impact on individuals generally may in fact have a significant effect for certain groups of society, such as minority groups or vulnerable adults." (p. 22) [full-text]
- Contract exception: "Routine human involvement can sometimes be impractical or impossible due to the sheer quantity of data being processed." (p. 23) [full-text]
- Reviewer's authority: "Any review must be carried out by someone who has the appropriate authority and capability to change the decision." (p. 27) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Guidance, not law; the examples are hedged ("could", "for example"). No hospitality example.
- Uses "prolonged or permanent impact" as a significance marker, which is a reversibility dimension in regulators' own words; it says nothing about timing of the safeguard.

## Where it enters the manuscript

§5 check-in and pricing examples; §9 boundary conditions. It lets the typology say that refusal or
cancellation at the door is a legal effect on the regulators' reading, while ordinary dynamic pricing is
not, unless it bars access.
