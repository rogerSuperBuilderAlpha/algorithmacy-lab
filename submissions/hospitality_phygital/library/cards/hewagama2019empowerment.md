## Summary

A mixed-method study of thirty hotels in Sri Lanka: initial qualitative interviews, then a large dyadic
survey analysed with structural equation modelling. It tests whether HR practices raise
service-recovery performance and job satisfaction by empowering frontline staff to respond to service
failures. HR practices and management styles build job competence, which relates to recovery
performance and satisfaction; but empowerment to fix failures is rationed by hierarchical level.

## Critical notes & threats

- Answers R3.9 with hotel evidence: empowerment to address a failure grows with rank. The abstract
  does not say frontline staff hold none of it, so "not held by whoever the guest meets" would
  overstate it; it is graded by rank ("stage-managed and 'staircased'", "deliberately graduated according to rank"; "escalates" is my gloss, not the abstract's word). That is the face-to-face baseline the paper should
  describe instead of "all four sit in one person" (PO §2.4).
- Abstract only. The sample sizes, the measures of empowerment and the content of the "staircase"
  (which ranks may grant what) are unknown. Do not state them.
- One country; the abstract says only "thirty hotels in Sri Lanka" and does not give their grade.
  Whether the authors discuss the cultural context is unknown until the body is read.
- It shows graded authority, not that guests could or could not reach the higher rank. For the paper's
  "address" claim the question is whether escalation (my gloss) stays reachable; this source does not say.
