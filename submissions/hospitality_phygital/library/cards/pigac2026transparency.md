## Summary

Fifty semi-structured interviews (September-December 2024) with guests of four luxury hotel brands
in Asia, Europe and North America, stratified by the CEW technology comfort scale, used to build
and illustrate a Dynamic Transparency Protocol (DTP). Transparency should adapt through three
mechanisms (user-centric adaptation, situational sensitivity, emotional matching) and be tiered
by segment: skeptics want staff-mediated verbal summaries, enthusiasts want detailed algorithmic
insight, privacy-conscious elites want opt-in, user-controlled dashboards.

## Critical notes & threats

- (rr-2026-11) Correction: the abstract-era fact "Covers transparency only, with no adjustability, announced change, contestability or bypass dimension" is wrong. The paper includes real-time control options and opt-in or opt-out choice (pp. 289, 291) and human override by staff (pp. 291-292). It has no announced-change or real-alternative dimension and no contestability procedure. The claim that the overlap "leaves four affordances ... untouched" should become "touches adjustability and a staff override, as trust devices."
- The deeper difference survives: Pigac et al. treat override and control as trust and comfort devices (pp. 291-292), not as answerability to a guest with standing. That is the "what the framework adds" argument, and it now has to be made against a paper that does mention override.
- (rr-2026-11) A second use is available. Theme 4 (p. 291) is guest testimony for the fourth principle (a person who can step in), from luxury hotel guests.
- Luxury only; it offers no budget comparison, so it supports the luxury side of the E.7 boundary condition, not the contrast.
