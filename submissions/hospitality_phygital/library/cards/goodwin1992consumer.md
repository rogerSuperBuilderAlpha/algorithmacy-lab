## Summary

Experiment (2x2x2x4 between-subjects) on consumers' reactions to a service failure, manipulating complaint outcome, apology, voice and service type. Voice and apology help when the outcome is favourable and help less, sometimes hurt, when no tangible offer is made.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- "Procedural fairness is operationalized as the consumer's opportunity to present information and express feelings, or 'voice.'" (abstract) [abstract]
- "Apology and voice appeared to enhance fairness and satisfaction perceptions in the 'favorable outcome' condition, when consumers were offered a discount or gift after service failure." (abstract) [abstract]
- "When no tangible offering was made, apology and voice had lesser effect and in some instances were associated with lower perceptions of fairness and satisfaction." (abstract) [abstract]

## Where it enters the manuscript

Section 7 (v4 numbering), on the value of voice: "Voice offered after a decision was taken, with the decision explicitly unchanged, still raised judgments of both procedural and outcome fairness (Lind et al. 1990; Goodwin and Ross 1992).
