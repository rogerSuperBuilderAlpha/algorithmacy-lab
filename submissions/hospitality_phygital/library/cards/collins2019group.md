## Summary

Not read. On the publisher's abstracts, Collins asks whether groups are apt to bear moral duties and what follows for their members, and defends a 'Tripartite Model'. Combinations are collections of agents with no goals or decision procedures in common; coalitions share goals and lack decision procedures; collectives have group-level procedures for making decisions. Only collectives can bear duties. Duties that people attribute to combinations and coalitions are to be recast as duties held by each member: to reason for oneself given what others will do, or to do one's part in a group pattern on the presumption that others will. A collective's duties entail 'membership duties', held by members because they are members and performed as components of the collective's doing its duty.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The two questions: "This book asks (i) whether such groups are apt to bear duties and (ii) what this implies for their members. It defends a ‘Tripartite Model’ of group duties, which divides groups into three fundamental categories." (book abstract) [abstract]
- Combinations: "First, combinations are collections of agents that do not have any goals or decision-making procedures in common. Combinations cannot bear moral duties. Instead, we should re-cast their purported duties as a series of duties—one held by each agent in the combination." (book abstract) [abstract]
- Coalitions: "Second, coalitions are groups whose members share goals but lack decision-making procedures. Coalitions also cannot bear duties, but their alleged duties should be replaced with members’ several duties to ‘we-reason’: to do one’s part in a particular group pattern of actions, on the presumption that others will do likewise." (book abstract) [abstract]
- Collectives: "Third, collectives have group-level procedures for making decisions. They can bear duties. Collectives’ duties imply duties for collectives’ members to use their role in the collective with a view to the collective doing its duty." (book abstract) [abstract]
- Why non-collectives cannot: chapter 3 argues "for the Agency Principle: groups that are not moral agents cannot bear duties", by "the Decision Argument", which "states that duties imply a specific kind of ability: the ability to make morally sensitive decisions. Non-collective groups lack this specific ability. So, they cannot bear duties." (ch. 3 abstract, 'Against Combinations’ and Coalitions’ Duties', pp. 60-95) [abstract]
- What members of non-collectives owe instead: 'coordination duties', of two species, 'responsiveness duties' and 'collectivization duties'; "Coordination duties are held by singular agents and do not require the positing of any group duty." (ch. 4 abstract, 'Coordination Duties', pp. 96-125) [abstract]
- The conception of a collective is "permissive (i.e. its conditions are easy to satisfy)". The argument for collectives' duties "has two planks. First, collectives are able to make decisions based on duties. Second, those decisions are to produce an outcome that would not be produced if each of the collective’s members severally made decisions: the outcome of robust multilateralism (coordinated role-performance) amongst members." (ch. 6 abstract, 'Collectives and Their Duties', pp. 153-180) [abstract]
- The content of a collective's duty in general: "At a general level of description, a collective’s duty will be a duty to produce multilateralism amongst members—a decision that no member can sensibly take on their own." (ch. 6 abstract) [abstract]
- Membership duties: "This chapter argues that collectives’ duties entail membership duties, which are duties (i) held by agents in virtue of the fact that they are members of a collective that has a duty and (ii) whose performance is a component of the collective’s doing its duty. It argues that collectives’ duties entail membership duties by arguing that whenever a collective fails to do a duty, a member has also failed to do an individually-held duty." (ch. 7 abstract, 'Membership Duties', pp. 181-206) [abstract]
- Scope of 'duty': chapter 1 "differentiates the issue of group duties from the issues of group causal responsibility and group moral retrospective responsibility." (ch. 1 abstract, 'The Tripartite Model', pp. 1-26) [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Abstract depth.** Everything above comes from abstracts the publisher deposited. I saw no chapter text, no argument, no example and no page. The abstracts say what each chapter argues; they do not show how, or what objections Collins answers. Nothing here supports a quotation with a page.
- **What an abstract cannot tell us.** Whether Collins counts a commercial firm as a collective (the abstracts' examples are a state, environmentalists and the affluent); whether 'role' in "to use their role in the collective" means an office or a contribution; what a member owes when the collective fails; how membership duties relate to an employee's contract. Each matters for K8 and none is answerable at this depth.
- **Members, not contractors.** Membership duties belong to members. The abstracts do not say whether a contractor, a franchisee or a software vendor is a member of the collective it serves. K8's cases turn on that.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K8 (a firm as holder; allocation among several firms): supports the first, narrows the second, at abstract depth.** On the abstracts a group with "group-level procedures for making decisions" can bear duties, and the conditions are "easy to satisfy" (book abstract; ch. 6 abstract). A hotel company plausibly meets them, though the abstracts do not say so. A brand, an operator, a platform and a vendor that share no decision procedure are at most a coalition, and "Coalitions also cannot bear duties" (book abstract). If that is Collins's view, the host's duty cannot sit on the assembly of firms as a group. It sits on one firm, or on each, with coordination duties among them (ch. 4 abstract). That agrees with K8's allocation by ground and warns against any sentence that makes 'the assembled host' a duty-bearer.

**K2 (the duty attaches to the role and can bind a firm): supports at abstract depth.** A collective's duty entails duties on members "to use their role in the collective with a view to the collective doing its duty" (book abstract). That gives the paper a published route from the firm's duty to the conduct of the people who staff it.

**K7: nothing at this depth.** The abstracts do not mention oversight, monitoring or delegation.
