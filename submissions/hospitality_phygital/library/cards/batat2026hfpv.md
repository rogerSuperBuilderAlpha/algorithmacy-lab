## Summary

An article headed "Editorial" (p. 269) that introduces the *Journal of Macromarketing* special issue "Phygital Marketing for Positive Impact" (p. 279), developed with the 2025 American Phygital Association Summit (p. 269). It proposes "phygital science" as a paradigm and the Human First Phygital Value (HFPV) framework as its architecture, with four components: inputs, processes, outcomes and systemic feedback loops (p. 273; Figure 1, p. 274). The argument runs at the macro level, against "technocentric and efficiency-driven" digital transformation (p. 269). Of the three Batat texts read for v8, this one says most about governance. It gives stewardship to firms, to a new Chief Phygital Officer (CPO), to institutions and regulators, and to communities, and it calls for "algorithmic accountability" (p. 278). It never mentions hospitality, hotels, guests or hosts.

## Critical notes & threats

- **It narrows the gap statement as now worded.** HFPV names both who acts and for whom: firms (pp. 277-278), the CPO (pp. 271, 275), "human-first phygital organizations" (p. 275), regulators and policymakers (p. 278) and communities (p. 277), acting for individuals, communities, vulnerable groups and society (pp. 275-278). "Says neither to whom they are owed nor which ... owes them" is therefore false of this text.
- **What survives, after a full read** (my reading). HFPV divides no duty among several firms that together deliver one service. The CPO secures coherence "within the phygital enterprise" (p. 271), a single organization. Beneficiaries appear as populations and outcomes, never as individuals holding a claim. The call for "algorithmic accountability" (p. 278) names no party that renders the account and no party to whom it is rendered. Every feedback mechanism is aggregate and prospective (pp. 275-278). None is a route by which one person's failed case reaches someone able to correct it.
- **It contests the wording of `framing-argued-past`.** "Technologies must be understood as sociotechnical actors" (p. 273) directly rejects treating technology as an instrument. My reading: the agency HFPV grants technology is participation in value creation, not standing as a party that answers. The paper can turn this into its point, but the slug's word "instrument" will not survive a reader who knows this text.
- **Roederer et al. (2026) threatens `not-that-triad`.** As reported here (pp. 271-272), their human-AI-organization triad makes AI the third party in a phygital service setting. The paper has not read Roederer et al.; it must, before claiming that existing service triads place only a context or a participant third.
- **Bibliographic.** The PDF is the issue version: *Journal of Macromarketing* 46(3), 269-281 (PDF metadata "Journal of Macromarketing 2026.46:269-281"). The bibliography note still says "OnlineFirst 2026-05-14, no volume/issue" and needs updating; this card does not edit it. HFPV's own labels clash with this library's: its 2024a is the QMR editorial and its 2024b is PH-CX (p. 280). It also cites PSR as both "Batat, 2025a" (p. 270) and "Batat, 2025" (p. 272), where this library has batat2026psr.
- **Genre.** It is an editorial and programmatic, with no data. Its examples are the special-issue articles (Table 1, p. 272).
- The author's semicolons, which stand in for dashes, are printed so and kept in the quotations.

#### correction 2026-11-13 (lead, after independent verification)
Verified by an independent reader (`manuscript/v8_2026-11/stage2/VERIFY_stage2_cards.md`): every quotation verbatim; 0 unsupported; 5 partly supported. Corrections the lead accepts:
- The Chief Phygital Officer is not confined to one enterprise. HFPV gives the CPO an ecosystem role: the CPO 'orchestrates interdependencies among physical environments, digital platforms, human experience, and societal impact' (p. 271) and 'plays a critical stewardship role, overseeing ethical AI, data governance, and algorithmic accountability' (p. 275). So 'algorithmic accountability' does have an overseer (the CPO); what HFPV never names is to whom the account is rendered.
- The feedback loops are aggregate but not only prospective: they 'allow systems to respond to lived experience in real time' (p. 275). The defensible point is narrower: the loops act on the system through aggregate signals; none is a route by which a failure in one guest's case reaches someone able to correct it.
- Safer wording for the paper: 'HFPV charges a Chief Phygital Officer with "ensuring coherence within the phygital enterprise" (271) and with overseeing "algorithmic accountability" (275), and does not say to whom that account is owed or which of several firms' officers answers.'
