## Summary

A conceptual, interdisciplinary paper (psychology, law, philosophy, computer science). Taking risk
mitigation as oversight's main objective, it defines an oversight person as effective if and only
if they have causal power, epistemic access, self-control and fitting intentions, which it
compresses to "Effectiveness = Moral Responsibility + Fitting Intentions". It lists facilitators
and inhibitors in technical design, individual factors and environment, and tests the account
against Article 14 of the EU AI Act.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Working definition: oversight is "supervision of a system by at least one natural person, typically with the authority to influence its operations or effects", including "reversal of faulty decisions" (p. 2497) [full-text]
- Four conditions: "(a) sufficient causal power with regards to the system and its effects, (b) suitable epistemic access to relevant aspects of the situation, (c) self-control over their own actions, and (d) fitting intentions for their role" (p. 2496) [full-text]
- No mere observer: "it is not enough if the oversight person is a mere observer of the system ... Also, it is not sufficient if the human only has 'pseudo-options', i.e., if they can only seemingly influence the system but not really make a relevant difference" (p. 2498) [full-text]
- "An oversight person is effective if and only if they are morally responsible and have fitting intentions" (p. 2500) [full-text]
- Competence is kept out of the definition but tied to it: "Without the necessary competence, an oversight person will arguably be unlikely to be effective, if they even can be effective at all" (p. 2500) [full-text]
- Training of the oversight person is listed as a facilitator (Table 1, p. 2501) [full-text]
- Accountability appears as a facilitator, "an obligation to explain and to justify [one's] conduct", not as the objective of oversight (p. 2502) [full-text]
- Peer-reviewed conference publication, which is what fink2025oversight lacks [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- (rr-2026-11) Causal power maps onto the manuscript's authority condition, and p. 2498 is a near-exact warrant for "can listen but cannot change anything." The mapping of epistemic access to transparency holds too.
- (rr-2026-11) Two limits. Sterz et al. analyse an oversight person supervising a system for risk mitigation, not a frontline employee answering a guest; accountability to the affected party is set aside as another objective (p. 2498, "accountability and responsibility, or liability. Discussing them is beyond the scope"). And they treat training and competence as facilitators (pp. 2500-2501), so the paper does not support "institutional, not trainable" as a whole; it supports "causal power is not trainable."

## Support verdicts against v4 and the revision plan (rr-2026-11)

- v4 §7 (line 66): "An employee who can listen with great sympathy but cannot change anything becomes a place to deposit frustration, not a source of accountability (Sterz et al. 2024; Li and Sun 2025)."
