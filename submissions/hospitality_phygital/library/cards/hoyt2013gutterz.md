## Summary

Curtis Hoyt drank at a bowling alley and tavern in Guthrie Center, Iowa, taunted another customer, was refused further service and was told by the owner to leave. In the car park the other customer struck him from behind and broke his ankle. Hoyt sued the assailant and the tavern. The district court gave summary judgment for the tavern because the assault was unforeseeable. The Supreme Court of Iowa reversed and sent the case to trial. It held that the tavern owed its patron a duty of reasonable care under Restatement (Third) of Torts § 40, which it quotes in part, that foreseeability belongs to breach and scope of liability and not to duty, and that a jury could find breach. Three justices dissented on breach and scope; they did not dispute the duty.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- **Court, date, vote.** Supreme Court of Iowa, No. 11-0085, decided 5 April 2013; 829 N.W.2d 772. Hecht, J., for the court. "All justices concur except WATERMAN, J., CADY, C.J., and MANSFIELD, J., who dissent." (782). The opinion does not state the size of the bench. [full-text]
- **Jurisdiction and law applied.** Iowa tort law, on further review of an Iowa Court of Appeals decision that had reversed the district court (773–774). [full-text]
- **Parties and claim.** A patron sued "a bar for injuries sustained when he was assaulted in the bar's parking lot" (773). The defendant is "a bowling alley and tavern" (773), not a hotel. [full-text]
- **Facts that matter.** The waitress obtained the owner's permission to stop serving Hoyt and his companion; the owner "grew concerned" that "an altercation might occur" and asked them to leave; Hoyt was struck from behind in the car park and suffered "a compound fracture of his ankle" (773; the CAP text prints "ah altercation"). Whether the owner walked them to their vehicles was disputed (n. 1). [full-text]
- **Restatement (Third) of Torts § 40, as quoted by this court** (776; the introductory sentence begins on 775), which gives its title as "Duty Based on Special Relationship With Another" (774): "(a) An actor in a special relationship with another owes the other a duty of reasonable care with regard to risks that arise within the scope of the relationship. (b) Special relationships giving rise to the duty provided in Subsection (a) include: (1) a common carrier with its passengers, (2) an innkeeper with its guests, (3) a business or other possessor of land that holds its premises open to the public with those who are lawfully on the premises,". The court's quotation ends on that comma and cites "Restatement (Third) § 40, at 39 (2012)". [full-text]
- **Comment g, as quoted** (776): "The duty described in this Section applies regardless of the source of the risk. Thus, it applies to risks created by the individual at risk as well as those created by a third party's conduct, whether innocent, negligent, or intentional." [full-text]
- **Comment j, as quoted** (776): "[b]usinesses ... who hold their land open to the public owe a duty of reasonable care to persons lawfully on their land who become ill or endangered by risks created by third parties." [full-text]
- **Holding on duty.** "Tavern owners fit squarely within the class of business owners contemplated by section 40(b)(3)." and "we conclude Gutterz owed Hoyt a duty of reasonable care under the circumstances presented here" (777). The court adds: "we emphasize again our adoption of the duty analysis of the Restatement (Third)" (776–777). [full-text]
- **The basis for the duty, in words the court quotes from a Restatement comment** (777): "[T]he relationship identifies a specific person to be protected and thus provides a more limited and justified incursion on autonomy, especially when the relationship is entered into voluntarily. In addition, some relationships necessarily compromise a person's ability to self-protect, while leaving the actor in a superior position to protect that person. Many of the relationships also benefit the actor." The court adds: "We are convinced these justifications are applicable to tavern owners" (777). The CAP text garbles the comment letter in the citation ("cmt. ⅛, at 43"). [full-text]
- **Limits the court states.** "While taverns are not insurers of patrons' safety against third-person criminal attacks, various jurisdictions have explained taverns must make reasonable efforts to maintain order and supervise and control patrons." (777). "We emphasize that our recognition of a duty in this case does not give rise to strict or absolute liability on the part of Gutterz. Instead, to fulfill its duty in this context, Gutterz was merely required to employ reasonable safety precautions." (780) [full-text]
- **Scope.** The duty in § 40(a) as quoted covers "risks that arise within the scope of the relationship" (776). The court applied it to an assault in the car park on a patron the tavern had just told to leave (773, 779–780). [full-text]
- **What reasonable care might have required** is left to the jury: "It may be that quick intervention was all that was necessary under the circumstances." "Alternatively, a call to the police may have been warranted." (780) [full-text]
- **Did the claim succeed?** Not decided. "Genuine issues of material fact remain regarding each element of Hoyt's negligence claim and summary judgment was therefore inappropriate. Accordingly, we reverse the district court's dismissal of this claim and remand this case for trial." (782) [full-text]
- **Dissent (Waterman, J., with Cady, C.J., and Mansfield, J.).** It accepts the duty: "The duty of care invoked by the majority today is found in section 40(b)(3) of the Restatement (Third)." "The duty owed is one of reasonable care." (784). It would affirm because "Uncontroverted facts establish the defendant acted reasonably as a matter of law." (782; the CAP text hyphenates "Uncontro-verted"). "Bars are not insurers strictly liable for injuries on their premises inflicted by others." (782) [full-text]
- **Dissent on a patron's request.** Distinguishing an Iowa Court of Appeals case in which patrons "asked the bartender to call the police while they waited inside until the police arrived" and "The bartender refused", the dissent writes: "In sharp contrast here, no one asked for the police to be called" (783). The earlier case, *Regan v. Denbar*, was not read. [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **A bar case.** Section 40(b)(2), "an innkeeper with its guests", is quoted and not applied. For hotels the card supports only this: the Iowa court adopted a duty analysis whose list of special relationships, as the court quotes it, includes the innkeeper.
- **The quotation of § 40(b) is partial.** It stops after item (3) with a comma. The scout found no judgment that quotes the rest. Nothing may be said about the remainder of the list from this card.
- **The innkeeper is not special in this list.** As quoted, the same duty lies on carriers and on any "business or other possessor of land that holds its premises open to the public". The relationship that grounds the duty is not peculiar to hosts.
- **Duty found, liability open.** The result of the trial on remand was not checked. The dissent would have ended the case on breach.
- **The quoted reasons are the Restatement's, adopted by the court for taverns.** They are attributable as "the Iowa Supreme Court, quoting a comment to § 40". The comment letter must be checked against the printed reporter before it is named.
- **Iowa's method is its own.** The court removes foreseeability from the duty question (775–777). Colorado keeps it there (*Groh*, n. 5, card groh2015westin). A sentence about "US law" cannot rest on either.
- **Later treatment not checked.**

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K4 (reception tier): supports for a bar, in Iowa.** A tavern owes a patron reasonable care for his safety, third parties' acts included (776–777). The duty is one of reasonable care and no more (780).
- **K3 (grounds): supports, and narrows.** The reasons the court quotes match the paper's three grounds closely: a relationship "entered into voluntarily"; one that may "compromise a person's ability to self-protect, while leaving the actor in a superior position"; and a business that "holds its premises open to the public" (776–777). The same passage adds a fourth reason the paper does not list, benefit to the actor, and the list of relationships shows that none of these grounds is specific to hosts.
- **K5 (directedness): supports.** The court's first reason is that the relationship "identifies a specific person to be protected" (777), and the patron sues in his own name.
- **K7: a lead only.** The dissent treats a patron's request for help as what made the later attack foreseeable in another case (783). That is one judge's description of a case not read here.

**What the paper may say** (for Iowa, naming the court):
- The Supreme Court of Iowa held that a tavern owes its patrons a duty of reasonable care, which extends to harm done by other patrons (776–777).
- Restatement (Third) of Torts § 40, as quoted in *Hoyt* at 776, lists "an innkeeper with its guests" among special relationships that give rise to "a duty of reasonable care with regard to risks that arise within the scope of the relationship", beside carriers and businesses open to the public.
- The court adopted, for taverns, the reasons that such a relationship "identifies a specific person to be protected", may "compromise a person's ability to self-protect", and often "benefit the actor" (777); voluntariness qualifies only the first reason ("especially when the relationship is entered into voluntarily").
- The duty is not strict liability (780).

**What the paper may not say:**
- That *Hoyt* decides anything about hotels or innkeepers.
- That the tavern was held liable.
- That § 40(b) contains only the three relationships quoted, or anything else on the Restatement's own authority.
- That the court was unanimous.
- That the reasons quoted at 777 come from a named comment, until the letter is checked in print.
