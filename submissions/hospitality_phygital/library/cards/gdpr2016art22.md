## Summary

Article 22 gives a data subject the right not to be subject to a solely automated decision with legal or
similarly significant effects. It then lets a controller take such decisions anyway where they are
necessary for a contract or rest on explicit consent, provided the controller offers human intervention,
a hearing and a way to contest. Art. 22(3) does not say when intervention occurs; reading the safeguard as coming after the decision is my inference, not the text.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Art. 22(1): "The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her." [full-text]
- Art. 22(2)(a): para. 1 does not apply if the decision "is necessary for entering into, or performance of, a contract between the data subject and a data controller" [full-text]
- Art. 22(3): in cases (a) and (c) the controller "shall implement suitable measures to safeguard the data subject's rights and freedoms and legitimate interests, at least the right to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller, to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision." [full-text]
- Recital 71 examples: "automatic refusal of an online credit application or e-recruiting practices without any human intervention"; safeguards include the right "to obtain an explanation of the decision reached after such assessment and to challenge the decision." [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Applies only to *solely* automated decisions; a clerk who rubber-stamps may take a hotel's decision outside it, though the WP29 guidelines say token involvement does not count (card `article292018guidelines`, p. 21).
- My reading, not the text: a hotel's booking, check-in and pricing decisions may sit under the contract exception, but only where the decision is "necessary for" the contract (Art. 22(2)(a)); if so, the guest's legal protection is the Art. 22(3) right to intervention and contest, whose timing the Article does not state. Whether a given hotel decision is "similarly significant" is untested; I found no case on hotel decisions.
- EU-only (and the UK's retained version, not read). Jurisdiction is already a §9 boundary condition.
- Not read: CJEU case law (e.g. SCHUFA, C-634/21, cited by Baum and Laux p. 4 n. 7; I did not open it).

## Where it enters the manuscript

§9 jurisdiction boundary and §7 "reachable authority to reverse": one sentence saying the law of the
paper's main jurisdiction secures a later appeal, not a person at the moment of decision, so immediacy has
to come from the host's obligation rather than from data-protection law.
