## Summary

Traces platformed professionalization: hosts investing labour and assets into a livelihood while
gaining no claim on how the platform is governed.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Conceptualizes professionalization 'as a distinct platform logic […] that creates and maintains unequal power relations both between hosts themselves, and between platform' and hosts — the ellipsis covers an inline citation to Andersson Schwarz [full-text]
- The logic 'benefits Airbnb and hosts who are able to take part, while reinforcing existing inequalities' [full-text]
- Method is qualitative fieldwork in Berlin following three hosts, against a literature dominated by scraped quantitative data [full-text]
- *Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space* 54:4, pp. 595-610 [metadata]
- "Host" names several roles on the platform: "‘host’ might refer to users with different roles: ‘primary hosts,’ ‘listing admins or owners,’ and ‘co-hosts’ (Airbnb Help Center, n.d.)"; the co-host "help[s] listing owners ... take care of their home and guests", while "the crucial difference between co-hosts and listing owners is that the latter have access to the payout from Airbnb, while a co-host does not" (p. 601) [full-text]
- The co-host takes instructions from the app, not the owner: the platform mediated "an ambiguous relation between asset owner and hosting laborer by creating a particular form of distributed agency" (p. 601) [full-text]
- Hosting decomposed: "the figure of the ‘host’ and the work of ‘hosting’ is increasingly being broken down into separate types of labor that can or cannot be outsourced, automated, and optimized"; Airbnb's integration of these practices "suggest[s] that Airbnb is willing to further decentralize the figure of the host" (p. 605) [full-text]
- Unstaffed arrival: the Host Assist programme lets smart-lock providers integrate with the platform, "allowing guests to enter their holiday apartments without meeting anyone in person. To be eligible for business travelers, such a self-check-in option is required by Airbnb ... ultimately transforming hosting into ghosting" (p. 605) [full-text]
