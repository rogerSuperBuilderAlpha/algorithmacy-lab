## Summary

A viewpoint paper, written for a JOSM special section that Batat and Hammedi guest-edited (p. 10). It sorts "extended reality technologies" into four extension processes: cognitive/behavioural (self-tracking, AI, IoT), social (avatars, chatbots, voice assistants), sensory (3D visualisation and audio, haptics, digital taste and smell) and contextual (VR, AR, XR). It then sets a service research agenda on design and innovation, well-being, and the metaverse.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Abstract: the extension "processes can enhance the cognitive, social, sensory and contextual dimensions of the phygital experience and thus create a continuum in terms of customer value from physical to digital settings and vice versa." (p. 10). The v4 quotation is verbatim and correctly paged [full-text]
- Stated goal: "Our goal is to offer a new framework ... which can help service researchers and practitioners to design engaging phygital experiences that guarantee a continuum in customer value from physical to digital settings and vice versa." (p. 11) [full-text]
- Smoothness as requirement: "Flawless interactivity and connectivity are thus necessary to guarantee an enjoyable and emotionally engaged customer and service experience." (p. 14); sensory technologies help "by preventing disconnects in their shopping journeys" (p. 18); "adopting a phygital experience perspective smooths customers' transitions" (p. 24) [full-text]
- The framework criticises its tools, though not their smoothness: "technology disintermediation has contributed to the escalation of the sense of objectification and separation" (p. 24); "companies should never replace services delivered through humans by virtual agents, even if they are highly humanized" (p. 18); Table 1 asks "What are the downsides of ERTs?" (p. 23) [full-text]
- Taxonomy is of devices, platforms and tools ("a set of technological devices, platforms and tools", p. 13), not of intermediaries that perform hosting functions [full-text]
