## Summary

Argues that algorithmic accountability needs a triad, not a dyad: the actor (developer) answers to a
forum (regulator), and the forum must itself answer to the public(s), usually through the courts.
Courts are "the primary route—and the primary roadblock" to redress, because plaintiffs struggle with
documentation, with standing, and with expert evidence. Impact-assessment regimes could create
procedural rights that give publics standing to contest documentation and regulators' judgments.
Their "standing" is juridical and collective: a plaintiff's or a public's admissibility before a court.

- Scope: US courts, except where comparison helps (p. 1451) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **The nearest rival use of the word.** Their standing differs from ours on three axes: it is juridical (admissibility before a court, p. 1455), it is held by publics or classes rather than by one guest, and it is addressed to a forum that judges the actor, not to the party in the position of host. Ours is the guest's position to address a claim to the host in the encounter. Two sentences in §3 can say this.
- **The overlap is real and must be credited.** "developers who are obligated to listen" (p. 1453) is our counterparty condition in their words. The difference is where the listening happens, not whether it is owed.
- **Their remedy is our point.** Courts refuse standing for diffuse algorithmic harms (p. 1458): the juridical route fails exactly where the encounter-level route is also gone. This supports §6's claim that standing is conferred by institutions, since they propose that an AIA regime *create* procedural rights.
- **Bibliographic defect in references.bib.** The bib entry gives the fourth author as "Tafesse, Elizabeth". Crossref (fetched 2026-11-08) and the paper's title block both give **Emnet** Tafesse. Crossref orders the authors Metcalf, Singh, Moss, Tafesse, Watkins; the paper's own ACM Reference Format line orders them Metcalf, Moss, Singh, Tafesse, Watkins. The bib follows Crossref. The authors should fix the given name and pick one order.
- Distinct from Metcalf et al. 2022 (arXiv 2203.01455, "A relationship and not a thing"); do not conflate.
