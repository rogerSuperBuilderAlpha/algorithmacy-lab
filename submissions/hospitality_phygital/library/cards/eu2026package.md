## Summary

The amending Directive leaves the organiser's responsibility for performance (art. 13), the remedies (art. 14), the retailer channel (art. 15), the duty of assistance (art. 16) and the contact-point clause (art. 7(2)(d)) as they were. It adds art. 16a, headed "Complaint-handling". Paragraph 1 obliges the organiser to be easily contactable through the contact points named in the contract and able to respond efficiently to queries about assistance and about a lack of conformity occurring during the package. Paragraph 2 sets separate deadlines for other complaints: acknowledgement within seven days, a reasoned reply within sixty. The Directive also abolishes the category of linked travel arrangements, deems a trader an organiser if it invites the traveller to add a second type of service without saying the result is no package, rewrites the standard information form under a heading "Liable trader and contact points", and gives the organiser a seven-day refund claim against a supplier that cancels. Member States must transpose by 29 September 2028 and apply the measures from 29 March 2029.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**The duty to be contactable**

- Art. 1(10), inserting art. 16a(1): "The organiser shall ensure that it is easily contactable through the contact points indicated in accordance with Article 7(2), point (d), and able to respond efficiently to queries from, or on behalf of, travellers, and in particular those in relation to the provision of assistance under Article 16, where a traveller is in difficulty, or in relation to any lack of conformity occurring during the performance of the package and the related remedies provided for in Article 13." [full-text]
- Art. 16a(2), first and second subparagraphs: "The organiser shall also have in place arrangements for the effective handling of types of complaints other than those referred to in paragraph 1." and "The organiser shall acknowledge receipt of such complaints, on a durable medium, within seven days from their receipt and shall provide a reasoned reply to the traveller, on a durable medium, within 60 days after the date of the complaint." [full-text]
- Art. 16a(2), third subparagraph: "Member States may provide for shorter periods than those referred to in the second subparagraph of this paragraph." [full-text]
- Art. 16a(3): "Unless the organiser fully accepts the traveller’s complaint, the organiser shall inform the traveller, in the reasoned reply, of any ADR mechanisms pursuant to Directive 2013/11/EU available to the traveller in the matter at issue." [full-text]
- The recital on art. 16a speaks only to paragraph 2. Recital 28: "In order to enhance the effectiveness of Directive (EU) 2015/2302, organisers should be required to comply with certain standards when handling complaints, including an obligation to acknowledge receipt within a period of seven days and to provide a reasoned reply within a period of 60 days." The word "contactable" occurs once in the whole instrument, in art. 16a(1); no recital explains that paragraph. [full-text]
- Art. 16a sets no time within which the organiser must answer a query under paragraph 1, and attaches no remedy to a failure to be contactable. [full-text]

**Contact details and the information form**

- Art. 1(5)(b), replacing art. 5(1)(b): "the trading name and geographical address of the organiser and, where applicable, of the retailer, as well as their telephone number and, where applicable, an electronic address, enabling the traveller to contact the organiser and, where applicable, the retailer quickly and communicate with them effectively;". [full-text]
- Art. 1(7)(a), replacing art. 7(2)(b): the contract must state "that the organiser is responsible for the proper performance of all travel services included in the package travel contract in accordance with Article 13, for any refunds due as a result of the termination of, or changes to, that contract, and for providing assistance in accordance with Article 16 if the traveller is in difficulty;" and "where applicable, that the traveller can also contact the organiser via the retailer;". [full-text]
- Art. 1(7)(b), replacing art. 7(2)(g): "information on complaint-handling arrangements, including the language or languages in which a complaint can be submitted and will be handled and the physical or electronic address to which complaints can be submitted". [full-text]
- Art. 1(7)(c), inserting art. 7(2a): "The relevant information form set out in Annex I shall be attached to the package travel contract and made available to the traveller on a durable medium at the time of the conclusion of that contract." Recital 27 gives the reason: the form is attached "so that it is available to travellers after the conclusion of that contract, together with contact details of the relevant traders". [full-text]
- New Annex I, Part A, under the heading "Liable trader and contact points": "There is always at least one trader who is liable for the proper performance of all travel services included in a package. That trader is the organiser of the package as identified by the text in the box above and in the package travel contract. The contract must contain the organiser’s contact details." [full-text]
- Same heading: "Travellers will be given an emergency telephone number or details of a contact point that they can use to get in touch with the organiser or the retailer while they are on their trip or holiday." [full-text]
- New Annex I, Part A, under "Problems during the trip or holiday": "The organiser is obliged to remedy problems occurring during the performance of the package. Travellers must inform the organiser of problems they encounter." [full-text]

**Who is the organiser**

- Art. 1(13): "Chapter VI, containing Article 19, is deleted". Recital 5 gives the reason: the rules on linked travel arrangements "have considerably complicated the legislative framework", and "There is no evidence that those rules on linked travel arrangements have resulted in tangible benefits for travellers." [full-text]
- Art. 1(6), inserting art. 5a(1): where a booking does not create a package and "a trader invites a traveller to purchase an additional type of travel service for the same trip or holiday", the trader must say, at the time of the invitation, that the services "will not constitute a package and the traveller will not benefit from the rights applicable to packages under this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 5a(2): where the trader makes that invitation before the traveller agrees to pay for the first service and does not give the information, and the traveller agrees to pay for the additional type of service "at the same point of sale within 24 hours after agreeing to pay for the first type of travel service, the relevant travel services shall constitute a package, and the trader shall be deemed to be the organiser of that package." [full-text]
- Art. 1(16), replacing art. 23(1): "A declaration by an organiser of a package that it is acting exclusively as a travel service provider, as an intermediary or in any other capacity, or that a package does not constitute a package, shall not absolve that organiser from the obligations imposed on it under this Directive." [full-text]
- Art. 1(4)(a), replacing the second subparagraph of art. 3(2): one travel service plus other tourist services is not a package if those services "do not account for at least 25 % of the value of the combination and are not advertised as and do not otherwise represent an essential feature of the combination". [full-text]
- Recital 10 on hotels: "other tourist services that are related to accommodation, are not considered to be travel services in their own right. Consequently, their inclusion cannot give rise to the creation of a package, even if their value represents at least 25 % of the total price." And services "selected and purchased only after the performance of a travel service, such as accommodation, has started will not lead to the creation of a package". [full-text]
- Between the firms, art. 1(15), inserting art. 22(2): "where a travel service provider cancels a service forming part of a package or fails to provide the service, that service provider refunds to the organiser any payments made by the organiser for that service within seven days." [full-text]

**What is left untouched, and the dates**

- The amending points in art. 1 are, in order: the title; arts 1, 2, 3, 5; new art. 5a; arts 7 and 12; new art. 12a (vouchers); new art. 16a; arts 17 and 18; deletion of Chapter VI; arts 21, 22 and 23(1); Annex I replaced; Annex II deleted. No point amends art. 13, 14, 15 or 16, or art. 7(2)(d). [full-text]
- Art. 3(1): "By 29 September 2028, Member States shall adopt and publish the measures necessary to comply with this Directive." and "They shall apply those measures from 29 March 2029." [full-text]
- Art. 4: the Directive enters into force "on the twentieth day following that of its publication"; the header gives publication on 8.5.2026. Art. 5: "This Directive is addressed to the Member States." [full-text]
- Recital 30: the Directive "respects the freedom to conduct a business laid down in Article 16 of the Charter, while ensuring a high level of consumer protection". [full-text]

**What the text does not contain** (whole-text searches of the saved file, 11 Nov 2026)

- No provision says that a person must answer at the contact point or that whoever answers must have authority to decide. "natural person" occurs once, in the definition of trader (art. 3(7) as replaced); "human" occurs once, in "human health" (recital 18); "automat" does not occur. [full-text]
- No provision obliges the organiser to supervise or monitor suppliers. "supervise" and "monitor" occur only for Member States' oversight of insolvency protection (recital 23; art. 17(3) as replaced), and "supervision" only for Member States' supervision of organisers (art. 18(2) as replaced). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Not yet applicable.** Until national measures apply from 29 March 2029 (art. 3(1)), the law in force is the 2015 text: a contact point must be named, and the organiser must remedy and assist. The paper may describe art. 16a as adopted; it may not describe it as binding on organisers today.
- **Packages only**, as for the parent Directive. Recital 10 repeats that services related to accommodation do not make a hotel stay a package.
- **"Easily contactable" and "able to respond efficiently" are the whole standard.** No time limit, no person, no authority to change the outcome. K7's conclusion asks for a channel to a person with power over the single case. Art. 16a(1) requires that the channel work and that the organiser can answer.
- **The split in art. 16a is by subject, not by date.** Paragraph 1 covers queries about assistance and about "any lack of conformity occurring during the performance of the package and the related remedies provided for in Article 13". Paragraph 2 covers "types of complaints other than those referred to in paragraph 1". The scout's note reads paragraph 2 as "afterwards"; the text does not say so. The tie to the time of the trip comes from art. 7(2)(d), whose contact points serve a traveller "in difficulty" or a failure "perceived during the performance of the package" (card eu2015package), and from the form's words "while they are on their trip or holiday".
- **No rationale is given for paragraph 1.** Recital 28 explains the deadlines of paragraph 2 only. The paper cannot quote the legislator on why an organiser must be reachable during performance; the nearest statement is recital 34 of the 2015 Directive, "The traveller should be entitled to have problems resolved".
- **Art. 5a supports a holding-out reading of who becomes organiser.** A trader that prompts the second purchase and stays silent is deemed the organiser. The rule is confined to an invitation made before the traveller agrees to pay for the first service, at the same point of sale, within 24 hours.
- **The United Kingdom.** The Directive is addressed to the Member States (art. 5). The UK Regulations read on card uk2018package contain no counterpart to art. 16a.
- Directive (EU) 2025/2647 is cited in footnote 8 as amending Directive 2015/2302 as well; I did not read it.
- Later treatment not checked. No national transposition was read.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports the conclusion, for packages, from 2029; narrows it on the person.** Art. 16a(1) is a statutory duty on the one answerable party to keep the channel working and to be able to respond, tied to help and to failures occurring during performance and to "the related remedies provided for in Article 13", which are the remedies available while the trip runs. Art. 16a(2) handles other complaints by acknowledgement and reasoned reply on fixed dates. That is the nearest thing in the texts I read to K7's line between interruptibility and accountability, with the caveat that the statute divides by subject and the paper divides by whether performance is still possible. The provision confirms that a legislature, having made one party answerable for others' performance, went on to require that party to be reachable. It does not state P3, and it requires neither a person nor power over the single case.

**K8 (holder under delegation): supports.** The new form states the rule in a sentence, "There is always at least one trader who is liable for the proper performance of all travel services included in a package" (Annex I, Part A), art. 5a(2) deems a silent inviting trader the organiser, and art. 23(1) as replaced keeps the bar on the label "intermediary". Deleting linked travel arrangements removes the category in which each provider answered alone.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- In 2026 the EU legislature added to package-travel law a duty on the organiser to be easily contactable through the contact points named in the contract and able to respond efficiently to queries about assistance and about a lack of conformity occurring during the package (art. 16a(1), inserted by art. 1(10)).
- The same article treats other complaints separately, requiring acknowledgement within seven days and a reasoned reply within sixty (art. 16a(2)).
- Member States must apply the new rules from 29 March 2029 (art. 3(1)).
- The revised information form tells travellers that one trader is always liable for all the travel services in a package and that they will be given an emergency number or contact point for use during the trip (Annex I, Part A).
- A trader that invites a traveller to add another type of travel service, before the traveller has agreed to pay for the first, without saying that the result is not a package is deemed the organiser if the traveller buys within 24 hours at the same point of sale (art. 5a(2)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That organisers are under this duty now. It applies from 29 March 2029.
- That the duty covers hotels, or any stay sold alone.
- That art. 16a gives the traveller a right to a human being, or to someone with authority to change the decision. Neither appears in the text.
- That art. 16a(2) governs complaints "after the trip". It governs complaints "other than those referred to in paragraph 1".
- That the legislator explained why an organiser must be contactable during performance. Recital 28 explains only the complaint deadlines.
- That a traveller has a stated remedy for an unanswered contact point. The article attaches none.
- That this Directive is law in the United Kingdom.
