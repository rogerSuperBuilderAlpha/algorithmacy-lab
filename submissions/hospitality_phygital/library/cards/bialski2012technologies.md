## Summary

Ethnography of CouchSurfing (participant observation 2005-2010, twenty interviews, a survey of about 3,000 members). Coins 'technologies of hospitality' for tools that let strangers plan encounters, and argues that such encounters can be enriching and can also enable the abuse of host power. Describes three stages of familiarization and the hosts' 'fantasies of control'.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The term: tools that allow planned encounters between strangers are "what I term 'technologies of hospitality'" (p. 246) [full-text]
- Thesis: interaction "can be enriching for the host and guest ... yet can also be problematic, fostering moments of awkwardness, misunderstanding, distrust and the abuse of power" (p. 247) [full-text]
- Platform design and the host's choice: "The online profile offers hosts the power to choose one member over the other" (p. 257) [full-text]
- Platform rules on hosts: the website "calculates a user's response rate", and a low rating "'bumps' a user to the bottom of the search list for their city", so a host must write back to all requests, even those rejected (p. 253) [full-text]
- No formal rules: "Without formal rules to regulate the host–guest encounter, hosts often abide by their own set of rules of safety" (p. 253) [full-text]
- "Mobile hosts are less accountable to any one given sociocultural setting or social network, and can create their own set of rules of hospitality." (p. 250) [full-text]
- Crossref: Hospitality & Society 1(3), pp. 245-260, 2012 [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- CouchSurfing is non-commercial; the host's power here is a private person's, not a firm's. Use it for what a platform's design does to hosting, not for commercial hosts' duties.
