## Summary

A grounded theory of algorithmic management at Uber, from drivers' online forum posts and
interviews with drivers in London and New York plus Uber executives and engineers. Algorithms do
matching and control at once. Drivers experience three tensions: work execution (autonomy versus
algorithmic supervision), compensation (uncertainty versus repeatability) and belonging, and they
respond with market-like and organization-like behaviours.
