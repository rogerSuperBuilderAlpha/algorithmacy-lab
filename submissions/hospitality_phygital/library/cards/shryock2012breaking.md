## Summary

Working from Balga Bedouin hospitality (karam) to Kant and Derrida, argues that hospitality is a test
of sovereignty, that bad hosts and bad guests reveal its component parts, and that bad hospitality
accumulates where the scale of the house shifts: houses absorbing houses, states taking over what
citizens owed as hosts, and the market in hotels taking over the care of strangers.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Hospitality "is also a test of sovereignty." (p. S20) [full-text]
- The good host's capacities: "The man who is karim (hospitable, generous, noble) is able to feed others, project an honourable and enviable reputation, and protect guests from harm." (p. S20) [full-text]
- Bad hospitality "accumulates in the gaps created by shifts in scale" (p. S20, abstract) [full-text]
- Guests "depend on their hosts for protection and respect" (p. S23) [full-text]

- "sovereignty is manifest in the ability to act as host" (p. S25) [full-text]
- "hosts and guests cannot interact as equals, even when they are" (p. S28) [full-text]
- On Derrida: "The problem, for Derrida, is one of accepting Others, yet retaining the power to accept them, a stance that re-creates a sovereign space that cannot be occupied on the same terms by guest and host." (p. S30) [full-text]
- Bad hosts and guests "are first of all people who refuse to accept the proper role of host or guest"; the refusal arises "when guest and host cannot agree on who controls the space of interaction, who is sovereign, who belongs, and who owes or should offer respect." (p. S30) [full-text]
- Names the commercial successor: "How does one care for people in this space? Who does so? The answers, obvious to us now, are provided by the hospitality industry (the market in hotels, restaurants, and other sites of temporary accommodation)" (p. S30) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- The bib note's gloss, "host authority is constituted by guest obligation", overstates the text. Shryock says sovereignty is *manifest* in the ability to host (p. S25) and that hospitality breaks down when the parties refuse their roles or dispute who controls the space (p. S30). He does not say the guest's obligation constitutes the host's authority. Use the narrower wording.
- It supports *Holder* well: the obligation attaches to whoever holds sovereignty over the space, and p. S30 hands that position, in modern conditions, to the hospitality industry. It supports *Origin* only indirectly: arrival is the occasion for the host's test, but Shryock does not phrase it as a trigger of obligation.
- "Shifts in scale" (p. S20) is the anthropological ancestor of the paper's own diagnosis: when the house is reorganized, bad hospitality collects in the gap. The paper may borrow the structure, but algorithmic mediation is the authors' application, not Shryock's.
- Out of preferred venue (anthropology); see the hold rationale.
