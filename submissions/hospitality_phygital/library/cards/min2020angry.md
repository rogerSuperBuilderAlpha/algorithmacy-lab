## Summary

A three-page research report in which Min and Kim summarise experiments built on a restaurant service failure, published in full as Min, Joireman and Kim (2019). The point: anger alone does not make a customer complain. Customers who feel powerful complain face to face as soon as they are angry; customers who feel powerless stay silent although very upset. A slogan on a fictional restaurant's menu ("where the customer is always king" against "only one bite and we got you") shifted participants' sense of power, and those in the low-power condition complained less in public. The authors advise operators to design the setting, including employees' body language, so that guests feel able to speak up on site. The report gives no sample sizes, procedures or statistics.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Design as described: "Through experimental designs using a failed service in the restaurant, we investigated whether all angry customers complain to the service provider and if not, what prevents them from raising their voice" (printed p. 1) [full-text]
- Finding: "However, we found that not all angry customers complain. The key factor that determines whether angry customers will speak up their mind or not is a sense of power." (printed p. 1) [full-text]
- "The results show that customers who feel powerful indeed complain about a service failure as soon as they are angry; on the other hand, customers feeling powerless are not comfortable making face-to-face complaints in the restaurant although they are very upset." (printed p. 2) [full-text]
- Manipulation and result: three menu slogans for the restaurant 'Atena' set low, high and control power conditions; "participants in the low-power condition indeed complain less in public, reducing an opportunity for the service provider to hear from customers." (printed p. 2) [full-text]
- The authors' view of why on-site reports matter: "Handling on-site complaints is much more efficient and effective than negative word-of-mouth or negative online reviews because hospitality operators have an opportunity to please customers before their departure, and this prompt resolution minimizes the possible negative word-of-mouth or reviews." (printed p. 2) [full-text]
- Advice: "Customers may feel powerless if treated by employees with assertive postures." (printed p. 2) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **A summary, not the study.** No N, no statistics, no description of participants; whether the experiments used students, panels or diners is not stated. The citable evidence is Min, Joireman and Kim (2019), which this run could not open.
- **Vignette restaurant, not a hotel.** 'Atena' is a fictional restaurant; the report speaks of hospitality operators generally (printed p. 2).
- **Intention, not behaviour.** "Complain less in public" (printed p. 2) is presumably a stated intention in a scenario; the report does not say.
- **Venue.** ICHRIE Research Reports are accepted "by an authorized editor" (cover sheet, PDF p. 1); peer review is not stated.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7, P4, first half: narrows (rival).** The guest's own report, which P4 treats as the timely signal, depends on the guest feeling able to make it. Angry guests who feel powerless stay silent, and the operator loses the chance "to hear from customers" (printed p. 2). The report does not refute that the guest's report is often the only signal; it shows that the signal is often not sent, and that the setting, staff included, affects whether it is.

**K7, P4, second half; K9: no bearing.**
