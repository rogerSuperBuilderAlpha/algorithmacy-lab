## Summary

The Regulations transposed Directive (EU) 2015/2302 and came into force on 1 July 2018, replacing the 1992 Regulations for contracts concluded from that date. They bind organisers, retailers and other traders, and they work in two ways. Part 4 writes terms into every package travel contract: the organiser is liable to the traveller for the travel services whoever performs them (reg. 15(2)), must remedy a failure within a reasonable period the traveller sets unless that is impossible or disproportionately costly (reg. 15(4)), owes a price reduction and compensation subject to three defences (reg. 16), must accept messages through the retailer (reg. 17) and must assist a traveller in difficulty without undue delay (reg. 18). Part 2, by contrast, makes the content of the contract, including the contact point of Schedule 5, a matter of criminal enforcement by trading standards and the Civil Aviation Authority. No traveller can waive the rights, and an organiser cannot avoid them by calling itself an intermediary (reg. 30). An amending instrument made in April 2026 abolishes linked travel arrangements from 6 April 2027 and leaves Part 4 and Schedule 5 as they are.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Scope, parties and commencement**

- Reg. 1(2): "these Regulations come into force on 1st July 2018". Reg. 37(2) keeps the 1992 Regulations for older contracts: "The revocations made by paragraph (1) do not have effect in relation to any contract which was concluded under the 1992 Regulations before the commencement date." [full-text]
- Reg. 3(1): the Regulations apply to "packages offered for sale or sold by traders to travellers" and to linked travel arrangements. Reg. 3(2) excludes arrangements of under 24 hours without overnight accommodation, those offered "occasionally on a not-for-profit basis for a limited group of travellers", and those "purchased on the basis of a general agreement" for business travel. [full-text]
- Reg. 2(5): a package is "a combination of at least two different types of travel services for the purpose of the same trip or holiday". Reg. 2(1) lists as one travel service "the provision of accommodation which is not intrinsically part of the carriage of passengers and is not for residential purposes". Reg. 2(6)(b) excludes tourist services "selected and purchased after the performance of a travel service" has started. [full-text]
- Reg. 2(1): the organiser is "a trader who combines and sells, or offers for sale, packages, either directly or through another trader or together with another trader"; the traveller is "any individual who is seeking to conclude a contract, or is entitled to travel on the basis of a contract concluded, within the scope of these Regulations"; lack of conformity is "a failure to perform, or the improper performance of, the travel services included in a package". [full-text]

**Who answers for services performed by others (implied terms)**

- Reg. 15(1): "The provisions of this regulation are implied as a term in every package travel contract." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(2): "The organiser is liable to the traveller for the performance of the travel services included in the package travel contract, irrespective of whether those services are to be performed by the organiser or by other travel service providers." [full-text]
- Reg. 36: "Where it is provided in these Regulations that a term or condition is implied in the contract it is so implied irrespective of the law which governs the contract." [full-text]
- Reg. 27: where the organiser is established outside the United Kingdom and a UK retailer sells its packages, "the retailer is subject to the obligations for organisers set out in Parts 4 and 5, unless the retailer provides evidence that the organiser complies with those Parts." Otherwise Part 4 lays liability on the organiser alone. [full-text]
- Reg. 29: an organiser that pays compensation, grants a price reduction or meets its other obligations "may seek redress from any third parties which contributed to the event triggering compensation, a price reduction or other obligations." [full-text]
- The contrast case. For a linked travel arrangement the trader must state that the traveller "will not benefit from any of the rights applying exclusively to packages under these Regulations and that each service provider will be solely responsible for the proper contractual performance of the service" (reg. 26(7)(a)(i)). [full-text]

**Remedies while the trip is under way (implied terms)**

- Reg. 15(3): "The traveller must inform the organiser without undue delay, taking into account the circumstances of the case, of any lack of conformity which the traveller perceives during the performance of a travel service included in the package travel contract." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(4): "If any of the travel services are not performed in accordance with the package travel contract, the organiser must remedy the lack of conformity within a reasonable period set by the traveller unless that— (a) is impossible; or (b) entails disproportionate costs, taking into account the extent of the lack of conformity and the value of the travel services affected." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(5): "Where the organiser does not remedy the lack of conformity within a reasonable period set by the traveller for a reason mentioned in sub-paragraph (a) or (b) of paragraph (4), regulation 16 applies." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(6): "Where the organiser refuses to remedy the lack of conformity or where immediate remedy is required, the traveller— (a) may remedy the lack of conformity; and (b) is entitled to reimbursement of the necessary expenses." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(7): such a traveller "is not required to— (a) set a reasonable period pursuant to paragraph (4), and (b) if such a period has been set, wait until the end of the period, before the traveller remedies the lack of conformity." [full-text]
- Reg. 15(8): where the organiser cannot provide a significant proportion of the services, it "must offer, at no extra cost to the traveller, suitable alternative arrangements". Reg. 15(11): where a lack of conformity substantially affects the package and "the organiser fails to remedy the lack of conformity within the reasonable period", the traveller may terminate without a fee. [full-text]
- Reg. 18(1)-(2): implied as a term, "Where a traveller is in difficulty, the organiser must give appropriate assistance without undue delay, including in the circumstances referred to in regulation 15(14), in particular by— (a) providing appropriate information on health services, local authorities and consular assistance; and (b) assisting the traveller to make distance communications and helping the traveller to find alternative travel arrangements." [full-text]
- Reg. 17(1)-(4): implied as a term, "The traveller may address messages, requests or complaints in relation to the performance of the package directly to the retailer through which it was purchased."; "The retailer must forward those messages, requests or complaints to the organiser without undue delay."; and receipt by the retailer counts "as receipt by the organiser" for time limits. [full-text]

**Remedies afterwards, and the defences (implied terms)**

- Reg. 16(2): "The organiser must offer the traveller an appropriate price reduction for any period during which there is a lack of conformity, unless the organiser proves that the lack of conformity is attributable to the traveller." [full-text]
- Reg. 16(3): "The organiser must offer the traveller, without undue delay, appropriate compensation for any damage which the traveller sustains as a result of any lack of conformity." [full-text]
- Reg. 16(4): no compensation "if the organiser proves that the lack of conformity is— (a) attributable to the traveller; (b) attributable to a third party unconnected with the provision of the travel services included in the package travel contract and is unforeseeable or unavoidable; or (c) due to unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances." [full-text]
- Reg. 16(6): the contract may limit compensation as long as the limitation "does not apply to personal injury or damage caused intentionally or with negligence or does not limit any liability that cannot be limited by law" and "does not amount to less than 3 times the total price of the package." [full-text]

**The contact point and the other information duties (criminal enforcement)**

- Reg. 4(2): "Where a package travel contract is not sold through a retailer, the organiser must perform the information duties imposed by regulations 5, 6 and 7." Where a retailer sells, organiser and retailer must between them ensure the duties are performed (reg. 4(1)). [full-text]
- Sch. 1, para. 11 (before the contract): "The trading name and geographical address of the organiser and, where applicable, of the retailer, as well as their telephone number and, where applicable, e-mail address." [full-text]
- Reg. 7(2): "The relevant person must ensure that the package travel contract sets out the full content of the package and includes— (a) the information specified in Schedule 1; and (b) the information specified in Schedule 5." [full-text]
- Sch. 5, para. 4: "The name, address, telephone number, e-mail address and, where applicable, the fax number of the organiser's local representative, of a contact point or of another service which enables the traveller to contact the organiser quickly and communicate with the organiser efficiently, to request assistance when the traveller is in difficulty or to complain about any lack of conformity perceived during the performance of the package." [full-text]
- Sch. 5, para. 2: information that the organiser is "responsible for the proper performance of all travel services included in the contract in accordance with regulation 15" and "obliged to provide assistance if the traveller is in difficulty in accordance with regulation 18". Para. 5: "Information that the traveller is required to communicate any lack of conformity which the traveller perceives during the performance of the package in accordance with regulation 15(3)." [full-text]
- The standard pre-contract wording, Sch. 3, Part 2: "There is always at least one trader who is liable for the proper performance of all the travel services included in the contract." and "Travellers are given an emergency telephone number or details of a contact point where they can get in touch with the organiser or the travel agent." (Sch. 2, Part 2, has "an emergency number".) [full-text]
- The contact point is not an implied term. Reg. 7(10): "It is an implied condition (or, as regards Scotland, an implied term) of the contract that the relevant person complies with paragraphs (1), (3) to (6) and (9)." Paragraph (2), which carries Schedule 5, is not in that list. [full-text]
- It is enforced as an offence. Reg. 7(12): "Where the relevant person fails to comply with paragraph (2), (7) or (8), the organiser or, where the package travel contract is sold through a retailer, both the organiser and the retailer, commit an offence". Reg. 31(1) names the enforcers: "Every local weights and measures authority in Great Britain is to be an enforcement authority for the purposes of regulations 5, 7, 19, 25 and 26"; reg. 31(2) adds the Civil Aviation Authority. [full-text]
- The offence has a defence and gives no civil claim. Reg. 32(1): "it is a defence for that person to show that the person took all reasonable steps and exercised all due diligence to avoid committing the offence." Reg. 35: "No contract is void or unenforceable, and no right of action in civil proceedings in respect of any loss arises, by reason only of the commission of an offence under regulations 5(5), 7(12), 19(9), 25 or 26(10)." [full-text]
- Reg. 8(1): in a dispute about compliance with Part 2, "it is for the organiser or the retailer, as appropriate, to show that the provision was complied with." [full-text]

**Whether the provisions can be excluded by contract**

- Reg. 30(1): a declaration by an organiser that it "is acting exclusively as a travel service provider, as an intermediary or in any other capacity", or that a package is not a package, "does not absolve that organiser or trader from the obligations imposed upon them under these Regulations." [full-text]
- Reg. 30(2): "A traveller may not waive any right granted to the traveller by these Regulations." Reg. 30(3): any arrangement or statement that "directly or indirectly waives or restricts the rights conferred on travellers pursuant to these Regulations" or "aims to circumvent the application of these Regulations" "is not binding on the traveller." [full-text]

**Amendments made and not yet in force: SI 2026/455**

- The Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/455), made 22 April 2026, "come into force on 6th April 2027" (reg. 1(1)) and do not affect a package travel contract or linked travel arrangement "entered into before the commencement date" (reg. 1(4)). [full-text]
- They remove linked travel arrangements (reg. 2(2)(b), (3), (4), (13)) and add a limb to the definition of package: services "provided under separate contracts with individual service providers if a single trader has facilitated, on the occasion of a single visit to, or contact with, the trader's point of sale, the separate selection of and separate payment for each of the travel services by the traveller." (reg. 2(2)(c)(ii)). [full-text]
- They replace reg. 29 with two rules between firms. New reg. 29A(1): a third party that fails to perform or cancels "must refund to the organiser or, in a case under regulation 27, the retailer any payments which the organiser or retailer has made to the third party within 14 days." New reg. 29B: "the organiser or retailer has a right of redress, including damages, from any third party which contributed to the event triggering the obligation to pay compensation, provide a price reduction or meet other obligations under these Regulations and may bring a claim in civil proceedings to enforce that right." (reg. 2(6)). [full-text]
- They do not amend regs 4-8 or 15-18 or Schedules 1-5, and they add no duty to be contactable. The 24 Oct 2026 contents page of SI 2018/634 lists the affected provisions as regs 2, 3, 26, 28-35 and Schs 6-10 only. [full-text]

**What the text does not contain** (whole-text search of the body capture and the Schedules read, 11 Nov 2026)

- Nothing says who or what must answer at the contact point: "human", "natural person" and "automat" do not occur. Nothing obliges the organiser to supervise or monitor suppliers: "supervis" and "monitor" do not occur. [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Packages only.** A hotel stay booked alone is one travel service and outside the Regulations (reg. 2(5)). A hotel is caught as organiser only where it combines and sells the stay with another type of travel service.
- **In English law the channel is not something the traveller can sue for by itself.** The contact point of Sch. 5, para. 4 enters the contract through reg. 7(2), which is left out of the implied conditions (reg. 7(10)), enforced by prosecution (reg. 7(12); reg. 31), excused by due diligence (reg. 32(1)), and barred as a ground of civil action (reg. 35). What the traveller holds as contractual rights are the organiser's liability (reg. 15(2)), the duty to remedy (reg. 15(4)), the right to self-help (reg. 15(6)), assistance (reg. 18) and the retailer channel (reg. 17). For K5 and K6 this is a per-duty answer: remedy and assistance are owed to the traveller; the contact point is owed to the public authorities.
- **The UK wording of the remedy duty differs from the Directive's.** Reg. 15(4) builds the traveller's period into the duty ("must remedy the lack of conformity within a reasonable period set by the traveller"); art. 13(3) of the Directive states the duty without a period and puts the period in art. 13(4). Reg. 15(6) gives self-help where the organiser "refuses" or "immediate remedy is required"; it does not repeat the Directive's first case, where the period set by the traveller simply runs out. This is my comparison of the two texts.
- **No person, no authority, no monitoring duty**, as in the Directive.
- **Cost and impossibility release the organiser from remedy** (reg. 15(4)(a), (b)), after which money is the remedy (reg. 15(5); reg. 16).
- **No counterpart to art. 16a of the amended Directive.** Neither the 2018 text nor SI 2026/455 obliges the organiser to be contactable.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K8 (holder under delegation): supports the contract limb in English law, for packages.** Reg. 15(2) is a statutory implied term that the organiser answers for the travel services whether they are performed "by the organiser or by other travel service providers"; it survives a choice of foreign law (reg. 36) and a self-description as intermediary (reg. 30(1)), and the supplier's share goes to redress between firms (reg. 29; from 6 April 2027, reg. 29B with a civil claim). The defences of reg. 16(4) and the cap of reg. 16(6) narrow it. From 6 April 2027 a single trader that facilitates separate selection and payment at one visit becomes an organiser (SI 2026/455, reg. 2(2)(c)(ii)), which moves a facilitator into the answerable role.

**K7 (channel from the guest): supports in part, and narrows.** The Regulations join an answerable party to duties to remedy within a period the traveller sets and to assist without undue delay, both enforceable by the traveller as contract terms (regs 15(4), 18), with money as the fallback (reg. 16). The channel itself, the contact point, is a regulatory requirement with no civil action (regs 7(10), 7(12), 35). So in the United Kingdom the law gives the traveller a claim to correction during the trip and leaves the means of asking for it to trading-standards enforcement.

**K5 (directedness): narrows per duty.** Within one instrument some duties are implied terms owed to the traveller and one, the contact point, is a public duty enforced by indictment or summary conviction with a due-diligence defence.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- In United Kingdom law every package travel contract contains a term that the organiser is liable to the traveller for the travel services whether the organiser or another provider performs them (reg. 15(1)-(2)).
- The same contract contains terms that the organiser must remedy a failure within a reasonable period set by the traveller, unless that is impossible or disproportionately costly, and must give appropriate assistance without undue delay to a traveller in difficulty (regs 15(4), 18(2)).
- Where the organiser refuses or immediate remedy is required, the traveller may remedy the failure and recover the necessary expense (reg. 15(6)).
- The contract must name a local representative, contact point or other service through which the traveller can reach the organiser quickly during the trip (reg. 7(2); Sch. 5, para. 4), and failure to include it is a criminal offence (reg. 7(12)).
- The traveller cannot waive these rights, and an organiser's statement that it acts only as intermediary does not release it (reg. 30).

**What the paper may not say**

- That these Regulations apply to a hotel booked directly.
- That a traveller can sue in the United Kingdom for the absence of the contact point as such. The requirement is outside the implied conditions, and there is no civil action "by reason only of the commission of an offence" (regs 7(10), 35). Failure of a named contact point to answer is not addressed.
- That the Regulations require a person, or a person with authority, at the contact point.
- That United Kingdom law contains the duty to be "easily contactable" found in art. 16a of the amended Directive.
- That the organiser must supervise the hotel. The Regulations impose liability for performance, not a duty of oversight.
- That *Kuoni* was decided under these Regulations.
- That linked travel arrangements remain part of United Kingdom law after 6 April 2027 for new contracts.
