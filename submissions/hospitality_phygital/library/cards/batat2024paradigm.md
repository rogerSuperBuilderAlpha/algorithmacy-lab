## Summary

A short guest editorial (pp. 357-365) introducing the *Qualitative Market Research* special issue "The Future of Interpretivist Research Methodology is Phygital" (p. 358). It proposes the "phygital research paradigm", a guide to *studying* consumers in hybrid physical-digital settings, built on four philosophical foundations: liquid ontology, dual axiology, interpretivist epistemology and multimethodology (p. 357; Figure 1, p. 359). Each foundation is illustrated by an article from the issue. The editorial concerns research design from first page to last. It prescribes nothing to firms, platforms, hosts or regulators. Hospitality, employees, governance and AI in service delivery are not discussed. AI appears once, as a research tool (Schmitt 2023, p. 362).

## Critical notes & threats

- **The PSR attribution does not hold.** The call for papers writes "phygital service research (PSR) (Batat, 2024b)" and lists this editorial as its 2024b (CfP, PDF p. 2). The editorial never uses the phrase. Batat makes the same attribution herself. HFPV writes "phygital service research (PSR) paradigm ... (Batat, 2024a)", and HFPV's 2024a is this editorial (batat2026hfpv, pp. 270, 280). PHIVE cites "The phygital research paradigm (Batat, 2024b, 2025)", where 2025 is the PSR article (batat2026phive, p. 6). The author evidently treats this paradigm as the philosophical base of PSR, but the term and its content are in batat2026psr. The bibliography note's instruction stands: cite the two separately, and cite batat2026psr for anything PSR says.
- **Year labels clash across Batat's papers.** The call for papers, PHIVE and this library label this editorial 2024b and PH-CX 2024a. HFPV reverses them, labelling this editorial 2024a and PH-CX 2024b (batat2026hfpv, p. 280). A citation copied from HFPV with its label would point to the wrong paper.
- **`dual-place-process` is carried only in part.** The editorial speaks of transitions "from one setting to another, and vice versa" (p. 357) but never names digitalization or physicalization; those terms come from PH-CX (batat2024phcx, pp. 1230-1231). The slug stays, as the CLAIMS convention requires, with this note.
- **`not-that-triad` is supported.** Liquid ontology's object-subject-context triad (p. 359) places a context third, which is the kind of triad the paper distinguishes itself from.
- **The editorial is not evidence for the gap statement.** My reading: it prescribes no safeguards for guests or consumers, so it cannot support "phygital research prescribes safeguards". Its silence on who owes what is the silence of a methods editorial. Citing it as evidence that phygital service research ignores the question would be unfair to the author.

## Where it bears on the argument

Peripheral to the v8 thesis. Because the call for papers names it as the source of PSR, a referee may expect to see it cited. It supplies two usable lines: the restated aim of "a seamless delivery of value" (p. 357), for the seamlessness argument, and liquid ontology's object-subject-context triad (p. 359), for distinguishing the paper's triad. On who owes what to whom it says nothing, and it should not be cited on that question either way.
