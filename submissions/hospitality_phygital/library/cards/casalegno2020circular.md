## Summary

Argues the circular economy is an open and dynamic loop of relationships rather than a materials process, turning on the reallocation of stakeholder roles whose power and responsibilities overlap and converge, and enabling emergent joint value creation.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Names "the relationships among stakeholders and the reallocation of stakeholder roles" as the analytic object [full-text]
- Holds that the reallocated power and responsibilities should "overlap and converge" rather than transfer cleanly [full-text]
- Co-authored with R. Edward Freeman, whose stakeholder framework the argument runs on [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- Roles reallocating while responsibilities converge is precisely the structure the paper says fails under algorithmic mediation, where the functions move and the obligation does not follow. The editor supplies the normal case and the paper supplies the pathological one.
- Circular economy is a distant setting. The transfer is at the level of the relational premise and the manuscript should keep it there rather than importing circular-economy vocabulary.
