## Summary

A two-stage interpretivist interview study of frontline employees in 4–5-star hotels in
eastern Australia (22 interviews, then 6 follow-ups). Most employees could not define
hospitality or tell it from service; they defaulted to service-management terms. The authors
treat hospitality as an "essence, or spirit" and service as a "process", identify five themes
(contact moments, exceeding expectations, personality traits, power (in)equality, emotional
labour) and five managerial enablers, and call for an inter-paradigmatic view.

## Critical notes & threats

**Settled: a hospitality-vs-service distinction usable in §2?** Yes for the *displacement* claim, no for the *content* claim. The paper states the distinction plainly (pp. 1283, 1287) and finds service winning in hotel practice (p. 1296, pp. 1299–1300). But its "essence" is an attitude: genuine care, warmth, generosity, freedom from ulterior motive (pp. 1286–1287). Obligation is listed among other definitions and set aside (p. 1284). Citing it for "hospitality is an obligation, not a service" would misstate it. It also supports the paper's line that the field's distinction rests on felt welcome and motive, which the paper argues past.

**Host–guest negotiation?** No. The study is of employees' understandings. The closest material is the power (in)equality theme (pp. 1294–1295, 1298) and the manager's "they cannot demand everything" (p. 1297), which bounds guest claims and does not describe negotiation.

**What pp. 1286 and 1297 support (rr-2026-11-h, H4).**

- p. 1286 is the only located hospitality-management statement of the commercial guest's duties: pay and behave reasonably. It is the authors' paraphrase of King (1995), and the "absolving the guest of mutual obligation" phrase is their quotation of Lashley (2000, p. 14). Neither King nor Lashley has been read on this project; King is now carded at abstract depth (`king1995what`) and the sentence is not in its abstract (King 1995: Crossref 10.1016/0278-4319(95)00045-3, IJHM 14(3-4), 219-234, closed, no abstract in OpenAlex). Cite as "Golubovskaya et al. 2017: 1286, drawing on King 1995", not as King.
- p. 1297 shows a manager bounding guest claims ("they cannot demand everything ... we have own standard"), as one participant (P-7) reports it, from upscale Australian hotels (p. 1300). The authors present the remark as leadership that gives staff "confidence and power to feel like a 'host'": it hands staff a ground for refusing a guest. Reading it as a bound on *staff* discretion, and so as a located instance of organisationally bounded authority for R3.9, is H4's inference; the "empowering" phrase H4 paired it with comes from another participant (P-15) in another paragraph.
- p. 1298 supplies a hotel employee's statement that discourteous guests "will be eventually removed" and otherwise get "a bare minimum at most". It is a stated stance (P-9), not an observed removal, and the authors read it as evidence of "rights' equality between the host and the guest" (p. 1298). The parallel with Pitt-Rivers's infringement rule (card `pittrivers2012law`, p. 517) is H4's.
