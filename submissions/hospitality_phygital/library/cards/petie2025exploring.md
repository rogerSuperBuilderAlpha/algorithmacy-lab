## Summary

Builds a framework joining hospitality and home (four pillars: type of accommodation, food, host-guest relationship, safety) and applies it to two cases built from news, inquiry and NGO reports: the Glasgow hotels used to house asylum seekers during the Covid-19 pandemic, and a far-right attack on a hotel in Knowsley, Merseyside, in 2023. Finds that inhospitality on each pillar produced a sense of unhome, and that poorly managed hosting arrangements bred fear in hosts and guests.

## Critical notes & threats

- The evidence is secondary: the authors read inquiry, evaluation and news reports; they did not interview residents or staff. Attribute the findings to the reports the authors read.
- The guests are not the hotels' customers and the hotels did not hold themselves out to them; the Home Office pays. Use the card for a divided host's unanswered question, not for the commercial host's role obligation.
- Accepted manuscript: paraphrase, or take a journal page from the version of record before quoting.
