## Summary

Twenty-one vignette-prompted interviews (July-August 2023) with people who rent out their homes
short-term, balanced across low, medium and high AI literacy. The case is a risk-scoring system
the municipality of Amsterdam proposed for detecting illegal holiday rentals; it was never
deployed. Participants imagined being flagged, received a mock warning letter and an information
sheet, and said what they would contest and what they would need. Three themes: cooperation in
sense-making, social support in contestation acts (organizational, third-party and peer), and
distributed responsibility. AI literacy shaped information needs but not views on responsibility.

## Critical notes & threats

- (rr-2026-11) The abstract-era fact "clarity about who is accountable is reported as a precondition for contesting at all" overstates the text. What the paper calls a prerequisite is social transparency, and only for ex-ante mechanisms (p. CSCW234:18). The usable finding is narrower and still strong: responsibility claims did not vary with AI literacy (pp. 17-18), and subjects held that correcting the system is not their job (p. 18).
- (rr-2026-11) The paper does not set capability against institution. It treats both as needed: literacy-tailored explanations and third-party help (pp. 17, 21) alongside institutional accountability. "More education is a misdiagnosis" is the manuscript's claim, not theirs.
- (rr-2026-11) Stronger use available: p. CSCW234:16 (a human reviewer preferred because AI "was seen as unable to change the output") and p. 17 (an independent third party) bear directly on v4's fourth principle, where FABLE_fable_2 item 3 found Yurrita et al. 2023 does not carry "a person must stay reachable". These are stated preferences in a hypothetical, not measured effects.
- Hospitality-adjacent only: the decider is a municipality, and the hosts are enforcement subjects, not guests.
