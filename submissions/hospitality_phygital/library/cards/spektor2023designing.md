## Summary

Workshops, interviews and participatory prototyping (2020-2022, 75 unionized guest room attendants, all women) about a widely used algorithmic room-assignment system (pseudonym UpKeep), followed by worker-generated design ideas organised under self-efficacy, transparency and workload. Most configurations the participants used did not let them order their own assignments; most let them see the whole room list, but the most limited configurations showed one room at a time.

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "The clearest documentation comes from the back of house, where housekeeping systems lock task order and, in their most limited configurations, withhold the day's full task list from the very workers carrying it out (Spektor et al. 2023, 2025)." SUPPORT: YES at full text for the 2023 paper, near-verbatim (verifier_2's reading confirmed by Fable): locked order is p. 628 ("most of the configurations… do not allow GRA's to order their own assignment order"); the withheld list is p. 629 ("Only in the most limited configurations, GRAs were instead shown one assignment at a time"). Two cautions. (1) These are workers' reports across properties the authors could not observe (p. 635), not system documentation. (2) The same page says many workers negotiated the one-room-at-a-time configuration away with management, and §5.2 says self-sequencing existed in the software but was not switched on; v4's "systems lock task order" is better written as a configuration choice by the property than as a property of the system.
