## Summary

Five sections of the German Civil Code govern things a guest brings to an innkeeper. An innkeeper who lodges strangers as a business must make good loss of, destruction of or damage to things brought in by a guest received in the course of that business (§ 701). Liability is capped at a hundred times the daily room price, between 600 and 3,500 euros, and is unlimited where the innkeeper or his people are at fault or where the innkeeper took the things into safekeeping or wrongly refused to (§ 702). The innkeeper must accept valuables for safekeeping (§ 702(3)). Liability cannot be waived in advance below the cap (§ 702a). The guest's claim lapses unless the guest notifies the innkeeper without delay (§ 703). The innkeeper has a lien on the guest's things for the bill (§ 704).

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- **The liability.** "Ein Gastwirt, der gewerbsmäßig Fremde zur Beherbergung aufnimmt, hat den Schaden zu ersetzen, der durch den Verlust, die Zerstörung oder die Beschädigung von Sachen entsteht, die ein im Betrieb dieses Gewerbes aufgenommener Gast eingebracht hat." (§ 701(1)). My paraphrase: an innkeeper who takes in strangers for lodging as a business must make good the damage that arises from the loss, destruction or damaging of things brought in by a guest received in the running of that business. [full-text]
- **The official translation of the same sentence.** "An innkeeper who accommodates strangers commercially is to provide compensation for the damage incurred by the loss of, destruction of or damage to things brought in by a guest accommodated in the course of operating such a business." (§ 701(1), translation on gesetze-im-internet.de, under the heading "Bringing things onto the premises of innkeepers") [full-text]
- **What counts as brought in.** Things brought, while the guest is lodged, into the inn or to a place outside it that the innkeeper or his people direct or that the innkeeper has generally appointed, or taken into care outside the inn by the innkeeper or his people; and things taken into care by them within a reasonable time before or after the stay (§ 701(2) nos. 1–2; my paraphrase). [full-text]
- **Staff whom the guest may treat as authorised.** "Im Falle einer Anweisung oder einer Übernahme der Obhut durch Leute des Gastwirts gilt dies jedoch nur, wenn sie dazu bestellt oder nach den Umständen als dazu bestellt anzusehen waren." (§ 701(2), last sentence). My paraphrase: where the direction or the taking into care is by the innkeeper's people, this applies only if they were appointed for that purpose or were, in the circumstances, to be regarded as appointed for it. [full-text]
- **Exclusions.** "Die Ersatzpflicht tritt nicht ein, wenn der Verlust, die Zerstörung oder die Beschädigung von dem Gast, einem Begleiter des Gastes oder einer Person, die der Gast bei sich aufgenommen hat, oder durch die Beschaffenheit der Sachen oder durch höhere Gewalt verursacht wird." (§ 701(3)). My paraphrase: no liability where the loss is caused by the guest, a companion of the guest or a person the guest has taken in, or by the nature of the things, or by force majeure. Vehicles, things left in a vehicle and live animals are outside (§ 701(4)). [full-text]
- **The cap.** "Der Gastwirt haftet auf Grund des § 701 nur bis zu einem Betrag, der dem Hundertfachen des Beherbergungspreises für einen Tag entspricht, jedoch mindestens bis zu dem Betrag von 600 Euro und höchstens bis zu dem Betrag von 3.500 Euro; für Geld, Wertpapiere und Kostbarkeiten tritt an die Stelle von 3.500 Euro der Betrag von 800 Euro." (§ 702(1)) [full-text]
- **When the cap falls away.** "Die Haftung des Gastwirts ist unbeschränkt," where "der Verlust, die Zerstörung oder die Beschädigung von ihm oder seinen Leuten verschuldet ist" or where the things are ones "die er zur Aufbewahrung übernommen oder deren Übernahme zur Aufbewahrung er entgegen der Vorschrift des Absatzes 3 abgelehnt hat" (§ 702(2) nos. 1–2). My paraphrase: unlimited where he or his people are at fault, and for things he took into safekeeping or refused to take contrary to subsection (3). [full-text]
- **A duty to accept valuables.** "Der Gastwirt ist verpflichtet, Geld, Wertpapiere, Kostbarkeiten und andere Wertsachen zur Aufbewahrung zu übernehmen, es sei denn, dass sie im Hinblick auf die Größe oder den Rang der Gastwirtschaft von übermäßigem Wert oder Umfang oder dass sie gefährlich sind." (§ 702(3), first sentence). My paraphrase: the innkeeper is obliged to take money, securities, valuables and other valuable things into safekeeping, unless they are of excessive value or bulk for the size or standing of the inn, or dangerous. [full-text]
- **No contracting out below the cap.** "Die Haftung des Gastwirts kann im Voraus nur erlassen werden, soweit sie den nach § 702 Abs. 1 maßgeblichen Höchstbetrag übersteigt." (§ 702a(1), first sentence). Even above the cap it cannot be waived for loss caused "vorsätzlich oder grob fahrlässig" by the innkeeper or his people, or for things wrongly refused for safekeeping (§ 702a(1), second sentence). "Der Erlass ist nur wirksam, wenn die Erklärung des Gastes schriftlich erteilt ist und wenn sie keine anderen Bestimmungen enthält." (§ 702a(2)). My paraphrase: liability can be waived in advance only so far as it exceeds the cap; the waiver is valid only if the guest's declaration is in writing and contains no other terms. [full-text]
- **What the guest must do.** "Der dem Gast auf Grund der §§ 701, 702 zustehende Anspruch erlischt, wenn nicht der Gast unverzüglich, nachdem er von dem Verlust, der Zerstörung oder der Beschädigung Kenntnis erlangt hat, dem Gastwirt Anzeige macht. Dies gilt nicht, wenn die Sachen von dem Gastwirt zur Aufbewahrung übernommen waren oder wenn der Verlust, die Zerstörung oder die Beschädigung von ihm oder seinen Leuten verschuldet ist." (§ 703). My paraphrase: the guest's claim under §§ 701 and 702 lapses unless the guest, without delay after learning of the loss, notifies the innkeeper; this does not apply where the innkeeper had taken the things into safekeeping or where he or his people are at fault. [full-text]
- **The innkeeper's lien.** "Der Gastwirt hat für seine Forderungen für Wohnung und andere dem Gast zur Befriedigung seiner Bedürfnisse gewährte Leistungen, mit Einschluss der Auslagen, ein Pfandrecht an den eingebrachten Sachen des Gastes." (§ 704, first sentence) [full-text]
- **Ground stated.** None. The sections give rules and no reasons. Liability attaches to a trade ("gewerbsmäßig") and to reception ("aufgenommen"); § 701 does not mention a contract or payment. [full-text]
- **Persons and admission.** §§ 701–704 concern "Sachen" (things) throughout. They contain no rule on the guest's bodily safety and no rule requiring an innkeeper to receive a traveller. [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Five sections, not German law.** I read no other provision of the Code, no other statute, no commentary and no judgment. Whether German law protects a hotel guest's person through its general rules of contract or delict, and whether any rule obliges a hotel to accept a guest, cannot be said from this card either way.
- **Liability without fault is my reading of the structure.** § 701 states the duty to compensate without mentioning fault, and § 702(2) no. 1 makes fault the condition for lifting the cap. The sections do not use a term for strict liability.
- **Lodging only.** The innkeeper of § 701 is one who takes strangers in "zur Beherbergung". On the text a restaurant or bar that lodges nobody is outside. That is a reading of one phrase.
- **The published translation is a convenience.** At § 701(2) no. 2 it has "when the guest was first accommodated" where the German has no word for "first"; it renders "Leute" as "helpers" and "bestellt" as "ordered". Its stated version (2021) and the German text served in November 2026 agree on the amounts and structure of these sections.
- **Relation to the 1962 Convention not checked.** The cap of a hundred times the daily price matches an option in art. 2(a) of the Council of Europe convention (card coe1962hotelkeepers), and §§ 702(3), 702a and 703 run parallel to arts 2, 6 and 5 of its annex. I did not verify that Germany ratified the convention or that these sections implement it.
- **Amounts and wording are as served on 11 Nov 2026.** Later amendment is possible; the single-section pages carry no date.
- **Later treatment not checked.**

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K4 (two tiers): supports the goods limb of reception in a civil-law code; gives nothing on admission or on the person.** The claim that the host's duty is not an Anglo-American accident holds, on this text, for the guest's goods only.
- **K5 (directedness): supports.** The code speaks of "Der dem Gast ... zustehende Anspruch", the claim belonging to the guest (§ 703).
- **K3 (grounds): narrows voluntary assumption.** The liability attaches to the trade and to reception and cannot be waived below the cap even by agreement (§ 702a). A duty the holder cannot disclaim is imposed, and assumed only in the sense that he chose the trade. That fits K3's claim that the role specifies and something outside the role binds; it tells against resting the binding force on the host's consent.
- **K7 (channel): two narrow legal analogues, both for goods.** The guest's own prompt report to the innkeeper is made the signal on which the claim depends (§ 703), which puts the burden of reporting on the guest and presupposes that the innkeeper can be told. And the innkeeper must act on one kind of guest request, to take valuables into safekeeping, on pain of unlimited liability if he refuses (§ 702(2) no. 2, (3)). Neither provision requires the innkeeper to keep a person reachable; that step is an inference.
- **K8 (holder): supports for goods.** The innkeeper answers for "seinen Leuten" (§ 702(2) no. 1), and is bound by staff who were, "nach den Umständen", to be regarded as appointed (§ 701(2)). The sections do not say whether "Leute" includes independent contractors.

**What the paper may say** (citing the section):
- German law makes an innkeeper who lodges guests as a business liable for loss of or damage to the things a guest brings in, up to a cap, whether or not the innkeeper is at fault, subject to the exclusions in § 701(3)-(4) (BGB §§ 701(1), 702(1)–(2)).
- That liability cannot be excluded in advance below the cap (§ 702a(1)).
- The innkeeper must accept a guest's valuables for safekeeping, unless they are of excessive value or bulk or dangerous, and loses the cap if he refuses (§ 702(2) no. 2, (3)).
- The guest's claim lapses unless the guest notifies the innkeeper without delay after discovering the loss, except where the things were taken into safekeeping or the innkeeper is at fault (§ 703).
- The sections on innkeepers in the German Civil Code concern the guest's things; they contain no duty to admit and no rule on the guest's person (§§ 701–704).

**What the paper may not say:**
- That German law imposes no duty on hotels to protect guests or to receive them. Only five sections were read.
- That the code requires the innkeeper to provide a contact point or to be reachable.
- That §§ 701–704 implement the 1962 Convention, without a source.
- That the liability covers restaurants or bars.
- My paraphrases as quotations, or the euro amounts as current without a fresh check.
