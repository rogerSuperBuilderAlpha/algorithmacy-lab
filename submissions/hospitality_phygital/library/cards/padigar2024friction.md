## Summary

A conceptual paper that defines friction as "the effort customers exert to complete tasks related
to their consumption goals" and types it by task desirability and task value: frustrating (low,
low), constructive (low desirability, high value), preference-based (high, low) and rewarding
(high, high). A topic model of 66 company announcements (2016-2020) about frictionless
experience shows firms removing friction from low-desirability tasks, including some where
friction protects the customer.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Typology: "frustrating friction (low desirability, low value), constructive friction (low desirability, high value), preference-based friction (high desirability, low value), and rewarding friction (high desirability, high value)" (p. 26) [full-text]
- Frustrating friction: "for businesses to enhance customer experience, it is imperative to eliminate frustrating friction" (p. 28) [full-text]
- Constructive friction and automation: "for companies that intend to implement more automation and AI-powered technologies in the customer journey, removing friction may lead to harm from privacy and surveillance concerns to ethical issues such as algorithmic bias" (p. 28) [full-text]
- Their examples of constructive friction are payment security, authentication, privacy and data-sharing choices (pp. 28, 32) [full-text]
- Contacting service to fix a problem is classed the other way: "customers 'have to' contact customer service and support center when there is a problem"; that topic and others "represent tasks that customers do not necessarily enjoy doing ... and are of relatively low task value ... reducing or eliminating friction in these tasks can enhance customer experience" (p. 32) [full-text]
- "businesses have been mindlessly removing constructive friction" (p. 32) [full-text]
- Classification "is subjective and contingent on the type of customer journey" (p. 29) [full-text]
- *Psychology & Marketing* 42(1), pp. 21-43 (2025) [metadata]

## Critical notes & threats

- Year corrected from 2024 to 2025 on 8 August 2026 (citekey unchanged).
- Without this the anti-seamlessness section reads as a humanistic objection. With it, the argument has been made in a marketing venue.
- (rr-2026-11) Correction to the earlier note "Negotiating an unusual request is constructive friction in exactly the paper's sense." The paper never discusses negotiation, recognition or exceptions. The nearest task it names, contacting customer service to resolve a problem, it files under low-value friction to reduce (p. 32). Reading negotiation as constructive friction is the manuscript's extension and should be argued as one, perhaps via the paper's own subjectivity caveat (p. 29).
