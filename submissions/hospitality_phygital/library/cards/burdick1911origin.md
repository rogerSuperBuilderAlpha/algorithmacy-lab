## Summary

Argues, against Wyman's monopoly theory, that the duties to serve all comers and to serve with care arose because a person who held himself out to serve the public made a general undertaking (assumpsit) to do so; the word "common" in "common innkeeper" pleaded that undertaking. As holding out became universal, the duty to serve survived only for carriers and innkeepers.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The distinguishing duties of the common callings were "to serve all applicants for their services, and to perform such services with care without a special assumpsit to that effect." (p. 515) [full-text]
- "It was because a person held himself out to serve the public generally, making that his business, and in doing so assumed to serve all members of the public who should apply, and to serve them with care, that he was liable in an action on the case for refusal to serve or for lack of care" (pp. 515-516) [full-text]
- Blackstone, as quoted: "if an innkeeper, or other victualler, hangs out a sign and offers his house for travellers, it is an implied engagement to entertain all persons who travel that way" (p. 518, quoting 3 Bl Comm 165) [full-text]
- "So it seems safe to believe that originally anyone who held himself out to serve all who might apply was conceived of as assuming a public or common calling" (p. 522) [full-text]
- The survival: the duty to serve "now attached only to common carriers by land and water (including ferrymen) and innkeepers." (p. 523) [full-text]
- "Generally speaking, however, an innkeeper is one who holds himself out to supply all travellers with food and lodging." (p. 524) [full-text]
- Inn against boarding house (quoting Schouler): the boarding-house keeper "reserves the choice of comers and the terms of accommodation, contracting specially with each customer" (p. 524 n. 45) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Bogen calls this theory "not so much a theory as a tautology" (bogen1996innkeeper, p. 52). Use for activation (holding out), not for justification.
- p. 523 is the strongest located statement for R2.4: once the action of assumpsit developed, and "the liability of the common surgeon, the common tailor, the common farrier, etc., was no longer recognized", the duty to receive all comers bound carriers and innkeepers and not the other common callings. Burdick's "now" refers to that post-assumpsit (early modern) period, not to 1911; do not date it to the twentieth century.
