## Summary

Argues luxury firms choose between direct control of their own e-commerce and indirect distribution through specialized intermediaries, with the choice differentiated by segment, and that digital channels integrate with traditional ones rather than replacing them.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Frames the direct-versus-indirect choice as active management rather than a market outcome [abstract]
- Differentiates the choice by segment, with inaccessible luxury using digital for heritage signalling and affordable luxury for volume [abstract]
- Holds that digital "does not intend to overcome traditional marketing" but integrates with it [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- The transfer is at the level of the encounter rather than the channel, and the manuscript should make the scale change explicit rather than letting the analogy slide.
- A firm choosing to preserve an indirect channel is not the same as a guest retaining an alternative, since the first is a strategy and the second is a standing. Useful, but it needs the qualifying clause.
