## Summary

A conceptual discussion of empowerment in the service-management literature followed by a study of
five-star hotels in Amsterdam. Empowerment is read as a compromise between control and autonomy, and
split into "choice" (discretion over how work is done) and "voice" (involvement in organisational
decisions). In the hotels studied there was little empowerment in practice despite managerial rhetoric.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The tension: the debates "flow from the attempt to reconcile the need for control to secure employee compliance with the need to cede a degree of autonomy to secure co-operation and initiative." (abstract) [abstract]
- The distinction: empowerment "is conceived primarily in terms of 'choice' - increase discretion over how work if performed - rather than 'voice' - greater involvement in organizational decision-making" (abstract; "if" sic for "is") [abstract]
- Control is relocated, not removed: empowerment is "increasingly regarded as a particular and contingent management strategy in which control via regulation and supervision is replaced by more indirect controls." (abstract) [abstract]
- Finding: "despite managerial rhetoric, there was little 'empowerment' in practice and what there was amounted to increased employee responsibility rather than greater choice over how work was done or more voice in organizational decisions and that supporting forms of recruitment, training and remuneration were mostly absent." (abstract) [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- Its choice/voice split maps onto the paper's discretion/authority split (PO §4): "choice" acts on the
  case, "voice" on the rule. The paper can borrow the parallel but should not claim Hales and Klidas
  made it.
- The finding also bears on the paper's own baseline: staff gained neither "choice over how work was
  done" nor "voice", so on this abstract the staffed five-star desk did not hold the discretion that PO
  §2.4 and v4 §4 credit to the face-to-face encounter. [added by Fable, rr-2026-11-h]
- Abstract only: no sample size, no methods detail, no quotations from staff. Amsterdam five-star hotels
  in the late 1990s.
- Search results show an issue listing that attaches a different abstract to this title; use the
  Crossref/OpenAlex abstract above.
