## Summary

An exploratory survey study in the Journal of Services Marketing that places immersive-technology adoption in hospitality inside Batat's phygital customer experience (PH-CX) framework. Suh and colleagues showed 208 Prolific respondents a scenario of an AI virtual assistant guiding a metaverse hotel journey, from booking through the stay to feedback, and used a Bayesian network to find which questionnaire items sit next to stated intention to use. Intention was conditionally linked to two usefulness items, one collaborative-value item and one item on responding to hotel staff's requests for ideas (§4.1). The authors read this as evidence that adoption involves more than usefulness, while stressing that the links are associations and not causes (§4). The conceptual sections add 'ethical safeguards' as a seventh PH-CX principle with four parts: transparency, agency, equity and privacy (§2.3). The practical sections then give hotels a three-phase roadmap: an AI concierge pilot, cross-channel integration and a co-creation hub (§6.2). Ethical implementation includes "a visible route to human assistance" and escalation procedures (§6.2). Policymakers get one sentence in the limitations (§7). The article never says who owes the safeguards to the guest or why. Its implementer is "hotels" or "hospitality managers", and no vendor, platform or second firm appears.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Question, design and evidence base**

- Aim: "This study aims to explore immersive-technology adoption in hospitality through the Phygital Customer Experience (PH-CX) framework." (abstract, PDF p. 1). [full-text]
- Design: "Using Bayesian Networks, the study examines probabilistic dependencies among perceived usefulness, collaborative value, responsive co-creation attitudes and intention to use immersive technologies. Data was collected from 208 participants exposed to an AI-supported immersive hotel journey scenario. The analysis is exploratory and identifies conditional associations rather than causal effects or latent-variable relationships." (abstract, PDF p. 1). [full-text]
- Data: "Data was collected via an online survey administered through Prolific" (§3.2, PDF p. 6); the scenario "measures anticipated engagement rather than actual adoption behavior. Findings should therefore be interpreted as conditional associations with stated intention, not evidence of realized technology use." (§3.2, PDF p. 7). [full-text]
- Sample: "The sample comprised 208 participants, predominantly aged 25–34 (62.0%) and male (71.6%)", "primarily from North America (52.4%) and South America (30.8%)" (§3.3, PDF p. 8). [full-text]
- Finding: "BINTUSE is conditionally linked not only to two TAM-aligned usefulness items, but also to collaborative value (UPVCE #1) and responsiveness to service innovation (RESPATT #4)." (§4.1, PDF p. 9). [full-text]
- Provenance: "This research was conducted as part of the American Phygital Association Summit 2025, sponsored by the American Institute of Business Experience Design (AIBXD) – New York." (Acknowledgements, PDF p. 15). [full-text]

**Safeguards: what, for whom, addressed to whom**

- "A seventh principle, ethical safeguards, is necessary to preserve the human-centric orientation of PH-CX. Immediacy, immersion, interaction, personalization, continuity and data-driven adaptation should be designed within guardrails that protect transparency, customer agency, equity, privacy and experiential authenticity." (§2.3, PDF p. 5). [full-text]
- Their status: "PH-CX therefore treats ethical safeguards not as an implementation afterthought but as a condition for legitimate and sustainable phygital value co-creation." (§2.3, PDF p. 5). [full-text]
- The four principles: "Operationally, ethical PH-CX design requires four principles. Transparency requires clear disclosure when guests interact with AI or when data is collected. Agency requires meaningful opt-in choices and the ability to decline phygital features without service penalties. Equity requires non-digital alternatives that maintain comparable service access and quality. Privacy requires data minimization, secure handling and guest control over the deletion of sensitive or biometric information." (§2.3, PDF p. 5). [full-text]
- Their purpose: "These safeguards help ensure that digital augmentation supports, rather than substitutes for, human dignity, autonomy and authentic hospitality (Saragih, 2025; European Parliament and Council, 2016)." (§2.3, PDF p. 5). [full-text]

- To hotels: "Ethical implementation should accompany each phase. Hotels should collect only data required for a clearly specified service purpose and provide understandable consent choices. Biometric tracking should require explicit opt-in consent rather than default enrollment." (§6.2, PDF p. 14). [full-text]
- To policymakers: "Policymakers have a role to play in promoting inclusive infrastructure, establishing guidelines for responsible automation and mitigating exclusion risks stemming from uneven digital literacy and access." (§7, Limitations, PDF p. 15). [full-text]
- Abstract versions: "Ethical implementation requires transparency, opt-in participation, privacy safeguards, accessibility and human escalation options." and "Transparency, agency, equity, privacy and non-digital alternatives are essential for inclusive and trustworthy service innovation." **Escalation**

- "AI concierge systems should provide a visible route to human assistance and establish escalation procedures for inaccurate, unsafe or sensitive responses." (§6.2, implementation roadmap, PDF p. 14). [full-text]
- P3: "An AI-powered phygital concierge will enhance perceived service efficiency and satisfaction for routine, low- to moderate-severity service requests. Its positive effect will weaken as service-failure severity, emotional sensitivity or the need for discretionary human judgment increases, unless immediate human escalation is available." ## Critical notes & threats

- **To whom: named. Why: not argued.** Suh and colleagues name guests as the protected party (§2.3) and hotels, managers and firms as implementers (§§6, 6.2). The manuscript's phrase "says neither to whom they are owed" therefore fails for this article. What the article lacks is a ground. Safeguards are a "principle" of a framework and "a condition for legitimate and sustainable phygital value co-creation" (§2.3, PDF p. 5): legitimacy of value creation, not something the guest can claim. (My reading.)
- **One firm only.** The roadmap's actor is "hotels" or "firms" throughout (§6.2). The AI concierge, cross-platform system and cloud and edge computing (§6.2) are described as the hotel's own, though in practice vendors supply them. No passage asks which firm answers when a supplier's system fails. (My reading.)
- **Escalation without authority.** The route goes to "human assistance" (§6.2), triggered by "inaccurate, unsafe or sensitive responses". Nothing says who the human is or what authority they hold. P3 makes the concierge's benefit depend on "immediate human escalation" (§6.2), so a reviewer could say immediacy is already in the literature. It appears there as a moderator in a proposition, not as anything owed. (My reading.)
- **Misattributed principles.** The article says "According to Batat (2024), the PH-CX framework is grounded in six principles – immediacy, immersion, interaction, personalization, omnichannel continuity and data-driven adaptation" (§2.3, PDF p. 4). Its reference list identifies Batat (2024) as the PH-CX article in the Journal of Strategic Marketing 32(8), held here as `batat2024phcx`. In the library's copy of that article the framework rests on "six main pillars ... practicality, sociability, immersivity, technicality, sensoriality, and affectivity" (Batat 2024: 1234), and 'immediacy' does not occur. Do not use this article as a source for Batat's principles.
- **Weak evidence for the safeguards.** The data are stated intentions in a scenario (§3.2), the sample skews male and American (§3.3), and the safeguards, roadmap and propositions are not tested; the authors write that propositions "guide interpretation within the exploratory nature of the analysis" (§6, PDF p. 12). Cite it for what phygital research prescribes, not for evidence that the safeguards work.
- **Same summit as Berthon et al.** Like `berthon2026accesship`, this article came out of the American Phygital Association Summit 2025 (Acknowledgements). Unlike Berthon, it names no co-author of the submission and no special-issue editor, so there is no anonymity question.

## Where it bears on the argument

**The v8 gap statement.** This is the article a reviewer close to the special issue will check first. It prescribes safeguards for trust-adjacent values (transparency, agency, equity, privacy) and addresses them to hotels, so "phygital research prescribes safeguards" holds, and "says neither to whom" does not. The defensible gap is narrower: phygital research does not say why the guest is owed the safeguards, and it treats the hotel as the only firm. See manuscript/v8_2026-11/stage2/READ_gapworks.md. (My reading.)

**`framing-argued-past`.** The study is an adoption model: perceived usefulness plus co-creation dispositions predicting stated intention (§§3-4). The paper argues past exactly this frame.

**`seamlessness-uncritiqued`.** Mixed. Seamlessness is endorsed as a design aim in §§1, 4, 4.2, 6.1 and 6.2 (twelve uses of 'seamless' or 'seamlessly' before the references, three of them describing the omnichannel literature), and once set aside as omnichannel's goal rather than PH-CX's (§2.3). The article also warns that reduced friction may erode "warmth and discretionary care" (§2.3) and expects diminishing returns from integration (P4). It does not question seamlessness itself as a goal, but the slug's 'have not questioned it' needs care when citing this article.

**K7, a channel from the guest.** "A visible route to human assistance" (§6.2) is a channel, addressed to hotels as good practice, with no named recipient, authority or claim behind it.

**What the paper may say** (third person, Intellect Harvard; the article prints no page numbers, so cite by section or n.pag. as the journal's style requires)

- Suh et al. (2026) add 'ethical safeguards' to the phygital customer experience framework as a seventh principle, comprising transparency, agency, equity and privacy for guests.
- They treat these safeguards as 'a condition for legitimate and sustainable phygital value co-creation' (Suh et al. 2026).
- They ask hotels to give guests 'a visible route to human assistance' and to 'establish escalation procedures for inaccurate, unsafe or sensitive responses' (Suh et al. 2026).
- Their recommendations are addressed to hotels, hospitality managers and, in one sentence, policymakers (Suh et al. 2026); they do not consider the several firms that supply a hotel's systems.

**What the paper may not say**

- That phygital research does not say to whom safeguards are owed. Suh et al. name guests.
- That the article frames safeguards as owed, as duties or as rights. It frames them as design principles and conditions of legitimate value creation.
- That it says who receives an escalation or with what authority.
- That its safeguards or roadmap are empirically supported. The data concern stated adoption intention in a scenario.
- That Batat (2024) lists immediacy, immersion, interaction, personalization, omnichannel continuity and data-driven adaptation as PH-CX principles, on this article's authority.
