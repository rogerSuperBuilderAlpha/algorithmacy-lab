## Summary

Scenario experiment in a hotel setting crossing recovery timing (immediate or delayed), recovery means
(psychological or economic) and failure type.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Design: "A 2 (recovery timing: immediate/delayed) × 2 (recovery means: psychological/economic) × 3 (type of service failure ...) between-subject experimental design was used with 456 participants." (abstract) [abstract]
- Setting: "under hotel service scenarios" (abstract) [abstract]
- Result: "immediate and economic recovery effectively raises the service recovery evaluations from customers with low-intensity negative emotions, whereas delayed and psychological recovery helps customers with high-intensity negative emotions to give higher evaluations." (abstract) [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- Vignettes, not guests; abstract only; "delayed" is not defined in the abstract.
- It is about the timing of the remedy, not of the decision. It does not tell against a reachable person at the moment of a decision; it tells against equating "immediate" with "best" for complaints.
- Contrast: Jin et al. (2019), *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Management* 38: 149-158 (record verified, abstract seen in Consensus only, not carded): recovery in 30 minutes beat recovery in 2 hours across three strategies.
