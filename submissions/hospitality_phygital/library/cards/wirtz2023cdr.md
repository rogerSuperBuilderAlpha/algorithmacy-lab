## Summary

A conceptual article in the Journal of Service Research that brings corporate digital responsibility (CDR) into service research. Wirtz and colleagues define CDR as "the principles underpinning a service firm's ethical, fair, and protective use of data and technology when engaging with customers within their digital service ecosystem" (Introduction). They make five contributions. They argue CDR matters most in service because of data volume and the opacity of AI. They sort ethics, privacy and fairness risks by data and technology life-cycle stage. They draw a digital service ecosystem in which the focal firm sits between customers (front end) and complementing and external business partners (back end), and trace risks to that structure. They explain poor CDR by a 'CDR calculus' of profit against responsibility, with regulation stepping in when the calculus is too negative. Last, they give managers strategies and tools (Table 2) for culture, management structure, digital governance, the customer interface and the partner ecosystem. Responsibility runs through the focal firm. It orchestrates and governs the ecosystem, works with technology vendors on CDR risks, and, when powerful, should answer for users "throughout the chain" (quoting Martin 2015). Hospitality appears in one example (hotel access by biometrics) and one illustration of online travel agents' 'Recommended' rankings.

## Critical notes & threats

- **The ground is position and power.** The focal firm governs because it sits "in the center orchestrating value creation" and because firms "have effectively become private regulators of their ecosystems". The sentence that makes a firm "responsible for the conduct and treatment of users throughout the chain" is Martin's (2015, p. 75), applied to "large service firms that can exert power within an ecosystem" (Business Partner Ecosystem). This is a ground the manuscript does not use. A reviewer may ask why holding out, reliance and control of the guest's space should beat power and orchestration as the basis of responsibility. (My reading.)
- **Not a sole bearer.** The gap search says Wirtz et al. make the focal firm "the orchestrator and governor of CDR across its vendors and partners". That holds, with two qualifications. Table 2 asks for "Bilateral sharing of digital governance between partners" and an agreed code of conduct, and the CDR calculus hands the hard cases to regulation. The focal firm leads; it is not the only bearer. (My reading.)
- **Principles and trade-offs, not directed duties.** CDR is "the principles underpinning" a firm's use of data (Introduction), weighed in a calculus against profit (A Service Firm's CDR Calculus). The one rights sentence reports how fair treatment "is increasingly viewed", for healthcare and utilities (Customer Interface). The article does not say what a particular firm owes a particular customer, or that the customer holds a claim. (My reading.)
- **Redress without a route.** Compensation is to be formalized and human oversight embedded (Table 2), but no channel by which a customer raises a failure, and no recipient with authority to correct it, is described. (My reading.)
- **Locators.** The copy read has no page numbers. Any page-cited quotation from this article must be checked against the PDF (pp. 173-190) first.
- **Year clash.** The bibliography already holds `wirtz2023digital` (Wirtz, Hofmeister, Chew and Ding, The Service Industries Journal 43(15-16)). Both render as Wirtz et al. (2023); citing both needs 2023a and 2023b.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K8, allocation among several firms.** This is the service-research statement that several firms stand behind the customer interface and that the focal firm governs the rest. It describes front-end and back-end partners, including the vendors of chatbots and robots whose designs "often dictate how a service firm interacts with its customers" (Complementing Partners). It is the natural citation where the paper introduces the several firms. (My reading.)

**K3, grounds of the duty.** It offers a rival ground, power and orchestration, for why one firm answers for the chain. The paper can acknowledge it and say what holding out, reliance and control of the guest's space add for hosting. (My reading.)

**K7, a channel from the guest.** Nothing here. Oversight and compensation are firm-side tools; no customer-facing route to correction appears. (My reading.)

**What the paper may say** (third person, Intellect Harvard; page numbers to be added from the PDF)

- Wirtz et al. (2023) observe that 'in addition to the focal service firm often several other players are involved' in digital service, so that responsibility concerns 'can originate from different corners of a firm's digital service ecosystem'.
- They place the focal service firm 'in the center orchestrating value creation, exchange and capture, and governing the ecosystem of customers, complementors, and external business partners' (Wirtz et al. 2023).
- They note that customer-facing technologies are typically sourced from outside vendors, whose designs 'often dictate how a service firm interacts with its customers', so that these risks 'need to be discussed between service firms and these partners' (Wirtz et al. 2023).
- They describe service firms as having 'effectively become private regulators of their ecosystems' (Wirtz et al. 2023), and, citing Martin (2015), hold that powerful firms should answer for the treatment of users throughout the chain.
- They give hotel access by biometrics as an example of a technology that acts on customers (Wirtz et al. 2023).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the article concerns hospitality. It concerns service firms in general; hotels and online travel agents appear as examples.
- That it states duties owed to customers or grounds responsibility in a role, holding out, reliance or control of space. Its ground is orchestration and power, and its register is principles and trade-offs.
- That the focal firm is the sole bearer. The article also asks for shared governance among partners and expects regulation to step in.
- That it prescribes a route by which a customer's complaint reaches someone able to correct it.
- That "responsible for the conduct and treatment of users throughout the chain" is Wirtz et al.'s own wording. It is Martin's (2015, p. 75), quoted.
