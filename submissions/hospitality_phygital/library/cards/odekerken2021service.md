## Summary

Field study (n=108) and scenario experiment (n=361) of a robot-customer-frontline-employee triad in fast casual dining. Asks whether the robot augments or substitutes the employee and how that shapes value and repatronage.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Defines the triad: "service triads consisting of technology, customers and frontline employees (FLEs) are becoming more common (Li et al., 2021)" (accepted manuscript, PDF p. 3, introduction) [full-text]
- "In the case of service robots, the FLE can be either substituted or augmented by the service robot (Lariviere et al., 2017)." (PDF p. 3) [full-text]
- Claims originality as "one of the first field studies on the service triad consisting of service robots – customers – frontline employees" (PDF p. 3) [full-text]
- Conclusion: the study "provides evidence for the potential of FLEs to augment service robots that exhibit lower levels of functional performance" (PDF p. 3) [full-text]
- Notes hospitality research "mainly focuses on welcoming or greeting hotel customers" (PDF p. 4) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "Service research has quietly handed the host's role to the employee (Larivière et al. 2017; Odekerken-Schröder et al. 2022)." SUPPORT: PARTIAL. Odekerken supports the factual base: service research treats the encounter as technology-customer-employee, with the employee as the human party the robot augments or replaces. The word "host" appears nowhere in the text (0 hits), and the employee is never described as holding a host's role. "Handed the host's role to the employee" is the authors' reading, and "quietly" is their gloss; the source does not say it.
- The robot is a service robot in a restaurant, with utilitarian and hedonic value as outcomes. Not a hotel and not an algorithmic intermediary.
- The paper cites Larivière et al. 2017 and Li et al. 2021 for the triad and the augment/substitute split, so it is secondary evidence for those two, not independent.
