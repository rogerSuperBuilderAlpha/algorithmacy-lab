## Summary

Chapter 1 of Part 4 replaced the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, which it revokes (s. 251(1)), with a statutory prohibition of unfair commercial practices (s. 225). A practice is unfair if it is likely to cause the average consumer to take a transactional decision he would not otherwise have taken as a result of a misleading action, a misleading omission, an aggressive practice or a contravention of professional diligence; if it omits material information from an invitation to purchase; or if it is listed in Schedule 20 (s. 225(4)). In deciding whether a practice is aggressive, account must be taken of, among other things, "whether the practice requires a consumer to take onerous or disproportionate action in order to exercise rights that the consumer has in relation to a product" (s. 228(2)(f)). A transactional decision includes a decision on "the exercise of contractual rights in relation to a product" (s. 245(c)). Weights and measures authorities must, and the CMA may, enforce the prohibition (s. 231); engaging in an aggressive practice is an offence (s. 237(3)). Consumers have rights of redress only where four conditions are met, including that the prohibited practice was a significant factor in the decision to enter the contract or make the payment (s. 232); the rights themselves are to be set by regulations (s. 233). Sections 232, 234 and 235 are marked 'Prospective' in the text read. The chapter applies to acts on or after 6 April 2025; the 2008 Regulations' Part 4A rights of redress are saved until the first regulations under s. 233 come into force (s. 252).

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

**Scope and definitions**

- "This Chapter confers protections on consumers from unfair commercial practices." (s. 224(1)); "Sections 232 to 235 confer rights on consumers in relation to unfair commercial practices and make related provision." (s. 224(5)). [full-text]
- "Unfair commercial practices are prohibited." (s. 225(1)). [full-text]
- "“commercial practice” means an act or omission by a trader relating to the promotion or supply of— (a) the trader’s product to a consumer, (b) another trader’s product to a consumer, or (c) a consumer’s product to the trader or another person;" (s. 225(3)). [full-text]
- "“consumer” means an individual acting for purposes that are wholly or mainly outside the individual’s business;" (s. 225(3)). [full-text]
- "“trader” means— (a) a person (“P”) acting for purposes relating to P’s business, or (b) a person acting in the name of, or on behalf of, P for purposes relating to P’s business." (s. 225(3)). [full-text]
- Timing: "It is immaterial for the purposes of the definition of “commercial practice” in subsection (3) whether the act or omission takes place— (a) at the time of the promotion or supply in question, or (b) before or after that time." (s. 225(5)). [full-text]
- "“transactional decision” means any decision made by a consumer relating to— (a) the purchase or supply of a product ...; (b) the retention, disposal or withdrawal of a product ...; (c) the exercise of contractual rights in relation to a product (including whether, how or on what terms to exercise such rights)." (s. 245). [full-text]
- "“product” means— (a) goods; (b) a service; (c) digital content." (s. 248(1)). [full-text]
- Vulnerability: "a group of consumers may be vulnerable as a result of (among other things)— (a) their age; (b) their physical or mental health; (c) their credulity; (d) the circumstances they are in." (s. 247(4)). [full-text]

**Aggressive practices**

- "For the purposes of this Chapter, a commercial practice involves an aggressive practice if it uses harassment, coercion or undue influence." (s. 228(1)). [full-text]
- "In determining whether a commercial practice uses harassment, coercion or undue influence, account must be taken of— (a) the nature of the practice; (b) the timing and location of the practice; (c) whether the practice involves the use of any threatening or abusive language or behaviour; (d) whether the practice exploits any vulnerability of a consumer (including any vulnerability of a kind mentioned in section 247(4)); (e) whether the practice involves a threat to take action which cannot legally be taken; (f) whether the practice requires a consumer to take onerous or disproportionate action in order to exercise rights that the consumer has in relation to a product." (s. 228(2)). [full-text]
- "“undue influence” means exploiting a position of power in relation to a consumer so as to apply pressure in a way which significantly limits the consumer’s ability to make an informed decision." (s. 228(3)(b)). [full-text]
- Unfairness test for aggressive practices: a practice is unfair if "it is likely to cause the average consumer to take a transactional decision that the consumer would not have taken otherwise as a result of the practice involving ... (iii) an aggressive practice (see section 228)" (s. 225(4)(a)). [full-text]
- Schedule 20, unfair in all circumstances: "Requiring a consumer who wishes to claim on an insurance policy to produce documents which could not reasonably be considered relevant as to whether the claim was valid, or failing systematically to respond to correspondence, in order to dissuade a consumer from exercising the consumer’s contractual rights." (Sch. 20 para. 29; in force 6.4.2025). [full-text]
- Complaints arrangements in an invitation to purchase: material information includes, "to the extent that the trader’s practice in relation to any of the arrangements mentioned in subsection (3) departs from the trader’s published practice in relation to those arrangements, the practice which the trader is currently operating" (s. 230(2)(i)), and the arrangements include "(d) the arrangements for handling complaints" (s. 230(3)). [full-text]

**Public enforcement and offences**

- "It is the duty of every local weights and measures authority in Great Britain to enforce in its area the prohibitions in section 225." (s. 231(1)); "The CMA may also enforce the provisions of this Chapter." (s. 231(3)). [full-text]
- "A trader commits an offence if, in breach of section 225(1), the trader engages in an unfair commercial practice which involves an aggressive practice (see section 225(4)(a)(iii))." (s. 237(3)). [full-text]
- Contracts stand: "Except as resulting from a consumer’s rights of redress under this Chapter, a contract or any other agreement is not void or unenforceable by reason only of a breach of the prohibition in section 225(1) or (2)" (s. 244). [full-text]

**Consumers' rights of redress (ss. 232, 234, 235 marked 'Prospective'; s. 232 commencement note: "not in force at Royal Assent, see s. 339(1)")**

- "A consumer has rights of redress under this Chapter if each of the following four conditions is met." (s. 232(1)). [full-text]
- First condition: the consumer enters into a contract with a trader under which the trader supplies a product to the consumer (a "business-to-consumer contract"), or a consumer-to-business contract, or "the consumer makes a payment to the trader for the supply of a product (a “consumer payment”)" (s. 232(2)). [full-text]
- Second condition: "the trader engages in a prohibited practice in relation to the product" (s. 232(3)(a)); "“prohibited practice” means an unfair commercial practice involving— (a) a misleading action, or (b) an aggressive practice." (s. 232(7)). [full-text]
- Third condition: "the prohibited practice is a significant factor in the consumer’s decision— (a) to enter into the contract mentioned in subsection (2)(a) or (b), or (b) to make the payment mentioned in subsection (2)(c)." (s. 232(4)); and for this purpose s. 225(4)(a) applies "as if the reference to a transactional decision were a reference to any decision taken by a consumer to— (a) enter into a business-to-consumer contract, (b) enter into a consumer-to-business contract, or (c) make a consumer payment." (s. 232(8)). [full-text]
- The rights are made by regulations: "The Secretary of State may by regulations provide for any of the following descriptions of rights to be exercisable by a consumer who has rights of redress under this Chapter— (a) a right to unwind ...; (b) a right to a discount ...; (c) a right to damages in respect of financial loss, distress or physical inconvenience or discomfort." (s. 233(1); in force for specified purposes at Royal Assent and otherwise from 6.4.2025). [full-text]
- "A consumer with a right to unwind, a right to a discount or a right to damages by virtue of regulations under section 233 may bring a claim in civil proceedings to enforce that right." (s. 234(1)). [full-text]
- "Nothing in this Chapter affects the ability of a consumer to make a claim under a rule of law or equity, or under an enactment, in respect of conduct constituting a prohibited practice." (s. 235(1)), subject to no double compensation (s. 235(2)). [full-text]

**From when**

- The commencement notes record ss. 224-231, 236, 237 and 244-252 as in force at 6 April 2025, e.g. for s. 228: "S. 228 in force at 6.4.2025 by S.I. 2025/272, reg. 2(1)(3)" (note I10). [full-text]
- "This Chapter applies only in relation to an act or omission which takes place on or after the commencement date." (s. 252(1)); "The CPUTR 2008 continue to have effect in relation to any act or omission which takes place before the commencement date." (s. 252(2)); for the saving of Part 4A of the 2008 Regulations the commencement date is "the date on which the first regulations made under section 233 come into force" (s. 252(3)(a)). [full-text]
- "The Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (S.I. 2008/1277) are revoked." (s. 251(1)). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **Does the art. 9(d) language survive? In substance, reworded and wider.** UCPD art. 9(d) reads "any onerous or disproportionate non-contractual barriers imposed by the trader where a consumer wishes to exercise rights under the contract" (eu2005ucpd). Section 228(2)(f) reads "whether the practice requires a consumer to take onerous or disproportionate action in order to exercise rights that the consumer has in relation to a product". "Onerous or disproportionate" survives. "Non-contractual barriers imposed by the trader" becomes a practice that "requires a consumer to take ... action"; "rights under the contract" becomes "rights that the consumer has in relation to a product", which on its words is not confined to contractual rights *(my comparison of the two texts; no judgment or Explanatory Note read)*.
- **Private redress is tied to the decision to contract or to pay.** Even when in force, a consumer has redress only if the prohibited practice was "a significant factor" in the decision to enter the contract or make the payment (s. 232(4), (8)). A barrier met during the stay, after the room was bought, gives no right of redress unless it drives a further payment *(my reading of s. 232(2)(c), (4))*. The obstruction itself is answered by public enforcement (s. 231) and the offence (s. 237(3)).
- **Not in force on the text read.** Sections 232, 234 and 235 are marked 'Prospective' on the 12 Dec 2025 capture, and the rights depend on regulations under s. 233. Whether any have been made or commenced since is not established. The interim private regime is Part 4A of the 2008 Regulations as saved by s. 252, which I did not read.
- **Consumers only.** "consumer" means an individual acting "wholly or mainly outside the individual’s business" (s. 225(3)). The business traveller is outside it.
- **The vendor may be a trader.** "trader" includes "a person acting in the name of, or on behalf of, P for purposes relating to P’s business" (s. 225(3)(b)). A vendor or brand running the guest's interface on the hotel's behalf may on that wording be a trader for this Chapter *(my inference; no case read)*.
- **Schedule 20 para. 29** is drafted around insurance claims; whether "failing systematically to respond to correspondence" stands on its own is a question of construction not settled by the text.
- **Complaints arrangements.** Section 230(2)(i), (3)(d) makes a trader's actual complaints-handling practice material information only where it departs from its published practice and only in an invitation to purchase. It does not require a trader to have complaints arrangements.
- Explanatory Notes, guidance and later case law not read.

## Where it bears on the argument

**K7 (channel from the guest): narrows; supplies a United Kingdom legal correlate of the right kind.** In England a host that requires a consumer guest "to take onerous or disproportionate action in order to exercise rights" during the stay is engaging in conduct the statute directs to be weighed as harassment, coercion or undue influence (s. 228(2)(f)), in relation to a transactional decision that includes exercising contractual rights (s. 245(c)), at any time relative to the supply (s. 225(5)). The correlate is a prohibition enforced by public bodies and the criminal law (ss. 231, 237(3)). It does not require a channel or a person with power to correct.

**K6 (the guest's claim): narrows.** The guest's own redress under this Chapter is confined to cases where the practice was a significant factor in contracting or paying (s. 232(4)), was not in force on the text read, and depends on regulations (s. 233). T4's claim that every channel the law creates is either the consumer's own remedial right or a duty to a regulator fits this Act: here it is mainly the second.

**K4 (legal tiers): narrows.** Consumers only (s. 225(3)); economic transactional decisions, not the guest's person.

**What the paper may say on this source's authority**

- In the United Kingdom, whether a commercial practice "requires a consumer to take onerous or disproportionate action in order to exercise rights that the consumer has in relation to a product" must be taken into account in deciding whether it is aggressive (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, s. 228(2)(f)).
- The Act treats a consumer's decision on "the exercise of contractual rights in relation to a product" as a transactional decision (s. 245(c)), and it is immaterial whether the practice occurs before, at or after the supply (s. 225(5)).
- Aggressive practices are enforced by local weights and measures authorities and the CMA (s. 231) and are an offence (s. 237(3)). (Narrowed after CV-G: the offence requires the practice to meet the unfair-practice test, s. 237(3).)
- A consumer's statutory rights of redress under the Act require that the prohibited practice was "a significant factor" in the decision to contract or to pay (s. 232(4)), and the rights are to be set by regulations (s. 233(1)). (Narrowed after CV-G: ss. 225-231 and 233 have been in force since 6 Apr 2025, but S.I. 2025/272, reg. 2(3), left out ss. 232, 234 and 235, which every capture read still shows as prospective; until regulations under s. 233 come into force, a consumer's private redress runs under Part 4A of the 2008 Regulations, saved by s. 252, which was not read.)
- The Act revoked the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (s. 251(1)).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the Act requires a host to keep a channel, a contact point or a person with power to correct performance.
- That the Act gives a guest a private claim for being obstructed during the stay. Redress is tied to the decision to contract or pay (s. 232(4)) and was prospective on the text read.
- That the UCPD wording "non-contractual barriers imposed by the trader" is the English statutory text. The Act says "requires a consumer to take onerous or disproportionate action" (s. 228(2)(f)).
- That the rights of redress in ss. 232-235 are in force, without a current check.
- That it protects business travellers.
