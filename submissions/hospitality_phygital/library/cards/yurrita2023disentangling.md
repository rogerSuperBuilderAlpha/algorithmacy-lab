## Summary

Online experiment (n=267) on a loan-approval scenario. Explanations raise informational fairness, contestability raises procedural fairness, and human oversight shows no effect. A qualitative analysis finds that participants still ask for more human involvement.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Design: user study (n = 267) of "the individual and combined effects of explanations, human oversight, and contestability on informational and procedural fairness perceptions for high- and low-stakes decisions in a loan approval scenario" (CHI '23, p. 1, abstract) [full-text]
- Result: "explanations and contestability contribute to informational and procedural fairness perceptions, respectively, but we find no evidence for an effect of human oversight" (p. 1, abstract) [full-text]
- Contestability conditions: appeal to the same system with extra information, or to "a human (different from the one who oversaw the process if there was already a human involved in the initial decision)" who "would make a completely new decision" (p. 7, section 4.2.1) [full-text]
- Both contestation mechanisms raised procedural fairness over no contestability, and the two did not differ (Tukey-adjusted p = 0.45) (p. 10, section 5.2) [full-text]
- Qualitative: regardless of oversight, participants "were still asking for a higher degree of human involvement (75/267)" (p. 11) [full-text]
- Authors conclude human oversight did not matter partly because the first prediction was still made by the algorithm, so participants may have seen the decision-maker as non-human (p. 13, discussion) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence: "The fourth: a person must stay reachable who can actually reverse or revise the decision (Yurrita et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2019)." SUPPORT: PARTIAL. Yurrita supports an appeal route raising procedural fairness (contestability, p. 10). It does not show that a reachable human matters: the appeal to a different human and the appeal back to the same system did not differ (p = 0.45), and "human oversight" had no effect. The 75/267 requests for more human involvement are qualitative and point the right way, but the quantitative result does not isolate a person who can reverse the decision. A careful reading is "an appeal route that reaches a real re-decision", not "a person".
- Scenario study of a bank loan with a fictional applicant, not a service encounter. Perceived fairness is the outcome, not behaviour.
- Not to be confused with Yurrita et al. 2025 (CSCW), a different paper in the v4 reference list; the §7 sentence "Others ask institutions to become more answerable (Yurrita et al. 2025)" cites that one.
- Bib vs Crossref: all fields match.
