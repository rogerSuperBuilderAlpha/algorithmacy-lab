## Summary

The Court upheld Title II of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 against a motel that refused Black guests, on the commerce power. Along the way it treated state public-accommodations statutes as codifications of the innkeeper's common-law duty to receive.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Quoting the Civil Rights Cases: innkeepers, "by the laws of all the States, so far as we are aware, are bound, to the. extent of their facilities, to furnish proper accommodation to all unobjectionable persons who in good faith apply for them." (p. 260, Clark J; the stray full stop after "the" is in the CAP text; LII reads "to the extent") [full-text]
- "These laws but codify the common-law innkeeper rule which long predated the Thirteenth Amendment." (p. 261, Clark J) [full-text]
- Douglas J, concurring, quoting the Senate Report: the English common law "reasoned that one who employed his private property for purposes of commercial gain by offering goods or services to the public must stick to his bargain." (p. 284) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- The conditions survive the move to statute: "to the extent of their facilities", "unobjectionable persons", "in good faith apply". Conditional hospitality again.
- The ground stated is commerce and holding out ("offering ... to the public must stick to his bargain"), not the traveller's vulnerability; contrast sandovalstrausz2005travelers, who argues the travel rationale carried the litigation (pp. 89-91).
- US only.
