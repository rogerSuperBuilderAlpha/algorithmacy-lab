## Summary

Within-subjects laboratory study of an interface that explains an allocation algorithm and lets people adjust the allocation in a goods-division task. Outcome control improved perceived fairness, while transparency alone did not.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- "standards clarity alone did not increase perceived fairness; outcome explanation had mixed effects, increasing or decreasing perceived fairness and reducing algorithmic accountability" (abstract) [abstract]
- "outcome control universally improved perceived fairness by allowing people to realize the inherent limitations of decisions and redistribute the goods to better fit their contexts, and by bringing human elements into final decision-making" (abstract) [abstract]
