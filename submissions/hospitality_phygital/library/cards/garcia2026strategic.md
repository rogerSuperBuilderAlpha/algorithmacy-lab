## Summary

Economics paper using hotel-room pricing data. Managers adjust prices rarely because adjustment is costly, so the algorithm's recommendations are shaded away from its own estimate of the right price to induce them to move; the authors quantify the loss and the gain from full automation.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Abstract: "price setting frictions, arising from adjustment costs of human decision makers, induce a conflict of interest with the algorithmic advisor" (working paper, PDF p. 4) [full-text]
- "in equilibrium, algorithmic price recommendations are strategically biased and lead to suboptimal pricing by human decision makers" (PDF p. 4, abstract) [full-text]
- "Managers adjust prices only infrequently, on average, once every 35 days" while recommendations change "once every seven days" (PDF p. 14, Observation 1) [full-text]
- Pass-through is imperfect: "a one-percent change ... in the recommended prices leads, on average, to a 0.72 percent change in the same direction in the realized price" (PDF p. 7) [full-text]
- Algorithms act "as advisors to human managers who retain the final decision right" (PDF p. 5) [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- v4 sentence (section 4): "in hotel pricing data an algorithm's recommendations turned out to be biased in anticipation of managers who moved prices too rarely (Garcia et al. 2026)". SUPPORT: YES. The working paper says exactly this: recommendations are strategically biased because adjustment is costly and managers update every 35 days on average (PDF p. 4, p. 14).
- v4 sentence (section 5): "A recent study of algorithmic pricing found the recommendations strategically biased: every price change cost the managers effort, they changed prices too rarely, and so the recommendation was pitched away from the price the algorithm itself judged right in order to make them move (Garcia et al. 2026)." SUPPORT: YES. Matches the model and Observation 1. "Effort" is the authors' gloss on "adjustment cost" and "opportunity cost of the manager's time or cognitive resources" (PDF p. 7, footnote 5).
- v4 sentence (section 5): "That tells us almost nothing about encounters where the technology decides admission, verifies identity, sets the price, assigns the room, or grants an exception (Garcia et al. 2026; Kim et al. 2021)." SUPPORT: WEAK. In Garcia the algorithm advises and the manager keeps the final decision right (PDF p. 5), so it is evidence about advice, not about technology that "sets the price". It is also guest-invisible: no guest meets the algorithm. Use it for the one-sided predictability point, not for deciding technology.
- Counter-flow note from the bib: managers keep persistent discretion, which cuts against any reading that the algorithm displaces the manager here.
