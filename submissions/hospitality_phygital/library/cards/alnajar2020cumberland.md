## Summary

The injured guests appealed against Dingemans J's finding that the Cumberland Hotel had not broken its duty. By the time of the appeal the case had narrowed to one point: whether the lobby security officer's failure to greet or challenge the intruder as he crossed the lobby was a breach. McCombe LJ, with whom Flaux and Newey LJJ agreed, held that the trial judge's assessment was an evaluation of fact with which an appellate court should not interfere, and that the narrower duty now alleged ("where possible, alternatively where reasonably practicable") had not been the case put to the witness at trial. The appeal was dismissed. The existence of the duty was not argued: it had been admitted on the pleadings and the finding at para 187 of the trial judgment was not challenged.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Court, date, judges: Court of Appeal (Civil Division), McCombe, Flaux and Newey LJJ; heard 5 November 2020; handed down 18 December 2020; England and Wales (cover sheet). [full-text]
- The question on appeal: "whether the Respondent is responsible in law for the injuries sustained because of a failure to take reasonable care to protect the Appellants against what occurred" (para 3). [full-text]
- Breach was the only live issue: "Issue (4), (breach of duty) was the issue on which, in the end, the case turned before the judge, as it does before us." (para 13). [full-text]
- The duty was not in dispute: "The legal duty, as the judge said, was not in dispute." (para 19). [full-text]
- No challenge to the trial judge's formulation: "At para. 187 (see above), he put the duty slightly differently, but the difference does not matter and there is no challenge to the finding. What was in issue was what that legal duty required on the facts of this particular case." (para 19). [full-text]
- The duty rested on agreement: "The legal duty upon the Respondent in this case was agreed on the pleadings (as I have said in para. 8 above) and the issue on all the various aspects of security considered, was whether that agreed duty had been broken." (para 34). [full-text]
- Reasonable care, not a guarantee: "the duty on the hotel was to take reasonable care to prevent attacks such as this; it was not (and was not alleged to be) an absolute duty to prevent an attack" (para 28). [full-text]
- Breach is a question of fact and degree: the points taken "cannot properly be classed as errors of law at all, the findings criticised were merely assessments, on the facts of the case, of whether the admitted legal duty had been broken" (para 18). [full-text]
- The ground of appeal put supervision in issue: the failure to greet the intruder was said to be "operational negligence by Mr Zafar or systemic negligence by the defendant for failing properly to train, supervise and/or monitor Mr Zafar" (para 18, quoting ground 3(c)). The court did not rule on that alternative as such. [full-text]
- Staffing: "At the material time, the Respondent only engaged one lobby officer at any one time." The trial judge's rejection of the criticism that one was too few was not appealed (para 24). [full-text]
- A hotel's manner as a limit on challenge: it had not been explored whether stopping the intruder "would have been consistent with the ambience of a hotel such as this, in the absence of a specific cause for suspicion" (para 30). [full-text]
- Holding: "I do not see that his final conclusion that (on the basis of the primary facts found by him) there was not a breach of the duty alleged, can be faulted." (para 36). [full-text]
- Disposition: "For these reasons, I would dismiss this appeal." (para 37); Flaux LJ, "I agree." (para 38); Newey LJ, "I also agree." (para 39). No dissent. [full-text]
- The court calls the defendant the "Respondent owner of the hotel" (para 8) and the premises "the Respondent’s hotel" (para 3). [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- **What was decided.** Only that the trial judge was entitled to find no breach on the case put to him. The existence, basis and scope of the duty were common ground and were not decided by the Court of Appeal.
- **It does not approve the basis.** McCombe LJ quotes para 187 of the trial judgment in full (para 10) and says the difference between it and the pleaded duty "does not matter" (para 19). That is not approval of the assumption-of-responsibility reasoning; the point was not argued.
- **Two transcription variances.** The court's quotation of para 9 of the trial judgment reads "did not include a liability to protect guests" (para 8); the trial judgment as published reads "a responsibility to protect guests". The court refers to "para. 239" of the trial judgment for the statement that the duty was not absolute (para 28); that statement is at para 229.
- **Ownership.** The court calls the respondent the "owner of the hotel" (para 8). The trial judgment says the defendant "operates a hotel" (alnajar2019cumberland, para 183) and that it is a subsidiary in a group (para 14). Neither judgment turns on who owned the building.
- **Later treatment** not checked.

## Where it bears on the argument

- **K4 (reception tier): supports the bare proposition, narrows its authority.** A hotel's duty of reasonable care to guests against attack was admitted and treated as settled between the parties (paras 19, 28, 34). The Court of Appeal's authority extends to the result on breach, not to the duty.
- **K3 (grounds): neutral.** The court says nothing on why the duty binds.
- **K7 (channel): narrows slightly.** A complaint of failure "to train, supervise and/or monitor" an officer (para 18) was treated as part of one question of degree about reasonable care (para 34), and it failed.

**What the paper may say**

- On appeal the hotel's duty to take reasonable care to protect its guests was "not in dispute" and the trial judge's finding on it was not challenged (para 19).
- The Court of Appeal dismissed the guests' appeal: the trial judge was entitled to find no breach (paras 36-37).
- The duty was one of reasonable care and "not (and was not alleged to be) an absolute duty to prevent an attack" (para 28).

**What the paper may not say**

- That the Court of Appeal held that a hotel assumes responsibility for its guests by inviting them. It did not consider the basis.
- That the Court of Appeal "confirmed" or "affirmed" the duty as a matter of law. It recorded an admission.
- That the case establishes a duty to train, supervise or monitor staff as a separate head. The allegation was pleaded and failed.
