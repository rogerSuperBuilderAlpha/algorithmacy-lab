## Summary

A two-stage qualitative study in one upscale hotel in south-eastern China (video ethnography with
ZMET interviews of 11 staff; then 24 staff and 14 guest interviews, November 2020 to June 2021).
Read through Collins's interaction ritual chains, check-in under COVID-19 rules becomes a negotiated
encounter: guests refuse, conceal or argue; staff explain, absorb anger and offer compensations;
and the encounter ends with the guest admitted as an "insider" or leaving as an "outsider".

## Critical notes & threats

- The best fit found for Reviewer 1's request: commercial hotel, guest refusals shaping the encounter, value beyond function (the guest who got the paid test wrote the praise), and in a premier tourism journal.
- It also shows the paper's own mechanism in miniature, with one correction to the gloss. The remedy came from "checking with hotel management": the frontline employee had to reach a party with authority over the hotel's response (paying for the test, arranging transport). It was not an exception to the rule: the state-imposed testing rule held, the guest was still tested, and Xiang et al. call it a "compensatory measure". Do not write it up as a negotiated exception. The guest who could reach nobody escalated to the general manager and head office the next day. Neither point is the authors'; they read both as ritual outcomes.
- The rules being negotiated were state-imposed, and the authors say their Chinese setting (high power distance, collectivism, one upscale property) limits generalisation (Conclusions, limitations paragraph). The paper should cite it for the form of the encounter, not for guest compliance norms.
- "Host" here is the hotel and its staff together; the study does not separate the institution's obligation from the employee's performance, which is the paper's section 2 distinction.

**For Q6/Q7 (rr-2026-11-h, H4).** Beyond the compensation case, the study gives located hotel evidence on both H4 questions. Guests owe compliance with the stay's rules and regard for other guests (Discussion); a guest who will not comply after "repeated communications" is asked to leave and becomes an "outsider" (Findings, two sections). Frontline staff explain the rule ("the only thing we can do", H8; the passage is about staff keeping their composure while guests shout, so "cannot alter the rule" is H4's inference), and the one accommodation found required "checking with hotel management". The rules were state-imposed, so the bound on staff authority here is legal as well as organisational.
