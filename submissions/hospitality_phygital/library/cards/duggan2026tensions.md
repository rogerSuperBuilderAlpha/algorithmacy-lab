## Summary

Thirty-two semi-structured interviews with food-delivery platform workers, mostly in Ireland. The study finds algorithmic HRM producing "enforced silence", which it treats as structural rather than chosen. Three sources produce it: no human managerial representation, third parties (customers, restaurants) who compound or mitigate the silence, and the system's technical design. Workers move to resignation and detachment and fall back on informal group chats.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Abstract: algorithmic systems "can foster 'enforced silence' and a sense of voicelessness amongst workers, inhibiting their ability to express concerns, contest decisions, and/or participate in shaping their work environment" (p. 1432) [full-text]
- Definition: "what we term 'enforced silence', extending Van Dyne et al. (2003) typology ... where the organization overrides employee volition, recasting silence from a 'motivated' act to an outcome produced by organizational structures" (p. 1446) [full-text]
- Futility leads to silence: "At first, I used to complain all the time. Now I don't bother. It's just how the app works – you can't change it." (FD03, p. 1447) [full-text]

- Sample: "a total of 32 in-depth, semi-structured interviews" (p. 1441) [full-text]

## Support verdicts (rr-2026-11)

- v4 §6: "platform workers describe a silence their researchers call enforced, an inability to contest decisions at all (Duggan et al. 2026)". PARTLY. "enforced" is the authors' term (p. 1446), and the inability to contest is central (p. 1432). But the source says workers "rarely" have the opportunity (p. 1448) and lack "credible channels" (p. 1449); it does not say they cannot contest "at all". Drop "at all", or quote "rarely having any opportunity to contest".
- v4 §6 inference sentence (the employees who see most clearly stop talking is "our inference ... not a finding any of them reports"): the hedge is accurate. Duggan reports resignation after repeated futile complaint (p. 1447), which is close to that inference but not the same.
- Revision plan §9 (whether the two silences compound): directly relevant. Customer ratings act as "extensions of managerial control" over workers (p. 1448). In hospitality terms, the guest's voice can be one of the mechanisms that silences staff.
