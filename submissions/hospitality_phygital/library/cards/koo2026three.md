## Summary

Not read beyond the abstract. On the abstract, a conceptual reflection that treats hospitality as 'an ethical and philosophical process' and not as a service outcome, divides its history into three 'ages' ending in a machine age, and argues that artificial intelligence and robotics do not remove hospitality's ethical dimension but relocate ethical responsibility 'within socio-technical design'. It proposes a concept, 'socio-hospitality', and five forms of hospitality through which AI-mediated systems 'sustain, narrow or transform' that dimension. It reports no empirical work.

## Critical notes & threats

- **What the abstract does and does not say about a host's duty.** It speaks of 'ethical responsibility' in hospitality and of its relocation. It does not use the words host, guest, duty, obligation or organization. It does not say that anyone owes anything to a guest, who would hold such a duty, or how a guest could press it.
- **'Relocates ... within socio-technical design' can be read two ways.** It may mean that designers and deploying firms now carry the responsibility, which would be a holder claim close to the paper's. It may mean that responsibility is dispersed into the system, which is the condition the paper criticizes. The abstract does not decide between them. Do not report either reading as the authors' until the text is read.
- **Register.** Ethics and philosophy, by the abstract's own description. No legal register and no account of standing is signalled.
- **Nearness to the paper.** Three points of contact: hospitality as more than service outcomes; algorithmic mediation of the encounter; the location of responsibility. The paper's own step, a holder's duty to keep a channel from the guest to a person with power over the single case (K7), is not signalled in the abstract.
- **Date.** Published online 24 July 2026. It post-dates most of the paper's reading and will be known to reviewers at this journal's neighbours.
