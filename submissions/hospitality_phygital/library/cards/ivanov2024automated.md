## Summary

Survey of 130 Bulgarian hoteliers on whether 23 decisions in 8 hotel departments should be taken by
humans only, human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop or human-out-of-the-loop.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Design: "applied to 23 decisions in 8 hotel departments. The sample includes 130 respondents from the Bulgarian hotel industry." (abstract) [abstract]
- Four approaches: "‘human only’, ‘human-in-the-loop’, ‘human-on-the-loop’ and ‘human-out-of-the-loop’" (abstract) [abstract]
- "Hoteliers show a preference to delegate decisions to AI for decisions that do not require much emotional intelligence or interactions with customers/employees." (abstract) [abstract]
- "general attitudes towards AI are the best indicator of whether an individual prefers the use of AI for a particular type of decision" (abstract) [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- Manager preferences, one country, not guest outcomes or guest recourse. The abstract does not say whether stakes, reversibility or timing were coded, nor which 23 decisions were asked about.
- The sorting criterion it reports (emotional intelligence, interaction) is not the paper's criterion (effect on the guest before review). It can illustrate that the industry already sorts decisions by loop position; it cannot ground which ones need a reachable person.
- Same first author as `ivanov2019ultimate` (held, WS_D) and Ivanov (2023) *foresight* 25(1): 4-19, "Automated decision-making" (record verified, abstract only, not carded; its abstract says the out-of-the-loop approach suits "quick, standardised, frequent decisions with low negative consequences of a wrong decision").
