# Red team: critical hospitality scholar

Locators follow the dossier's conventions. 'AM', 'AV' and 'MS' pages are the saved author versions. Journal articles cited as 'corpus' are the saved texts under `manuscript/rewrite_2026-11/journal/corpus/`. Every passage marked (a) I read this session in the saved text, or in a card whose quotation I checked against the saved text.

## 1. Verdict

The argument determines a duty, but not the host's duty. What it determines is a legally analogued moral duty: a commercial lodging firm must keep an empowered complaint route open, during performance, for guests who are articulate and who hold a contract. That is service recovery with a deontic ground. It is carefully hedged and may well be right, but nothing in it is drawn from hospitality. The dossier is weakest at the bridge between Step 1 and Step 6. No hospitality text supports any premise of Step 6. The one customary footing, Pitt-Rivers's plate, misreads its source. The law that Step 1 says only "tests" the canon in fact cuts the canon's content down: welfare drops out, by way of *Rasnick*. The dossier also quotes Derrida's conditional laws by halves, omitting the sentence on the same page in which the laws mark 'power' and 'transgress' hospitality. Finally, the scope leaves out the cases this journal actually studies: placed asylum seekers, dependent and silent guests, household hosts on platforms, and the workers who are the channel's far end. I do not attack the legal and moral chain (P1–P3) on its own terms; other attackers will. My objections concern whether that chain is hospitality theory at all, and whom it leaves out.

## 2. Objections, ranked

### F1. The plate does not support the conclusion, and a diplomatic convention cannot bind a firm

- **Step attacked.** Step 6, 'The customary footing' (lines 272–274), and the objections table rows that rest on it (lines 316–317).
- **Objection.** Pitt-Rivers does not describe a channel through which a failure reaches the host. The plate gesture was a way for the guest to withdraw from the role in place. Turning the plate made it 'impossible to serve him', so that 'he retired from his role' until the error was corrected. The convention guaranteed only that 'his protest had been registered', and its subject was an 'error of protocol' about precedence (honour), not performance or safety. The same code requires the host always to maintain that his hospitality 'is the best he can do' and 'prohibits [the] expression' of conflict. Attending, in that code, means anticipating needs without being asked, so 'a host that cannot be told is not attending' turns the code upside down. A convention 'once furnished in diplomatic etiquette', at a table where the host is present and nothing is delegated, cannot ground a customary duty of a commercial firm. Step 1 itself concedes that for commercial hosts the responsibility is 'prescribed, not observed'.
- **Footing.** (a) `pittrivers2012law`, pp. 513–514 ('intermediary solution … making it impossible to serve him. In this way he retired from his role until the error was corrected, or at least until his protest had been registered'); p. 513 ('prohibits its expression'); p. 516 ('it must always be maintained that … it is the best he can do'). (b) Step 1, Narrowing bullet 2 (line 39) against Step 6 (line 274).
- **Severity.** Fatal to the customary footing as stated. C is left standing in the moral register only.
- **What would answer it.** A concession: drop the plate, or keep it only as an illustration of oblique protest. If a customary or critical footing is wanted, it needs a new source. The journal's founding editorial, following Dikeç, asks that spaces of hospitality 'enable contestation and conflict' (Lynch et al. 2011, corpus PDF p. 9). Dikeç 2002 must be read before that line is used.

### F2. The law supplies and limits the keystone's content, so the 'innkeeper law' objection stands

- **Step attacked.** Step 1, 'Footing' (line 34). The objections table row 'Innkeeper law in hospitality's clothes … Answered' (line 316).
- **Objection.** The dossier says the content comes from the canon and the law only tests whether it is enforced. Step 6 does the opposite.
  - **Supply.** No premise of Step 6 draws on a hospitality text: P1 rests on *Groh*, CRA s. 49 and the BGB; P2 on *Wong*, *Kuoni*, *Ferguson* and Brandt; P3 on Hindriks, Hormio and legal analogues; P4 on service-failure studies; the timing on Gardner and consumer remedy ladders.
  - **Limit.** The canon's content is cut by law. The guest's health is excluded on the strength of *Rasnick*, and the dossier says so: 'Concede for Georgia law … Step 6 relies on that limit.'

  The objection is therefore confirmed, not answered.
- **Footing.** (b) Line 34 against line 107 (Step 3, objection 5), line 266 ('No duty to monitor the guest') and line 316.
- **Severity.** Fatal to the 'Answered' status. The argument survives if it is re-described.
- **What would answer it.** A concession, with re-description: present the keystone as a legal and moral argument about commercial lodging, in which hospitality supplies the roles and the setting. Otherwise a new argument: derive C from the canon's own content (protection, attending, welfare), and then argue why *Rasnick*'s limit binds outside Georgia tort law rather than assuming it does.

### S1. The channel is service recovery, and deriving it from the host's duty makes the guest a sensor

- **Step attacked.** Step 6, C (lines 232–235), and Step 4, 'What follows for Step 6'. The objections row 'A demanding guest is a consumer … Answered' (line 317).
- **Objection.** C runs as follows: the guest reports, an empowered frontline employee corrects, and otherwise the guest gets a price reduction or compensation, in the order the CRA and package law set out. That is the service-recovery model, and the dossier's main hotel source is a 'case study on service recovery … the role of empowerment'. The power it documents is mostly discretion over discounts, which on the dossier's own timing line is repair, not performance. Grounding the guest's voice in the firm's need to perform, rather than in the guest's standing, is the managerial justification of complaint: voice is information the firm uses to recover. It treats the guest as a source of quality-control data, which is more consumerist, not less. The reply 'the classical guest … still had the plate' fails for the reasons in F1. Hemmington, whom Step 1 relies on, marks complaint as the very point at which commercial relations depart from host and guest.
- **Footing.** (a) `schumacher2016case` (title; pp. 121–123: discounts up to '10–20%', limits 'not clear'). `hemmington2007` AV p. 9 (Darke and Gurney's domestic rule 'not complaining' is 'clearly at variance with the commercial manager-customer relationship') and AV p. 14 (guests should not 'dictate their requirements'). Lynch et al. 2011, corpus PDF p. 4 (Aramberri: 'service providers' and 'customers'). (b) Line 224 ('discretion over discounts') against lines 251–260 (performance, then repair). (c) Hirschman 1970; Fuller and Smith 1991 (both already in the paper's reference list, neither read); Tax, Brown and Chandrashekaran 1998.
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** A new argument stating what hospitality adds to service recovery. One candidate is that the object of the channel is the guest's need, not conformity to the specification, so that the person reached owes attention to the guest rather than to the contract. The argument should engage Aramberri and the service-recovery literature by name. Otherwise, concede that C is a thesis in consumer law and ethics.

### S2. 'Conditional hospitality' is quoted by halves

- **Step attacked.** Step 1, warrant 5 (line 32) and Narrowing bullet 5 (line 42). The objections row 'A duty fixed by rules negates hospitality (Derrida) … Answered, on quotations' (line 319). The uncertainty list, item 5 (line 330).
- **Objection.** The dossier quotes p. 77 for 'rights and duties that are always conditioned and conditional'. It omits the sentence from the same page that its own cited source prints and its own reader logged (R1, row D10b): the laws, 'in marking limits, power, rights, and duties, consisted in challenging and transgressing the law of hospitality'. On the quotation the dossier does use, the laws are those 'defined by … all of law and all philosophy of law', so calling the duty 'conditional hospitality' adds nothing to calling it law. This journal holds that the laws stay laws *of hospitality* only while they keep 'the reference point' of the unconditional. Without it they are 'merely laws'. The dossier's rule 'It may not claim the unconditional law for it' therefore concedes the critic's objection. Shryock, the sole carrier of 'not that it condemns', locates Derrida's critical power in an ideal 'realized not in the language of "rights"'. Item 5 says the dossier relies on Derrida for no premise, yet the 'Answered' verdict rests on him entirely.
- **Footing.** (a) `westmoreland2008interruptions`, p. 8 (the p. 77 sentence quoted above; the laws 'attempt to ensure the preservation of a state or master') and p. 6 (p. 55: sovereignty 'can only be exercised by filtering … and doing violence'). `ogorman2007phenomenon`, AM p. 8 ('by all of law and all philosophy of law'). Bulley 2015, corpus AM p. 4. Kekstaite 2022, corpus AM p. 4 ('mark powers'; the laws 'would cease to exist if they were not guided by … the law of unconditional hospitality'). `shryock2012breaking`, p. S22. (b) Line 32 against lines 319 and 330.
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** A new source and a new argument. Read *Of Hospitality* whole. State that the paper's duty belongs to the laws, which mark power and filter guests. Then argue how C relates to the unconditional law. One route: the single case the host's arrangements did not foresee is where the laws are exposed to what exceeds them. That would also give sub-step 5, the dossier's weakest link, a conceptual footing. Otherwise, drop Derrida.

### S3. The 'line of hospitality scholarship' for commercial hosts is managerial prescription, but it is spent as custom

- **Step attacked.** Step 1, the claim and warrant 1 (lines 24, 28), against the three-register rule (line 15).
- **Objection.** For commercial hosts, the 'line' rests on two texts, both addressed to managers.
  - **Morrison and O'Gorman.** One bullet in a figure of an unpaginated accepted manuscript. The authors 'translated' it 'into more modern business terminology' from a monastic chapter which, as their own Figure 1 prints it, has no verse on guests' safety.
  - **Hemmington.** His responsibility is 'for the experience', the trade overlooks safety, and the stated reward is competitive advantage.

  O'Gorman's history documents protection only for guest-friendship, and grounds it in self-protection. Inns served those without hospitality ties, and Ulpian's edict exists to curb 'the dishonesty of this kind of men'. The history fits the opposite reading just as well: commercial lodging is where hospitality's protections lapsed and law stepped in. Step 1 is labelled 'customary' while its Narrowing says 'prescribed, not observed'. It proves a prescription and spends it as custom, which breaks the dossier's own rule.
- **Footing.** (a) `morrison2008hospitality`, AM pp. 6, 14–15. `hemmington2007`, AV pp. 15–17. `ogorman2005modern`, pp. 142 ('one protects the stranger in order to be protected from him'), 146 ('violence and danger'). `digest4_9`, D. 4.9.1.1 and 4.9.3.1 ('reprimendae improbitatis hoc genus hominum'; checked in the saved Latin). (b) Lines 15, 22 and 39.
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** Narrowing: relabel Step 1, for commercial hosts, as 'scholarly prescription' (a moral register), not custom, and give the counter-history its due. Alternatively, a new source: ethnographic evidence of what commercial guests and hosts actually expect. O'Gorman's 'Discovering commercial hospitality in Ancient Rome' is saved and unread.

### S4. Welfare is dropped twice, and the guest who cannot speak is left out

- **Step attacked.** Step 1, the claim (safety only), against K1 as fixed ('safety and welfare'). Step 6: P4, 'What P4 does to C' (line 230), and 'No duty to monitor the guest' (line 266).
- **Objection.** K1 was fixed as 'safety and welfare'. Step 1 states safety alone and records no narrowing. Telfer, as quoted, grounds hospitableness in 'concern for the guests' pleasure and welfare', and this journal reads her as assigning 'a responsibility for a guest's welfare and needs'. C serves only failures that the guest perceives and reports, and the exclusion of monitoring rests on Georgia law. C therefore does nothing for:
  - a guest in crisis or ill and alone (*Rasnick*; Badreddin in Petie et al.);
  - a guest without the language ('in which he is heard, if he is');
  - a dependent guest who 'cannot leave'.

  Yet the protection of 'vulnerable strangers' is the canon's core. The dossier already holds a tool it does not use: the duty of adjustment in s. 20 would require a route that a disabled guest can use.
- **Footing.** (a) `ogorman2007phenomenon`, AM p. 6 (Telfer 1996: 82, as quoted) and AM p. 20 (Derrida, as quoted). Petie et al. 2025, corpus MS pp. 3, 12. Moysidou and Stanley 2023, corpus MS p. 7. `ogorman2005modern`, p. 147. (b) CLAIMS_FIXED K1 against line 24; line 60 (ss. 20–21) against line 230.
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** A new argument. Either show a duty of attentiveness that does not wait for a report, and say why the workability and privacy limits in *Rasnick* bind the moral and customary registers. Or state C's scope plainly, as a duty to guests who can report, and require that the channel be accessible under ss. 20–21. Goodin 1985 is already on the dossier's list of debts.

### S5. Placed and unpaid guests: the derivation from the duty to perform does not reach them, and Petie et al. cut against C

- **Step attacked.** Step 3, table row 'Placed guest' (line 119). The objections row 'Unpaid and placed guests … Answered for guests' (line 318). C, 'The guest's claim is to performance' (line 235). The paper's use of Petie et al. (DRAFT_v6, line 72).
- **Objection.** C derives the channel from the duty to perform what the holder undertook. For an asylum seeker in a hotel, that undertaking runs to the payer: 'it is the Home Office who are paying customers', and 'the providers do not' hold the legal duty. The operator owes the guest only care, on the control ground, and for that duty the dossier concedes P3 is not the law. 'Package law protects a traveller who did not pay' reaches only package travellers. Petie et al. show more than a failed path: Badreddin's queries 'were dealt with appropriately', yet 'cumulatively they should have acted as a warning sign', and 'There was no communication or joined up approach between services'. The single-case channel worked. The failure lay in aggregation across agencies, and in the guest's welfare, which C excludes.
- **Footing.** (a) Petie et al. 2025, corpus MS pp. 4, 11–12. `dma2020sshd`, para 241 (checked in the saved judgment). `eu2015package`, art. 3(6). (b) Lines 119 and 318 against line 235.
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** For placed guests, concede that the host is the placing body and that its duty is public (*R (DMA)*). Or make a new argument that grounds the operator's channel in control rather than in performance. In either case, add a duty to aggregate reports across cases and agencies, and stop citing Petie et al. as a failed path without noting that the queries were handled.

### S6. The household host on a platform

- **Step attacked.** Step 3, table row 'Selective rental host' (line 121). Step 6: 'A small host meets the duty with a telephone number that the proprietor answers' (line 268), and P4's 'without a confrontation' (line 230).
- **Objection.** In a household, the person with power over the single case is the host in their own home, so any report is a confrontation. The journal's homestay research finds that guests who see themselves as having little power choose 'indirect and passive approaches' and stay silent because they 'cannot leave'. The other possible addressee is the platform. The dossier's table says the platform is not the holder unless it controls supply, and its channel is a reputation system that makes hosts and guests 'monitor one another' under 'a logic of social surveillance and interpersonal monitoring'. C therefore either demands something the household cannot give without confrontation, or sends the guest into a surveillance system that holds no duty. The dossier's only evidence that guests fall silent is a practitioner summary (Min and Kim), although the journal's own studies are better evidence.
- **Footing.** (a) Moysidou and Stanley 2023, corpus MS pp. 6–7. Germann Molz 2018, corpus pp. 236–237. (b) Line 117 (the platform 'Otherwise … owes the channel duties the DSA sets') against line 268. (c) Chen and Tussyadiah 2021 (abstract only, read through Consensus: peer-to-peer 'relational' failures, with recovery including 'no actions').
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** Narrowing (exclude household hosts, and say so), or a new argument assigning the channel to the platform where the host is a household. The paper also needs a source on hosting as gendered labour, since a telephone answered round the clock is that labour.

### S7. Workers cannot be outside the scope of a duty they perform and are disciplined by

- **Step attacked.** The objections row 'Workers fall outside … conceded for workers' (line 318). C's 'someone with power' (line 232).
- **Objection.** In every case the dossier uses, the person with power over the single case is a worker:
  - reception staff whose discretion was unwritten and whose limits they did not know (Schumacher);
  - the worker who sent guests back to 'do it yourself' (Xu);
  - the receptionist who 'lacked the skills and knowledge' (Petie et al.);
  - the guards in *Groh*.

  A guest's report is also a report on a worker, and customer feedback is a known instrument for managing workers. Morrison and O'Gorman's own taxonomy says the server 'is seen by the guest as representing the business as a whole', so the worker is the host in the guest's eyes. Conceding that workers are out of scope is therefore not an answer.
- **Footing.** (a) `schumacher2016case`, pp. 122–123. `xu2024disciplined`, p. 5022. Petie et al. 2025, corpus MS p. 11 (staff 'victims of decisions made by management'). `morrison2008hospitality`, Fig. 2. Lynch et al. 2011, corpus PDF p. 8 (labour issues). (c) Fuller and Smith 1991 (in the library, abstract only).
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** A new argument stating what the duty requires of the holder towards the workers at the channel's end: authority in writing, training, and protection from becoming the object the channel disciplines. Otherwise, narrow C.

### S8. The guest reaches the person with power, and the power is used against the guest

- **Step attacked.** Step 6: P4 'Cases' (line 226), C, and 'The person reached must be able to correct, not to adjudicate' (line 267).
- **Objection.** In the dossier's own cases the report did reach a person with power over the single case, and that person used the power against the guest:
  - In *Groh* the guards and the 'guest services manager-supervisor' heard 'We are drunk. We can't drive', evicted the group and refused them the lobby.
  - In Xu the worker redirected guests to the kiosk.
  - In Petie et al. staff were 'largely reported as unhelpful'.

  In *Groh* the hotel's own watchfulness started the harm: 'no other guests complained about noise. Still, the group captured the attention of a Westin security guard.' This journal puts the host's power after the threshold at the centre of the question ('how is the power of hospitality exercised to manage and control the home?'). C does not govern how the power it reaches is used, and the cases contradict 'correct, not adjudicate'.
- **Footing.** (a) `groh2015westin`, ¶¶ 6–8 (saved text). `xu2024disciplined`, p. 5022. Petie et al. 2025, corpus MS p. 4. Bulley 2015, corpus AM p. 10. Lynch et al. 2011, corpus PDF p. 8 (the guest 'subject to domination by the host').
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** Narrowing plus a new argument. Say that the person reached owes the guest care in exercising the power, and then say what C adds to the duty of care. Name the same power's capacity to expel.

### S9. Step 7 leaves out what the algorithm changes according to its own source: discipline and identification

- **Step attacked.** Step 7, the claim and 'Guests, on open evidence' (lines 280, 284). P4, 'Correction, and who did it' (line 224).
- **Objection.** The dossier uses Xu and Greiffenhagen's data only to route failures. It counts as a failure 'cured … by the guest's own retrying' what the authors analyse as discipline: guests are 'disciplined by a technology that uses voice commands to tell them what to do with their bodies'. 'After reception … what can fail is the channel' therefore leaves out the change the source reports, which is the host's increased power to verify, identify and govern the guest's body. Derrida's conditional laws turn on asking the new arrival's name, and the identity-card check at the kiosk is that condition automated.
- **Footing.** (a) `xu2024disciplined`, p. 5016. `ogorman2007phenomenon`, AM pp. 8–9 (Derrida, pp. 25, 59f., 77, as quoted: hospitality 'without asking a name'; 'a basic and limiting jurisdiction'). el Hajal 2025, corpus AM (AI 'raises concerns about surveillance').
- **Severity.** Serious.
- **What would answer it.** Narrowing Step 7's claim, and engaging the source's own thesis. Count the guest's adjustment of their body as labour and compliance, not as correction.

## 3. Citations I found wrong

1. **Line 44.** `hemmington2007` is cited for naming the business responsible for guests' safety. The card forbids this: Hemmington's phrase is responsibility 'for the experience' (AV p. 16).
2. **Line 32.** '(pp. 25, 27, as quoted in Shryock 2012: S22, one carrier)' is only partly right. One carrier is true only for the p. 27 block ('not that it condemns … indissociable'). Shryock's version carries an ellipsis. The paraphrase also drops 'as strangely heterogeneous to it', which is the point of the passage.
3. **Line 32.** The dossier cites p. 77 as the conditional laws' 'rights and duties' and omits the sentence from the same page, printed in `westmoreland2008interruptions` at p. 8, in which the laws 'in marking limits, power, rights, and duties' consist 'in challenging and transgressing the law of hospitality'. 5. **Line 30.** 'and in Lynch et al. 2011' has no citekey (the card is `lynch2011theorizing`). Lynch et al. also end the quotation at 'extortionately' (corpus PDF p. 8), so in quotation they carry 'commercial host' but not the clause that makes 'hospitable' depend on motive and price.

Art. 3(6) covers a person 'entitled to travel on the basis of a contract concluded, within the scope of this Directive'. It does not reach a placed asylum seeker or a non-contracting companion.
