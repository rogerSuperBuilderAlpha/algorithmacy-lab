4. **What the Directive says, against the card.**
   - *How a level is set.* A level is "determined by the algorithmic impact assessment" (6.1.2). Each Appendix B level has three limbs: the risk of "The context in which the system is operating"; the decision's likely impacts ("The decision will likely have ..."); and the risk of "The data used by the system". The impact limb grades magnitude and duration as well as reversibility. The Summary's "grades a decision's impact in four levels by how reversible and lasting it is" and the hold rationale's "only where impacts are difficult to reverse or irreversible" are narrower than the text.
   - *Human involvement below Level III.* At Level I, "Humans are to be involved in system quality assurance and can intervene in the making of decisions by the system where appropriate" (Appendix C); Level II is "Same as level I".

Explanations "must also inform clients of relevant recourse options, where appropriate" (Appendix C, Explanation, Level I). It also requires a feedback loop: "Documenting client feedback, unexpected impacts, human overrides ..." (6.3.4) and "Using findings from outcome monitoring and documented feedback ... to identify issues and take corrective actions" (6.3.4.1). The card understates the Directive as a neighbour to K7, much as CV-H found for Baum et al.
   - *Status.* The Directive "applies to all institutions subject to the Policy on Service and Digital" (8.1), and TBS raises "any compliance issues" with the deputy head (7.4). The card rightly says it binds federal institutions; it is not soft law.
5. **May say / may not say.** May say 1: LICENSED-NARROWER (level-setting and version; see Corrections). May not say 1: LICENSED. A second 'may not' is needed: that the Directive lacks a route from the person affected.

## 6. asy2024homeoffice

**Verdict: PASS-WITH-NOTES**

1. **Identity.** [2024] EWCA Civ 373, CA-2023-000383, 18 April 2024; ASY & Ors v Home Office; on appeal from May J, [2023] EWHC 196 (KB). Before Bean LJ, Fraser LJ and Sir Nicholas Patten. "Lord Justice Fraser:" gives the judgment; "Sir Nicholas Patten: 110. I agree. Lord Justice Bean: 111. I also agree."
   - *Disposal* (the card says it was not recorded). The appeal succeeded on Ground 3 (paras 103, 109); Ground 2 was dismissed (para 103). 2. **Read depth.** Honest for paras 1-4, 60-72 and 84-101; the disposal is at 102-111.

4. **What the court said, against the card.**
   - *The duty.* "Any duty that were to be found to arise here would be a low-level systems duty to adopt administrative measures to prevent a person falling into the severe state of destitution that would constitute inhuman and degrading treatment" (para 86). The Home Office owed it "to these claimants" (para 90), and a claimant at "immediate or imminent risk" may rely on it (para 95). The court rejected May J's view that a violation required a wrong or unreasonably delayed decision on the change-of-conditions (CoC) application (para 94).
   - *Notice and damages.* Para 97 sets two alternatives: "(a) they have suffered inhuman and degrading treatment; or (b) they have been at immediate risk ...; have notified the Home Office of this by making a CoC claim; have not had a positive and prompt response to that claim; and have suffered severe distress during the period before the claim is resolved". Para 104 restates them, and para 105(1) confirms that actual inhuman and degrading treatment suffices. Notice is a condition only in limb (b). The hold rationale ("ties a damages claim to the person's having notified the Home Office") and the 'may say' leave limb (a) out.
   - *R (DMA).* The Court of Appeal neither adopts nor rejects May J's reading of DMA; it mentions DMA in its own voice only to approve the damages method "adopted by Knowles J in DMA" (para 100). "It does not narrow DMA" holds.
5. **May say / may not say.** May say 1: LICENSED-NARROWER (see Corrections). May not say 1: LICENSED.
6. **Bearing.** "A duty to act on notice, promptly, before harm lands" is the card's gloss. The court's duty is to adopt administrative measures to prevent destitution amounting to inhuman and degrading treatment (para 86). Notice and a prompt response enter as conditions of damages where that treatment was not suffered (para 97(b)). The analogy to K7 holds for limb (b) only.
7. **Use in DRAFT_v7.** Not cited, as the card says.
