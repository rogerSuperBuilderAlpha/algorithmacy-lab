# Response to the red team

## What the three reports agree on

All three attackers find the same fault from three sides.

- **The philosopher (F1)** and **the lawyer (T1)** both find Step 6's conclusion spent in the legal register on a moral premise, against the dossier's own rule.
- **The philosopher (S8)** and **the critic (F2, S1)** both find nothing in Step 6 that is specific to hosting. The philosopher shows the argument would bind every firm that delegates; the critic shows it reads as service recovery.
- **Both** find the Pitt-Rivers plate misread (PHIL S11; CRIT F1).

I accept all of this. Draft 2 rebuilds Step 6 rather than patching it.

## The rebuilt Step 6, in outline

1. **Two registers, kept apart.** In the moral register, C becomes a derivative duty owed to the guest. The guest's claim to performance (Step 4) justifies a further duty to keep a means of correction once performance runs through others and systems, because the claim's security then depends on it. The structure is reported in Herstein (§1, para. 8; §8.4, para. 2) [R11]. In the legal register, the channel's footing is the guest's own remedial rights, which need an addressee (CRA s. 55; package regs 15(4), 17), the rules on accessible aids and information (Equality Act s. 20(5)-(6)), and the rule against barriers to exercising contract rights (UCPD art. 9(d)) [R10]. The two registers reach the same practical requirement, and neither is used to prove the other.
2. **The premise specific to hosting.** The guest is inside the host's controlled space, subject to its ordering and away from home for the length of the stay. That is Step 3's control ground and the critical literature's own theme of the host's power after the threshold [R9]. This premise does three jobs.
   - It narrows C to hosts and similar custodial relations (PHIL S8).
   - It sets C's time limit at the stay (PHIL S7).
   - It answers the 'service recovery' reading: the channel answers to the guest's subjection to the host's power, not to the firm's interest in quality (CRIT S1).
3. **The wrong, located once.** Two wrongs are distinguished. A host that keeps no means of correction during the stay wrongs its guests by leaving their claim insecure. If a correctable failure then goes uncorrected, that is a further wrong against the primary duty. This ends the luck problem (PHIL F2).
4. **Necessity graded, not assumed.** The strength of the derivative duty follows the guest's dependence on the channel (Herstein: 'The stronger the dependency, the stronger the justification'). C is required in full where the guest's report is the main or only timely route (P4), and is disjunctive elsewhere: the host must have some timely means, and a guest channel is one (PHIL S2).
5. **Power over the single case, neutral on persons.** C requires power to depart from the arrangements' output in the single case. It requires a natural person where no configured process covers the type of failure and the stakes warrant it. Kong's type-level foreseeability (p. 5) makes a fall-back for unforeseen single-case failures a foreseeable precaution (PHIL S5, S6).
6. **One party routes.** Among several firms, the party in control of the guest's space owes the duty to route a guest's report to the firm with power over the failing part. This is a proposal (PHIL S9).
7. **The person reached owes care, and the holder owes the workers authority.** The person reached owes the guest care in exercising the power, which can be used against the guest (*Groh*), and the holder must give that person real authority, in writing, with training and protection (CRIT S7, S8).
8. **Aggregation.** The holder must learn across cases as well as in the single case. The UN principle on 'analysing trends and patterns in complaints' (Principle 29, Commentary) and Hormio's duty 'to try to fix its design' once problems emerge (pp. 20-21) give the form, which Petie et al. show failing across agencies (CRIT S5).
