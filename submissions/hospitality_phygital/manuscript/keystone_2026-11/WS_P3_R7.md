## (a) Synthesis

### Short answers

**(i)** Yes. The principle is in print in law and in philosophy, in four forms: wilful blindness in United States law, blind-eye knowledge in English law, culpable ignorance traced to a 'benighting act' in moral philosophy, and an organisational version of that tracing account. A fifth source, Delaware corporate law (card), states the nearest legal duty to keep a means of learning, though not as a doctrine about knowledge.
- Locators: *Global-Tech*, 563 U.S. at 766, 769; *The Star Sea* paras 3, 25, 112-116; Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2 ¶3-¶4, §1.2 ¶7; Hormio 2018: 1, 16-17; *Marchand*, slip op. at 30 (card).

**(ii)** The two legal forms require a suspicion or high-probability belief about a specific fact and a deliberate decision not to confirm it, and both exclude negligence in terms. The philosophical forms divide: volitionists require that the ignorance trace back to an act done in awareness, while epistemic-vice and capacitarian accounts accept a breached duty to inquire or a 'should and could have known'. Hormio adds that the benighting act can be an organisation's, with no bad will needed.
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 769-770; *The Star Sea* paras 25, 115-116; Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2 ¶4-¶6, §3.2 ¶3, §3.4 ¶1; Hormio 2018: 16-18.

**(iii)** No legal knowledge doctrine I read reaches a host that designs a service with no route for a guest's report and has no particular suspicion. *The Star Sea* decides almost that case against the knowledge route. Only the philosophical forms reach that host: the tracing account, where a duty to inquire is accepted, and Hormio's organisational version most directly. One legal analogue reaches it too, the Delaware duty to make a good-faith effort to have a reporting system, which runs to the corporation and not to customers.
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 769-770; *The Star Sea* paras 116-119; Rudy-Hiller 2022: §3.4 ¶7; Hormio 2018: 15-17, 20-21; *Marchand*, slip op. at 4, 30 (card).

**(iv)** The strongest objection is that the knowledge route becomes circular below the line of deliberate avoidance. The law calls a missing means of learning negligence, and negligence is measured by what care required. To say that a host with no channel 'had reason to know' already assumes that care required a channel, which is P3 itself. P3 must therefore stand as a claim about what care requires. It cannot be derived from the knowledge triggers.
- Locators: *The Star Sea* paras 115-119; *Global-Tech* at 769-770; *Ferguson* p. 8; *Rasnick* at 569-570.

### The knowledge condition in the host's duties, in the courts' words

Every knowledge trigger I re-read has a constructive limb; none requires actual knowledge. Lord Keith made the occupier's duty to act turn on whether it "knows or has reason to suspect that the contractor is using an unsafe system of work", and in the next sentences on whether the council "knew or ought to have known" of the contractor's practice. Neuberger LJ gave two examples of when reliance on a licensed contractor could be negligent: it "might, for instance, have become notorious that the scheme was ineffective and discredited", or the owner "may have had experience of a particular door supervisor who ... had shown himself to be wholly unsatisfactory and unreliable". Rix LJ acquitted the leisure-centre operator because nothing suggested "that the council had any reason to contemplate" a deficient inspection, and nothing "about what is known or might have been known by the council about these machines" called for "any special measures in controlling or supervising" its contractor.
- Locators: *Ferguson* p. 7 (Lord Keith), pp. 7-8; *Naylor* para 50 (Neuberger LJ); *Maguire* para 30 (Rix LJ).

The innkeeper's duty to aid in the Restatement has the same form. As quoted in *Rasnick*, § 314A puts a carrier under a duty "to give them first aid after it knows or has reason to know that they are ill or injured, and to care for them until they can be cared for by others", and adds: "(2) An innkeeper is under a similar duty to his guests." *Groh* quotes the same clause. The Georgia majority read the out-of-state § 314A cases as ones where the owner "had knowledge that its invitee was in imminent danger because of observation of the physical peril; no manner of investigation or inquiry was at issue". It declined "the additional duty upon innkeepers to investigate or check on their guests", even though the guest's wife had telephoned repeatedly. The dissent would have let a jury decide whether "the motel had reason to know that Mr. Rasnick was ill or injured". In North Carolina the negligent-selection test asked whether the owners "knew, or in the exercise of due care should have known" that their plumber was not competent.
- Locators: *Rasnick*, 289 Ga. at 566 n. 1, 568, 569-570, 572; *Groh*, 347 P.3d at 613, para 29; *Page*, 281 N.C. at 703.

Constructive triggers change the question R5a posed. Where the law already accepts 'reason to know', arranging not to know is not met by treating ignorance as knowledge. The live question is what information the holder had reason to have. The courts measured that against what reached the holder or was notorious. They did not measure it against what the holder would have learned through a route it had not kept. In *Ferguson* what the architects saw on site did not count against the council because no one "informed the council". In *Rasnick* the reports did reach the motel, and the majority still found no duty to check.
- Locators: *Ferguson* p. 8; *Naylor* para 50; *Maguire* para 30; *Rasnick* at 566 n. 1, 570.

### (i) Is the principle in print, and in what forms?

**Wilful blindness (United States).** The Supreme Court states the principle in general terms: courts "hold that defendants cannot escape the reach of these statutes by deliberately shielding themselves from clear evidence of critical facts that are strongly suggested by the circumstances". It gives two rationales. The wilfully blind "are just as culpable as those who have actual knowledge", and such persons "in effect have actual knowledge of those facts". The Court carried the doctrine from criminal law into a civil patent claim. One of the old authorities concerns a duty-holder: a bank officer who "purposely keeps himself in ignorance of whether the drawer has money in the bank". As the dissent quotes the same sentence, it continues "or is grossly indifferent to his duty in respect to the ascertainment of that fact".
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 766, 767, 768, 773-774.

**Blind-eye knowledge (England).** The House of Lords treats as knowing a person who, "suspicious of the truth, turns a blind eye to it, and refrains from inquiry - so that he should not know it for certain" (Lord Denning MR, as quoted by Lord Hobhouse). Lord Hobhouse states his own test as a question: "why did he not inquire?" If the reason was that the person did not want to know for certain, he is privy to the facts.
- Locators: *The Star Sea* paras 24-26 (Lord Hobhouse), 3 (Lord Clyde), 112-116 (Lord Scott).

**Culpable ignorance by tracing (moral philosophy).** On Holly Smith's account, as Rudy-Hiller reports it, ignorance is culpable when it results from a 'benighting act', "an action or omission the consequence of which is the lack of a true belief about the impermissibility of one’s conduct". Rudy-Hiller's examples are omissions: not reading the booklet, not asking a colleague. Rosen calls the duty breached a "‘procedural epistemic obligation’" to inform oneself. Several philosophers reject any view on which "one could shield oneself from blame simply by" not thinking. Rudy-Hiller classes obligations to "undertake information-gathering actions" as duties of inquiry, which concern "activities", as against norms of awareness, which concern "states of mind". In those terms P3 is a duty of inquiry.
- Locators: Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2 ¶3-¶4, §1.2 ¶7, §3.4 ¶7.

**Culpable ignorance in organisations (moral philosophy).** Hormio extends Smith's account so that "the benighting act" can be "done by a different person than the morally inferior act that follows", or by the organisation as a whole. She argues that "sometimes organisational design alone can create individual ignorance". An organisation that runs a known risk without adequate checks "could not justify this way of operating by appealing to ignorance".
- Locators: Hormio 2018: 1, 16, 17.

**A duty to arrange to learn (Delaware corporate law, by analogy).** Directors "must make a good faith effort to implement an oversight system and then monitor it". The system must be one the board judges "adequate to assure the board that appropriate information will come to its attention in a timely manner" (quoting *Caremark*). This is not a doctrine about knowledge. It is the one legal text in the run that makes a means of learning part of a duty to oversee.
- Locators: *Marchand*, slip op. at 29 n. 100, 30 (card).

### (iii) Which form, if any, reaches a host that designs a service with no route for a guest's report, without any particular suspicion?

**Wilful blindness does not.** Its first requirement is a subjective belief that a specific fact is highly probable. The Court built the doctrine to exclude the negligent defendant "who should have known of a similar risk but, in fact, did not". The act the Court found "telling" was a manipulated inquiry, not an absent one, and it presupposed Pentalpha's belief that the fryer was patented.
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 769-771.

**Blind-eye knowledge does not, and *The Star Sea* is almost the case.** After two ships were lost in similar fires, the fleet managers changed crews but "took no steps to check" the new master's knowledge of the CO2 system. They "did not take any special steps" on maintenance and gave no instruction to check the dampers. The trial judge thought "some system should have been in place". Lord Scott held that the earlier losses did not put the managers "in a position in which they were bound to enquire into the competence of every other master". The findings went "to negligence, perhaps gross negligence", and were "no basis for a finding of blind-eye knowledge". A host whose general awareness that guests need help is "untargeted" stands where those managers stood.
- Locators: *The Star Sea* paras 30-31, 116-119.

**The constructive triggers in the law of hosts do not reach the host by themselves.** They ask what reached the holder or was notorious, and the courts imposed no duty to arrange to be told. There are two qualifications. First, where the duty is non-delegable, knowledge drops out. The North Carolina court made the motel owners liable for their contractor's negligent repair "as if they had made the repairs themselves", and their plea that defects "could not have been known to them" was no answer on that basis. That reaches the host's liability for the delegate's carelessness, but it does not require a channel. Second, Neuberger LJ's "notorious" example implies that general notoriety can supply reason to know. If it were notorious that a type of guest-facing system leaves reports unheard, a host relying on it might have reason to know. That inference is mine, not the court's.
- Locators: *Ferguson* p. 8; *Maguire* para 30; *Rasnick* at 570; *Page* at 699, 704; *Naylor* para 50.

**The tracing account reaches the host only if two contested premises hold.** The host must be under a duty of inquiry, which in the entry's terms is what P3 is, and the account must not require awareness at the benighting act. Volitionists would excuse a host that never considered the risk. Epistemic-vice and capacitarian accounts would not. The entry adjudicates neither question.
- Locators: Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2 ¶4-¶6, §3.2 ¶3, §3.4 ¶1, §3.4 ¶7, §4 ¶3.

**Hormio's organisational account reaches the host most nearly, in the moral register.** On her account the benighting act can be the organisation's design, and no bad will is needed. An organisation that runs a known risk "without adequate safety checks" "could not justify this way of operating by appealing to ignorance". For ignorance that design produces without intent, she places blame for negligence on failing to fix the design after a harm, or after "a likelihood of bad outcomes is pointed out". She also accepts Smith's limit that the wrong must fall "within the known risks" of the benighting act. Hosting meets that limit easily, since guests needing to report a problem and be helped is a known risk of the trade. That application is mine. Her algorithm, which receives a report "via an internet form" and files it under "acceptable risk parameters", is the closest image in the read sources to an automated host.
- Locators: Hormio 2018: 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20-21.

**The Delaware duty reaches the host by analogy only.** A board that makes no good-faith effort to have a reporting system breaches its duty without any red flag. But the duty runs to the corporation, needs bad faith and concerns board-level information, not a customer's report.
- Locators: *Marchand*, slip op. at 4, 30-31, 34 (card).

### (iv) The strongest objection

The objection runs as follows. The law will not let a party escape a knowledge requirement by deliberately avoiding confirmation of what it suspects. Below that line it calls the failure to learn negligence, however gross, and that includes failing to have any system for learning. Negligence is judged against what reasonable care required. So the claim that a host with no channel from the guest 'had reason to know' presupposes that care required a channel, which is P3. The principle that a knowledge-conditioned duty cannot be met by arranging not to know therefore cannot ground P3 for the host without a particular suspicion. It grounds only the narrower rule that a host which suspects a particular failure may not block the report.
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 769-770; *The Star Sea* paras 25, 115-116, 118-119.

Three texts sharpen the objection. Under *The Star Sea*, earlier failures across a fleet did not oblige the managers to enquire into every master. Under *Ferguson*, knowledge held by others on site was not the council's unless someone "informed the council". In *Rasnick* the majority gave policy reasons against any duty to check on guests: it would be "unworkable as a matter of fact and practicality", and it would raise "issues implicating the guest’s privacy". Kennedy J adds a moral doubt: whether the wilfully blind are as culpable as the knowing is "a difficult one".
- Locators: *The Star Sea* para 117; *Ferguson* p. 8; *Rasnick* at 569; *Global-Tech* at 773.

**What survives.** The objection defeats P3 as a derivation from the knowledge doctrines. It leaves P3 standing as a claim about what care requires of a holder that performs through others and systems. That claim has moral-philosophical support: duties of inquiry, Hormio's organisational benighting act, and her known-risk rule. It also has one legal analogue, the Delaware oversight duty. The knowledge doctrines keep a narrower but useful job: they close off deliberate blocking, and a host that switches a channel off because it expects complaints is in the doctrine's territory.
- Locators: Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2 ¶4, §3.4 ¶7; Hormio 2018: 16-17, 20-21; *Marchand*, slip op. at 30 (card); *Global-Tech* at 769, 771; *The Star Sea* para 116.

### (v) A wording for P3 the sources would bear (my suggestion, not a finding)

> **P3 (the paper's step).** A holder whose duties to the guest turn on what it knows or has reason to know may not keep those duties from arising by arranging not to know. The law already holds this of deliberate avoidance: a party that suspects a specific fact and decides not to confirm it is treated as knowing it (*Global-Tech*, 563 U.S. at 769; *The Star Sea* [2001] UKHL 1, para 116). The law does not hold it of ignorance that careless design produces, which it treats as negligence (*The Star Sea*, paras 118-119). The courts read here also impose on occupiers and innkeepers no general duty to arrange to be told (*Ferguson* p. 8; *Rasnick*, 289 Ga. at 570). The paper's step goes further, with support in moral philosophy but not in law. A holder that performs through others and through systems is under a duty of inquiry: keeping a means of learning from the guest is part of the care its duties require. A design with no such means is then itself the failure, as an organisation's own design can be the act that makes its ignorance culpable (Rudy-Hiller 2022: §§2, 3.4, reporting Rosen and Holly Smith; Hormio 2018: 16-17).

This wording keeps the legal claim within *Global-Tech* and *The Star Sea* and states their limit. It marks the step past that limit as the authors' own. The one legal statement of a duty to keep a means of learning, *Marchand* (card), can be added as an analogue from corporate law, with its limits stated.

### Where this leaves K7 and K8

**K7, P3: the paper's own step, with narrow legal support and broader moral support.** The legal knowledge doctrines support only the deliberate-blocking case, and they refute the extension to negligent design in terms. The philosophical literature supports a duty of inquiry, but in a debate it leaves open. Hormio supports the organisational form, in the moral register.
- Locators: *Global-Tech* at 769-770; *The Star Sea* paras 115-119; Rudy-Hiller 2022: §2, §3.4 ¶7, §4 ¶3; Hormio 2018: 16-17.

**K8, 'without that defence where the duty is non-delegable': holds in a named jurisdiction, narrowed.** In North Carolina in 1972, the duties to keep premises reasonably safe and to warn of hidden perils were stated to be non-delegable. That rule was one of three bases on which the motel owners could be liable at trial, in a decision that only reversed summary judgment. The authority is an encyclopedia and the Restatement as quoted, and the court cited no earlier North Carolina case for the rule. Later treatment was not checked. ARGUMENT.md's sentence ("calls the innkeeper's premises-safety duty non-delegable, as one of three alternative grounds") is accurate. It should add "and to warn of hidden perils" and note that the decision only reversed summary judgment.
- Locators: *Page*, 281 N.C. at 702-704, 706, 708-709.
