# WS P4, reader R6a: package-travel law and a trader's duty to be reachable (K7, K8)

I read the five items on my list in full and wrote six cards: `eu2015package`, `eu2026package`, `uk2018package`, `kuoni2021uksc`, `kuoni2021cjeu`, `amazon2019cjeu`. The scout's notes (`WS_P4b_scout.md`) served to find the texts; every statement below rests on my own reading of the saved text, and every quotation was matched by script to the saved file (see the notes for the verifier). Locators name the card and the place in the source.

**The result in three sentences.** Package-travel law fixes one party that answers to the traveller for services other firms perform, and the same instruments give the traveller a named channel to that party and a duty on it to put failures right while the trip is running. That is K8's contract limb and K7's conclusion as enacted law, for packages. The law stops before three things the paper needs: a hotel stay sold alone, a person with power over the single case at the end of the channel, and the premise (P3) that a duty to see work done includes the means to learn that it is not being done.

---

## (a) Synthesis against K7 and K8

### K8. What stays with the role-holder under delegation

**Verdict on these sources: the contract limb holds in named jurisdictions (EU law; United Kingdom law), narrowed to packages and subject to stated defences.**

1. EU law makes the organiser of a package responsible to the traveller for the travel services in it, whether the organiser or another provider performs them. United Kingdom law writes the same rule into every package travel contract as an implied term.
   - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(1) ("irrespective of whether those services are to be performed by the organiser or by other travel service providers"); `uk2018package`, reg. 15(1)-(2), reg. 36. For the earlier law: `kuoni2021cjeu`, para 7 (Directive 90/314, art. 5(1)); `kuoni2021uksc`, para 6 (1992 Regulations, reg. 15(1)).

2. The legislature states its reason: a package has one trader responsible for the whole, and the earlier Directive had left it unclear which trader was liable. The courts add two reasons. The Court of Justice refuses a distinction between a supplier's acts and its employees' acts because it "would enable an organiser to avoid its liability". The Supreme Court prefers "a simple rule" against the party the traveller contracted with to an inquiry into a foreign hotel's vicarious liability.
   - Locator: `eu2015package`, recitals 22, 23; `kuoni2021cjeu`, para 49; `kuoni2021uksc`, para 50.

3. The answerable party is identified by what it does and how the services are presented, and it cannot escape by describing itself as an intermediary. The organiser is the trader who "combines and sells" the package; recital 22 makes the test the trader's "involvement in the creation of the package, and not on how the trader describes his business"; recital 10 ties the definition to what "travellers may reasonably expect".
   - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 3(8), recitals 8, 10, 22, art. 23(1); `uk2018package`, reg. 2(1), reg. 30(1).

4. Recent amendments move sellers who prompt or facilitate a second purchase into the answerable role. In EU law, from 29 March 2029, a trader that invites the traveller to add another type of service before the first is paid for, and does not say the result is no package, "shall be deemed to be the organiser". In United Kingdom law, from 6 April 2027, services under separate contracts form a package where "a single trader has facilitated" their separate selection and payment at one visit.
   - Locator: `eu2026package`, art. 1(6) inserting art. 5a(2), art. 3(1); `uk2018package`, SI 2026/455, reg. 2(2)(c)(ii), reg. 1(1).

5. The liability cannot be excluded by contract or waived by the traveller.
   - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 23(2)-(3); `uk2018package`, reg. 30(2)-(3); `kuoni2021cjeu`, para 34 (Directive 90/314, art. 5(3)); `kuoni2021uksc`, paras 6, 18 (1992 Regulations, reg. 15(5)).

6. The firms that actually perform answer to the organiser by redress. This is K8's allocation, "suppliers who deal only with the host, who answer to the host", in statutory form. The 2026 instruments harden it: a supplier that cancels must refund the organiser within seven days (EU) or fourteen (UK), and the UK gives the organiser a civil claim for redress "including damages".
   - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 22, recital 36; `uk2018package`, reg. 29, and SI 2026/455, reg. 2(6) (new regs 29A, 29B); `eu2026package`, art. 1(15) inserting art. 22(2).

7. The Supreme Court applied the rule to a hotel. A tour operator was liable, under the 1992 Regulations and for breach of contract, for a rape committed by the hotel's electrician while he purported to guide a guest to reception. Guiding was "a service falling within the scope of the holiday arrangements which Kuoni contracted to provide", and the assault was "improper performance of that contract".
   - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, paras 3, 4, 35, 45, 46, 49.

8. That liability "does not depend on vicarious liability for the acts of an employee". The Supreme Court said that bringing vicarious liability into the scheme "would defeat its purpose".
   - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, para 50.

9. The Court of Justice had ruled that a supplier's employee is not himself a supplier, that his acts in performing the contract nonetheless make the organiser liable, and that they fall within the "sphere of control of the organiser or the supplier of services", so the organiser could not plead an event beyond foresight or prevention.
   - Locator: `kuoni2021cjeu`, paras 41-43, 48, 50, 60-62 and the operative part; `kuoni2021uksc`, para 26.

10. The claimant in *Kuoni* alleged no failure by the operator or the hotel to supervise or select the employee. The operator answered for the result all the same.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, para 8.

**Where these sources narrow or cut against K8**

11. All of this governs packages. A hotel stay bought alone is a single travel service; a hotel's meals, cleaning, pool, spa and gym are part of that one service; extras bought after arrival do not make a package. None of the six sources says who answers to a guest who booked the hotel directly.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 3(2), recitals 15, 17, 18; `eu2026package`, recital 10; `uk2018package`, reg. 2(5), reg. 2(6)(b).

12. Where the law finds no package it lets the seller who brought the services together escape. For a linked travel arrangement the trader must tell the traveller that "each service provider will be solely responsible for the proper contractual performance of his service". Both jurisdictions have now legislated the category away, the EU from 29 March 2029 and the UK for contracts made from 6 April 2027.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 19(2)(a), recital 43, Annex II Part A; `uk2018package`, reg. 26(7)(a)(i), and SI 2026/455, regs 1(4), 2(4); `eu2026package`, art. 1(13), recital 5.

13. The party the traveller actually dealt with is not always the answerable one. A retailer who sells an organiser's package is liable for performance only if national law says so, or where the organiser is established outside the European Economic Area (in the UK text, outside the United Kingdom). Otherwise the retailer is a channel for messages. K8 assigns the reliance ground to "the one that held out"; package law assigns liability to the one that combined the services.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(1) second subparagraph, arts 15, 20; `uk2018package`, regs 17, 27.

14. Where K3's grounds pick different parties, package law picks the promisor and is silent on the party in control of the guest's person. In *Kuoni* the hotel controlled the premises and employed the wrongdoer, and it was not sued. The only finding on its own liability is the trial judge's obiter view that it would not have been vicariously liable, which the Supreme Court left alone.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, paras 10, 50; `kuoni2021cjeu`, para 44.

15. The organiser keeps three defences (the traveller's own fault; an unconnected third party; unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances) and may cap compensation at not less than three times the price, except for personal injury and for damage caused intentionally or negligently.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 14(3)-(4), art. 3(12); `uk2018package`, reg. 16(4), (6).

16. *Kuoni* is narrower than its headline. It was decided on Directive 90/314 and the 1992 Regulations; Kuoni's own conditions accepted responsibility for the fault of "our agents or suppliers"; the parties agreed the contractual and statutory defences were coextensive. The sentence that a contracting party "retains liability for his side of the bargain even if he performs it through others" is Longmore LJ's in dissent below, reported by the Supreme Court and not adopted in terms. The trial judge and the majority of the Court of Appeal had found for the operator, so the point was open in English law until 2021.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, paras 2, 4-6, 9, 10-13, 15(5), 18, 46.

17. Neither *Kuoni* judgment uses the term "non-delegable", and the Supreme Court does not cite *Woodland*. The paper cannot join the two lines of authority on the strength of these texts.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc` and `kuoni2021cjeu`, whole-text searches recorded on the cards.

### K7. The holder's duty of oversight requires a channel from the guest

**Verdict on these sources: the conclusion C is enacted for packages, without the words "to such a person"; P3 is not stated anywhere in them and stays the paper's own step; P4 is presupposed by the legislation and not evidenced by it.**

*How far the text goes*

18. The instrument that fixes one answerable party also requires a channel from the traveller to that party for use during the trip. The package contract must give the contact details of "the organiser's local representative, of a contact point or of another service which enables the traveller to contact the organiser quickly and communicate with him efficiently, to request assistance when the traveller is in difficulty or to complain about any lack of conformity perceived during the performance of the package".
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 7(2)(d); `uk2018package`, reg. 7(2) and Sch. 5, para. 4.

19. The traveller is told of the channel before contracting and is given a second one through the seller. The standard form says travellers "are given an emergency telephone number or details of a contact point"; messages sent to the retailer count as received by the organiser.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 5(1)(b), Annex I Part A, art. 15, recital 24; `uk2018package`, Sch. 1 para. 11, Sch. 3 Part 2, reg. 17.

20. The legislation treats the traveller's own report as the signal. The traveller "shall inform the organiser without undue delay" of a failure perceived during the trip, the contract must say so, and recital 34 lets silence count against the traveller's later claim "where such notice would have avoided or reduced the damage". This is P4's assumption written into law. It is not evidence that guests' reports are in fact the only timely signal.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(2), art. 7(2)(e), recital 34; `uk2018package`, reg. 15(3), Sch. 5 para. 5.

21. The organiser must then correct the failure while the trip is running. It "shall remedy the lack of conformity"; if it does not do so "within a reasonable period set by the traveller" the traveller may put it right and recover the cost, and need set no period "if immediate remedy is required"; it must offer alternative arrangements where a significant part of the services cannot be provided; and it must give "appropriate assistance without undue delay to the traveller in difficulty". In United Kingdom law these are implied terms of the contract, enforceable by the traveller.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(3)-(6), art. 16, recitals 34, 37; `uk2018package`, regs 15(1), 15(4)-(8), 15(11), 18.

22. Money follows where correction fails. If remedy is impossible or disproportionately costly, "Article 14 shall apply": price reduction for the period of the failure, and compensation. The order of the two, correction first and money after, matches K7's line between the duty to perform and the duty to repair.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(3) second subparagraph, art. 14(1)-(2); `uk2018package`, reg. 15(5), reg. 16(2)-(3).

23. In 2026 the EU legislature made reachability a duty in its own right. From 29 March 2029, "The organiser shall ensure that it is easily contactable through the contact points indicated in accordance with Article 7(2), point (d), and able to respond efficiently to queries", in particular about assistance and about "any lack of conformity occurring during the performance of the package and the related remedies provided for in Article 13". Other complaints get a separate regime: acknowledgement within seven days and a reasoned reply within sixty.
    - Locator: `eu2026package`, art. 1(10) inserting art. 16a(1)-(2); art. 3(1).

24. Outside package travel, the Court of Justice has read general consumer law as requiring a channel. The Consumer Rights Directive "necessarily requires traders to put at the disposal of all consumers a means of communication which allows the latter to contact them quickly and to communicate with them efficiently", a possibility the Court calls "of fundamental importance for ensuring and effectively implementing consumer rights". This supports K7's second route (a claim needs an addressee able to respond) more than its first.
    - Locator: `amazon2019cjeu`, paras 41, 46, and the operative part, second indent.

*Where the text stops*

25. **Packages only, and the channel runs to the organiser.** Nothing in package law gives a guest a channel to a hotel booked directly, or to the hotel at all; the hotel is a travel service provider to whom the traveller is given no route.
    - Locator: as at item 11; `eu2015package`, art. 7(2)(d) ("to contact the organiser").

26. **No person, and no power over the single case.** The contact may be a representative, "a contact point" or "another service". The 2026 duty is to be "easily contactable" and "able to respond efficiently". Neither Directive, nor the UK Regulations, says that a human being must answer or that whoever answers must be able to change the outcome; the words "human" (outside "human health"), "automat-" and any requirement of a natural person at the contact point do not occur. K7's conclusion asks for a channel "to such a person"; these texts stop at a channel that works.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 7(2)(d) and the term searches recorded on the card; `eu2026package`, art. 16a(1) and term searches; `uk2018package`, Sch. 5 para. 4 and term searches.

27. **The Court of Justice declined to require a person or a telephone.** It held an unconditional duty to provide a telephone number "disproportionate", accepted "an electronic enquiry template", "instant messaging or a telephone callback system", and weighed the consumer's interest against "the competitiveness of undertakings" and "the undertaking's freedom to conduct a business". A lawyer could cite *Amazon EU* against K7 as readily as for it.
    - Locator: `amazon2019cjeu`, paras 44, 48, 51, 52, and the operative part, first indent.

28. **No P3.** The Directives make the organiser liable for the result and oblige it to remedy on notice. They state no duty to supervise or monitor suppliers, and no principle that a duty to see work done includes the means to learn of its failure. *Kuoni* shows the same structure from the other side: liability with no failure of oversight alleged. On these sources the law reaches K7's conclusion for one sector without K7's premise.
    - Locator: `eu2015package` and `eu2026package`, term searches on "monitor" and "supervis-"; `kuoni2021uksc`, para 8.

29. **A cost exit K7 does not have.** The duty to remedy yields not only where remedy "is impossible" but where it "entails disproportionate costs". K7's timing line turns only on whether performance is still possible.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 13(3)(b); `uk2018package`, reg. 15(4)(b).

30. **Until 2029 the EU text requires a contact point to be named, not answered.** Art. 7(2)(d) governs the content of the contract. The duties to act are remedy and assistance. Art. 16a applies from 29 March 2029 and is not United Kingdom law; the UK Regulations, including their 2026 amendment, contain no counterpart.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 7(2)(d); `eu2026package`, art. 3(1), art. 5; `uk2018package`, regs 15-18 and SI 2026/455 (read whole).

31. **In United Kingdom law the channel is not something the traveller can sue for.** The contact point enters through reg. 7(2), which is left out of the list of implied conditions, enforced as a criminal offence by trading standards and the Civil Aviation Authority, excused by due diligence, and expressly barred as a ground of civil action. What the traveller holds as contractual claims are remedy within the period set, self-help, assistance and the retailer channel.
    - Locator: `uk2018package`, regs 7(10), 7(12), 31(1)-(2), 32(1), 35; compare regs 15(1), 17(1), 18(1).

32. **No stated remedy for an unanswered channel in EU law either.** Art. 16a attaches none; enforcement and penalties are left to Member States. *Amazon EU* was an injunction sought by a consumer federation, not a consumer's claim.
    - Locator: `eu2026package`, art. 16a; `eu2015package`, arts 24, 25; `amazon2019cjeu`, para 2.

33. **The legislator gives no reason for the duty to be contactable during performance.** Recital 28 of the 2026 Directive explains the complaint deadlines of art. 16a(2) only. The nearest stated rationale is recital 34 of the 2015 Directive: "The traveller should be entitled to have problems resolved".
    - Locator: `eu2026package`, recital 28; `eu2015package`, recital 34.

34. **The split in art. 16a is by subject, not by date.** Paragraph 2 covers "types of complaints other than those referred to in paragraph 1". The tie to the time of the trip comes from art. 7(2)(d) ("perceived during the performance of the package") and from the new form ("while they are on their trip or holiday"). The paper may say the statute separates help and in-trip failures from other complaints; it may not say the statute divides "during" from "after".
    - Locator: `eu2026package`, art. 16a(1)-(2), Annex I Part A; `eu2015package`, art. 7(2)(d).

35. **General consumer law is a pre-contract information rule, and it may not reach an automated hotel at all.** Art. 6(1)(c) of the Consumer Rights Directive tells a distance or off-premises seller what to disclose before the consumer is bound. The Directive does not apply to package contracts or to contracts "concluded by means of automatic vending machines or automated commercial premises". I read nothing that decides whether a kiosk check-in is such a contract; the exclusion is a caution, not a finding.
    - Locator: `amazon2019cjeu`, para 10, and the note on Directive 2011/83, art. 3(3)(g), (l).

36. **On the remedies objection.** *Kuoni* gives damages after the event and nothing else. The package instruments are the exception among the sources I read: they give remedies while performance is possible (items 21, 22). They are statute and contract, not tort, and they are sectoral.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, para 49; `eu2015package`, art. 13(3)-(4); `uk2018package`, reg. 15(4)-(7).

### Findings outside K7 and K8

37. **K4, the sentence on attending.** K4 says attention to the particular guest is customary and not legal. The Supreme Court held that a package holiday contract includes "the service of looking after and serving holidaymakers courteously in matters relating to their holiday experience", and agreed that at the standard sold "hotel staff must be helpful to guests when asked for assistance". For package holidays attending is a contractual service for which the organiser answers. The paper should confine K4's sentence to the general law.
    - Locator: `kuoni2021uksc`, paras 30, 31, 32.

38. **K5, per duty.** Inside one UK instrument the duties to remedy and to assist are terms owed to the traveller, and the duty to name a contact point is owed to the public authorities and enforced by prosecution.
    - Locator: `uk2018package`, regs 15(1), 18(1), 7(10), 7(12), 35.

39. **K4, placed guests.** A traveller is anyone "entitled to travel on the basis of a contract concluded", so package duties run to a person who did not pay. Business travel bought under a general agreement is excluded.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, art. 3(6), art. 2(2)(c); `uk2018package`, reg. 2(1), reg. 3(2)(c).

40. **K3.** The recitals rest the organiser's liability on assumed responsibility and on what the traveller may reasonably expect from the presentation, and art. 23(1) removes disclaimer by notice. The Court of Justice's limit on the organiser's defence is the "sphere of control" of the organiser or its supplier, which sits nearer K3's dependence ground than its reliance ground.
    - Locator: `eu2015package`, recitals 8, 10, art. 23(1); `kuoni2021cjeu`, paras 60-61.
