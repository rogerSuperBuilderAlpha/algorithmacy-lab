# WS P4, reader R6b: law that requires a route to a person, law on automated decisions, and acting before a loss that cannot be undone (K7, K9)

I read the eight items on my list and wrote seven new cards: `es2025clientela`, `ca2026ab1609`, `schufa2023cjeu`, `eu2024platformwork`, `uk2025datause`, `mathews1976eldridge`, `americancyanamid1975ethicon`. I upgraded `eu2024aiact` in place, keeping every earlier field and line. The scout's notes (`WS_P4b_scout.md`) served to find the texts. Every statement below rests on my own reading of the saved text, and a script matched every quotation in the cards and in this report to the saved files (see the notes for the verifier). Locators name the card and the place in the source.

**The result in three sentences.** The laws on this list enact K7's conclusion in pieces and never whole: Spain and California require a route to a natural person within minutes and give that person no stated power; the EU's AI Act requires a person with power to override and to stop a system and gives the person affected no route to them; the EU's platform work directive joins a contact person, an overseer with power, reasons no later than the decision's effect and rectification ahead of compensation, for workers. None of them addresses a guest, and each is enforced by a public authority or for a class that does not include guests. The two cases on timing state the principle K7 needs, that intervention comes first where later repair cannot make the loss good, and both state it as an exception; the United Kingdom has meanwhile moved its general rule on automated decisions from prohibition to safeguards about decisions already taken.

### K7, premise by premise

**Verdict on these sources: P3 has two statutory counterparts, both about automated systems and neither about hospitality; P4 is presupposed by three of the instruments and contradicted in form by one; the conclusion C is enacted nowhere on this list for a consumer; the timing line has one close statutory analogue (for workers) and two judicial statements that make it the exception. K7 remains the paper's own step, with partial legal analogues.**

*P3: a duty to see that something is done includes the means to learn that it is not being done and to correct it*

1. For high-risk AI systems, EU law gives 'oversight' the content P3 gives it. The overseer must be able to "duly monitor" the system's operation "in view of detecting and addressing anomalies, dysfunctions and unexpected performance", to "disregard, override or reverse the output" in "any particular situation", and to stop the system.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, art. 14(4)(a), (d), (e).

2. The firm that uses the system carries the duty. "Deployers shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support." The deployer "shall monitor the operation of the high-risk AI system", and on reason to consider that it presents a risk "shall suspend the use of that system".
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, art. 26(2), art. 26(5).

3. For platform work the directive does the same. Platforms must "oversee" their automated systems and evaluate their effects "regularly and in any event every two years", through persons with "the competence, training and authority necessary to exercise that function, including for overriding automated decisions", and must act on what the oversight finds, "including, if appropriate, the modification of the automated monitoring system or the automated decision-making system or the discontinuation of its use".
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, art. 10(1)-(3).

4. Neither instrument lets the user of a system discharge the duty by buying from a competent supplier. The platform's obligations "apply irrespective of whether such systems are managed by the digital labour platform itself or by an external service provider". Under the AI Act the system's maker must meet the oversight requirement and the deployer must still assign, monitor and suspend. Spain's statute takes the same line for customer service, which binds the firm "independientemente de que sean gestionados por la propia empresa o por un tercero".
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, recital 55; `eu2024aiact`, arts 16(a), 26(2), 26(5); `es2025clientela`, art. 3(11), art. 2(3).

5. These are enactments for named classes of system. They show that two legislatures wrote monitoring and a power of correction into the meaning of oversight. They do not state P3 as a general proposition about duties, and neither class is defined so as to reach a hotel's guest-facing systems.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, Annex III (existing Key facts) and Critical notes; `eu2024platformwork`, art. 2(1)(a), art. 2(2).

*P4: the guest's own report is often the only timely signal*

6. Three instruments are built on the affected person's own initiative. In Spain the person asks for an operator and the firm's deadlines run from the complaint; in California the customer uses a feature "to request to connect to a customer service agent"; under the platform work directive the worker has "the right to request the digital labour platform to review" a decision. Each presupposes P4. None is evidence that P4 is true.
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, arts 8(2), 4(2)(b), 17(1); `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22627(a); `eu2024platformwork`, art. 11(2).

7. The AI Act is built the other way. The deployer monitors "on the basis of the instructions for use" and keeps the system's logs. No provision requires it to receive or act on a report from the person a decision concerns.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, art. 26(5), art. 26(6); Critical notes (added rr-2026-11-k).

*C: a channel from the guest to a person with power over the single case, for as long as the duty can still be performed*

8. A route to a natural person, on request and against a clock, is law in Spain for large firms and basic services. "Se prohíbe el empleo de contestadores automáticos u otros medios análogos como medio exclusivo de atención a la clientela." The customer must be able to ask for a person "en cualquier momento de la interacción, desde su inicio", and 95 per cent of requests must be met "en un plazo inferior a tres minutos". (My paraphrase of the first sentence: answering machines or similar means may not be the only means of customer service.)
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, art. 8(1), art. 8(2).

9. California has enacted a weaker version. A large business must offer "a simple customer service feature" for asking for a human agent, and "shall make a good faith effort" to connect the customer "within 15 minutes of the request" or give an appointment "within one business day".
    - Locator: `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22627(a), s. 22627(b)(1). Enrolled bill; chaptered text not reached.

10. Neither statute says what the person reached can do. Spain defines the operator by training and real-time presence; California defines the agent as a natural person who provides service or support. Both leave the firm free to staff the line with people who cannot change the outcome.
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, arts 3(14), 9(2), and 'What the text does not contain'; `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22625(c), and 'What the text does not contain'.

11. A person with power over the single case is law for high-risk AI systems and for platform work. The AI Act's overseer can "disregard, override or reverse the output"; the directive's overseer has authority "for overriding automated decisions", and the gravest decisions "shall be taken by a human being".
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, art. 14(4)(d); `eu2024platformwork`, art. 10(2), art. 10(5).

12. The AI Act gives the person affected no route to that overseer. That person is told the system is in use, may obtain "clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken", and may complain to a market surveillance authority.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, arts 26(11), 86(1), 85; and the whole-text search recorded under 'to whom the duties are owed'.

13. The platform work directive comes nearest to joining the two, for workers. It gives the worker "access to a contact person designated by the digital labour platform to discuss and to clarify the facts, circumstances and reasons having led to the decision", and requires that contact persons "have the competence, training and authority necessary to exercise that function". The function is discussion and clarification. The duty to rectify lies on the platform, and the text does not say the contact person can change the decision.
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, art. 11(1), art. 11(3); Critical notes, 'Three different people, three different powers'.

14. Data protection law gives a route to "human intervention" with no clock and no stated power. In the EU the controller must provide "at least the right of the data subject to obtain human intervention, to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision"; in the UK the safeguards must "enable the data subject to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller in relation to such decisions".
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, para 54; `uk2025datause`, art. 22C(2)(c).

15. No instrument on this list requires, for a consumer or a guest, all three things K7's conclusion joins: a channel from the person affected, a person at the end of it with power over the single case, and availability before the effect or while performance can still be put right.
    - Locator: the table above, row by row.

*The timing line: a channel while performance is possible, repair afterwards*

16. Four provisions put a person, or a pause, before the effect. A human must take the decision to restrict, suspend or terminate a platform worker's account, and written reasons are due "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect". Two natural persons must verify a remote biometric identification before "action or decision is taken". In Spain, under a contract for continuing services, a complaint about the ground of an intended suspension bars the suspension until a reasoned resolution is communicated. And in the EU a solely automated significant decision outside the three exceptions "is prohibited".
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, arts 10(5), 11(1); `eu2024aiact`, art. 14(5); `es2025clientela`, art. 4(4); `schufa2023cjeu`, paras 52, 64.

17. One provision orders the remedies as K7 orders them. The platform "shall rectify that decision without delay", and "Where such rectification is not possible, the digital labour platform shall offer adequate compensation for the damage sustained."
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, art. 11(3).

18. The rest are met by a response after the event. The UK safeguards concern "decisions described in paragraph 1 taken in relation to the data subject". The AI Act's explanation is of "the decision taken". Article 22(3) of the GDPR states no time, and the Court of Justice added none. California is satisfied by an appointment "within one business day". Spain allows fifteen working days to resolve.
    - Locator: `uk2025datause`, art. 22C(2)(a); `eu2024aiact`, art. 86(1); `schufa2023cjeu`, paras 54, 66, and 'The ruling and what it left open'; `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22627(b)(1)(B); `es2025clientela`, art. 17(1).

19. *Mathews* states the principle for hearings: "A claim to a predeprivation hearing as a matter of constitutional right rests on the proposition that full relief cannot be obtained at a postdeprivation hearing." It fixes timing by three factors: "First, the private interest that will be affected by the official action; second, the risk of an erroneous deprivation of such interest through the procedures used, and the probable value, if any, of additional or substitute procedural safeguards; and finally, the Government’s interest, including the function involved and the fiscal and administrative burdens that the additional or substitute procedural requirement would entail."
    - Locator: `mathews1976eldridge`, 424 U.S. at 331, at 335.

20. The same case holds against the earlier hearing. "We conclude that an evidentiary hearing is not required prior to the termination of disability benefits". "Only in Goldberg has the Court held that due process requires an evidentiary hearing prior to a temporary deprivation", and the ordinary principle is "that something less than an evidentiary hearing is sufficient prior to adverse administrative action."
    - Locator: `mathews1976eldridge`, at 349, at 340, at 343.

21. What the Court found adequate did include a channel before the effect. The agency sent its proposed decision with reasons, and "Opportunity is then afforded the recipient to submit additional evidence or arguments". The dispute was over how formal the prior process had to be.
    - Locator: `mathews1976eldridge`, at 324, at 346; Critical notes, second bullet.

22. The doctrine cannot be carried to a private host as law. "Procedural due process imposes constraints on governmental decisions", and the third factor is "the Government’s interest". It can be carried as a form of reasoning: earlier process is owed where later repair cannot make the person whole. A borrower of the first factor should expect to be held to the other two, error rate and cost.
    - Locator: `mathews1976eldridge`, at 332, at 335, at 348; Critical notes.

23. *American Cyanamid* states the principle for remedies. "The object of the interlocutory injunction is to protect the plaintiff against injury by violation of his right for which he could not be adequately compensated in damages recoverable in the action if the uncertainty were resolved in his favour at the trial". Its limit is as plain: "If damages in the measure recoverable at common law would be adequate remedy and the defendant would be in a financial position to pay them, no interlocutory injunction should normally be granted, however strong the plaintiff's claim appeared to be at that stage."
    - Locator: `americancyanamid1975ethicon`, transcript p. 3, p. 5.

24. That principle cannot be carried to a private host as law either. It tells a court when to restrain a defendant, on a claimant's application, after weighing the defendant's uncompensatable loss as well, and at the claimant's risk under an "undertaking to pay damages to the defendant". It lays no duty on a business to make itself stoppable. It can be carried as reasoning, and it brings its limit with it: the paper must argue that the losses it has in mind (shelter for the night, safety, access) are ones money cannot mend.
    - Locator: `americancyanamid1975ethicon`, transcript p. 3; Critical notes.

25. Both cases make the timing of intervention depend on whether later repair can be full. That is K7's criterion ("whether performance is still possible") in two other settings. Both treat earlier intervention as the exception, to be shown case by case, and neither imposes a standing arrangement on a private party.
    - Locator: items 19-24.

### How far the law already enacts K7's conclusion, and where it stops

K7 concludes that the holder must keep a channel from the guest to a person with power over the system's output in the single case, for as long as the duty can still be performed. On the eight texts read:

- **Enacted: a route to a natural person, on request, within minutes.** Spain, for basic services and large firms (art. 8); California, as a duty of good-faith effort on businesses above 500 million dollars in revenue (s. 22627). A large hotel group is inside both on the words of the size tests; that is my reading, and neither text names lodging.
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, arts 2(2), 8, Critical notes; `ca2026ab1609`, ss. 22625(g), 22627, Critical notes.
- **Enacted: a person with power to override the system in the single case.** The EU AI Act for high-risk systems, from 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems; the EU platform work directive, to be transposed by 2 December 2026.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, arts 14(4)(d), 26(2), application dates; `eu2024platformwork`, arts 10(2), 29(1).
- **Enacted for workers only: the two together, with a timing rule.** Platform work directive, arts 10 and 11.
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, arts 10(5), 11(1)-(3).
- **Enacted as a floor: a right to obtain human intervention in an automated decision, with no time and no stated power.** EU GDPR art. 22(3) as restated by the Court of Justice; UK GDPR art. 22C.
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, paras 54, 66; `uk2025datause`, art. 22C(2).

The law stops at five points.

- **The person reached and the person with power are different people.** The statutes that secure a route do not give its end any authority, and the statutes that create authority do not give the affected person a route. Only the platform work directive puts both in one instrument, and even there the reachable person's function is to discuss and clarify.
    - Locator: items 10, 12, 13.
- **The ground is consumer protection, product safety, data protection or labour protection.** None of the instruments derives the route from the firm's duty to perform with care, which is the ground K7 gives. Spain's preamble speaks of resolving disputes arising from the performance of contracts and of service quality.
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, preamble II and IV (card, 'Where it bears'); `eu2024aiact`, art. 14(2); `eu2024platformwork`, art. 1(1).
- **The duty is mostly not owed to the person it protects.** California says so in terms. Spain and the AI Act enforce by administrative sanction. The platform work directive and data protection law do give the protected person rights, and the protected person there is a worker or a data subject.
    - Locator: `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22628(d); `es2025clientela`, art. 23(1); `eu2024aiact`, art. 99(4); `eu2024platformwork`, art. 18.
- **Before the effect is the exception.** Items 16 and 18 set the provisions side by side; the two cases state the same as doctrine.
    - Locator: items 16, 18, 20, 23.
- **No hospitality.** No text on this list names lodging, hotels, guests or travellers. The platform work directive excludes accommodation-sharing platforms by example, and the existing AI Act card records that no Annex III area names hospitality.
    - Locator: `es2025clientela`, `ca2026ab1609`, `schufa2023cjeu`, `mathews1976eldridge`, `americancyanamid1975ethicon`, each under 'What the text does not contain' or the last Key facts bullet; `eu2024platformwork`, art. 2(2), recital 20; `eu2024aiact`, Annex III.

Reported against the claim: **K7 is the paper's own step.** These sources supply partial analogues for P3 (two statutes), for the channel (two statutes), for the person with power (two statutes) and for the timing line (one statute, two cases as principle). On K7's own test ("If the legal analogues all point to later repair, K7 stands on P3 alone and says so"), the analogues do not all point to later repair, and most do.

### K9: what mediation changes before and after reception

**Verdict on these sources: the before-reception limb is supported in EU law and narrowed in UK law; the after-reception limb has a legislative statement of its premise, about workers.**

26. EU data protection law treats an automated step taken before any contract exists as a decision in its own right. A vendor's score is "automated individual decision-making" where the user "draws strongly on that probability value to establish, implement or terminate a contractual relationship", and the Court's example is a refusal to contract.
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, para 73, paras 15, 48.

27. The Court reached the vendor because a three-party arrangement would otherwise leave no one answerable: the vendor said its partners "made the actual contractual decisions", and the user "would not be able to provide that specific information because it generally does not have it". The Court called the result "a lacuna in legal protection". This is the paper's divided-host problem decided in another field; the Court bound the vendor and did not say the user answers for the vendor's system.
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, paras 16, 63, 61; 'Where it bears', K8.

28. In the United Kingdom the same automated refusal is now lawful unless it rests on special-category data or on Article 6(1)(ea), subject to safeguards about the decision "taken".
    - Locator: `uk2025datause`, arts 22B(1), 22B(4), 22C.

29. For workers, the EU legislature recorded as a finding what K9 says of guests: automated systems "increasingly replace functions that managers usually perform", and people subject to them "are not able to obtain an explanation for those decisions, to discuss those decisions with a human contact person, to contest those decisions or to seek rectification or, where relevant, redress."
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, recital 8; also recital 47.

30. The AI Act is an instance of the after-reception failure K9 describes: oversight is secured inside the deploying firm, and the person outside it receives an explanation after the event.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, arts 26(2), 86(1); 'Where it bears', K9.

### What refutes or narrows K7, stated plainly

31. **UK data protection law no longer starts from a prohibition.** Section 80 substitutes Articles 22A to 22D for Article 22 of the UK GDPR. A significant decision may be taken with "no meaningful human involvement" unless it uses special-category data or relies on Article 6(1)(ea), provided safeguards exist about decisions "taken". In force for all purposes from 5 February 2026.
    - Locator: `uk2025datause`, s. 80(1), arts 22A(1)(a), 22B, 22C(2)(a); S.I. 2026/82, reg. 2(j), reg. 5.

32. **The statute that requires a human channel in so many words denies the customer a claim to it.** "This chapter does not establish a private right of action."
    - Locator: `ca2026ab1609`, s. 22628(d).

33. **The leading United States authority on timing refused the earlier hearing, and binds only government.**
    - Locator: `mathews1976eldridge`, at 349, at 332.

34. **The leading English authority on acting before rights are decided withholds relief where damages are adequate.**
    - Locator: `americancyanamid1975ethicon`, transcript p. 5.

35. **Where the law creates an overseer with power, it gives the affected person no access to that overseer.**
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, arts 14, 26, 85, 86; item 12.

36. **Scope and dates.** Spain: large firms and basic services; adaptation period not yet elapsed on 11 Nov 2026. California: enrolled text read, operative date not verified. AI Act: high-risk systems, Annex III systems from 2 December 2027. Platform work directive: workers, transposition due 2 December 2026.
    - Locator: the 'Critical notes' of each card.

The EU position after *SCHUFA* cuts the other way and should be stated as prominently: Article 22(1) "lays down a prohibition in principle, the infringement of which does not need to be invoked individually", so for a solely automated significant decision outside the three exceptions EU law requires more than a later appeal.
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, para 52.

### Sentences in DRAFT_v6 that these texts touch (flagged for the authors; I changed nothing)

37. Conclusion: the stop-button requirement "is owed to overseers, not guests". The Act states what the persons assigned to oversight must be "enabled" to do and does not say to whom the requirement is owed; it is met by the provider, the deployer must assign the persons, and public authorities enforce it. A wording the text would bear: the Act requires that an overseer can override and stop a high-risk system, and gives the person affected no route to that overseer.
    - Locator: `eu2024aiact`, arts 14(4), 16(a), 26(2), 85, 99(4); Critical notes, first added bullet.

38. 'When a person must be reachable': "Under the General Data Protection Regulation, a person has the right not to be subject to a solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effects". Correct for the EU. For the United Kingdom the corresponding article was replaced on 5 February 2026; a reader there no longer has that right in that form.
    - Locator: `uk2025datause`, s. 80(1); S.I. 2026/82, reg. 2(j).

39. Same passage: "The law can therefore be satisfied by a later review rather than a person at the threshold." This holds inside the contract and consent exceptions. The Court of Justice's reading of art. 22(1) as "a prohibition in principle", and its holding that a vendor's score can itself be the decision, are not yet in the draft.
    - Locator: `schufa2023cjeu`, paras 52-54, 73.

40. If the authors want one statute that states their timing line, the platform work directive is the one on this list: a human takes the gravest decisions, reasons are due "at the latest on the date on which it takes effect", and compensation is owed only "Where such rectification is not possible". It protects workers, and the paper would have to say so.
    - Locator: `eu2024platformwork`, arts 10(5), 11(1), 11(3).
