# Claim audit of DRAFT_v7, part A: Introduction, Section 2 and Section 3 (S-001 to S-151)

**Summary.** I audited every sentence of DRAFT_v7 from S-001 to S-151, except S-027, S-058, S-093 and S-097, which were cut at assembly. Each sentence was read as it stands in `DRAFT_v7.md`, against the saved full text wherever one exists. Card-only checks are marked as such. The table has 127 rows. Twenty-one rows are the authors' own steps, definitions or hooks; each is marked as theirs, and none states the thesis above 'can'. The other 106 rows were checked against sources: 74 SUPPORTED, 1 SUPPORTED (card only), 21 PARTIAL, 4 LOCATOR WRONG, 4 OVERSTATED, 1 MISATTRIBUTED, 1 CONTRADICTED. There were no QUOTE MISMATCH verdicts: every quoted string I looked up is in the source at or next to its locator. The three most serious problems:
1. **S-122** says that neither the law of delegation nor the moral literature says what a host owes while a failure can still be put right. The same Directive the paragraph cites (2015/2302, art. 13(2)–(4)) does say it, as does CRA 2015 s. 55, and so does the draft's own Section 4.
2. **S-139** grounds the further duty's being owed to the guest in Herstein §8.4, as 'claims justify duties owed to the claimant'. Herstein says rights justify correlative duties only 'at times', and the same section reports that dynamically justified duties arguably break correlativity.
3. **S-110 and S-111** use *Crinkley* and *Orlando* as cases of several parties holding the duty of care through control. In both, the brand answered vicariously, on apparent agency. The 'control' passage in *Crinkley* is evidence for that agency, and *Orlando* did not hold that the brand 'represented the motel as its own'.

S-089 repeats a fault the v6 adjudication recorded: the paper says hospitality studies pictures the host as one person, while its own S-011 and S-094 to S-096 cite the journal's names for hosting divided among several parties.
