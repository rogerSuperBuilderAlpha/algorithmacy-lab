- **S-182.1, premises.** P1 The host owes the guest attention to the guest's needs and wishes (S-062; a sourced row in another batch; the paper itself says the law did not carry this duty). P2 The duty stays with the holder under delegation (S-129, S-135; earlier inference). P3 Attending to a guest requires that some party can see how a decision about that guest was made (assumption, unstated). C A touchpoint must keep common understanding, which Q3 tests as 'Who can see how it was made?' (S-187).
- **Counter-model.** A concierge resolves a guest's problem without anyone at the desk seeing how the system reached its outcome: attention without visibility. A fully documented allocation rule ignores a guest's need: visibility without attention. P3 fails both ways.
- **Terms.** 'Common understanding' has three senses in the batch: the host's attention to the guest (S-182); the guest's understanding of the system ('a guest who understands a system', S-183); the visibility of how a decision was made, to parties unnamed (Q3, S-187). None is the source's (a shared view of the task among co-workers).
- **Register.** The paper's own source for the duty to attend gives the guest no claim to press: the classical guest 'has no right other than to respect', and one who 'makes claims or demands' usurps the host's place (S-086, Pitt-Rivers 2012: 514–515). A condition for *exercising standing* is here drawn from a duty that, on that source, the guest cannot demand. K4 and K5 place attending in the customary register for the same reason.
- **Overgeneration.** A bank's relationship manager and a hospital have duties of attention at least as strong. The paper concedes that the mechanism is general (S-066, S-070). **Undergeneration.** In a self-check-in rental, hosting by attention is what the arrangement removes, so the derivation has least purchase where S-195 applies the test.
- **K claim.** K7 (its customary footing: 'a host that cannot be told is not attending') and K4.

- **S-182.2, premises.** P1 To ask, object or request an exception (S-133) a guest must know the terms that apply (assumption). P2 Terms that change without notice cannot be known (assumption). P3 The holder owes the guest terms held steady, or notice of change (missing: no sentence in the paper states this duty and no source is cited for it). C Predictability is a condition the touchpoint must keep.
- **Why invalid as written.** The sentence announces a derivation from the host's duties and then names a state of affairs, not a duty. P1 and P2 would derive predictability from what pressing a claim requires, which is a different ground. The only nearby statements cut against P3: 'None of these sources makes a negotiated exception something the guest is owed' (S-064), and the guest can expect 'a hearing by someone with authority, not the exception itself' (S-148).
- **Counter-model.** A hotel reprices daily and a duty manager with authority answers every query: terms are not steady and standing is exercisable. Fixed terms with no one to address: steady and not exercisable.
- **Overgeneration.** If unsteady terms defeat predictability, every revenue-managed price fails it (airlines, ride-hailing, any dynamic pricing); the paper does not concede this. S-192 applies the condition in exactly that way.
- **K claim.** None. No K claim carries a duty of stable terms.

- **S-182.3, premises.** P1 The duty stays with the holder when the work is delegated (S-116 to S-129; earlier inference from *Woodland*, another batch). P2 Responsibility is the holder's retained duty to see that the obligation is performed (S-101; stipulation with a source). P3 A duty to see that something is done includes the means to learn that it is not being done and to correct it (K7's P3; the paper's own step, not stated in v6 in these words; nearest are S-123 and S-148). P4 Correction needs someone with authority in S-102's sense (assumption). C The holder must answer to the guest through someone with authority.
- **Terms.** 'Accountability' moves through four senses: task responsibility among co-workers (S-181); a relation in which an actor must explain and justify to a forum that can question and judge (S-103; Bovens 2007: 450: "the actor has an obligation to explain and to justify his or her conduct, the forum can pose questions and pass judgement, and the actor may face consequences"); a party's being obliged to answer (S-183, Q5); a reachable party with authority to change the decision (S-182, S-192, S-195). Bovens's relation needs no power to change the decision. A duty manager who must explain every refusal and can change none satisfies 'the sense defined above' and fails S-182's.
- **'The host's duties'.** The duty to answer is not among the four duties of S-065. It is the product of the paper's own argument in 'Delegation and standing', so for this condition the derivation runs from the paper's conclusion, not from the duties hospitality studies or the law supply.
- **Overgeneration.** The same argument holds for any holder of a retained duty (school, hospital, bank); conceded at S-066. **Undergeneration.** A guest placed and paid for by a third party, and a short-term rental in a host's own home (S-226, 'at its weakest'): the derivation does not say who the holder is, so Q5 has no determinate answer there.
