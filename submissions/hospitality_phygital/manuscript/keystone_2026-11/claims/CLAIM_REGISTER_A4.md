### 2. The fifteen rows that most endanger the paper

1. **S-116.1** The slogan is true of every duty in a sense that costs the holder nothing, and true in the sense the paper needs only of named classes; an adult guest fails three of *Woodland*'s five criteria.
2. **S-111.1** Vendors are called 'arrangers'; in *Woodland* the arranger is the party on the host's side and the vendor is the contractor.
3. **S-112.3** 'A vendor answers to the host' has a false premise, and in *Woodland* itself the contractor was sued by the child.
4. **S-112.1** 'Holding out' means a proprietor's public profession in the previous section and a brand's estoppel here; the reliance condition is dropped; *Hayman* is a counter-model.
5. **S-118.3** Morgan's sentence is used for the opposite of his point: careful delegation ordinarily performs a duty of care.
6. **S-110.2** The 'arranger' rule is a dictum about schools with a condition (no negligence of their own) that the paraphrase drops.
7. **S-118.2** Two of three characteristics are quoted; the court says those two do not make a duty non-delegable (para 12).
8. **S-120.2** A liability for goods, through servants, is read as a liability for the host's duty, through anyone.
9. **S-119.2** The structure is borrowed without the features that produce it, and the disclaimer is not kept at L12, L22 and L149.
10. **S-108.5** A negligence verdict under the general law of premises is presented as an allocation of the host's obligation.
11. **S-103.2** and **S-103.1** Bovens's definition loses 'the actor may face consequences'; his accountability is retrospective and Table 2 makes it real-time.
12. **S-101.2** and **S-101.1** 'Responsibility' is defined from an exceptional class of duty and then never used in the defined sense.
13. **S-102.1** 'Authority' changes its object and its source; under the definition the single host has none.
14. **S-108.3** 'Held liable' compresses a jury verdict affirmed on a sufficiency standard, in a federal court applying state law.
15. **S-121.1** Moving from servant to vendor changes the rule of liability in tort, not only the performer.
