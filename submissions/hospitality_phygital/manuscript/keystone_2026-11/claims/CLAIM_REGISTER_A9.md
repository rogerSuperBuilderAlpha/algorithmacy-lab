What readers in neighbouring fields would say the paper owes them (records fetched 11 Nov 2026; queries in closing list 4):

- **Human–computer interaction.** 'Interruptibility' there is a person's availability to be interrupted by a system or another person. Hudson et al. (2003, CHI, doi:10.1145/642611.642657; abstract read): systems 'typically have no way to take into account the interruptibility of the user'. OpenAlex lists 103 works with the word in the title, and the most cited are in this sense. The paper uses the reverse direction and should say so. Its own hotel source reports the HCI sense: Pigac's guests 'preferred minimal interruption' at check-in (p. 291).
- **AI safety.** 'Safe interruptibility' is an operator's ability to stop an agent without the agent learning to resist: Orseau and Armstrong (2016, 'Safely interruptible agents'; OpenAlex W7225219372; abstract read: 'it may be necessary for a human operator to press the big red button'); Leike et al. - **Contestability by design.** This literature already holds the idea. Hirsch et al. (2017, DIS, doi:10.1145/3064663.3064703; abstract read) 'identify "contestability" as a new principle'. (2023, *Minds and Machines* 33(4); full text in the library, not cited in v6), summarizing Hirsch et al., write that 'Contestability ensures such failures are avoided by allowing human controllers to intervene before machine decisions are put into force' (§2; the interveners are the system's human controllers), and, crediting Brkan 2019, that 'human controllers who respond to intervention request should have the authority and capability to actually change previous decisions' (§6.1.4). The paper's addition is an intervention the affected person starts before the decision takes effect, owed as part of a host's duty. It should claim that and credit the rest. v6 already cites Lyons et al. 2021 and Shabnam et al. ('algorithmic governance must remain contestable') without connecting them to the term.

## Notes on the five points of care

**The five principles.** Two follow from the paper's argument: principle 3 from question 2 of the test and principle 4 from K7. Principle 5 follows from question 6 on a premise credited to a book I could not read. Principle 1 is tied to evidence that points away from it (S-231.4). Principle 2 is attached: nothing in the test asks for it, and its one source argues against remedies that rest on individual control (S-231.6). The empirical support under the five is thin in the same way throughout: fifty luxury-hotel interviewees (Pigac), online raters of hypothetical decisions outside hospitality (Martin and Waldman), consultation submissions (Lyons), three conceptual papers (Wilson-Nash, Shabnam, Baum and Laux), a philosophy paper about clinicians' tools (Hatherley), one abstract (Kim), and a 1990 laboratory study of male undergraduates (Lind). Only Pigac and Kim concern hospitality.

**Interruptibility.** S-242 and closing list 4.

**Accountability and the forum.** Bovens requires a forum that is a person or body, an obligation to explain and justify, questioning, judgement and possible consequences (pp. 450–452). He says accountability in his sense is 'in nature retrospective' (p. 453). 'The place where a guest's claim can be lodged' is not a forum in his sense (S-249.1), and accountability 'in real time' is what he calls control (S-250.3).

**The roadmap and Table 2.** The stages do not follow from the principles (S-246.1). 'Rising order of cost' is asserted (S-246.2, T2.title.2). Three of five 'condition it secures' cells do not match the paper's guest-side definitions (T2.r1.c3, T2.r2.c3, T2.r3.c3), and 'Accountability, through exit' matches neither Bovens, nor Wilson-Nash, nor the paper's own question 6 (T2.r5.c3).

**The captivity and voice citations.** All four Wilson-Nash locators are right and the quoted words are exact; the faults are that propositions of an untested conceptual paper are stated as facts (S-237.3, .4) and that a hedged remark about abandoning a service is used for a guest who declines a route (S-238.1). The Lind result is accurately reported but unlabelled as a student laboratory study (S-245.2).
