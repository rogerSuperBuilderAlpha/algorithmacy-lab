# Claim audit of DRAFT_v6: adjudication

## What was done

- **Audit.** Ten auditors split the 286 ledger sentences and both tables into **632 propositions** (`CLAIM_REGISTER_A1.md` to `A10.md`). Each row carries the passage from the source's own text, the mismatch classes failed, a verdict and a severity.
- **Calibration first.** The method was tested blind on 24 sentences of known status and passed (10 of 12 known faults caught, no sound sentence faulted; `DECISIONS_K.md`, K-D3). The brief was then tightened for the three blind spots the test showed.
- **Verification.** Three fresh agents re-did **178 rows** from the sources (`VERIFY_V1.md`, `V2.md`, `V3.md`): every fatal row, every row an auditor flagged as doubtful, a fixed sample of faulted rows, a fixed sample of passed rows, and named disputes.

## How far the registers can be trusted

| Selection | Rows re-done | Verifier agreed |

| Faulted rows, fixed sample | 94 | 92 |
| Passed rows, fixed sample | 29 | 27 |
| Fatal rows | 19 | 16 upheld; 3 downgraded to material |

- **Faulted rows are reliable.** A verdict of partial, equivocal or invalid can be acted on.
- **Passed rows carry a small miss rate.** Two of 29 sampled 'supported' rows should have been 'partial'. Across some 150 passed source rows, that suggests about ten faults still unseen. v7's new and carried-over sentences are audited again by an agent that did not write them.
- **Every passage the verifiers looked up was at its locator and said what the auditor reported.**

**Limits.** Auditors read sources by passage and by search, seldom cover to cover, so a statement that a source is silent rests on searches. Some twenty citations rest on abstracts only. Auditors and verifiers are the same model, so their errors may run together. Twenty rows drawn at random are listed at the end for the authors to check by hand.

## The fatal findings, after verification

Sixteen stand. All but one are faults in the paper's own inferences. They fall under five heads.

**1. Non-delegability stated as a general rule** (A-4.1, S-015.1, S-116.1, S-257.1).
'The work can be delegated and the duty cannot' is true of every duty of care only in the sense in which careful delegation discharges it. In the strong sense it is *Woodland*'s exceptional category, and an adult hotel guest fails three of the court's five criteria. Attaching a duty to a role does not make it non-delegable.

**2. The step to a reachable person and a hearing** (S-123.1, S-148.1, S-144.1, S-127.4).
The retained duty does not yield 'the guest must be able to reach the holder'; *Woodland* itself is the counter-model. None of the four duties is a duty to hear. The general rule about requests rests on two anecdotes. *Woodland* para 24 cuts against the paper's use of it.

**3. The role, its holders and 'holding out'** (S-053.3, S-065.1, S-092.1, S-112.1).
'Role' slides from a customary host to the legal status of innkeeper. The four duties mix registers and jurisdictions. Four possible holders are named against a statute that names one. 'Holding out' is a proprietor's offer in one section and a brand's estoppel in the next.

**4. Vendors miscast** (S-111.1, S-112.3).
*Woodland*'s arranger is the school, which stands where the host stands. In that case the child sued the contractor directly, so 'the vendor answers to the host' has no support there.

**5. The foil and one case** (S-047.1; S-192.3).
'The debate, on both sides, turns on the host's motive' is false: Slattery rejects the motive test, and four hospitality sources state a host's duty. In the hotel-pricing case, 'or reach the managers who held it' is not in the source, whose hotels are small and run by the manager who sets the prices. That sentence was written on 11 Nov as a correction and is itself wrong.

## Where I overrule a register

- **S-175.1** ('authority over the rule drifts towards … a vendor'): material, not fatal. The claim is partial. Managers keep the formal decision in the two hotel studies, yet one of them says machines 'hold real authority' (Garcia et al., working paper, p. 7).
- **S-250.3 and T2.r4.c2** (accountability 'in Bovens' sense' working 'in real time'): equivocal and material, not contradicted and fatal. Bovens calls accountability 'in nature retrospective' (p. 453) but allows a forum's veto (p. 452) and an account before the act (p. 453). The fault is that the paper uses 'accountability' for a reachable person with authority.
- **A5's gloss of *Woodland* para 24** inverts the sentence; the verifier's reading governs.
- **A10 on Kong.** 'Address' is on pp. 3, 4 and 10 of Kong (2026), and 'addressee' on pp. 10 and 13. The 11 Nov citation to p. 3 stands. I checked the saved text.
- **S-076.3.** Holt's 'If the Inn be full' sentence is on 12 Mod 484, not 485. I checked the running heads in the saved report and corrected the card.

## Disputes settled from the primary texts

- **The 1956 Act.** It deems only its defined hotel an inn, attaches the duties 'to the proprietor' and to no other person, and its definition includes 'without special contract'. An operating company as holder goes beyond the Act. A brand contradicts it only if said to hold the innkeeper's duties alongside the proprietor.
- ***Crinkley*.** The sentence on vicarious liability is at reporter page 160.
- ***Page v. Sloan*** (N.C. 1972). It calls the innkeeper's premises-safety duty non-delegable, as one of three alternative grounds on summary judgment. To be carded.
- **GDPR art. 12(3).** A request under art. 22 must be answered within one month, extendable by two. 'States no timing' needs that qualification.
- **42 U.S.C. § 2000a(b)(1).** It removes small owner-occupied lodgings from an anti-discrimination definition. It draws no line between commercial and household hosting.
- **AI Act art. 14.** Its oversight duties are owed to no named person and apply from 2 December 2027 (Annex III systems) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I).
- **The relay.** Batat (2024) may be credited with two named processes that shift a customer's experience between physical and digital settings. The relay of records and rulings is the paper's own.
- **The gap claims in the Introduction.** Each stands only in a narrower form (`VERIFY_V1.md`). Cobbe et al. (2023: 1187) refute 'ties it to harm', and the paper's own 'This journal already has words for the result' contradicts its statement that the debate has no way to say where the obligation goes.

## What follows for v7

1. **The two theory sections are rebuilt** on the argument in `ARGUMENT.md`. The registers for A2 to A5 say, source by source, what each may still be used for; the verifier confirmed those passages and narrowed four of them (Morrison and O'Gorman's Figure 2; Pitt-Rivers p. 514; the emergency-treatment statute; one truncated quotation from Lynch et al.).
2. **The other sections are revised row by row** from registers A1 and A6 to A10: every fatal and material row is changed or cut, and each change is recorded in v7's ledger with the row id.
3. **The terms are fixed once.** 'Authority' (power over the single case, and power over the rule, named apart), 'answerability', 'accountability' and 'holding out' each get one sense, and every use is checked against it.
4. **The thesis is stated at one strength.** v6 states it ten times at four strengths; the body supports 'can'.
5. **The test and both tables are rebuilt** on the new argument's question: can the guest's report reach, in time, a person with power over the single case?
