## The authority–answerability split

### S-154 · The authority–answerability split ¶1
> By the authority–answerability split we mean an arrangement in which the party that can change a decision, or the rule behind it, is not a party the guest can reach.

| Row | Proposition | Type | Source, version, locator | Passage (from the source) | Classes failed | Verdict | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S-154.1 | Definition of the split: the party able to change a decision, or its rule, is not a party the guest can reach | Definition or stipulation | none; compare S-102, S-103 | — | — | equivocal | material | Fix four things before the term is used again: decision or rule; who must be reachable (the holder, or anyone with the power); what 'reach' covers; and what 'answerability' adds to 'reach'. The uses in other senses are listed below and in 'Changes of sense'. |

Uses in another sense, in this batch:

- **S-165** asks whether the claim reaches 'someone with authority over the rule': the first half of the definition ('a decision') is gone.
- **S-167** says 'The split did not begin with algorithms' on evidence of authority that is graded or divided, with every party in reach (see S-167).
- **S-176** gives power over the single case a different name, discretion, and keeps 'authority' for the rule. Under S-154 a guest who meets someone able to change the decision has reached 'the party that can change a decision'; under S-176 the same guest has met discretion and no authority.
- **S-177** restates the split as the point 'where a decision is made' coming apart from 'any point where the guest can be answered': making a decision replaces the power to change one, and being answered replaces being reached.

Tests on the definition as it stands:

- **Against S-102.** There authority is 'the power, assigned by an organization, to change a decision or the rule behind it'. S-154 keeps the two objects, drops 'assigned by an organization', and turns a power into 'the party that can'.
- **Against S-103.** 'Answerability' is in the name and not in the definiens, which speaks only of reach. S-103's accountability is the holder answering 'for how the obligation was performed', with explanation and judgement. The holder does not appear in S-154.
- **Overgeneration inside hospitality.** Every property that licenses a pricing or allocation system has the split, however reachable its manager, because the supplier 'can change … the rule' and no guest reaches a supplier. S-112 proposes that such a party 'answers to the host', not to the guest. The definition counts as a failure what S-112 treats as the proper order.
- **The 'or'.** The definition does not say whether the split is absent when the guest can reach someone who can change the decision and no one who can change the rule. S-176 implies it is present; the abstract ('a reachable party with authority to change the decision'), S-208 and K7's P4 ('a person with power over the system's output in the single case') imply it is absent.
- K claim: K7, K9.

## Changes of sense: authority, answerability, discretion and the terms beside them

The comparison is with 'Delegation and standing' ¶2: S-102 ('Authority is the power, assigned by an organization, to change a decision or the rule behind it') and S-103 ('Accountability is the arrangement through which the holder answers to the guest for how the obligation was performed', with Bovens's actor who 'must explain and justify its conduct to a forum that can pose questions and pass judgement').

**1. Authority.**
