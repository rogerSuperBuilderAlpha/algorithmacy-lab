Inference S-123.1:
1. P1 (earlier inference; *Woodland* para 7, borrowed with the disclaimer of S-119): when the work of hosting is delegated, the duty stays with the holder.
2. C: the guest must be able to reach the holder.
- **Does it follow?** No. P1 says who owes the duty and who is liable for its breach. C adds a requirement that the person owed can reach that party. On the kindest reading the sentence only picks the party (if a guest must reach anyone, it is the holder and not the performer); that reading presupposes the requirement and does not derive it.
- **Premise that would make it follow.** K7's P3 with P4: a duty to see that something is done includes having the means to learn that it is not being done and to correct it while performance is still possible (P3), and in hospitality the guest's own report is often the only timely signal (P4). With both, C follows for the length of the stay. v6 states neither.
- **Counter-model.** *Woodland* itself. The education authority's duty stayed with it; the child had no channel to the authority during the swimming lesson; the law answered with damages years later. The doctrine starts from the claimant's lack of any say (para 23(3)) and makes up for it with strict responsibility, not with access. P1 is true there and C is false.
- **Terms.** *Reach* has three senses in the batch: (i) identify and sue afterwards; (ii) contact during the stay; (iii) put a request before a person with power to change the decision. P1 supports (i) at most. The test in the next section needs (iii). *Holder* is a firm; reaching a firm means reaching some person, and which person is left open until S-144 ('someone with authority').
- **Quantifiers.** 'The party' assumes one holder. S-108 (*Crinkley*) and the paper's own proposal in S-112 give at least two (the party that holds itself out and the party in control). The sentence does not say which must be reachable.
- **Overgeneration.** The same step would prove that a pupil must be able to reach the education authority, a patient the hospital trust, an employee the employer and a bank customer the bank, wherever a duty survives delegation. The paper concedes that the mechanism is general (S-066: 'What separates hospitality from other services… is not this mechanism').
- **Undergeneration.** A guest placed and paid for by a third party (who is the holder?), a restaurant (no non-delegable duty in the sources), a short-term rental where the platform denies being the host.
- **K claim.** K7 (conclusion C), reached from K8 alone.

Inference S-148.1:
1. P1 (earlier inference, S-065): the host's obligation is a set of duties to receive, to sustain, to protect and to attend.
2. P2 (S-144): requests that change outcomes reach someone with authority.
3. C: from the host's obligation a guest can expect a hearing by someone with authority.
- **Which listed duty is a duty to hear?** None. The nearest is the duty to attend, which Pitt-Rivers states as concern for the guests' 'needs and wishes' (p. 516), and which K4 holds to be customary and not legal. The same code forbids the guest to make 'claims or demands' (p. 515). The test section later draws accountability 'from the duty to answer through someone with authority'; that is a fifth duty, which the role section never lists or derives.
- **Do the cases show hearings?** In Xiang's first case the guest was heard by a security employee and received compensation. In the second he sought a hearing from the general manager; the source does not say he got one. Lugosi's bar shows none.
- **Counter-model.** A hotel that grants or refuses every request at the desk under fixed rules, takes care of every guest, and never puts a guest before a manager. P1 and P2 hold (the few requests that succeed are granted by those able to grant them) and no guest gets a hearing by someone with authority. Nothing in P1 is breached.
- **Terms.** *Can expect* slides from prediction (what happens when requests succeed) to entitlement (what the obligation gives). *Hearing* is undefined: on Darwall's account a bare listening does not satisfy the person owed (p. 38), and the paper itself later cites Lind et al. on voice that changes nothing. *Authority*: as S-144.
- **Quantifiers.** P2 concerns requests that succeed. C concerns every guest.
- **Overgeneration.** The argument gives a bank's or an airline's customer the same hearing; conceded.
- **Undergeneration.** A placed guest who did not pay; a restaurant diner; a rental with no one present.
- **What would make it follow.** K7's P1 to P4: the holder owes continuing duties; it keeps a duty to see them performed; that duty includes the means to learn of failure in time; the guest's report is often the only such means. The conclusion is then a channel to a person able to correct performance, for as long as performance is possible. That differs from 'a hearing' on a request for an exception.
- **K claim.** K7 (and K6 for the second route).

### 2. The fifteen rows that most endanger the paper

1. **S-123.1** (fatal). The duty's staying with the holder does not yield a requirement that the guest can reach the holder; *Woodland*, the paper's own authority, is the counter-model.
2. **S-148.1** (fatal). 'A hearing by someone with authority' is drawn from three cases; no listed duty of the host is a duty to hear, and the cases show no hearing by authority.
3. **S-144.1** (fatal). A universal from two anecdotes and a non-instance; false on the paper's stipulated sense of authority, empty on the loose one; the paper's own later evidence (Bendoly) contradicts it.
4. **S-127.4** (fatal). *Woodland* para 24 undercuts the application: a hotel controls the environment, and Lord Sumption's category needs custody of the vulnerable; OLA 1957 s. 2(4)(b) lets an occupier escape by reasonable entrustment.
5. **S-129.1**. 'Role' and 'answering' change sense; the accountability writers license answerability for outcomes, not the role-holder's duty to the guest.
6. **S-126.4**. Kong's argument that roles matter only derivatively (p. 4) goes unanswered, and S-127 then grounds the paper's duty in control.
7. **S-133.2**. 'Standing exercised' includes requests for what is not owed and builds the addressee into the definition, so the abstract's finding is true by definition.
8. **S-139.1**. Payment as what makes a claim proper fails on the paper's own evidence: the paying guest of S-138 did not complain.
9. **S-134.2**. Darwall p. 35 is used against its context; the next sentences say the authority to demand is part of the right.
10. **S-132.2**. Thompson's form does not show that a directed duty is independent of answering; Thompson ties directed duties to grounds of complaint (p. 344).

14. **S-126.3**. 'From the moment of reception' conflicts with a duty to receive and with the trigger stated in the role section.
15. **S-148.2**. 'Not the exception itself' meets a statutory duty of adjustment owed to disabled guests in Great Britain.
