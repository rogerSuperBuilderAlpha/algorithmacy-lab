# Response to reviewers

**Who hosts the guest? Algorithmic mediation, standing and responsibility in phygital hospitality**

We thank the three reviewers for careful and demanding reports. Reviewer 2's request for a theory of where the host's obligation comes from, and Reviewer 3's challenge to our assumptions about hosting and authority, changed the paper's foundation rather than its surface. Two claims of the submitted version did not survive the revision, and we have replaced them: that the host's duty can never be delegated, which the law we read contradicts, and that the guest's standing carries the argument, which Reviewer 2 showed to be circular.

## Summary of the main changes

1. **The argument starts inside hospitality studies.** The first main section, 'The host's power and the host's duty', sets out what hospitality scholars and this journal have said about the host's duty to protect and the host's power over the guest. It argues that the role of host specifies the duty, and that three things bind it to a particular party: holding out, the guest's reliance and the host's control of the space the guest is in. The law shows the duty's content in two tiers, admission and reception.
2. **Delegation, stated duty by duty.** The second section, 'Delegation and the guest's claim: Beyond the host in person?', shows that what stays with a host that performs through others depends on the duty and on the doctrine that governs it, and that several parties, owners, operators and brands among them, may hold the duty of care at once.
3. **The paper's own step, marked as ours.** The same section argues that during the stay the guest's claim to performance justifies a further duty: to keep some timely means by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it. The ground is the guest's position inside the host's space, which this journal has made hospitality's own question. The law's part is stated separately, and the paper says where no law we read reaches.
4. **One application section, on documented cases.** 'Assembled hosts at the touchpoint' locates the person with power over a guest's case, says who must be at the end of the route, sets out six questions for examining a touchpoint with four documented cases, says when the route is owed (Table 1) and what the law already requires, and closes on design and five stages (Table 2).
5. **Evidence and style.** Every case is taken from a published study or judgment. Every quotation and locator was checked against the source's text, and sources we could read only in abstract are cited for what the abstract says. The vocabulary is fixed and defined at first use, and the thesis is stated at one strength throughout.
