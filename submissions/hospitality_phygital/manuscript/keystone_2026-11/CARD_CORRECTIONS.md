**The readers' six conclusions, as CV-C judged them.**
- *A non-delegable duty has no oversight content*: narrowed. The liability is 'based not on personal fault' and 'a higher standard of care' (*Armes* paras 31–32), not strict.
- *No ordinary duty to supervise a competent contractor; knowledge ends the protection*: narrowed. The first half holds. The second is Lord Keith's guarded 'might well', which Lord Goff did not accept.
- *The party that promised answers for others' performance*: narrowed to a party that undertook to provide, and to care rather than safety. *Kuoni* treats cleaning and guiding within the hotel as contracted services, so the contract limb reaches some dangers on the premises when a package promises the service.
- *Package law: the organiser answers, a contact point, assistance*: confirmed. In the UK the contact point is enforced only by prosecution.
- *No text requires a person with authority at the contact point*: confirmed.
- *Dates*: confirmed. Directive 2026/1024 applies from 29 March 2029; SI 2026/455 from 6 April 2027, for contracts made from then.

**Effect on ARGUMENT.md.** Steps 5 and 6 now give the knowledge passage as Lord Keith's guarded dictum, with Lord Goff's dissent from it and the Court of Appeal's 'reason to contemplate'. 'Answers for the result' becomes 'answers for the delegate's want of care'. *Kuoni* para 30 is an example, not a holding. The R5a report's claim that the leisure-centre operator had contracted away its power to open its machines is wrong (see `maguire2006sefton`), and the dossier does not use it.
