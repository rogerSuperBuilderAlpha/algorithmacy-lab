## alnajar2019cumberland

**PASS-WITH-NOTES**

1. **Identity.** The cover sheet matches the card: [2019] EWHC 1593 (QB), case no. HQ17P00870, Queen's Bench Division, Dingemans J, judgment dated 21 June 2019, and ten hearing days listed between 7 and 22 May 2019. The parties match too. The record is re-fetched and its hash matches.
2. **Read depth.** The saved text holds the whole judgment, paras 1-240, with the cover sheet. The card's claim is honest.
3. **Quotations.** 48 strings, all MATCH at the stated paragraphs. One of them, "no challenge to the finding", comes from the Court of Appeal judgment. The card attributes it there, and it matches CA para 19. One non-quotation claim is unsupported. The card's critical notes call it a "slip" that the judgment cites *Robinson* para 34 at para 182 and para 35 at para 187. But para 182 itself cites both paragraphs, para 34 for the four exceptional situations and para 35 for the general rule absent them. Without *Robinson* I cannot call either citation a slip.
4. **Holding.** Issue (1) asked whether the admitted duty "extended to a duty to take reasonable steps to prevent the attack" (para 11). The defendant denied that it did (para 9), and the judge decided the point (paras 187, 240(1)). The basis is his own. He calls it a "responsibility" type case under *Robinson* para 35, "because the hotel invited guests to come and stay at the hotel and thereby assumed a duty to take reasonable care to protect guests". He adds that the result is "consistent with" *Chordas* and with *Everett*, "the latter of which is binding on me", and accords with "reasonable expectations" (para 187). The guests lost on issue (4), breach (paras 229, 237, 240). The duty finding was not needed for that result, so a strict reader may call it obiter. It was nonetheless a contested issue that the judge decided at a preliminary-issue trial. The card's account of who won is correct.
5. **May say / may not say.** Sentence 1 is LICENSED-NARROWER (see the table). Sentences 2-5 are LICENSED. Among the 'may not say' items, (1), (2), (4), (5) and (6) are right. Item (3) ("flows from the hotel's status as an inn or from the contract") is right as to the 1956 Act and the contract. It is too cautious if read to forbid any link to hotel-keeping. The judge began "from the fact that the Defendant operates a hotel" (para 183) and ended on "the common law relating to hotel proprietors has developed since 1604" (para 187). The paper may say that he treated the duty as a development of the common law of hotel proprietors, reached through assumption of responsibility.
6. **K1-K9.** I agree with the card on K4 (supports, for a hotel), K5 (supports) and K7 (narrows: paras 229-233). On K8 I also agree that the support is silence. The text bears out the card's figures: three security officers were on duty (para 42), two of them supplied by agencies (paras 63-64; the night shift is listed at para 135). On K3 I agree that the stated basis is assumption by invitation and that dependence appears only in a witness's evidence (para 40).
7. **Reader's inferences.** R4a says the duty "does not flow from the role of innkeeper" and so "fits K3's claim that the role specifies and something outside it binds". The text leaves this open. The act the judge treats as assuming responsibility, inviting guests to stay, is the defining act of keeping a hotel (paras 183, 187). Two readings fit his words: the role specifies and the assumption binds, or the role binds through its defining act. A later case would settle the question if it applied para 187 to an occupier that is not a hotel, or refused to.

The capture of s. 5 (20250613194624), which I re-fetched, has the same text as the saved copy, and the page states no outstanding effects for s. 5.
2. **Read depth.** The saved copies hold s. 2(1)-(6) and s. 5(1)-(4) in full. Honest.
3. **Quotations.** All in-scope strings MATCH. They come from s. 2(1), (2), (4) opening words, (4)(b) and (5), and s. 5(1)-(4).
4. **Holding.** Not applicable.
5. **May say / may not say.** Sentence 4 is LICENSED-NARROWER (see the table). Sentences 1-3 are LICENSED. The five 'may not say' items are right.
6. **K1-K9.** I agree on K4, K8 and K7.
7. **Reader's inferences (the brief's example).** The claim is that s. 5(1) cuts back the warranty in *MacLenan*. The texts **support** it on the words. Section 5(1) governs a duty "in respect of dangers due to the state of the premises", owed to those who enter "in exercise of a right conferred by contract with a person occupying", "in so far as the duty depends on a term to be implied in the contract by reason of its conferring that right". McCardie J's warranty is exactly such a term: it arises "Where the occupier of premises agrees for reward that a person shall have the right to enter and use them" (*MacLenan*, pp. 332-333). The fire came from the state of the premises. Section 5(1) makes that duty "the common duty of care", so the "on the part of any one" extension goes. The reader's mechanism needs one correction. Section 2(4)(b) covers only work by "an independent contractor employed by the occupier". On *MacLenan*'s own facts the builder was "employed by the landlord" (p. 328). The occupier's position would fall under the general standard of s. 2(2), which includes his own reasonable inquiries, not under the entrustment rule.
