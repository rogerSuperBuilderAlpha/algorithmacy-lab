# Implications for v6

## In one paragraph

v6's two theory sections are rebuilt on the dossier. Its touchpoint section keeps its place and its purpose, but its central terms change. The 'authority-answerability split', 'interruptibility' and 'the relay' go; 'power over the single case' and 'correction during the stay' replace them. Its test now asks one question: can a failure in the guest's case reach, in time, someone able to correct it? Its four cases become documented ones. The introduction and conclusion change to match, and the thesis is stated at one strength, 'can'. Every fatal and material row in the registers is changed or cut, and the v7 ledger records which.

## The material rows outside the theory sections

Changed or cut sentence by sentence in v7; the ledger cites each row id. The groups:

- **The phenomenon (A1).** Sourced instances only (price advice to managers; identity checks; self-check-in); 'often' for several firms; no universal 'each places'.
- **The gap claims (A1: S-011c.3, S-012.2, S-012.4, S-023.1-2).** Restated against the journal's literature (R9's gap statement) and the neighbours at abstract depth; Cobbe et al. not cited for 'ties it to harm'.
- **The split and its senses (A6: S-154.1 to S-177.1).** Power over the single case and over the rule named apart; Batat credited with two named processes only; the drift of authority to vendors is stated as a possibility unless a hospitality source shows it.
- **The test (A7: S-179.1 to S-202.3).** Six new questions; the pricing study described as what it is (managers of family-run hotels hold the final decision; the bias an estimate); Hirschman not cited unread.
- **When a person must be reachable (A8).** Rebuilt as 'when the route is owed'; Baum and Laux credited with the timing test only; Table 1 rebuilt; the GDPR's art. 22 stated with its conditions and the UK change; the 1964 Act's carve-out described as the statute draws it.
- **Design (A9).** The 'five principles line up with five stages' claim and 'rising cost' dropped; Bovens's forum used for accountability after the stay only; Table 2 rebuilt.
- **The conclusion (A10).** One strength; the AI Act's dates and addressees stated; 'every case comes from published studies' made true by the new cases.

## What v7 adds that v6 did not have

- The guest's subjection to the host's space as the premise that makes the argument about hosting (`bulley2015ethics`; `moysidou2023theorizing`).
- The law's two tiers, with every service-provider's partial admission duty (Equality Act s. 29) and the Equality Act's adjustment duty.
- Concurrent holders, brands as principals, product liability for vendors, and the placed guest in public and private law.
- What the person reached owes the guest; what the holder owes the workers at the end of the route; learning across cases.
- The law's floor, stated separately: the guest's remedial rights, the bar on obstruction, and causation.
- The limits: the guest who cannot report; household hosts.

- The claim that the work can be delegated and the duty cannot.
- The two constructed or weakly sourced cases (the admission case 'we construct'; the projected chef).
- The rising-cost order of the stages.
