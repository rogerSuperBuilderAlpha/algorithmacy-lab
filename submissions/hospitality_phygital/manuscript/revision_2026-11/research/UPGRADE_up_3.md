## For the authors

The items are ranked by how much the argument leans on each.

1. **§7, seamlessness paragraph (the paper's design-value claim).**
   - Both quotations and page numbers are exact. Batat and Hammedi (2023: 10) and Batat (2024: 1220) are verbatim abstract text.
   - Mameli et al. counts hold: "57 academic articles" and "84 practical-phygital examples" (p. 1; p. 25).
   - v4: "contains no discussion of friction at all". PARTLY. The word never appears, but the article discusses "risks and challenges" (p. 13), "drawbacks such as technical glitches and privacy concerns" (p. 14) and "negative aspects of engagement" (p. 26).
   - v4: "The extended-reality framework describes its purpose as …". PARTLY. The p. 10 sentence says what its "processes … create". The purpose sentence is p. 11: "Our goal is … phygital experiences that guarantee a continuum in customer value …".
   - A reviewer may cite Batat (2024: 1238): the framework "should not consider a positive phygital experience to be the aim".
   - For support, the same frameworks say "seamless" outright: Batat 2024: 1233; Mosca et al. 2026b: 427, 440. ERT p. 14 says "Flawless interactivity and connectivity are thus necessary".
2. **§5, the restaurant case.** v4: "the technology adds delight to an encounter whose rules stay steady and whose staff keep their discretion (Batat 2021)". Batat supports delight (p. 10) and staff present and sociable (pp. 7–9). It says nothing about rules or discretion, so that part is the authors' reading.
3. **§5, transparency.** v4: "A perfectly transparent algorithm can still replace hospitality, if no one is obliged to answer for it …". Ananny and Crawford support only the insufficiency claim: "transparency alone cannot create accountable systems" (p. 984). The obliged answerer comes from Bovens. They never use "forum".
4. **§6, Duggan.** v4: "an inability to contest decisions at all". The source says workers "rarely having any opportunity to contest" (p. 1448).
5. **§8, well-being.** v4: "how service ecosystems shape … economic, cognitive, emotional, and social lines".
   - Batat 2022 is about "the luxury ecosystem" (p. 609).
   - Batat also lists "economic, cultural, emotional, and social" (p. 612).
   - Mosca's dimensions are "individual, social, and societal" (p. 439).
6. **§3, Germann Molz 2018.** v4: "what hosts feel they must offer". The article is "exploratory reflections … illustrative" (p. 235). It shows the reputation system's demands, not what hosts feel (p. 237).
7. **§1, Batat 2024.** v4 cites it for presenting "artificial intelligence … as tools". PH-CX does not discuss AI.
8. **§7, Belanche.** v4 says "guests". They are US vignette respondents. The firm-responsibility interaction is n.s. (p. 275).
