## For the authors

Ranked by how much the argument leans on the sentence.

1. **v4 §7 (line 71):** "Voice offered after a decision was taken, with the decision explicitly unchanged, still raised judgments of both procedural and outcome fairness (Lind et al. 1990; Goodwin and Ross 1992)." Lind's abstract supports only that postdecision voice "led to higher fairness judgments than no voice"; "explicitly unchanged" is not in the abstract, and the abstract does not report procedural and distributive judgments separately. Goodwin and Ross do not test post-decision voice at all: their abstract finds voice helped when the outcome was favourable and "in some instances" lowered perceptions when no tangible offer was made. Move Goodwin to the next sentence ("An invitation to speak that was never going to bear on anything lowers those judgments"), and even there it supports "lesser effect, sometimes lower", not "never going to bear on anything". Lind's full text is needed for "explicitly unchanged".

2. **v4 §4 (line 36):** "Skilled front-desk employees often manufacture a guest's sense of control through small acts of discretion (Bendoly 2013)." The abstract concerns reservation agents' latitude to accept below-prescription rate bids in a laboratory experiment on feedback regimes. Nothing about front-desk staff, skill, or a guest's sense of control. Mis-citation unless the full text (closed, no repository copy) shows otherwise. The first Bendoly use (line 33, sanctioned latitude) is supported nearly verbatim.

3. **v4 §4 (line 36):** "Staff are still asked to produce the guest's sense of control, while no longer holding the controls that would produce it (Fuller and Smith 1991; Möhlmann et al. 2021)." Fuller and Smith's abstract is about managers using customer feedback to monitor and discipline workers. It does not say staff produce a guest's sense of control. Not checkable beyond the abstract; doubtful.

4. **Revision plan §9 / AI Act sentence (planned, not in v4):** do not print that the Art. 86 right to explanation "applies from 2 August 2026" without qualification. Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 (OJ L, 24.7.2026) point (40) leaves Art. 86 and the general date unamended but moves the high-risk obligations for Annex III systems to 2 December 2027 and for Annex I systems to 2 August 2028. The card's own earlier line "Art. 6(1) ... from 2 August 2027" is superseded (now 2 August 2028). Whether Art. 86 can be exercised for an Annex III system before the classification rules apply is a legal question the text does not settle.

5. **v4 §1 (line 8):** "The host's power over the guest runs through the whole stay, and so does what the host owes (Bulley 2015)." Bulley supports the power half (accepted MS PDF p. 3: distinctions "need to be policed, managed and controlled, even after the threshold of the home is crossed"; p. 11). He treats hospitality as "ethical responsibility-taking for others" (p. 7), so an ethics continues past the threshold, but a continuing commercial host obligation is the authors' inference, and his setting is borders and camps.

6. **v4 §7 (line 66):** "a person must stay reachable who can actually reverse or revise the decision (Yurrita et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2019)." Lee's abstract supports outcome control exercised by the affected group adjusting allocations themselves "as a group", not a reachable person who reverses a decision. Supports reversibility, not reachability.

7. **v4 §1 (line 9):** "Service research has quietly handed the host's role to the employee (Larivière et al. 2017; Odekerken-Schröder et al. 2022)." No abstract exists for Larivière in any record; the claim is reconstructed second-hand from Odekerken, who never uses "host". Not checkable.

8. **v4 §7 (line 72):** "reputation records carry their own well-documented distortions, including inflation over time (Filippas et al. 2022)." The one-sentence abstract supports inflation making records less informative; "well-documented" rests on one paper here.

9. **v4 §8 (line 88):** Christou et al. 2020 is cited for mediation that can "enchant, disenchant, or re-enchant depending on the surrounding arrangements". The abstract shows mixed outcomes (enhanced value alongside frustration, sadness, disappointment) and says nothing about enchantment or surrounding arrangements. One of four sources; carries ambivalence only.

10. **Revision plan §1 (line 19):** opening on Garcia et al. (2026) as a case "leaving a rule no one at the desk could state". The working paper's actors are managers and an algorithmic advisor; it says nothing about desk staff or anyone's ability to state the rule. The bias-in-anticipation-of-rare-repricing claim itself is supported (WP pp. 4, 14). Also: the Management Science version of record has not been read; check wording and pages before quoting.

11. **v4 §1 (line 8):** Bitner et al. 2000 for "a tool" and Introna 2010 (line 29 context, "machine as a guest") have no abstract in any record; both uses are NOT CHECKABLE this run. The Semantic Scholar machine summaries are consistent with both readings but cannot be cited.

12. **v4 §4 (line 29):** "Studies of 'robotic hospitableness' test whether a robot can make guests feel welcomed, attended to, and reassured (Liu et al. 2026)". Supported, except "test": the study builds a construct from interviews.
