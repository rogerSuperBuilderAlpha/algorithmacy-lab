## Synthesis (a): the hospitality-versus-service debate, in six sentences

Brotherton (1999, abstract) opened the definitional argument by proposing new definitions of "hospitality" and "hospitality management". Slattery (2002, p. 21) answered that in the business "the critical relationship" is not between host and guest: "The buyers are not guests, they are customers."

The side Slattery attacked held that genuine hospitality leaves a person "genuinely wanted and welcome", which "is not the same thing as being welcomed as a valued customer" (Lashley and Morrison 2000: 13, quoted in Slattery 2002, p. 22; *secondary quotation, not checked against Lashley and Morrison*).

Later work kept that line and gave it content. Hemmington (2007, abstract) centred commercial hospitality on "the host–guest relationship, generosity, theatre and performance, 'lots of little surprises', and the security of strangers". Lugosi (2008; quote on the `lugosi2008` card, full-text, page not recorded) wrote that "hospitable interaction is an acknowledgement of the other".

Golubovskaya, Robinson and Solnet (2017, abstract) then found the argument losing ground in hotels, where "service management terminology colonizes hospitality within a commercial context".

Xiang et al. (2022, Literature review, "A hospitality paradigm of host-guest interactions") state the surplus in the form closest to this paper's: hotels, as hosts, "are obliged to provide care, comfort, ease, welcome, and safety to the guests" (citing King 1995). The same study documents a manager-approved exception for one guest (Findings, Stage II, "Performance pathways: managing ritual violations").

**Flag: the field already says that hospitality is an obligation and not only a service.** The paper cannot claim "an obligation owed to the guest" as its addition. In the queries listed above, I found no work stating the narrower claim: that the negotiated exception belongs to what is owed to this arrival, and that it needs a party with authority whom the guest can reach. The field records such exceptions as generosity and "little surprises" (Hemmington 2007, abstract) or as compensatory repair of a ritual (Xiang et al. 2022, Findings, "Integrated model…"), not as a debt.

**Consequence for the §2 Distinctness sentence.** It should claim the exception and its address, not the obligation as such. It should credit King/Xiang and Lashley for the obligation. ## Synthesis (b): §3, host–guest negotiation as the practice standing makes possible

> Hospitality scholarship describes the host–guest encounter as negotiated rather than delivered. In the commercial home, Di Domenico and Lynch (2007, abstract) find host and guest building "negotiated normative practices" through domestic symbols, spatial management and "unspoken protocols", with the owner present to settle them. Lugosi (2014, abstract) carries the point into organizations: hospitality can "establish power relations and invoke obligations" both to conform and to reciprocate, and hospitable acts can also "reconfigure power relationships". The hotel case comes from Xiang et al. (2022). Under COVID-19 rules, guests who refused, concealed or argued drew the staff into "more negotiation efforts" and long cycles of interaction (Findings, "Integrated model of host-guest interaction rituals"). One guest who arrived without a test report was received after a front-office employee checked "with hotel management", and the hotel paid for his test; he then praised the employee's professionalism in an online review. Another guest, whom no one had been able to reach in advance, was refused, and the next day took his complaint to "the general manager and the group head office" (both in Findings, Stage II, "Performance pathways: managing ritual violations"). On this evidence, negotiation is the practice that standing makes possible. A guest's claim, request or refusal changes the encounter, and adds value beyond function, only when a party that is obliged to receive it, and able to grant an exception, can be reached. That condition is what phygital arrangements put at risk, and Section 4 traces how.

**Locator caveat.** Di Domenico and Lynch and Lugosi are abstract-only. Before this paragraph enters the draft, check both quotations against the full texts. The "owner present" gloss is an inference from the commercial-home setting, not a finding the abstract states.
