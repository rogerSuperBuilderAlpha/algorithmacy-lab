# Response to reviewers

**Who hosts the guest? Algorithmic mediation, standing and responsibility in phygital hospitality**

We thank the three reviewers for careful and demanding reports. Reviewer 2's request for a theory of where the host's obligation comes from, and Reviewer 3's challenge to our assumptions about hosting and authority, changed the paper's foundation. Reviewer 1's account of what the paper offers phygital hospitality told us what to keep. Two claims of the submitted version did not survive the revision, and we have replaced them: that the host's duty can never be delegated, which the law we read contradicts, and that the guest's standing carries the argument, which Reviewer 2 showed to be circular.

## Summary of the main changes

1. **The paper now starts inside phygital hospitality research.** Its theoretical context opens on Batat's definition of the phygital and on the guest's movement between physical and digital places, treats AI as a constitutive mediator of the hospitality relationship, and reads this journal's work on network hospitality and on the divided host before it turns to the law.
2. **Where the host's duty comes from.** The role of host specifies the core duties, and three facts bind them to a particular party: holding out, the guest's reliance and control of the guest's space. The law is read as a record of what the role has been held to require, not as hospitality's ground.
3. **The parties, one by one.** A new section walks the phygital arrangement party by party, from the property to the brand, the platform, the vendor and the state, and closes each subsection on what that party owes. What stays with a host that performs through others depends on the duty, and several parties may hold the duty of care at once.
4. **The paper's own step, marked as ours.** During the stay, the guest's claim justifies a further duty: to keep some timely route by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it. A section on the stay gives the ground and the boundary (Table 1), and says where no law we read reaches.
5. **The touchpoint.** The last section locates the person with power over a guest's case, says who must be at the end of the route, sets out one test in seven questions with documented cases, states the law's floor, and argues that interruptibility is what the further duty asks of design. It closes on five stages (Table 2).
6. **Evidence and style.** Every case is taken from a published study or judgment, and every quotation and locator was checked against its source; works we could read only in abstract are cited for what the abstract says. The paper follows the plan of the conceptual articles this journal publishes.
