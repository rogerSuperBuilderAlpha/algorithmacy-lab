## Title

**Who hosts the guest? Algorithmic mediation, standing and responsibility in phygital hospitality**

## Abstract (100–200 words)

*Roger's text: 186 words by whitespace count. With the bracketed sentence: 196. Verify against Word's counter before upload. Copied from DRAFT_v8.md.*

Commercial hosts increasingly hand parts of hosting, from room pricing to check-in, to algorithmic systems assembled from services supplied by several firms. Hospitality scholarship defines what a host owes and recognizes that hosting can be divided among multiple parties, yet it rarely specifies which party owes what. We argue that the role of host specifies the core duties: care for the guest's safety and diligent performance of what was undertaken. Holding out, guest reliance and control of the guest's space then bind those duties to a specific party. Where others carry out the work, what stays with the host depends on the duty at issue, and several parties may owe care at once. During the stay, the guest's claim also justifies a further duty to keep some timely route by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it, a person algorithmic mediation can move out of the guest's path. Drawing on hospitality studies, case law and empirical accounts of automated lodging, the article offers six questions for examining a touchpoint, states what the law requires and shows what hosts can do.
