## Title

**Who hosts the guest? Algorithmic mediation, standing and responsibility in phygital hospitality**

## Abstract (100–200 words)

*177 words by whitespace count; verify against Word's counter before upload. Copied from DRAFT_v7.md.*

Hosts now hand parts of the work of hosting, from pricing to check-in, to algorithmic systems, which can be assembled from services that several firms supply. The hospitality texts we read in full describe what a host owes and name hosting divided among several parties, but none says which party owes the guest what. We argue that the role of host specifies the duty, care for the guest's safety and in what the host undertook, and that holding out, reliance and control bind it to a particular party. When others do the work, what stays with the host depends on the duty, and several parties may hold the duty of care at once. During the stay, we argue, the guest's claim justifies a further duty to keep some timely means by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it, a person algorithmic mediation can move out of the guest's path. The article offers six questions for examining a touchpoint, states what the law already requires, and sets out what hosts can do.

## Statement of Contribution (100–150 words, anonymised; sent to reviewers)

*149 words of answers, counted. It names no author, institution or prior work of the contributors.*

**What is the contribution to hospitality knowledge, theory, policy or practice?** The paper develops an account of what a commercial host owes a guest, and who holds that duty, when the host performs through other firms and through algorithmic systems. Drawing on hospitality studies, the law of innkeepers and the philosophy of special obligations, it argues that the role of host specifies the duty and that holding out, reliance and control bind it to a particular party. Under delegation, what stays with the host depends on the duty, and several parties may share the duty of care. During the stay, the guest's claim justifies a further duty: to keep a timely route by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it. It offers six questions for examining a touchpoint.

**How does the paper offer a multidisciplinary social science perspective?** It reads hospitality studies alongside anthropology, law, moral philosophy and research on algorithmic accountability, asking of each what a host owes the guest it has received.
