## Three notes we owe you

**On the dual-place process.** This was the suggestion that changed the paper rather than
supplementing it. Our triad, as originally drafted, would have run identically for a purely digital
encounter, so nothing in it was distinctively phygital. §4 now argues that the encounter travels
between realms repeatedly within a single stay, that physical moments are constantly turned into
digital records and digital rulings are constantly turned back into physical facts, and — the part
that made the addition load-bearing rather than decorative — that the paper's central image, a chain
running from guest to employee to system to whoever configured the system, has a break in it at
exactly these crossings, where the party who decides comes apart from the party who can be asked.

**On the American Phygital Association.** We looked for a citable APA publication series and could
not establish one: the *Phygital Business Review* carries no ISSN, no DOIs and no indexing, and no
proceedings volume exists for the Paris summits. Rather than cite an outlet a referee cannot
retrieve, we have cited the summit-linked special-issue literature in *Journal of Macromarketing* and
*Journal of Services Marketing*, which is where that research reaches print. If we have misread what
you had in mind, we would welcome a pointer and will add it gladly.

**On foundational *Hospitality & Society* work.** Lynch, Germann Molz, McIntosh, Lugosi and Lashley's
2011 founding statement now opens §2's hospitality-theory discussion, so the paper runs from the
journal's opening proposition rather than arriving at hospitality theory from outside it. Bulley
(2015) qualifies Derrida in these pages, since host authority is exercised continuously through the
stay rather than spent at the threshold, which is what licenses a paper about mediation after
check-in. Riordan (2024) and Germann Molz (2026) frame the paper's positioning in §1 directly: the
most recent of the two describes 'guests without hosts', and we ask the next question, what happens
to the obligation once the host can be erased. Weaver (2025) supplies the paper's concession that
this journal is not uncritical of the smoothness it studies.

## On length

The body, §1 through §9, runs to approximately 8,990 words against the ruling that only the body
counts toward the journal's 6,000–9,000 range; the reference list beyond it holds 125 entries.
Whether the Notes for Contributors intend references, notes and front matter to count toward that
range as well is a question we would rather have answered than assume — if they do, the total with
the reference list, abstract, keywords and biographies will run over 9,000 and something will need
to come out. We have kept the body inside the range on either reading.

## What the paper does not claim

We should flag one boundary, because the roadmap invites a reading we do not intend. Section 8 states
that its examples illustrate the distinctions rather than test them, and that every relationship the
paper proposes between the design conditions and guest or employee outcomes still awaits evidence.
The roadmap inherits that limit: it sequences a diagnosis the framework licenses, not an intervention
we have tested. We have written it so that each stage derives from the paper's own analysis, and so
that its central claim — that the later stages cannot be discharged by procurement alone — follows
from the competence-and-standing distinction rather than from consulting practice.
