## Three notes we owe you

**On the dual-place process.** This was the suggestion that changed the paper rather than
supplementing it. Our triad, as drafted, would have run identically for a purely digital encounter,
so nothing in it was distinctively phygital. Section 3 now argues that the encounter travels between
realms repeatedly within a single stay, that digitalization turns the guest's conduct into record
while physicalization turns the intermediary's determinations into physical arrangement, and — the
part that made the addition load-bearing rather than decorative — that the withholdings the paper
identifies occur precisely at the crossings. A guest cannot ask the desk why the application decided
what it decided. That sentence exists because of your letter.

**On the American Phygital Association.** We looked for a citable APA publication series and could
not establish one: the *Phygital Business Review* carries no ISSN, no DOIs and no indexing, and no
proceedings volume exists for the Paris summits. Rather than cite an outlet a referee cannot
retrieve, we have cited the summit-linked special-issue literature in *Journal of Macromarketing* and
*Journal of Services Marketing*, which is where that research reaches print. If we have misread what
you had in mind, we would welcome a pointer and will add it gladly.

**On foundational *Hospitality & Society* work.** The draft you have not yet seen already cited Lynch
et al. (2021) in both parts, Lugosi, Lashley and Hemmington. We have added the 2011 founding
statement so that §2 runs from the journal's opening proposition to its ten-year reprise, which
seemed the most useful form the suggestion could take.

## On length

The article runs to approximately 6,500 words, with a reference list of 124 entries beyond it.
Whether the Notes count that list against the 6,000–9,000 range decides whether we sit comfortably
inside it or a little above, and we would rather be told than guess. Accommodating seven additions
required removing material, and we took it from redundant second citations and from corroborating
evidence rather than from the argument. Should you prefer any of the additions developed further, we
can find the room by trimming elsewhere.

## What the paper does not claim

We should flag one boundary, because the roadmap invites a reading we do not intend. Section 8 states
that the relationships we propose between the five design features and guest and employee outcomes
are theorized and await measurement, and the roadmap inherits that limit: it sequences a diagnosis
the framework licenses, not an intervention we have tested. We have written it so that each phase
derives from the paper's own analysis, and so that its central claim — that the sequence cannot be
discharged by procurement — follows from the competence-and-standing distinction rather than from
consulting practice.
