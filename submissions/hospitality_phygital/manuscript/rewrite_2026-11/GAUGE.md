# v5 against the journal's model

## Verdict

v5 has the argument and almost none of the journal's furniture around it. Three departures matter, in this order:

1. **v5 grounds the host's obligation in law and analytic philosophy and passes over the hospitality canon.** A reader from this journal will look for Derrida, Lashley's domains, Telfer and the journal's own articles, and will not find them.
2. **v5 delivers its contribution as a management apparatus**: three conditions, a six-question test, five principles, five stages, four tables. No article in the sample carries its argument that way.
3. **v5 is planned in nine numbered parts with a 'Discussion' that lists contributions.** The journal's articles run to five or six parts, name them for the argument, and put everything after the argument into one Conclusion.

Each can be changed without changing a claim. The second has to be handled with care, because the editor asked for the roadmap and two reviewers asked for the test to be explained and bounded.

## What v5 lacks

**1. The hospitality canon.** v5 never cites Derrida, Lashley's three domains, Telfer, Selwyn, Bell or Aramberri. Four of the six sample articles start from Derrida or Lashley. The founding editorial names Aramberri's 'service providers' and 'customers' as the rival view, and v5 answers that view without naming anyone who holds it. The claims v5 needs are already in the journal:

- the host 'had an obligation not only to provide a place for and assure the safety of their guests, but also to assure their comfort' (Varley et al. 2018, on King 1995);
- hospitality 'entailed a sacred obligation … to protect the stranger' (Lynch et al. 2011);
- several agencies can 'perform the role of an assembled host' (Bulley 2015), which is our several-firm host in the journal's own words;
- 'micro-hosts' and 'macro-hosts' (Lynch et al. 2011, citing Robinson and Lynch 2007), which is our employee and organization;
- a guest's sense that a complaint will be acted on is 'latent' power (Moysidou and Stanley 2023).

**2. The journal's conversation about technology.** v5 cites Germann Molz (2026) and Weaver (2025) from their abstracts, and dropped Riordan (2024) and Bulley (2015). The special-issue editors summarize Germann Molz (2026) as showing 'the erasure of the host'. Our claim differs: the work moved and the obligation stayed. v5 does not say so.

**3. A roadmap** at the end of the introduction (5 of 6).

**4. Things happening to people.** v5's concrete moments are few and brief: a key card that fails, a door code that fails at night, a guest without a test report. The sample's case sections run on such detail.

## Register

- **What already fits:** 'we' at 5.9 per 1,000 words; plain verbs; key terms held; quotations with page numbers.
- **Self-grading survives in places.** 'the framework is stronger for saying which ones do' (§6); 'a result that sounds backwards at first' (§5); 'can then be stated without circularity' (§3.1). The sample has no such sentences, and this is the habit Reviewer 3 objected to in v4.
- **Scholars are mostly in parentheses.** v5 names the scholar as subject where it leans on law ('Bogen calls', 'Morgan states'), less so for hospitality writers.
- **Paragraphs are even.** 39 paragraphs between 118 and 275 words, mean 211. The sample's means run 130 to 200 with more spread.
- **Figures of speech carried from v4** sit in §4: the line that 'bends', the system 'dropped in between', the 'relay'. They are conventional enough for this journal. Whether they stay is decided against the model article.

## The reviewers' points, read against the model

The 43 points fall into four groups, and the model explains three of them.

| Group | Points | What the model says |
|---|---|---|
| The paper does not speak the field's language | R2.4, R3.8, R3.10, R3.11, R1.2 | Ground the obligation in the canon first; let the law be the evidence for the paper's own move |
| The apparatus is asserted, not introduced | R1.3, R1.4, R1.5, R3.12, R3.13, R3.14 | The journal's articles introduce each construct in prose, with its origin, and say at the end of each part what it showed |
| The paper does not say what it achieved | R3.15–R3.21 | One Conclusion that opens 'In this article we have argued …' and holds limits, research and practice |
| The theory itself | R2.1–R2.3, R2.5, R3.9 | Not a matter of form. v5's §§2–3 and §6 answer these, and v6 keeps every claim |

Style (R3.1–R3.7, R1.6) is the fourth group; the sentence-level method addresses it directly.

## What v6 must do

1. Open the argument inside hospitality studies and move to the law from there.
2. Say how the paper differs from 'the erasure of the host'.
3. Keep every claim of §§2–3, in fewer and named parts.
4. Show the split and the test through cases across formats, at more length than v5 gives them.
5. Keep the boundary typology and the roadmap; turn the rest of the apparatus into prose.
6. Close in one Conclusion: what was argued, limits, three studies, what organizations can do, well-being.
7. End the introduction on a roadmap.
8. Stay under 9,000 words; the sample's range is 6,950 to 9,490.

Items 1 and 2 need sources v5 does not yet hold at full text. That is the job of the research step.
