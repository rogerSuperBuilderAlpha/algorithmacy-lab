# How a conceptual article in *Hospitality & Society* is built

## What this rests on

Six regular articles read end to end (Bulley 2015; Kesimoğlu 2015; Germann Molz 2018; Varley et al. 2018; Kekstaite 2022; Moysidou and Stanley 2023) and two editorials (Lynch et al. 2011; Tzanelli and Germann Molz 2026). Four of the six articles are conceptual; two report interview data and are used for framing, opening and closing only. Only Germann Molz (2018) was read as typeset. The texts still missing are in `CORPUS.md`; the most important are Lugosi (2014) and Germann Molz (2026).

## What the journal says it wants

The founding editorial states it (`notes/lynch2011.md`):

- theory before application;
- prose a reader from another discipline can follow;
- hospitality treated as a lens on power and social relations, against the managerial definition of food, drink and a bed.

## The plan of an article

| Part | What it does | Weight across the six |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | Opens on a figure, a scene, a phenomenon or a gap. States the aim ('This article seeks to …'). States the thesis in the first person. Ends on a roadmap | 7–18% of the body; 530–1,280 words; 3–7 paragraphs |
| **Grounding** | Defines hospitality and the paper's key terms from the field's own canon, with scholars named as agents. Ends by naming what the canon leaves out | 12–30% in one section; up to 50% when spread over two or three (Varley; Kesimoğlu) |
| **The paper's own move** | A second body of theory is brought in and the extension is made: 'beyond Derrida', a reconceptualization, a lens | Usually inside the grounding or the case, not a section of its own |
| **Case or application** | The extension shown in a setting. Material is borrowed: other people's fieldwork, company documents, trade reports. Subsections appear only here, one per parallel case | 22–57%; the longest part in five of six |
| **Conclusion** | 'In this article I have argued …'. Then reach, limits, research, and sometimes practice, all in prose | 4–13%; 280–1,090 words; 2–9 paragraphs |

Body length runs from 6,950 to 9,490 words.

## What all six do, and what most do

| Trait | Count | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| An aim sentence with the article as subject | 6 of 6 | 'This article seeks to' (Bulley); 'The article asks' (Varley) |
| One closing section, headed 'Conclusion' or 'Conclusions' | 6 of 6 | |
| The conclusion says what the article did, in the past tense | 6 of 6 | 'In this article we have confronted' (Varley) |
| The paper's limits are stated somewhere, in a sentence or two | 6 of 6 | In the method section (Germann Molz; Kekstaite), or in the conclusion (the other four) |
| No tables | 6 of 6 | |
| Scholars under discussion are the subject of the sentence | 6 of 6 | 'Derrida holds back'; 'Lashley's (2000) work distinguishes' |
| The introduction ends on a section roadmap | 5 of 6 | Not Varley |
| The thesis and the main moves are in the first person | 5 of 6 | 'I argue', 'we argue', 'we reject'. Not Kesimoğlu |
| Headings unnumbered | 4 of 6 as read | The one typeset text is unnumbered; two manuscripts are numbered |
| Future research named in the conclusion | 3 of 6 | Germann Molz gives four directions in prose |
| Rhetorical questions in the body | 3 of 6 | In a block at a turn (Bulley), or singly (Germann Molz) |
| A figure that draws the paper's scheme | 2 of 6 | Varley (one); Moysidou and Stanley (two) |
| Practical implications in the conclusion | 2 of 6 | Varley; Moysidou and Stanley, who announce them as such |

**What none does:** a section headed 'Discussion', 'Limitations', 'Contributions' or 'Implications' on its own; a numbered list of contributions; propositions; a test or checklist; a table that carries the argument.

## Paragraphs

Mean length differs by author: about 130 words (Germann Molz), 180 (Varley), 185 (Kesimoğlu), 200 (Bulley). The two editorials measured for the structure check ran 153 and 163. So the band is **130 to 200**, and v5's mean of 211 sits just above its top. The earlier memo set v5 against the editorials alone and overstated the gap.

## Voice and register

- **First person carries the claim.** 'I argue that' and 'we argue that' are the ordinary way to state a thesis: seven such moves in Bulley and nine in Varley et al., by the count in the structure check. An inclusive 'we' brings the reader along.
- **Other scholars are praised and disputed by name.** 'beautifully outlined by Derrida'; 'I disagree with Gideon Baker's claim' (Bulley). First names and trades are given: 'geographer Eugene McCann'.
- **Key terms are held fixed.** No article varies its central term for elegance.
- **Verbs may reach a little; metaphors stay conventional.** 'tame', 'ward off', 'stitches', 'bolster'. Idiom is tolerated in a single-author essay ('geographers were at it again').
- **No author grades their own argument.** Idiom describes the world. Nothing in the sample calls its own answer obvious, boring or sharp. This is the line Reviewer 3 saw v4 cross.
- **Hedges attach to the author's own claims**: 'perhaps', 'arguably', 'tentative', 'my wager'.
- **Relation words open many sentences**: However, Yet, Thus, Furthermore, Indeed, Nonetheless.
- **Restatement is signalled**: 'In other words', 'This is to say', 'To summarise'.
- **Signposts recur inside the body**: 'as noted earlier', 'the next section explores'.

## What a reviewer from this community expects to find

1. **The canon, used.** Derrida on conditional and unconditional hospitality; Lashley's three domains; Telfer on the host's motive; Selwyn; Bell; Lynch et al. (2011). Four of the six articles start from Derrida or Lashley.
2. **The journal's own conversation.** Germann Molz (2018) builds on Lynch et al. (2011) and Bell (2012). Kekstaite (2022) builds on Bulley (2015). Articles answer earlier articles here.
3. **A stand against the service view**, named: Aramberri's (2001) 'service providers' and 'customers' is the rival the editors themselves set up.
4. **Power, treated critically.** Every article in the sample is about who controls whom.
5. **Concrete things.** Headcounts in fenced lots; a pencil drawing of a cat; mugs of tea in a tent.

## Rulings for v6

Each replaces or confirms a rule in `REGISTER.md`, which was measured in August on two texts that are not regular articles in this journal.

| # | Ruling | Basis | Effect on the August rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | End the introduction on a roadmap | 5 of 6 | Reverses 'no roadmap paragraph' |
| 2 | State the thesis and the main moves with 'we argue' | 5 of 6 | Reverses 'spend the first person once' |
| 3 | Name the scholar as subject when the paper leans on or disputes that scholar; parentheses for background | 6 of 6 | Narrows 'parenthetical by default' |
| 4 | One 'Conclusion' holding summary, limits, research and practice; no 'Discussion' heading | 6 of 6 | New |
| 5 | Headings unnumbered, two levels, named for the argument | Typeset practice | New. The response letter will cite sections by name |
| 6 | No table carries the argument. A figure may draw the scheme | 6 of 6; 2 of 6 | New. See `GAUGE.md` for the tables reviewers and the editor asked for |
| 7 | Paragraph mean between 130 and 200 words, with spread | Four articles | Lowers the 150–250 band |
| 8 | No sentence grades the paper's own argument | 6 of 6 | New |
| 9 | Questions: at most one or two, where the model article uses them | 3 of 6 | Keeps the August rule in spirit |

Rulings 1 to 3 depart from `REGISTER.md` and ruling 2 from the house rule that first person marks the author's labour and not the claim. The journal is nearly unanimous on each, so I am following the journal and recording the departure in `DECISIONS.md` (D4).

## Version 1.1 (10 Nov 2026): a seventh regular article

Bialski (2012), 'Technologies of hospitality', read in full in its typeset version after v6 was first assembled (`notes/bialski2012.md`). It confirms the plan and corrects one statement.

- **Confirms:** a roadmap closes the introduction ('This article first outlines … The discussion section explains …'); 'I argue' carries the thesis; headings are unnumbered and set in small capitals; no tables.
- **Corrects:** the claim above that none of the sample has a section headed 'Discussion'. Bialski, an ethnography, has one. Among the seven regular articles, then, a 'Discussion' heading appears in one empirical study, and a 'Findings and discussion' heading in another (Moysidou and Stanley 2023); none of the four conceptual articles has either, and nor does Kekstaite's (2022) interview study. Ruling 4 stands for v6, which is conceptual.
- **Adds to the journal's conversation:** platform design as an instrument of the host's control (profiles give hosts 'the power to choose', p. 257; a response-rate rule disciplines hosts, p. 253). v6's introduction now cites it.

## Version 1.2 (10 Nov 2026): a short conceptual article

Baah et al. (2023), read in full in its accepted manuscript (`notes/baah2023.md`). At about 2,700 words it is a short piece, and it does three things the full-length conceptual articles do not: numbered headings, a figure of its model, and a section headed 'Implications'. Its introduction ends on a one-line pointer rather than a roadmap. The rulings above are for a full-length conceptual article and stand for v6; the statement that no article in the sample has an 'Implications' heading now holds for the full-length articles only.
