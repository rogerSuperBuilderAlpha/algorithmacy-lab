# Baah, Filep, Lin and Badu-Baiden (2023), 'Introduction of generosity into commercial hospitality: Conceptual foundations'

*Hospitality & Society* 13:2. Short conceptual article, four authors. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript, Griffith Research Online (about 2,700 words of body).

## What it asks and argues

- **Aim:** 'this paper aims to contribute to preliminary understanding of generosity in commercial hospitality contexts' (MS p. 2).
- **Thesis:** generosity, part of hospitality's history, can be built into commercial hospitality through a 'generosity economy', and it raises the well-being of givers and receivers.
- **Shape:** a concept carried from another literature into commercial hospitality, then drawn as a model.

## Skeleton

Six numbered sections: 1 Introduction; 2 Generosity economy; 3 Well-being as an outcome; 4 A model of generosity in commercial hospitality (with Figure 1); 5 Implications; 6 Conclusion (with limitations). No roadmap: the introduction ends on one sentence naming the next section.

## What it shows about the journal

- A short conceptual piece can carry numbered headings, a figure of its model and an 'Implications' section. The long conceptual articles in the sample do none of these, so the model's rulings stand for a full-length article such as ours.
- Like Varley et al. (2018), it reports King (1995) for the classical host's duty: hosts 'had the duty of welcoming and protecting strangers from robbery, untoward weather conditions, harmful animals and even assassination' (MS p. 1).
- It treats 'managers and employees through whom services are delivered' as hosts in commercial hospitality (MS p. 3), a looser use of 'host' than v6's role holder.

## Register

'The authors' and 'this paper' as agents; one 'We established'. Plain verbs: aims, examine, emphasises, recommends, highlights, suggests, explore. No metaphor.
