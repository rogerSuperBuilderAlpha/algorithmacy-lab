# Petie, Phillimore and Allsopp (2025), 'Exploring hospitality, hostility and (un)home in hotel accommodation for asylum seekers in the UK'

*Hospitality & Society* 15:1–2, 23–44, doi 10.1386/hosp_00088_1. Regular article, three authors. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript under CC BY, University of Birmingham repository (body about 7,200 words, Introduction to Conclusions).

## What it asks and argues

- **Question:** 'how do asylum seekers experience hospitality and home within UK hotel accommodation?' (MS p. 2).
- **Contribution, in the first person:** 'Our original contribution is a new conceptual framework' with four pillars: type of accommodation, food, the host–guest relationship, and safety (MS p. 2).
- **Thesis:** in the two cases, (in)hospitality on each pillar produced (un)home, and hosting arrangements that no one managed well bred fear in hosts and guests alike.
- **Shape:** two literatures (hospitality; home) brought together as a framework, drawn as a figure, then applied as a 'heuristic device' to two cases built from documents.

## Skeleton

Introduction (309 words, 4%); Background (9%); Hospitality and hotel accommodation, with two subsections (10%); Home and hotel accommodation, with three (14%); Hospitality and home in the context of hotel accommodation, where the framework and Figure 1 sit (8%); Methodology (8%); Case studies, two, each with the same five subheadings (38%); Conclusions (9%). Headings unnumbered in the manuscript. One figure, no tables.

- **Introduction** ends on a full roadmap ('The paper begins by … We then … We move to … Our conclusion considers …').
- **Conclusions** says what the article did in the past tense ('This article has brought together …'), then states the framework's uses for scholars, policymakers and providers, then three directions for research. No 'Discussion', 'Limitations' or 'Implications' heading.
- **Methodology** is a section because the cases rest on a documented search: 33 news reports, inquiry reports and NGO blogs, coded deductively against the four pillars.

## What it shows about the journal

- **The plan holds** for a regular article with cases: roadmap at the end of the introduction, first person for the contribution, unnumbered headings, one closing section, cases taking the largest share.
- **A figure may carry a framework.** Figure 1 draws the four pillars of (in)hospitality around the domains of (un)home. That makes three regular articles in the sample with a figure of the scheme (Varley et al.; Moysidou and Stanley; Petie et al.), and still none with a table that carries the argument.
- **The journal's own conversation on the commercial host.** The article treats hotels as hosts and draws on Lashley (2000, 2008) and Telfer (2000) for what hosting includes, among it responsibility for the guest's welfare (MS pp. 3, 12).

## What it says that bears on our paper

This is the nearest work in the journal to v6's assembled host, and the title-and-abstract screen did not flag it (`research/hs_screen.tsv` codes it 0 on all three families).

- **Hosting divided by outsourcing.** Under the Home Office's contracts, accommodation providers acquire hotels and run them with subcontracted housing companies; catering, cleaning and security go to further subcontractors (MS p. 3).
- **Several hosts, one guest.** Asylum seekers are guests of the state and, inside the hotels, also of the hotel, its staff, the contractors and the community, while 'it is the Home Office who are paying customers' (MS p. 4).
- **No clear answer to who is responsible.** In the Glasgow hotels during the pandemic, outsourcing made what the authors call a web of hosts; the reports they read found it unclear what each agency was responsible for, residents did not know where to turn for help, and the receptionist they often turned to first lacked the skills and knowledge to handle complex problems (MS p. 11, citing the Scottish inquiry of 2022 and the Home Office evaluation of 2020).
- **Responsibility placed on management, not the desk.** The inquiry panel treated hotel staff as victims of management's decisions rather than individually responsible (MS p. 11).
- **Missed signals across a divided host.** One resident's queries were each handled appropriately but together should have been a warning; services did not communicate (MS p. 12).

The article does not ask where the host's obligation sits once hosting is divided; its question is the guest's experience of hospitality and home. It shows the division and its cost in a setting with no algorithm in it.

## Register

'We' and 'our' about 30 times; the article and the framework as agents ('This article responds', 'Our framework is useful'). Plain verbs: unite, apply, explore, examine, illustrate, demonstrate. Scholars as subjects ('Telfer (2000) argues', 'Baxter and Brickell (2015) define'). One long block quotation from a journalist. No self-grading beyond 'original contribution'.
