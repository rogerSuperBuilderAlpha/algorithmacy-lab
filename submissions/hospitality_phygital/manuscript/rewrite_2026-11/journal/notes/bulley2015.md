# Bulley (2015), 'Ethics, power and space: International hospitality beyond Derrida'

*Hospitality & Society* 5:2–3, pp. 185–201. Regular article, single author. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript, Queen's University Belfast portal (18 pages of text). The version of record is not yet read.

## What it asks and argues

- **Aim, stated once, in the second paragraph.** 'This article seeks to reaffirm the importance of hospitality … but also to suggest a way in which we can push beyond the limitations imposed by Derrida' (p. 2).
- **Thesis.** 'I argue that if we are to retain Derrida's aim … we must pay attention to the ways that his assumptions are being disrupted' (p. 2). It is restated twice more before the first heading (p. 3).
- **Shape of the thesis: with a named theorist, then beyond him.** Grant what the canonical account gets right; name its limit (hospitality tied to the threshold and to sovereign mastery); extend it in two steps; show the extension in a case.
- **The body's driving question arrives mid-paper**, as a run of five questions: 'But what becomes of the power of hospitality once this threshold is crossed?' (p. 10).

- 37 paragraphs in about 7,520 words of body: roughly **200 words a paragraph**.
- Two heading levels, unnumbered. Subsections appear only in the case section.
- No tables or figures. Two displayed quotations. Eight notes, two of them carrying argument (n. 2 against Baker; n. 7 on Foucault).

## Introduction, move by move

1. **¶1: the canonical figure and the gap in him.** Opens on Derrida as 'perhaps the concept's foremost theorist' (p. 2), quotes his best-known claim, then turns: the claim 'is not really unpacked' and its link to sovereign mastery 'is potentially very limiting' (p. 2).
2. **¶2: aim, argument in brief, thesis.** 'This article seeks to …', three sentences compressing the argument, then 'I argue that …'.
3. **¶3: roadmap by section, then the thesis again.** 'The article begins by exploring … The second section outlines … The third section examines …' (pp. 2–3). It ends 'As such, I argue that a micro-ethico-politics of hospitality must become the centre of our analysis' (p. 3).

## How it handles other people's work

- **The theorist under discussion is the grammatical subject**: 'Derrida warns us', 'Derrida holds back', 'Derrida shows some awareness'. Background literature sits in trailing parentheses, often as a cluster after 'e.g.'.
- **Scholars are introduced by first name and sometimes by trade**: 'the structural linguist, Émile Benveniste' (p. 8), 'Doreen Massey', 'Liisa Malkki', 'Jennifer Hyndman'.
- **Disagreement is personal and named**: 'I disagree with Gideon Baker's … claim' (p. 6).
- **Praise is ordinary**: 'beautifully outlined by Derrida' (p. 9), 'elegantly illustrated by Gideon Baker' (p. 9), 'for an excellent analysis, see Nyers' (n. 5).

## How it builds theory

- **Definition by a necessary feature**: 'Hospitality is necessarily a spatial practice because it involves the crossing of borders and thresholds' (p. 5).
- **Definition by five things it is not**, each a one-sentence case: a sandwich offered in the street, a fight broken up in a park, food aid, a rights campaign, a military intervention (p. 6).
- **A question the author puts to himself**: 'But what is a "home"?' (p. 7).
- **Etymology** (Benveniste on *hosti-pet*, p. 8).
- **A second theorist's vocabulary imported whole**: Foucault's sovereignty, discipline and governmentality (p. 14).

## Cases and evidence

- No fieldwork of his own. The case rests on two ethnographies (Hyndman 2000; Agier 2011) and on UNHCR figures, each given with source and page.
- The case comes last and takes about a third of the article.
- Concrete detail carries it: headcounts in fenced lots, 'situation reports', video shops, land the first arrivals farmed and then would not give up (pp. 15–17).

## Conclusion, move by move

1. The theorist's value, then his limit, again.
2. 'In this article I have argued that …', with the two findings numbered 'firstly … secondly' in the sentence. It names two routes it did not take (Levinas; Esposito). It ends on a quotation from Foucault.
3. A guard against misreading: 'There is a definite danger … of romanticizing refugee camps' (p. 18). Then the normative claim as a bet: 'my wager is that "better" forms would …'. The last words concede: 'halting, temporary and all-too reversible'.

There is no heading or sentence labelled limitations, implications or future research.

## Register

- **Verbs (thirty):** warns, rebels, unpacked, links, seeks, reaffirm, push beyond, interrogate, reveal, policed, managed, retain, disrupted, outlines, examines, restrict, heeding, overlook, favouring, holds back, negotiate, tame, ward off, delimit, filtering, channelling, cordoning off, traces, exposes, venture. Several reach a little (tame, ward off, cordon off, venture); none is developed into a figure.
- **Metaphors:** few and conventional: a 'web of paradoxes' (p. 2), spaces that 'take on a life of their own' (p. 17), 'my wager'. No extended metaphor, no anecdote, no joke.
- **Key terms are held fixed.** 'Sovereign mastery' recurs unchanged about ten times. He coins by hyphen: 'micro-ethico-politics', 'ethico-spatial', 'being-at-home-with-oneself'.
- **First person carries the claim.** 'I argue', 'I would suggest', 'I want to argue', 'I disagree', 'For me', 'my wager': about 4 per 1,000 words. An inclusive 'we' enlists the reader about 9 times per 1,000: 'we must pay attention', 'we can perhaps begin to see'.
- **Hedges are attached to the author's own moves**: 'perhaps', 'arguably', 'potentially', 'tentative'.

## Constructions

- **Relation words open many sentences**: Yet, Thus, Furthermore, However, Indeed, As such.
- **Rhetorical questions are used, in a block**, to turn the argument (five in one paragraph, p. 10).
- **Chains of paired opposites**: 'as private rather than public, as individual rather than communal, as home rather than away' (pp. 5–6).
- **Signposts inside the body**: 'as we have seen', 'described earlier', 'To take one example', 'in a first step "beyond" Derrida'.
- **What it never does:** numbered or bulleted lists, tables, a list of contributions, a managerial implication, a formal displayed definition.
