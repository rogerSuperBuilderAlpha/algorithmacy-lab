# Varley, Farkić and Carnicelli (2018), 'Hospitality in wild places'

*Hospitality & Society* 8:2, pp. 137–157. Regular article, three authors. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript (Greenwich repository copy). This is the sample's closest match to our paper in kind: co-authored, conceptual, no data of its own, about commercial hosts.

## What it asks and argues

- **Question, stated at the end of the introduction.** 'The article asks the broad question of how the sense of temporary belonging and comfort might be achieved on guided adventure tours' (§1 ¶5).
- **Thesis.** Outdoor guides are hosts, and three concepts explain what they deliver: 'we argue that the concept of hospitality is an appropriate point of departure for scholarly explorations of guiding practices' (§1 ¶5).
- **Shape of the thesis: an overlooked setting, read through three borrowed concepts.** Dwelling (Heidegger), communitas (Turner) and hygge (Danish usage) are defined one at a time and then joined in a single figure, a 'conceptual triumvirate' (§4, last ¶).

- 42 paragraphs in about 7,650 words of body: roughly **180 words a paragraph**.
- One heading level, numbered in the manuscript. One figure (three linked terms). No tables. Nine displayed quotations. One note.
- **No section roadmap.** Signposts are local: 'To summarise,' (§2 ¶6); 'Following this, the next section explores …' (§4, last ¶).

## Introduction, move by move

1. **The gap, in the first sentence.** 'Hospitality research has thus far paid scant attention to adventure tourism and outdoor leisure' (§1 ¶1).
2. **The phenomenon**, from industry definitions and reports.
3. **Their own earlier concept**, cited by name, then narrowed: 'In this analysis, however, we focus on one of these … comfort' (§1 ¶3).
4. **Two 'we argue' sentences** that state the thesis before the review begins.
5. **Gap, question and argument together** in the last paragraph: 'This article therefore confronts the gap …', 'The article asks …', 'we argue that …'.

## How it handles other people's work

- **A dedicated section defines hospitality from the field's own canon.** Telfer (2000), King (1995), Selwyn (2000) and Lashley's (2000) three domains appear, mostly with the scholar as subject: 'Lashley's (2000) work distinguishes between three … domains' (§2 ¶4).
- **It states the commercial host's duty in one sentence, on King's authority**: 'Historically, the host had an obligation not only to provide a place for and assure the safety of their guests, but also to assure their comfort (King 1995)' (§2 ¶2). This is the claim our paper builds on, already made in the journal.
- **It names the commercial motive plainly**: commercial hospitality 'is aimed at extracting surplus monetary value for the service provider' (§2 ¶3, citing Ashness and Lashley 1995).
- **It meets an objection in the first person**: 'We acknowledge this potential criticism, but also view …' (§4 ¶1). Later: 'This is of course a somewhat utopic hope' (§4 ¶4).

## How it builds theory

- **Each concept gets an origin**: who coined it, or where the word comes from. Hygge 'emanates from the Old Norse'; comfort 'is derived from the medieval French' (§4 ¶6; §5 ¶2).
- **The concepts are joined in one figure** and one sentence, not in a table.
- **Evidence is borrowed and vivid**: trade-body reports, a market analysis, and a displayed anecdote from a popular book. The authors' own images do the rest: 'steaming mugs of tea shared in a tent, as the storm rages and the flysheet flaps' (§5 ¶6).

## Conclusion, move by move

1. **What is still missing**, then the paper's answer as two principles: 'must follow two critical principles: firstly … and secondly …' (§6 ¶1).
2. **'In this article we have confronted the gap …'** (§6 ¶2), then a paragraph of recap.
3. **Wider reach**: 'Beyond outdoor adventure tourism, hygge may offer …' (§6 ¶4).
4. **Research and limits in one paragraph**: 'Tourism and hospitality scholars have much to gain from closer investigations …'; 'Further possible criticisms of these conceptual forms may stem from issues of group size and trip duration' (§6 ¶5).
5. **Practice, last**: what a bar, a hotel reception or a menu could do differently, in concrete props. The article ends on two fragments: 'The warm embrace of people and place. People in place.'

Limits, future research and practical implications all sit inside the one closing section, in prose, with no sub-headings.

## Register

- **'We' carries the argument**: 'we argue' about seven times, plus 'we suggest', 'we claim', 'We acknowledge' and, twice, 'opine'. The abstract is in the first person too: 'We contribute … We argue … we suggest'.
- **Verbs (thirty):** contribute, reconsidering, encompass, overlooked, shifted, rooted, stems, render, bolster, lurking, assuaging, confronts, appraise, augments, mask, hovering, distinguishes, blur, nudging, encapsulate, meld, coined, accentuate, jar, emanates, summon, harking, hunkered, conspire, expunged. More figurative than Bulley's.
- **Metaphor is allowed, and sometimes extended**: 'a lowering cloud of concern' (§1 ¶4); 'thicken the rich satisfying broth that is hygge' (§6 ¶4).
- **Colloquial touches are few and marked**: a 'bunch of "tickets"', glossed at once (§1 ¶4); 'props if you will' (§5 ¶7).
- **Key terms are held fixed** and italicized as foreign words.

## Constructions

- **Relation words at the head of sentences**: However, Thus, Therefore, Likewise, Similarly, Nonetheless, Furthermore, Indeed.
- **'For example,' introduces most evidence.**
- **No rhetorical questions.**
- **What it never does:** tables, propositions, a contributions list, separate limitations or implications sections.
