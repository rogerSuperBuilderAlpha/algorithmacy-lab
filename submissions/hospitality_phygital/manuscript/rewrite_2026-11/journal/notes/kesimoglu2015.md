# Kesimoğlu (2015), 'A reconceptualization of gastronomy as relational and reflexive'

*Hospitality & Society* 5:1, pp. 71–91. Regular article, single author. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript, City Research Online (the repository notes 'some (mostly formatting) differences' from the published paper).

## What it asks and argues

- **Aim, stated three times in the introduction.** 'The main objective in this article is to illustrate the value in incorporating food's symbolic and cultural value' (p. 4). 'this mostly exploratory article aims to present a reconceptualization of gastronomy' (p. 4).
- **Thesis.** Gastronomy is 'relational, reflexive and negotiable – not fixed' (abstract, p. 2). Restated at the hinge of the paper: 'What is proposed here is to see gastronomy in this same manner' (p. 10).
- **Shape of the thesis: a managerial literature corrected by a sociological theory.** Review the applied literature; name what it leaves out; bring in a theory from outside (Beck and Giddens on reflexivity; Hobsbawm and Ranger on invented traditions); show it in two cases.

## Skeleton

| Section | Paragraphs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 5 | The phenomenon; the paper's interest; aim; roadmap |
| Gastronomic tourism | 7 | Literature review ending on a named gap |
| Reflexivity, consumption and identity | 10 | The imported theory; ends on the proposal and a preview of the cases |
| A reconceptualization of gastronomy in the case of British cuisine | 3 | Case one, from one published study |
| A reflexive look at gastronomy in Turkey | 12 | Case two, from sources and the author's fieldwork |
| Conclusion | 2 | Status of the paper; restatement; a closing quotation |

- 39 paragraphs in about 7,160 words of body: roughly **185 words a paragraph**.
- One heading level, unnumbered. No tables or figures. Six displayed quotations.
- This is the most conventional plan in the sample: review, gap, theory, cases, conclusion.

## Introduction, move by move

1. **The phenomenon and its spread**, with a long cluster of citations: 'It is without a doubt that gastronomy … has become a ubiquitous theme' (p. 3).
2. **The paper's interest**: 'This article is interested in the spillovers of these debates' (p. 3).
3. **Aim**, then a concession about what the literature has already done.
4. **Status and roadmap**: 'the article is divided into six parts including this introduction and a conclusion. The next section is … This is followed by … before concluding the article' (p. 4).

## How it handles other people's work

- **The review runs on parenthetical clusters**, six to eight sources to a sentence.
- **A scholar becomes the subject only when the paper leans on him**: 'Warde manages to present gastronomy relationally' (p. 11); 'Beck applies the same logic' (p. 8).
- **The gap is announced as a gap**: 'Ultimately, a gap is perceived in the literature' (p. 6).
- **Judgement comes through sentence adverbs**: 'Unfortunately' (p. 13), 'Lamentably' (p. 6), and 'Ultimately' at the head of five paragraphs.

## Cases and evidence

- Case one retells a single published study. Case two draws on journalism, culinary history and 'in-depth interviews' from fieldwork that the paper mentions and does not report (p. 15).
- A two-item numbered list sits inside a sentence: '(1) Those who identify … (2) Those who find them vulgar' (p. 14).

## Conclusion, move by move

1. **It opens on the paper's status**: 'mostly conceptual and thought-provoking, but not necessarily conclusive' (p. 15).
2. It restates the aim and the argument, then says what the two cases showed.
3. It ends on a displayed quotation from two other scholars (pp. 15–16).

There is no list of future research; the introduction says only that gaps are pointed out along the way.

## Register

- **No first person.** 'This article' is the agent throughout: 'This article proposes', 'This article has already positioned itself against' (p. 7). Many sentences have no agent at all: 'a gap is perceived', 'What is suggested here'.
- **Verbs (thirty):** focuses, proposes, draws on, mirrored, emphasizes, posed, elevate, advocating, refine, overcome, adheres, illustrate, entails, trace, highlight, deciphering, invoked, normalize, reinforce, neglecting, underline, favour, imposes, operationalize, stems, instigated, intensifies, dissolves, attests, critiques. Plain and abstract.
- **Metaphors:** almost none ('spillovers'; cooking shows as 'gems of media channels', p. 3).
- **Key terms are held fixed**: 'reflexive', 'relational', 'situated'.

## What this text shows about the journal

- A paper can be published here with a wholly impersonal voice. It is the exception in the sample, and the prose is the weakest for it: the reader often cannot tell who holds a view.
- A review-and-gap section and an explicit roadmap are acceptable.
- A conclusion can be short (381 words) and can open by conceding that the paper is exploratory.
