# Lugosi (2011), 'From food, work and organization to the study of hospitality and organization'

*Hospitality & Society* 1:1, pp. 85–89. Review essay, single author. **Read in large part**: repository copy (Bournemouth eprints), about 2,300 words. A short piece, read for what an editor of the journal says about hospitality in organizations.

## What it does

It re-reads a 2008 special issue of *Human Relations* on food and work and shows, article by article, that each is about hospitality without saying so. Aim in the first paragraph: 'I seek to demonstrate how studies of work and organizations can benefit by drawing on hospitality studies' (¶1).

## Passages that bear on our argument

- **Hospitality can run between a person and an organization.** Transactions 'mobilize asymmetric hospitalities (Lugosi 2009), where relationships are not between individuals who give and receive, but between individuals and broader, more ambiguous, entities – in this case the organization' (¶2).
- **Organizational hospitality is conditional.** 'transactions within organizational contexts are always conditional and mobilize reciprocity' (¶3, after Derrida and Dufourmantelle 2000 and Lashley et al. 2007).
- **Shelter is basic.** 'The offer of shelter is fundamental to the principles of hospitality' (¶6).
- **A working definition.** Hospitality as 'interactive activities involving transactions … of food, drink, intoxicants … offers of safety and engaging and entertaining social intercourse' (¶2, drawing on Lashley et al. 2007 and Lugosi 2008).

## Register

- First person singular for intent: 'My intention is not to undermine …; rather, I seek to demonstrate'.
- The reviewed author is the subject of most sentences: 'Driver … draws on', 'Parker … demonstrates'.
- Plain verbs: draws on, subjected, explores, argues, highlights, stress, facilitates, connects, examines, reflects, enables.
- 'Firstly … Secondly …' organizes two-part points.
