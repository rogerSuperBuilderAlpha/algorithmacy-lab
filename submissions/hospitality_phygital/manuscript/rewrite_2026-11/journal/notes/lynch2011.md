# Lynch, Germann Molz, McIntosh, Lugosi and Lashley (2011), 'Theorizing hospitality'

*Hospitality & Society* 1:1, pp. 3–24. Founding editorial, five authors. **Read in full**: repository copy (Bournemouth eprints). The journal's most-cited text. It is not a model for an article's form. It is the journal's own statement of what it wants, and a map of the literature a submission is expected to know.

## What the journal says it wants

- **Theory first.** 'Our intention is to balance theory and applications; however, it is ultimately concerned with developing theoretical perspectives on hospitality and hospitableness' ('Journal ambitions', ¶1).
- **A style for outsiders.** The journal aims 'for articles written in a style accessible for a multidisciplinary audience' (same ¶).
- **Against the managerial definition.** Hospitality defined as food, drink and accommodation 'reduces hospitality to an economic activity, just as it reduces the interactions between hosts and guests to commercial exchanges' (Introduction, ¶3).
- **Hospitality as a lens on society.** 'we see hospitality as both a condition and an effect of social relations, spatial configurations and power structures' ('An agenda', ¶2). Germann Molz (2018) quotes this sentence.

## Passages that bear on our argument

**The host's obligation**
- In the historical traditions, 'hospitality entailed a sacred obligation not just to accommodate the guest, but to protect the stranger who arrived at the door' (Introduction, ¶2, citing O'Gorman 2007 and Still 2006).
- The word's roots carry 'sacrifice, army, power, obligation, reciprocity and protection' (Introduction, ¶4, citing Benveniste 1973 and O'Gorman 2007).
- Managing strangers 'locates the act of hospitality within social and cultural discourses regarding duties, obligations and moral virtues involving two key participants: the host and the guest' ('Social control', ¶1, citing Telfer 2000).

**The rival view, already named in the journal**
- Aramberri (2001) says the host should 'get lost': people in commercial tourism are 'service providers' and 'customers' ('Social control', ¶2). The editors answer: 'Nevertheless, hospitality remains a powerful term for describing social arrangements amongst strangers both within and beyond the commercial realm.'

**More than one host**
- 'the hospitality relationship often involves micro-hosts … and macro-hosts such as the train company' ('Exchange', ¶3, citing Robinson and Lynch 2007). This is the journal's own vocabulary for the employee and the organization behind the employee.

**The guest's constrained will**
- An 'exchange paradox': the guest 'is enticed to consume hospitality of their own free will but the highly regulated experience may cause a loss of free will' ('Exchange', ¶3, citing Robinson and Lynch 2007).
- The guest, 'while symbolically elevated, is subject to domination by the host, and to the rules of being a guest' (same ¶, citing Sheringham and Daruwalla 2007).

**Technology**
- The editors apply hospitality to machines through Ciborra (1999, 2004): 'technology can dominate the host'; 'humans and technologies can become hostages of each other' ('Social control', ¶7).
- The agenda asks for exactly our topic: 'surprisingly little research has applied a hospitality perspective to these emerging phenomena', and doing so for 'human–machine interactions' lets us 'ask important questions about belonging, exclusion, power and identity' ('An agenda', bullet 'Hospitality and virtuality').

## Register

- **Editorial 'we'**, about 8 per 1,000 words: 'we have been struck', 'We invite readers', 'we have also found … particularly useful', 'We welcome'.
- **Verbs (thirty):** motivated, struck, stems, infuse, fill, invite, share, reviews, propose, articulate, foster, frame, hark back, entailed, traced, marking, reduces, constrains, unlock, direct, permeated, depicted, converts, moors, smothered, conceives, transcends, enticed, imposes, mobilize.
- **Metaphor is declared and worked**: the editorial as an 'open door'; hospitality 'operates on a knife edge'; metaphors as 'vehicles'.
- **Scholars are subjects**: 'Bell employs', 'Ciborra acknowledges', 'Dikeç suggests', 'Telfer associates'.
- **A run of five questions** sets the agenda (Introduction, ¶4).
- **Pairs of opposites are listed at length**: 'stranger/friend, inclusion/exclusion, welcome/non-welcome' and seven more ('Social control', ¶1).

## What this text shows about the journal

- A paper on algorithmic hosting answers a call the founding editors made in 2011. The introduction of v6 can say so and cite it.
- Reviewers from this community will expect the paper to place itself against the 'service provider and customer' view, and to know Lashley's three domains, Telfer on motive, and Derrida on conditional and unconditional hospitality.
