# Bialski (2012), 'Technologies of hospitality: How planned encounters develop between strangers'

*Hospitality & Society* 1:3, pp. 245–260 (the typeset footer reads 2011; Crossref dates the issue 16 Feb 2012). Regular article, single author, ethnography with a survey. **Read in full: version of record** (University of St Gallen repository copy of the typeset proof). The journal's second-most-cited article on technology (93 citations in OpenAlex).

## What it asks and argues

- **Term coined in the introduction:** tools that let strangers plan encounters are 'what I term "technologies of hospitality"' (p. 246).
- **Thesis:** 'I argue that interaction among strangers who use these websites can be enriching … yet can also be problematic, fostering moments of awkwardness, misunderstanding, distrust and the abuse of power' (p. 247).
- **Shape:** a new setting read through the canon's account of host power (Derrida; Germann Molz and Gibson 2007), with an empirical typology of stages.

## Skeleton

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| (Introduction, no heading) | Epigraph from a respondent; the term; thesis; roadmap ('This article first outlines … The discussion section explains …', p. 247) |
| Technologies of hospitality: Methodological approach | Five years of participant observation; twenty interviews; a survey of about 3,000 members |
| Opening the doors of hospitality | Theory: host power over space (Simmel; Derrida); home and authenticity; ends on four questions |
| Setting boundaries: Accommodations and reciprocities | Findings |
| Setting boundaries: Selective hospitality | Findings |
| A new familiarization process | Findings: three stages (Introduction, Insight, Embedding) |
| Discussion | 'Fantasies of control'; reciprocity |
| Conclusion | What was shown; reach; further research |

- About 7,800 words of body. One heading level in the typeset text, set in small capitals; unnumbered.
- **A 'Discussion' heading.** The one article in the sample with a section headed 'Discussion' alone, and it is an empirical study. The conceptual articles have none.
- No tables. One bulleted list in the introduction, one in the method.

## Passages that bear on our argument

- **The platform's design gives the host the power to choose.** 'The online profile offers hosts the power to choose one member over the other' (p. 257); the profile helps a host 'discern if the given person will be someone they want to interact with' (p. 257).
- **The platform's rules discipline the host.** The site 'calculates a user's response rate', and a low rate 'bumps' a host to the bottom of the search list, so a host must answer every request, even those refused (p. 253).
- **Without formal rules, hosts make their own.** 'Without formal rules to regulate the host–guest encounter, hosts often abide by their own set of rules of safety' (p. 253); one host turned a guest out at 11 at night (p. 253).
- **Mobile hosts are less accountable.** 'Mobile hosts are less accountable to any one given sociocultural setting or social network, and can create their own set of rules of hospitality' (p. 250).
- **The canon's laws of hospitality, quoted.** Laws that 'mark the limits, powers, rights and duties of the host-guest relationship' (p. 250, citing Derrida in Westmoreland 2008).

## Register

- First person singular for labour and for the claim: 'I term', 'I argue', 'It is my argument that'.
- Verbs (twenty): collide, interact, coordinated, enable, blur, foster, exert, renegotiate, outlines, explores, surface, engage, elicited, traced, adopt, accommodate, compromise, judge, reject, discern.
- Many respondents quoted at length in display blocks; first names given.
- 'Undoubtedly' twice in the closing paragraph; 'interestingly enough' once.

## What this text shows about the journal

- The roadmap at the end of the introduction is the norm in empirical articles too.
- A 'Discussion' section is acceptable in an ethnography; the conceptual articles keep to one Conclusion.
- The journal's first technology issue already framed platform design as an instrument of the host's control.
