# Tzanelli and Germann Molz (2026), 'Life in the age of extremes: Reconsidering biopolitical imaginaries in hospitality and tourism'

*Hospitality & Society* 16, pp. 3–12. Special-issue introduction, two authors. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript, White Rose repository (CC BY). The most recent text in the sample. Its register is dense theory and is not a model for ours. I read it for what the journal's current editors say about digital hospitality.

## Skeleton

Two parts under two headings: a theoretical statement of about twelve paragraphs, then 'The special issue at a glance', which summarizes four articles and closes on a question. About 3,200 words of body. No roadmap, no tables.

## What it says about the article nearest to ours

Germann Molz (2026), 'Guests without hosts', appears in the same issue. The editors summarize it as follows. This is their summary; I have not yet read the article itself.

- Her analysis shows 'how digital biopolitical governance operates through the erasure of the host' (p. 10).
- 'she argues that in a world of absent hosts, technologies sterilise tourism vacationing, turning living places into spaces of play for strangers' (p. 10).
- The erasure can 'gesture toward mutual and collaborative hospitalities' as well as toward capitalist logics (p. 10).

So the journal's latest word on platforms is that the host has been erased. Our paper's claim is narrower and different: the work has moved, the obligation has not, and what is missing is someone the guest can reach. v6 has to state that difference once the article itself is read.

## Other points of contact

- Hospitality is 'both an absolute norm' (Derrida's unwritten law 'of providing safety and nourishment to strangers') 'and a context-based practice' (pp. 7–8).
- Power is described as moving 'from a sovereign's power to determine who is or is not welcome to a more unevenly and arbitrarily distributed power' exercised by drivers, conductors and border agents (p. 9, on Minca's article).

## Register

- 'We' for editorial moves: 'We endeavour to look past', 'We invited them', 'We leave readers to ponder'.
- Abstract nouns in long chains; many coinages in quotation marks; Greek terms glossed in brackets.
- Verbs (twenty): haunted, governs, reduce, grant, scaled, decipher, equips, flooded, diagnoses, discarding, subsume, entangle, inflected, suppressed, cultivated, codified, partake, geolocate, sabotaging, peregrinate.
