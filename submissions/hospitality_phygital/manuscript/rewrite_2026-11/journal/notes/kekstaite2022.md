# Kekstaite (2022), 'Beyond Derrida: Fragments of feminist hospitality in residents hosting illegalized migrants in Belgium'

*Hospitality & Society* 12 (DOI 10.1386/hosp_00049_1). Regular article, single author. **Read in full**: accepted manuscript, Ghent University repository (13 pages of text). It is an interview study, so I use it for how a recent article frames theory, introduces itself and closes, not for its middle.

## What it asks and argues

- **Aim, at the end of the introduction.** 'this paper responds to Berg and Fiddian-Qasmiyeh's … call to "transcend and resist the fatalistic invocations of hos(ti)pitality"' (p. 3).
- **Thesis.** 'I argue that their discourses and practices of hospitality – while being limited and imperfect – constitute alternative spaces of inclusion' (abstract).
- **Shape of the thesis: the same as Bulley's.** Grant the canonical account (Derrida), name where it 'falls short' (p. 3), and add a second body of theory (feminist ethics of care) as a complement. The title repeats Bulley's phrase, and the article defines hospitality in his words three times (pp. 5, 7, 12).

- The conclusion runs to about 330 words and names no limitations and no agenda; both were dealt with in the methods section (p. 7).

## Introduction: the roadmap

The last paragraph walks the whole paper in the first person: 'In the following pages, I analyse how … To convey the argument, I first outline … Then, I summarise … The first part of the analysis elaborates … The second part moves on to … while the third part looks at … Lastly, I end with some concluding thoughts' (p. 3).

## How it handles other people's work

- **The literature is sorted into camps**: 'Some scholars deem such practices as apolitical … While other researchers … insist of painting a more nuanced picture' (p. 2).
- **The received theorist is the subject**: 'Derrida insists that violence is always inherent in every act of hospitality' (p. 4).
- **The complement is organized by count**: 'The discussion circumvents two limitations of Derrida's framework. First of all … Secondly …' (p. 5).

## Passages that bear on our argument

- Conditional laws of hospitality 'set limits, mark powers, rights, and duties' (pp. 3–4, paraphrasing Derrida and Dufourmantelle 2000: 77).
- The host 'sets rules and limits for hospitality relationship' (p. 7), and the guest 'has agency to appropriate hospitality language and negotiate hospitality relationships' (p. 6, after Rosello 2001).
- A host on doing the state's work: 'we do the work of the state' (p. 12).

## Register

- **First person singular for moves**: 'I analyse', 'I first outline', 'I consciously chose', 'I would like to stress'. An impersonal 'one notes that' carries the readings of the data.
- **Verbs (twenty, from the framing sections):** administer, hastened, curb, magnifying, deem, insist, showcased, posit, transposed, falls short, responds, revisit, outline, summarise, elaborates, fleshed out, cautioning, circumvents, criticised, complement.
- **Metaphors:** few ('painting a more nuanced picture', 'shedding light').

## What this text shows about the journal

- The 'with Derrida, then beyond him' argument is a recognized form in this journal: Bulley (2015) set it, and this article, seven years later, cites and repeats it.
- A full first-person roadmap closes the introduction.
- Limits can be stated where the method is described and left out of the conclusion.
