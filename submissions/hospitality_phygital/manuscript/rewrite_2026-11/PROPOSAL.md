# Proposal for v6

## In one paragraph

v6 keeps v5's argument and rebuilds the paper on the plan of Bulley (2015). It opens inside hospitality studies, with the host's power and the host's obligation as the field already states them. It then takes two steps beyond the field's account: the obligation belongs to a role and not to a motive, and it stays with the role's holder when the work is delegated. It shows the result at the touchpoint, across formats, in one long section. It ends in a single Conclusion. Five sections replace nine; two tables replace four.

## The model: Bulley (2015), 'Ethics, power and space'

**Why this article.**

1. **Same kind of argument.** Bulley grants a canonical account, names its limit, and extends it in two steps before showing the extension in a setting. Our argument has that shape already: the field's account of the host, then role in place of motive, then delegation, then the touchpoint.
2. **Our question is his, turned over.** He asks 'what becomes of the power of hospitality once this threshold is crossed?' We ask what becomes of the host's obligation once the work of hosting is handed on. A reader of this journal will recognize the move; Kekstaite (2022) made it once already.
3. **He supplies a term we need.** The agencies that run a refugee camp 'perform the role of an assembled host'. Our several-firm host is an assembled host.
4. **The right weight.** 7,520 words, five sections, 37 paragraphs, with the case taking 37%. Our material fits those proportions at about 8,400 words.
5. **The right register for Reviewer 3.** Measured, argued in the first person, concrete in its case, with no anecdote and no self-grading.

**Alternatives considered.** Germann Molz (2018) is the only version of record and is about platforms, but its conversational register is the one Reviewer 3 objected to in v4, and its plan (a lens walked across scales) is not ours. Varley et al. (2018) is co-authored and conceptual, but it applies borrowed concepts to a setting and makes no argument against a received view. Lugosi (2014) could not be read.

## What v6 argues

- **Question.** Where does the commercial host's obligation to the guest go when the work of hosting is delegated to algorithmic systems spread across several firms, and what must an arrangement keep for the guest to remain a guest?
- **Thesis.** The obligation stays with whoever took up the role of receiving the guest. What delegation removes is the guest's path to anyone with authority to answer for it.
- **Shape.** With the field's account of the host, then two steps beyond it, then the touchpoint.
- **Moves, in order.**
  1. The field knows the host's power and the host's obligation, and argues about commerce in terms of motive.
  2. First step: the obligation is a role obligation. The law of innkeepers shows what it contains, when it comes into force and who holds it.
  3. Second step: hosts are assembled, the work is delegated, and the duty is not. The guest's standing survives; its exercise needs a reachable party with authority.
  4. The authority–answerability split names what fails. Three conditions and six questions examine a touchpoint; a typology says when a person must be reachable; five stages say what an organization must do.
  5. Conclusion: what was argued, where it sits among neighbouring fields, limits, three studies, practice and well-being.
