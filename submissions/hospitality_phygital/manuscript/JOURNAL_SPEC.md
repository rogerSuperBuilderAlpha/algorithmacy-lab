## What the Notes themselves say, for the record

> "Articles should normally be between 6–9000 words **including notes, references, contributor
> biography, keywords and abstract**."

The journal's web page says "5-8000 words." The Notes for Contributors say 6,000–9,000 **all-in**.
The Notes are the authority and they are more recent. Two consequences:

1. The ceiling is higher than assumed, but it is **inclusive**. A 7,600-word body plus ~1,600 words
   of references, an abstract, keywords, and two contributor biographies lands at roughly 9,600 —
   over the ceiling.
2. The floor is 6,000 all-in, which the paper clears comfortably.

**Ruled body budget: 6,800 words**, reserving the balance as follows.

| component | budget |
|---|---|
| Body (§1 introduction through §9 conclusion) | 6,800 |
| References (~60 entries in Intellect Harvard) | ~1,620 |
| Abstract | 180 |
| Keywords (six) | 12 |
| Contributor biographies (two authors) | 200 |
| AI acknowledgment statement | 50 |
| Endnotes | 50 |
| **Total** | **~8,912** |

That leaves ~90 words of headroom under 9,000. Every reference added costs ~27 words of body.

## Mandatory components most manuscripts do not have

**Statement of Contribution.** A paragraph of 100–150 words answering each of two questions: what the
contribution to hospitality knowledge, theory, policy or practice is, and how the paper offers a
multidisciplinary social science perspective. Sent to reviewers, so it "should exclude any material
that would reveal the identity of the contributor(s)."

**Highlights — mandatory for this journal.** Three to five bullet points conveying the core findings,
maximum 85 characters including spaces per bullet, supplied in a separate file with 'Highlights' in
the file name.

**AI acknowledgment.** See the policy section below. A separate labelled section at the end of the
article, before the References.

## Format rulings

| item | requirement |
|---|---|
| Abstract | 100–200 words |
| Keywords | **six**, one- or two-word phrases |
| Contributor biography | 50–100 words each |
| Language | Standard British English, **'ize' endings** ("organize", not "organise") |
| File | Word, Times New Roman 12 point |
| Title | Bold at the beginning of the file, no quote marks |
| Headings | Bold, Times New Roman 12 point. The Notes say nothing about numbering |
| Notes | **Endnotes, not footnotes**; discouraged generally; note call in superscripted Arabic placed *after* the punctuation |
| Quotations | Single quote marks; double for a quotation inside a quotation; quotations over 40 words displayed as an indented paragraph with no quote marks, with the bracketed reference after the full stop |
| Anonymity | "Strict anonymity is accorded to both contributors and referees" |
| ORCID | Required for each contributor |

## Referencing — Intellect Harvard, and it is not APA

In-text: Harvard embedded in the text, **no comma between author and year, colon before the page**.

> "(Bordwell 1989: 9)"

Reference-list rules stated explicitly in the Notes:

- Year of publication **in brackets**.
- **Commas, not full stops**, between parts of each reference.
- **No 'in'** after a chapter title when the reference is to an article in a journal or newspaper.
- Translator's name in brackets after the title, preceded by 'trans.', not 'transl.' or 'translated by'.
- **No 'no.'** for the journal number — a **colon** between journal volume and number.
- **'pp.'** before page extents.
- 'Anon.' where there is no author.
- The list is titled **'References'** and contains only works cited in text. Anything else goes in a
  second 'Further Reading' list.

Worked example in this journal's form, from an entry the arm already holds:

> Cheng, Mingming and Foley, Carmel (2019), 'Algorithmic management: The case of Airbnb',
> *International Journal of Hospitality Management*, 83, pp. 33–36.

> Lugosi, Peter (2021), 'Exploring the hospitality-tourism nexus: Directions and questions for past
> and future research', *Tourist Studies*, 21:1, pp. 24–35.

Note what this means for the arm: `literature/references.bib` is stored in a BibTeX-ish APA shape and
**must be rendered into Intellect Harvard by hand** for the manuscript. The DOIs that were verified
stay in the `.bib` as provenance; the journal's examples do not carry DOIs except where the source is
online-only.

## AI policy — read this before drafting

> "Generative artificial intelligence (AI) and AI-assisted technologies should only be used to
> enhance readability and language, with human oversight and careful review to correct any errors or
> biases. **AI should not be considered or used as an author or co-author.** Authors must disclose
> their use of AI in a new section in their article(s), taking full responsibility for the content.
> Basic grammar and spelling tools do not require disclosure."

The required statement goes in a section headed 'Acknowledgment of the Use of Generative AI and
AI-Assisted Technologies in the Writing Process', at the end of the article and before the
References, in this form:

> "Throughout the preparation of this work, the author(s) employed [NAME OF TOOL / SERVICE] to
> [PURPOSE]. Following the utilization of this tool/service, the author(s) thoroughly reviewed and
> edited the content as necessary, assuming full responsibility for the publication's content."

The journal also points to its own AI page and to COPE's position statement on authorship and AI
tools.

**This bears directly on how the manuscript gets written.** The policy permits AI for readability and
language with human oversight, and requires disclosure of any use beyond grammar and spelling. It
does not contemplate an AI drafting the article. Whatever process the authors choose, the
acknowledgment section must describe it accurately — the disclosure is the authors' statement, and it
has to be true.
