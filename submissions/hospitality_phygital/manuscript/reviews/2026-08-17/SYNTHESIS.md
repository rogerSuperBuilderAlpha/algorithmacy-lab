## The spine: §3's duty paragraph

Three reviewers arrived at one paragraph from three literatures. It carries the paper's thesis and
it is the weakest writing in the manuscript.

The paragraph now reads that care is owed for need, fiduciary duty for trust, governance duty for
risk, and that 'a welcome is owed simply because someone has arrived, and it is the only one of
these duties that defines the role of the party who owes it.'

- **Furtado: the uniqueness claim is false, and the counterexample is one clause earlier.** Fiduciary
  duty is the standard case of an obligation constituting its bearer's role. Care and governance
  survive the test; fiduciary does not. Her repair moves the claim to an axis that works — welcome is
  the only duty triggered by bare presence, and the only one whose *ownership*, as distinct from its
  performance, cannot be delegated without transferring the role.
- **Ellery: 'owed simply because someone has arrived' imports unconditional hospitality into a
  commercial setting.** No hotel owes a welcome to everyone who arrives; Lashley's commercial domain
  is defined by that condition. Her repair is not a hedge but the tradition's own distinction:
  conditional at admission, unconditional in reception. That produces the sharpest available
  statement of the paper's thesis — algorithmic scoring reopens the condition on the inside of the
  door and keeps recomputing it.
- **Reviewer 2: §1's promise is discharged by assertion.** The paper asks whether a system that takes
  the functions inherits the obligations and answers no without argument. He drafted the missing
  argument from a source already in the paper: a system cannot stand in a forum, so the duty does not
  transfer.

The three repairs compose into one rebuilt paragraph. Furtado's axis fixes the logic, Ellery's
distinction fixes the hospitality theory, and Reviewer 2's Bovens move supplies the argument §1
promised. Ellery's insertion also retires two smaller findings: Bulley is currently cited for the
threshold-moment account he wrote his article to argue *against*, and Derrida currently decorates.

**Related and cheap: Kropf is the paper's sharpest adversary and is cited as a friend** (Furtado). If
care robots can hold role responsibility from social function without consciousness, a check-in
system can hold role responsibility for welcome and the thesis fails. Kropf's own conclusion —
'without displacing human accountability' — answers it, and using it rescues the thesis from reading
as true by definition.

## Correctness findings — fix regardless

1. **§2 cites a housekeeping study for a front-desk claim, and §3 contradicts it** (Reinholt). Spektor
   et al. 2023 studies unionized guest room attendants and housekeeping supervisors; its 'room
   assignment' allocates rooms to housekeepers to clean, and the front desk appears as the party
   *sending* assignments. §3 gets this right — 'the clearest documentation comes from back-of-house
   work… Front-desk work appears different.' The paper contradicts itself forty lines apart. **This
   is the finding that costs the most credibility.**
2. **Zhou is misdescribed, and the correction strengthens the paper** (Reinholt). 'Gathering evidence
   in order to appeal' is an interview exemplar from the scale-development table, not a validated
   item. The three surviving items are self-help, peer help, and using in-app reporting and appeal
   functions — so remediating, as validated, is mostly workers routing *around* the institution,
   which illustrates the competence/standing point better than the current version.
3. **'The first validated scale of algorithmic competency'** is refutable by Dogruel et al. 2022,
   already in the reference list. Four words.
4. **§4's decision rule contradicts its own illustration** (Reviewer 2). 'Substitutive when all three
   conditions are withheld at once' rules out the transparent algorithm that still substitutes, where
   only accountability fails — the paper's most quoted sentence. Make accountability necessary and
   demote the other two, which is what the rest of the manuscript already argues.
5. **The silence chain measures no competence** (Reinholt). Cheng measures AI career-threat awareness,
   Lin exposure to four functions, Duggan qualitative enforced silence — all cited for a claim about
   understanding. Lin is also cited for half its result: algorithmic feedback *raises* voice.
6. **Padigar et al. is 2025**, 42:1, pp. 21–43, not 2024 (Sarkisyan).
7. **§2's 'no instrument measures conduct flowing in that direction'** is refuted by Yi and Gong 2013
   on customer value co-creation behaviour (Reviewer 2). Narrow to the commercial host relation.
8. **§7's 'it has not yet been examined as one'** is refuted by the paper's own Phillips et al.
   (Sarkisyan). Scope to phygital hospitality. Eight words.
