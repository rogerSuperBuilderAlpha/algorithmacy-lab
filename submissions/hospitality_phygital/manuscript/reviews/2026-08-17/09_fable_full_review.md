## 1. The verdict

**Publishable in this journal as it stands, and likely to survive referees with minor revisions.**
The argument is sound end to end, the one claim that makes the paper — a system that takes over the
host's functions does not inherit the host's obligations — is now argued rather than asserted, and
the prose is good: plainer and cooler than the venue's own average, but controlled, concrete, and
free of the imitation gloss that sinks most register-matched drafts. I checked several suspected
faults against the models and the models cleared them; those checks are reported below rather than
suppressed, because a review that manufactures faults on strong writing is useless.

**The single thing most worth fixing:** the paper names its two constructs — hospitality
algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty — in Section 5 and never uses either again. Sections 6,
8 and 9 revert to "competence" and "standing." The abstract promises the constructs; the body
mentions them once and drops them. A referee will read this as branding, and the fix costs one
sentence in §6 and a clause in §8 (drafted in Section 5 below). Second priority: the spine
paragraph of §3, where the panel's three repairs were welded in with a dangling referent and four
argumentative moves now share one 366-word paragraph.

One mechanical catch the earlier passes missed: **Padigar et al. is cited in-text as 2025 (§7) but
the reference entry still reads 2024.** The panel's Tier-1 correction reached the body and not the
list; this is currently the only citation in the manuscript that does not resolve.

## 2. The reasoning, section by section

**§1 — holds.** The chain is clean: hospitality is relational, not just service (Lashley, Lynch et
al.); the field's names for technology — "tools, mediators, facilitators, co-creators, or forms of
social presence" — "describe what technology is capable of doing, but they do not identify what, if
anything, technology owes the guest"; employee and host are distinct roles; so when systems take
the host's functions, the inheritance question opens. The differentiation from Germann Molz and
Riordan is exact and generous. The one weak joint is the last sentence: "the resulting gap has
important consequences for how phygital hospitality should be understood and designed" — the
paper's highest-stakes sentence ends on its emptiest phrase (see Section 5, item 6).

**§2 — holds, with one paragraph whose work for the thesis is indirect.** The standing claim (a
guest "can ask questions, decline an offer, request an explanation, challenge a decision, or ask to
be treated as an exception") is the right foundation and is well cited. The counterparty paragraph
is precise: the guest "can be admitted as a customer while having nobody with whom she can
negotiate as a guest." The platform-accommodation paragraph earns the panel's protection. The
strain: the Manfreda/Shi paragraph documents that nobody asks what the *guest owes*, but the
paper's question is what is owed *to* the guest. The paragraph does support the section's claim
that obligation flows both ways in hospitality and one way in service — but that link is left for
the reader to draw, and its closing absence claim ("Neither asks what, if anything, a guest owes a
commercial host") is never used again. Not a break; a referee may ask what it is for.

**§3 — the spine holds; the welding shows.** Three joints, in order:

- *The duty-sources move has a dangling referent.* "Neither asks which party, once the rules have
  done the redistributing, still owes the guest a welcome. Each of these responsibilities has a
  different source." — "these responsibilities" points backward at nothing; care, fiduciary and
  governance duties are introduced only in the clause that follows. This is the seam where the
  panel's rebuilt paragraph was inserted. One clause repairs it (Section 5, item 1).
- *The conditional/unconditional paragraph is the best reasoning in the paper.* "No hotel owes a
  welcome to everyone who arrives... Those conditions govern admission rather than reception. Once
  the guest is inside, the house is not supposed to keep recalculating the terms of her welcome
  against what she is worth to it." The qualification that could have killed the thesis is turned
  into its sharpest statement. Protect this paragraph.
- *The Bovens argument discharges §1's promise but compresses one step.* The paper argues: role
  responsibility (Kropf) is proposed "without displacing human accountability"; accountability is a
  relation to a forum; "A system can perform a welcome. It cannot stand in a forum and answer for
  one." Why it cannot is left to the Bovens definition one sentence earlier — a forum that can
  "question it, judge it, and attach consequences." A referee in the ethics of technology will
  press: systems generate explanations, and firms can decommission a system, so which limb fails?
  The answer the paper implies — judgement and consequence can only land on a party able to revise
  the rule, and that party is never the system — is one sentence away from being on the page
  (Section 5, item 2). The conclusion the argument earns ("it is ownership rather than performance
  that makes the role") is the conclusion drawn. The step from there to §9's positive answer — the
  host is "whoever can be called to account for the terms on which the guest is received" — follows:
  if the obligation cannot transfer to the system, it stays with whoever controls the rules, and §3
  says exactly where control went ("Authority... moves towards whoever designs, configures, or
  controls the rules"). The paper is consistent that the gap is a *separation* of capacity from
  obligation, not a disappearance of the obligation — "the reallocation carries the functions and
  leaves the obligation behind." That is the right way to have it.
- *The Introna paragraph is a one-sentence orphan* (31 words), the only one-sentence paragraph in
  the manuscript; both measured exemplars contain none. It belongs at the end of the Kropf/Bovens
  paragraph, where it lands as a coda to "ownership... makes the role." Zero-cost move.

**§4 — holds after the panel's repair, with two small self-inflicted wounds.**

- *The rule now overshoots in the other direction.* "Mediation becomes substitutive wherever
  [accountability] is withheld, whatever happens to the other two, and remains augmentative where
  it is preserved somewhere within the relationship." The first half is the corrected necessity
  claim. The second half asserts sufficiency — yet the section's own augmentative example requires
  more than accountability ("its rules are reasonably stable *and* an empowered employee can
  question or override its decisions"). A referee can construct the counterexample: an accountable
  but capricious system. The next sentence ("Common understanding and predictability determine how
  much the guest can do with the standing") already contains the repair; the rule just needs "can
  remain augmentative" (Section 5, item 4).
- *The Garcia example is billed under the wrong condition.* "Hotel pricing provides the clearest
  case of one-sided predictability" — but the lesson the paragraph draws is a common-understanding
  failure: "what they lost was any account of the rule they were adjusting." The example is
  excellent; the label is off by one condition (Section 5, item 5).
- *"Two examples show how the questions work"* promises a walk through Table 1 that never happens;
  the examples demonstrate the augment/substitute verdicts instead. Fix the promise, not the
  examples (Section 5, item 8). The closing confirm-versus-score distinction is strong, and the
  mild tension with "at any phygital touchpoint" (the questions "apply... hardly at all to the
  system that merely confirms her") reads naturally as "return trivial answers there" — leave it.

**§5 — the strongest section, and the panel was right to protect it whole.** The Zhou passage is a
model of how to differentiate against a collision: "Every validated item locates the remedy in the
worker, and most of them route around the institution rather than through it." The silence chain is
now scoped honestly ("a proposition this framework offers rather than a finding it reports"). One
real strain: **the definition of coordinative sovereignty is stronger than the paper's own setting
warrants.** "The standing to be answered within a coordination one cannot leave" — a hotel guest
can leave, and §6's fifth feature says exactly that: the alternative exists but "carries a
significant financial, practical, or social penalty." The paper's premises point to *costly* exit,
not *no* exit. As written, the construct's defining condition excludes the paper's own cases
(Section 5, item 3).

**§6 — holds.** The Mosca-against-Mosca opening settles the disclosure disagreement on the merits;
the Choi and Chao move ("Preserving a human face may in fact matter less than preserving human
authority") is the section's best inference; the competence/standing division across the five
features ("Some problems require people to become more capable; others require institutions to
become more answerable") is the right closing. This is also where the unnamed constructs should be
named (Section 5, item 9).

**§7 — holds throughout, and is the best-written section.** The narrowed absence claim survived the
panel's verification; the Star/Ruhleder inversion, the Nguyen scripting argument, and the
remedy-through-the-same-instrument paragraph (Cui, Filippas) each do exactly the work claimed. No
reasoning faults found.

**§8–§9 — the conclusion is the one the argument earns.** §9's answer — the system "performs the
welcome without being able to answer for it"; the host is "whoever can be called to account" — is
licensed by §3 and only by §3, which is why §3's seams matter. One miss in §8: contribution two
restates the three conditions flat ("Common understanding, predictability, and accountability
provide a way of examining...") without the hierarchy §4 argued and the abstract now states. The
discussion is vaguer than the abstract on the paper's own rule (Section 5, item 10). The
limitations paragraph is flat, specific, and free of self-congratulation — right.

**Checks that cleared.** Three faults I went looking for are not there. The "important" drumbeat I
suspected runs at exactly the venue's own rate (2.0/1k; Hemmington 2.0, Lugosi 2021 1.9). Sentence
variance is healthy (SD/mean 0.56 against the models' 0.45–0.55) — this is not uniform punchiness.
And ending paragraphs on an unattributed sentence rather than a citation is what the models do too
(Lugosi 2021: 0% citation-final). The two slop passes did their work; the lexical layer is clean.

**Three real divergences, and what they amount to.**

1. **The draft is the most staccato text of the ten.** Nearly one sentence in four runs twelve
   words or fewer, against 7–15% everywhere in the corpus, and the sentence mean sits 2–7 words
   below every model. The venue's default music is Lugosi's: "The transformation of our conceptions
   has driven academics to extend their contributions beyond the traditional boundaries of
   hospitality (management) scholarship, creating new concepts, exploring new contexts and engaging
   new audiences (Lugosi, 2020)." The draft's is: "A system can perform a welcome. It cannot stand
   in a forum and answer for one." `NOTES.md` records this as deliberate — the second author's
   readability governs — and about half the short-sentence runs earn their snap. The other half are
   scaffold seams ("Table 1 summarizes these questions. Two examples show how the questions work.")
   where the snap is doing nothing.
2. **The draft nearly refuses the first person the venue expects.** Two authorial first persons in
   6,650 words (0.5/1k) against 2.3–9.5/1k in every corpus text that has any. Lynch et al. open
   their journal with "As each of us has explored questions of hospitality from within our
   different areas of research, we have been struck..."; even Lugosi, the community's most reserved
   conceptual writer, runs "our understanding," "our conceptions," "invites us" throughout. The
   draft has no disciplinary *our* at all. `REGISTER.md`'s own warning applies: "A conceptual paper
   in this literature that refuses the first person is not being rigorous. It is being
   conspicuous." The draft sits at the conspicuous edge.
3. **The draft runs almost bare of connectives.** 3.6 sentence-initial connectives per 100
   sentences against 9–30 in every conceptual model; Lugosi 2021 opens nearly a third of his
   sentences with "However," "Moreover," "Arguably," "Importantly," "Specifically." The draft's
   cohesion is carried instead by tail-head linkage and renamed thread entities, which is the
   harder and better craft — but combined with divergences 1 and 2 it produces a temperature
   difference a reader of this journal will feel.

**Where the register ruling and the governing voice disagree.** The task asked for this explicitly.
`REGISTER.md` was measured for `manuscript.md` and the current draft departs from it at five
points: first person expected (rule 1) versus two instances; paragraphs targeted at 150–250 versus
a 129 mean with median 110 — though the *second* measurement's Lugosi figure (129) matches the
draft exactly, so the earlier target was superseded by the ruling's own remeasurement; sentence
means of 26–30 versus ~20–23; no rhetorical questions in running prose (rule 2 of the second
measurement) versus §9's "Who hosts the guest?"; and the reversed citation ruling reserved
narrative citation "for the rare case where a named scholar's argument is itself the object of
discussion" — the draft has three such cases (Kropf, Introna, Bulley) and converts none, running
0:161. On the first three, the governing voice wins by instruction and the result is defensible.
The §9 question should stay: it is the title being answered, it is one sentence, and it lands. The
Kropf citation should convert (Section 5, item 7) — his argument *is* the object of discussion for
four sentences, and the conversion also removes the draft's only agentless-passive opener.

**The judgment.** The prose is good — say it plainly. It does not read as an imitation of the
journal; it reads as a distinct, disciplined voice publishing *in* the journal, plainer and cooler
than the community's norm. That is the right failure mode if it is one at all, because the
imitation alternative — Moreover-drumbeat, "focuses upon," evaluative garlands — is detectable and
this is not. The prose goes slack in exactly three places: the scaffold sentences around the two
tables, the contribution paragraphs of §8 ("provide a way of examining," "This shifts analysis
away from broad questions"), which are the flattest writing in the paper at the moment the paper
states its worth, and the congested §3 spine paragraph. It does its best work in §3's
qualification paragraph, §5's Zhou passage, and all of §7. One warmth adjustment — three or four
disciplinary first persons ("our understanding of what a host owes," "invites us to ask") at the
joints where the paper enlists its field — would close most of the remaining distance to the venue
at a cost of perhaps ten words.

## 4. Passages that work — protect these

1. *"Algorithmic mediation reopens the condition on the inside of the door and holds it open,
   scoring the admitted guest continuously against a standard she cannot see."* (§3) The thesis as
   an image, and the image is doing analytic work: it locates the wrong precisely (inside, after
   admission, continuous) where a lesser sentence would have said "raises concerns." This is the
   sentence the paper should be known by.
2. *"A system can perform a welcome. It cannot stand in a forum and answer for one. The duty can be
   discharged by a machine and cannot be owned by one, and it is ownership rather than performance
   that makes the role."* (§3) The argument's hinge, with the performance/ownership distinction
   compressed into parallel syntax that is carrying content, not cadence.
3. *"An employee who can listen sympathetically but has no authority to change anything becomes a
   place where frustration is deposited rather than a meaningful source of accountability."* (§6)
   The second author's canonical formulation; "a place where frustration is deposited" is concrete,
   ugly in the right way, and unforgettable.
4. *"Algorithmic personalization industrializes the scripting while removing the improvising
   person."* (§7) Eleven words that compress the whole Nguyen argument. The follow-through is
   equally good: *"She loses the pause at the desk during which an experienced employee might once
   have recognized that the standard rule did not fit and made an exception."* The pause at the
   desk is the paper's best concrete anchor.
5. *"A remedy that requires the guest to have been legible all along is not available to the guest
   whose problem is that the system cannot read her."* (§7) The reputational-records critique
   closed in one sentence, and the legible/read pairing makes the circularity visible.
6. *"guests describe the digital route as work they have been assigned rather than service they
   have received"* (§6) — the shadow-work literature rendered in hospitality's own vocabulary.
7. *"A property that stops after improving interfaces and competencies may have automated its
   hospitality without ever deciding who answers for it."* (§8) The practitioner payoff with teeth.
8. The final sentence: *"However sophisticated, personalized, or seamless the technology becomes,
   somebody still owes the guest a welcome."* Earned by everything before it. Do not touch it.

One caution on the collection as a whole: §7 closes three consecutive paragraphs on aphorisms
("...opportunities for voice," "...made an exception," "...in an ordinary way"). Each is good;
three in a row is the one place the draft's verdict-sentence habit shades toward uniformity of
optimization. If one converts to a forward hook, make it the first.

## 5. Passages that do not work — quote, diagnosis, replacement

Replacements are drafted in the second author's voice. Running word cost is tracked; the body sits
roughly 6,450–6,650 against a 6,000–6,800 band, so headroom is real but thin.

1. **§3, the dangling referent.** *"Neither asks which party, once the rules have done the
   redistributing, still owes the guest a welcome. Each of these responsibilities has a different
   source. Care is owed because someone is in need..."* — "these responsibilities" has no
   antecedent; the trio arrives only afterwards. Replace the middle sentence:
   > "Neither asks which party, once the rules have done the redistributing, still owes the guest a
   > welcome. The duties these literatures do theorize each have a different source: care is owed
   > because someone is in need, fiduciary duties are owed because someone has been trusted, and
   > governance duties exist because a system creates risks (Kropf et al. 2026; Santoni de Sio and
   > Mecacci 2021)."
   Cost: +5 words.
2. **§3, the compressed Bovens step.** After *"It cannot stand in a forum and answer for one,"* the
   inference rests on an unstated premise. Insert one sentence:
   > "An explanation can be generated on demand; judgement and consequence can only land on a party
   > able to revise the rule in response, and that party is never the system itself."
   Cost: +30 words. Funded by cuts in items 6 and 8, or by trimming the §3 knowledge paragraph's
   citation apparatus. This is the only addition I would defend at full price: it is the paper's
   central inference, and referee 2's earlier finding ("discharged by assertion") shows what
   happens when this step is left implicit.
3. **§5, the overreaching definition.** *"Coordinative sovereignty is the standing to be answered
   within a coordination one cannot leave, and it is what an institution must confer."* — the
   paper's own guests can leave; §6's fifth feature prices the exit rather than denying it.
   Replace:
   > "Coordinative sovereignty is the standing to be answered within a coordination one cannot
   > leave without cost, and it is what an institution must confer."
   Cost: +2 words. If the flagship project's definition requires strict no-exit, then the sentence
   needs the opposite repair — a clause saying hospitality presents the *graded* form of the
   condition — at about +15 words.
4. **§4, the sufficiency overshoot.** *"...and remains augmentative where it is preserved somewhere
   within the relationship."* Replace:
   > "...and can remain augmentative where it is preserved somewhere within the relationship."
   Cost: +1 word. Necessity stays sharp; the false sufficiency goes; the next sentence already
   explains what else augmentation needs.
5. **§4, the mislabelled example.** *"Hotel pricing provides the clearest case of one-sided
   predictability."* The paragraph then argues a common-understanding failure. Replace:
   > "Hotel pricing provides the clearest case of a rule in operation that nobody on the spot could
   > state."
   Cost: +5 words.
6. **§1, the limp closing clause.** *"The central argument is that it does not, and that the
   resulting gap has important consequences for how phygital hospitality should be understood and
   designed."* "Has important consequences" is the emptiest phrase in the paper at its most
   prominent address. Replace:
   > "The central argument is that it does not, and that the gap between performing a welcome and
   > answering for one changes what phygital hospitality should be designed to protect."
   Cost: +3 words, and it seeds "answering for" thirty lines before §3 needs it.
7. **§3, the passive-opener Kropf citation.** *"Care robots have been argued to hold role
   responsibility grounded in the social function they occupy rather than in any consciousness they
   lack (Kropf et al. 2026)."* The only agentless-passive opener in the draft, on the one source
   whose argument is itself the object of discussion — the exact case the reversed citation ruling
   reserved narrative citation for. Replace:
   > "Kropf et al. (2026) argue that care robots hold role responsibility grounded in the social
   > function they occupy rather than in any consciousness they lack."
   Cost: −2 words.
8. **§3, the preceding self-narration.** *"...and the strongest case against a negative answer
   should be stated first."* A triple negation plus a comment on the paper's own organization —
   the one habit `REGISTER.md` retained a zero for. Replace:
   > "Whether the duty travels with the function is the question this paper began with, and the
   > strongest case for inheritance comes from the ethics of care robots."
   Cost: −1 word.
9. **§6, the unnamed constructs (the verdict's headline fix).** *"There is an important division
   between these principles. The first three can largely be addressed through interface design,
   organizational communication, and training. The final two cannot be supplied by individual
   competence, however skilled the guest or employee becomes."* Insert after the third sentence:
   > "The first three, that is, extend hospitality algorithmacy; the final two are matters of
   > coordinative sovereignty, and they require institutional decisions about staffing, authority,
   > review, and responsibility (Martin and Waldman 2022; Alfrink et al. 2023; Hirsbrunner et al.
   > 2025)."
   This replaces the existing "They require institutional decisions..." sentence rather than adding
   to it. Cost: +14 words.
10. **§8, contribution two's missing hierarchy.** *"Common understanding, predictability, and
    accountability provide a way of examining what remains available to guests and employees at
    particular phygital touchpoints."* Replace:
    > "Common understanding, predictability, and accountability provide a way of examining what
    > remains available to guests and employees at particular phygital touchpoints, with
    > accountability as the condition that decides between augmentation and substitution."
    Cost: +12 words. §8 then matches the abstract and §4. Contribution three is also the natural
    place for the constructs' second appearance: "Third, the paper separates competence from
    standing — hospitality algorithmacy from coordinative sovereignty." Cost: +6.
11. **§3, the Introna orphan.** Fold the one-sentence paragraph into the end of the Kropf/Bovens
    paragraph, unchanged. Cost: 0.
12. **§4, the scaffold seam.** *"Table 1 summarizes these questions. Two examples show how the
    questions work."* Replace the second sentence:
    > "Two examples show the distinction at work."
    Cost: −2 words.

Net cost of all twelve: roughly **+73 words**, inside the band from a 6,450–6,650 start. If the
band is tight after the author's own pass, items 2, 9 and 10 are the ones to keep at full price;
items 1, 3–8, 11 and 12 are near-free.

## 6. What a referee at this journal will say

Three bets, in descending confidence:

1. **"You name two constructs and never use them."** The abstract promises hospitality algorithmacy
   and coordinative sovereignty; §5 names them in two sentences; §6, §8 and §9 proceed without
   them. Either the constructs organize the design features and the contributions, or the referee
   asks why they exist. Items 9 and 10 above are the whole repair.
2. **"The argument is about algorithmic mediation; what work does *phygital* do?"** The
   accountability argument would run unchanged for a pure online platform. The paper has its
   answer — the relay paragraph, "the mediation examined here does most of its work after
   admission," the room key, the pause at the desk — but it is distributed rather than stated. A
   referee for a phygital special issue will want one declarative sentence saying what the physical
   half changes: that the guest is *inside the house* when the scoring happens, which is why the
   hospitality tradition and not platform studies owns the question. §3's relay paragraph can carry
   that clause.
3. **"A paper on the host's obligation in this journal engages Derrida in one decorative
   citation."** The admission/reception distinction in §3 *is* the conditional/unconditional
   distinction, unnamed. The critical-hospitality referee will notice that the tradition's central
   pairing is used without attribution while its source sits in the reference list. One sentence in
   §3's qualification paragraph — naming the conditional/unconditional pairing and citing Derrida
   and Dufourmantelle alongside Lashley — closes it for +15 words; the panel's Ellery repair
   pointed here and the draft stopped one step short.
