# Referee report, round two (Reviewer 3)

*Locations: "Host's power" = 'The host's power and the host's obligation'; "Delegation" = 'Delegation and standing'; "Split", "Test", "Reachable" and "Design" = the four subsections of 'Assembled hosts at the touchpoint'. Paragraphs are counted within each, including one-sentence lead-ins, as the letter does.*

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that the commercial host's obligation belongs to a role, not to a host's hospitable motive. A party takes that role up by holding out to travellers, as the innkeeper's common-law duties show. The work of such a role can be handed to others but the duty cannot, so algorithmic mediation leaves the guest's standing intact. What it puts at risk is the guest's ability to use that standing by reaching someone with authority to change a decision. From this the authors derive three things: a six-question test of touchpoints, a table that matches the recourse a guest needs to how much a decision matters and whether it can be undone, and a five-stage plan for organizations.

## 2. Recommendation

**Major revisions.** The paper is now coherent and far better written. However, the step from the host's obligation to a duty to hear the guest is asserted rather than argued, and the test and the roadmap both depend on it. Two of the four cases also go beyond their sources.

## 3. My round-one points

**Style, flourishes and the "boring" answer (R3.1–R3.4). Addressed.** The anecdote, "sitting ducks" and "bite" are gone. The question of whether a machine can be a host is now a reasoned decision about scope: "Whether a machine could itself bear a host's duties we set aside" (Introduction ¶4). The letter's "one question remains" is not accurate: the body keeps "But why was the innkeeper bound at all?" (Host's power ¶9) and a second question at Delegation ¶4. The letter does not respond to my remark about AI assistance.

**"We", evidence and Bitner (R3.5, R3.6, R3.22). Mostly addressed.** "We" now means the authors, Bitner is gone, and nearly every claim carries a page locator. Some case descriptions still go beyond their sources (issue 3).

**Technology treated reductively (R3.7). Addressed.** Introduction ¶1 separates "the technologies and their jobs". One trace remains: Split ¶3 says the path to authority is something "an algorithmic system can lengthen, obscure or remove". It never considers that a system could shorten that path.

**Hosting equated with hotel reception (R3.8). Partly addressed.** Split ¶2 adds short-term rentals and restaurants, and the Bosma material is good. However:
- restaurants rest on a single augmented-reality case (Test ¶4);
- Table 1 lists only lodging decisions;
- the paper's own legal foundation excludes restaurants: "the shopkeeper remained free to refuse" (Host's power ¶5). See issue 4.

**Authority treated as absolute (R3.9). Addressed in substance.** "Authority in commercial hospitality has long been graded and organizational" (Split ¶3) is the correction I hoped for. The definition then drifts (issue 2).

**What guests owe; welcome as one duty (R3.10, R3.11). Addressed** (Host's power ¶10 and ¶6). The guest's duties are never used again, though, not even in Table 1's rows on admission and removal, where they would matter.

**Meandering Parts 6–7 (R3.12). Largely addressed.** The letter says each subsection now opens on its claim. Test ¶1 ("To examine a touchpoint, then, we need to know") and Design ¶1 do not.

**Roadmap, forum and Table 2 (R3.13, R3.14). Partly addressed.**
- **Forum.** "In a hotel the forum is the place where a guest's claim can be lodged" (Design ¶4) describes a complaints channel. Bovens' forum is a body that questions an actor and passes judgement.
- **Which table.** The letter answers R3.14 as though I meant the six questions. I meant the roadmap, which is now Table 2.
- **The roadmap still rests on one paragraph.** Its premise, "the five principles line up as a roadmap of rising cost" (Design ¶4), does not hold. The three interface principles and two institutional principles do not map onto the five stages, and the order of rising cost is asserted, not shown.
- **"Condition it secures" column.** An internal audit is said to secure common understanding, but Test ¶1 defines that condition from the guest's side.

**Contributions, research question and conclusion (R3.15–R3.17, R3.21). Largely addressed.** The aim is stated (Introduction ¶3) and answered (Conclusion ¶2). But the third of the "three things" bundles three separate products (a test, a typology and a roadmap). And the final sentence restates the question rather than the answer: "The question for phygital hospitality is therefore not whether technology can do" (Conclusion ¶5).

**Well-being (R3.18, R3.19). Partly addressed.** The individual-level claim, that a missing path "turns a claim into a private cost, borne alone" (Conclusion ¶5), is asserted without evidence. The community-level claim rests on De Vos et al. (2024), a study of students with disabilities in an online MBA.

**Limitations and agenda (R3.20). Addressed.** Conclusion ¶3–4 are specific. The proposed study that would "follow a guest's request or complaint from the person who receives it" is the right next study.

Elsewhere the letter's claims match the manuscript, and its locators are accurate.

## 4. Style and register now

The prose now reads as scholarship. Some habits, repeated often, still give it a mechanical rhythm:

- **A refrain.** The thesis recurs in the Abstract, Introduction ¶3 ("what it removes is the path from the guest to anyone") and Conclusion ¶2 and ¶5. "The work can be delegated, and the duty cannot" (Delegation ¶4) appears twice more.
- **Contrast pairs.** There are more than a dozen "not X but Y" or "rather than" constructions, often closing a paragraph. One example: "The problem is not the length of the chain but a break in it" (Split ¶4).
- **Numbered inventories.** The paper counts constantly, from "four things that need to be kept apart" (Delegation ¶2) to the "three things" of Conclusion ¶2, with six questions, five stages and four limits in between. It reads as a checklist.
- **Strings of citations.** Some sentences run "X (cite), Y (cite) and Z (cite)" and list sources instead of arguing with them: Introduction ¶2 ("This journal has studied platforms since its first volume"), Test ¶5 and Design ¶2.
- **Aphoristic paragraph endings.** Examples: "To host, on these accounts, is to take the guest into one's charge" (Host's power ¶1); "what an organization must keep for its guests to remain guests" (Introduction ¶4).
- **Uniform rhythm.** Sentences average about 29 words and a fifth exceed 40. Almost the only short sentences are the six questions.
- **"Therefore."** It appears eleven times and is sometimes unearned. "It is useful, therefore, to ask which decisions need a person" (Test ¶5) does not follow from the preceding material on employee silence.
- **Second-hand citation.** "Varley et al. (2018) put the point on the authority of King (1995)" (Host's power ¶1). King is in the reference list and should be cited directly.

## 5. New or remaining issues

1. **The step from obligation to a hearing is asserted, not argued.**
   - Delegation ¶9 concludes "What a guest can expect from the host's obligation is therefore a hearing" from two descriptive cases.
   - The bundle of duties in Host's power ¶6 (receive, sustain, protect, attend) contains no duty to answer. Yet Test ¶1 derives accountability "from the duty to answer through someone with authority".
   - The only duty that could carry this is attending, which is the one "the law did not carry".
   - Historically, "a refused traveller could sue for damages" (Delegation ¶7). On the paper's own evidence, the place to press a claim was a court, not the inn.
   - **Fix:** derive the hearing from the duty to attend, say plainly that the claim is normative rather than legal, and explain why a later outside route is not enough.
   - Test ¶1 also says the guest "has still been hosted by no one if no party is obliged to answer". That contradicts Delegation ¶6, where the obligation persists. Changing "obliged to answer" to "placed to answer" would fix it.

2. **Authority and discretion are defined two ways.** Delegation ¶2 defines authority as power "to change a decision or the rule behind it". Split ¶4 says "Discretion acts on the single case and authority on the rule". On the second definition, Table 1's admission row asks for discretion, not authority. **Fix:** name the two levels (power over the single case, power over the rule) and say which one each row of Table 1 requires.

3. **The test has no decision rule, and the cases go beyond their sources.**
   - The conditions can be "present in degrees" (Test ¶1), yet "the touchpoint passes" (Test ¶4). The paper should state what passing requires.
   - **Pricing case.** In Garcia et al. the algorithm advises managers, who keep the final decision. The study says nothing about front-desk staff or guests, so "no one at the desk could explain the price" is the authors' own gloss.
   - **Check-in case.** Xu et al. examine facial-recognition check-in through security, privacy and trust. "One that weighs the guest's history" is a hypothetical and should be labelled as one.
   - **The payoff.** None of the four cases shows that "a touchpoint can satisfy guests and harm no one" while failing the test, yet Conclusion ¶2 rests its payoff on that claim. **Fix:** Xiang et al.'s guest who arrived without a test report, re-set in an app-only hotel, would supply such a case.

4. **The role account does not reach several of the contexts the paper uses.** A duty owed to paying travellers leaves out:
   - restaurants and bars;
   - placed, non-paying guests, whom critical hospitality studies cares most about. Two examples come from the paper itself: Petie et al.'s asylum seekers (Assembled hosts ¶1) and Bulley's refugee camp, the source of the term "assembled host".

   **Fix:** show how the role reaches these guests, or mark them as outside scope (Reachable ¶4).

5. **The paper says little to this journal's own work.**
   - Germann Molz (2026) and Riordan (2024) are the closest work in *Hospitality & Society*, yet they appear only in a parenthesis (Introduction ¶2).
   - Given that work, and the paper's own use of Bulley and of macro-hosts, the claim that "the debate about commercial hospitality has had no way to say where it goes" (Introduction ¶4) is overdrawn.
   - **Fix:** a paragraph engaging these papers, and the conditional hospitality Bulley writes beyond.

6. **Conclusion ¶2 brings in new material.** The AI Act and "Human-centricity, on this reading, concerns what an arrangement protects or withholds" appear nowhere in the body. **Fix:** move the AI Act to Reachable ¶4, and either develop human-centricity in the body or cut it.

## 6. Minor points

- **Design ¶1:** this rests on a study of fashion and luxury production and a five-page piece (Mosca 2026). Use hospitality evidence.
- **Design ¶2:**
  - Martin and Waldman did not compare their five mechanisms on adverse decisions.
  - Their appeal went to an internal review board, which supports a later appeal more than on-the-spot authority.
- **Test ¶4:** Bosma (2022: 605) reports that Airbnb requires self-check-in for listings to qualify for business travellers. That shows the platform holding authority over the rule, and would strengthen this case.
- **Conclusion ¶3:**
  - Some of the statutes cited are UK or Great Britain law rather than English law.
  - Crinkley applied North Carolina law.
- **Conclusion ¶4:** cite "the studies of unstaffed hotels we found".
- **Host's power ¶10:** reorder "hotels ask guests to leave who will not follow".
- **Table 1:**
  - Define "Mostly" and "Partly".
  - Add a restaurant decision.

## 7. Confidential comments to the editor

This is a genuine and substantial revision, and the letter locates its changes accurately. What remains sits at the centre of the argument and in the evidence for the cases. Of the five sources I spot-checked, three claims go beyond what the sources report: two case descriptions (Garcia et al.; Xu et al.) and the community-level well-being claim (De Vos et al.). The letter does not answer my round-one remark on AI assistance; I draw no inference from the prose and leave disclosure to the journal's policy. If issues 1–3 are met, I expect to recommend acceptance.
