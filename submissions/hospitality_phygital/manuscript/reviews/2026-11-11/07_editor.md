# Panel 07, the special-issue editor: DRAFT_v6, its front matter and its two letters

## Step 0. Register note

I judge the prose against the journal's conceptual articles as the brief describes them. That means a roadmap at the end of the introduction, 'we argue' for the thesis, scholars as the subjects of sentences, unnumbered headings, paragraphs of 130–200 words, and no sentence that grades the paper's own argument. Beneath that I apply `~/.claude/writing-style.md`, which puts cohesion first. As editor I add a standard of my own. The front matter, the body and the two letters must say the same thing, because I read all four and the referees read three of them.

## Part 1. Rigour and argument, read from the editor's chair

### Front matter

**Title.** In August I asked for a shorter title. The September letter declined, and the v6 note declines again, giving one reason: 'The subtitle names the three things the paper turns on' (RESPONSE_TO_EDITOR_v6, note, row 1). v6 does not turn on responsibility. Delegation and standing ¶2 (line 54) defines responsibility as one of four terms. After that, the body uses the word only to report others' accounts or to set its own duty against them ('not a responsibility for harm', line 60). The argument runs on obligation, standing and authority. The subtitle therefore names one of the paper's terms, one of its rivals' terms ('responsibility' is how Kong and Cobbe et al. frame the problem, Conclusion ¶2, line 149) and the setting. Declining an editor's suggestion twice is fine, but the reason given has to hold. A ten-word title would meet the request and name the construct: *Who hosts the guest? The host's obligation in phygital hospitality*. **[CONSIDER]**, 0 body words. The sentence case is correct.

**Abstract (175 words).** It fronts the argument of the first two sections well and the rest of the paper poorly.

- *Sentence 2* (line 12): 'Each places the party that decides at a distance from the guest it affects.' Its antecedent is unclear: 'each' could be a firm or a system, and 'it' could be the party or the decision. Reviewer 1 complained in round one about exactly this kind of 'it' (R1.6). The sentence also repeats the closing clause of the introduction's first paragraph almost word for word (line 18).
- *Sentence 1* repeats the introduction's opening list, 'from admission and pricing to check-in, room allocation and complaints', verbatim (line 18). Abstract and introduction should not open on the same sentence.
- *Standing* is used without a gloss. Readers of this journal do not know Darwall's sense of the word, and the abstract does not supply it.
- *The paper's named constructs are missing.* The authority–answerability split heads the longest section, and interruptibility is one of the six keywords. Neither appears in the abstract.
- *The test is named, not stated* ('It offers a test of phygital touchpoints, scoped by the type of decision'). Reviewer 1 asked for the test to be presented in the abstract (R1.4), and the letter says 'the abstract states the test' (RESPONSE_TO_REVIEWERS_v6, line 41). It does not. (also R1, R3)
- *'Phygital' appears once.* For a special issue on theorizing phygital hospitality, the abstract never says what the paper adds to phygital theory.

The fix appears as rewrite 1 in Part 3. **[FIX]**, abstract only, about +16 words, still under 200.

**Keywords.** Six, each one or two words, as the Notes require. 'Phygital hospitality' and 'algorithmic mediation' repeat the title, which the database already indexes. 'Role obligation' or 'delegation' would add a search term the title lacks. **[CONSIDER]**, 0 words.

**Statement of Contribution.** It says the paper 'shows that algorithmic mediation rarely removes the host's obligation'. A conceptual paper argues, and 'rarely' is a frequency the paper never measures (see the thesis, below). The multidisciplinary answer lists anthropology, law, moral philosophy and accountability research but not phygital or service research. Yet this statement goes to referees for a special issue on phygital hospitality. **[FIX]**, 0 words: change 'shows' to 'argues' and add 'phygital service research' to the second answer.

**Highlights.** Highlight 5 reads 'A decision that cannot be undone in time needs someone with authority on site.' The body never says 'on site' of the authority. It says the guest needs 'a reachable party with authority before it takes effect' (When a person must be reachable ¶1, line 105), and that 'What guests need is not a human presence as such but someone with the power' (Designing ¶2, line 129). In line 131, 'on site' describes the guest. The highlight turns the paper's most careful distinction, reachable authority as opposed to human presence, into the claim the paper argues against. The front-matter note says 'each highlight still matches v6'. This one does not. **[FIX]**: 'Decisions that cannot be undone in time need a reachable person with authority.' (79 characters, counted.) No referee has caught this.

### The thesis says three things

The thesis appears in four places with three different quantifiers:

- Abstract (line 12): mediation removes 'rarely the host's obligation and more often the guest's path'.
- Introduction ¶3 (line 22): it 'rarely removes the host's obligation; what it removes is the path'.
- Conclusion ¶2 (line 149): it 'leaves the obligation in place and takes away, where it takes anything, the guest's path'.
- Statement of Contribution: the paper 'shows' the first version.

The argument supports only the third. If the work can be delegated and the duty cannot (Delegation and standing ¶4, line 58), mediation never removes the obligation; that is the whole point of a non-delegable duty. 'Rarely' concedes cases the paper never describes. 'More often' is a comparative frequency claim in a paper without data, and Reviewer 2, who asked for a theory, will ask how often. Use the conclusion's version everywhere. **[FIX]**, 0 words. (The repetition itself was raised by R3; the drift in quantifier is new.)

## Part 2. Slop audit

The prose is far plainer than the September version. I found no agentless passive openers, no emphasis markers, and none of the phrases Reviewer 3 quoted. What remains reads as mannered in five places, and an editor who has read the round-one reports will look for them.

1. **The roll-call sentence.** Introduction ¶2 (line 20) runs 60 words and lists Bialski, Germann Molz (2018), Germann Molz (2026) and Riordan to show that 'This journal has studied platforms since its first volume'. It reads as proof of having cited the journal, not as an argument that uses it. Two of the four sources are abstract-only. Group them under the claim they support, or let one of them do work.
2. **Grading the field and the paper.** 'Hospitality scholars have been right about what a host owes' (line 52); 'remains the right place to begin an account of phygital hospitality' (line 147, which also uses 'account' twice); and 'The argument catches an arrangement that works exactly as designed and harms no one' (line 149). The last grades the paper's own argument, which the register target forbids. It also repeats a claim that no case shows (also R1, R2, R3, item 3).
3. **'Not X but Y' closers** (also R3). Delegation ¶9 ends 'a hearing by someone with authority, not the exception itself' (line 68); Split ¶4 ends 'not the length of the chain but a break in it' (line 82); Designing ¶2 ends 'not a human presence as such but someone with the power' (line 129); and the paper ends 'not whether technology can do the jobs … but where' (line 155). Each is defensible alone. Together they give four of the paper's paragraph endings the same cadence.
4. **Agents that do not act.** 'Phygital hospitality hands' (line 18), and 'the five principles line up as a roadmap of rising cost' (line 133). Principles do not line up; the authors order them, and Reviewer 3 asked why the order is what it is.
5. **'Therefore', eleven times** (also R3). It does no harm where the inference is real (line 62). At line 38, though ('The welcome is therefore a set of duties'), it marks a stipulation as a conclusion.

I part company with Reviewer 3 on one point. My rough sentence split gives a mean of about 28 words with a standard deviation of about 13, which is not uniform. The monotony lies in the paragraph endings, not the sentence lengths.
