The essay is a conceptually ambitious, legally grounded intervention in hospitality theory. It derives two core host duties (safety and reasonable care) from the institutional role of host, binds them to specific firms via holding-out, reliance, and spatial control, and then extends a further duty of interruptibility during the stay. The argument is largely coherent, the deployment of constructs (standing, phygital relay, knowledge/discretion/authority) is precise, and the author correctly labels the further duty as a normative extension rather than settled law. The main theoretical risk is that the derivation of the further duty rests on a relatively thin empirical base and a brisk application of instrumental normativity. Stylistically the prose is cleaner than typical “AI slop,” but several paragraphs bury the lead, a handful of em-dashes and long descriptive openings remain, and a few sentences can be tightened into more muscular claim-first form.

### 1. Theoretical Rigor & Argument Structure

**Introduction.**  
The contrasting cases (Garcia et al. pricing algorithm versus Batat’s Le Petit Chef) efficiently establish the central tension: algorithmic mediation can separate the party that shapes a decision from the party the guest can reach. The three claims are stated cleanly. One paragraph, however, opens with description rather than the problem:

> “Commercial lodging today routinely runs through several firms at once… When a failure occurs at one of these touchpoints—an unexplained charge, a malfunctioning digital key, an unaddressed safety hazard—each firm can point to another…”

Rewrite to front-load the claim:  
“When a failure occurs at a touchpoint in multi-firm lodging—an unexplained charge, a malfunctioning digital key, or an unaddressed safety hazard—each firm can point to another, leaving the guest without an answerable counterparty. Commercial lodging routinely runs through the property, the brand, the platform, and the vendors.”

No Luddite framing appears. The language of “separation” and “answerable counterparty” is structural rather than polemical.

**Theoretical Context.**  
Batat’s phygital definition and Germann Molz’s “guests without hosts” are used accurately and to purpose. The distinction between standing and neighboring concepts (consumer rights, procedural justice, contestability, customer participation) is one of the paper’s sharper conceptual contributions; it clarifies what algorithmic mediation actually disrupts.  

One paragraph in “The host” leads with the counter-view:

> “At first glance, commercial lodging looks like ordinary customer service…”

Rewrite:  
“Retaining the term host remains essential despite commercial complications, because it identifies who owes the guest care and who must answer when that care fails.”

The legal subsection is rigorous. Blackstone, the Hotel Proprietors Act 1956, Al-Najar, Everett, and Wheat v E Lacon are deployed to establish the three factual conditions. The treatment of delegation (non-delegable duties as exceptional; spatial control surviving outsourcing) is accurate and carefully bounded.

**Approach.**  
Method and limits are transparent. The admission that the further duty is not yet required by law outside specific domains (package travel, platform moderation, disability accommodation) strengthens rather than weakens the piece. The reliance on secondary accounts of Raz and Telfer is noted; a primary citation would be preferable but is not fatal.

**The Parties to a Phygital Arrangement.**  
The party-by-party analysis is systematic and consistent. The core rule—spatial control anchors the duty of care, and multiple parties share it proportionally—is well supported by Wheat v E Lacon and occupiers’ liability doctrine. Distinctions between duties that travel with the work and duties that stay with the host are clear.  

Minor structural issue: several subsections open with historical or descriptive setup. Example from “The property”:

> “Hospitality law begins with the physical house itself. As Sandoval-Strausz notes…”

Rewrite:  
“Physical control over the premises attaches the duty of care to the property, even when outside contractors perform operational tasks.”

The brand, platform, vendor, and state subsections correctly apply the holding-out / control tests and note the insulation of software vendors under product-liability rules. No significant polemics.

**During the Stay.**  
This is the paper’s main theoretical contribution. The move from an underlying claim-right (Raz via Herstein) plus instrumental normativity (Kiesewetter) to a further duty of a timely discovery-and-correction route is conceptually coherent for a theory article. The author correctly distinguishes the duty from Hindriks’s collective duty of control and from commercial service recovery, and correctly limits it to the duration of spatial subjection.  

The derivation can be tightened. Current formulation:

> “A phygital host must therefore preserve a timely channel for discovering and correcting failures during the stay…”

Stronger version:  
“Because algorithmic mediation and outsourcing make discovery of individual failures a necessary means to fulfilling the host’s core duty of care, a phygital host must preserve a timely, accessible reporting route during the stay.”

The empirical base (two small hotel studies) is thin; the paper acknowledges this, but the claim should be framed more explicitly as a theoretical necessity rather than an empirically demonstrated regularity. Table 1 is useful and appropriately bounded.

**At the Touchpoint.**  
The practical test—“can a failure in an individual guest’s case reach someone with power to resolve it?”—is clear and operationalizable. The seven diagnostic questions are a genuine contribution. The critique of “seamlessness” is precise: friction creates the pause in which standing can be exercised; interruptibility is the design consequence of the further duty. Applications to the Chinese automated hotels and to Westin Operator v Groh correctly show that a route can fail at different links.  

One opening is descriptive:

> “Automated check-in shows how easily that duty can slip out of reach.”

Rewrite:  
“Automated check-in can push the person with override authority behind the machine, even when that person remains on site.”

The legal-floor discussion (DSA, EU AI Act partial coverage, Equality Act) is accurate and appropriately minimal.

**Conclusion.**  
The three claims are restated cleanly. Policy implications stay within the paper’s evidentiary and doctrinal bounds. The four suggested studies map directly onto the admitted gaps. The final sentence is effective and non-moralizing.

Overall argument structure is strong. The principal theoretical vulnerability is the step from existing doctrine to the further duty; tightening the instrumental-normativity bridge and more explicitly labeling the claim as a normative extension would strengthen it.

### 2. The Stylistic & “AI Slop” Audit

The prose is cleaner than most contemporary academic writing. Rhetorical inflation (“tapestry,” “delve,” “nuance,” “testament,” “crucial,” “foster”) is almost entirely absent. Filler transitions (“furthermore,” “moreover,” “it is important to note that,” “additionally”) do not appear. Structural predictability is low; most paragraphs are claim-oriented rather than platitude-to-moral.

**Em-dashes (—).**  
These are the most frequent stylistic defect. Every instance should be removed by restructuring:

- Introduction: “When a failure occurs at one of these touchpoints—an unexplained charge, a malfunctioning digital key, an unaddressed safety hazard—”  
  Rewrite: “When a failure occurs at one of these touchpoints (an unexplained charge, a malfunctioning digital key, or an unaddressed safety hazard), each firm can point to another…”

- Theoretical Context (phygital definition and elsewhere): several parenthetical interruptions use em-dashes. Convert to commas or parentheses, or split the sentence.

- During the Stay and At the Touchpoint: long sentences with em-dash interruptions appear; split them so the claim stands alone.

**Passive voice.**  
Not excessive for legal-academic style, but several can be activated:

- “duties are anchored to the property” → “spatial control anchors the duty of care to the property”  
- “a further duty is owed” → “the host owes a further duty”  
- “the route is owed for as long as…” → “the host owes the route for as long as…”

**Other.**  
Occasional long descriptive openings (noted above). One mild inflation: “genuinely human-centered.” Prefer “human-centered” or simply state the requirement. The analogy to the au pair in Moysidou and Stanley is illustrative rather than rigorous; it can stay if flagged as such, or be cut.

No Luddite framing. No moralizing summaries that reverse the claim.

### 3. Line-Level Revisions

Selected exact rewrites that strip bloat and front-load claims:

1. Introduction (multi-firm paragraph):  
Original: “Commercial lodging today routinely runs through several firms at once: the property that houses the guest, the brand on the sign, the platform that takes the booking, and the vendors whose systems set the price or unlock the door… When a failure occurs at one of these touchpoints—an unexplained charge…”  
Revised: “When a failure occurs at a touchpoint in multi-firm lodging (an unexplained charge, a malfunctioning digital key, or an unaddressed safety hazard), each firm can point to another, leaving the guest without an answerable counterparty. Commercial lodging routinely runs through the property, the brand, the platform, and the vendors.”

2. The host (retaining the term):  
Original: “At first glance, commercial lodging looks like ordinary customer service, and frontline workers often view it that way… Retaining the term host remains essential…”  
Revised: “Retaining the term host remains essential despite commercial complications, because it identifies who owes the guest care and who must answer when that care fails. Frontline workers often default to the language of service, and some scholars argue that paying visitors are customers rather than guests.”

3. The property (opening):  
Original: “Hospitality law begins with the physical house itself. As Sandoval-Strausz notes…”  
Revised: “Physical control over the premises attaches the duty of care to the property, even when outside contractors perform operational tasks. Under common law the traditional innkeeper took custody of the traveler’s person and belongings overnight.”

4. During the Stay (further duty):  
Original: “A phygital host must therefore preserve a timely channel for discovering and correcting failures during the stay, even though no single technical design is required.”  
Revised: “Because algorithmic mediation and outsourcing make discovery of individual failures a necessary means to fulfilling the host’s core duty of care, a phygital host must preserve a timely, accessible reporting route during the stay.”

5. At the Touchpoint (automated check-in):  
Original: “Automated check-in shows how easily that duty can slip out of reach. At two automated hotels in China…”  
Revised: “Automated check-in can push the person with override authority behind the machine, even when that person remains on site. At two automated hotels in China, service workers stayed present for legal and technical reasons, yet guests whom the kiosk failed to process had to track a worker down.”

6. Em-dash removal (general pattern used above): replace with parentheses, commas, or a new sentence. Example:  
Original: “the further duty we propose—preserving a timely route to someone who can correct a failure during the stay—is not yet required by law”  
Revised: “the further duty we propose (preserving a timely route to someone who can correct a failure during the stay) is not yet required by law.”

7. Passive-to-active (During the Stay):  
Original: “This further duty lasts for the duration of the stay…”  
Revised: “The host owes this further duty for the duration of the stay…”

These revisions preserve every substantive claim while producing tighter, claim-first prose. The paper’s theoretical architecture is already strong; the suggested changes mainly improve readability and the precision of the further-duty derivation.
