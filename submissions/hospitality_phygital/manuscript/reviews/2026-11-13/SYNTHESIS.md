## 2. What ranks first

**The rewrite restates the thesis in a form the body does not support, and in doing so it reopens Reviewer 2's first-round objection.** Seven reviewers converge here (R1, R2, R3, 01, 05, 07; 04 from the staff side).

- **The ground of the further duty.**
  - The abstract, introduction and conclusion say the guest's *standing* grounds the further duty (ll. 5, 19, 177).
  - The section that argues for the duty derives it from the guest's *claim*, by means–end transmission (ll. 115, 119).
  - Line 117 rests the guest-usable route on subjection and exit costs.
  - Line 183 calls it a derivation 'directly from the host's underlying duty'.
  - Standing is defined as the guest's position 'when that obligation is recognized' (l. 41). As a ground, it is therefore circular, which is R2.3 from round one.
  - v8 carried a sentence saying standing 'does not ground the duty', and v9 dropped it.
- **The delegation claim.**
  - The abstract says the firm 'keeps them even when contractors or algorithms carry out the work' (l. 5).
  - The body says liability 'narrows' (l. 71).
  - The front matter, unchanged since v8, says what stays 'depends on the duty'.
  - A referee would receive two theses in one package (07).

The cheapest fix is about 12 words (05, 07): restore 'the guest's claim' at ll. 5, 19 and 177, and restore v8's sentence at l. 41. A fuller fix is 05's 124-word paragraph at l. 51, which says what stays with the host under delegation using only sources already in the reference list.

## 3. Adjudications

These are the points where reviewers disagreed or where the brief was wrong. The lead checked each against the saved source text.

1. **Garcia's 0.72 figure carries a condition, and the condition is right.** R1–R3 (and the brief) said 'When a manager manually changed a rate' (l. 11) is not in the source. It is: the working paper says 'When the manager updates prices manually, a one-percent change … leads, on average, to a 0.72 percent change' (PDF p. 7; lead-checked). The card's ellipsis hid the condition. 03 and 08 were right, and **l. 11 needs no change.**
2. **Groh's 'intoxicated guests' stands.** Groh 'registered as a guest' and checked in with two friends, and the court summarizes that 'Groh and her companions protested that they were drunk and could not drive' (lead-checked). R1's reading (friends only, non-guests) is too narrow; 03 is right.
3. **Groh's training allegation is at ¶ 13**, which v9 cites: 'In support of her negligent hiring and training claim, Groh alleges …' (lead-checked). R1's ¶¶ 11, 23, 45 is not needed. Two points remain: the ¶ 7 locator, and 'evicted them into the cold'. The court lists 'the weather' among the factors that bear on an eviction; whether that night was cold is **VERIFY**.
4. **Xu and Greiffenhagen: all recognitions succeeded.** The study's Figure 11 reports the 'timing of facial recognition success (n = 610)'. Of 674 check-ins, 51 fell on days the kiosk was off, and 13 were checked in by hand for other reasons (lead-checked against the paged text; the breakdown of the 13 is the card's, at p. 5035 n. 4). So three sentences are wrong:
   - 'most facial-recognition errors cleared up' (l. 142);
   - 'When retrying fails … the thirteen guests whom the kiosk rejected' (l. 142);
   - 'authorized to override the kiosk' (l. 156), which is the authors' inference (03, R1, R3).
5. **Dashes: change the form, do not remove them.** Grok says to remove every em-dash. The authors' style rules allow em-dashes for welding a gloss onto a term. 08 found that the one printed article from this journal among the saved texts (Germann Molz 2018) uses spaced en rules 59 times and no em rules. So the answer is to convert the 21 unspaced em-dashes to spaced en rules ( – ), and to keep them where they weld a gloss.
6. **Two of the brief's 'US spellings' are correct.** Under the journal's -ize rule, 'organizer' and 'realized' are right (08). The brief also missed four US forms: neighboring, toward (×4), afterward (×2) and centered.
7. **Grok's line rewrites.** Four of seven should not be taken as written:
