## The bar

The author's instruction stands: this is a good paper and the job is to make it better, crisper and
cleaner before it goes. Take that seriously in both directions.

- **Do not manufacture problems.** If a section is sound, say so in a sentence and move on.
- **Do not propose restructuring.** Section-level surgery is out of scope the day before the window
  closes.
- **Crisper means the argument.** Where a claim does less work than the words spent on it, say so.
- **Cleaner means what a referee will catch.** A misdescribed source, an overclaim, an absence claim
  a single citation would refute, a construct that collides with a published one.

## Standing rules

- **Verify before you assert.** Every source has been read and carded; cards live in
  `submissions/hospitality_phygital/library/cards/`. If you claim a source says something, check
  the card. If you cannot confirm, mark **VERIFY** and say what you could not confirm. Do not invent
  citations, page numbers or findings.
- **Anonymity is strict.** The body may not name the special issue, its editors, or the paper's own
  positioning in it. If you find a breach, that is a finding.
- **The register is the second author's.** Sentence mean about 20, zero em-dashes in the body,
  parenthetical citation as the default, no bold in running text, first person plural spent on
  decisions and not on decoration. Judge content against that voice, not against it. Read
  `~/.claude/writing-style.md` for the house rules on cohesion and slop, and apply them as the
  harness in the peer-review skill directs: register first, then rigor, then the slop audit, then
  line-level rewrites in the author's own voice. Your rewrites must not be more nominalized, more
  passive, or more jargon-laden than what they replace.
- **Read the whole manuscript.** Not the abstract, not a summary. All nine sections and the
  reference list.

## Output

## The panel

Names are invented. Each stands for a real position in a real literature.

### 01 — Ruth Ellery · critical hospitality theory
`01_critical_hospitality.md`

Reads and publishes in this journal. Lashley's domains, Derrida, Bulley on the threshold, Lynch on
mundane welcome, Germann Molz on network hospitality. She judges whether a paper about algorithms
has said something about *hospitality* or has borrowed the word to dignify a technology argument.
The core move, that a party can take a host's functions without inheriting a host's obligations,
is hers to accept or reject. Press whether 'a welcome is owed for one reason only: someone has
arrived' survives the conditional-hospitality tradition, whether the §2 definition of welcome
(recognition, admission, responsiveness, exception) is a definition this journal would recognize,
and whether Derrida is doing work or decorating.

### 02 — Davit Sarkisyan · phygital service research
`02_phygital_service.md`

Works in the frameworks the paper argues past: phygital customer experience, extended reality,

### 05 — Ana Beatriz Furtado · ethics of responsibility and machine agency
