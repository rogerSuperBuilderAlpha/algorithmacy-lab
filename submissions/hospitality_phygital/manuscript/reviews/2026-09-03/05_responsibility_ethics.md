## Step 0 — Register note

The governing voice is the second author's: short-breathed declaratives, a long sentence followed by a very short one, parenthetical citation, the first person plural spent on decisions ('We take the opposite line', line 118; 'We do not dispute the mechanism', line 98), no bold, no dashes. The paper is in that voice from the vignette to the last line, and the audited repairs the last panel worried about (Zhou, Garcia, Manfreda) now read as the same hand. I have not proposed anything that changes the register. Every rewrite below runs at or under a twenty-word mean, uses parenthetical citation except where a named scholar's argument is itself the thing under discussion (which REGISTER.md reserves narrative citation for), and is no more nominalized or passive than the sentence it replaces.

Two register-level observations that matter for the findings. First, the paper's own §2 stipulates four words (obligation, responsibility, authority, accountability) and then uses one of them, responsibility, in a looser sense later; that is a consistency matter, not a style one, and I treat it in Part 1. Second, the paper's paragraphs close on an aphorism far more often than the exemplars do (lines 65, 73, 83, 102, 110, 128, 130, 134, 168). Several of those lines are the paper's best and must stay. But the habit is dense enough to register as a shape, and where the closer is a third restatement of a point already made twice (line 110), I have proposed the cut.

## Part 1 — Rigor and argument, section by section

### Abstract and §1 (lines 7–27)

Sound, with one overclaim in my field. The paper's smartest move is at line 23: the inheritance question ('does a system that takes over the functions of hosting also inherit the obligations of the host?') is deflated ('Put that way, the answer is boring') into the practical question of whether the obligation stays findable. That is the right move philosophically as well as rhetorically. It is what the responsibility-gap literature itself did when it stopped asking whether machines can be blamed and started asking where forward-looking obligation lands (Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021 call this the active responsibility gap, verified in the 18 August audit against the TU Delft version of record). The paper is standing on that turn without saying so, and it does not need to say so.

The overclaim is the sentence that does the deflating: 'Almost no one thinks software picks up moral duties, and we do not argue that it does' (line 23). The paper itself cites, at line 61, a 2026 article whose thesis is that AI care robots 'can still hold role responsibility based on their social function in caregiving contexts' and 'are appropriate targets for (thin) praise and blame … because they operate within roles tied to morally relevant duties' (Kropf et al. 2026, abstract, fetched and verified verbatim today). A machine-ethics referee will read 'almost no one' against Floridi and Sanders, Coeckelbergh, and now Kropf, and will note that the paper's own reference list contradicts it. The fix is not to enter the debate. It is to concede that some do hold this, and to say the paper does not need to settle it. Fifteen words. See F2.

Line 21, the Lynch et al. (2011) paragraph, is well placed and compressed; the last review said so and I agree. Line 25's itinerary is a roadmap paragraph, which REGISTER.md notes neither exemplar has, but it is one compact first-person paragraph and the brief rules restructuring out. Leave it.

### §2 (lines 31–41)

The construct-collision paragraph (line 33) is the paper's hardest defensive work and it holds. One clause I would soften from my side: 'Contestability is a feature of a system: a route by which a decision can be challenged' (line 33). Alfrink et al. (2023), whom the paper cites at line 124, define contestability by design as both a system quality and a development practice across the lifecycle (card, full-text read), and Hirsbrunner et al. (2025) argue that it needs organizational culture and structural capability on the stakeholder's side (card, abstract). So the strongest published version of contestability is not merely a system feature, and a referee from that literature will say the paper has set up the weak version to knock it down. Seven words fix it: 'Contestability, at least as design work mostly uses it, is a feature of a system'. [CONSIDER]

The four-way separation at line 35 is the question the brief put to me directly: real taxonomy or rhetorical figure? My answer is that it is a real taxonomy, on one condition the paper has not yet met. The four are distinct: obligation is the content of what is owed; responsibility is who carries the job of meeting it (this is Hart's role-responsibility in all but name, and stipulating it rather than citing Hart is fine); authority is the power over the decision or the rule; accountability is answerability to someone entitled to ask, which is Bovens's, correctly attributed. The test of a taxonomy is whether it sorts cases, and the paper's own story sorts perfectly into it: under mediation the employee keeps the responsibility, the vendor or the corporate office keeps the authority, no one keeps the accountability, and the obligation has nowhere to land. That sentence is the taxonomy at work, and it does not appear anywhere in the paper. Line 35 promises it ('Much of what follows is the story of how algorithmic mediation pries these apart') and §3 tells the story in other words (chain, link, break). One sentence in §3, at line 49, would close the loop and turn the figure into an instrument. [CONSIDER], thirty words, drafted in Part 3.

What does need fixing tonight is a drift in the stipulated word. Line 35 says responsibility 'is about who carries the job of meeting that obligation'. Line 166 then says 'Organizations need to accept responsibility for the rules their automated systems apply', which in the paper's own terms means answer for, not carry the job of. Line 152 ('institutional rights, authority, or responsibility') is looser still. A referee who takes the stipulation seriously will catch the conclusion using the word in the sense the paper said it did not want. One-word fix at line 166. [FIX TONIGHT]

Line 37's definition of welcome (recognition, admission, responsiveness, exception) is a definition I can work with, and it matters for §5 below: responsiveness and the capacity for exception are already answerability by another name. The paper has put the forum inside the welcome and has not yet noticed.

Line 41: the Pijls passage is good. 'We know of no instrument built for the second' is scoped correctly.

### §3 (lines 45–51)

The chain and break image is right and stated once. One absence claim is refutable by a source the paper itself cites ten lines later. Line 51: 'What it has not yet asked is what that third party owes, and where the obligation goes when the party that performs the reception is not the party that can answer for it.' Sharma and Mattila (2026), cited at line 61, is titled 'Rights and responsibilities of hospitality service robots' and its abstract (fetched today) asks 'whether—and with what consequences—hospitality service robots should be afforded rights and responsibilities'. That is a hospitality journal asking what the third party owes. The second half of the sentence, where the obligation goes, is the paper's question and remains unasked by anyone. Drop the first clause. Nine words saved. [FIX TONIGHT]

### §4 (lines 55–73)

This is the section the brief assigned me, and the four-duty passage at line 61 is the load-bearing step. I take it clause by clause.

**The Sharma and Mattila characterization holds.** 'lists the responsibilities of hospitality robots, but frames them as governance: liability, safety, data protection, consent' (line 61). The abstract's own emphasis is 'governance and risk-management for the industry for eventually clarifying accountability (liability, safety, data protection, consent)'. The paper omits their stakeholder-theory framing and the moral-and-legal register, but what it says is accurate. The synthesis of 17 August called this misdescribed; on the abstract I fetched it is not. Full text remains unread (SAGE blocked the audit), so VERIFY stays on anything beyond the abstract.

**The antecedent is still broken.** 'Each of those duties has a different source' (line 61). 'Those duties' are liability, safety, data protection and consent, which the paper has just called governance duties, and which by the next-but-two sentence share one source, risk. The sentence then supplies three different duties. My predecessor flagged this and it was not fixed. 'Duties differ in what triggers them' names the axis and repairs the pronoun. [FIX TONIGHT]

**The citation placement is now half right.** 'Governance duties exist because a system creates risk (Kropf et al. 2026; Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021)' (line 61). Santoni de Sio and Mecacci now sit on the governance clause alone, which is where they belong: their active responsibility gap is the forward-looking obligation to ensure systems do not damage others' rights and interests, and that is a risk-grounded duty. Good. Kropf does not belong there. Kropf is about care, and his thesis is that a machine occupying a caring role acquires the role's duties. Attached to the governance clause he supports nothing; attached anywhere as a friend he is the paper's sharpest adversary cited as background. See F2.

**The four-duty pattern is argued enough for what it claims, and asserted where it overclaims.** The pattern is a contrast set on one axis, the triggering condition: need, trust, risk, arrival. That is consistent and it is legitimate to stipulate. The bare-presence claim ('It is the only one of the four triggered by bare presence, asking nothing of the guest except that they be there', line 61) is true by inspection of the axis and needs no more argument than it gets. Ellery's conditional-at-admission point bears on it (a booking precedes the arrival), and it survives if 'arrived' is read as arrived as a guest; that is her finding to press, not mine.

**The second 'only' is false, and not only against fiduciary duty.** 'It is also the only one whose ownership, as distinct from its performance, cannot be handed off without handing off the role itself. A trustee who delegates the work of a trust is still the trustee. A host who gives away the answering has stopped being the host' (line 61). The last review thought the trustee counterexample looms. It is worse than that: ownership non-delegability is a generic feature of duties, not a peculiarity of welcome. A data controller who appoints a processor still owns the data-protection duty; hand off the ownership and you are no longer the controller. A hospital that outsources nursing still owns the duty of care; hand off the ownership and you are no longer the care provider. A trustee who delegates investment decisions remains trustee and remains liable; a trustee who hands off the duty of loyalty itself has ceased to be trustee. On this axis welcome is not unique at all. It is ordinary.

The paper's real point is different and is stronger. When a trustee hands the role off, the beneficiary is left with a successor they can name. When a controller hands off, the data subject is left with a new controller. When a host hands the answering to a system, the guest is left with nobody, because a system cannot occupy the role that was handed off. The peculiarity of the threshold is not that ownership cannot be delegated. It is that the delegation goes to a party that cannot receive it. That is exactly §1's 'missing answerable counterparty' and §9's 'obligation without an address', and it is what the trustee pair should be made to say. Drop 'also the only one whose', keep the non-delegability as a property of hosting, and sharpen the pair to contrast transfer-to-a-successor with transfer-to-no-one. Net about twenty-five words. Drafted in Part 3. This is my most important fix. [FIX TONIGHT]

**Kropf as adversary.** With the uniqueness claim rebuilt on the right axis, the paper's thesis becomes: a system can perform the functions of reception and cannot be the party that answers for it. Kropf's thesis is that a system can hold role responsibility from social function alone. If that is right at the bedside it is right at the desk, and a check-in system holds role responsibility for welcome. The paper has to answer this or its central claim reads as true by definition (systems cannot bear obligations, so the obligation does not transfer). Kropf's own abstract supplies the answer: recognizing role responsibility 'helps narrow existing responsibility gaps in care environments without displacing human accountability'. That last clause is the paper's position. The machine can occupy the function; the answering stays with a human institution. Fifty-five words at line 61 convert the adversary into a witness and pre-empt the one referee who knows this literature. NOTES.md records that an earlier lab version of the manuscript made exactly this move ('Kropf moved from friend to adversary and is answered from his own limit'); the second author's 17 August draft did not carry it. [FIX TONIGHT]

**'Across the hospitality and service literatures we could find no one imagining it as the keeper of the threshold'** (line 61). Scoped correctly now. Brown's *Ethical Programs* (2015), which my predecessor raised, is rhetoric and software studies and no longer refutes the claim as scoped. Sound.

**The redistribution paragraphs (lines 63–69) hold.** 'Discretion acts on the single case. Authority acts on the rule' (line 65) is the paper's cleanest analytic sentence and it is the distinction my field would call discretion versus rule-making authority. One verb: 'Research on stakeholder relationships shows that roles and their responsibilities can be handed around without the responsibilities dissolving (Casalegno et al. 2020)' (line 69). The card describes a conceptual argument about the circular economy, read in full; it argues this, it does not show it. [FIX TONIGHT], one word.

**The language-habit paragraph (line 71).** The brief asks whether it earns its 120 words. From my lens it does, and better than the well-being paragraph does. What it describes is the 'many hands' problem carried out in prose: 'a system forecasts the guest' launders the designers and approvers out of the sentence, which is how responsibility diffuses in the first place. It is the only place the paper connects its argument to its own writing, and it is the paragraph a responsibility-ethics reader will quote. Keep it. One tic inside it: 'and it is worth resisting' is on the house banned list. [CONSIDER] keep; [FIX TONIGHT] the tic.

### §5 (lines 77–102)

**The both-ways sentence, and the equivocation under it.** 'Three things settle it, and they are best introduced not as imports from the study of organizational coordination but as what the host-guest relationship itself requires (Okhuysen and Bechky 2009)' (line 77). The last review flagged the both-ways shape. What sits under it is more serious and is the joint a referee in my field will press. Okhuysen and Bechky's accountability is task accountability: knowing who is responsible for which element of the work. The paper's accountability, defined at line 35 with Bovens, is answerability: the obligation to explain and justify to someone entitled to ask. Those are different concepts sharing a word, and the paper borrows the triad from one and fills the third slot from the other without saying so. CLAIMS.md item 18 already records this as 'the most attackable joint' and calls for 'one explicit translation sentence'. The draft does not have it. Own the borrowing of the names, and say that the third changes meaning on the way in. Net about fifteen words, and the both-ways sentence disappears in the process. [FIX TONIGHT]

**The forum.** 'Third, some identifiable party has to be obliged to receive and answer a challenge, because standing is empty if no one must respond. Call this accountability (Bovens 2007)' (line 79). Bovens's definition, verified in the 18 August audit at p. 450 of the version of record, is a relationship between an actor and a forum in which the actor is obliged to explain and justify, the forum can question and judge, and consequences may follow. The obligation in Bovens comes from an institutional relation: the forum is a parliament, a court, an auditor, a professional body. The guest is none of those. So the referee's question is: obliged by what? My predecessor answered from Bovens's typology (social accountability, the one relation reaching clients, is characterized as lacking hierarchy and formal obligation and as largely voluntary), and the author refused the concession because that characterization was unverified. I could not open the article today either (Wiley 403, Utrecht DSpace 403), though two independent secondary reports give the same characterization. It stays VERIFY, and I do not build on it.

The paper does not need it. The answer is inside the paper's own definitions. Line 37 defines welcome as recognition, admission, responsiveness, and the capacity for exception. Responsiveness and exception are answerability: a host who must stay responsive to what the guest brings and must keep the power to make an exception is a host who must receive and answer a challenge. And line 61 says the welcome is owed in virtue of arrival. So the obligation to answer is not borrowed from Bovens's institutional relation. It is part of the welcome, and the guest carries the standing to ask through the door with them. What the arrangement must supply is the party and the place where the asking lands, and line 136 ('to constitute a forum … to build an institutional place where a claim can be made, someone is obliged to receive it, reasons can be asked for, authority exists to change an outcome, and responsibility is recorded') already builds it, complete with Bovens's three elements. §5 should say in one breath what §7 concedes two sections later. Fifty words, depending on nothing unverified. [FIX TONIGHT]

**The asymmetry result holds.** 'Accountability cannot be bought back with the other two' (line 81) and the transparency inversion (line 83) are the section's payoff, and they are correct. They are also Bovens's own point that transparency is not accountability (confirmed in the audit), which the paper could claim as agreement rather than as its own discovery, but it cites him at line 79 and that is enough.

**The Garcia concession (line 98)** is now in voice and is the right move: 'We do not dispute the mechanism. We disagree about what a rule nobody at the desk can state amounts to.' Sound.

**Hemmer (line 81)** is differentiated on the right axis, the person the decision is about never enters the calculation. Sound.

## Part 2 — Slop audit

Measured on the body with citations stripped: 430 sentences, mean 18.5, 31 per cent under twelve words, zero em-dashes. The hand is consistent and it is not Claude's. What follows is what a read-aloud catches.

**Agentless passive.** Seven hits on `been \w+ed` in 8,600 words is low. Two are worth fixing because they hide the agent the paper's own line 71 says not to hide. **Emphasis markers and the banned openers.** One 'Strikingly' (line 122). Two 'it is worth' (lines 71, 130). No 'Importantly', 'Notably', 'Crucially'.

**Self-narrating rigor.** The formula 'we should be clear / plain / say so plainly' appears four times: line 33 ('We should be clear about what "standing" adds'), line 37 ('We should also be plain about the word "welcome"'), line 55 ('Before going further, we should be clear about what we are studying'), line 67 ('and we should say so plainly'). One is voice; four is a tell. Keep line 33, where it introduces the construct-collision defence, and vary or cut the others. Line 136's 'chosen with care' is the same habit. Line 130's 'it is worth saying plainly' is the same habit.

**Verbatim repetition.** Line 152 repeats line 112 (see §8). Line 110 states one distinction three times in three sentences. The six-questions list appears in prose (line 85) and in Table 1 (lines 87–96); the last review flagged the duplicate and the author kept both, which under the body-only ruling is a defensible choice.

**Uniformity of shape.** Paragraph closers on an aphorism at lines 65, 73, 83, 102, 110, 128, 130, 134, 168. Most are earned; the last review listed several as passages to protect. The cure is not to blunt them but to make sure the paragraph that follows opens on a backward hook rather than a fresh subject, and the paper mostly does that (line 128 opens 'It deserves the scrutiny because…'; line 130 opens 'Put that way…'). No change proposed beyond line 110.

**Nothing else.** No hedging stacks, no roadmap beyond line 25, no rhetorical questions in running prose except the vignette's 'Have you been welcomed?' (line 13), which is the paper's hook and the exemplars would allow it there.

### R1. §4, line 61, the uniqueness claim and the trustee pair (F1) [FIX TONIGHT], net +25

Replace:

> It is also the only one whose ownership, as distinct from its performance, cannot be handed off without handing off the role itself. A trustee who delegates the work of a trust is still the trustee. A host who gives away the answering has stopped being the host.

> Its ownership, as distinct from its performance, cannot be handed off without handing off the role itself. That much is true of any duty that makes a role. ## 1. Who I am

I work on responsibility gaps and machine agency: what happens to a duty when its bearer is an arrangement rather than a person. Bovens on the actor-forum relation, Hart on role-responsibility, van de Poel on many hands, Santoni de Sio and Mecacci on the four gaps, and the care-robot literature that Kropf now belongs to are my daily reading. I came to this draft having read my predecessor's report from 17 August and expecting to find her load-bearing finding either fixed or ignored. It was fixed, by moving the uniqueness claim to a new axis, and the new axis has the same problem in a milder form. I also came believing, as she did, that this paper has a genuine normative thesis rather than an honorific use of 'responsibility', and a full read confirmed it. The paper knows the difference between a capacity and an obligation, it knows that answerability is a relation and not a property, and its practical turn in §1 is the same turn my own field made a decade ago. My findings are mostly about making the paper say what it already knows in the three places a referee from my side will press: the second 'only' at line 61, the adversary cited as a friend at the same line, and the forum at line 79 whose provenance the paper has left to §7.

## 3. Where it is strong

Four things must survive any last-night edit.

The arrival trigger. 'A welcome is owed for one reason only: someone has arrived' (line 61). It is the thesis in nine words, it is true by inspection of the paper's own axis, and it is what makes the transplant of Bovens defensible once R4 is written.

The practical turn in §1. 'The problem is not the missing human face. It is the missing answerable counterparty' (line 23). It is the right move in my field, not merely a rhetorical escape from the philosophy of mind, and it is why the paper can use the responsibility-gap apparatus without owing it a section.

The hinge. 'Skill cannot conjure a counterparty' (line 110) and the Zhou reading around it, which credits what the instrument measures before naming what no instrument of the person can measure. That paragraph, minus its third restatement, is the paper.

The split. 'Some problems ask people to become more capable. Others ask institutions to become more answerable' (line 124), and the roadmap's stage 4 (line 136), which builds the forum with Bovens's three elements in it. Together with the transparency inversion at line 83, which separates epistemic access from normative standing in a way most of the transparency literature does not, this is the practical payoff. Do not cut any of it to make room for what I have proposed.

## 5. Verdict

**Minor revision, and it can be done tonight.** The argument survives a hostile read from my field. The four-duty pattern is a legitimate contrast set on one axis, the taxonomy in §2 is real, the practical turn is the right one, and the two things the paper says an institution must do (build a forum, keep an alternative) are the things my literature says. What remains is three places where the paper asserts what it could argue in a sentence, one adversary cited as a friend, and a handful of mechanical faults the last review already named.

The single most important fix is F1: drop the second 'only' at line 61 and rebuild the trustee pair so it contrasts a handoff that leaves a successor with a handoff that leaves no one. As written the sentence is false for every duty in the paper's own list, and the true version is the thesis of §1 and §9 stated at the point where the paper needs it.
