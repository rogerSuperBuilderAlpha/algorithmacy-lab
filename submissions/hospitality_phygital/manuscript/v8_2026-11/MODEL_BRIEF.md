## Thesis, and its shape

**The claim.** Scale is not a neutral container; actors invoke it to claim belonging and to place or shed responsibility. The compact form comes after the theory, not in the introduction: 'In other words, scale is a power play' (GM.092, p. 234).

**The shape.** One borrowed concept, defended against its critics, is used as a lens and walked across one case, one scale at a time (home, neighbourhood, city, nation, anywhere; then time; then the digital). She states the status of the lens herself: 'a kind of heuristic device' (GM.094). The conclusion restates the thesis as 'scales are thoroughly relational and thoroughly political' (GM.310) and as a list of who gains and who pays at each scale (GM.315).

**What this means for v8.** v8's thesis has a different shape: an argued claim in four steps (the user's abstract), not a lens. Venue ruling 2 (`JOURNAL_MODEL.md`, 5 of 6 articles) puts the thesis in the introduction with 'we argue'. v8 follows the venue there and follows her everywhere else: concept in the theory, a walk across cases in the body, a takeaway paragraph at each stop.

## Contribution moves, in her order

1. Two contrasting scenes, then why they open the article (GM.001–GM.013).
2. The lens named in a short sentence (GM.016) and read into both scenes (GM.017–GM.018).
3. Focus and reach, then the aim (GM.021–GM.022), then a three-sentence roadmap (GM.023–GM.025).
4. Her own concept defined, glossed by restatement, given a lineage in the journal's literature, and characterized feature by feature (GM.026–GM.042).
5. A neighbouring concept: its origin, its critics, her stance in the first person, a question, and a key interlocutor whose call she reads as a call for her analysis (GM.043–GM.072).
6. An outside body of theory brought in by a bridge, its history, its challengers, her return to it and her stated use of it (GM.073–GM.094).
7. Method: how the question arose, what she read and collected, the status of the claims and their limits, the analytical frame (GM.095–GM.110).
8. The walk across the case, each stop opened by placing it in the literature and closed by a takeaway (GM.111–GM.303).
9. Conclusion: what was done, what the lens is not, the thesis restated by actor, the field's obligation, four directions for research (GM.304–GM.338).

## Move vocabulary, read off this model

Named in `MODEL.md`; the recurring ones:

- **SCENE-OPEN** — year + named actor glossed by appositive + act (GM.001, GM.007, GM.202, GM.212). Used for every narrated case.
- **WHY-THE-SCENES** — 'I use these two videos to introduce this article because …' (GM.013).
- **NAME-THE-LENS** — a short sentence after a long one that brings the key term in (GM.016, nine words).
- **FOCUS + REACH** — 'My focus … is on X, and specifically Y, but my approach can be applied to Z' (GM.021).
- **ROADMAP** — 'The article begins by … After outlining … This analysis starts by … before turning to …' (GM.023–GM.025).
- **DEFINE-OWN-TERM → GLOSS** — the term with its author, then 'In other words, these are …' (GM.026–GM.027).
- **LINEAGE** — 'This view … is inspired by the work of David Bell who argues …' (GM.033), repeated not varied (GM.034).
- **ANCHOR-IN-JOURNAL** — a quotation from the journal's founding editorial with page (GM.032).
- **STANCE-TAKE** — 'I think it is worth holding on to the term …' (GM.057); 'I am not convinced that …' (GM.085); 'I sympathize with …, but …' (GM.087).
- **CRITIC** — claim first and name trailing ('as Russell Belk points out', GM.050), or name as subject; reporting verbs vary (*argue, warn, worry*, GM.194–GM.197).
- **QUESTION** — single after a long sentence (GM.060, GM.135), or a run that ends a section (GM.265–GM.267).
- **READ-PREDECESSOR-AS-CALL** — 'Without explicitly stating it, Dredge seems to be calling for …' (GM.071).
- **BRIDGE-TO-NEW-LITERATURE** — '…, so I turn now to another body of scholarship: …' (GM.073).
- **REFORMULATE** — 'In other words, …' four times (GM.027, GM.092, GM.178, GM.273).
- **PLACE-IN-LITERATURE** — 'Much of the … research on X takes as its point of departure …' (GM.119); 'With the notable exception of …, scholars have not often …' (GM.152).
- **GROUPED-EVIDENCE** — many sources in one sentence by sub-type (GM.154, seven sources; GM.181, four jurisdictions).
- **TAKEAWAY** — participial opener naming the stop + 'It reveals … It also illustrates …' (GM.149–GM.151).
- **HAND-OFF** — '…, but it is more so the city scale that is invoked …' (GM.173–GM.175).
- **SIGNPOST** — 'We will start our journey at …' (GM.118); 'Later, I will come back … But first, I want to finish …' (GM.224–GM.225); 'As I noted earlier' (GM.259, GM.262).
- **RETURN-TO-OPENING-SCENE** — 'as my opening example illustrated' (GM.168); 'We saw this in the promotional video I described at the beginning' (GM.230); a scene reread for a new dimension (GM.238).
- **WHAT-WAS-DONE** — 'In this article, I have offered …' (GM.304); 'We have seen how …' (GM.306).
- **DIRECTIONS** — 'For one thing … Another important avenue … Third … Finally …' (GM.320–GM.338).

**Metaphors.** Stock and mostly dead: 'two sides of the same coin' (GM.013), 'casts a wide net' (GM.026), 'a factory for new kinds of tourism experiences' (GM.124), 'a power play' (GM.092), 'lands at the doorstep of city government' (GM.178), 'legal cover' (GM.292), 'scratch the surface' (GM.318), 'watch this space' (GM.334). One hard figure, 'tourism's murder victim' (GM.018), is licensed by the slogan she has just quoted.

**The key term.** Held fixed. 'Scale' and 'network hospitality' recur unchanged through the article; she never varies them for elegance. When she restates, she says so ('In other words').

**Abstraction level.** Low. Every abstraction touches a document or a detail: a red scarf (GM.003), beer-and-paella menus (GM.009), a pencil drawing of a cat (GM.122), 1,400 castles (GM.112), named jurisdictions and their rules (GM.181).

**Naming or coining.** She coins one term, from her own earlier work ('network hospitality', GM.026), and one in passing ('accelerated belonging', GM.242). Everything else is borrowed and attributed: 'pseudo-sharing' (Belk), 'politics of scale' (Smith), 'lean platforms' (Srnicek), 'roll-with-it-neoliberalization' (Grisdale).

**What it never does.** No sentence grades her own argument as obvious, sharp or boring. No tables, numbered propositions or list of contributions. No 'Discussion', 'Implications' or 'Limitations' heading; the limits sit in the method section (GM.105–GM.106). The article does not end on an epigram: its last sentence is a list of forms the research could take (GM.338).

**What she does that v8 will not.** Second-person 'your' in a list (GM.121–GM.122), the exclamation mark (GM.234), irony about the reader (GM.312), 'Note how' and 'It is worth' as openers (GM.282, GM.322; banned in house style), and the moves Reviewer 3 named in v4 (D4 keeps these excluded). Where house style and her register collide, house style wins and the ledger records it (skill precedence rule).
