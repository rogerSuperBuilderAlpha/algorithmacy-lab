# Reading report: three Batat texts against the v8 gap statement

Stage 2 of v8, 13 November 2026. A reading agent wrote this report. Every sentence in it outside a quotation is the reader's, not the author's (Batat's) and not the user's. The cards are `library/cards/batat2024paradigm.md`, `batat2026hfpv.md` and `batat2026phive.md`.

**Gap statement under test.** "Phygital research prescribes safeguards for trust, fairness and well-being, but says neither to whom they are owed nor which of several firms owes them."

**How the texts were read.** I read the call for papers first, then each text in full from its version-of-record PDF (`manuscript/revision_2026-11/research/sources/<citekey>/vor.pdf`), using `plain.txt` and the rendered pages for figures and tables. Every quotation below was checked by script against the text of the printed page it cites, tolerating only ligatures, typographic quotation marks and dashes, and line-break hyphens. Figure and table text was checked against the page images. Locators are printed page numbers:

- QMR editorial: pp. 357-365.
- HFPV: issue pages 269-281.
- PHIVE: online-first pages 1-21, with the publisher cover sheet skipped.

Batat writes semicolons where a dash would stand ("support; rather than replace;"); they are printed so and kept.

**Context from the call for papers** (PDF p. 2 unless stated). The call attributes PSR to the QMR editorial: "Building on recent developments in phygital service research (PSR) (Batat, 2024b)", where its 2024b is that editorial. It states that "hospitality settings must support seamless transitions between physical and digital touchpoints, ensuring continuity, trust, and emotional engagement", and it places "guests, hosts, employees, and communities, at the center of the experience design process". Among its topics is "Power structures and guest-staff relationships in phygitally mediated hospitality" (PDF p. 3).

## 1. batat2024paradigm: the QMR guest editorial (2024)

Batat, W. (2024). Guest editorial: The phygital research paradigm. *Qualitative Market Research* 27(3), 357-365. doi 10.1108/QMR-06-2024-202. Emerald; 9 PDF pages, no cover sheet. It introduces a special issue on phygital research methodology (p. 358).

### Q1. Definitions

- Phygital, restated from Batat 2022a (the PH-CX article): "Batat (2022a) defines phygital as a human-centric holistic ecosystem that blends physical, human, digital and various other elements from a consumer perspective. Its aim is to ensure a seamless delivery of value from digital to physical realms, and vice versa." (p. 357)
- The paradigm: "The phygital research paradigm is a novel approach to studying consumer behaviors in hybrid environments, where consumption and shopping experiences may transition between physical and digital settings." (p. 358). It rests on four foundations: "the phygital research paradigm, which is anchored in four key philosophical foundations – liquid ontology, dual axiology, interpretivist epistemology and multimethodology – offers researchers a comprehensive and flexible framework for exploring phenomena occurring in hybrid contexts" (p. 357).
- Liquid ontology: "Instead, it promotes a triadic understanding that encompasses the object, the subject and the context." (p. 359)
- **Phygital service research (PSR).** The editorial never uses the phrase or the abbreviation. The call for papers and HFPV (p. 270) both cite this editorial for PSR. The term and its content belong to batat2026psr (JSM), which HFPV also cites for "the PSR dimensions" (p. 270). Anything the paper says about PSR's content must cite batat2026psr.

### Q2. Safeguards, obligations, design requirements

None for consumers, guests, employees or firms. Its only ethics are research ethics:

- Axiology "steers the ethical considerations researchers must address when designing their study frameworks." (p. 360)
- Figure 1 lists "Ensures research protocol's value and ethics" and "Guarantees authentic interpretation of participants' voices" (p. 359).
- "This guarantees that the participants' voices are authentically represented and interpreted from their unique personal perspectives." (p. 361)
- The future-research agenda calls for "An inquiry into ethical considerations during research planning and execution in these environments" (p. 363).
- The editorial reports Schmitt's (2023) concerns about generative AI *as a research tool*: "hallucinations, ethical issues about data ownership, data sharing and authorship, selection bias and the broader 'black box' problem" (p. 362).

### Q3. To whom, by whom, division among firms

**Not found after full read.** The only duty-bearer the text names is the researcher, and the only party owed anything is the research participant (pp. 360-361). I checked:

- §1 Introduction (p. 357).
- §2 Defining the paradigm (p. 358).
- §3 Philosophical foundations, with 3.1 liquid ontology (pp. 359-360), 3.2 dual axiology (pp. 360-361), 3.3 interpretivist epistemology (pp. 361-362) and 3.4 multimethodology (p. 362).
- Figure 1 (p. 359).
- §4 Future research directions (pp. 362-363).
- The references, further reading and author biography (pp. 363-365).

Outside the biography's list of journals, the words host, hospitality, employee, trust, fairness, governance, accountability, responsibility, complaint and recourse do not occur.

### Q4. Seamless, continuity, smooth, friction; interruption, recourse, feedback

There are four uses, none of which questions the term:

- "These spaces seamlessly blend physical and digital elements, thereby creating unique consumer experiences and leading to paradoxical behaviors." (p. 357). This is the author's own description, endorsed as a defining property.
- "Its aim is to ensure a seamless delivery of value from digital to physical realms, and vice versa." (p. 357). It is restated from Batat 2022a and endorsed as phygital's aim.
- The paradigm lets researchers "discern subtle patterns associated with the continuity or discontinuity between physical and digital study settings." (p. 357). This is neutral: both continuity and discontinuity are objects of study, and the context is research design, not service.
- "blended contexts (the seamless integration of digital and physical spaces)" (p. 360). Reported descriptively from Mele et al. (2024).

"Frictionless" and "smooth" do not occur. The only self-questioning concerns fluidity as a methodological problem: "Finally, it is crucial to address the challenges posed by the subjectivity and conceptual fluidity inherent in the phygital research paradigm." (p. 363).

Interruption, handover, escalation, recourse, complaint, correction and redress do not occur. "Feedback" does not occur either. The nearest notion is the voice of research participants (p. 361).

### Q5. Agency, employees, power, space

- **Agency** appears only as research participation: participants act "as co-researchers, content creators, curators and collaborators" (p. 361).
- **Employees** are absent.
- **Power and governance** are absent. Hierarchy appears only in ontology: "Holarchism expands upon realism, materialism and idealism by integrating holism's interconnectedness with hierarchical structures across various contexts – social, organizational or natural – to dynamically form reality." (p. 360)
- **Space** appears as "phygital spaces" (p. 357) and as liquid ontology's object-subject-context triad (p. 359).

### Q6. Contradictions and usable ground

- The editorial does not contradict the gap statement. It is not evidence for it either, because it prescribes no safeguards. Citing its silence on duty-bearers against "phygital research" would be unfair to a methods editorial.
- **Usable ground.** The restated aim, "a seamless delivery of value" (p. 357), serves the seamlessness argument. The object-subject-context triad (p. 359) shows an existing triad whose third term is a context, which serves `not-that-triad`.
- **It carries `dual-place-process` only in part.** It describes travel "from one setting to another, and vice versa" (p. 357) without the terms digitalization and physicalization, which come from PH-CX (pp. 1230-1231).
- **Citation hazard.** If v8 follows the call for papers and cites "Batat 2024b" for what PSR claims, a referee who checks will find nothing there.

## 2. batat2026hfpv: Phygital science and the HFPV framework (2026)

Batat, W. (2026). Phygital science and the future of macromarketing: The Human First Phygital Value (HFPV) framework for integrated physical-digital ecosystems. *Journal of Macromarketing* 46(3), 269-281. doi 10.1177/02761467261450501. SAGE; headed "Editorial"; part of the special issue "Phygital Marketing for Positive Impact" (p. 279).

The PDF is the issue version. It carries 46(3) pagination and prints no online-first date. The bibliography note ("OnlineFirst 2026-05-14, no volume/issue") is out of date and should be updated by whoever owns `references.bib`.

### Q1. Definitions

- Phygital science: "a cross-disciplinary field explaining how integrated physical-digital ecosystems shape human experience, organizational structures, innovation, and societal well-being" (p. 269).
- HFPV: "Accordingly, HFPV is defined as: the poly-creation of positive societal value across phygital ecosystem entities; spanning micro (individual), meso (organizational), and macro (societal) levels; through a human-first, systemic, and ethical orientation designed to enhance human flourishing, innovation, organizational growth, marketplace resilience, and well-being." (p. 271)
- Its architecture: "a multidimensional macro-level architecture that conceptualizes value as poly-created through the entanglement of human, technological, institutional, and contextual forces" (p. 269). It has four components: inputs, processes, outcomes and systemic feedback loops (p. 273; Figure 1, p. 274).
- PSR: "this orientation is reflected in phygital service research (PSR) paradigm, which positions human experience; not technological capability; as the primary unit of analysis in blended physical-digital ecosystems (Batat, 2024a)" (p. 270). HFPV's 2024a is the QMR editorial (p. 280). The next column cites "the PSR dimensions of hybrid embodiment, contextual fluidity, and multidimensional entanglement (Batat, 2025a)" (p. 270), and 2025a is the JSM PSR article. HFPV places PSR at the micro and meso levels: "phygital science bridges micro-macro gaps via integrating insights from phygital services research (micro/meso) with macromarketing's societal orientation (macro)." (p. 277)

### Q2. Safeguards, obligations, design requirements

HFPV prescribes extensively:

- **Evaluative criteria.** "HFPV foregrounds human dignity, emotional safety, ethical design, and societal well-being as primary evaluative criteria for phygital integration" (p. 270).
- **Human needs.** "human needs, ranging from autonomy and dignity to emotional resonance and psychological safety, must be treated as the primary input for societal value creation across physical and digital ecosystems" (p. 273).
- **Governance, privacy, transparency, fairness, trust.** "Phygital ecosystems are poly-produced by institutional arrangements, governance structures, and regulatory frameworks that shape fairness, access, accountability, and trust across integrated physical-digital environments. Institutions play a critical role in determining how phygital systems operate, who benefits from them, and how risks are managed. Ethical responsibility becomes a structural requirement rather than an optional consideration. Governance models are therefore required to address issues such as privacy, autonomy, transparency, and emotional safety, ensuring that phygital ecosystems support societal well-being rather than undermine it." (pp. 273-274)
- **Inclusion and accessibility.** Orchestration must ensure "that phygital experiences remain inclusive, accessible, and emotionally coherent across physical and digital ecosystems" (p. 275). Policy should produce "infrastructure standards that ensure safety, accessibility, and interoperability across environments" (p. 278).
- **AI governance and human oversight.** "Ethical governance requires a human-first logic that prioritizes agency, fairness, transparency, and emotional safety. Here, the CPO plays a critical stewardship role, overseeing ethical AI, data governance, and algorithmic accountability to ensure that technological systems remain aligned with human dignity and societal priorities." (p. 275). Also: "Moreover, phygital science highlights the importance of balancing automation with human dignity." (p. 277)
- **Protection from harm.** Sustainable ecosystems "ensure equitable access to technological affordances, protect individuals from harm" (p. 276).
- **Regulation.** "Second, phygital science calls for robust "algorithmic accountability". As integrated physical-digital ecosystems rely on data-driven systems that shape perception, behavior, and opportunity, regulatory frameworks must ensure transparency, fairness, and emotional safety. Policies should mandate ethical standards for AI design, data governance, and automated decision-making." (p. 278)
- **Employees.** Technocentric organizations "risk eroding trust, diminishing employee well-being, and creating stiff systems unable to adapt to contextual shifts." (p. 276). Gustafsson et al.'s PH-WE is reported as "supporting employee well-being" (p. 271).

### Q3. To whom, by whom, division among firms

This is the question on which the gap statement turns, and HFPV answers more of it than the statement allows.

**By whom: found.**

- **Firms.** "As AI systems, data infrastructures, and algorithmic decision-making increasingly shape phygital life, firms must ensure that technological affordances reduce cognitive load, enhance emotional connection, and support autonomy rather than undermine them." (pp. 277-278)
- **The Chief Phygital Officer.** "Central to this transformation is the emergence of the Chief Phygital Officer (CPO), an integrative leadership role responsible for ensuring coherence within the phygital enterprise (Batat, 2026)." (p. 271). The CPO also has the stewardship role quoted under Q2 (p. 275), and "serves as a cross-functional integrator who coordinates these participatory processes (Batat, 2026), ensuring that diverse voices are included in decision-making" (p. 275).
- **Organizations.** "Human-first phygital organizations institutionalize these adaptive mechanisms" (p. 275).
- **Regulators and policymakers.** "regulatory frameworks must ensure transparency, fairness, and emotional safety" and "Policymakers must address disparities in access, literacy, and infrastructure to prevent the deepening of social inequalities." (p. 278)
- **Communities.** "In this way, communities act as co-governors of phygital ecosystems, ensuring that institutional structures remain responsive to lived experience and societal expectations." (p. 277)

**To whom: found, as populations.** Individuals ("protect individuals from harm", p. 276), "communities, institutions, vulnerable groups, and civil society" (p. 275), and "diverse populations", whom ecosystems should "serve ... fairly and respectfully" (p. 278).

**Division among several firms: not found after full read.** HFPV acknowledges many actors, "Value in phygital ecosystems is poly-created, emerging from the contributions of multiple human and non-human actors." (p. 275), but places coherence and ethical stewardship in one office "within the phygital enterprise" (p. 271). It leaves the allocation question to research: "Macromarketing scholars must examine how institutions (public, private, and civil society) shape the rules, norms, and accountability structures that govern phygital life." (p. 278). "Algorithmic accountability" (pp. 275, 278) names neither who renders the account nor to whom.

**An individual's claim: not found after full read.** No safeguard is framed as owed to a particular person who can demand it. I checked:

- The abstract and introduction (pp. 269-270).
- The sections on digital transformation (p. 270), on integrated marketplaces (pp. 270-271) and on why macromarketing needs phygital science (pp. 271-272).
- Table 1 (p. 272).
- The HFPV components: inputs (pp. 273-274), Figure 1 (p. 274), processes (pp. 274-275), outcomes (pp. 275-276) and feedback loops (pp. 276-277).
- Contributions (pp. 277-278), the research agenda (pp. 278-279) and the conclusion (p. 279).

### Q4. Seamless, continuity, smooth, friction; interruption, recourse, feedback

Every occurrence is listed below. None questions the goal.

- **Seamless.** Two uses, both endorsing the term as a goal:
  - "networked systems in which information flows seamlessly across physical and digital layers" (p. 271), as organizational design.
  - "designing seamless, coherent, and emotionally resonant transitions that honor human perception and sense-making" (p. 275), as a value-creating process.
- **Friction.** Two uses, both endorsing friction reduction:
  - orchestration as "a societal mechanism for enabling inclusion, reducing friction, and fostering trust" (p. 275);
  - "Systems that support clarity, reduce friction, and honor human dignity contribute to psychological safety and flourishing." (p. 276)
- **Continuity.** Endorsed throughout as a named construct:
  - "Technological suitability and multimodal continuity", and "creating conditions for multimodal continuity" (p. 273); also in Figure 1 (p. 274);
  - "These outcomes reflect the lived consequences of hybrid embodiment, contextual fluidity, and multimodal continuity" (p. 275);
  - experiential value is "shaped by the continuity and coherence of transitions across modalities" (p. 275);
  - continuity is listed among the paper's constructs (p. 277).
  - The research agenda opens continuity's effects to inquiry without questioning it as a goal: "Phygital well-being research must therefore investigate how hybrid embodiment, contextual fluidity, and multimodal continuity affect psychological flourishing, cognitive load, and the lived experience of autonomy, belonging, and trust." (p. 278)
  - "cultural continuity" (p. 278) is used in a different sense.
- **Discontinuity** appears once, as a threat: "ensuring that participation, access, and well-being are not disrupted by technological or contextual discontinuities" (p. 275).
- **Smooth and frictionless** do not occur.
- HFPV recognizes that technology can cut either way, without applying the point to seamlessness: "it can reduce cognitive load or intensify it, enhance emotional connection or erode it, expand access or reinforce exclusion." (p. 273)
- **Feedback and correction.** All feedback is systemic, aggregate and aimed at redesign:
  - "ensuring that feedback is continuously translated into organizational learning and system redesign" (p. 275);
  - "When systems generate exclusion, cognitive overload, emotional dissonance, or inequitable access, these outcomes signal the need for redesign." (p. 276);
  - "societal outcomes function as structural feedback mechanisms" (p. 276);
  - "When well-being indicators reveal harm or exclusion, governance models must adapt to address these issues." (p. 277);
  - "integrating community feedback into continuous system redesign" (p. 278).
  - Correction belongs to the system: markets as institutions "capable of learning, self-correction, and ethical evolution" (p. 276). "Corrective" on p. 270 is intellectual ("Phygital phenomenology (Batat, 2025a) offers a corrective").
- Interruption, human handover, escalation, recourse, complaint and redress do not occur.

### Q5. Agency, employees, power, space

- **Agency.** People "seek experiences that support their sense of agency and humanity" (p. 269) and are not to be treated "as passive participants in technological systems" (p. 269). Participation is "a form of shared authorship" (p. 275), and communities are "co-governors" (p. 277).
- **Employees.** The networked organization HFPV proposes is staffed by mixed teams: "These systems rely on cross-modal teams integrating design, data science, operations, ethics, and human-experience expertise (Batat, 2026)." (p. 271). The text also reports PH-WE and employee well-being (pp. 271, 276).
- **Power.** "Institutions play a critical role in determining how phygital systems operate, who benefits from them, and how risks are managed" (pp. 273-274). The special issue is reported as revealing "paradoxes (empowerment versus surveillance, access versus exclusion)" (p. 271), and the agenda asks "how algorithmic systems shape visibility and opportunity" (p. 278).
- **Space.** "Cultural norms, spatial inequalities, and community-level dynamics further influence how individuals access and benefit from integrated physical-digital systems." (p. 273)
- **AI as actor.** "Technologies must be understood as sociotechnical actors that participate in the poly-creation of phygital value for human flourishing and the structuring of societal life." (p. 273). Ahuvia et al. are reported as "conceptualizing AI agents as relational actors embedded in hybrid interactions" (p. 271), and Roederer et al. as showing "how AI becomes a "third agent" in financial services" (p. 271). Table 1 reports that they reframe "service ecosystems as sociotechnical triads (human-AI-organization)" (p. 272).
- There is no hospitality content: the words hospitality, hotel, tourism, guest and host do not occur.

### Q6. Contradictions and usable ground

- **The statement's claim that the field "says neither to whom ... nor which ..." is false of HFPV** at the level of firms in general, of one enterprise, and of regulators (see Q3).
- **It contests the wording of `framing-argued-past`.** HFPV calls technologies "sociotechnical actors" (p. 273), not instruments.
- **It threatens `not-that-triad`.** Roederer et al.'s human-AI-organization triad (pp. 271-272) is a published service triad with AI third. It must be read before the paper says existing triads place only a context or a participant third.
- **Usable ground.** This is the paper's strongest editors'-own ground. "Ethical responsibility becomes a structural requirement rather than an optional consideration" (p. 274), the call for "algorithmic accountability" (p. 278) and the agenda item on "accountability structures" (p. 278) are the editor's own premises. The paper can take them up for a single stay with several firms. HFPV's levels (p. 271, p. 277) confirm that the paper's difference is one of level: HFPV is macro, PSR micro and meso, and the paper is at the encounter.

## 3. batat2026phive: the PHIVE Matrix (2026)

Batat, W. (2026). The phygital intelligence value experience (PHIVE) matrix: The good, the bad, and the ugly of AI marketing for customer experience design. *Journal of Strategic Marketing*, online first 2 June 2026 (cover sheet), pp. 1-21. doi 10.1080/0965254X.2026.2682414. It was received 19 May 2026 and accepted 22 May 2026 (p. 1). It describes itself as a "position paper" (p. 2).

### Q1. Definitions

- Phygitality: "Phygitality, an ontological condition in which physical and digital environments are inseparably entangled, extends beyond the simple integration of channels." (p. 2)
- PHIVE: "Accordingly, the PHIVE Matrix is defined as: a strategic decision-making tool that evaluates how firms' AI-driven tools and strategies generate, sustain, or undermine experiential value in phygital environments by assessing the interplay between two main pillars/axes, namely AI phygital integration depth and AI human-value alignment." (p. 7)
- Phygital intelligence: "PI refers to a system's capacity to sustain experiential continuity, emotional coherence, ethical human-centric design, and embodied engagement across intertwined physical and digital realms." (p. 7). Also: "AI optimizes tasks; PI orchestrates meaning." (p. 7)
- The four quadrants are Air (fracture; low integration, high alignment), Earthquake (erosion; low, low), Water (support; high, low) and Fire (amplification; high, high) (Figure 1, p. 9; Table 4, p. 16).
- PSR is not named as such. The article cites "The phygital research paradigm (Batat, 2024b, 2025)" (p. 6), where 2025 is the JSM PSR article (p. 20).

### Q2. Safeguards, obligations, design requirements

- **Harms to avoid.** AI "may contribute to dehumanization, emotional dissonance, surveillance, identity distortion, cognitive overload, or experiential fragmentation (Song et al., 2026) when technological efficiency supersedes human-centric experience design principles (Addis et al., 2022)." (p. 2). In the Earthquake quadrant, "AI may reinforce stigma, foster surveillance and loss of autonomy, induce dehumanization, or impose unattainable norms of self-optimization." (p. 11)
- **Evaluation beyond performance.** "These outcomes show that AI-mediated experiences must be evaluated not only through technical performance but also through their emotional, ethical, symbolic, and cultural consequences." (p. 4)
- **Trust, transparency, agency, well-being.** "Value-amplifying technologies operate sensitively and transparently, respecting agency, emotional well-being, and cultural diversity (Anderson, 2026). Rather than imposing norms or exploiting data, they empower consumers and support authentic self-expression. Trust emerges from both technical reliability and the sense that AI genuinely enriches lived experience." (p. 16)
- **Human presence kept.** This is the nearest the article comes to human fallback, and it concerns presence, not failure: "It also reinforces the importance of AI systems that support; rather than replace; human creativity, empathy, and relational presence." (p. 17). Also "intentional, human-centric AI integration that enriches rather than replaces human presence and fosters trust and continuity across phygital journeys" (p. 19). Replacement is named as a harm: "Mental-health apps that replace human care" (p. 4).
- **Remedies** (Table 5, "Repair & Rebuild", p. 18): "Use phyginography to generate smart insights and restore trust and emotional safety"; "Eliminate harmful features or extractive mechanisms".
- **Privacy and inclusion** appear through the special-issue synthesis. Lim (2025) is reported on "risks related to bias, misinformation, privacy, and depersonalization" (p. 4). Foroudi et al. (2026) are reported as "underscoring emotional vulnerability and exclusion risks" (p. 4), and Table 1 lists "Digital exclusion, complete trust breakdown, vulnerability exploitation" (p. 5).
- **Employees.** "technological extensions should support the holistic nature of both consumer and employee lived experiences rather than amplify isolated dimensions of optimization." (p. 10). Table 1 lists "workforce displacement" (p. 5).

### Q3. To whom, by whom, division among firms

**By whom: found, as one firm's leaders.** The matrix "helps leaders evaluate how AI shapes experiential value in phygital marketplaces" (p. 2). It evaluates "firms' AI-driven tools and strategies" (p. 7), and "For brands and organizations, understanding this configuration is essential for responsible AI marketing strategies." (p. 10). "Each quadrant carries explicit managerial implications" (p. 17).

**To whom: found only as "consumers" in general**, as experiencers of value (e.g. "they empower consumers", p. 16).

**Division among several firms: not found after full read.** The nearest passage concerns integration across touchpoints, framed as experiential coherence, not responsibility. The matrix "cautions against deploying AI in isolated touchpoints without considering the broader phygital ecosystem" (p. 17).

**An individual's claim, recourse or duty: not found after full read.** I checked:

- The abstract and introduction (pp. 1-3).
- The limits of AI marketing (pp. 3-4) and why AI marketing needs a phygital perspective (pp. 4-6), with Table 1 (p. 5).
- Introducing the matrix (pp. 7-8), with Table 2 (p. 8).
- The quadrants: Air (pp. 8-10; Figure 1, p. 9), Earthquake (pp. 10-12), Water (pp. 12-13) and Fire (pp. 13-16; Table 3, p. 15), plus Table 4 (p. 16).
- Contributions and implications (pp. 16-18; Table 5, p. 18) and the conclusion (pp. 18-19).

My reading of one case. Table 1's synthesis of Klaus (2025) records a failure exactly where one party's AI plan meets another party's physical execution. The table gives the role "AI concierge tools shape luxury phygital journeys by enhancing digital planning but failing in physical execution", Bad "Emotional disconnect, lack of human warmth, misalignment between AI plan and physical service", and Ugly "Service failures, unmet expectations, erosion of luxury value, trust breakdown" (p. 5). It records the failure as lost value; who answers for it is not discussed.

### Q4. Seamless, continuity, smooth, friction; interruption, recourse, feedback

Continuity is the matrix's master value, and its loss is the harm diagnosed. Seamlessness appears both in the merely "good/fair" Water quadrant and in the Fire benchmark. Every occurrence follows.

- **Seamless.** Five uses, all endorsing it as at least a functional good:
  - "contemporary transportation platforms combine AI-driven navigation, predictive traffic management, voice assistants, and personalized mobility recommendations to create seamless travel experiences across physical and digital spaces." (p. 13, Water). This is endorsed, and then judged insufficient: "Yet while these systems substantially improve functional quality, they do not necessarily create emotionally transformative experiences." (p. 13)
  - Fire: the human experience "is elevated through the seamless convergence of technological precision, emotional resonance, and experiential meaning." (p. 13). Endorsed.
  - Fire: "AI becomes embedded within the 'phygital flow', defined as the seamless, continuous poly-creation of meaning across humans, technology, environment, and context." (p. 14). Endorsed as the benchmark.
  - Table 4, Water: "AI-enabled mobility or hospitality systems that create seamless, efficient, and coherent phygital journeys", with outcome "Experience becomes useful, seamless, and moderately engaging" (p. 16). Endorsed as good, ranked below transformation.
- **Smooth.** Four uses, all in the Water quadrant, all endorsing smoothness as a good while capping its value:
  - "experiences that flow smoothly, stabilize interactions, and create coherence without necessarily reaching emotional depth" (p. 12);
  - "AI contributes positively by making interactions smoother, more connected, and more responsive" (p. 12);
  - "Flows, stabilizes, smooths" (Table 4, p. 16; Table 5, p. 18);
  - "Smooth, efficient, coherent experiences" (Figure 1, p. 9).
- **Friction.** "AI reduces friction, enhances responsiveness, and expands experiential possibilities" (p. 15, Fire). Endorsed.
- **Frictionless** does not occur.
- **Continuity.** Endorsed throughout; its absence is the harm:
  - "without weakening authenticity or experiential continuity across physical and digital environments" (p. 2);
  - "without supporting continuity across the broader phygital journey" (p. 3), a limitation;
  - "a phygital, human-centric logic that foregrounds continuity and meaning-making across blended experiential realities" (p. 4);
  - "its capacity to sustain phygital value continuity" (p. 6);
  - "sustain experiential continuity" (PI definition, p. 7);
  - "sustaining experiential continuity across physical and digital realms" (pp. 7-8);
  - "Orchestrates continuity across physical-digital experiences" (Table 2, p. 8);
  - "it fails to sustain the continuity required across blended physical-digital environments" (p. 9);
  - "moments of engagement disconnected from the lived continuity of everyday experience" (p. 9);
  - "Weak phygital continuity" (Figure 1, p. 9, Air);
  - "experiential value depends on continuity between physical and digital realities. When AI disrupts this continuity, experiences may remain emotionally engaging while becoming psychologically destabilizing." (pp. 9-10);
  - "an inability to sustain experiential continuity across interconnected realms" (p. 10);
  - "necessary to sustain coherence, continuity, and embodied meaning across physical and digital environments" (p. 10);
  - "limited continuity between physical and digital realms" (p. 11, Earthquake);
  - "AI contributes to continuity across touchpoints" (p. 12);
  - "They support continuity, contextual adaptation, and experiential fluidity across multiple touchpoints." (p. 13);
  - "AR try-on tools or chatbots that engage emotionally but disrupt experiential continuity" (Table 4, p. 16);
  - "activating multiple extensions to ensure continuity across phygital environments" (p. 17);
  - "Restore phygital continuity" (Table 5, p. 18);
  - "fosters trust and continuity across phygital journeys" (p. 19).
- **Interruption, human handover, escalation, recourse, complaint and redress** do not occur.
- **Feedback** appears once, as part of a harm: wellness systems that "support well-being through personalized feedback and continuous monitoring" overwhelm consumers (p. 10).

## Verdict on the gap statement

The statement survives in part and needs rewording. Its first clause, that phygital research "prescribes safeguards for trust, fairness and well-being", is true of HFPV (pp. 270, 273-278) and PHIVE (pp. 16-19; Table 5, p. 18) but false of the 2024 QMR editorial, whose only ethics are research ethics (pp. 359-361). Its second clause, that the field "says neither to whom they are owed nor which of several firms owes them", fails as worded for HFPV. There Batat names who must act: firms (pp. 277-278), a Chief Phygital Officer "responsible for ensuring coherence within the phygital enterprise" (p. 271), human-first organizations (p. 275), regulators and policymakers (p. 278), and communities as "co-governors" (p. 277). She also names for whom: individuals, communities, vulnerable groups and "diverse populations" (pp. 275-278). PHIVE likewise addresses "firms' AI-driven tools and strategies" (p. 7) for the sake of consumers. What survives a full and fair read of all three texts is narrower and sharper. None divides any duty among several firms that together deliver one service; HFPV puts stewardship inside a single enterprise and leaves "accountability structures" to future research (p. 278). None treats a safeguard as owed to an individual guest who can claim it. And every feedback mechanism is aggregate and prospective: feedback is translated "into organizational learning and system redesign" (HFPV, p. 275), never sent along a route by which one guest's failed case reaches someone able to correct it. That much holds for three texts by one author. Before the paper writes "phygital research", the same full read is owed to batat2026psr, the article that actually defines PSR and is so far read only at abstract depth, and to shabnam2026tpsr.

**A possible rewording, drafted by the reader for the author to accept, change or reject.** "Phygital research prescribes safeguards for trust, fairness and well-being and assigns their stewardship to the firm, a Chief Phygital Officer and the regulator. It does not say which of several firms that jointly host a guest owes them, or what the guest may claim when the system fails her; its feedback loops redesign the system for the next guest rather than correct the case of this one."
