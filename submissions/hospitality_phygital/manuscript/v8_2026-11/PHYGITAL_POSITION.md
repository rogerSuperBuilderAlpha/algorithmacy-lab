# Where v8 sits in phygital research

## 1. The term, and v8's use of it

**Batat's definition.** 'phygital can be defined as: a holistic and integrative ecosystem that adopts a consumer standpoint as a starting point and then integrates a combination of physical, human, digital and media content elements, platforms, technologies, and extended realities, among others; the goal of phygital is to offer unique and compelling customer experiences that should guarantee a coherent continuum in the delivery process of consumer value (intrinsic/extrinsic) provided from digital to physical and vice versa' (Batat 2024: 1229). The special issue's call cites the same article for the term.

**The dual-place process**, which the editor called central: 'The customer's experience is created and shaped through two processes: shifting from physical to digital ("digitalization") and from digital to physical ("physicalization.")' (Batat 2024: 1230–1231; defined again at 1236). Zheng et al. adopt it for tourism (2025, AAM p. 4). Pierre's relay (ledger I-22) is the same movement seen from the guest's side: each crossing is a handoff at which someone holds authority, or no one does.

**'Phygital arrangement'.** v7 coined it for 'the guest, the staff, the property, the firms and the systems together'. v8 ties it to Batat's definition: her ecosystem 'integrates a combination of physical, human, digital and media content elements, platforms, technologies'; the arrangement is the same ecosystem seen as a set of parties, each holding part of the power over the guest's stay. Pierre's sentences carry it (I-20: 'It is not the algorithm as a lone gadget. It is the whole arrangement …').

**AI's status.** Batat's latest work treats AI 'not as a functional tool but as an "experiential agent"' (PHIVE: 17) and technologies as 'sociotechnical actors' (HFPV: 273). This is the editor's own ground for Pierre's 'constitutive mediator' (I-48), which the editor endorsed and the locked abstract carries. v8 says it this way: AI is not a neutral tool; it can change who in the arrangement is able to answer the guest.

**Reading the table.** The field prescribes safeguards (most texts), names bearers (most) and protected parties (most). Three texts come near v8's question: Sharma and Mattila ask which of several parties answers and leave it to liability; Parhi spreads the obligation across franchisor, franchisee and vendor by contract; Ariyogi et al. give it to the brand the guest deals with and ask for escalation with authority. None derives the duty from the role of host, none binds it to a party by holding out, reliance or control of the guest's space, and none states the in-stay route to correction as a duty of that role. The documented search (GAP_SEARCH §3) found no work that does.

## 4. Seamlessness, and where interruptibility stands

**Who endorses it.** Batat (2024), Batat and Hammedi (2023), Mameli et al. (2026), Zheng et al. (2025), Mosca et al. and Liu et al., all as goals; Gursoy endorses 'removing friction' (2026: 156); the call requires 'seamless transitions' (p. 2).

**Who questions it.** Shabnam et al.: 'The dominant emphasis on efficiency and experience design leaves underexplored the deeper socio-technical mechanisms …' (2026: 2), though friction remains a cost to them (p. 10). PHIVE: seamless journeys are good, not sufficient (pp. 13, 16). Weaver (2026, this journal, abstract only) criticizes technological smoothing. Batat herself: the framework 'should not consider a positive phygital experience to be the aim … Instead, its aim should be to identify the dynamics of consumer value in the shifting process' (2024: 1238).

**v8's stance, narrowed (ledger I-43, I-44).** Pierre's claim that smoothness 'has stayed a goal rather than a suspect' narrows to the phygital frameworks, and Shabnam et al. and PHIVE are cited as allies. What v8 adds is not a critique of seamlessness but its condition: a seamless crossing still needs an interruptible moment and a counterparty there to catch the interruption. Put in the call's terms: continuity and trust across a transition require that someone answerable before it is still answerable after it. Interruptibility is the further duty's design consequence.

## 5. Feedback: design input, or a route in the guest's case

The call wants feedback to 'shape phygital hospitality design' (p. 2). HFPV makes it a loop that lets systems 'respond to lived experience in real time', 'ensuring that feedback is continuously translated into organizational learning and system redesign' (p. 275). The loops act on the system through aggregate signals; none of the mechanisms in the texts read is a route by which a failure in one guest's case reaches someone able to correct it while the guest is still there (READ_batat_new, corrected by VERIFY_stage2_cards; POSITION_set2 on the call). v7's further duty asks for both, in order: correction in the single case during the stay, and learning across cases (question 6; stage 4 'Learn and route'). v8 can put this in one sentence as the difference it makes to the call's 'feedback'.

## 6. Threats a phygital referee could raise

- **'Triad'.** Roederer et al., as HFPV reports them, describe a human–AI–organization triad in financial services (HFPV: 271–272). Pierre's 'three-party relationship' (I-21) must not claim to be the first triad with AI as the third party; v8 cites the relationship as Pierre does, without priority.
- **AI as tool.** v8 must not say phygital research treats AI as a mere tool: Batat's 2026 work calls it an agent and an actor. v8's point is narrower — AI can change who is able to answer the guest — and it aligns with her.
- **PSR.** As in §2: cite Batat (2026, JSM) for PSR, within its abstract.
- **The editors' work.** v8 engages Batat (several texts), Mosca and De Vos. The August review warned that heavy citation of the editors can read as an authorship signature under anonymous review; Berthon et al. (2026) came from the 2025 APA Summit with Batat as associate editor (READ_berthon2026). **For the authors.**

## 7. The gap sentence

Narrowed after the re-reads and the search (V8-D6). A proposal for Stage 5, to be written in the model's register and checked against READ_gapworks when it reports:

> Phygital research prescribes safeguards for guests and addresses them to firms and managers. Where hospitality research takes up the several firms behind one stay, it asks which of them answers and leaves the answer to liability (Sharma and Mattila 2026), spreads the obligation across them by contract (Parhi 2026), or gives it to the brand the guest deals with (Ariyogi et al. 2026). None derives what the guest is owed, or which firm owes it, from the role of host.

The sentence names the nearest neighbours, as GM.152 ('With the notable exception of …') and GM.071 ('Without explicitly stating it, Dredge seems to be calling for …') do, and it rests on the documented search, as the research rules require for any claim of absence.
