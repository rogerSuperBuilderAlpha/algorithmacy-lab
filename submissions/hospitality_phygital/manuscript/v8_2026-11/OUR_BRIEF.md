## The thesis, and its shape

**The claim** (Roger's abstract; v7's argument):
1. The role of host specifies the core duties: care for the guest's safety and diligent performance of what was undertaken.
2. Holding out, the guest's reliance and control of the guest's space bind those duties to a particular party.
3. Where others carry out the work, what stays with the host depends on the duty at issue, and several parties may owe care at once.
4. During the stay, the guest's claim justifies a further duty to keep some timely route by which a failure in the guest's case reaches someone able to correct it — a person algorithmic mediation can move out of the guest's path.

**The phygital layer** (D1): phygital hospitality joins physical and digital places and moves the guest between them (Batat 2024: 1229–1231). Where the joins are made by several firms' systems, the arrangement can separate the party with power over a decision from the party who can answer the guest — Pierre's 'missing answerable counterparty'. The further duty is what phygital design owes at the crossings: an interruptible moment and a counterparty there to catch it (Pierre's interruptibility), which is what makes the trust and continuity the special issue asks for warranted.

**The shape.** The model's is one concept used as a lens on one case, walked scale by scale. v8's is an argued claim in four steps, but it can take her walk: after the theory, the argument walks the phygital arrangement **party by party** (her spatial scales), then **through the stay** (her temporal scales), then **to the touchpoint** (her digital scales), each stop opened by placing it in the literature and closed by a takeaway paragraph. The order of v7's argument is kept (D1): the host's duty, then delegation, then the further duty during the stay, then the touchpoint.

**The compact form** (GM.092's slot, 'In other words, …'): the host's duties stay where the guest's space is controlled, and the guest is owed a way to reach whoever can put a failure right. **Proposal; Stage 5 writes it in the model's register.**

## The gap, stated against the phygital literature

From the Stage 2 re-reads (`stage2/POSITION_set1.md`, `POSITION_set2.md`; the documented search in `stage2/GAP_SEARCH.md` is still running):

- The working statement — phygital research 'says neither to whom [safeguards] are owed nor which of several firms owes them' — **fails as worded**. Shabnam et al. (2026: 14) address 'organisations', 'Managers', 'firms' and 'policymakers' and ask for 'escalation pathways and human oversight'. Sharma and Mattila (2026: 911) name what the customer is owed and ask which of several parties answers, then leave the answer to liability and risk management. Gursoy (2026: 155, 158) puts hospitality firms under a social contract with guests.
- **What survives every full read:** no text says which of several firms supplying one experience owes a safeguard, and none treats a safeguard as something the guest can claim. Sharma and Mattila pose the several-parties question only after harm; Shabnam et al. list 'responsibility, accountability' among what existing theory cannot explain (p. 5) and do not carry them into their own resolution.
- **v8's gap sentence** therefore names its nearest neighbours and states the narrow absence, with the search behind it. Drafted in `PHYGITAL_POSITION.md` once the gap search reports.
