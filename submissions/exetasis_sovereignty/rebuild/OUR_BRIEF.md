## Research question
Can a competence modelled on reading (literacy, now "technology literacy") secure sovereignty once algorithms coordinate people rather than inform them? It is stated once, in O4, as the model states its question in I10.

## Thesis and its shape
- **The thesis:** literacy was necessary for democratic sovereignty and never sufficient. Algorithmacy stands in the same relation to sovereignty under algorithmic coordination. It becomes sovereignty only through institutional standing, coordinative sovereignty, in a mediation participants cannot bypass.
- **Its shape:** like the model's, a coined concept, defined, then tied to a condition. Unlike the model's, it is carried by an analogy (literacy is to the written state as algorithmacy is to platform governance). Its concession matches the model's I28: an old problem in a new medium.
