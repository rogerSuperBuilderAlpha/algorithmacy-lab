## What the research changed
- **The old claim to novelty is taken.** The starting-point abstract put its weight on "sovereignty need not mean independence". That move has already been made by several authors:
  - Hobbes: subjects who cannot leave (*Leviathan* ch. XVIII).
  - Habermas: "subjectless" sovereignty.
  - Edler et al. (2023) and Hao (2026): state sovereignty inside dependence.
  - Lazar (2025): exit cannot ground authority (*P&PA* 53(2), verified quotations).
  - Verhulst & González Otero (2026): "agency, rather than mere ownership" (abstract read today).
- **What is still unclaimed** (in OpenAlex, Crossref, arXiv and the web, 24 Oct 2026):
  1. Sovereignty held by the *coordinated actor*, inside a mediation it cannot bypass.
  2. A *bypass test* that separates a gate one should leave from a mediator one cannot.
  3. A precise account of why voice, and not exit, changes the actor's position.

  "Algorithmacy" has no scholarly hits.
- **The formal claim must be restated.** With a lone necessary mediator, the core gives the mediator everything: the core secures *the mediator's* hold. Only a coalition that removes the coordinated parties' substitutability changes that. "Core membership" for a player is a category error, because the core is a set of allocations.
- **"Competence alone confers no standing" survives.** It is the best-supported sentence in the abstract (Rouvroy & Berns pp. 175, 181; Foucault 1982 p. 790).

## Recommendation
**A as the frame, with C's bloc result as a one-sentence formal coda, and B's "competence alone confers no standing" kept as one move.**
- **Why A:** it carries the only novelty that survived the search, which is who holds the sovereignty (the bearer) plus the bypass test.
- **Why C's coda:** it gives a Lazar-reading referee something new. The coda would say that voice works by making the coordinated parties a joint veto player, and that who then picks the split is a procedural question.
- **Fallback:** A with D's infrastructure case, if you want the abstract to face the call's state-sovereignty themes (J2, J8). It would cost bearer novelty and lean on grey sources.

## Roger's ruling, 24 Oct 2026 (his words, verbatim)
> "the idea is that literacy is necessary for soverengty in a demoncratic government, but insufficient in a triadic, opaque, cooptive environment. just as poeopel needed to develop literacy to have soveregnty in demoncarcy, they need to develop algorithmacy to have soverengty in this new environment"

[DRAFTED by Claude: how the ruling maps onto the research.]
- **The thesis is an analogy of necessity:** literacy is to democratic sovereignty as algorithmacy is to sovereignty in algorithmic mediation. It supersedes options A–D as the frame. B comes closest, and A and C supply supporting moves.
- **Each of the three adjectives has a verified source:**
  - *triadic*: Stark & Vanden Broeck's "triangular structure" (p. 14);
  - *opaque*: Burrell 2016;
  - *cooptive*: Stark & Vanden Broeck, "on platforms they are coopted" (p. 5).
- **The ruling is consistent with "competence alone confers no standing"** as long as the claim is *necessary, not sufficient*. Literacy was necessary but not sufficient too, since it needed published law, a press and a franchise.
- **The open gap:** no verified source yet backs the first half, that literacy was *necessary* for democratic sovereignty. The Habermas line is still an unchecked paraphrase.
