# S7: Venue due diligence for *Exetasis* ("AI and Sovereignty" call)

Researched 2026-10-24. I ran every check read-only and contacted no one. Two kinds of source appear below. "Site" means a page on exetasis.net. "Downloaded file" means one of the two author PDFs linked from https://forauthors.exetasis.net, which I fetched and converted to text.

## Who publishes and edits it, who sits on the board, and where it is based

### Takeaway
The site names no publisher, no editor-in-chief, no institution and no country. Its board page lists three names with no roles or affiliations, and none of the three ties to philosophy in any source I could find. The author-guideline PDFs carry the name of a University of Bologna student in their file metadata. That is the only trace of who builds the journal, and it points to Italy.

### Cited Findings
- The home page describes the journal and links to the subpages "The Journal", "Board members", "For Authors" and "Calls for papers". It names no publisher, editor or location. The footer reads only "Exetasis © 2026." — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- Self-description: the journal takes its name from the Socratic *exetasis*. It is "Published annually" and covers "politics, economics, law, philosophy, and sociolinguistics", with attention to "the particularity of peoples and the fluidity of borders". It accepts papers in Italian, English, German, French and Spanish. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- Board page, in full: "Exetasis is managed by an editorial team responsible for editorial oversight, manuscript management, and the peer-review process." The names listed are "Muhammad Adel Zaky, Priyanka Gadia, Rudresh Teotia". The page gives no roles, affiliations or bios. — [boardmembers.exetasis.net](https://boardmembers.exetasis.net)
- The call names no individual editor. It refers only to "the Exetasis editorial board" and calls the journal "an independent journal". — [callsforpapers.exetasis.net](https://callsforpapers.exetasis.net/)
- PhilEvents lists Simone Dipasquale as the contact for the call and describes the organiser as "an independent philosophy journal". — [PhilEvents 154909](https://philevents.org/event/show/154909)
- A web search for "Simone Dipasquale" with "philosophy" found no academic profile. The closest hit was Simone Di Pasquale, an Italian dancer and TV personality, clearly a different person. — [search hit: Wikipedia, Simone Di Pasquale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Di_Pasquale)
- A web search for the three board names (each in quotes) found no academic profile for any of them. — [search results, no matching hits; example irrelevant hit](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/nia-arrests-key-accused-in-bengaluru-rss-activists-murder-case-from-mumbai-airport-2919380)
- A search for "Mohammed Adel Zaky" mathematics returned only a differently named numerical analyst, Mahmoud A. Zaky. I could not confirm whether either person is connected to the board. — [research.com, Mahmoud A. Zaky](https://research.com/u/mahmoud-a-zaky)
- Downloaded file: both PDFs ("BLIND REVIEW.pdf" and "EDITORIAL GUIDELINES FOR AUTHORS.pdf") give their Author field as "Marco Catardi", with a `studio.unibo.it` email address. That is the University of Bologna's student mail domain. Both were created in Microsoft Word 2019 on 25 May 2026. — [forauthors.exetasis.net (PDF links)](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- A web search for "Marco Catardi" with "filosofia" returned no matching person. — [search hit list, none relevant; example](https://www.iris.unina.it/retrieve/e268a730-0c2f-4c8f-e053-1705fe0a812c/2018%20Recensione-a-Saccardi.pdf)

### Inferences

## ISSN, indexing and DOI

### Takeaway
I found no ISSN, no DOI prefix and no indexing anywhere. The site makes no indexing claims, so it is not inflating its status. It simply has no standing in any index yet.

### Cited Findings
- The home page and the call give no ISSN, no DOI and no indexing statement. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net); [callsforpapers.exetasis.net](https://callsforpapers.exetasis.net/)
- DOAJ API search "exetasis": `"total": 0`. — [DOAJ API](https://doaj.org/api/search/journals/exetasis)
- Crossref journals search "exetasis": 0 results. Crossref works with container-title "exetasis": 0 results. So the journal has no registered DOI prefix or deposits. — [Crossref journals](https://api.crossref.org/journals?query=exetasis&rows=5); [Crossref works](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.container-title=exetasis&rows=5)
- OpenAlex sources search "exetasis": 0 results. Since OpenAlex covers most Crossref-registered and many non-Crossref journals, this is a proxy for absence from bibliometric indexes. — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/sources?search=exetasis)
- A general web search for "Exetasis" journal philosophy returned only similarly named journals (*Ekstasis*, UERJ; *Existenz*). It returned nothing from Exetasis itself. — [Ekstasis, UERJ](https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/Ekstasis/article/download/51733/34081/178332); [Existenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existenz_(journal))
- A web search for "exetasis.net call for papers" returned nothing from exetasis.net. The near-matches were Ekstasis and Exetazo (a 2005 undergraduate journal). — [Exetazo CFP 2005](https://call-for-papers.sas.upenn.edu/cfp/2005/03/02/cfp-exetazo-an-online-journal-of-undergraduate-research-undergrad-41105-new-journal)

### Inferences
- With no ISSN, the journal cannot appear in ANVUR's lists of scientific or Class A journals, which are keyed by ISSN. Nor can it be in Scopus, Web of Science or ERIH PLUS, all of which require an ISSN. This follows from those indexes' entry rules. I did not verify the rules this session.
- A domain registered in May 2026 makes indexing in Scopus or WoS impossible in the near term, since both require a publication track record.

### Gaps
- UNKNOWN (blocked): the ISSN portal. portal.issn.org returned an "Access Denied" bot-protection page to WebFetch, and curl got no parseable result. The site prints no ISSN, which suggests none exists, but the author should check portal.issn.org by hand in a browser.
- UNKNOWN (blocked): PhilPapers. philpapers.org/s/Exetasis returned a Cloudflare challenge. Check by hand.
- I did not check Scopus Sources, the WoS Master Journal List, ERIH PLUS or the ANVUR lists directly. Each needs a browser, but the inference above makes a listing very unlikely.
- I could not query Google Scholar programmatically.

## Review process, open access, fees and AI policy

### Takeaway
On paper, the review process is standard and credible: an editorial desk check, then double-blind peer review, with reports sent even on rejection, under the COPE guidelines. No document mentions fees, APCs, open access, licensing or AI use.

### Cited Findings
- Code of ethics: Exetasis "adopts the code of publication ethics developed by COPE: Best Practice Guidelines for Journal Editors". — [thejournal.exetasis.net](https://thejournal.exetasis.net)
- The Editorial Board first checks fit with the journal's "project design, scientific rigor" and objectives. Submissions that pass are "subjected to a peer-review process (Double-Blind Review Process)". The criteria are relevance, originality, coherence and formal accuracy. Anonymous reports are sent "regardless of whether the outcome is positive or negative, or in the case of a preliminary rejection". "The Editorial Board retains ultimate responsibility for the final decision." — [thejournal.exetasis.net](https://thejournal.exetasis.net)
- The ethics page also speaks of not disclosing information to anyone but the author, the referees "and the publisher". Yet the site names no publisher anywhere. — [thejournal.exetasis.net](https://thejournal.exetasis.net)
- Downloaded file, BLIND REVIEW.pdf: this repeats the same two-stage process. The Board will "verify their consistency with the philosophical nature and scientific style of the journal" before double-blind review. — [forauthors.exetasis.net](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- For the call itself, full papers "will undergo the editorial evaluation process established by Exetasis". — [callsforpapers.exetasis.net](https://callsforpapers.exetasis.net/)
- Downloaded file, EDITORIAL GUIDELINES: submissions go in Word format to editorial@exetasis.net. "Contributions selected by the Journal's Editorial Board will undergo a Double-Blind Peer Review process." "The Journal does not provide authors with paper proofs". — [forauthors.exetasis.net](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- None of the pages I read (home, call, journal, board, for-authors, and both PDFs) mentions fees, APCs, open access, a licence, copyright transfer or an AI-use policy. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net); [forauthors.exetasis.net](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)

### Inferences
- Nothing points to pay-to-publish. Because the journal says nothing about access or licence, though, it is unclear whether articles will be free to read or what rights the author keeps.
- With no named reviewers or editors, the author cannot judge who would referee a paper in political philosophy or philosophy of technology.

### Gaps
- UNKNOWN: whether there is an APC or other fee (none is stated).
- UNKNOWN: the open-access model, the licence (for example CC BY) and copyright terms.
- UNKNOWN: any AI-use policy.

## Back issues, register, languages and article length

### Takeaway
I found no back issues. The site lists no issues, articles, volumes or archive, and no index holds any record. The AI and Sovereignty issue looks set to be the journal's first.

### Cited Findings
- The site navigation offers Home, The Journal (the code of ethics), Board members, For Authors, Calls for papers and Contacts. There is no Issues or Archive section. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- The home page says only "Published annually" and lists no issues. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- The domain was created 2026-05-20, and the author PDFs on 2026-05-25. See the WHOIS and metadata findings in the first section. — [forauthors.exetasis.net](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- Crossref, OpenAlex and DOAJ hold no records (see the previous section). — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/sources?search=exetasis)
- Article length limits: "40,000 characters (including notes and spaces) for articles" and "20,000 characters ... for reviews". Each article needs an English abstract of no more than 1,000 characters, five English keywords and an English bio of no more than 700 characters. — [forauthors.exetasis.net, EDITORIAL GUIDELINES PDF](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- The call asks for full papers of "approximately 8,000 words". — [callsforpapers.exetasis.net](https://callsforpapers.exetasis.net/)
- Citations use a humanities footnote style: angle quotation marks for journal titles, place of publication in its original language, and "Translation mine/ours". — [forauthors.exetasis.net, EDITORIAL GUIDELINES PDF](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- The call's topic areas are political sovereignty and AI infrastructures; AI, authority and decision-making; AI, autonomy and individual sovereignty; and rethinking sovereignty, including "machine sovereignty". It welcomes political philosophy, philosophy of technology, ethics, epistemology, philosophy of law, and social and political theory. — [PhilEvents 154909](https://philevents.org/event/show/154909)

### Inferences
- The two length rules conflict. Forty thousand characters including spaces is roughly 6,000–7,000 English words, while the call asks for about 8,000 words. The author would need to ask which applies, and I did not ask, because the task forbade contact.
- The register looks Italian-humanities and continental-leaning: the Socratic framing, the conventions of Italian university presses and the emphasis on peoples and borders all point that way. This is an inference from the guidelines alone, since no published articles exist to confirm it.

### Gaps
- No representative articles exist to name. UNKNOWN: the register, quality and length of published work, because nothing has been published that I could find.

## Red flags and signs of legitimacy

### Takeaway
This is not a predatory pay-to-publish venue: it charges no stated fees and claims no indexing. It is, however, a brand-new journal with no ISSN, no back issues and no named, checkable editors or publisher. A slot spent here buys a first-issue publication with no indexing, no DOI and, as things stand, no ANVUR standing. That is a weak return for a full 8,000-word paper, though a low-cost one if the abstract alone is the bet.

### Cited Findings
Red flags:
- The domain was registered 2026-05-20, five months before this check (WHOIS, see the first section). — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- There are no back issues or archive. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)
- There is no ISSN or DOI, and DOAJ, Crossref and OpenAlex hold no records. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/api/search/journals/exetasis); [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/journals?query=exetasis&rows=5); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/sources?search=exetasis)
- The board page lists three names with no affiliations, none of whom I could trace to philosophy. — [boardmembers.exetasis.net](https://boardmembers.exetasis.net)
- The ethics page mentions "the publisher", yet the site names none. — [thejournal.exetasis.net](https://thejournal.exetasis.net)
- The newsletter runs on systeme.io, a marketing platform. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net)

Signs in its favour:
- The journal states no fees or APCs and makes no false indexing claims. — [exetasis.net](https://exetasis.net); [callsforpapers.exetasis.net](https://callsforpapers.exetasis.net/)
- It states a full COPE-based ethics code and a double-blind review policy, with reports sent on every outcome. — [thejournal.exetasis.net](https://thejournal.exetasis.net)
- Its detailed style guide follows the conventions of Italian academic humanities. — [forauthors.exetasis.net](https://forauthors.exetasis.net)
- The call is listed on PhilEvents, though anyone can post a listing there. — [PhilEvents 154909](https://philevents.org/event/show/154909)

### Inferences
- The profile fits a sincere student or early-career start-up journal better than a predatory operation. There is no money ask and no fake metrics, and the guidelines are careful in the Italian manner. Even so, it is unproven: no editor of standing has put their name to it.
- The 8,000-word "by invitation" model, with no dates and no stated reviewers, leaves the author carrying the risk. The paper could wait for a first issue that may slip or never appear.
- Before committing anything beyond an abstract, the author would want written answers to four questions: who the editor-in-chief is and where they are affiliated; whether an ISSN application is pending; what the open-access and licence terms are; and when notification, the full-paper deadline and publication fall. Asking them requires contacting the journal, which I did not do.

### Gaps
- UNKNOWN: whether an ISSN application is pending.
- UNKNOWN: ANVUR status. I did not check the list directly, and with no ISSN the journal cannot appear on it.
- UNKNOWN: whether any established scholar is involved. None is named.
