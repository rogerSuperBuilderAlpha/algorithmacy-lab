## Q1. Rouvroy & Berns (2013): does algorithmic government operate without subjects, through infra-individual data and supra-individual profiles?

### Takeaway
Yes, in their own words. But the thesis is narrower than the slogan. Rouvroy and Berns say algorithmic governmentality "produces no subjectivation" and runs from infra-individual data to supra-individual profiles (pp. 173–174). They then explicitly reject the "desubjectivation" reading (pp. 175–176, 180). The problem is a *scarcity of occasions to become a subject*: the system leaves no place or grip ("ni lieu, ni prise") for a reflexive subject who could legitimate or resist it (p. 181). Their remedy is "le commun", not individual rights, which they call "aussi inefficaces que mal fondées" (as ineffective as they are ill-founded, p. 193).

- **Supra-individual correlation produces no people (p. 191):** "Seules comptent les relations entre les données, qui ne sont que des fragments infra-individuels, miroitements partiels et impersonnels d'existences quotidiennes que le datamining permet de corréler à un niveau supra-individuel, mais qui ne dessine aucun dépassement de l'individu, aucun peuple donc." — [p. 191](https://pure.unamur.be/ws/files/54965417/7413.pdf)
- **"Multiple without alterity" (p. 192):** "chaque sujet est lui-même une multitude, mais il est multiple sans altérité, fragmenté en quantité de profils qui tous, se rapportent à « lui-même »". - **Remedy: the common, not person-centred rights (p. 193):** "plutôt que d'en revenir à des approches personnologiques (dont l'individualisme possessif des régimes juridiques de protection des données est tout à fait exemplaire), qui seraient aussi inefficaces que mal fondées, l'enjeu fondamental […] est « le commun », entendu ici comme cet « entre », ce lieu de com-parution dans lequel les êtres sont adressés et se relatent les uns aux autres dans toutes leurs dissymétries, leurs « disparations »." — [p. 193](https://pure.unamur.be/ws/files/54965417/7413.pdf)
- They place the shift in Foucault's third model of power, the security apparatus, and quote *Sécurité, territoire, population* (Foucault 2004, pp. 48–49) in a footnote on p. 175 — [p. 175 n.](https://pure.unamur.be/ws/files/54965417/7413.pdf)

### Inferences
- **For the CFP's "produces subjects" framing:** Rouvroy and Berns are the counter-thesis. Algorithmic power bypasses the subject and does not produce one. They hedge carefully, though. On their account it starves subjectivation without destroying the subject, and it coexists with "hyper-subjects". An abstract should cite pp. 173–174 *and* p. 180, so it is not caught over-reading the slogan.
- **Strongest support for "competence alone confers no standing":** p. 175. Power addresses people through profiles "whatever their capacities of understanding, will, expression". A highly algorithmate actor is still addressed as a profile, so competence raises no claim. On p. 181 the system gives "no place, no grip" to a subject who could "legitimate or resist" it. In the author's terms, competence lacks an addressee. That supplies the gap that a political form ("coordinative sovereignty") would fill.
- **The challenge to answer:** algorithmacy could be read as exactly the "rapport" with one's statistical double that p. 181 says is missing, which would make it a counter-subjectivation. But footnote 15 warns that a reflexive self-tracker "refines a presupposed subject" rather than producing one, and that its reflexivity is foreign to the "non-rapport". An abstract can turn this into the thesis: algorithmacy builds the relation, and without an institution that relation stays private refinement, which is why standing needs a political form.
- **Fit with the political-form move:** their p. 193 refusal of "personnologiques" (individual data-rights) approaches in favour of "le commun" lines up with the author's turn from individual competence to a collective form. The author should name the difference: Rouvroy and Berns want a relational common built on disparateness, not a sovereignty. "Coordinative sovereignty" therefore needs a sentence on why a sovereign form rather than a commons.
- Algorithmacy as defined, "infer how [the intermediary] evaluates both sides", presupposes a stable enough evaluator to model. Rouvroy and Berns stress real-time, viral self-refinement (p. 174, "plus on s'en sert, plus le système algorithmique s'affine"). The third component of algorithmacy, tracking shifting rules, answers this directly.

### Gaps
- I did not read the English translation, so I have no English page numbers or published English wording. The abstract should quote the French with its locator, or the English translation should be checked first.
- Rouvroy (2011), cited on p. 174, and Rouvroy's later English pieces (e.g., in Hildebrandt & de Vries eds., 2013, *Privacy, Due Process and the Computational Turn*) were not checked this session.

## Q2. Foucault ("The Subject and Power", *Discipline and Punish*, "Technologies of the Self"): subjectivation and resistance inside power. Does this support or undercut "competence confers no standing"?

### Takeaway
Both. Foucault supports the claim in one sense: freedom, and with it the capacity to read the field and act within it, is the *precondition and permanent support* of power, not a title against it (p. 790). Competence is the very thing government works on. He also names struggles against power "linked with knowledge, competence, and qualification" (p. 781), which warns that algorithmacy can become a new privilege. He undercuts the move from competence to *sovereignty*, however. His programme is to "refuse what we are" and to promote "new forms of subjectivity" (p. 785), with resistance immanent to every power relation (p. 794), not standing conferred by a sovereign form. "Technologies of the Self" defines governmentality as the contact between technologies of domination and of the self (p. 19). That definition makes algorithmacy, read as a self-technique, the hinge of governmentality rather than its outside.

### Inferences
- **Supports "competence alone confers no standing":** on Foucault's account, the capacity to act within a structured field (p. 790) is what government needs. It is power's "permanent support", not a counter-title. Algorithmacy is that capacity specialised to an opaque intermediary. The better someone reads the field, the more fully government "structures the possible field of action" through them. So competence is *material for* power, not standing *against* it. This is the cleanest Foucauldian warrant for the author's claim.
- **Supports a second, sharper point:** p. 781 lists struggles against power "linked with knowledge, competence, and qualification". A competence that sorts actors into the algorithmate and the rest creates a new "privilege of knowledge", so a political form must not rest standing on competence either. This pairs well with Petre et al. (2019) and Ziewitz (2019) in Q5, where the platform decides which competence is legitimate.
- **Undercuts the move to sovereignty:** Foucault's remedy is refusal and new subjectivities, liberating "us both from the state and from the type of individualization which is linked to the state" (p. 785), not a new sovereign form. Resistance is always present in power (p. 794), so in one sense the competent actor already has an "agonistic" position. An Exetasis reviewer could press this: why does standing need *sovereignty* if agonism is immanent? A defensible answer is that p. 794 resistance consists of "means of escape" and "points of possible reversal", not *recognised* claims, so standing names what agonism lacks.
- **Technologies of the self:** algorithmacy fits definition (4), self-operations on one's conduct, with skills *and* attitudes (p. 18). Where it meets platform technologies of domination is, by Foucault's p. 19 definition, governmentality itself. Calling algorithmacy "a technology of the self that algorithmic governmentality needs and cannot certify" is a defensible formulation (my inference).
- **Tension with Rouvroy and Berns:** for Foucault, power produces subjects through self-knowledge (p. 781, second meaning). For Rouvroy and Berns, algorithmic government no longer needs that (pp. 173–174, 181). Algorithmacy sits in the gap: it is self-knowledge about how one is being read, which the system neither demands nor recognises.

## Q3. Deleuze, "Postscript on the Societies of Control" (1992): "dividuals"

### Takeaway
Deleuze supplies the vocabulary the CFP invites. Control replaces the signature/number pair with "a code", and "the code is a password … that mark[s] access to information, or reject[s] it". Individuals become "dividuals". Deleuze ends by asking whether unions can adapt or "will give way to new forms of resistance", and he leaves it "up to" the young to "discover what they're being made to serve". In other words, he poses discovery (a reading competence) as necessary, and he places resistance in collective institutions, not in the discovery itself.

### Cited Findings
Source: Deleuze, G. (1992). Postscript on the societies of control. *October*, 59, 3–7. JSTOR stable https://www.jstor.org/stable/778828. **Full text read** (Purdue Deleuze archive PDF: https://deleuze.cla.purdue.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Deleuze_Gilles-Postscript-on-the-Societies-of-Control.pdf). The copy gives the publication history (L'Autre Journal, May 1990; *Pourparlers*, 1990; *October* 59, Winter 1992, pp. 3–7) but **no October page numbers**, so I give section locators. The October page for each quotation is UNVERIFIED.

The numerical language of control is made of codes that mark access to information, or reject it. We no longer find ourselves dealing with the mass/individual pair. - **Resistance and institutions (§3):** "One of the most important questions will concern the ineptitude of the unions: tied to the whole of their history of struggle against the disciplines or within the spaces of enclosure, will they be able to adapt themselves or will they give way to new forms of resistance against the societies of control?" … "Many young people strangely boast of being "motivated"; they re-request apprenticeships and permanent training. It's up to them to discover what they're being made to serve, just as their elders discovered, not without difficulty, the telos of the disciplines. The coils of a serpent are even more complex that [sic] the burrows of a molehill." — [§3](https://deleuze.cla.purdue.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Deleuze_Gilles-Postscript-on-the-Societies-of-Control.pdf)

### Inferences
- "The code is a password … that mark[s] access … or reject[s] it" gives the author a precise image: under control, standing becomes *access*, granted or refused by modulation. Algorithmacy is the skill of producing the right password, which wins passage but not standing. That is the author's distinction in Deleuze's terms.
- Deleuze's "it's up to them to discover what they're being made to serve" frames discovery, a reading competence, as the first task. His open question about unions puts *resistance* in collective forms that have to be reinvented. This reads naturally as "competence is necessary; an institution is needed for it to count", and supports the author's step to a political form (my inference, not Deleuze's claim).
- "Dividual" pairs with Rouvroy and Berns's "infra-individual" (pp. 174, 191). Rouvroy and Berns cite Deleuze repeatedly (e.g., p. 168 quoting Deleuze 1968 on "mise en communication des disparates"; p. 192 on the "peuple manquant").

## Q4. Cheney-Lippold (2011, 2017), Couldry & Mejias (2019), Zuboff (2019), Hildebrandt (2015), Bucher (2017), Cobbe (2021)

### Takeaway
This cluster divides on the CFP's question. Cheney-Lippold argues that algorithms *produce* identities, "soft biopolitics", by inferring categories that then regulate the person. Bucher argues that users' *imaginaries* feed back and "mould" the algorithm. Zuboff and Couldry and Mejias describe extraction and behavioural modification, in which the subject is raw material. Cobbe reads algorithmic moderation through Foucauldian power, but on a platform-as-agent footing that an agentic (republican) account can use. All entries here are abstract-level unless stated, so none should carry a page-located quotation in the abstract.

## Q5. Workers and users who learn to "read" algorithms: resistance, compliance, or self-subjection?

### Takeaway
The literature reads it all three ways, often in the same study. The dominant finding is *co-constitution*: competent reading and gaming both resist control and reproduce it. Kellogg et al. name "algoactivism" (resistance). Bucher, Schou and Waldkirch find "anticipatory compliance" that "co-construct[s]" algorithmic power (self-subjection). Rahman finds "reactivity", either experimenting or withdrawing, inside an "invisible cage". Ferrari and Graham find "fissures" that show power is "only ever partial" but rarely "render algorithms impotent", and they place the lever in collective sense-making and regulation. Magalhães calls one version "algorithmic ethical subjectivation" that can be "potentially harmful". Petre et al. and Ziewitz show that the *platform* decides which reading counts as legitimate optimisation and which as illegitimate "gaming". Read together, these findings support "competence alone confers no standing".

Control works through the "6 Rs" (restricting, recommending, recording, rating, replacing, rewarding). — [AOM](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/annals.2018.0174)

### Inferences
- **Resistance, compliance or self-subjection?** In this literature the answer is "all three, depending on who reads and whether the reading is collective". Individual reading tends toward anticipatory compliance (Bucher et al.), withdrawal (Rahman; Magalhães 2022) or self-harmful ethical subjectivation (Magalhães 2018). Reading becomes resistance mainly when it is *collective* (Ferrari & Graham, p. 824) or when it routes through the counterpart (Cameron & Rahman). Bonini and Treré's tactical/strategic split says the same thing in other words: competence without institutional position stays tactical.
- **The gaming line shows standing directly:** Petre et al. and Ziewitz show that the platform alone decides whether an inference-and-encoding move counts as legitimate optimisation or illegitimate gaming. The competent actor has no standing to contest that classification. This is the strongest empirical evidence for "competence alone confers no standing".
- Ferrari and Graham's p. 827 call for regulation ("regulated into those systems") concedes, from inside resistance studies, that fissures do not become standing without external institutional design.

- I did not check Rahman's body text for whether he frames experimentation as resistance or compliance. The abstract uses "reactivity", which is neutral.

## Q6. Has anyone framed algorithmic literacy or competence as counter-subjectivation, or argued that literacy-type competences historically needed political institutions to confer standing?

### Takeaway
I found no verified source that explicitly frames *algorithmic literacy* as *counter-subjectivation* in Foucault's sense. The nearest are Magalhães (2018), whose "algorithmic ethical subjectivation" is the opposite, potentially self-harmful, and Pangrazio and Selwyn's "data tactics". The canonical warrant for counter-subjectivation is Foucault's own call to "promote new forms of subjectivity" (1982, p. 785), so the author's framing appears open, though only to the limits of this search. The historical point, that literacy did not by itself confer standing, is well supported by the literacy-studies cards (Graff, Street, Collins and Blot, Paglayan, Vee). It is also supported dramatically by U.S. voting literacy tests: institutions withheld standing from literate people at will, until the Supreme Court and Congress intervened.

### Inferences
- **Counter-subjectivation seems to be an open framing, though only as far as this search went.** No verified source calls algorithmic literacy a counter-subjectivation. Foucault 1982 p. 785 ("promote new forms of subjectivity through the refusal of this kind of individuality") plus Rouvroy and Berns p. 181 (no "rapport" with the statistical double) give the author a two-source warrant for the claim. Magalhães (2018) is the necessary foil, since his users' algorithm-reading produced self-harm, not emancipation.
- **The literacy-test case is the cleanest historical illustration of "competence alone confers no standing".** Literacy was formally the qualification, yet registrars with "virtually uncontrolled discretion" withheld the vote from "the most brilliant man". It took a court and a statute, political institutions, to turn competence into standing (and then to abolish the competence test altogether). The structure maps closely onto platform "gaming" adjudication (Petre et al.; Ziewitz): an intermediary with unreviewable discretion decides whether a demonstrated competence counts. Use with care. The analogy is structural, and the racial-domination history has to be stated, not borrowed for effect.
- Paglayan and Graff cut the other way against any lineage story in which literacy *produced* democratic standing. Literacy spread for order and integration, and standing came from political struggle and institutions. That supports the author's claim and warns against an oracy → literacy → algorithmacy narrative that implies competence leads to sovereignty.
