## Call
- **Title on the journal page:** AI and Sovereignty: Philosophy, Politics, Law, Economics and Sociolinguistics
- **Journal:** *Exetasis*. The journal page calls it "an independent journal"; PhilEvents calls it "an independent philosophy journal".
- **PhilEvents contact:** Simone Dipasquale, through the site's contact form.

## Requirements (verbatim, journal page)
- "Authors are invited to submit an abstract of 300–500 words, accompanied by a brief biographical profile and institutional affiliation."
- "Abstracts should clearly present the research question or problem addressed, the main argument, and, where appropriate, the relevant theoretical, conceptual, or methodological framework."
- "Deadline for abstract submissions: December 20, 2026."
- "Abstracts should be sent to the Exetasis editorial board at: editorial@exetasis.net"
- "Please indicate the following in the subject line of the email: CFP — AI and Sovereignty"
- "Selected abstracts will subsequently be invited to submit a full paper of approximately 8,000 words. Full submissions will undergo the editorial evaluation process established by Exetasis."
- "The CFP welcomes contributions in Italian, English, German, French, and Spanish."

PhilEvents words the second requirement as "the philosophical question or problem addressed, the main argument, and, where appropriate, the relevant theoretical framework."

## The eight themes (journal page), with the bullets nearest the lab's work
1. **AI and the Philosophy of Sovereignty:** "technological mediation and autonomy"; "philosophical approaches to technological dependence"; "the possibility and limits of concepts such as 'machine sovereignty' or 'algorithmic sovereignty.'"
2. **AI, Politics, and the Transformation of Sovereignty:** "AI and the transformation of the relationship between public and private power"; "algorithmic mediation of public discourse"; "technological dependence and political autonomy."
3. **AI, Law, Regulation, and Responsibility:** "transparency, explainability, and procedural fairness"; "the relationship between public regulation and private technological governance."
4. **AI, Economics, Markets, and Technological Power:** "AI and the distribution of technological rents"; "AI and the transformation of labor and production"; "the political economy of foundation models."
5. **AI, Language, and Sociolinguistic Sovereignty:** not ours.
6. **AI, Individual Autonomy, and the Production of Subjects:** "human agency and automated systems"; "the relationship between individual autonomy and technological dependence."
7. **AI, Knowledge, Communication, and Epistemic Sovereignty:** "algorithmic selection, classification, and visibility."
8. **AI Governance and New Forms of Sovereignty:** "sovereignty beyond the territorial state"; "distributed and transnational forms of sovereignty"; "the possible emergence of new … concepts for understanding power in the age of artificial intelligence."
