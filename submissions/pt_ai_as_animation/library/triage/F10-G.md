## F10 Triadicity: lab criterion, Simmel, many animators (c9)

Both FACETS anchors turned out to be in the registry already. Stovel & Shaw (2012) is `coordsov/stovel2012brokerage`
(abstract depth; the search tool tagged the hit `in_registry`), and Nissenbaum (1996) is
`slacker/nissenbaum1996accountability` (metadata-only, no DOI in the registry row; the record is
10.1007/bf02639315). Under FACETS rule 3 both become pointers, which frees F10's two new slots. I gave them to the
two candidates the brief named, and both are stronger on c9 than the anchors they replace, because each puts the
**makers** inside the triad.

Clark & Fischer's target article says people interacting with social robots recognise "three classes of agents –
the characters depicted, the intended recipients of the depictions (those who view or interact with the robots),
and the authorities responsible for the robots (the designers, makers, and owners)" (abstract). That is §4.4's
studio-between-viewer-and-character, stated for robots, in a depiction theory that also names "ventriloquist
dummies, hand puppets, virtual assistants" (abstract), so it serves §5 too. Schumann & Du, inside *P&T*, already
claim "the triadic interaction of end user, development team, and product leads to a successful human–machine
interaction" (abstract): the venue has seen a development-team triad, which c9 must cite and distinguish (their
third is information-pragmatic, built on "triangulation"; the lab's criterion asks for binding determination and
an interest of the third's own).

The counter stays stocked by pointers: L. Stark's many animators and Cameron et al.'s deployer triad (dxda), and
Halevy's brokerage review (peircean). If §4.4 leans on "many hands", Nissenbaum is a metadata-only pointer; the lead
can add it as a fifth upgrade, or promote Thompson (1980) from reserve.

## F11 LLMs as characters and role play (c12, c10)

The three FACETS anchors all confirmed, none is in the registry, and all are open. Each reads the character the user
meets, which is what c12 positions against, but two of them reach toward the making, and the cards should record how
far. Bernini distinguishes "designed and emergent fictionality" (abstract); Magee et al. study InstructGPT by
"detailing the layers of its construction" and read the model as "the condensation of often competing social
desires ... which must then be regulated and repressed" (abstract). Neither frames construction as craft. Shanahan
(2024) is the anti-anthropomorphic counter: such agents "can create an illusion of being in the presence of a
thinking creature" (abstract).

The S2 hits for F11 were noise (chatbot applications, role-play benchmarks), apart from the anchors and registry hits.
The dxd triage had Bernini as G reserve R4, uncarded; it is carded here.

## F12 Venue: *Philosophy & Technology* 2019–2026 (c12, c7, c5)

I chose the ten by what each forces the paper to say, not by citations. The journal's conversation runs on four lines,
and the shortlist stocks each:

- **Life and organism inside the venue (c4, c12).** Rolla (2026) concludes that "creating a genuinely cognitive
  artificial intelligence would require achieving artificial life first" (abstract): the venue already joins AI to
  artificial life, in the embodiment sense the paper sets aside. Gubelmann (2024) holds that agents "need to be
  autonomous organisms while LLMs are heteronomous mechanisms", yet sees LLMs taking "a first step away from
  mechanistic artificiality to autonomous self-constitution" (abstract): the organism/mechanism line c4 must cross.
  Coeckelbergh (2021) proposes "to understand AI as narrative, as process, or as the emergent outcome of processes
  and narratives" (abstract), the venue's process ontology of AI. Rolla also carries a G tag.
- **Who stands behind the machine (c9).** Arora (2024): some machine utterances are "'proxy assertions', made on
  behalf of the designers and/or deployers" (abstract). Popa (2021), by title, holds that "human goals are
  constitutive of agency in AI"; the record has no abstract, so the read must establish the argument. Schumann & Du
  (F10) is the third.
- **The stance and its errors (c5).** Floridi (2026), "semantic pareidolia" (no abstract on either record), and
  Howdle (2026), whose "pragmatic mode" adopts "the intentional stance without ontological commitment, yet still
  risk[s] error when another interpretive strategy would better serve" (abstract). A vital stance is exactly such a
  strategy, so these are the reviewer the paper will meet. Bottazzi Grifoni & Ferrario (2025) name the illusion
  "bewitchment" (c8, c7).
- **Opacity (c7).** von Eschenbach (2021; 924 citations on OpenAlex; closed, no abstract) and Zednik (2021; green on
  arXiv). F7 already holds Zerilli et al. (2019) from this venue and seven opacity cards, so I kept two here and moved
  Vaassen to reserve.

Floridi 2023 and 2025 are counted under F3 and are pointers here, as `triage/F1-F4.md` asked. I did not card
Villalobos & Ward (2015), offered by F1–F4: it predates the facet's 2019 window, and F2 already carries the
autopoiesis line with nine cards. It heads the reserve, to be carded once, in F2 or here, if the lead wants it.

### Verdict

**(A) I found no work that frames the development of an AI system (the model together with the harness, infrastructure
and efficiencies built around it) as animation-like craft, in Crossref and arXiv (six `search.py` queries each), the
Crossref *P&T* ISSN filter, Consensus (30 + 50 + 50 prior, and two new queries of 50), three targeted arXiv queries,
two targeted Crossref queries, and the record lookups above; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar list searches failed (429)
and are missing.** The nearest works put animation craft inside the building of an AI-driven character's expressive
behaviour (Ribeiro & Paiva 2012, 2015; Curtis et al. 2022), map animation onto AI architecture and conclude illusion
(Govind 2026), or call AI systems puppets credited to their builders and treat that as the defect to overcome
(Hayes-Roth 2006).

**(B) I found no work that argues AI development in general is better conceived as making artificial life, in the
same sources.** But the engineering side already practises it: Masumori et al. (2026) build "living AI" by surrounding
a stateless LLM with memory, perception, evaluation and "a budget-based metabolism"; Sun, Hong & Zhang (2025) turn
"artificial life into implementable design requirements" for an LLM wrapper; Karelin et al. (2025) propose designing
infrastructure-level indeterminism in as a basis for "lifelike autonomy"; Zhang (2026) proposes agents "progressively
grown". The philosophy runs the other way: Rolla (2026, *P&T*) makes artificial life a precondition of AI cognition,
and Gubelmann (2024, *P&T*) and Barandiaran & Almendros (2025) deny LLMs the organism's self-constitution.

So c12 survives as a conceptual claim, not as a claim that nobody has built life around an LLM. The wording should be:
**AI development as a craft of animation, argued as a claim about what AI is: not the interface (Stark), not the
illusion (Govind), not an artificial-life experiment (Masumori et al.).** Abstracts were read, not full texts; the
cards for #1 and #2 must confirm that neither body makes the conceptual claim, and S9 must open the MacCormick chapter.
