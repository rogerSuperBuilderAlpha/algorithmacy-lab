# Floridi (2023), AI as agency without intelligence

## What it argues

Luciano Floridi, writing an editorial for *Philosophy & Technology*, argues that large language models
such as ChatGPT are a new form of agency that works without intelligence. He opens with a chain of "old
ideas" (texts in the dictionary, words in the alphabet, letters in binary code, code in electromagnetic
properties) and calls the fifth revolutionary: AI now handles those properties to produce texts often
indistinguishable from human ones. LLMs, he says, "do not think, reason or understand"; they work
statistically on form, not meaning. He grants their abilities (a ChatGPT summary of *The Divine Comedy*)
and catalogues their limits: they are fragile, fail "catastrophically", invent references and stumble on
trivial inference. He finds the "stochastic parrot" analogy only partly apt, because LLMs synthesise, and
prefers two others: the lazy student who stitches references into a coherent essay, and the trickster in
Manzoni's *The Betrothed* who pulls endless ribbon from his mouth. He lists the ethical, legal, labour,
educational and environmental stakes, then names the philosophical questions, among them "LEGO-like AI
systems" with LLMs as a bridge between them ("confederated AI"). His conclusion is that reinforcement
learning gives these systems a successful agency that can learn and improve "without having to be
intelligent". Humanity, he says, has always treated agency of this kind as natural or supernatural; now
humans have made it, as "demiurges". "We have liberated agency from intelligence", and the new workers
will be "shepherds of AI systems". He names the result *Agere sine Intelligere*. The piece is short and
programmatic; it argues by analogy and example, not by sustained analysis.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c5** (intelligence frame versus life frame is a stance choice): Floridi is the venue's own counter,
  and what he would object to is the binary. He rejects the intelligence frame for LLMs, as the paper
  does, but he explains their success with a third category, agency, and never appeals to life. On his
  account the paper's choice between "intelligence" and "life" is a false pair: the right frame is
  artificial agency, which is neither. His remark that humanity has always seen learning agency as
  "natural or even supernatural", and that this agency is "alien to any culture in any past", gives a
  sharper objection, which is my inference, not his words: a life frame repeats the old habit of
  assimilating learning agency to the living, the habit Floridi says this agency breaks. The source also
  cuts the other way. His "shepherds of AI systems", in charge of an agency that fails catastrophically,
  sits close to the life frame's "tend" and "expect failure"; the paper can cite him for the stance it
  adopts while disputing his label.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, a claim about what AI is): Floridi offers a
  rival answer to the question c12 answers, in the paper's own venue: AI *is* agency without
  intelligence. The paper must say why "animation" improves on "agency". Two contrasts are available. His
  agency belongs to the model, trained by reinforcement learning, which is the model-centrism c2 attacks;
  yet his "confederated AI" passage puts LLMs inside modular systems, so he is not a pure model-centrist,
  and the paper should not paint him as one. And his trickster analogy treats the output as something that
  only "looks to us as" information, which is closer to the illusion reading c12 sets aside (Govind 2026)
  than to a making. Floridi says nothing about animation, life or development as a craft; any claim that
  he denies those readings stretches him.

## Caution

- **Genre.** The PDF labels the piece "EDITORIAL"; Crossref types it journal-article. It is a short
  position piece without an abstract, not a research article, and a reviewer may weigh it accordingly.
- **Dates.** Crossref: issued 2023-03, online 2023-03-10; the PDF: "Received: 1 March 2023 / Accepted:
  1 March 2023 / Published online: 10 March 2023". No `registered_year` is needed.
- **Licence.** The copy is free to read (bronze) but carries "under exclusive licence to Springer
  Nature"; it is not CC-licensed.
- **Two Floridi papers with one main title.** `floridi2025ai` (*P&T* 38, Article 30) shares the title "AI
  as agency without intelligence" with a different subtitle. Cite with year and subtitle. `floridi2026ai`
  (semantic pareidolia, F12) is a third, separate paper; the pareidolia objection to a "vital stance" is
  that paper's, not this one's.
- **Floridi & Sanders (2004)**, which he cites for artificial agency, is held as
  `designeth/floridisanders2004` (registry, full_text); use that card for the earlier argument.
- **Possible further use, for the author to decide.** The "confederated AI" passage (p. 5 and footnote 10)
  could also support c1 (a working AI is the LLM plus surrounding systems). Triage did not assign c1, so
  `claims` stays [c5, c12]. If c1 takes it, note that Floridi describes LLMs bridging other AI systems,
  not harnesses or VMs.
- **Quotations of ChatGPT** inside the editorial (the DALL-E description, p. 4; the figures) are ChatGPT's
  text, not Floridi's, and should not be quoted as his.
