# Hayes-Roth (2006), Puppetry vs. creationism

## What it argues

This is an abstract card; I read no text of the article. The abstract on the OpenAlex record speaks of "the
author" in the third person, so it is not in the author's own voice. Crossref gives a three-page item (pp. 7–9)
in *IEEE Intelligent Systems* 21(5), October 2006, with the surname Hayes-Roth only.

According to the abstract, AI began by asking what problems the new computing machinery could solve. For its
first fifty years it programmed computers to do tasks only humans could do before, then raced to match or
surpass human performance. It succeeded at math word problems, checkers and chess championships, natural
language understanding, and planning and scheduling. The author calls these "supercapable machines" puppets,
"not to derogate the machines but to respect the importance of the programmers and builders who were actually
responsible for their accomplishments". The field has met its "rate-limiting factor" under several names: the
knowledge-acquisition bottleneck, machine learning, system bootstrapping, artificial life and self-organising
systems. The abstract says these efforts have mostly had limited success, and that their learning "paled in
comparison to the puppeteers' laborious inputs". The title sets puppetry against "creationism" and says AI
"must cross the chasm". The abstract does not say what "creationism" means.

The Crossref record lists three references: Hayes-Roth's own *Hyper-Beings* (Booklocker.com, 2006), Vinge's
1993 "Essay on the Singularity", and Kurzweil's *The Singularity Is Near* (2005). The reference list places
the column in the singularity debate of its day; the abstract itself does not mention the singularity.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c5** (intelligence frame versus life frame is a stance choice): on the abstract's account, the column
  draws a line from inside AI between puppet systems, whose accomplishments belong to their programmers and
  builders, and machine learning and adaptation, which the abstract says have so far "paled in comparison to
  the puppeteers' laborious inputs". It lists "artificial life" among the names under which the field has met
  that limit. The title's
  "creationism" presumably names the other side of the line, but the abstract does not define it, so what lies
  across the chasm is unread. That maps loosely onto §2's intelligence/life split.
  But the use stretches him, and in part cuts against the paper. His line divides *who supplies the
  competence*, not two stances a developer may take. On his terms, the paper's life frame (developers who
  animate, tend and nurture the system) describes puppetry, the condition he says AI must leave. The paper has
  to say whether it accepts his puppet label for developed AI and rejects his call to cross the chasm, or
  whether it reads its own life frame as the crossing.
- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness; puppetry; the LLM makes its own in-betweens): Hayes-Roth is AI's
  own use of the puppet figure, and an early one. §5 can cite him for the claim that the puppet comparison
  already exists in the field. Whether an LLM that produces its own in-betweens has begun to "cross the chasm"
  is the paper's question, not his. The abstract predates LLMs, so any reading of his line onto an LLM is the
  paper's inference.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation): this is a near prior. He describes AI systems as
  puppets whose accomplishments belong to their builders, which is close to treating AI development as a craft
  of animating a figure. The difference, on the abstract, is that he treats puppetry as a limit to overcome.
  The paper treats the craft as the right description of what AI development is. The G check must cite and
  distinguish him. As far as the abstract shows, he makes no claim about harnesses, pipelines or the system
  around a model.
