# Sun & Gualeni (2026), Between puppet and actor

## What it argues

Yuqian Sun and Stefano Gualeni ask what role an LLM-based agent plays in creative work. They start from
Hermann Schmidt's 1954 ladder of tools, machines and automata, and from the classical conditions for an
author: deliberate production of aesthetic effects, and voluntary participation. They hold that today's
agents are neither conscious nor able to choose to take part, so they cannot be authors or true co-creators.

They then offer two metaphors, the puppet (an "inanimate figure made to move by human effort", after Baird)
and the actor, who brings an interpretation of a role while following a director. An LLM agent falls between
them. Their evidence is practice: a GPT-4 chatbot playing "Lily", character.ai and the GPT Store, the
Generative Agents town, ChatDev, the Showrunner TV project, and their own ORIBA study, in which artists
configured chatbots as their original characters. The authors keep the agents' autonomy "ultimately
human-directed", yet they also say the agents improvise, build world models and bring a scene to life. They
also say they do not coin new terms. They cite Luke Stark's reading of LLMs as animated characters, but only
second-hand, through a Daily Nous collection, and Coeckelbergh's "quasi-others". Their chapter ends on the
ethics of labour and data, and on the hope that artists will treat the agents' autonomy as a resource.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness; puppetry; the LLM makes its own in-betweens). Contrast and
  support. Sun and Gualeni give the paper its nearest source for the puppet-versus-actor axis, which is the
  edge §5 needs: a puppet is moved at every moment, an actor interprets. They say outright that an LLM
  "improvises" what a human author once supplied, which fits the paper's point that the LLM makes its own
  in-betweens. They would object to any picture of the LLM as a purely puppeted figure: they call the
  puppet analogy unsuitable because it "does not suitably capture" the autonomy of contemporary LLMs (p. 56).
  If the paper's animation is read as full authorial control of every pose, they would say it fits the
  puppet and misses the actor. The stretch: their puppet is a
  theatre puppet and their question is authorship, not liveness, and they do not mention in-betweening or
  production pipelines.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, argued as a claim about what AI is). Contrast. They
  are a near neighbour in the G search because they borrow performing-arts metaphors for AI and name the
  animator (Mickey Mouse and its animators). They differ from the paper on three points. Their question is
  who counts as an author, not what an AI is or how it is made. They stay on the interface side, where the
  user directs an agent. And they say agents are not conscious or sentient and are "ultimately
  human-directed", so they would resist the paper's life frame and its claim that the studio, not the
  character, decides what the viewer gets (c9). This reading of what they would object to is mine, drawn
  from the quoted passages; they do not discuss the paper's thesis.

## Caution

- **Version.** I read the author pre-print, not the Springer chapter. Wording, figures and pagination may
  differ in the version of record. A reviewer should check every quotation against the published chapter
  before the paper cites it. The first page of the pre-print dates the chapter 2025 and names pp. 49–63.
- **Record corrections applied.** The key is `sun2026between`, not `sun2025between`. DECISIONS.md and
  Crossref give the version of record as a 2026 print chapter (published-print 2026), online 30 July 2025.
  The card therefore sets `year: 2026` and `registered_year: 2025`. The pre-print says "(2025)".
- **Editors.** The Crossref record names no editors, and the pre-print does not name them. The APA string
  omits them, which APA 7 normally requires. The reference builder in S8 should fill them from the book's
  title page. The pre-print gives the volume title as 'Artificial Media: Emerging Trends in Narratives,
  Education and Creative Practice'; Crossref gives only 'Artificial Media'.
- **Stark at second hand.** The authors report Stark's view through a Daily Nous collection, not through his
  FAccT paper. Do not cite Stark through them; use `dxda/stark2024animation`.
- **Surname order.** Crossref and the pre-print agree: Sun first, Gualeni second.
- **AI-assisted text.** The authors say the chapter was itself written with the help of an LLM agent (§'On
  Creative Agency'). That does not change what it says, but a careful reader may ask.
- **What the paper may not claim from it:** that Sun and Gualeni treat AI as animation, or that they address
  development as a craft. Their object is authorship by a user working with an agent.
