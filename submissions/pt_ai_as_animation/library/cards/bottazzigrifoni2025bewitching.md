# E. Bottazzi Grifoni and R. Ferrario (2025), The bewitching AI

## What it argues

Bottazzi Grifoni and Ferrario read human-LLM conversation through the later Wittgenstein. They treat
understanding as a public practice that shows in what speakers do, so they can set aside whether an LLM
"thinks" or is conscious and ask only whether communication happens (§3). Their key move rests on
*Philosophical Investigations* §242: communication needs agreement in definitions *and* in judgments, and
what holds that agreement in place is "constancy", the ability to keep reference points fixed across a
conversation while changing what needs changing. Statistical regularity in word use is not the same thing
(§3). The authors then argue that LLMs lack constancy about negation and contradiction (§4). Humans
can recover from a contradiction, because the parties notice it and repair it (§5). LLM-based systems
cannot, so a contradiction that is harmless between people is destructive in a human-LLM exchange (§6).
They lean on others' benchmarks and studies for the empirical side (a chess counterfactual test, the
MultiChallenge e-reader task, work on negation and hallucination); they run no experiment of their own.

Their answer to why people believe otherwise is "bewitchment" (§7). LLMs model stereotyped contexts well,
and people follow the rules of familiar language games blindly and apply the practical principle that
understanding holds until proven otherwise. The two together produce a feedback loop in which users read
stereotyped answers as meaningful. Because the bewitchment deepens as LLMs improve at the stereotyped
parts of conversation, the authors fear loss of meaningful control. They reject benchmarks as the remedy
and suggest probing for derailment, "akin to an art", in the conclusion (§8).

## What the paper uses it for

- **c8** (co-optation: dependency grows with use; the viewer is co-opted): the card is a contrast source, and
  here is what it would object to. Bottazzi Grifoni and Ferrario agree with part of c8: repeated use
  deepens the pull ("this repeated return inevitably causes us to fall back into bewitchment", p. 23). They
  would reject its frame in three ways. (1) They locate the cause in the user's own habit of following
  language-game rules blindly, plus the model's statistical fit to stereotyped contexts; no maker needs to
  compose the illusion, and nothing in their account resembles the consent that suspension of disbelief
  involves. The paper's reading of co-optation as a made, narrative-bound suspended reality therefore
  adds a maker and a story that they do not need. (2) They call the result an *illusion of
  understanding*, and conclude that "no communication can occur" with LLMs (p. 17). A paper that treats
  the LLM system as a character the viewer is rightly drawn into has to say how it differs from this
  verdict: the character is not claimed to understand. (3) Their remedy is exposure of inconsistency,
  not tending. Their "scripted exchanges" passage (p. 18) is also a bridge: actors "perform understanding",
  and an LLM answering with stereotypes may do the same. That fits Stark's stereotype trait, but the
  authors do not mention animation.
- **c7** (necessary opacity: no frame holds the motion, no component the intelligence): the use stretches
  the source. The authors use opacity only as a reason to adopt a public criterion of understanding ("We
  can observe how an LLM responds, but not its internal processes", p. 5). They do not argue that
  opacity is in principle or that intelligence sits in no component. The paper can cite them for a
  philosophical response to opacity (judge by conduct over time, and by constancy), not for the thesis.
  They would object to c7's last clause if it implied that the composed system has the capacity that
  no part has; their view is that the system lacks it.

## Caution

- **Name.** The family name is the two-word "Bottazzi Grifoni" (Crossref; DECISIONS correction), not
  "Bottazzi". FACETS line 468 and the F12 shortlist wrote "Bottazzi & Ferrario"; those files stay as they are.
- **Title case.** Crossref and the PDF print "The bewitching AI: The Illusion of Communication with Large
  Language Models". The APA string uses sentence case after the colon.
- **Footnote 28 is a direct life-frame rejection, in passing.** It says analogies between humans and
  machines on training, networks or non-determinism "ignore evident differences between these forms of
  life", and adds "assuming LLMs can be considered life at all". It sits in a footnote about intentionality
  and is not argued. The paper may cite it as a Wittgensteinian doubt about the life frame (c5), but may not
  present it as the authors' thesis.
- **Scope.** The argument concerns understanding in multi-turn dialogue, negation and contradiction. It
  does not address liveness, character or production. Its benchmark figures (for example "approximately
  equal to 50% accuracy" for frontier models, p. 17) are dated to early 2025.
- **Homonym risk.** The title echoes Wittgenstein's "bewitchment"; the card concerns this paper only. It is
  not Govind's "illusion" paper (c12).
