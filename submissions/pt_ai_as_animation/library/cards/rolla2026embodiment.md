# Rolla (2026), The embodiment challenge for artificial intelligence

## What it argues

Giovanni Rolla argues from radically embodied, enactive cognition that current AI is not cognitive and
that a cognitive AI would need artificial life first. He opens with a distinction (§2): a system can be a
useful model of cognition without displaying cognition, just as a planetary model helps teach the solar
system without being one. He then grants the weaker claim for now and attacks the stronger. On the problem
of meaning, he reads Jones and Bergen's Turing-test study as evidence that users over-impute meaning to
LLMs, and he rejects Mollo and Millière's teleosemantic solution to vector grounding, because the meaning in
question still comes from human practice and the training goals "depend solely on what tools engineers wish
to develop" (p. 6).

His §3 sets out the enactive view: cognition emerges from a living system's struggle to persist under
precarious conditions, and it changes the organism's structure. Machine learning only updates parameters;
"the network’s structure remains the same" (p. 10). Catastrophic forgetting is his evidence of the gap.

§4 states "the embodiment challenge". AIs have no body of their own and no environment, so they cannot
exchange matter and energy to stay viable, and they do not care about their existence. Rolla then answers
the reply that servers and hardware might be the AI's body. That body faces "a blatant border problem": the
chips, the servers, the cables, the energy, the building? Any line would be arbitrary, whereas a living
body draws its own border by keeping itself alive. He also rejects Chalmers's functionalism as circular.
His conclusion: "creating a genuinely cognitive artificial intelligence would require achieving artificial
life first". He ends on a modest note: AIs are tools we think "with and through" ("thinging", after
Malafouris).

## What the paper uses it for

- **c4** (an organizational account of life fits the system around the LLM): Rolla is the counter c4 must
  answer head-on, and he answers it in advance. The paper's move, that life sits in the organization around
  the model (harnesses, VMs, efficiencies), is close to the reply Rolla anticipates: that "the servers that
  host the AI and all the hardware that is connected to it could be its artificial body" (p. 11). He would
  object that the system has no self-drawn border (where does the harness end?) and that nothing in it
  struggles to persist. The paper must either give a non-arbitrary boundary for "the AI as composed" or
  argue that its organizational account does not need the system itself to be precarious, only its makers'
  continuous work. Either answer departs from Rolla's enactivism and should say so.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, a claim about what AI is): Rolla stakes out the
  nearest venue position on the life side. He agrees that AI and life must be thought together, and his
  conclusion even makes artificial life the precondition of artificial cognition. But he means literal
  artificial life, biological or self-maintaining (he points to xenobots and protocells, p. 13, note 11).
  The paper's "creating AI is better conceived as creating artificial life" must be told apart from
  Rolla's "create artificial life first". Rolla argues about whether AI can be cognitive; the paper argues
  about what kind of making AI development is. Rolla also has nothing to say about the craft or the makers,
  so he does not close the gap.
- **c3** (no consensus definition; defend a theory): Rolla shows how to do what §2 promises. He picks one
  theory (radical embodiment) and defends it, without settling the definition of intelligence. His
  footnote 1 swaps "intelligence" for "cognition" and says why, which the paper can cite as a model of the
  move.

## Caution

- **"Cognition", not "intelligence".** Rolla argues about cognition and says so (footnote 1). The paper's
  life-versus-intelligence frame (c5) should not report him as arguing about intelligence.
- **Two senses of artificial life.** Rolla's "artificial life" is a living, self-maintaining, embodied being.
  The paper's is a frame for a craft. Quoting his conclusion beside the thesis without that distinction would
  misstate him.
- **Dates.** The PDF prints "Received: 16 November 2025 / Accepted: 24 June 2026" and no online date; Crossref
  gives online 2026-06-30 and print 2026-09. The volume year (2026) agrees, so no `registered_year`.
