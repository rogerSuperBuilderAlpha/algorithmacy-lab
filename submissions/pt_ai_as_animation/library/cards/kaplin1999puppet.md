# Kaplin (1999), A puppet tree

## What it argues

This is an abstract card. The abstract in the Crossref record is one sentence, written about Kaplin in the
third person: "Kaplin proposes a new theory of puppet theatre based on distance and ratio—where distance is
the degree of separation between performer and object, and ratio the number of objects compared to the
number of performers." So Stephen Kaplin offers two measures for placing any form of puppet theatre. Distance
says how far the performer stands from the object, and ratio says how many objects each performer works. The
title adds that the result is a "tree", a model for the whole field. The record lists one reference,
Edward Gordon Craig's *The Marionette* (1918). Nothing in the record says how Kaplin draws the tree or which
forms he places where.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness; puppetry): the anchor for §5's placement of an LLM. Kaplin's two
  measures would let the paper say where an LLM sits relative to the puppet and the animated character, in
  place of the sketch's bare claim that "Puppetry and live performance capture may be the closer case"
  (SKETCH §5). The triage reading is that an LLM served to
  millions has an extreme ratio and an unclear distance. That is the paper's application, and it stretches
  the source twice: the abstract defines both measures for a performer and an object in a theatre, and it
  gives no sign that Kaplin extends them to recorded media, software or objects that act between the
  performer's moves. Whether his axes can hold a system that makes its own in-betweens is the paper's
  question, not his answer.
