## What the paper uses it for

- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness; puppetry is the closer case): triage chose Tillis for the F5 counter.
  On that reading, the puppet is seen as object and as living character at once, together with its operator
  (the "visible double"). That reading comes from FACETS and the triage file, not from any record I saw. If
  the book bears it out, Tillis would object to the paper at this point. If the puppet's defining effect is
  that the audience sees the object and its life at the same time, a live system that answers back and shows
  its operator's hand sits nearer the puppet than the drawn cartoon. The paper would then have to defend
  making animation, not puppetry, the governing analogy in §3, rather than leaving puppetry to the limit case
  in §5. Until the book is read, the paper may only say the work exists.
- **c6** (animation is the craft of composing a living character from inert parts): the same objection applies
  from the other side. Puppetry also makes an inert object live. If Tillis's aesthetics fits that description
  better than animation does, c6's choice of craft needs a reason. This rests on the title and subject
  headings alone.
