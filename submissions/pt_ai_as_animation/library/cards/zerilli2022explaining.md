# Zerilli (2022), Explaining machine learning decisions

## What it argues

Abstract only; I have not read the article. John Zerilli says deep networks are widely acknowledged to be
inscrutable, and that Explainable AI (XAI) arose in response. Because of the nature of that opacity, XAI has
had to put interpretability ahead of completeness and even realism, so its explanations are often interpretable
without resting on fuller explanations that are faithful to how the network computes. Others take this as a
shortcoming. Zerilli argues it is "broadly the right approach to the problem".

## What the paper uses it for

- **c7** (necessary opacity: no frame holds the motion, no component the intelligence): contrast. The abstract
  grants the premise, since the opacity is real and comes from "the nature of the opacity in question". It
  would object to the next step if the paper treats opacity as a stopping point. Zerilli holds that a
  partial, interpretable explanation is an adequate response, and that the field has chosen well to give up
  completeness. The paper has to say what the "missing frames" cost beyond what XAI already accepts. The card
  cannot say more: his reasons for "broadly the right approach" sit in the text.
