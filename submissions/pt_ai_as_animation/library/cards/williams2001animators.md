# Williams (2001), The animator's survival kit

## What it argues

This is an abstract card; I read no text of the book. The only description I saw is the publisher's blurb that
the Library of Congress holds for the edition record (LCCN 2003446095). The Open Library edition record
OL3753630M gives Richard Williams as author, Faber, London, 2001, x + 342 pages, ISBN 0571202284 and
0571212689.

The blurb presents Richard Williams as an Academy Award-winning animator who worked on *Who Framed Roger
Rabbit?* and links Disney's hand-drawn animation to computer animation such as *Toy Story*. It says the book
grew from his master classes in the United States and Europe and sets out "the underlying principles of
animation" for every kind of animator, from beginner to expert and from classical to computer animation.
According to the blurb, Williams urges readers to "invent but be believable", illustrates his points with
hundreds of drawings, and distils the masters' secrets "into a working system". The blurb is marketing copy. It
names no chapter, technique or term.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c6** (animation is the craft of composing a living character from inert parts): Williams is the
  practitioner's manual, so he supports c6 as evidence that the craft exists and can be taught as a "working
  system" of principles. The triage file wants him for timing and spacing, that is, how in-betweens are placed
  between keys. Nothing I read mentions timing, spacing, keys or in-betweens. Using him for those terms, or for
  §3's "decoding = in-betweening" row, stretches the source until a copy is read. "Invent but be believable"
  sits close to the paper's life frame (believability as the test of a living character), but that phrase
  comes from the blurb's report of his advice. Its context in the book is unread.
