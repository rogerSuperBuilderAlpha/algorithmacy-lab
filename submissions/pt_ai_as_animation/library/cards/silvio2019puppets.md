## What the paper uses it for

- **c6** (animation composes a living character from inert parts): the paper takes its definition of animation
  from Silvio, through L. Stark. As the dxda `stark2024animation` card quotes it (unchecked this run), the
  definition is the projection of human qualities "outside of the self, and into the sensory environment".
  That is projection by maker and viewer, not composition from parts. Using Silvio for composition stretches
  the definition; whether the book also treats animation as a craft of making is unread.
- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness; puppetry): the title puts puppets first, and Stark reports that
  Silvio sets animation against performance. That contrast is what §5 needs to place an LLM between a
  rendered character and a live performer. On the same report (unchecked this run), Silvio concedes the two
  are hard to separate in practice, which §5 should concede too. Nothing here comes from Silvio's own text.
  [S6 verifier: per the dxda `stark2024animation` card's Caution, Stark cites the "[hard] to separate"
  concession and his other animation-versus-performance lines to his ref. [88], Silvio (2010), a different
  work; only the p. 52 definition is cited to this book ([89:52]). Unchecked this run. Until ch. 1 is read,
  §5 must not credit the concession to this book.]
