# Govind Reddy (2026), The illusion of mind

## What it argues

This card rests on the abstract in the DataCite record only; I have not read the paper.

The author (a single name string, "Govind Reddy"; see Caution) presents "a philosophical framework
that draws a precise structural parallel between" Disney's principles of animation, as formalised in
Thomas and Johnston's *The Illusion of Life*, "and the architecture of modern Artificial
Intelligence". Both, the abstract says, perform one act: "sampling continuous dynamic flow, freezing
it into discrete mathematical units, and resequencing those units to reconstruct the illusion of the
original flow." Disney captured the outward physical flow of life; AI tries to capture the inward
cognitive flow of thought. Just as animation yields the illusion of life and not life, AI yields
"only the illusion of thinking rather than thinking itself". The missing element, "the inbetween
frames of cognition", is emotion "in its individual, embodied, and infinitely varied form". Because
mind is individual and individuality cannot be generalised without being destroyed, AI has
replicated "not mind but a framework of generality: the illusion of the approximation of mind", and
in the end "the illusion of reality". The record gives version 1.0, issued 4 March 2026, on Zenodo,
under CC BY 4.0. Nothing in the record shows peer review.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, argued as a claim about what AI is, "not the
  illusion (Govind 2026)"): the nearest prior on the animation-AI parallel, named in Roger's accepted
  c12 wording. What it would object to in the paper's thesis: Govind Reddy uses the animation
  analogy to *deny* that AI is more than an illusion. On the abstract's reading, animation shows
  that AI is the illusion of mind, so the craft of animation makes appearances, never the thing.
  The paper argues that the system around the LLM is a craft that makes something better conceived
  as artificial life. Govind Reddy would say the analogy proves the opposite: the animator's product
  is by definition not alive, so the life frame inherits the illusion. The paper must answer that
  objection head-on, not only distinguish itself by topic (development versus the product).
- **c7** (necessary opacity: no frame holds the motion, no component the intelligence): contrast on
  the same image. Both use missing in-between frames. Govind Reddy reads the missing in-betweens as
  a *lack* (emotion that AI cannot supply). The paper reads them as where the motion is made, by
  perception and by the system as a whole. The paper can cite the abstract to show the image is
  already in use with the opposite moral, which sharpens c7.
- **Also touches c10** (the LLM makes its own in-betweens): the abstract claims AI lacks the
  in-betweens of cognition. That is a direct counter to SKETCH §5's "an LLM produces its own
  in-betweens", though the two use "in-between" at different levels (cognition versus output).
