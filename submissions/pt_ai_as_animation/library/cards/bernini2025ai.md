# Bernini (2025), AI as character

## What it argues

This card rests on the abstract only, as printed in the Crossref and OpenAlex records. According to that
abstract, Marco Bernini (Durham University) develops "a fictionality-based approach to artificial
intelligence": human–AI interaction unfolds as engagement with a character. He draws on narrative theory,
cognitive science and AI studies, and he examines ChatGPT, Claude and LAURA (modeled on Laura Palmer) as
"fictional objects of relation", emergent agents whose apparent minds arise through interpretive stances,
narrative framing and "phenomenological opacity". He joins Dennett's real patterns and intentional stance to
theories of characterization and fictionality, and he names four illusions (of independent agency,
independent identity, codependency and independent poetics) through which users build minds and probe worlds
with AI. He distinguishes designed from emergent fictionality, and AI "immersivity" from "emersivity". He
grants that these engagements extend cognition and creativity, warns of "counterfeit people", and offers
fictionality as training for "double exposure", holding the real and the fictive together. He ends with
human–AI interaction as "introspection by computation".

## What the paper uses it for

- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, not the interface): Bernini, as his abstract
  describes the article, reads AI from the user's side: the character arises in the user's stance and
  framing. That places him with Stark (2024) on the interface side of the line c12 draws. He would object to
  the thesis that the character is not made in the studio but in the encounter, so a production-side
  account misses where the apparent mind arises. The paper must show that what the makers compose (prompts,
  harnesses, persona files) shapes the character before any user meets it. Whether Bernini's "designed
  fictionality" already covers that is unknown until the article is read.
- **c5** (intelligence frame versus life frame is a stance choice): Bernini uses Dennett's intentional stance
  as the paper plans to use a "vital stance". He is a precedent for treating AI's apparent mind as the yield
  of a stance, which supports the stance framing. He applies it to users, not to makers, so citing him for a
  makers' stance would stretch him.
- **c10** (the analogy breaks at liveness): "emergent fictionality" and the "illusion of independent poetics"
  suggest that Bernini addresses the LLM's own contribution to the character, the analogue of the paper's
  "the LLM makes its own in-betweens". This is inference from two phrases in the abstract; the card claims
  nothing until the article is read.
