# Coeckelbergh (2021), Time machines

## What it argues

Mark Coeckelbergh (University of Vienna) asks what AI *is*, not what it should do. He sets the
usual picture, AI as an object (a computer, a robot, software), against two traditions: Bergson's
and Whitehead's process philosophy, and Ricoeur's narrative hermeneutics. He then names three
relations between AI and time. "The time of AI" covers the stories told about AI, from the
Singularity to a personal story of being misrecognised; by telling them, he argues, people partly
make what AI becomes. "AI in time" treats AI as a data-science process: the steps divide time into
spatial boxes, yet humans live through every step, so "What AI 'is', then is this process, or even
the outcome of the process". The process also shapes the people in it: "The data scientist is
shaped by the data science process." "AI-time" casts AI as a co-narrator and a "time machine": by
classifying, predicting and deciding on past data, it fixes people in their past and shapes their
present and future.

Coeckelbergh then radicalises the process view. AI and humans are not fixed relata; both emerge
from the process. In his manipulation example AI is "the cake, not (just) the mixer". Developers do
not fully control what their AI becomes, much as authors do not control a text's meaning. He marks
three differences from text: AI makes statistical rather than linguistic knowledge, it is wrongly
taken to be hermeneutically neutral, and it faces the past where hermeneutics faces the future. The
normative part says that narratives about AI, the steps of the data-science process, and the time
machine role all carry values, and that "'AI-time' is human work". He ends by calling AI a "lived
technology" ("there is no such thing as an AI-in-itself") and by proposing a moral and political
hermeneutics of AI. The paper is conceptual. It cites Bergson's *élan vital* once, in exposition,
and says nothing about artificial life, animation, organisms or engineering pipelines beyond the
generic data-science steps.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c4** (an organizational account of life fits the system around the LLM): weak support, and the
  use stretches him. Coeckelbergh supplies the move away from AI-as-object toward AI-as-process, and
  a venue precedent for asking what AI is. But his process is Bergsonian becoming and Ricoeurian
  narrative, not biological organization, closure or self-maintenance. He denies that AI is a being
  at all ("no being at all but rather a process"), which leaves no organism to organize. The paper
  can cite him for "AI is the outcome of a process, not a thing", not for any account of life.
- **c6** (animation as the craft of composing a living character from inert parts): indirect, and a
  stretch. He makes developers and data scientists co-makers of what AI becomes, and he speaks of
  "lived technology", narrative and characters (AI "becomes a character in the story" when it
  decides). None of this concerns composing a character from parts; his "character" is a role in a
  plot, not an animated figure. Use him only for the claim that the making of AI is a human,
  temporal practice the maker does not fully control.
- **Note for Fable** (outside the two tags DECISIONS assigns): the fit is closest to **c2**. "AI
  cannot be 'lifted out' of time" and "It is the cake, not (just) the mixer" argue against locating
  AI in a component, which is c2's anti-model-centrism, in the venue's own pages. His "we are not
  fully in control of AI" (developers' interpretations versus what AI becomes) also bears on c7.
  I have not added these tags; the claims list follows DECISIONS.

## Caution

- **Page range.** Crossref gives pp. 1623–1638, and the PDF's running heads run 1623 to 1638. The
  PDF's own first-page header reads "Philosophy & Technology (2021) 34:1623–1628". I take 1623–1638
  from Crossref and the running heads; the header's "1628" looks like a typesetting error.
- **Corrected publication.** The held PDF says "corrected publication 2022". crossref.json has no
  `update-to` or `relation` entry, so the record does not say what was corrected. The copy read is
  the corrected version; quotations should be checked against it, not an earlier download.
- **Dates.** Received 10 June 2021, accepted 8 September 2021, published online 23 October 2021;
  Crossref print date December 2021. Volume year 2021, so no `registered_year`.
- **Not the coordsov key.** DECISIONS notes this is not `coordsov/coeckelbergh2024ai`, a different
  Coeckelbergh work. Howdle (2026) also cites a third, Coeckelbergh (2012), *Growing moral
  relations*; neither is this paper.
- **Typo in the source.** Coeckelbergh writes "GTP-3" (p. 1628). Any quotation that includes it keeps
  it with [sic].
- **What the paper may not claim from it:** any account of life or organism, any claim about
  animation, or any claim about LLMs (the paper predates them in this form and discusses data-science
  classification, prediction and recommendation).
