# Green & Brock (2000), The role of transportation

## What it argues

Abstract card. The full text was not read. The OpenAlex abstract says Melanie Green and Timothy Brock
propose "transportation", absorption into a story, as a way narratives change beliefs. They define it as
imagery, affect and attentional focus together. They build and validate a transportation scale. In Experiments 1,
3 and 4 (N = 97, 274 and 258), the more readers were transported, the more they held beliefs consistent with
the story and the better they judged its protagonists. In Experiment 2 (N = 69), highly transported readers found fewer
false notes in a story. When the authors manipulated transportation with processing instructions, less
transportation meant fewer story-consistent beliefs. Transportation and the related beliefs were generally
unaffected by whether the story was labelled fact or fiction.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c8** (co-optation: dependency grows with use; the viewer is co-opted into the suspended reality of the
  narrative). Support, at abstract level. The abstract makes transportation a measured state that changes what
  a reader believes, which is an empirical counterpart to "coopted into the suspended reality". It also says
  a fiction label did not reduce the effect, so knowing a story is made up does not protect the reader. That
  bears on the SKETCH's Coleridge strain (willing suspension of disbelief). The stretches are large. The
  abstract concerns readers of written stories and says nothing about animation, film or AI. It shows
  absorption changing beliefs within a session, not dependency that grows with repeated use. It treats
  transportation as something readers undergo and can be asked to reduce, so it gives no verdict on whether
  the reader consents. The paper should say "transportation" is borrowed and not claim the study
  measured dependency.
