# Floridi (2025), AI as agency without intelligence

## What it argues

Luciano Floridi frames a dilemma for anyone interpreting AI. One horn expands the concept of intelligence
to admit artificial kinds (the Artificial Realisability of Intelligence, ARI thesis). The other expands the
concept of agency to admit forms that lack cognition, intelligence, intention or mental states (the
Multiple Realisability of Agency, MRA thesis). He takes the second horn and argues that scientific
evidence, common sense, Ockham's razor and a growing literature favour it: AI is "Agency without
Intelligence". He declines to define agency, or intelligence, by necessary and sufficient conditions, and
reads both as adverbial: looking for intelligence means looking for processes that run intelligently, not
for something a system has.

His method is Floridi's own Method of Abstraction (levels of abstraction, LoA, treated as interfaces). He
fixes three observables for agency (interactivity, autonomy, adaptability; §4) and runs them through a
taxonomy: natural agency (rivers), biological agency (dogs), animal social agency (ant colonies),
artefactual agency (smart thermostats), human individual agency and human social agency (corporations)
(§§5–10). §11 compares them and closes by calling Artificial Agency a new form "blending aspects of artefactual
and biological agency" (p. 18). §12 defines it as "a computational, goal-driven form of agency defined by human purposes", with data-driven adaptability but
no consciousness, intelligence, understanding or self-chosen purposes. His example is LLMs such as the GPT
series. §13 adds Social Artificial Agency, or Agentic AI: networks of modules around an LLM "reasoning
engine", with perception and action modules, whose interactions produce behaviour their designers did not
anticipate. The conclusion (§14) calls for "agency-aware architectures", graduated autonomy with human
oversight, and governance built for artificial agents rather than borrowed from animal or human cases.
The paper is conceptual and taxonomic; it reports no study.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c4** (an organizational account of life fits the system around the LLM): Floridi is the venue editor's
  standing rival to any life frame, and he objects to it in three precise ways. (1) He counts biological
  comparisons as a fallacy that reconceiving AI as agency avoids (p. 1). (2) He says artificial agents lack
  the "evolutionary development" of biological agents (p. 18) and that an "unbridgeable gap" separates
  artificial from biological-human agency (p. 19). (3) He places AI's purposes outside it: its goals are
  "defined by human purposes" (p. 18). Against c4 he would say the paper calls life what is only designed,
  bounded agency. The use stretches if the paper claims Floridi as an ally: his multiple realisability is
  about agency, not about life or organization, and the triage note calling MRA "the nearest venue claim to
  c4's organizational reading" goes beyond what the text says. Point (3) may help c9 instead, since a
  studio that sets the purposes is what c9 needs; that tag is not on this card.
- **c5** (intelligence frame versus life frame is a stance choice): Floridi shares the paper's first move,
  rejecting the intelligence frame for AI, and then takes a third option the paper's binary leaves out:
  agency. His objection to c5 would be that "intelligence or life" is a false dichotomy and that agency
  without intelligence explains the same facts with fewer commitments (Ockham, p. 1). A *P&T* reviewer
  is likely to ask why life rather than agency. The paper must answer that, not just cite him. Floridi
  also treats his choice as a matter of the level of abstraction adopted (§2), which is close to c5's
  "stance", but he argues one level is better for a purpose; he does not say the choice is free.
