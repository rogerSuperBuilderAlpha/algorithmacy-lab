# Purves (2014), Stop-motion animation

## What it argues

This is an abstract card. The publisher's abstract in the Crossref record is all I read. It describes a
practical book. Barry Purves, it says, "shows how to make the most of the movement, characters and stories
that typify stop-motion", and the book explores how "camera work, design, colour, lighting, editing, music
and storytelling" come together in "this unique art form". The abstract calls it "this new edition". Each
chapter has a project, covering story and characters, script and storyboard, "constructing puppets" and the
practicalities of film-making. The projects lead the reader through "the creation of your first one-minute
stop-motion animation". The abstract states no thesis about what animation is.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c6** (animation is the craft of composing a living character from inert parts): Purves would supply the
  plainest case, in which a person builds a puppet and moves it by hand, frame by frame. The abstract
  supports only the outline: puppets are constructed, and movement and character are what the craft works
  on. Any claim about how Purves describes giving a puppet life needs the text.
- **c10** (the analogy breaks; the LLM makes its own in-betweens): the contrast. The sketch (§3, §5) takes
  stop-motion as the case where "a person authors every frame". If that holds, stop-motion objects to the
  paper's mapping from the other side: the craft sits in exactly the frame-by-frame work that the mapping
  hands to the model. An LLM that produces its own in-betweens is then less like animation than the thesis
  needs, and the paper must say whether the developer's craft lies in the key poses alone. Neither "a person
  authors every frame" nor any view on in-betweening appears in the abstract; both are the sketch's, and the
  card cannot confirm that Purves holds them.
