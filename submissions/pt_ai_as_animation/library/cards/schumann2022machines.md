# N. Schumann and Y. Du (2022), Machines in the triangle

## What it argues

Nadine Schumann and Yaoli Du want a better concept of information for human-machine interaction than the
sender-receiver model, in which information is a syntactic quantity passed between two symmetrical ends.
They start from triangulation in social cognition, following Davidson and Tomasello: two agents and a shared
world form a three-way relation, which a child needs in order to reach shared intentionality. They add a
second form, in which a third person gives a view "from the outside as observer or witness". They then
carry the triangle into human-machine interaction with help from 4E cognition, Ihde's four human-technology
relations, Malafouris's material engagement theory and Aydin and colleagues' "Active Technological
Environments". Information, on their account, is a "pragmatic action pattern": embodied, generated in
interaction and stabilised in use. Gibson's and Norman's affordances show how designers can make such
patterns perceptible.

The software-development section (§7) names the triad that matters here: end user, development team and
product. The user and the team "share the same intentional goal". They work through iterative, agile
cycles, with reciprocal contact between user and team, and the product is itself developed in that
three-way exchange. The Outlook adds that the roles of consumer and producer blur, since users also produce
software by using it ("prosumers"). The article is a conceptual synthesis. It reports no study and does not
discuss characters, animation, or large language models.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c9** (triadicity, narrowed: the studio determines what the viewer gets; distinguished from Schumann and
  Du by the lab criterion): contrast. The card records exactly what their third party does, and where the
  paper's thesis would meet an objection from them. In their triangle the three corners are the end user,
  the development team and the product. The team and the user share one goal, talk to each other, and
  shape the product together. No corner binds another, none has an interest that sets it against the
  others, and none keeps the other two apart. In the interpersonal version the third corner is the shared
  object or world, or an onlooker who offers a "view from the outside". So the paper cannot cite them as
  a prior case of its own triad. On the lab criterion (binding determination, own interest, separation of
  the parties) their team is a collaborator, not a determining third party. What they would object to in
  the paper: a one-way account in which the studio fixes what the viewer gets. They present the triangle
  against exactly the sender-receiver dyad that such an account resembles, they give the end user a part in
  making the product, and they let consumer and producer blur. The paper's reply has to argue that
  animation differs from agile software: the viewer is shut out of making, and the studio finishes the work
  before the viewer meets it. That reply is the paper's to make; the source does not make it. This is my
  reading of their text; they never address animation or a determining studio.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, argued as a claim about what AI is): venue
  positioning only. A triad of user, team and product already sits in *Philosophy & Technology*, so the
  paper must not present triadicity itself as new. The gap it can claim is the animation reading, and the
  card says what Schumann and Du lack: any treatment of animation, any claim about what the AI is, and any
  role for the team beyond co-developer. The use stretches them if the paper cites them for more than the
  triad's existence.

## Caution

- **Not a foil on the lab criterion alone.** DECISIONS says S5 should flag this card for opus if §4.4 makes it
  the main foil. This card is a sonnet read. It settles what the team does in their triad, which the text
  states plainly, but a stronger test of the objection (whether agile co-development really differs from
  studio production) needs a decision from Roger on how central the foil is.
- **Two triads in one paper.** The authors use "triangulation" for (a) two agents plus a shared object or
  world and (b) two agents plus a third person. §7 then maps user, team and product onto it without saying
  which form applies. Citing "their triad" without naming the pairing invites a misreading.
- **Triage wording.** The triage row paraphrases the point as "a team that co-produces information with the
  user". The text speaks of co-development of the product and of pragmatic information in use; "co-produces
  information" is the triage's gloss. The abstract sentence and "pragmatic action patterns" it quotes are
  accurate.
- **Dates and version.** Received 2 April 2021, accepted 9 March 2022, online 6 April 2022; volume 35(2),
  June 2022 (Crossref). Online and volume year agree. Publisher version of record.
- **Read depth.** `full_text` covers the article (pp. 1-15). The reference list was not read; all
  attributions (Tomasello, Fuchs, Ihde, Aydin and others) are the authors', seen only in their text.
- **Clark and Fischer (2023).** The companion contrast card is `clark2023social`. This card does not compare
  the two.
- **What the paper may not claim from it:** that Schumann and Du describe a determining or separating third
  party, that they treat animation or characters, or that the triad is new in the venue.
