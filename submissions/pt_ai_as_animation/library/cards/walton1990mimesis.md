## What the paper uses it for

- **c8** (co-optation; the viewer is co-opted): the contrast to §4.3, chosen because the triage file expects
  Walton to "drop belief altogether", treating a fiction as a prop in a game of make-believe rather than as
  something one believes. If that is his position, he objects to the paper at a precise point. The sketch
  says the viewer is "coopted into the suspended reality" and reads that through Coleridge's suspension of
  disbelief. On a make-believe account there is no disbelief to suspend: the viewer knows throughout that
  the character is drawn and plays along by choice. Being drawn into a film would then be an act the viewer
  performs, not something done to the viewer, and the analogy with platform dependency, which the user does
  not choose to play, weakens. The objection would sharpen the consent gap that the Coleridge card already
  marks. None of this is checked: the record names no thesis, so "drops belief" and "props" are the triage
