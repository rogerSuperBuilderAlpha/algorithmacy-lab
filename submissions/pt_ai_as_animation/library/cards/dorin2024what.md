# Dorin & Stepney (2024), What is artificial life today, and where should it go?

## What it argues

This is an editorial for the 30th-anniversary issue of the journal *Artificial Life*, set as "Editorial"
in the running head. Alan Dorin and Susan Stepney, the journal's editors, describe the field's history
and scope. They sketch a taxonomy of ALife in five parts: artificial organisms (wet, robotic and virtual),
ecosystems and societies, open-endedness, history and philosophy, and tools and applications (§2). They
summarise the issue's articles (§3) and urge the community to build policy and ethics frameworks before
ALife systems exist, so as not to repeat the "reactive policy path" of AI (§4, p. 10). On the question of
what counts as alive, they report a standing disagreement. The field has "sustained a diverse range of
opinions", biology has never agreed a definition either, and many ALife researchers set the question
aside in favour of whether a system "behave[s] in ways we deem to be relevant and engaging" (p. 3). They
also name the research goal for virtual ALife: the entities should "actually live" and not merely
simulate life (p. 5). They quote Langton's "life as it could be". Dorin and Stepney are editors framing a
field. They run no study and do not defend a definition of life.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c3** (neither life nor intelligence has a consensus definition; defend a theory): support, for the
  life half only. The editors state outright that biology has not agreed "the necessary and sufficient
  characteristics, hence a definition, of life itself" (p. 1), and that ALife researchers still disagree
  on whether something is "really alive" (p. 3). They also list "relationships between intelligence and
  life" among the field's open topics (p. 7), which ties the two halves of c3 together. This is an
  editor's remark, not an argument: it does not count definitions (that is `trifonov2011vocabulary`'s
  job) and does not weigh them (`machery2012why`, `cleland2002defining`). It cannot carry c3 alone.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation, argued as a claim about what AI is: not the
  interface, not the illusion, not an ALife experiment): support for the third exclusion. Dorin and
  Stepney show what an ALife claim looks like in the field's own words: build or study systems whose
  entities "actually live" (p. 5), explored by wet, robotic and virtual experiment (§2.1), and "life as
  it could be" (p. 1). The paper makes no claim of that kind, and the contrast marks its edge. The
  editors also treat LLMs as a neighbouring technology outside ALife, "close to artificial life" (p. 9)
  and a field where "applications beyond our field" have spread (p. 8). The use stretches the source:
  the editors say nothing about animation, craft, AI development as making, or OpenLife, and the
  "not an ALife experiment" line is the paper's own inference from how they define ALife.

## Caution

- **Genre.** The running head says "Editorial". It is an editors' overview and was not peer-reviewed as a
  research article would be. Cite it for the field's self-description, not for evidence.
- **Version.** Publisher PDF from MIT Press, held through the Berthon arm (HELD_COPIES.tsv; provenance.txt),
  with a download stamp dated 6 November 2026. No Crossref or OpenAlex record sits in the folder. The title,
  authors, volume, issue, pages and DOI come from the PDF itself (title page; the footer line "Artificial
  Life 30: 1-15 (2024) https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_e_00435"). DECISIONS.md's APA matches them. S8's DOI check
  should confirm against Crossref.
- **Langton is second-hand.** The "life as it could be" lines and the Langton 1988b p. xvi quotation
  come from Dorin and Stepney's report of Langton, not from Langton. If the paper quotes Langton, it
  should quote the source (R8 in triage F1-F4.md, `langton2019artificial`, is a metadata-only card).
- **Homonym.** Triage F1-F4.md (line 51) notes a 2023 Dorin and Stepney editorial on LLMs that shares
  the first author and two title words. I did not see that item; the year and the page range keep this one
  apart.
- **What the paper may not claim from it:** a definition of life, a view on whether LLMs are alive, or
  any statement that the editors link ALife to AI development. They keep the two apart.
- **Not read.** The reference list (pp. 10-15) and Figure 2's data. I read all of §1-§4.
