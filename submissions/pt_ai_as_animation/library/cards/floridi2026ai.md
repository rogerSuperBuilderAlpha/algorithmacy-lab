# Floridi (2026), AI and semantic pareidolia

## What it argues

Luciano Floridi, writing an editor letter for *Philosophy & Technology*, names "semantic pareidolia": the
human tendency to attribute intelligence, consciousness and emotion to AI systems that lack them. He models
it on ordinary pareidolia, which makes people see faces in clouds. Here the projection runs into meaning:
people perceive intentionality where he finds only statistics and pattern matching. He places most of the
cause in human nature, not in the designers' skill, though he grants designers "a crucial role" and says
generative AI is built "to make us believe that it is intelligent".

Floridi expects the tendency to grow, driven by four forces: life lived "onlife", the profit motive of large
companies, loneliness and gullibility, and better AI. He takes Replika as the paradigm case and warns that
embodied companions, from sex dolls to Mattel's planned OpenAI toys, will amplify the risk. Pareidolia can
then slide into "idolatry", the worship of AI, and he cites fringe movements (Way of the Future, Terasem, the
Turing Church) as early signs. His remedies are education, industry standards built from current good
practice (he names Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind), and design that is "useful and non-deceptive".
His verdict: AI is "a powerful tool, not a proto-sentient being". The letter is four pages long, carries no
reference list, and runs no study.

## What the paper uses it for

- **c5** (intelligence frame versus life frame is a stance choice): Floridi is the strongest venue voice
  against the life frame. He would object that calling AI development "the creation of artificial life"
  feeds semantic pareidolia: it invites users to project life onto "a powerful tool". His letter treats the
  projection as an error to correct, not as a stance one may choose. The paper must therefore show that the
  life frame is a stance its makers adopt toward their own craft, not a belief it asks users to hold about
  the machine. The fit is partial: Floridi writes about users' attributions of consciousness and
  intelligence, not about developers' framing of their work.
- **c12** (gap: AI development as a craft of animation): Floridi helps define the gap by contrast. He also
  places a maker behind the effect ("its designers do so intentionally"), and he names puppets as an
  earlier object of projection. But he never treats the making as a craft worth studying; designers appear
  only as a source of deception to restrain. The paper cannot cite him for any claim about how AI is built.
  Using his "puppets" line for the animation analogy would stretch him: he mentions them only as evidence of
  human credulity.
