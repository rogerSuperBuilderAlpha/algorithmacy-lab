# Reviewer 2 report

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of
organizational wholes"
**Venue:** *Organization Theory*, Special Themed Section "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of
Organizational Phenomena"

## 1. Verdict

**REJECT AND RESUBMIT.** The manuscript is the most ambitious submission I have reviewed for this
section and the most overextended. Its central claim — that it supplies a *criterion* for constitution —
does not survive the manuscript's own evidence, and its most attractive payoffs are delivered by
equivocating between what a toy model shows and what an organization is. A resubmission that recasts the
claim could be a strong paper. This version is not it.

## 2. What the paper claims

The authors argue that configurational theorizing rests on an unformalized commitment: a configuration
is one causal thing, a single joint determination rather than an aggregate of smaller ones. QCA tests
across-case regularity; complementarity, fit, and NK capture other properties of wholes; nothing in the
tradition decides, of one arrangement modeled as its interacting parts, whether it is one whole. The
paper imports the formal core of integrated information theory — cause-effect structure, partitions, Φ,
the complex — to fill the gap. From the import it derives three things: a constitution criterion
(irreducibility over every partition), a membership account (the complex, with pivotality grading cores
from peripheries), and a dissolution theory (three operators — substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting —
none of which is formation reversed). The constraint-lifting operator yields the paper's flagship
distinction between necessary irreducibility (an element does integrating work others cannot reproduce)
and contingent irreducibility (power forbids the others to meet). Section 6 lines the criterion up with
Shapley values, platform economics, bargaining theory, and communication theory in a five-row
correspondence table. Everything runs on three- to six-element Boolean models, exactly solved, offered as
"the microscope" of a theory essay.

## 3. Attacks

**3.1. The criterion is not a criterion. The manuscript proves this itself.** Section 2 names three
modeling commitments — the application-layer encoding, the state-individuation rule, the party
partition — and states that they "carry every verdict." It then reports that in a ten-case
demonstration, four cases changed verdict under "a defensible re-encoding of their rules." Four of ten.
Hold that number against the promissory notes elsewhere in the text: the complex is "computed rather
than assumed" (§2); the boundary becomes "an output" rather than an input (§4); disagreements about
membership "become, on a stated model, decidable" (§4); a constitution claim "has a definite truth
value" (§2). None of this is true in the sense a reader will take it. The verdict is definite *given an
encoding*, and the encoding is exactly where the analyst's discretion went. The paper diagnoses QCA's
weakness as "the boundary of a configuration is set by research design" and then reproduces that
weakness one level down, in transition rules and individuation conventions that are *less* visible to a
reader than a truth table's condition list. The authors' defense — the sensitivity "locates where the
constitution lives" — concedes my point while dressing it as a feature. An instrument whose output flips
on 40% of test cases under defensible alternative inputs is an exploration device. Calling it a
criterion, a "decision procedure at the foundation," is the overclaim on which the paper is built, and
the title question ("when is a combination a configuration?") is answered only as "when your encoding
says so."

**3.2. The paper equivocates between model and organization at exactly the points where the payoff
lives.** Section 7's first boundary is admirably blunt: the verdicts "are evidence about the models; no
organization has been measured." I agree. Now read Section 5 against it. There the necessary/contingent
test is "applied to a hand-assembled catalog of fifty-one intermediary types, real and stylized," sorts
them into quarters and halves, and claims "retrodictive traction" against the internet's disintermediation
wave. Read Section 7's agenda: ride-hailing "holds the dispatcher in the core"; a marketplace "leaves the
platform outside the core." These are claims about organizations. Either they are empirical claims — in
which case I want the catalog's construction, its coding protocol, its inter-rater checks, and the
provenance of each historical outcome, none of which appears — or they are readings the authors built
into their own encodings, in which case the retrodiction is the authors grading homework they assigned
themselves. The internet retrodiction is the weakest moment in the paper: everyone has known since the
late 1990s that classified advertising died and clearinghouses did not. A test "confirmed" against
outcomes known before the test existed, on encodings constructed by people who knew those outcomes,
confirms nothing. The manuscript wants the modesty of "the models are models" and the authority of "the
sort has retrodictive traction" simultaneously. Pick one.

**3.3. Not one computational claim in the paper can be checked.** The quorum theorem (irreducible at
exactly k=1 and k=n), the 660-form enumeration and its membership gradient (four in ten to nine in ten),
the ten-case flip demonstration, the five design moves, the fifty-one-intermediary sort — no appendix, no
repository, no truth table, no citation to prior work reporting them. The manuscript asserts that "a
reader can rebuild any one of them from its stated encoding," but no encoding is stated; every model
lives in prose. This matters more here than it would in an ordinary theory essay, because the paper's
epistemic posture is that its numbers are *exact* ("every number cited as an illustration is exactly
computed"). Exactness is doing rhetorical work — it is what distinguishes this from coupling imagery — so
it must be inspectable. And anyone who has touched this formalism knows the verdicts depend on choices
the paper never discloses: which version of the theory (IIT 3.0 and 4.0 differ in their partition
schemes and distance measures, and the paper cites both), how severed connections are noised, which
state the system is evaluated in, whether the reported Φ is system-level or the major complex's. The
quorum result in particular could flip under a different convention, and the reader has no way to know.
For a paper whose whole contribution is a formal test, "trust us" is not an available citation format.

**3.4. The criterion can never reach the phenomena that motivate it, and the manuscript never says so.**
Exact Φ is super-exponentially expensive; the paper's own methods citation (Mayner et al., 2018) makes
computation feasible to roughly a dozen elements, and the illustrations stop at six. Section 1 motivates
the gap with "platforms, algorithms, and human–AI assemblages now coordinate millions of parties."
Section 7 proposes to "encode and test the canon" and "map the constraints" of the platform economy.
Nowhere does the manuscript state the consequence: the criterion is computable only for arrangements
reduced to a handful of aggregate roles, so every application will inherit not just an encoding choice
but a brutal aggregation choice, forever. This is not a quibble about method (methods are out of scope
for this section); it is a theoretical boundary on what the concept can mean. A "within-case test" that
is undefined in practice for any case of realistic grain is a thought experiment, and the paper should
either own that or explain what the theory licenses when exact computation is off the table.

**3.5. The necessary/contingent distinction relabels a counterfactual that economics and antitrust
already run, and Φ does no work in it.** The paper concedes the "verbal siblings": essential facilities,
multihoming, make-or-buy. Look at what the test actually is — restore the forbidden direct tie,
recompute, see whether the intermediary is still needed. That is the essential-facilities question
verbatim, and it is answerable without partitions, without Φ, without a complex: add the bypass route
and ask if the middleman still does anything. The fifty-one-case sort, as described, could be reproduced
by asking of each intermediary "does law, contract, or friction forbid the direct tie, and does the
intermediary compute a joint condition the tie cannot?" — a question Lafontaine and Scott Morton, Rochet
and Tirole, and the multihoming literature already ask in those words. The burden the paper never meets:
exhibit one case the counterfactual-with-Φ classifies correctly that the counterfactual-without-Φ gets
wrong. The same problem afflicts the brokerage payoff. The observation that a fully successful *tertius
iungens* dissolves its own position is in Obstfeld's construct (a broker who joins parties makes himself
unnecessary — Burt's work on brokerage decay circles the same drain), and the claim that verbal theory
"could describe but never derive" it is grandstanding: the derivation adds a formalism, not a
consequence.

**3.6. The correspondence table is sign-agreement on examples built by the people claiming the
agreement.** The paper is candid that only the Shapley row has computation behind it and the other three
are "structural correspondences, exhibited on the models built here rather than derived." Exhibited on
models built here — that is, the authors constructed toy systems to display the correspondences, and the
toy systems display them. This is not four literatures converging on one verdict; it is one team writing
the same idea in five notations and counting the notations. Even the computed row underwhelms once
stated plainly: Φ-complex membership and Shapley value are both marginal-contribution sensitivity
measures, so their sign agreement across an enumerated family is close to guaranteed by construction —
the paper itself calls the Null Player axiom "the substitutability collapse of section 3 in a second
notation," which is exactly my point. A second notation is not a second witness. The abstract's "one
correspondence with computation behind it" flirts with what I would call numerology if the paper claimed
more; since the paper explicitly downgrades the claim to "agree in sign," the table should be presented
as a translation aid, not as evidence, and the framing of Section 6 ("the same verdict recurs across
four other literatures," per the abstract) should be cut to match.

**3.7. The gap is real but narrower than advertised, and where the criterion's verdicts diverge from the
tradition's intuitions, the paper always sides with the formalism without arguing for it.** Two halves.
First, the gap: Simon's near-decomposability *is* a within-case measure (how far an interaction matrix
sits from block-diagonal), community detection gives within-case boundaries, and the
information-theoretic emergence literature (Hoel's causal emergence, partial information decomposition's
synergy) offers within-case irreducibility measures the manuscript never mentions. "No formal test
exists" is sustainable only under a definition of "test" gerrymandered to IIT's shape. Second, and more
serious: the quorum result invites a reductio the paper never confronts. A majority-vote board is the
tradition's paradigm of joint determination — no member decides alone, the outcome exists only at the
collective level — and the criterion scores it zero, an aggregate. The paper celebrates this
("connection is not constitution"), but celebration is not argument. When a formalism and a
century of theory disagree about the paradigm case, the possibilities are that the formalism found
something or that the formalism measures something else. The paper needs to argue that Φ's constitution
is *the tradition's* constitution — that Meyer, Tsui and Hinings would recognize the majority gate as a
heap — and it never does. It proves the two notions differ and asserts the formalism wins.

## 4. What survives

I attacked; some of it held. Honesty requires the list.

The dissolution section is a genuine contribution. The three operators are distinct, organizationally
meaningful, and none is formation reversed; "dissolution by slack" — a whole that factors when
unanimity becomes two-of-three, with no tie cut and no exit — is the best idea in the paper, and it
answers Quattrone and Zilber's disorganizing question with real content. The substitutability insight
generalizes it: interdependence, density, and fit are all compatible with a whole that factors, and no
prior formalization in this tradition separates those cleanly. Even stripped of Φ, that conceptual
point stands.

Contingent irreducibility as the formal shape of power — a configuration held together by the upkeep of a
constraint, with the forbidden tie as the place to look — is, whatever its debts to essential facilities,
a transposition this literature does not have, and the entrepreneurial corollary (build integration or
erect constraints, distinguishable only when the constraint falls) is crisp.

The QCA positioning is honest and correct: complement, not rival, across-case regularity against
within-case constitution. The paper does not oversell against set-analysis, and Section 7's four
boundaries anticipate more objections than most submissions survive. The writing and the command of both
literatures are exceptional. A reader who rejects IIT wholesale would still keep the operators and the
necessary/contingent question — which is precisely why the paper should stop claiming the formalism is
what delivers them.

## 5. What would change my verdict

1. **Recast the central claim.** "Criterion" and "decidable" must go, or be earned with
   encoding-invariance results: characterize the class of re-encodings under which verdicts are stable,
   or present the apparatus as a theoretical instrument whose sensitivity is part of its use. The second
   route is honest and, I suspect, the paper the authors actually have.
2. **Make every number checkable.** Full encodings (truth tables or code), formalism version, partition
   and noising conventions, evaluated states — repository or appendix. The 660-form enumeration and the
   ten-case flip study should be reported as results, with construction stated, or cut.
3. **Face intractability in the text.** Say what the theory licenses when exact Φ is impossible, which
   is every case of realistic grain.
4. **Show Φ earning its keep in the necessary/contingent test.** One case the plain restore-the-tie
   counterfactual misclassifies and the partition calculus corrects. Absent that, present the
   distinction as the contribution and the formalism as its scaffolding.
5. **Own or cut the empirical gestures.** The fifty-one-intermediary catalog and the retrodiction are
   empirical claims; give them methods or delete them. The platform verdicts in §7 should be flagged as
   conjectures on provisional encodings, in those words.
6. **Answer the majority-gate reductio.** Argue, against a skeptic, that a majority board really is a
   heap in the tradition's own sense — or concede that Φ measures a specific, narrow species of
   constitution and scope the title accordingly.
7. **Deflate Section 6** to a translation table, and align the abstract with the deflation.

## 6. Bottom line

There is a real paper here: three dissolution operators, a substitutability argument that cuts
interdependence away from constitution, and a formal seat for power in configurational theory. Wrapped
around it is a claim the manuscript cannot sustain — a within-case *criterion* with definite verdicts —
propped up by uncheckable computations, a self-confirming correspondence table, and empirical gestures
that violate the paper's own stated boundaries. The authors know where their weak joints are; Section 7
lists most of them and then the rest of the paper leans on them anyway. Rebuild the paper around what
the formalism explores rather than what it decides, make the models inspectable, and resubmit. I would
review the resubmission gladly, and harder.
