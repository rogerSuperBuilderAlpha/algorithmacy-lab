# Part I: Stage 1 Research Proposal (Prospectus)

## 1. The Foundational Question: When Does a Triad Refuse to Reduce to Dyads?

What happens when a third party steps between two people who work together? Georg Simmel (1902/1950) argued that the jump from two actors to three is the most radical threshold in social life: a dyad vanishes if either member leaves, whereas a triad can outlive a departure, play partners against each other, or fuse three actors into a unit inexplicable by its pairs alone. Organization theory builds on that insight, from Caplow (1956) on coalitions and Thompson (1967) on interdependence to Burt (1992) on structural holes, Coleman (1988) on closure, Obstfeld (2005) on *tertius iungens*, and Kellogg, Valentine, and Christin (2020) on algorithmic control.

Historically, formal coordination ran on written text. Standard operating procedures and contracts coordinated work on paper. Navigating a bureaucracy required **literacy**: reading shared rules that sat in plain sight. Even when a manager stood between two workers, work operated as a chain of dyadic obligations—Worker A reported to the manager under one rule, and the manager instructed Worker B under another—so anyone could understand the workflow by reading each pairwise link in turn.

Today, software platforms, automated dispatchers, rating engines, and continuous-integration gates sit in the middle of work (Cameron, 2024; Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen, & Neysen, 2020; Orlikowski & Scott, 2008; Rahman, 2021; Shestakofsky, 2017). Navigating these workplaces requires a different sensibility: **algorithmacy**. Unlike a static rulebook, an algorithmic mediator is an active, state-updating participant that watches both sides at once and conditions each human actor's next move on counterparts they may never meet.

Yet after a decade of fieldwork on algorithmic management, scholars remain deadlocked: when an algorithm mediates between two counterparts, does it create a genuinely irreducible triad or merely a fast broker stringing together two-party ties? One camp sees totalizing triads that fuse workers, clients, and software into an indivisible whole (Kellogg et al., 2020; Stark & Pais, 2020); a rival camp sees high-speed market relays unbundling work into arms-length dyads.

Organization science has not settled this debate because "triadic mediation," "reciprocal interdependence," and "systemic wholeness" have remained metaphors. Network analysis counts ties and brokerage paths, while regressions measure whether one actor's behavior correlates with another's. Neither can answer Simmel's causal question: can a three-party coordination arrangement be severed into pairwise channels without losing causal power, or does it act as an indivisible whole?

## 2. The Core Innovation: Bringing Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0) and PyPhi to Organizational Coordination

To answer that question, we bring a mathematical framework from theoretical neuroscience into organization science: **Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0)** (Albantakis et al., 2023; Tononi, 2004) and its exact computational engine, **PyPhi** (Mayner et al., 2018).

Neuroscientists confronted the exact puzzle organization theorists face today: given interacting elements whose states update over time, when does a collection act as one integrated unit rather than smaller modules operating side by side? Correlation cannot tell. Two wall clocks tick in lockstep without forming one mechanism, and falling dominoes pass a signal from first to last through purely pairwise contact.

IIT and PyPhi solve this problem by shifting from statistical correlation to counterfactual, interventional causation. Instead of asking how often actors move together on average, IIT models a workflow as an explicit state-transition mechanism: given every participant's current action state (such as whether a driver, platform, and rider commit to or withhold from a match), what state does each move to next? From those rules, PyPhi computes the intact system's cause-and-effect repertoire.

Then comes the decisive move that makes PyPhi transformative for organization theory: the **Minimum Information Partition**. To test whether a three-party workflow is genuinely triadic, PyPhi cuts the system across every possible seam that separates the parties, severing causal links across the cut and replacing them with noise to find the organization's weakest seam: the cut that loses the least cause-and-effect information.

The causal information lost across that weakest seam is **system integrated information (Φ)**. This yields an exact structural test:

• **When Φ = 0 across the weakest cut**, the three-party arrangement is causally reducible: no matter how entangled the prose sounds, the workflow factors into pairwise or unilateral links without losing causal information.
• **When Φ > 0 across the weakest cut**, even the gentlest partition breaks real causal structure: the three parties form an irreducible causal whole that cannot be recovered from its pairs.

Importing IIT 4.0 and PyPhi into organization science overturns a sixty-year assumption in our field. Since Thompson (1967), scholars have treated *joint influence* (whether two actors must combine inputs to determine a third actor's next step) and *systemic wholeness* (whether the group forms an indivisible unit) as if they were the same thing. Running PyPhi across all 256 possible three-party mediated coordination structures proves that joint influence and systemic wholeness are orthogonal dimensions, revealing four distinct coordination regimes that organizational prose routinely conflates (Technical Addendum, Section A.1 and Table 1):

• **Dyadic Relays (no joint determination, Φ = 0):** A mediator passes Partner A's input to Partner B down a one-way chain; PyPhi proves the workflow factors into pairwise links across its weakest seam, requiring only **literacy** (reading one's own direct rule).
• **Broadcast Cascades (no joint determination, Φ > 0):** No single actor combines two inputs at once, yet closed feedback loops bind all three into an indivisible cycle that cannot be cut without losing information.
• **False Dyads and Hidden Clearinghouses (joint determination, yet three-party Φ = 0):** One actor combines inputs from two counterparts, yet the three-party system splits across its weakest seam because one party is a passive sink or interchangeable pool around a two-party core.
• **Irreducible Triads (joint determination and Φ > 0):** The mediator fuses all three parties into a unit that resists every cut. Navigating it requires **algorithmacy**: no participant can steer outcomes by reading a pairwise contract, because causal power lives in the simultaneous state of all three actors.

We do not use artificial intelligence to "do research" for us, nor do we treat Large Language Models as theoretical oracles. LLMs fail as structural judges because they match semantic patterns rather than computing combinatorial causal partitions. Conversely, while IIT 4.0 and PyPhi provide the exact mathematical engine organization theory needs, applying them to qualitative fieldwork previously hit a wall: hand-coding ethnographies into transition tables could neither scale nor survive a skeptic's objection that the analyst rigged the encoding.

Instead, we built a **collaborative, falsifiable research harness** (algorithmacy-lab) to execute and apply IIT 4.0 and PyPhi to organizational life (Technical Addendum, Section A.2.1 and Appendix B). Any researcher can fork our repository, engage the harness, ask an organizational question, and have their AI agents run the experimental workflow: translating field narratives into quote-anchored transition models, generating the strongest skeptical counter-encoding, executing exact PyPhi partition cuts and four falsification controls (actor removal, link severing, pooling, and peer-to-peer bypass), and contributing verified results back to the shared research program.

In doing so, **our research architecture quite literally demonstrates the exact triadic coordination we are examining**. A lone scholar hand-coding a case, or prompting a chatbot in private, is a reducible dyad vulnerable to confirmation bias and hallucination. Inside our repository, the organizational scholar, the AI agents, and the deterministic PyPhi verification harness form an irreducible triad: the scholar supplies theoretical questions, competing AI agents translate field evidence and rival readings, and the PyPhi harness enforces exact partition laws and falsification gates that neither human bias nor AI fluency can bypass.

Four boundary conditions govern this program, each stress-tested in our preliminary audits (Technical Addendum, Section A.4 and Table 6):

• **Interpretive ambiguity:** When field prose leaves a transition rule underspecified, a skeptical encoding can flip Φ; our harness reports the spread between primary and skeptical verdicts to pinpoint what field observation is missing.

• **Combinatorial scale:** Because PyPhi evaluates every partition, exact IIT 4.0 scales exponentially and targets governance nuclei of three to ten interacting roles.

# Part II: Technical Addendum — Formal Mechanics, Worked Tables, and Verification Compendium

## Addendum Section A.1. Formal Representation, Boolean Transition Mechanics, and Regime Classification

In their inaugural editorial for *Organization Science*, Daft and Lewin (1990) urged organizational scholars to break out of the "normal science straitjacket." Thirty-six years later, generative AI threatens an inverted version of that trap: automating low-cost, incremental variations on familiar designs rather than opening scientific questions that were previously intractable (Gartenberg, Hasan, Murray, & Pierce, 2026; March, 1991; Girotra, Terwiesch, & Ulrich, 2010).

Our core contribution addresses a foundational measurement problem in organizational theory. Platforms, clearinghouses, and AI agents now sit at the center of workplace coordination (Kellogg, Valentine, & Christin, 2020; Rahman, 2021; Cameron, 2024; Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen, & Neysen, 2020; Anthony, Bechky, & Fayard, 2023; Orlikowski & Scott, 2008). Yet organizational science still lacks a reproducible way to test a basic structural claim: **when an intermediary sits between two parties, is the three-party arrangement genuinely triadic—held together only by all three parties acting in concert—or does it factor into pairwise dyads that merely pass through the middle?**

Nearly every major stream of organization theory stakes a claim on this distinction—Simmel (1902/1950) on *tertii* and super-individual wholes, Caplow (1956) on coalitions, Burt (1992) on structural holes, Obstfeld (2005) on *tertius iungens*, Granovetter (1973) on bridges, Coleman (1988) on closure, Ostrom (1990) on polycentric governance, Thompson (1967) on interdependence, and Wageman (1995) on hybrid tasks—without a common mathematical test to decide it on organizational evidence. Worse, when qualitative accounts of an arrangement disagree (for example, a platform dispatch manual describing joint matching versus a driver describing a one-way algorithmic dictate), researchers typically average two human coders into an inter-rater reliability score, burying the structural disagreement inside a consensus code.

### How We Represent and Test an Organizational Triad

Consider a recurring organizational act involving three parties—for instance, a Driver (*D*), a Dispatch Platform (*P*), and a Rider (*R*). At each step, each party has a binary action state (`1` = commits/participates; `0` = withholds/waits) and a deterministic rule for its next state (*D*′, *P*′, *R*′) as a function of the current states of the parties (`AND` [∧], `OR` [∨], `NOT` [¬], `XOR` [⊕]):

* **A Relay / Conduit (*P*′ = *D*):** Suppose the platform simply relays the driver's state to the rider (*D*′ = *P*, *P*′ = *D*, *R*′ = *P*). Here the platform never combines both sides' states; the rider is a downstream receiver, and severing the rider from the loop leaves the driver–platform interaction intact.
* **A Joint-Commitment Clearinghouse (*P*′ = *D* ∧ *R*):** Now suppose the platform commits a dispatch *only when both* the driver is available *and* the rider has confirmed (*D*′ = *P*, *P*′ = *D* ∧ *R*, *R*′ = *P*). Here no pairwise slice explains what the platform does next; the transition depends irreducibly on the joint state of both counterparts.

To test where any multi-party rule table falls, we compile the rules into a state-by-node Transition Probability Matrix (TPM) and compute exact **system integrated information Φ over the Minimum Information Partition (MIP)** in Integrated Information Theory 4.0 (Albantakis et al., 2023; Mayner et al., 2018). The MIP cuts the system into independent parts in every possible way, severs the causal links crossing the cut, and measures how much cause-and-effect information is lost under the least-destructive cut:

1. **Factorable / Dyadic (Φ(MIP) = 0):** If some cut loses zero causal information, the arrangement factors into simpler parts.
2. **Irreducible / Triadic (Φ(MIP) > 0):** If every cut loses causal information, the arrangement is dynamically irreducible, and IIT 4.0's **major complex** (`maximal_complex`) identifies the exact subset of parties that forms the irreducible core ({*D*, *P*, *R*} of size 3, or a two-party capture/bypass core {*P*, *R*}, {*D*, *P*}, or {*D*, *R*}).

### Two Foundational Guardrails (Algebraic Logic and Structural Regime Classification)

Our framework pairs exact Φ(MIP) with two theoretical guardrails (proved and benchmarked in Technical Appendix A):

* **Why Peirce vs. Quine Matters:** Peirce (1897/1933) argued that genuine triadic relations cannot be built from dyads alone, whereas Quine (1954) showed that any *n*-ary predicate can be rewritten as a single dyadic predicate *F* by expanding the domain with iterated Kuratowski ordered pairs. Modern universal algebra explains why both hold in their respective settings: in unrestricted primitive-positive clones [F]ₚₚ, the ternary diagonal Δ₃ is definable from binary equality via (*x* = *y*) ∧ (*y* = *z*) (Koshkin, 2024, 2025), whereas in Peircean Algebraic Logic without primitive teridentity (PAL ∖ {id₃}; Burch, 1991; Hereth Correia & Pöschel, 2004, 2006) and in bond algebra over a fixed set of parties (Koshkin, 2025), binary composition never creates a three-way junction. Because an empirical study fixes who the organizational parties are, irreducibility must be tested over that fixed party set.
* **Why Dynamical Wholeness (Φ(MIP) > 0) Must Be Paired with Mediator Regime (`regime.py`):** Our computational probes show that dynamical wholeness (Φ(MIP) > 0) and two-input joint determination cross in both directions: a cyclic three-party copy ring (*A*′ = *C*, *B*′ = *A*, *C*′ = *B*) uses only one-input copy rules yet scores Φ(MIP) = 2.0 because no cut can sever a closed ring without loss, whereas 11 of 30 two-input mediated forms (such as a unanimous three-voter majority triad) contain genuine two-input rules yet factor at Φ(MIP) = 0. Our pipeline therefore reports **both** Φ(MIP) with its major complex **and** the structural mediator regime (`PASSIVE`, `CONDUIT`, `BOTTLENECK`, `CAPTURE`, `ENRICHER`, `BYPASSED`), distinguishing cyclic one-input relays from genuine joint-commitment triads.
* **From Literacy to Algorithmacy:** This distinction grounds our construct of **algorithmacy**: whereas *oracy*, *literacy*, and *numeracy* suffice when technology acts as a passive dyadic conduit (Φ(MIP) = 0), coordinating through an active algorithmic mediator that binds parties into an irreducible causal complex (Φ(MIP) > 0) requires reasoning about joint commitment rules, outside options, and partition fault lines.

### A.2.1. AI-Enabled Research Loop Under Human Direction: The Six-Step Pipeline

The central methodological challenge in applying exact causal measures to organizations is translating qualitative evidence (platform manuals, interviews, workflow logs) into formal transition rules without smuggling the researcher's preferred conclusion into the encoding. Asking an LLM to directly label a case "triadic" or "dyadic" fails because LLMs hallucinate ungrounded rules and misguess multi-party partition math. Instead, our **six-step AI-and-PyPhi pipeline** separates **structured qualitative reading** (done by two LLMs under a deterministic code gate) from **exact partition math** (done by PyPhi):

1. **Step 1 — Bound and Name (`protocol/`):** Fix one recurring organizational act, name the *n* parties (typically 3 to 5), and define each party's binary action state (`1` = commits; `0` = withholds).
2. **Step 2 — Encode via Drafter, Adversary, and Verbatim Gate (`encode/`):** An LLM **Drafter** (`PROMPT_drafter.md`) extracts every defensible Boolean encoding supported by the source text over party labels (`and` [∧], `or` [∨], `not` [¬], `xor` [⊕], `0`, `1`). Every rule must attach a **verbatim quote** from the source document, state whether the mediator *commits* on a joint condition, *conveys* one side's state, or *stores* inputs, and record a pre-registered prediction before computation. A second LLM **Adversary** (`PROMPT_adversary.md`) searches for rival encodings across three structural forces: commit versus convey, interchangeable pooled parties (`or` [∨]), and outside options. A deterministic gate (`encode_check.py`) rejects any encoding with a missing rule, a quote not found character-for-character in the source text, a missing mediator alternative, or a missing prediction before PyPhi can run.
3. **Step 3 — Compute (`irreducibility/core.py`):** Compiles each rule set into a 2ⁿ × *n* state-by-node TPM and connectivity matrix and calls PyPhi (`wmayner/pyphi`, commit `b78d0e342`) to compute exact Φ(MIP) and the major complex across all reachable states.
4. **Step 4 — Validate First (`irreducibility/validate.py`):** Before any empirical result can be reported, the solver must pass four mandatory control structures (`controls.py`, shown in Table 2).
5. **Step 5 — Report the Spread (`irreducibility/spread.py`):** Rather than collapsing rival readings into a single point estimate, the pipeline reports verdict agreement across all defensible encodings, the Φ gap (max Φ − min Φ), and the **exact single-rule flips** that reverse the verdict.
6. **Step 6 — Read the Structure (`irreducibility/regime.py`, `contingency.py`):** Compares the three-party core against the two-party bypass reduction (`reduce_mediator`) to classify the mediator (`PASSIVE`, `CONDUIT`, `BOTTLENECK`, `CAPTURE`, `ENRICHER`, `BYPASSED`) and sweeps outside-option exit scenarios.

## Addendum Section A.4. Extended Technical Risk Audits and Boundary Protocols

Because our laboratory commits pre-registered hypotheses before running experiments and keeps refuted predictions in CI, we can state the exact boundary conditions and risks of our approach:

1. **Encoding Sensitivity on Contested Qualitative Evidence:** Because an evidence-constrained adversary can flip a single-encoding verdict on 101 of 101 contested accounts, any pipeline that outputs a single LLM encoding would create false precision. We mitigate this by forbidding single-encoding claims: `spread.py` always reports the full verdict spread across all verbatim-grounded encodings and identifies the exact empirical rule that settles the disagreement.
2. **Structural Irreducibility (Φ > 0) Does Not Mean Superior Task Performance:** Our own pre-registered multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) falsifications refuted the hypothesis that Φ(MIP) > 0 predicts task performance or faster learning (yielded near-chance rank-AUC of **0.485–0.555** across tabular and neural policies). High Φ(MIP) measures **causal non-factorability and lock-in**, which can reflect an inefficient `BOTTLENECK` or asymmetric `CAPTURE` rather than normative efficiency. Our empirical hypotheses therefore test structural lock-in, bypass contingency, and oversight exclusion—never equating Φ with welfare or performance.
3. **Unobserved Parties and Off-Channel Interactions:** Field records can miss off-platform ties. While generic network priors can restore cheap mutual-information screens (AUC 0.944), our stress tests under **exact Φ(MIP)** show that when a party is hidden, ring-prior imputation drops to AUC **0.806** (and omission to **0.750**). Imputation cannot substitute for observing all interacting parties; accordingly, `protocol/` enforces explicit node-completeness and off-channel checks before computing exact Φ.
4. **Combinatorial Scale and Solver Preset Pinning:** Exact IIT 4.0 MIP evaluation scales to roughly *n* = 10 binary nodes; we therefore bound analysis to local governance nuclei of 3 to 5 interacting parties. We also pin our solver to IIT 4.0's 2023 formulation (`b78d0e342`), as upstream PyPhi 2.0's `iit4_2026` preset returns φₛ = 0 on deterministic systems at zero noise (ε = 0).
5. **Ethics and Dual-Use Symmetry:** All verdicts evaluate institutional rule architectures rather than individuals, and fieldwork proceeds under IRB oversight. The strategic lever matrix (Table 4) is symmetric: the same calculation that reveals how a platform achieves `CAPTURE` reveals to workers and regulators which outside option or coalition dissolves it.

## Addendum Section A.5. Extended Theoretical Implications for Interdependence and Governance

If successful, this work changes organizational research in three ways:

* **From Metaphor to Falsifiable Structural Measurement:** Claims that a platform, clearinghouse, or AI agent creates an "irreducible triad" become computed, replicable, and contestable. Studies publish explicit rule tables tied to verbatim evidence, and any reader can rerun PyPhi under a rival reading to see whether the verdict holds or flips.
* **Auditing Human-in-the-Loop Governance and Classic Typologies:** Classic interdependence typologies can be tested against an exact partition standard—revealing, for example, that Thompson's (1967) sequential and reciprocal interdependence share identical triadic integration (Φ = 2.0)—and organizational AI deployments can be audited for "Potemkin" oversight where human reviewers are structurally excluded from the Φ > 0 causal complex (Rudin, 2019).
* **A Formal Calculus of Coordinative Strategy:** Answering Daft and Lewin (1990), combining adversarial AI reading with exact partition math equips organizational scholars with a rigorous calculus of how commitment rules, pooling, and outside options govern power in algorithmic organizations.

# Technical Appendix (Stage 1 Supplement)

## Appendix A: Algebraic Irreducibility, IIT 4.0 Wholeness, and Proxy Benchmarks

### A.1. Peircean Algebraic Logic (PAL), Bond Algebra, and Quine's Pairing Translation

Quine (1954, p. 180) translated any interpreted *n*-ary theory into a single dyadic predicate letter *F* by encoding tuples via iterated Kuratowski ordered pairs {{*x*}, {*x*, *y*}} over a universe closed under pairing. In standard primitive-positive relational clones [F]ₚₚ, variable identification is unrestricted, so the ternary diagonal Δ₃ = {(*x*, *x*, *x*) ∈ *X*³ : *x* ∈ *X*} is trivially generated from binary equality via (*x* = *y*) ∧ (*y* = *z*), and on finite domains with |*X*| ≥ 3 every *n*-ary relation reduces to binary relations under primitive-positive operations, whereas on Boolean domains ¬*I*₃ is primitive-positive irreducible to binaries (Koshkin, 2025, Theorems 2–4; see Koshkin, 2024 for infinite domains and projoins). In **Peircean Algebraic Logic** (introduced with a restricted proof in Burch, 1991), removing primitive teridentity (PAL ∖ {id₃}) caps every connected set of argument places generated from unary and binary relations at size ≤ 2, so id₃ cannot be generated over any fixed domain |*X*| ≥ 2 (Hereth Correia & Pöschel, 2004; Hereth Correia & Pöschel, 2006, Lemma 1 and Theorem 2). Likewise, in tabular **bond algebra** where composition quantifies out shared places, teridentity is bond-irreducible on any domain with |*X*| ≥ 2 (Koshkin, 2025, Theorem 6).

### A.2. What the Classifier Computes vs. What the Peirce Probes Test

In `irreducibility/core.py` (and `org_frontier/classifier/`), a structure is classified as `triadic` iff system integrated information over the Minimum Information Partition (Albantakis et al., 2023) satisfies Φ(MIP) > 10⁻⁹ in at least one reachable state, and `pyphi.new_big_phi.maximal_complex` identifies the major complex (which may bind all three parties {*D*, *P*, *R*} under `BOTTLENECK`/`ENRICHER`, two parties {*D*, *P*} or {*P*, *R*} under `CAPTURE`, or {*D*, *R*} under `BYPASSED`). Separately, our Peirce probes (`org_frontier/thinkers/peirce/probe_peirce_joint_determination.py`; CI checks `thinkers-peirce-h1-irreducibility`, `thinkers-peirce-jd`, and `thinkers-peirce-jd-full`) test how systemic wholeness (Φ(MIP) > 0) relates to two-input joint determination (a node whose rule depends non-trivially on two counterparts), showing that the two properties cross in both directions:

* **Wholeness without Joint Determination:** Across all 89 four-node one-input copy wirings, 66 reach genuine effect-side adicity 3 and 12 reach adicity 4 (while cause-side adicity never exceeds 2); among the 11 unbranched wirings (max adicity 2), 8 of 89 have Φ(MIP) > 0 (and the 3-node copy ring *A*′ = *C*, *B*′ = *A*, *C*′ = *B* scores Φ(MIP) = 2.0 with core {*A*, *B*, *C*}). Under `core.py`, these cyclic copy rings read as `triadic` wholes even though no rule has two inputs.
* **Joint Determination without Wholeness:** Across the 30 two-input mediated forms in `thinkers-peirce-jd-full`, 5 exhibit joint determination inside a whole, 11 have genuine two-input joint determination yet factor at Φ(MIP) = 0, and 1 has no joint determination (and in `thinkers-peirce-jd`, the 3-voter majority triad at state (1, 1, 1) scores Φ(MIP) = 0 with 4 partition ties). That is why Step 6 (`regime.py`) combines Φ(MIP), the major complex, and the bypass reduction rather than equating Φ(MIP) > 0 with a single algebraic property.

### A.3. Why Time-Series Information Decomposition (PID / ΦID) Cannot Replace Exact Φ(MIP)

Partial Information Decomposition (Williams & Beer, 2010) and Integrated Information Decomposition (ΦID; Mediano et al., 2025) decompose multivariate time-series information, and the ΦID-based measure Φᵣ corrects whole-minus-sum integrated information Φ(WMS) for double-counting redundancy. We tested these proxies in two separate benchmarks:

* In our prototype's `studies/proxies` check across the 256 three-party structures, simulated-trajectory ΦID Φᵣ and whole-minus-sum Φ(WMS) separate dyadic from triadic structures at rank-AUC **0.563** and **0.629**.
* In `foundations/phiid_vs_phi/FINDINGS.md`, exact-TPM Φ(WMS) correlates with exact Φ(MIP) at Spearman ρ = **0.283** (AUC = **0.669**), while finite time-series ΦID Φᵣ (CCS) correlates at Spearman ρ = **0.124** (AUC = **0.560**).

Furthermore, our canonical Φ(MIP) = 2.0 strict-mediation control (*D*′ = *P*, *P*′ = *D* ∧ *R*, *R*′ = *P*) uses a conjunctive `AND` (∧) rule, whereas pure `XOR` (⊕) parity triads exhibit the **parity blindspot** (`#4`, `BLINDSPOT_SURVIVES_RADIX`), scoring Φ = 0.5 at *n* = 3 and decaying as Φ = 2²⁻ⁿ through *n* = 10.

## Appendix B: The Six-Step Pipeline and Multi-Valued Engine Architecture

### B.1. The Six-Step AI-and-PyPhi Pipeline (`irreducibility/` & `encode/`)

* **Step 1 — Bound and Name (`protocol/`):** Fixes one recurring organizational act, names the *n* parties as uppercase identifiers, and defines binary participation states (0/1).
* **Step 2 — Encode via Drafter, Adversary, and Gate (`encode/SCHEMA.json`, `PROMPT_drafter.md`, `PROMPT_adversary.md`, `irreducibility/encode_check.py`):** Rules are deterministic Boolean expressions over party labels (`and` [∧], `or` [∨], `not` [¬], `xor` [⊕], `0`, `1`); `rules.py` rejects any other syntax so encodings cannot execute code. The Drafter writes rival and design encodings with verbatim source quotes, mediator alternatives (commit, convey, store), and fixed predictions; the Adversary tests commit-vs-convey, pooling via `or` (∨), and outside options; `encode_check.py` rejects any missing rule, non-verbatim quote, missing alternative, or missing prediction without computing Φ.
