# Review 4 — Platform studies / algorithmic management lens

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** Organization Theory, Special Themed Section "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena"
**Reviewer lens:** platform studies and algorithmic management (Kellogg/Valentine/Christin, Rahman, Cutolo & Kenney, Gu & Zhu, Rochet & Tirole)

## 1. Verdict

**MINOR REVISION before submission.** The platform material is mostly accurate and the scope limits are unusually honest; six fixable issues stand between this draft and a submission I would defend.

## 2. What the paper contributes

The paper imports the partition-irreducibility core of integrated information theory as a within-case criterion for when an arrangement of interacting parts is one configuration rather than an aggregate, then derives membership (the complex, graded by identity-sensitivity) and dissolution (substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting). For my field the live contributions are three. First, the dispatch result (section 3): whether the platform's commit reads the unseen third party separates a bound triad from a driver–app dyad with the same surface experience, which formalizes a distinction we currently make narratively between assignment platforms and browse-and-match marketplaces. Second, bypass-as-disintermediation (section 5): the operator is a correct rendering of what Gu & Zhu (2021) measure as leakage, and the necessary/contingent distinction — integrating work versus a held gate — is exactly the question the app-store, MLS, and Ticketmaster litigation keeps asking without a formalism. Third, the encoding-politics paragraph in section 7 ("a platform that gets its coordination encoded as pairwise contracts has factored itself out of accountability for the whole") is the sharpest sentence in the paper and will resonate with every algorithmic-management scholar who has read a platform's terms of service. The paper fits the section's remit: theory, cross-disciplinary synthesis, models as microscope rather than method.

Citation spot-checks from my shelf: Kellogg, Valentine & Christin (2020, *AoM Annals* 14(1), 366–410), Rahman (2021, *ASQ* 66(4), 945–988), Gu & Zhu (2021, *Management Science* 67(2), 794–807), Cutolo & Kenney (2021, *AMP* 35(4), 584–605), Rochet & Tirole (2003, *JEEA* 1(4), 990–1029), and Lafontaine & Scott Morton (2010, *JEP* 24(3), 233–250) are all bibliographically correct, and Rahman and Lafontaine & Scott Morton are used for exactly what they show. The problems below are about characterization and internal consistency, not about invented sources.

## 3. Submission-blocking issues

**B1. Section 1 understates platform economics, and the gap claim is weaker than it needs to be.** The manuscript writes that "each clause in a composite arrangement has a bilateral rationale, a termination-at-will term, a rating hostage, a unilateral price, and the economics explains them pair by pair." That is a strawman of the field's core result. Rochet & Tirole's (2003) non-neutrality of the price structure is precisely a claim that a two-sided arrangement does **not** decompose into two bilateral markets — the allocation of the fee across sides changes real outcomes because the parties cannot bargain around the platform. Two-sided market theory is already a within-arrangement non-decomposability result; what it lacks is a partition test over the parties and the mediator. Positioning the criterion as generalizing that result strengthens the paper; claiming the economics is pairwise invites a desk-stage objection from any economist reviewer. Paste-ready replacement for the offending clause: "…and the economics explains them pair by pair — with one important exception: two-sided market theory's non-neutrality result (Rochet & Tirole, 2003) already shows the price structure of a mediated arrangement does not factor into two bilateral markets. What even that result does not supply is a test for whether the parties, the clauses, and the mediator constitute one causal whole, rather than one joint surplus."

**B2. The section 6 table misattributes a native test to Rochet & Tirole, and catalog entry 23 compounds it.** The table row reads "Platform economics (Rochet & Tirole, 2003; Gu & Zhu, 2021) … the native test: disintermediation / leakage." Disintermediation/leakage is Gu & Zhu's test (and Cutolo & Kenney's strategic terrain); it is not Rochet & Tirole's, whose native test is price-structure non-neutrality. Meanwhile supplement entry 23 codes the generic "two_sided_platform" as *necessary* on the authority of "[Rochet-Tirole 2003]" — but nothing in that paper establishes that platforms compute a joint condition the direct tie cannot reproduce, and the paper's own leakage citation (Gu & Zhu) is evidence that a canonical two-sided freelance platform is bypassable. Fix, two parts: (a) in the table, either drop Rochet & Tirole from the row and cite "Gu & Zhu, 2021; Cutolo & Kenney, 2021," or keep both papers and change the native-test cell to "price-structure non-neutrality; disintermediation / leakage"; (b) in the catalog, either recode entry 23 or annotate it: "coded on the pure cross-side-externality form in which the platform's fee structure is the joint condition; platforms whose matching is separable from their pricing fall to the additive template (cf. entries 46–48)."

**B3. The ride-hailing verdict is stated twice and points two ways.** Section 7: "Strict-mediation ride-hailing holds the dispatcher in the core with all three parties bound." Supplement entry 46: `ride_hail_platform | additive | matching + bypassable | partial`. A reviewer who reads both — and this section's reviewers will — sees the paper's flagship platform case classified as a bound triad in the text and a bypassable partial mediator in the supplement. I understand the resolution (the catalog codes a marketplace-like template with a live direct channel; section 7's form forbids it), but the reader should not have to reconstruct it. Fix: rename entry 46 to `ride_hail_marketplace` or `ride_hail_offplatform_channel`, and add one sentence to section 7: "The catalog's ride-hailing entry (supplement, Part B) codes the partial-mediation variant in which an off-platform channel is live; the strict-mediation form modeled here forbids it, and the two verdicts differ for exactly that reason — which is the necessary/contingent distinction doing its work."

**B4. The "transmit / transform / commit ladder" is the authors' construction, presented as the literatures'.** Section 6 says the criterion recovers "the line the communication and algorithmic-management literatures draw between transmission and direction (Kellogg et al., 2020)." Kellogg et al. distinguish algorithmic direction, evaluation, and discipline as employer control mechanisms; Hancock, Naaman & Levy (2020) grade the magnitude of AI mediation in communication. Neither states a transmit/transform/commit ladder; the commit rung — the load-bearing one — is this paper's coinage, and a good one. Claiming it as found rather than built is the kind of misattribution this special section's reviewers will check. Fix: in the table header write "Communication theory (adapted from Hancock, Naaman & Levy, 2020; Kellogg, Valentine & Christin, 2020)," and in the text: "…the line these literatures gesture at between conveying and directing (Kellogg et al., 2020; Hancock et al., 2020), here sharpened into a transmit/transform/commit ladder whose top rung is ours: a system enters the core exactly when its output is a determination both parties must heed."

**B5. Part C of the supplement is a promissory note under a load-bearing number.** The four-of-ten verdict-flip figure appears three times in the manuscript (sections 2, 4, and 7) as the paper's honesty about encoding sensitivity — and the supplement's Part C says the per-case rules "will be included in the submission package." For a final pre-submission check this is the clearest blocker: a robustness statistic cited as computed must ship with its rules and both encodings per case, in the same reproducible form Parts A and B already have. While in the supplement: Part B cites Hagiu (2009), Rubinstein & Wolinsky (1987), Granovetter (1973), Gould & Fernandez (1989), and Simmel (1908) with no reference list anywhere; add a short supplement bibliography so every bracketed source resolves.

**B6. Fixed-rule stationarity is a real limit for algorithmic arrangements, and section 7's four boundaries do not name it.** Section 2 defines elements as "updating [their] state by a fixed rule." The central empirical fact of algorithmic management is that the rules are not fixed: platforms re-write dispatch, pricing, and rating logic continuously, often in response to the very worker reactivity Rahman (2021) documents — the cage is invisible partly because it moves. A Φ verdict is a property of a rule regime, and platform rule regimes have short half-lives. The paper's application-layer bet survives this (re-encode, recompute), but the boundary should be stated with the other four. Paste-ready addition to section 7: "**The rules are a regime, not a constant.** The formalism evaluates a fixed rule set, and algorithmic arrangements revise theirs continuously, sometimes in response to the parties' own reactivity (Rahman, 2021). A verdict therefore dates: it holds for the rule regime encoded, and tracking a configuration through time means recomputing across regimes — which turns rule revision itself into an observable dissolution or formation event."

## 4. Non-blocking suggestions

1. Gu & Zhu's mechanism is nearly a field test of the necessary/contingent distinction and the paper leaves the point on the table: clients disintermediate more as platform-built trust accumulates and less where the platform's ongoing services stay valuable — i.e., bypass fires where the mediator's work was contingent (reputation transferable) and stalls where it is still integrating. One sentence in section 5 would let the paper's best platform citation carry its full weight.
2. Cutolo & Kenney's dependence framing could anchor section 5's power discussion beyond the forbidden tie: platform dependence is a bundle of constraint types (technical, informational, contractual), which is exactly the "lifting a bundle of constraints at stated costs" generalization the paper defers to the agenda. Citing them there, not only for leakage, would place the deferral inside an existing literature.
3. The dispatch triad binds only at state 111 (supplement E6); the manuscript's existential convention ("positive Φ at some reachable state") is disclosed but not defended. One clause on why constitution is a capacity claim — the arrangement *can* jointly determine — rather than an every-state property would pre-empt a picky reader.
4. "A rating hostage" (section 1) is doing Williamsonian work without a citation; either name the hostage model or use plainer language.
5. Entry 32 (`app_store_30pct`) is a live natural experiment: the DMA and Epic-era remedies are partial constraint-lifts. The agenda paragraph could claim it explicitly as a forecast the framework stakes.
6. "Leakage" appears in the section 6 table before it is defined; define it at first use in section 5 ("what platform economics calls leakage").

## 5. Bottom line

Judged from platform studies, this paper does something our field needs: it gives disintermediation, gatekeeping, and algorithmic direction a common formal object, and it is honest — to a degree I rarely see — about where opacity and encoding discretion limit the verdicts. The dispatch and mediation models are recognizable, not caricatures; the necessary/contingent distinction is the right cut through the intermediary landscape and the catalog's self-assessment ("disciplines the distinction rather than testing it") is candid. The blocking list is real but shallow: one mischaracterization of two-sided market economics, one misattributed table row plus a contestable catalog coding, one internal inconsistency on the flagship case, one coinage presented as a finding, one missing supplement part, one unstated stationarity limit. All six are days of work, not months. Fix them and submit.
