# Review 3 — Classical organization theory / coordination lens

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** Organization Theory, STS "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena"
**Review round:** Final pre-submission

## 1. Verdict

**MINOR REVISION — submittable after the five blocking fixes below; none requires new architecture, but two require new computation or new supplement material.**

## 2. Contribution summary

The essay proposes a within-case criterion for the configurational tradition's founding claim: an arrangement is a configuration when its cause-effect structure survives no partition, an aggregate when some partition costs nothing. It borrows the partition calculus of integrated information theory (Φ, the complex, the minimum-information partition), explicitly severed from the consciousness semantics, and develops three theoretical products: a constitution criterion that cuts differently from interdependence, fit, density, and co-occurrence (the interior-threshold quorum factors; the cycle of copyists binds); a computed membership account with cores, peripheries, and a graded pivotality law; and a three-operator theory of dissolution (substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting) whose third operator yields the necessary/contingent irreducibility distinction — an element bound in by its integrating work versus an element held in by a constraint on the others.

From this reviewer's chair the essay has improved markedly across rounds. Thompson is now credited by name as "the nearest ancestor," with pooled/sequential/reciprocal mapped onto the computation; Simon gets both near-decomposability and the Simon–Ando aggregation mathematics, correctly distinguished from the counterfactual partition; the Weick treatment is exemplary — the essay claims only the responsiveness axis of Orton and Weick's two-dimensional construct and says so; Williamson's make-or-buy is placed as a verbal sibling of constraint-lifting rather than annexed. The face-validity paragraph in section 3 (Φ = 0 "classifies, does not condemn"; constitution and merit run orthogonally) answers the objection I pressed hardest previously. What remains blocking is precision in exactly these places: the Thompson mapping as stated is wrong on its own evidence, the cost-relativization of necessity contradicts the supplement's coding rule, and the neutrality the essay professes about designed aggregates is undercut by its own rhetoric.

## 3. Submission-blocking issues

**B1. The Thompson mapping is inaccurate as stated: the criterion cross-cuts the typology; it does not merely sharpen it.** Section 3 says "Pooled interdependence, contributions summed with each part substitutable, is the interior-threshold quorum: it factors." Two problems, both fixable from the essay's own results. (a) The definition smuggles the conclusion into the premise. In Thompson (1967) pooled interdependence is a wiring pattern — each part contributes to and draws on the whole without direct part-to-part ties — and by that definition the k=1 and k=n quorums are also pooled, yet the essay computes that they *bind*. So pooled interdependence does not factor; it *splits*, and the split (interior thresholds factor, extremes bind) is a genuinely new finding about Thompson's weakest category that the current sentence hides by redefining "pooled" as "substitutable." (b) The essay reports the closed cycle (binds) but is silent on the open sequential chain, and by the essay's own bidirectional-coupling law (section 4: spectators never belong; endpoints of an open chain lack bidirectional coupling) plus IIT's requirement of recurrent structure, the open chain should factor. If so, the criterion assigns Thompson's middle rank (sequential) the same verdict as his lowest (pooled interior), and the claim that "the computation honors the ordering and sharpens it" is false: it *reorders* — feedback, not rank of interdependence, is what constitution tracks. That is a more interesting result and an honest one. **Fix (paste-ready):** compute and report the open chain A→B→C alongside the rotation; then replace the Thompson paragraph's two claims with: "Pooled interdependence splits under the criterion: interior thresholds factor, while unanimity and any-one bind, so substitutability rather than pooling per se is what dissolves constitution. Sequential interdependence splits the same way: the open chain factors, the closed cycle binds. The criterion therefore cross-cuts Thompson's ordering rather than refining it — what it tracks is whether determination closes back on itself — and the typology's real successor here is its pairing of interdependence with coordination mode, since coordination by standardization is precisely the substitutability that makes interior thresholds factor." Credit Van de Ven, Delbecq and Koenig (1976), who already found Thompson's three slots insufficient and added team interdependence, when claiming the cycle "has no slot."

**B2. "Necessity relativized to cost" is asserted in the text and contradicted in the supplement.** Section 5 states the right semantics: the constraint family is heterogeneous, "franchise law prohibits the direct tie, search friction merely prices it," restoration happens "at a stated cost," and — the key sentence — "a middleman that only economizes a real cost is doing integrating work of Williamson's kind, not holding a gate." The supplement's Part B coding rule then codes an entry *contingent* "when an identifiable external constraint (statute, license, exclusive contract, standard, **friction**) is the only stated reason the parties cannot transact directly," and accordingly classifies `arbitrageur_friction` (entry 26, "search friction") and `freight_broker` (entry 37, "friction: fragmentation") as contingent conduits. By the manuscript's own sentence these two are Williamsonian integrators, not gate-holders. The distinction is coherent only if every verdict is indexed to a stated cost regime, and right now the text promises the index while the instrument ignores it. There is a second, related honesty gap: the manuscript says the test "reads about a quarter as necessary... and about half as contingent conduits," but the supplement concedes that class follows template *by construction* (margin is constant within template), so the computation added nothing beyond the hand coding — the coders read the catalog, not the test. **Fix:** (i) add one sentence to section 5 fixing the semantics — "necessary" means the element stays in the core for every restoration cost in a stated admissible range; "contingent" means some admissible cost evicts it — and note that on this semantics even the escrow agent is necessary only relative to the cost of reproducing simultaneity, which is the honest form of the claim; (ii) either recode entries 26 and 37 or state explicitly the cost threshold under which pricing frictions count as constraints; (iii) move the supplement's "by construction" admission into the manuscript sentence that reports the quarter/half proportions.

**B3. The load-bearing 4-of-10 encoding-flip figure has no verifiable backing.** Sections 2 and 7 both cite the demonstration ("four of the ten flipped under a defensible re-encoding"), and the essay leans on it twice: to price analyst discretion and to power the "encoding is political" argument. Supplement Part C says the per-case rules "will be included in the submission package." For a final pre-submission package this is not acceptable: a reviewer must be able to check what "defensible re-encoding" meant in each flipped case, since the whole argument of section 7 is that the flip locates where constitution lives. **Fix:** include the ten cases, both encodings, and both verdicts in Part C now, or delete the specific 4/10 figure and soften both citing sentences to "verdicts can flip under defensible re-encodings."

**B4. The professed neutrality about designed Φ = 0 forms is undercut by the essay's own rhetoric.** The section 3 paragraph is exactly right — majority rule, redundancy, and slack are designed so no individual is indispensable; Φ = 0 classifies, does not condemn; robust aggregate and fragile whole run orthogonally to merit. But the essay then calls triadic constitution "a special achievement," names the third operator "dissolution by slack" (so a deliberate move from unanimity to two-of-three sign-off — often maturation, on Weick's own account of the adaptive functions of looseness — arrives linguistically as decay), and closes on "the wholes of configurational theory can be told from the heaps." A majority-rule board is, on this vocabulary, a heap. Configurational scholars will read the honorifics as the essay's real view and the neutrality paragraph as insurance. **Fix (all one-liners):** (i) revise the closing sentence to a neutral form, e.g. "...the wholes of configurational theory can be told from the aggregates, and each valued for what it is"; (ii) in the substitution operator, add: "where the substitutability is designed, this 'dissolution' is the design succeeding — the classical account is March and Simon's (1958) performance programs, which make role occupants substitutable on purpose"; (iii) either cite Cyert and March (1963) for "slack" and reconcile with their sense (uncommitted resources, not redundant members) or rename the operator "dissolution by redundancy," which is what the mechanism actually is. The March–Simon citation also repairs the one conspicuous absence in the classical crediting: March currently appears nowhere in a paper about how organizations make people substitutable.

**B5. "Dissolution is not formation run backwards" overclaims; the operators are not shown exhaustive.** Section 5 opens with "dissolution has its own operators, and none of them is the inverse of building a joint determination." The three operators cover dissolution-with-everything-still-present, which is the novel and valuable class. But configurations also dissolve by the plainest route: a formation move reversed — a built tie severed, a core member exiting. The essay's asymmetry thesis survives in weakened form (dissolution *need not* be reversal, and the non-reversal routes are the cheap ones, which is what explains years-to-assemble/season-to-unravel), and only the weakened form is defensible. **Fix:** change the claim to "dissolution need not be formation run backwards: three operators dissolve a configuration while every element and tie that formation built remains in place," and add one clause conceding tie-severance and exit as the reversal cases the operators deliberately exclude.

## 4. Non-blocking suggestions

1. **Thompson's mediating technology gloss.** Section 1 defines it as linking "parties who would otherwise transact directly." Thompson's own formulation is linking clients "who are or wish to be interdependent" across time and space — parties who typically *could not* transact directly. The current gloss quietly pre-loads the bypass counterfactual into Thompson. Quote him instead.
2. **State-dependence of the quorum verdicts deserves a sentence.** The supplement shows the any-one quorum binds *only* in the all-off state and unanimity only at all-on. The manuscript's quantifier ("binds meaning positive Φ at some reachable state") is stated once in section 2 and never confronted where it bites. A critic will note that the alarm system is a configuration only while nothing is happening. There is a defensible reading (in the quiet state each party's individual capacity to trigger is exactly what the structure holds); give it.
3. **Perrow.** "A tightly constituted whole is also a fragile one" is Normal Accidents in one clause; citing Perrow (1984) would both credit the lineage and strengthen the face-validity defense, since Perrow is the classical authority for tight coupling as hazard rather than achievement.
4. **Williamson's fundamental transformation.** Section 5's "manufacture contingent irreducibility by erecting constraints" has an exact classical precedent: initially large-numbers (substitutable) bargaining becoming small-numbers dependency through the relationship itself. One sentence would show the necessary/contingent line handling *endogenous* constraints, currently its quietest weakness.
5. **Supplement consistency.** Part B cites "[Simmel 1908]" while the manuscript's reference list has only the 1950 Wolff translation; align them.
6. The operators-to-coordination-modes mapping suggested under B1 (standardization ↔ substitution; mutual adjustment ↔ what necessary integrators do) could grow into a short paragraph; it is the most natural bridge between the essay and the coordination literature that will actually cite it.

## 5. Bottom line

This is now a serious piece of theory that treats the classical canon as an interlocutor rather than a quarry, and its three products — the criterion, the membership law, the dissolution operators — are the kind of conceptual equipment the special section asked for. The five blocking items are all failures of follow-through rather than of conception: the Thompson paragraph claims harmony where the essay's own computations show a more interesting cross-cutting; the cost-relativized necessity semantics is promised and then not implemented in the one instrument that uses it; a twice-cited sensitivity figure ships without its evidence; the neutrality about designed aggregates is contradicted in the closing line; and the dissolution asymmetry is stated one quantifier too strong. Every fix is specified above and none threatens the architecture. Fix them and submit.
