# Review 5 — Systems / Complexity lens (final pre-submission)

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** Organization Theory, STS "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena"
**Reviewer lens:** systems and complexity (Simon, Simon–Ando aggregation, NK, Orton & Weick, Kimsey et al. 2025)

## 1. Verdict

**MINOR REVISION** — the paper is sound and submittable in substance, but six specific issues would draw fire from any systems-literate referee and should be fixed before submission. All six are fixable with sentences and one supplement section, not with structural rework.

## 2. Contribution summary

The paper imports the partition calculus of integrated information theory — cause-effect structure, minimum-information partition, Φ, the complex — as a within-case criterion for when a combination of organizational elements constitutes one causal whole rather than an aggregate. From the criterion it derives a membership account (computed cores and peripheries, a graded law tied to identity-pivotality), a dissolution theory with three operators (substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting), and the necessary/contingent irreducibility distinction that gives power a formal seat. From this reviewer's chair, the paper's strongest systems-theoretic move is the differentiation of the counterfactual partition from the realized-structure family (Simon–Ando aggregation, community detection), staged on the maximal-wiring result: density measures read traffic, the partition reads joint determination, and the two come apart on a fully connected three-node system that factors. The Shapley material is now presented honestly — as an exact decomposition of Φ and a translation into a familiar accounting, explicitly not an independent convergence — and the IIT 4.0/3.0 versioning is disclosed with a sign-robustness split that I verified against the supplement and found correctly reported. The quorum result (irreducibility at exactly k = 1 and k = n, factoring at every interior threshold) is the paper's sharpest single finding, and the identity-sensitivity vs. outcome-pivotality distinction pre-empts the obvious voting-theory objection well.

## 3. Submission-blocking issues

**B1. The Orton–Weick scoping sentence is wrong, and the paper's own lockstep result proves it.** Section 3 states: "their loose coupling is two-dimensional, pairing responsiveness with distinctiveness, and Φ speaks to the responsiveness axis alone." Two paragraphs earlier, the synchronized-veto result (E5) shows the opposite: a system with full bidirectional responsiveness factors to Φ = 0 because the worker and counterpart lose *distinctiveness* — "two parties that always agree carry the information of one," the manuscript's own words. In Orton and Weick's (1990) dialectic, responsiveness without distinctiveness is precisely their *tightly coupled* pole; so the same passage's claim that "tight coupling [is] the regime where every partition is expensive" also collides with their definitions — their tightly coupled system is one the criterion assigns Φ = 0. The truth is more interesting than the hedge: Φ requires *both* axes, which makes it a scale under the dialectic itself. Paste-ready replacement for the two sentences beginning "Loose coupling (Weick, 1976) is the regime...":

> "Coupling imagery (Weick, 1976) gestures at a strength the tradition has never priced, and Φ prices it: the cost of the cheapest partition. The pricing respects both of Orton and Weick's (1990) dimensions, not one. Positive Φ requires elements distinctive enough to carry different information and responsive enough to constrain one another jointly — their dialectical pair. Responsiveness without distinctiveness, their tightly coupled pole, factors: the synchronized veto above is exactly that regime. Distinctiveness without responsiveness, their decoupled pole, factors trivially. Φ is positive only where both dimensions hold, a scale under the dialectic rather than under either axis alone."

**B2. The exact zeros are knife-edge, and the paper never says so — a genericity objection Simon's own program supplies.** Near-decomposability is graded by construction; Simon and Ando (1961) proved approximation theorems precisely because exact decomposability is the measure-zero case. The paper's three headline factorings all sit on exact symmetries of deterministic Boolean rules: perfect substitutability at the interior threshold, perfect synchrony in the veto, exact cancellation in the maximal wiring. Perturb any of these stochastically and the zeros generically become small positive Φ. A systems referee will ask whether "aggregate" is an artifact of the encoding class, and the paper currently has no answer on the page. It has a good answer available — Φ prices the cheapest cut, so in noisy systems the criterion re-grades and "aggregate" relaxes to "nearly aggregate," which is exactly the Simon–Ando regime — and section 7's first boundary is the natural home. Suggested insertion (section 7, after "no organization has been measured..."):

> "A second consequence of the deterministic encodings should be stated. The exact zeros reported here live on exact symmetries of Boolean rules; exact decomposability is the measure-zero case, which is why Simon and Ando (1961) proved approximation theorems rather than factorization theorems. Under stochastic perturbation the zeros generically become small positive values, and the criterion returns to the graded world Simon described: 'aggregate' relaxes to 'nearly aggregate,' the regime where within-block and between-block dynamics separate approximately. The binary verdicts are properties of the deterministic encodings; what travels to noisy systems is the magnitude of Φ, not its sign alone."

While editing that region, sharpen the section 2 gloss of Simon–Ando from "blocks can be analyzed separately" to the actual two-timescale result — blocks analyzable separately in the short run and as aggregates in the long run — which is one clause and buys precision with this readership.

**B3. The versioning flag is promised but not carried to the two places where the interior-quorum factoring bears load.** Section 2 commits the paper to a discipline: "where it says *factors* against appearances, the claim is about the minimum-information partition, and is flagged as such." The flag is present at the section 2 disclosure and nowhere else. But the interior-threshold factoring — one of the three verdicts that do *not* replicate under IIT 3.0, per the paper's own split and confirmed by supplement Part A (E2: Φ₄.₀ = 0 at every reachable state, Φ₃.₀ = 1.2688) — is subsequently load-bearing twice without any flag: in the Thompson mapping ("Pooled interdependence... is the interior-threshold quorum: it factors," section 3) and in the substitution operator ("the whole factors... dissolution by slack," section 5). Dissolution-by-slack is therefore a measure-specific claim presented as if family-robust. Fix: one clause each. Section 3: append "(a 4.0 verdict; see section 2's versioning note)" after "it factors." Section 5: after "and the whole factors," add "— a verdict of the party-respecting partition family, per section 2's versioning note, not a family-robust zero." (For the record: I checked the reported sign-robustness split against Part A in full. All *binds* verdicts replicate under 3.0; the disconnection and ignored-party factorings replicate; E2, E5, and E8 do not. The split is correctly stated. The issue is propagation of the flag, not the accuracy of the disclosure.)

**B4. Supplement Part C is a placeholder for a number the manuscript uses three times.** The four-of-ten encoding-flip figure appears in sections 2, 4, and 7 — it is the paper's own price tag on analyst discretion, and section 4 leans on it ("a displacement section 7 prices"). Part C currently says the per-case rules "will be included in the submission package." For a final pre-submission draft that opens by asserting every cited number "reproduces from the model's stated encoding," a dangling placeholder behind a thrice-used figure is not submittable. Fix: populate Part C with the ten cases, both encodings, both verdicts (the format of Part A suffices), or cut the figure to a single hedged mention.

**B5. The Shapley integration game's worth function is nowhere defined, so the paper's headline decomposition is not reproducible from the stated encoding.** Section 4 claims an exact decomposition ("they sum to its Φ") and cites specific shares — mediator two-thirds, each party one-sixth of the strict-mediation triad's Φ. But v(S), the worth of a sub-coalition, is glossed only as "the integration of the sub-arrangement it induces," which is ambiguous in exactly the way IIT makes treacherous: Φ of a subset depends on what happens to the complement (frozen as background conditions, marginalized, or severed and noised), and different conventions give different shares. Neither manuscript nor supplement states the convention, which violates the paper's own reproducibility premise. Fix: one sentence in the supplement (a Part A footnote will do): "The integration game's worth function: v(S) = Φ of the subsystem on S with the complement [frozen at the evaluated state / severed and replaced by noise / ...], v(∅) = 0; shares computed at state 111." Since the honesty of the Shapley presentation is otherwise now exemplary — decomposition of Φ, translation not confirmation, stated twice (sections 4 and 6) with matching altitude — this is purely a specification gap, not a framing problem.

**B6. The cost-relativized counterfactual (manuscript) and the binary catalog test (supplement) are inconsistent, and the friction entries expose it.** Section 5 refines the constraint-lifting counterfactual: "restore the forbidden tie *at a stated cost*," because "franchise law prohibits the direct tie, search friction merely prices it," and "a middleman that only economizes a real cost is doing integrating work of Williamson's kind, not holding a gate." Part B then applies a binary, zero-cost bypass test with template-constant margins — and codes the friction-held entries (26 arbitrageur_friction, 37 freight_broker) as *contingent* conduits. By the manuscript's own refinement, those are precisely the entries the cost-relativized test would most plausibly re-grade toward Williamsonian integrating work. Fix (scope note in Part B): "The catalog applies the binary form of the counterfactual (tie restored at zero cost); the cost-relativized form of section 5 is not implemented here, and the friction-held entries (26, 37) are the ones it would most plausibly re-grade." Alternatively re-code those two entries; either move restores consistency.

## 4. Non-blocking suggestions

1. **Place NK's K explicitly in the realized-structure family.** Section 2 files aggregation tests and community detection under "reads realized structure"; K — a count of payoff couplings — belongs in the same family, and saying so ("K counts couplings; a high-K arrangement can still factor causally, as the maximal-wiring result shows for its topological analogue") would sharpen the counterfactual/realized line and make the section 3 NK sentence ("how hard a configuration is to reach by local search") read as a second difference rather than the only one. The current NK treatment is not wrong, just thinner than the Simon treatment beside it.
2. **Back the 4- and 5-party quorum claim.** Section 3 says the threshold result was "computed for every threshold at three, four, and five parties"; Part A shows only n = 3 (E1–E3). A one-line tally per (n, k) would close the gap.
3. **Document the 660-form enumeration.** The membership law's fractions (four in ten to nine in ten) and the pivotality metric behind them have no supplement entry. The manuscript only promises the supplement covers the constitution exhibits and the catalog, so this is not a broken promise — but a Part D pointer to the enumeration's definition of pivotality would pre-empt the request.
4. **Note the state-dependence of the extreme-threshold verdicts.** Per Part A, any-one binds only at the all-off state (E1: Φ₄.₀ = 3.0 at 0000, 0 elsewhere) and unanimity only at all-on (E3). This is consistent with the identity-sensitivity mechanism — the binding state is the one where each party is individually decisive — and saying so in one sentence would convert an apparent oddity into corroboration, since "binds = positive Φ at some reachable state" is doing quiet work here.
5. **The Kimsey et al. (2025) deployment is apt** — the partition test is a genuinely "meaningful use" of systems thinking in their sense — and section 6's two-strength honesty grading of the correspondence table (exact decomposition vs. structural correspondence) is exactly the right altitude. No change requested; flagged as something to preserve through revision.

## 5. Bottom line

The core systems-theoretic architecture is right and, in the counterfactual-vs-realized differentiation, better than right: the maximal-wiring exhibit gives the Simon–Ando and community-detection contrast a demonstration rather than an assertion, and the versioning disclosure with its sign-robustness split is accurately reported against the supplement. What remains are six local failures of carry-through: a scoping sentence that contradicts the paper's own lockstep result (B1), a missing genericity caveat that Simon's program hands the paper for free (B2), a versioning flag promised but not propagated to its two load-bearing uses (B3), a placeholder behind a thrice-cited number (B4), an undefined worth function behind an "exact" decomposition (B5), and a manuscript–supplement inconsistency on the cost-relativized counterfactual (B6). Every fix is sentences, not sections. Fix them and this is, in my judgment, an accept-quality contribution to the special section: a theory paper that gives the configurational tradition a decision procedure at its foundation and treats its borrowed instrument with more candor about versioning and encoding sensitivity than most native uses of Φ manage.
