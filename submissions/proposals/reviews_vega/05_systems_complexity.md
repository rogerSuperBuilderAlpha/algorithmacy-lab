## Part 1 — Theory and structure, abstract–§7

### Abstract and §1
The opening question is the right one for a systems referee: configurational theorizing presupposes wholes and has never stated a constitution criterion. Locating the gap under interdependence / fit / co-occurrence surrogates is disciplined, and tying it to platforms and human–AI assemblages matches the call’s terrain.

One caution: “a criterion that answers whether it is one configuration or an aggregate” (lines 68–69) promises a binary decision, while the body later gives Φ a degree reading and a graded membership law. The introduction should advertise both. A second: Quattrone & Zilber’s disorganizing thread is flagged, but §1 should preview the later affirmative claim — dissolution has operators formation lacks — as asymmetric dynamics, not only as a corrective.

### §2 — Borrowed formalism
The import rule (keep syntax, rework semantics; Cornelissen et al., 2021) matches Kimsey et al.’s (2025) demand that systems use be meaningful rather than ceremonial. The five-piece translation is clear enough that an OT reader can follow without buying consciousness science. Three systems-side pressures remain.

**Simon is cited for the right object and the wrong identity.** Lines 150–152 equate the partition with Simon’s near-decomposability “made exact,” and later Φ becomes “the price of the cheapest cut” (lines 256–259). Simon’s (1962) near-decomposability is a *hierarchical time-scale* claim: short-run dynamics are dominated by intra-subsystem interactions; inter-subsystem couplings are weaker and slower. Φ’s minimum-information partition noisifies cuts and scores information loss; it does not encode frequency separation or nesting. Near-decomposability and low Φ often travel together; they are not the same property. One paragraph distinguishing them would prevent modularity readers from treating Baldwin & Clark / Schilling as already answering the essay’s question.

**NK is dismissed as a surrogate too early and never re-engaged as a sibling.** Section 1 places Levinthal (1997) and Rivkin (2000) under interdependence-as-constitution. Fair as genealogy; it underplays what NK supplies: search, local optima, and imitation under epistasis. The quorum and density-without-constitution results correctly show high wiring can still factor. They should say what the criterion *adds*: NK grades how hard it is to *climb*; irreducibility grades whether the modeled parties *constitute one determining unit*. Without that sentence, NK readers hear “your surrogate failed” rather than “different dials.”

**Fixed-rule Boolean models omit endogenous rule change.** Encoding “what the coordination does” (lines 137–141) is honest. Complex-adaptive accounts often locate constitution in feedback that *rewrites* rules. Section 7 should say adaptivity-of-rules sits outside the microscope, or “meaningful systems use” claims more of the tradition than the formalism carries.

### §3 — Constitution
The analytic core largely earns its keep. The five microscopic results — quorum, rotation, synchronization, dispatch, maximal wiring — are the right kind of demonstration to move systems talk from ceremonial to checkable. Connection ≠ constitution is the claim coupling debates circled and never closed.

The Weick / Orton engagement is where a coupling specialist bites. Lines 256–258 equate loose coupling with cheap partitions and tight with expensive ones. Orton & Weick (1990) recast loose coupling as the *simultaneous* presence of distinctiveness *and* responsiveness — dialectical, not scalar. Collapsing both into Φ as tightness recovers Weick (1976)’s earlier metaphor and underplays the 1990 correction the essay cites. Better mapping: Φ formalizes how expensive it is to sever responsiveness; exclusion from the complex formalizes distinctiveness without belonging. Both readings together, not one scale position.

The population claim that genuine triadic constitution is rare among mediated forms (lines 260–264) is systems-relevant if encodings are accepted. State once that rarity depends on the party-partition commitment (lines 170–171). Tsoukas (2017) as the epistemic twin of causal irreducibility is well placed — keep it.

### §4 — Membership
The complex-as-boundary move is what systems theories of wholes have needed and what QCA/network designs treat as a design input. Exclusion of spectators and pivotality as graded predictor are clean.

Two refinements. First, classical near-decomposability centers on *levels*; the complex is a *flat* maximal subset graded by pivotality. The “coalitions inside configurations” result (lines 305–312) gestures at nested wholes — one sentence on whether recursive application of the complex is allowed, deferred, or ruled out would speak to the multilevel systems tradition Kimsey’s invitation evokes. Second, Shapley / Null Player convergence is powerful; name the integration game’s characteristic function in one clause so the convergence is checkable rather than suggestive.

### §5 — Dissolution and necessary / contingent
This is the payoff for the special section’s dynamics question. Substitution, bypass, and constraint-lifting as operators that are *not* formation run backwards is the best answer to Quattrone & Zilber, and it is asymmetric in the way complexity theories of collapse often are. Keep the design-move catalog (lines 360–370).

The necessary / contingent distinction gives power a formal seat. One pressure: much of the complexity tradition reserves “emergence” for patterns not attributable to a designer’s constraint. Contingent irreducibility is *imposed* non-decomposability. Both cases are configurations on present structure; only the necessary case is a candidate for emergence. Mark that, or systems readers hear “all positive-Φ systems are emergent.”

A second payoff available: restating the operators in landscape terms (substitution flattens epistasis; constraint-lifting removes a wall to a neighboring basin) would complete the NK re-engagement §2 deserved. Brokerage recovery (Simmel / Burt contingent; Obstfeld split) is sharp. No objection.

### §6 — Consolidation and Kimsey’s test
The five-vocabulary table is useful; the altitude distinction between computed Shapley agreement and structural correspondences for the other rows is appropriately modest.

Kimsey et al. (2025) underwrite “one meaningful use at the foundation” (lines 456–459). Intent granted; press whether the use changes *research practice* or only supplies a formal synonym for wholeness. The QCA division of labour is the best practical answer — elevate it. Temper the claim that systems / complexity / relational / configurational “distinction collapses into identity” (lines 452–453). Wiping program differences is exactly Kimsey’s ceremonial risk. Prefer: at the constitution question, programs can share a partition test; they keep their questions about search, meaning, and set-analytic regularity.

### §7 — Boundaries and agenda
The four boundaries are the right altitude. Agenda items are actionable. Sharpen: in re-deriving the canon, ask whether classic gestalts are near-decomposable hierarchies or flat irreducible complexes — different diagnostic failures. In theorizing the fringe, connect Siggelkow to Orton & Weick (fringe elements may retain distinctiveness while losing constitutive responsiveness). The ride-hailing vs. marketplace contrast (lines 533–536) is an excellent exhibit of surface homology / deep structural difference — keep it.

## Part 3 — Line-level revisions (paste-ready)

**R1 — §2, Simon vs. Φ (after line 152).** Add:
> Simon’s near-decomposability is a claim about hierarchical time scales: intra-module interactions dominate the short run, and inter-module couplings are weaker and slower. The partition test scores information lost when couplings are severed, without encoding that frequency separation. Low Φ and near-decomposability often co-travel; they are not the same property.

**R2 — §3, NK sibling contrast (after ~line 254).** Add:
> Levinthal’s and Rivkin’s NK instruments remain the right tools for search and imitability under epistasis. Interaction density grades how hard the landscape is to climb. Irreducibility grades whether the parties modeled are one determining unit. High K is compatible with Φ of zero.

**R3 — §3, Orton & Weick (replace lines 256–259).** Change to:
> Loose and tight coupling named a problem Weick (1976) first treated as a scale and Orton and Weick (1990) recast as the simultaneous presence of distinctiveness and responsiveness. The cost of the cheapest partition formalizes how expensive it is to sever responsiveness; exclusion from the complex formalizes distinctiveness without belonging. The richer reading needs both, not a single scale position.

**R4 — §4, multilevel complex (after line 312).** Add:
> The complex as computed here is a flat maximal set. Classical near-decomposable systems are nested. Whether applying the same criterion inside a complex yields lawful sub-complexes is left open; the counterpart-pair result shows the phenomenon, not yet the hierarchy theorem.

**R5 — §5, emergence vs. maintained constraint (after line 381).** Add:
> Both cases are configurations on the present structure. Only the necessary case is a candidate for emergence in the complexity sense of a joint condition no single maintainer holds in place. Contingent irreducibility is non-decomposability under an external rule. Calling both “wholes” is correct under the criterion; calling both “emergent” is not.

**R6 — §6, temper identity claim (lines 452–453).** Change “collapses into identity” to:
> At the constitution question, the programs can share a partition test without surrendering their other questions. Systems perspectives still own feedback and search; configurational set-analysis still owns across-case regularity.

**R7 — §7, adaptivity boundary (after ~line 494).** Add:
> Fixed transition rules are a further limit. Configurations whose mechanisms rewrite themselves sit outside the Boolean microscope until those meta-rules are encoded as elements. The criterion applies to a snapshot of rules; it does not yet theorize rule change.

**R8 — Self-narration and refrain:** apply Part 2 items 1–2 to lines 206, 372, 386, and the three “formation run backwards” hits.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions leaning major on three systems clarifications.** The essay does what Kimsey et al. (2025) ask for in spirit: a computable whole-or-aggregate test under configurational talk that had been ceremonial about systems while operational about set membership. The microscopic separations in §3 and the dissolution operators in §5 are genuine contributions.

What stands between this draft and a clean accept from a systems/complexity referee: (1) **Simon and Φ must be distinguished**; (2) **Orton & Weick must not be scaled away** — distinctiveness and responsiveness need membership and Φ together; (3) **NK must be re-engaged as a sibling** for search, and **emergence marked off from power-held constraint**. Tempering §6’s identity line is the Kimsey-compliance edit.

**Most important fix:** R3 + R1 — coupling and near-decomposability are the systems tradition’s own constitution vocabulary; if the essay mis-identifies them with Φ, meaningful use slides back toward ceremonial redescription.

## Biggest strength

The **five model separations in §3**, especially quorum and dispatch: every familiar systems surrogate (interaction, wiring, density, headcount, surface roles) can light up while the cause-effect structure still factors — and the reverse. That is the non-ceremonial payload Kimsey asked for.

## The one thing only the author can supply

Whether **recursive application of the complex** (complexes inside complexes) is intended as the bridge to Simon-style hierarchy, or whether the account deliberately stops at one flat maximal whole plus graded pivotality. Only the author, knowing the program’s nested or non-nested practice, can say which multilevel claims the fringe agenda item is allowed to make.
