## Part 1 — Theory and structure, platform lens

### §1 The unformalized foundation
The opening lands where platform scholarship feels the strain. Composite arrangements stack bilateral rationales — rating hostages, termination-at-will, unilateral prices, dispatch rules — while the lived apparatus delivers accountability and common understanding as one coordinated whole. Asking when that whole is one causal unity rather than an aggregate of dyads is a real contribution. Kellogg, Valentine and Christin (2020), Glaser, Sloan and Gehman (2024), and Hsieh and Vergne (2023) are the right anchors.

One caution: the charge that those literatures "still explain each clause, each channel, each contract pairwise" is too strong. Platform studies already runs multi-sided, ecosystem, and labor-process accounts. The defensible gap is narrower: they lack a *formal constitution test* for when the joint apparatus is one irreducible whole. Soften the pairwise indictment so the contribution is the missing criterion, not the claim that the field has only stacked dyads.

### §2 A borrowed formalism
The application-layer commitment fits platform method. Encoding committed determinations and party responses without opening the opaque scoring engine matches how much field work proceeds, and it aligns with Kellogg et al. on control practices readable at the interface. The three commitments (application-layer encoding, state-individuation at mediated commit, party partition) keep the borrowing inspectable.

Opacity is not only a modeling convenience; it is often the substance of platform power (Burrell; Rahman). Saying the model "runs on what the coordination does, not on what the algorithm is" brackets the opacity through which platforms withhold parameters parties need to contest. State once that the application-layer cut composes the binding rules — including rules a platform withholds — and that disclosure fights are fights over what belongs in the encoding. Without that sentence, algorithmic-management readers will hear evasion of the field's central object. The consciousness quarantine is handled correctly.

### §3 Constitution: the criterion
The **dispatch** result is the manuscript's best platform illustration: driver and rider each experience a dyad with an app; a commit that reads both sides yields irreducibility; dropping the unseen third party factors the same surface. Constitution and appearance come apart; headcount and channel counts miss it. Keep it.

Quorum, rotation, synchronization, and maximal wiring separate constitution from interdependence and wiring density. The claim that genuine triadic constitution is rare among mediated three-party forms is a configurational fact pairwise clause analysis cannot see. Scope it once beside the claim: rarity is about Boolean forms under the party partition, not a field rate for ride-hailing.

What §3 does not yet engage: Rochet and Tirole (2003) and Armstrong (2006) already treat the platform as internalizing cross-group externalities the sides cannot price directly. That is a native "why the third sits in the middle" answer. Say how Φ-constitution differs from (and when it agrees with) the two-sided-market rationale, rather than waiting for a table cell in §6.

### §4 Membership: cores and peripheries
Exclusion of spectators, bidirectional coupling as necessary for membership, and pivotality as graded predictor travel cleanly into platform settings. The finding that a corporate owner can sit outside an irreducible worker–mediator–counterpart triad while whole-system Φ collapses is gold: "the firm" and "the coordinating whole" are not coextensive.

What a platform referee wants next is **complementor power**. Cutolo and Kenney (2019) on platform-dependent entrepreneurship, and Rahman (2021) on the invisible cage, are membership talk in other vocabularies — who is constitutive of the joint determination, who can be written out. Neither is cited. Adding them in §4 or §6 would reconnect cores and peripheries to an active platform conversation, not only to Shapley.

### §5 Dissolution: operators, necessary and contingent
This is the section platform studies will use.

Substitution (threshold drift / loss of pivotality), bypass (direct channel, multi-homing, disintermediation), and constraint-lifting (exclusives, walled gardens, rate-parity, franchise law) map onto native platform phenomena. Necessary versus contingent — escrow-like joint conditions versus conduits held by forbidden ties — is the real payoff, and the brokerage recovery (tertius gaudens / structural holes as contingent; tertius iungens splitting into necessary integrator versus self-liquidating joiner) should stay.

Three demands.

1. **Multihoming as continuum.** Armstrong's bottlenecks and the multihoming literature treat bypassability as graded and segment-dependent. The manuscript writes bypass as open/closed. Say necessary and contingent are endpoints of a cost continuum, with partial substitution between. Otherwise §5 coarsens what platform economics already grades finely.

2. **Cutolo & Kenney / Rahman in the power seat.** Contingent irreducibility as "the formal shape of a configuration held together by power" will persuade platform readers only if it converses with complementary-asset capture and the algorithmic cage, not only franchise law.

3. **One algorithmic-management pass.** Name scheduling that binds without employing, rating hostages that raise exit costs, and dashboards decoupled from the commit as instances of the operators, so dissolution theory is visibly about Kellogg et al.'s contested terrain.

### §6 One test in four vocabularies
The transmit / transform / commit row with Kellogg et al. and Hancock, Naaman and Levy is the strongest platform-adjacent correspondence: a system enters the core when it stops conveying and starts committing. Good.

The platform-economics row is the weak joint. Rochet and Tirole and Armstrong are about pricing structure, externalities, and competitive bottlenecks — not primarily a binary "unbypassable / bypassable" screen. The honesty clause (Φ discards prices and tipping thresholds) almost rescues the row. Recast it: economics supplies a related partition (who can route around whom); Φ recovers the causal bound/separable cut; prices and welfare stay with the economic theory. Do not let the table imply two-sided price theory is a special case of partition irreducibility.

Also missing: Cutolo and Kenney, and Rahman — platform studies' home tests for who is bound. Leaving them out while listing Rochet/Tirole and Kellogg makes the conversation look thinner than it is.

### §7 Boundaries and agenda
The four boundaries hold the right altitude. The ride-hailing versus step-back marketplace contrast is the right first exhibit: strict-mediation dispatch holds the dispatcher in the core; a marketplace that matches and steps back can leave the platform outside while the worker–counterpart pair remains irreducible. Deepen "Map the constraints" by naming which constraints hold which popular forms as contingent, as hypotheses. Lead that agenda item with algorithmic management (dispatch, ranking, scheduling), so Organization Theory readers enter through Kellogg et al.'s contested terrain.

## Verdict

**Minor to major revisions — closer to major on platform engagement, minor on prose.** Constitution as partition irreducibility, membership via the complex, and dissolution via substitution, bypass, and constraint-lifting are useful to platform studies; the dispatch exhibit and necessary/contingent distinction are the right instruments. What stands between acceptance and a platform-literate referee is citation and altitude: (1) the pairwise indictment overstates Kellogg-era work; (2) Cutolo & Kenney and Rahman are absent where power and membership need them; (3) the Rochet/Tirole–Armstrong row over-compresses price-and-bottleneck theory into a binary partition; (4) bypass must be a multihoming continuum. None guts the thesis; each sharpens the affirmative platform case.

**Most important fix:** R4 + R7 — put platform dependence and the invisible cage into the membership/power argument, and stop letting the consolidation table imply that two-sided market theory is already Φ in other clothes.

## Biggest strength

The **dispatch / appearance-without-constitution exhibit** paired with **necessary vs. contingent** platform positions. Together they answer, formally, when the app is a coordinating whole that binds workers and counterparts, and when it is a gate held by a constraint that could lapse. The ride-hailing versus step-back marketplace contrast turns that answer into a program.
