## Part 1 — CfP alignment (three themes, theory bar, novelty)

### Fit to the section's premise
The CfP premise is that configurational traditions share a constitutional insight — phenomena are constituted through combinations, relations, and interdependencies — yet the insight remains siloed and under-theorized. The manuscript's opening question (*when is a combination a configuration?*) is precisely the prior question those traditions presuppose. Naming constitution as the unformalized foundation, rather than as another typology or another set-analytic refinement, is the correct altitude for Catalysing & Crystallizing: it crystallizes a foundational claim and catalyses conversation across lineages.

The essay also lands squarely on terrain the CfP names by name: platforms, algorithms, and human–AI assemblages (Hsieh & Vergne, 2023; Glaser, Sloan & Gehman, 2024). Section 1's clause-configuration gap and section 3's dispatch exhibit make that terrain load-bearing rather than decorative. That is a strong desk-pass signal for this section.

### Theme (3) — primary home: dialogue with other traditions
Theme 3 is the natural manuscript type. The CfP invites neighbouring disciplines that developed configurational ideas without the label (Elias, Bourdieu, Benedict are named; complexity, relational sociology, systems thinking, and others are invited). Integrated information theory is a bolder neighbour than Benedict or Elias — consciousness science rather than social theory — but the borrowing is executed in the form the CfP and OT pluralism literature already authorize: keep formal syntax, rework semantics for organization studies (Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl, 2021), refuse home-discipline metaphysics.

Section 6 converts the borrow into section currency: consolidation across cooperative game theory, platform economics, bargaining theory, and communication theory; bridge to Emirbayer, Tsoukas, and Kimsey et al. (2025) on meaningful rather than ceremonial systems use; explicit division of labour with QCA. That is the dialogue the section exists to host. For desk purposes, theme-3 fit is high and should be fronted in cover letter and abstract (it already is in the abstract's closing claims).

Residual editorial pressure on theme 3: the CfP also names critical realism / conjunctural mechanisms (Bhaskar), comparative political economy / institutional complementarities (Hall & Soskice; Mahoney & Thelen), and Bourdieu. The manuscript need not tour every neighbour, but one sentence locating the criterion relative to complementarity-and-path-dependence (already cited via Milgrom & Roberts on returns) would show the editors that the dialogue is with *configurational* adjacent fields, not only with information science. Optional, not blocking.

### Theme (1) — secondary line: dynamics and disorganizing
Section 5 answers the CfP's sharpest dynamic question almost verbatim: are processes of disorganizing simply the reverse of organizing? The answer — no; substitution, bypass, and constraint-lifting are operators of their own — is generative essay material and will travel with Quattrone & Zilber (2025) readers on the panel. Formation/dissolution asymmetry and the retrodiction on internet-era dissolution of unconstrained conduits are the theoretical payoffs, not the model catalog.

Formation and stabilization remain thinner than dissolution. The CfP asks how configurations emerge, acquire coherence, and endure; the manuscript theorizes coming-apart more fully than coming-into-being. That imbalance is acceptable for a foundation paper (constitution first, dynamics for an agenda), but the cover letter should claim theme 1 as *dissolution theory* rather than as a full dynamics account, so referees do not grade missing emergence theory as incomplete delivery.

### Theme (2) — partial but usable: agency, power, intentionality
Contingent irreducibility is the formal seat for power: a configuration held by a forbidden tie rather than by integrating work. Intentional design splits into building necessary irreducibility versus manufacturing contingent irreducibility. That speaks to the CfP bullets on power/politics and purposive design.

What theme 2 also asks — negative intentionality, unintended consequences, distributed practices that sustain configurations without anyone intending them — receives only a gesture ("some configurations persist because no actor intends them"). Do not expand into a second paper inside this one; one clarifying sentence that contingent constraint can be maintained intentionally or inherited as routine would inoculate against a theme-2 specialist who expected a fuller agency theory. Power is present; agency breadth is not the essay's center.

### Theory-not-methods bar
This is the desk decision that decides acceptance into review. The CfP excludes purely methodological contributions, empirical applications, and data-driven theorizing; limited empirical illustrations are welcome where they advance theory.

The manuscript's intended form — generative theory essay with models as microscope — matches scope. Concepts lead (constitution criterion; complex as membership; dissolution operators; necessary/contingent); computation warrants. Section 7's four boundaries (models are models; encoding is a commitment; consciousness is out; interpretive configurations are out) are exactly the hedges a section editor wants on file before sending to referees.

**Editorial risk nonetheless:** population counts ("660 strict-mediation forms," "fifty-one intermediary arrangements," "on the order of a tenth," Shapley predictive claims, "four of ten stylized cases flipped") can read as data-driven theorizing to a hostile methods gatekeeper, especially when Continuous Integration and "every number reproduces" surface in the title-block metadata. For section submission:

1. Demote program-labelling in the opening metadata; the OT reader need not know `ci/reproduce.json`.
2. Keep illustrations sparse and concept-first; treat population figures as warrants for *laws* (membership graded by pivotality; genuine triadic constitution rare among mediated forms), not as empirical results about organizations.
3. Never propose a procedure readers must run on field data. The essay already mostly complies; section 7's agenda items ("re-derive the canon," "map the constraints") must stay conceptual invitations, not a methods protocol.

If those three hold, the piece clears the theory bar. If population talk expands or a "how to compute Φ on your case" section appears, desk reject as methods drift is likely.

### Novelty
For this section, novelty is not "first use of Φ in management" as a methods claim; it is "formalizes the constitution presupposition configurational theorizing has lived on." That novelty is real and well-stated against interdependence, fit, and co-occurrence surrogates. Differentiating within-case causal unity from across-case QCA recipes is the move that will either delight or alarm set-analytic referees; either way it is section-relevant novelty rather than boutique formalism.

Consciousness controversy is manageable if section 7's hedge stays one of the first things a skimming editor sees mentioned in the abstract or early §2 (it is presently deferred to §7 — consider one early clause). No claim that organizations are conscious is made; keep it that way.

## Part 2 — Editorial risks and style

### Desk / panel risks (priority order)

**R1 — Methods trap (highest).** See Part 1. Population statistics and lab framing are the main tell. Fix by wording and by cutting submission-facing meta, not by gutting the affirmative formal case.

**R2 — Set-analytic misread as competition.** A Misangyi/Fiss/Furnari referee may hear "the claim has no formal statement anywhere" as erasure of necessity/sufficiency formalization relative to outcomes. The within-case / across-case handshake in §2 and §6 must be unmistakable in abstract and §1: QCA formalizes recipes for *Y*; the criterion formalizes whether a named combination is *one whole*. Elevate that sentence; it is the political insurance for half the likely panel.

**R3 — Overclaiming "program results" as field facts.** "Among mediated three-party coordination forms… on the order of a tenth" and the fifty-one-arrangement taxonomy are model-population facts. Section 7 says so; a few mid-body sentences still sound like landscape measurement. Align mid-body diction with §7 altitude wherever a number appears.

**R4 — Theme overpromise.** Opening ties the three CfP questions to the constitution prior — good — but readers may expect equal delivery on formation, agency, and dialogue. State primary/secondary theme hierarchy once so grading criteria stay fair.

**R5 — Consciousness allergy.** Low probability of desk reject if hedges stay; higher probability of one adversarial referee. Precedent paragraph (ecology, TCE) is the right defense; place a short version early.

**R6 — Length and packaging.** Body ~6.6k words excluding references is workable for OT theory essays but must be checked against current author instructions and any Catalysing & Crystallizing guidance. Abstract at ~230 words is likely over typical journal limits (often 150–200); trim before submission. Title question-form is generative and CfP-friendly; keep unless editors prefer declarative AMR-style titles.

**R7 — Citation hygiene for guest editors.** Campbell & Fiss (2026), Furnari et al. (2021), Misangyi et al. (2017), Kimsey et al. (2025), Quattrone & Zilber (2025), and the platform citations the CfP lists are present — good editorial manners. Simon, Weick, Thompson, Elias/Benedict appear where the call would expect them. No fabricated placeholders visible in the reference list as reviewed.

### Style / slop audit (register-aware)

Prose is clean and mostly claim-first. Antithesis density is moderate (`rather than` / `, not` / partitioning contrasts); many contrasts are earned because the wrong surrogate is exactly what readers will reach for. Residual fixes:

**1. Surrogate refrain in §1.** "Interdependence / fit / co-occurrence stand in for constitution" is the argument's engine once; later sections can say "the surrogates of section 1" rather than re-staging the three-way contrast.

**2. Self-narration — sparse.** Phrases that announce theoretical economy or altitude ("the altitude of the claim should respect"; "theoretical economy a pluralist field can actually use") can drop; the hedges already do the work.

**3. Mechanized taxonomy openers in §3.** Parallel result labels (quorum, rotation, synchronization, dispatch, maximal wiring) aid memory — keep — but vary one surrounding paragraph so the section does not read as five identically shaped exhibits.

**4. Closing cadence.** The final paragraph re-states constitution / membership / dissolution. Acceptable once; avoid a third full restatement if abstract + §1 already carry it.

**5. Submission metadata tone.** The manuscript header that every number reproduces from `ci/reproduce.json` is lab-correct and OT-strange. Strip or footnote for journal PDF.

Overall style verdict: **ready for polish, not rewrite.**

## Verdict

**Revise and submit — high section fit, conditional on clearing the theory bar in packaging.** The manuscript is the right *kind* of Catalysing & Crystallizing essay: it formalizes a presupposition the configurational traditions share, borrows from a neighbour in the CfP's invited way, answers the disorganizing question without reducing it to reverse formation, and gives power a seat through contingent irreducibility. Theme 3 is primary and strong; theme 1 is delivered as dissolution theory; theme 2 is partial and adequate if not overclaimed.

**Most important fix before submission:** packaging against methods drift (C4–C7) plus the QCA handshake in abstract and §1 (C5), so a set-analytic desk or panel does not read competition where the essay intends complement. Absolute word/abstract limits still need a live check against Sage OT instructions (C2, C11).

**Biggest strength for the section:** a foundation criterion that lets constitution, membership, and asymmetric dissolution be stated as concepts the field can use — illustrated, not measured — on the exact platform/algorithm terrain the call highlights.

**What only the authors and editors can settle:** final abstract word limit and whether Catalysing & Crystallizing prefers question titles; whether mid-body population counts stay or move entirely into a short online appendix. Those are production decisions, not conceptual ones. The affirmative theoretical claim — that integrated information supplies a formal account of configurational constitution — should not be traded away to look modest; altitude belongs in validation, not in demoting the apparatus.
