**Title:** Algorithmacy at Work: Measuring the Competence to Coordinate With Colleagues Through Generative AI

## Abstract
Generative AI now stands between colleagues. It can draft appraisals, screen submissions and gate work through automated checks, so much of the relational experience of generative AI at work is the experience of reaching another person through a system that evaluates both. Existing measures of competence with algorithms score a person's relation to the system alone. A validated scale of platform workers' algorithmic competency asks about understanding, embracing, leveraging and remediating algorithmic management. None of its items concerns coordinating with the human counterpart. We develop and test the Algorithmacy Competence Scale, a 12-item measure with three facets. The first is counterpart inference, which means reconstructing what a hidden counterpart wants from outcomes. The second is signal compression, which means expressing intent in the few signals the system accepts. The third is rule-change tracking, which means noticing changes the system makes without announcement. In a three-wave panel of a 16-week developer cohort, in which work passes peer review, votes and automated checks, we test the three-factor structure, measurement invariance across waves, growth over the program, and discriminant validity against the algorithmic-competency scale. The hypotheses and decision rules were fixed before data collection.
