### 1.1 "Meaning-affording design" / "meaning-hostile environment"

**Question.** Is "meaning-affording" anyone's term? If not, which real vocabulary from the
wellbeing-in-design literature carries the draft's pairing of an environment that supports meaning
against one that strips it?

**Hypothesis, sharpened.** "Meaning-affording" is almost certainly the draft's own coinage. The
replacement is not one term but a pairing the self-determination-theory (SDT) design literature
already owns: *need-supportive* versus *need-thwarting* environments, with autonomy, competence and
relatedness as the needs, and *eudaimonic* versus *hedonic* experience as the HCI gloss on meaning.
Peters, Calvo and Ryan (2018) is the paper most likely to carry that vocabulary in a design frame;
Mekler and Hornbæk (2019) supplies "meaning" as a first-class UX construct (their framework's
components are the ones to compare with Martela and Steger's coherence/purpose/significance, which
`martela2016.md` already holds). The draft's "meaning-hostile" is attributed in the Berthon section
to Berthon himself; `berthon2019.md` contains no such phrase, but Berthon, Pitt and Campbell (2019),
"Addictive De-Vices," is a real, uncarded, seven-author-word-away candidate for the "algorithmic
addictions," "vanity metrics" and "attention" material the draft hangs on Berthon.

**Sources.**
- Peters, D., Calvo, R. A., & Ryan, R. M. (2018). Designing for motivation, engagement and wellbeing
  in digital experience. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 9, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797 **(planner-
  verified: DOI, authors, journal, volume)**; article number 797 **[check]**. Open access.
- Calvo, R. A., & Peters, D. (2014). *Positive Computing: Technology for Wellbeing and Human
  Potential*. MIT Press. Not verified today; ISBN and the chapter that defines the programme
  **[check]**. Card only if the 2018 paper does not already carry the definitions.
- Mekler, E. D., & Hornbæk, K. (2019). A framework for the experience of meaning in human-computer
  interaction. *CHI 2019*, pp. 1–15, doi:10.1145/3290605.3300455 **(planner-verified)**. Their 2016
  CHI paper on eudaimonic vs. hedonic UX ("Momentary pleasure or lasting meaning?") **[check]** —

- Berthon, P., Pitt, L., & Campbell, C. (2019). Addictive de-vices: A public policy analysis of

### 1.2 Verbeek's "Constructive Technology Assessment (CTA)"

**Question.** Does Verbeek use CTA in his own voice as a constructive remedy, or is the draft
conflating him with Rip and Schot?

**Hypothesis, corrected.** The audit's conflation hypothesis is wrong at the level of the abstract.
`verbeek2006.md` quotes the abstract of *Materializing Morality* (STHV 31(3): 361–380) as
"elaborat[ing] two methods for anticipating technological mediation in the design process:
performing mediation analyses and using an augmented version of constructive technology
assessment." CTA is Verbeek's own adopted vocabulary — borrowed, and explicitly *augmented*, from
the Dutch STS tradition. The draft's sentence ("Verbeek's constructive remedies — anticipatory design
and CTA") is defensible. What the card cannot yet say is how Verbeek augments CTA, whether the
augmentation reaches the user as a participant or only the designer, and how much of the paper CTA
occupies (one paragraph or a section). That determines whether the draft's next sentence — that CTA
"presume[s] a participatory, democratic design context where designers and users collaboratively
negotiate" — is Verbeek's claim about his method or the draft's gloss on Rip and Schot's.

**Sources.**

  browser session; read pp. 361–380 whole; locate the CTA passage by page; upgrade the card from

### 1.5 AAVE and LLM bias

**Question.** What is the current, citable evidence that LLMs penalize African American English, and
does it support the draft's specific claim about *educational and evaluative* LLMs?

**Hypothesis, sharpened.** Hofmann, Kalluri, Jurafsky and King (2024), *Nature* 633(8028): 147–154,
doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07856-5 **(planner-verified)** is the anchor: matched-guise probing shows
LLMs assign AAE speakers lower-prestige jobs, more convictions and more death sentences, with covert
bias surviving alignment. But its decision domains are employment and criminal justice, not
education. The draft's "educational and evaluative LLMs routinely penalize AAVE … as logically
flawed or grammatically deficient" is a different claim, and I know of no primary study that shows
it for LLM-based grading with the confidence the draft asserts. The expected outcome is that the
draft narrows to what Hofmann et al. show, with Sap et al. (2019) as the earlier toxicity-detection
anchor. Read Hofmann's Discussion for any sentence about education; search once, briefly, for an
LLM-grading study of AAE **[check: find or drop, do not stretch]**.

**Sources.**
- Hofmann et al. 2024, *Nature* — paywalled; the arXiv version (2403.00742) is open; quotations must
  be located in the version of record or flagged as arXiv-located.
- Sap, M., Card, D., Gabriel, S., Choi, Y., & Smith, N. A. (2019). The risk of racial bias in hate

### 1.7 "Counter-agency"

**Question.** Does any literature anchor "counter-agency" in the sense the draft's closing line
needs — a party's own agent acting inside the coordination — or should the draft use its own
Principle I term?

**Hypothesis, sharpened and pessimistic.** The audit offers "algorithmic resistance" (Velkova &
Kaun) and DiSalvo's "adversarial design." Neither is likely to fit. Velkova and Kaun's article,
"Algorithmic resistance: media practices and the politics of repair," *Information, Communication
& Society* 24(4): 523–540 (online first 2019, print 2021; doi:10.1080/1369118x.2019.1657162
**(planner-verified)**), studies mundane user tactics that *repair* a recommender toward what the
user wants — individual, tactical, de Certeau-shaped — which is closer to the subject-side

agonistic publics through exhibition pieces; `disalvo2012.md` (secondary, via Kiem's hostile review;
S2 could not verify) already argues the fit is real at the level of intent and false at the level of
form. The prior harness dropped him because "the Simmel reading carries the politics without them."
The likely finding is that no external term anchors "counter-agency," and the draft should replace
the phrase with *counter-delegation* framed, per `RESEARCH_DOSSIER.md` §4, as the micro-instrument
of collectively won standing — a term this arm already owns and has grounded.

**On reinstating DiSalvo.** Reinstate only if the agent can open the book and the "articulating
collectives" chapter (ubicomp; *Natural Fuse*, *CCD-Me Not Umbrella*) turns out to describe a
user-side device acting against a monitoring party — which would put it beside Brunton and
Nissenbaum's obfuscation cluster, not beside Principle I. The prior drop decision stands unless the
primary text says something Simmel plus `bruntonnissenbaum2015.md` do not. The agent reports the
decision either way; silent reintroduction is what the audit warned against.

**Sources.**

  Card as `velkova2021.md`, dated 2021 with the online-first date noted.
- DiSalvo 2012 — MIT Press Direct returned 403 last round; try Internet Archive lending, Google

## 3. Harness design — three parallel passes

Three Fable research agents, run in parallel, each writing cards to
`literature/library/` in this arm's format: the header line, then
**Identifier · Read depth · Source-tier · Evidence basis · Parties modeled · Relation last checked**,
then *What it argues*, *Relation to the argument*, *Caution*. `Remedy locus:` and `Adaptivity:` are
not required on this pass (they served the RQ12 census); `Parties modeled:` is required and, for the
bias-audit papers, the honest value is "none — a corpus/model audit; no coordination modeled."
Relation sections are written against the specific draft sentence the card repairs, quoted, with the
replacement wording the agent proposes.

**Disciplines, carried over from `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §6 and binding on every pass:**

1. Primary source or nothing. Read depth recorded as `full_text`, `partial` (name the sections),
   `abstract_only` or `secondary` (name the carrier). A card below full text says so in its first
   line.
2. Access flags by name — paywalled, deposit behind a challenge page, lending-locked — and which
   route worked. Verbeek 2006 and DiSalvo 2012 have failed twice; a third failure is a finding,
   recorded, not a reason to paraphrase.
3. No invented anything. No page, quotation, DOI, volume or author list the agent did not see. The
   planner-verified facts in §1 may be reused; everything marked **[check]** is resolved or dropped
   and listed as dropped in the memo.
4. `Parties modeled:` on every card. `Remedy locus:` not required.
5. Relation section written against the draft sentence it repairs, with proposed replacement
   wording, and saying outright when the source cuts against the draft's use of it. For this pass
   that has teeth in three places: T1 is expected to report that "meaning-affording" is nobody's
   term; T2 that Hofmann does not cover education; T3 that Velkova and Kaun's resistance is
   subject-side repair, not counter-delegation.
6. Caution section on every card: genre, sample, what the source does not show.
7. A closing memo per pass, at most 300 words: the cards written, every source dropped and why, the
   proposed wording for each draft phrase the pass repairs, and anything that contradicts the
   hypothesis in §1.

**After the three report**, the coordinating agent appends a short "Cluster T — terminology pass

## 4. Adversarial verification — a targeted re-check, not a batch

The prior harnesses ran three S2 batches over 99 cards and caught roughly one real error per ten
cards — a misattributed MacIntyre citation, page slips, one paraphrase presented as an abstract's
wording. That rate argues for *some* second look here. The scale argues against a full batch:
fifteen cards, none of them load-bearing for the thesis (the category-error argument rests on the
fifteen-card census, which this pass does not touch), and most of them attribution repairs whose
bibliographic records the planner has already resolved.

The proportionate design is a single re-check step, run by one agent after the three passes report,
limited to the cards that will carry a **verbatim quotation** into the draft: `wangkosinski2018`
(the "inevitable" passage and its page break), `hofmann2024` (whatever sentence the draft quotes),
`verbeek2006` if upgraded (the CTA passage), and `berthon2019b` if T1 finds the "floor" sentence
there. Four cards, one re-opening each, string-match the quotation, check the page, write the
verdict block in the existing S2 format. Cards that carry only a bibliographic record and a
paraphrase (Caliskan, Buolamwini & Gebru, Sap, Holland, Schot & Rip, Zuboff) get no S2; their
Crossref records are already confirmed and the risk is a wrong paraphrase, which the author's own
read of the card catches. If the re-check finds an error in any of the four, extend it to the rest of
that pass's cards. Budget: under an hour of agent time, against roughly a day for the three passes.

## 5. Which item is most likely to fail

Counter-agency (1.7) is the item whose working hypothesis is most likely to be wrong, and the
failure will be a useful one. The audit's two candidates pull in opposite directions from what the
draft needs: Velkova and Kaun's "algorithmic resistance" is everyday repair by individual users of
recommenders — tactical, de Certeau-shaped, and exactly the subject-side accommodation this paper
says cannot carry the remedy — while DiSalvo's adversarial design builds agonistic publics through
exhibition pieces and, on the only account this arm has of it, fits the triad in intent but not in
form. Neither names a party's own agent acting inside a live coordination, and the prior harness's
own search (`counter_agent_search_notes.md`) found that idea only in 2025–26 preprints. The probable
result is that no external term anchors "counter-agency," and the draft should retire the phrase in
favour of *counter-delegation*, which this arm already grounds and already frames honestly as the
micro-instrument of collectively won standing. The audit's other risky guesses have already resolved
in the planning read: Gender Shades was the wrong referent for "Stanford" (Wang and Kosinski is
right, and says "inevitable" in print), and Verbeek's CTA is his own adopted term, not a conflation.
The residual risk in the remaining items is a narrowing, not a failure — Hofmann et al. will make
the draft say "employment and criminal-justice decisions" where it now says "educational and
evaluative LLMs."
