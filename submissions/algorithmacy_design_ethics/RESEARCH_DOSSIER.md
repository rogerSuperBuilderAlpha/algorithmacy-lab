# Research Dossier — Algorithmacy Is Not Literacy (category-error pivot)

Synthesis of the pivot's Fable-planned harness: 6 parallel research passes (P1–P6) against the 12
research questions in `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`, followed by 3 parallel adversarial-verification passes
covering all 99 touched cards. 167 total card files now sit in `literature/library/` (99 from the
first harness, 68 new from this pivot), indexed in `literature/library/INDEX.md`. This dossier maps
what the pivot's research found back onto the six critiques and the three design principles, runs
the census that tests the thesis itself, and closes with a confirmed thesis, an annotated outline,
and the target-journal decision.

## 1. The census that tests the thesis — and it holds

RQ12 asked the single question the whole pivot stands or falls on: do the six critiqued accounts
(Vallor, Verbeek, Winner, Martela & Steger, Berthon, and the Context Engine) all locate their remedy
in the subject, the artifact, or an adoption decision — never in the relation between the two
coordinated people? If even two of the six put the remedy in the relation, the paper has four
quibbles and a list of design preferences, not one argument.

**The census, run by grep across every card's `Remedy locus:` field:**

| Account | Card(s) | Remedy locus |
|---|---|---|
| Vallor | vallor2015, vallor2016, vallor2024 | subject, subject, subject |
| Verbeek | verbeek2014, verbeek2015, verbeek2008b, verbeek2011 | subject, artifact, artifact, artifact |
| Verbeek | verbeek2008 | none |
| Winner | winner1980, winner1977 | adoption, adoption |
| Winner | winner1993 | none |
| Martela & Steger | martela2016 | none |
| Berthon | berthon2019 | subject |
| Context Engine | mechanistic_interpretability_method, c2pa_content_provenance_standard | artifact, artifact |

**Zero of fifteen cards land in "relation."** The 24 cards across the whole library that *do* carry
`Remedy locus: relation` are, without exception, sources the paper cites for its own proposed
principles — Viljoen, Delacroix, Zong & Matias, Brunton & Nissenbaum, GDPR/DSA/DMA/DGA, Hirschman,
Simmel, Calacci & Pentland — not instances of the critiqued paradigm. That is exactly the shape the
thesis predicts: the establishment's remedies cluster at subject/artifact/adoption; the paper's own
proposed instruments are the only ones that reach the relation. The category-error claim is
confirmed as a checkable fact about these fifteen texts, not asserted as an impression.

## 2. The six critiques — what survived, what reformulated, what strengthened

**Verbeek (RQ1) — reformulated, and this was the pivot's highest-stakes verification.** The
inherited "static script" critique is dead, confirmed independently by two passes and cross-checked
by a third: Verbeek's mediators are explicitly active ("not merely silent 'intermediaries' but
active 'mediators,'" 2014, p. 77), and his "persuasive mirror" case already has technologies
"us[ing] human beings ... to do their work" (2014, p. 83). What survives, sharper and now
evidenced at census depth, is that Verbeek's vocabulary is **fixed, not adaptive**: a search of the
full text of *Moralizing Technology* and the 2014 chapter found zero occurrences of "adaptive,"
"algorithm," or "profile," and the very volume Verbeek co-edited contains both Floridi & Sanders'
adaptability criterion (a bot that "adapts its behaviour to our preferences" and must "learn the
user's preferences") and Introna's chapter on Turnitin ("updates itself at a rate of 60 million
pages per day") — his own chapter, in his own book, takes up neither. Multistability, his one
dynamic notion, varies on the human side over an artifact that holds still. A 2024 paper by Wellner
shows the adaptivity axis is nameable inside postphenomenology's own vocabulary ("different
responses ... even to the same user at the same time and place") — later, and outside Verbeek's own
schema, which sharpens rather than softens the critique: the school could have this term and
doesn't use it where Verbeek writes.

**Vallor (RQ2) — reformulated from capability-matching to occasions-not-capacity.** The archived
plan's version (a supervisor "must have more experience and practical wisdom than the supervisee")
is real but distinct from the new claim. The 2016 book, read to partial/snippet depth after every
full-text route failed, confirms the sharper version: virtue cultivation is slow and depends on
"sufficient opportunity for repetition," can be inhibited by "material and social deprivations," and
— the strongest single find — contains a section titled "Communicative Friction" (pp. 161–164)
naming "frictionless interactions" a "dubious ideal" and closing on the "Ignored Aunt," who "will
soon question why she's bothering ... and stop caring." That is the paper's disenfranchisement
mechanism in Vallor's own words, independent of the print-history analogy entirely. Institutional
grounding is partial, not absent (MacIntyre: "a moral practice is irreducibly a social enterprise,"
pp. 45–49) — the honest reading is that Vallor's remedy locus stays the subject because the
institutions her own book gestures toward aren't yet designed for a mediator that sets tempo, which
is exactly what this paper designs. The MacIntyre citation error from the prior harness (wrongly
attributed to the 2015 paper) did not reappear; both passes and S2 confirm it stays fixed to the
2016 book.

**Winner (RQ3) — confirmed and strengthened with a citation the paper didn't have before.** "No
micro-level design guidance" was already established in the prior harness (winner1980: "the
person's only political move is the adoption decision, which in the platform case is not theirs").
New this round: *Autonomous Technology* (1977) supplies "reverse adaptation" — "the adjustment of
human ends to match the character of the available means" (p. 229, verified twice, independently,
against the running heads) — which gives the paper an actual Winner citation for the mechanism its
historical analogy needs, rather than an unsupported assertion. Sharper still: Winner himself
already rejects the cultivate-the-subject remedy, a generation before Vallor, at pp. 304–305. The
paper can now say Winner and Vallor converge on the diagnosis from opposite directions and neither
supplies the design consequence.

**Martela & Steger (RQ4) — the second highest-stakes verification, and it inverted cleanly.**
Genuinely unverified before this pivot; now read at full text and confirmed independently twice
(the research pass and an S2 re-check, both string-matching the same five pages). Their own
definition of coherence, verbatim: "coherence centers on the perception that stimuli are predictable
and conform to recognizable patterns" (p. 534); "Human effort to find coherence is thus an attempt
to create accurate mental models of the world to facilitate predictability and consistency" (p.
536). Coherence on their account is **predictability-shaped, not explanation-shaped** — nothing in
it requires knowing *why* a regularity holds. Two consequences, both now settled rather than
hypothesized: the "generative AI makes coherence mathematically impossible, guaranteeing paralysis"
claim is the seeding report's inference, not Martela & Steger's own argument, and must soften to a
testable claim about strain on the comprehensibility facet; and — the more important result —
**Martela & Steger convert from a critique target into an ally of Principle III.** Operational
predictability is what their own construct names. A mechanistic-interpretability reading of
"coherence" would be the misreading.

**Berthon (RQ5) — confirmed exactly as suspected, now three times over.** Independently verified by
the planning read, the full research pass, and an S2 re-check: Berthon & Pitt (2019, *Business
Horizons* 62(2): 131–137) never use "technology fast," never discuss economic survival or an
inability to opt out. Their remedy is entirely subject-side — four modes of the user's own
attention, closing on "wise mindfulness ... is essential if we are to see how our minds are
undermined by the technologies and content we interact with" (p. 136). The "mandatory condition for
economic survival" framing is this paper's own extension of the typology and must be argued in the
paper's own voice, not attributed to Berthon. A genuinely useful find replaces the weak
companion-source idea: Williams (2018), *Stand Out of Our Light*, is a **contrast**, not a
companion — he names and rejects every subject-side remedy, including employee mindfulness by name
("as dangerous as it is futile," pp. 101–102), and supplies what may be the best existing precursor
statement of algorithmacy itself: platform logic "pushed below the surface of awareness to the
automation layer" (p. 90). Note for the draft: Berthon shares the author's institution
(Bentley); the paper should disclose this rather than leave it for a referee to notice.

**Context Engine (RQ6) — carries forward unchanged, now with the field explicit.** Both cards
confirmed clean from the first harness; this round only added the `Remedy locus: artifact`
classification to make the census answerable by grep. The finding stands: mechanistic
interpretability discloses to whoever holds the weights, C2PA discloses to a downstream validator;
neither has any representation of a second human party. Principle III's critique of the same target
runs on a different ground (predictability, not visibility) and the two should coexist in the draft,
neither absorbing the other.

## 3. The historical section — supportable, but not in the form the sibling arm already drafted

**The analogy is supportable at the register the paper actually needs.** Innis (*The Bias of
Communication*, read to full depth) is the cleanest institutional source: media are held by classes
and organizations, "monopolies of knowledge" are realigned by new media, and print installs a
monopoly of its own — not the end of monopoly. Havelock supplies the modest form: the binding
variable is "the supply of readers," which depends on schooling, which lags the technology.
Clanchy's documented case (trust migrating from persons to documents, England 1066–1307) is at
exactly the register the paper's bridging section needs. Johns confirmed as an **ally**: print's
"fixity" was "as much a product of social actions as of the inherent properties of the press" (2002,
p. 121) — an achievement of institutions, not a property of the technology — which converts directly
into the paper's own thesis: if fixity was institutionally built, so is algorithmacy, and Principles
I–III are that building.

**What must be conceded, explicitly, in one sentence where the analogy is used:** the "great divide"
cognitive thesis (that literacy remakes cognition itself) is contested by Street, Finnegan,
Halverson, and Scribner & Cole's empirical Vai study, and the paper's claim needs only the
weaker, institutional register (competences and record-keeping, not cognition) to survive the
concession entirely. Graff's "literacy myth" cuts closest to the paper's disenfranchisement claim:
his finding is that literacy did **not** deliver the mobility promised for it. The paper must claim
only that institutions came to *require* literacy, never that literacy *rewarded* the literate —
Graff does not contest the former.

**A finding that needs a decision, not just a note:** the sibling arm `algorithmacy_scaffolding`'s
own drafted conclusion — "clerical and scribal guild" monopoly, print "shattered that monopoly,"
readers left "stripped of agency" — is not merely unsourced (as already flagged twice in that arm's
own dossier) but **actively contradicted** by the sources now verified. Clanchy shows lay documents
in ordinary use thirteenth-century England; Goody's "restricted literacy" treats limited access as
writing's normal condition, not a guarded monopoly; Johns dates the "monopoly shattered by print"
story to a c. 1800 historiographic construction; Innis says print installed a new monopoly, not an
end to monopoly; "stripped of agency" has no source anywhere in this cluster and is close to the
specific myth Graff's book is against. This arm is sourcing the history and can supply corrected
cards; the scaffolding essay's conclusion needs its own attention separately, flagged here rather
than fixed here.

## 4. The three design principles — grounded, with one honest gap

**Principle I (agentic parity / counter-delegation) — real, but thinner than hoped, and the paper
should say so.** The research sorted the whole cluster cleanly into two mechanisms the draft must
not conflate: **collective standing** (Viljoen, Delacroix, Benn & Lazar, Fink, Micheli, Johnston,
Aloisi, the EU Data Governance Act — well-grounded, argues *why* standing must be collective) and
**counter-delegation proper**, a client-side agent acting inside the coordination in real time
(thin: only Brunton & Nissenbaum's TrackMeNot/AdNauseam and Calacci & Pentland's collective Shipt
Calculator are real, peer-reviewed instruments; no peer-reviewed work studies a personal agent
contesting a platform's agent in a live coordination — that idea currently lives only in 2025–26
preprints). The honest framing, and the one the paper should use: Principle I is the micro-instrument
of collectively won standing, not a substitute literature of its own. Keller (2021) supplies the
skeptic's case against middleware that the paper must answer, not just cite past.

**Principle II (structural refusal) — solid.** `lyons2021.md` anchors it cleanly (Australia's
robo-debt scheme, contested successfully case by case for four years "while the system was allowed
to remain in place" — contestability is "the last line of defence," and contest "places a burden on
the decision subject"). Zong & Matias (2024) supply a design framework for refusal; Hirschman's
exit/voice, GDPR Arts. 21–22, and DSA's non-profiling recommender option give it legal teeth. Both
previously-⚠ cards (`hirsch2017.md`, `alfrink2023.md`) are now ✓.

**Principle III (bounded outputs / operational predictability) — solid, and precisely distinguished
from its nearest rival.** `bainbridge1983.md`, `miller2019.md`, and `floridisanders2004.md` anchor
it; new sources give it real engineering grounding — Muir (1994, predictability/dependability/faith,
the source Lee & See themselves build on), Leveson (safety as constraint enforcement), documented
model drift (Chen, Zaharia & Zou), and SAE J3016's "operational design domain" for the driver
analogy's own engineering term. Rudin (2019) is confirmed as a **contrast, not an ally**, on three
precise grounds the draft can use directly: she constrains model *class* (interpretable internal
structure), Principle III constrains deployed *behavior over time*; she assumes an expert user,
Principle III assumes a lay coordinated party who — per Bainbridge — cannot check a faster decider's
criteria in real time; her Goodhart-style optimism assumes disclosure aligns the objective, which
fails exactly when the objective is the operator's, not the user's.

## 5. Housekeeping resolved this round

- **Simmel's "mediator" vs. this paper's "mediator":** resolved in body text, not a footnote — the
  draft states, on first co-occurrence, that Simmel's self-effacing mediator is this paper's
  moderator, and his arbitrator/tertius gaudens is this paper's mediator. Simmel's own pagination
  question (1902 AJS vs. Wolff 1950) is resolved: cite the 1902 AJS text for wording, Wolff at
  section level only — Wolff's translation could not be independently reached this round.
- **"Moderator" also risks reading as content moderation** (Gillespie's *Custodians of the
  Internet* is in this readership's working vocabulary) — the definition welds the intended
  panel-chair/reactor sense onto the term at first use; the fallback if a read-aloud still finds it
  misleading is "conduit."
- A handful of page-citation errors were caught and fixed across the three S2 batches (page slips,
  one attribution mixup between Lyons's own wording and Alfrink's paraphrase of her, one card
  downgraded for unverifiable secondhand quotation marks) — none touched a load-bearing claim. Full
  detail in each card's dated verification block.

## 6. Thesis, confirmed

**The category-error thesis survives its own test.** Six accounts, checked against primary sources
rather than a discredited secondary stack, locate their remedy in the subject (Vallor, Verbeek's use
passage, Berthon), the artifact (most of Verbeek), or an adoption decision (Winner) — one (Martela &
Steger) turns out to have no quarrel with the paper's own Principle III at all — and none locates it
in the relation between the two coordinated people. That is not an impression; it is now a
fifteen-card census. Three design principles follow with real, checkable literatures behind two of
them and an honestly-flagged thin spot in the third.

## 7. Target journal and structure

**One paper, *Philosophy & Technology* first, *Techné* second, 11,000–12,000 words**, using the
compression device the plan identified: the six critiques pair into three, each pair yielding one
principle (Verbeek's use-passage + Winner's adoption politics → Principle I; Vallor's cultivated
subject + Berthon's trained attention → Principle II; Martela & Steger's coherence + the Context
Engine's interpretability → Principle III). *Ethics and Information Technology*'s 8,000-word cap
rules it out for the full argument; if the draft exceeds 13,000 words, the pre-drawn cut line splits
it into a theory paper (construct, ladder, history, six critiques) for *Philosophy & Technology* and
a design paper (three principles' evidence) for *Ethics and Information Technology*.

## 8. Annotated outline

1. **Opening** — the print/oracy analogy stated as precedent for a *kind* of mistake, not evidence
   that generative AI is such a transition; the structural three-case ladder (carried over
   unmodified from the archived draft) is what actually demonstrates the distinction.
2. **The construct** — moderator/mediator, the Simmel disambiguation stated once in text, the two
   axes.
3. **The history** — Innis, Havelock, Clanchy at the institutional register; the great-divide
   concession in one sentence; Johns turned into support.
4. **The six critiques as three pairs** — each pair closing on the principle it motivates, with the
   RQ12 census stated as the section's own closing evidence.
5. **The three principles** — Principle I framed honestly as the micro-instrument of collective
   standing; Principle II anchored on Lyons/robo-debt; Principle III distinguished precisely from
   Rudin.
6. **Close** — the Simmel *tertius gaudens* reframing of who adversarial design targets; return to
   why the report that prompted this paper reached for the wrong century's remedy.
