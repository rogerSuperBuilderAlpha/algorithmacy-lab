## The new thesis

**Algorithmacy is not literacy extended to a new medium — the way literacy was not oracy extended
to a new medium.** Print did not give oral culture a mechanical speaker; it replaced an entire
episteme, and those who kept relying on oracy-era habits (memorization, personal testimony, spoken
rhetoric) were structurally disenfranchised. Generative AI is enacting the same kind of rupture, and
the AI ethics establishment — prompt engineering, mechanistic interpretability, model cards, C2PA,
media literacy campaigns — is making the mirror-image mistake: treating an active algorithmic third
party as a text to be read more skillfully. Six accounts (Vallor, Verbeek, Winner, Martela & Steger,
Berthon, and this arm's own prior "Context Engine" critique) are each, on this reading,
literacy-paradigm tools misapplied to a problem that is not textual. Three design principles follow:
agentic parity via counter-delegation, not legibility; structural refusal, not hermeneutics; and
bounded outputs / operational predictability, not mechanistic interpretability.

## What carries forward from the archived work, and what changes

**The structural core carries forward, redirected, not replaced.** The archived draft's channel/wire
(Φ=0) vs. party (Φ>0) distinction is the same distinction the new thesis names with different words:
a **moderator** is a channel — literacy suffices, the Φ=0 case; a **mediator** is a party — the
coordination is irreducible, Φ>0, and algorithmacy is what the situation demands. The three-case
ladder from the archived draft (the CPDLC party-line case, the 1986 Winograd-Flores Coordinator, gig
platforms) still carries the evidence for this distinction and is not being re-derived. What changes
is the target: the new thesis argues against an entire paradigm, not one technical remedy.

**One terminology collision, flagged here so it isn't rediscovered mid-draft:** Simmel
(`literature/library/simmel1950.md`, verified, already load-bearing for the archived draft's §6)
uses "mediator" as a technical term for the *benign, self-effacing* third party — one who "seeks to
eliminate himself... [so] the two disunited parties may unite directly." The new thesis's algorithmic
"mediator" is the opposite role — closer to Simmel's *arbitrator* or *tertius gaudens*. Citing Simmel
by name (which the archived work already found load-bearing for the politics of the arrangement)
while also using "mediator" as this piece's own term for the algorithmic third party is a built-in
confusion unless resolved explicitly. Proposed default, for the research pass to confirm or revise:
keep "mediator" for the algorithmic third party, and add an explicit one-sentence disambiguation
against Simmel's usage on first co-occurrence.

## The six critiques are hypotheses, not settled framing — verify before use

**This is the load-bearing caution for this pivot.** The report that originally seeded this arm was
already audited (`literature/library/gemini_report_citation_audit.md`) and found to reach its four
named theoretical anchors — Winner, Vallor, Verbeek, and Martela & Steger, the same four core names
in the new six-thinker list — through garbage secondary sources (a ResearchGate mirror, a
pseudonymous blog, an unlocatable predatory-journal paper, a Scribd upload), never once citing a
primary text. This lab already ruled that report inadmissible as a source. The new critiques' specific
*shape*, not just their citations, is at real risk of having inherited a mischaracterization from
that same discredited stack — and one instance of this is already confirmed:

**`verbeek2014.md`** (in the library, full-text read, S2 status "could not verify" pending
re-access — not dropped, just not yet re-confirmed) directly complicates the "static script" framing
before any new research even starts. Verbeek (2014, p. 77) writes technologies are "not merely
silent 'intermediaries' but active 'mediators'" — the opposite of static — and his "persuasive
mirror" case (p. 83: "technologies use human beings here to do their work") already reaches toward
the objective axis the new critique says he lacks. The defensible version of this critique is
probably not "Verbeek assumes a static script" but something narrower: his relational vocabulary
(embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, background, and the newer cyborg/persuasive/ambient relations)
has no explicit **adaptivity axis** — nothing distinguishes a fixed, one-shot mediation (a speed
bump) from a continuously-retrained one (an LLM). That reformulation is a hypothesis for the next
research pass to confirm, not yet a finding.

The other five critiques (Vallor, Winner, Martela & Steger, Berthon, and the Context Engine
critique) carry the same status — plausible, partially supported by what's already verified, not yet
checked against primary sources with the rigor this lab requires before a claim becomes a draft's
spine. See `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` (once the new Fable pass produces it) for the specific verification
question assigned to each.
