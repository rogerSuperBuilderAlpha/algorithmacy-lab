#### Helen Nissenbaum: Contextual Integrity Without a Counterpart

* **The Steel-Man:** Helen Nissenbaum's contextual integrity does not make the individual the remedy — "burdening individuals with the full weight of protecting their privacy online through notice-and-choice is unlikely to yield success" (Nissenbaum, 2011, p. 43) — nor let the mediator name the game: the Net "does not constitute ... a discrete context" (p. 38), and a platform calling itself "a shoe store, a church" thereby "invites evaluation against respective norms" (p. 44), so a dispatch algorithm answers to employment's norms, not its own. Her Walzer passage is this paper's mechanism — "a form of tyranny when goods of one sphere intrude into, or become dominant in, not only one sphere but many" — and her remedy is a "presumption in favor of the status quo" under which "we initially resist breaches" (2004, p. 145). A platform mediating employment, credit, housing and speech possesses that dominant good: information, "the raw materials of their industry" (2011, p. 44).

* **The Prescriptive Limit:** The benchmark is a benchmark for flows. Its parameters are "actors (subject, sender, recipient), attributes (types of information), and transmission principles" (2011, p. 33), and none of them names a determination — a match or a rank — that binds a second person. The residency match she analysed with Friedman (1996) is two flows and one binding determination; contextual integrity sees the flows. Determinations appear in 2019 only as "differential attention, be it decision, action, reward, or punishment" (p. 242): a harm to the data subject, never a relation between the two people one output binds. The heuristic — "locate contexts, explicate entrenched informational norms, identify disruptive flows, and evaluate" (2011, p. 38) — is run by "the evaluator" (2019, p. 232), never by the person inside it, and technologies now "outpace the capacity to proceed according to the discrete steps of the CI heuristic" by her own account (2019, p. 248). Her promotive term is institutional — "the ends, purposes, and values of respective contexts" (2011, p. 40), which "sometimes may disfavor the data subject's interests" (2019, p. 233). The user-side instrument this account lacks she built elsewhere: obfuscation (Brunton & Nissenbaum, 2015), one author's second answer to the same asymmetry.

**Notes for the coordinating pass.** (1) Rule (2019) is the one published critic; his objection — that a context's norms are "volatile and highly contested" (abstract) — attacks the heuristic's input, this paper's attacks its output and agent; a half-sentence placing the two side by side belongs in the section or in a note, not in the pair above. (2) Do not write "the platform is the dominant good": in Walzer's sense the good is information, and the platform is its possessor (`nissenbaum2004.md`, Relation). (3) The Principle III bridge holds in this form only: contextual integrity supplies the concept of a declared envelope (a commitment to partition holdings, 2011, p. 44) and assigns its enforcement to law and "reasonable expectations," with Google's commitment revocable "at any moment" (2011, p. 43) as her own example of the missing instrument.
