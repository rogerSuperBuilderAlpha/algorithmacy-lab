# IEEE Global Initiative on Ethics of Autonomous and Intelligent Systems (2019). *Ethically Aligned Design: A Vision for Prioritizing Human Well-being with Autonomous and Intelligent Systems*, First Edition. IEEE.

**Identifier:** no DOI; free PDF (294 pp., CC BY-NC 4.0) from standards.ieee.org · **Read depth:** partial — Executive Summary and Introduction (pp. 3–6), From Principles to Practice and General Principles (pp. 9–13), the Methods to Guide Ethical Research and Design chapter (pp. 124–134, sampled), the opening of Embedding Values into A/IS (pp. 169–171), and a passage in Classical Ethics (p. 60); the remaining chapters were searched by keyword, not read · **Source-tier:** consensus document of a professional body, not peer-reviewed · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Parties modeled:** designer–stakeholders (creators, operators, users, "affected parties") · **Relation last checked:** 2026-09-18

## What it argues

Eight General Principles "define imperatives for the ethical design, development, deployment, adoption, and decommissioning" of A/IS, addressed to "creators, i.e., those who design and manufacture, ... operators, i.e., those with expertise specific to use of A/IS, other users, and any other stakeholders or affected parties" (p. 11): human rights, well-being as "a primary success criterion," data agency, effectiveness, transparency — "The basis of a particular A/IS decision should always be discoverable" — accountability — "an unambiguous rationale for all decisions made" — awareness of misuse, and competence (p. 11). The Methods chapter is the institutional VSD: "A/IS embody values and biases that can influence important social processes like voting, policing, and banking," so design "should be instantiated through values-based research and design methods," which "will require ... a commitment ... of businesses to values that transcend narrow economic incentives" (p. 124); it cites IEEE P7000 as the process vehicle (p. 125). Its "Stakeholder inclusion" issue recommends "a planned and controlled set of activities to account for the interests of the full range of stakeholders or practitioners who will be working alongside A/IS" (p. 133), and its "Values-based design" issue says advocates of ethical design "should be seen as innovators seeking the best outcomes for the company, end users, and society" (p. 134). Embedding Values proposes that systems "adopt, learn, and follow the norms and values of the community they serve," that norms rather than values are the tractable target, and that norms' "dynamically changing nature ... requires A/IS to have the capacity to update their norms and learn new ones" (pp. 169–171). The one place the document asks the construct's second question is in Classical Ethics: "any notion of those machines working for or against human beings becomes contested. Does the machine work for someone in particular, or for particular groups but not others? Who decides on the parameters? Is it the machine itself?" (p. 60). Across 294 pages "contest" appears twice and "recourse" or "redress" three times.

## Relation to the argument

EAD is where VSD's consensual stakeholder model becomes institutional doctrine, and the doctrine is explicit: the company's, the end users' and society's interests are written as jointly optimisable, conflict is handled as organisational culture, and the p. 124 hope that business will adopt "values that transcend narrow economic incentives" is the document's whole answer to an operator whose objective is extraction. The p. 60 passage shows that the objective axis was visible to the drafters — "does the machine work for someone in particular ... who decides on the parameters?" — and was left as a question rather than made a principle. The Transparency principle is per-decision discoverability of "the basis of a particular decision," which warrants Seamful Boundary Disclosure at the level of the decision but not disclosure of the objective; Accountability's "unambiguous rationale" comes closer. Embedding Values assumes one served community with one norm network; a mediated coordination has two parties with different norms, and the document has no device for the case where the intermediary serves neither. Contestation is nearly absent, which places EAD at the consensual end of the axis that runs from VSD to Alfrink et al. (2023). None of this cuts against the affordances; it shows they would have no institutional hook for the objective axis if EAD were their only standard.

## Caution

A consensus document assembled by hundreds of volunteers, uneven in register, with recommendations at a high level of abstraction and "further resources" in place of argument; I read perhaps a tenth of it closely and searched the rest, so passages elsewhere (Policy, Law, Personal Data) may treat redress in ways this card does not capture. It is not evidence of anything about practice, and the P7000-series standards it points to are the operative texts, which are paywalled. Page numbers are the PDF's printed folios.
