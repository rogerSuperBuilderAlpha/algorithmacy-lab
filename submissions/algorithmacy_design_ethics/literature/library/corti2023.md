# Corti, L., Brizi, M. R., Pennacchini, M., & Bertolaso, M. (2023). Technological grandparents: How communication technologies can improve the well-being of the elderly? *AI & Society*, online first (14 March 2023), pp. 1–8.

## What it argues

Corti and colleagues set out Ihde's four relations (embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, background), quoting Ihde on alterity as "a quasi-otherness, stronger than mere objectness but weaker than the otherness found within the animal kingdom or the human one," and then object that the framework "offers an overall stratification of technological mediation, which takes place on the basis of an ecological assumption that neglects the main relational dimension of the human being and the communication issue." If the human–human relation is "a founding ontological fact, we realize that the analysis proposed by Don Ihde does not comprehend this articulation and, therefore, ignores the power of technological mediation, as represented in the human–human interaction." They propose "a fifth category, which we could call human relation," written "I−Technologies−Human," with two novelties: "the emphasis on the human being as the third element of relationship" and "the presence of at least two technological devices and two relationship, mediated by technology, which takes us from the technology-world relation to a threefold relation: technology-world-human." Each of the two mediated legs "can be described according to all the possibilities expressed by Don Ihde." What the category is for is care: its "essential feature" is "reciprocity," including "the generational and skills differences between the two users," and it "allows for the caring dimension in the human relationship." The empirical part is a questionnaire run in Italy during the first COVID-19 phase on grandparents' use of ICTs to reach grandchildren; I did not extract the sample size.

## Relation to the argument

RQ1 directly. The plan asked whether postphenomenology has a canonical two-human schema. The answer this source supports is: not in the canon, and one proposal at the margin. Corti et al. say in print that Ihde's framework does not comprehend human–human mediation, and their I–Technologies–Human is the only postphenomenological two-human notation I found. Their schema differs from the construct in three ways the paper can use. It has two devices and two mediated legs, not one intermediary reading both sides; it keeps the technology as a transparent relay between two mutual relations, so there is no third objective anywhere in it; and it is built for reciprocity and care, not for a mediator that commits determinations. The construct's bilateral hermeneutic relation is therefore not pre-empted, but the paper must cite this as prior art for the two-human extension and say what it adds: a single interpreting mediator, the objective axis, and a threshold. For RQ2, nothing.

Against the tightened definition (adaptivity pass, 2026-09-18). Corti et al. are the one source in the cluster whose remedy sits in the relation between two people: the fifth category exists so that "the caring dimension in the human relationship" can be described, and its "essential feature" is "reciprocity." That is the locus the paper says the six accounts miss, so this card is the paper's proof that the locus is nameable in postphenomenological terms. What Corti et al. put there, though, is a moderator in the construct's sense: two devices, two legs, each describable by Ihde's relations, carrying the grandparent's and the grandchild's signals without interpreting either. The mediator does nothing of its own and could not, since it never reads both parties at once, so the adaptivity question does not arise and the competence the schema calls for is literacy on each leg (the "generational and skills differences between the two users"). The construct's mediator is what you get when the two legs are fused into one interpreting party that pursues a third objective and re-fits itself to each user; Corti et al. supply the topology, not the party.

## Caution

A short paper in a general AI-and-society venue by authors from a bioethics group, not from the postphenomenology school; the field itself (Ihde, Rosenberger, Verbeek) has not, to my knowledge, adopted or replied to the fifth category, and I could not check the field guide (Rosenberger & Verbeek 2015) to confirm its absence there. The Ihde quotation is given through their text and I have no page number for it. The empirical section is a pandemic-period questionnaire whose design and n I did not verify; use the source for the schema, not for findings.
