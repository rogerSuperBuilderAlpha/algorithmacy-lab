## What it argues

The target is social constructivism as codified in Bijker, Hughes and Pinch (1987), which the essay's first footnote calls "the best statement of the general aims and approaches of this school of thought." Footnote 2 explains the title: "The use of black boxes is a common engineering practice, a way of abbreviating complex technical processes so that the work of design can proceed," and engineering education "is also filled with black boxes that students are taught to view as convenient 'plug-ins' for problem solving." Footnote 3 is the tone of the piece: Pinch and Bijker "show little awareness of the literature in philosophy and technology, past or present. That does not prevent them from delivering a peremptory judgment on the matter." Footnote 4 concedes the individual studies "could well stand on [their] own merits" and directs the attack at "a concerted push to affirm social constructivism as a coherent mode of analysis and to include or exclude writers according to their degree of adherence to this new canonical standard." Footnote 6 exempts Ruth Schwartz Cowan and Brian Martin's Wollongong group. (All six footnotes read directly on the SAGE page.)

The body, as carried by citing papers, makes four charges. (i) Consequences: constructivism "disregards the consequences of technology" and, per one carrier's page cite, Winner calls on future work "to place a greater emphasis on the social dynamics and social consequences of technical choices" (p. 371, as cited in a 2025 HCI paper). (ii) Excluded voices: its "relevant social actors who are engaged in the process of defining a technical problem" (p. 369, as quoted in Design Values in Action, 2023) leave out those who never became a relevant social group — "some groups are marginalised or excluded from participation, and some, despite being affected by a technology, are prevented from forming a relevant social group" (paraphrase in a 2022 SCOT review). (iii) Deeper origins: the method neglects "societal, economic, and cultural dynamics that are not evident in the immediacy of empirical reality" (pp. 370–371, paraphrase in a 2022 education paper). (iv) No standpoint: the method's "methodological bracketing of questions about interests and interpretations" (p. 372, as quoted in the 2022 SCOT review) means it takes no "evaluative stance or [adopts] any particular moral or political principles that might help people judge the possibilities that technologies present" (p. 371, as quoted in a 2022 *Business & Society* article), and so, "Unlike the inquiries of previous generations of critical social thinkers, social constructivism provides no solid, systematic standpoint or core of moral concerns for which to criticise or oppose any particular patterns of technical development" (p. 374, as quoted in a 2019 teaching-SCOT paper). The image that gives the essay its title is that after the "fascinating dynamics of conflict, disagreement and consensus formation that surround choices of great importance" have been described, the box is found empty (quoted without page in a 2025 *Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society* article; a 2012 architecture-discourse paper places the same sentence at p. 372).

## Relation to the argument

RQ3 asked whether the charge Winner presses against constructivism applies to his own account at the level of the enrolled person, and on the material available it does, charge by charge. Charge (iv) — no standpoint from which to judge — is the one the paper needs. Winner's own account has a standpoint at the polity level (1980: the adoption decision; 1977: collective withdrawal as inquiry), but at the level of the person already placed inside the form it has none; the 1977 book says as much when it reduces that person's role to "serving one's own function well and not meddling with the mechanism" (winner1977.md, p. 207). Charge (ii) turns on him with more force: the groups "prevented from forming a relevant social group" are exactly the users of a platform who never chose it and cannot exit it, and Winner names their exclusion as a defect of *other people's* method while leaving his own without a competence, a design principle, or a voice for them. Charge (i), consequences, is the one place his account is safe — the 1977 book is nothing but consequences — and it is the paper's opening: the consequence Winner cannot see is the one that lands *between* two coordinated people, because none of his units (system, polity, relevant social group) is a dyad. The essay is best cited in the paper as the standard by which Winner asked to be judged; the paper then shows that at the micro level he fails it.
