## What it argues

From the publisher's description: the book presents "the theoretical foundations of value sensitive design, which lead to a deep rethinking of technical design," then "seventeen methods, including stakeholder analysis, value scenarios, and multilifespan timelines," then reports from "experts from ten application domains," and finally "such open questions as the need for deeper investigation of indirect stakeholders and further method development." The chapters are Introduction, Theory, Method, Applications, Conclusion, with two "envois." The secondary literature adds a little. Umbrello and van de Poel (2021) cite it for VSD's "inherent self-reflexivity" and for its emphasis on "engagement with both direct and indirect stakeholders as a fundamental part of the design process," and for the method they call a "value-oriented mock-up, prototype, or field deployment" (their ref. 8, p. 62). Spiekermann and Winkler (2022) credit VSD, citing this book, with "over 200 case studies" and with the term "conceptual analysis," while saying it "has been unable to put forth a stringent process model that companies would be able to follow." The stakeholder theory the book consolidates is, on the evidence of Friedman, Kahn and Borning (2006), the direct/indirect distinction with power treated as orthogonal to it; whether the 2019 text revises that in its "open questions" on indirect stakeholders I cannot say.
