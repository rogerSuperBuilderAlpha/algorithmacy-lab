## What it argues

Verbeek answers "several 'friendly critics'" who worried that postphenomenology lacks a "political theory of technology" (p. 141) with a "political hermeneutics of technology," which "should be seen as yet another variant of Ihdean 'material hermeneutics': the technological mediation of interpretations and cultural frameworks has important political" implications (p. 142). Three dimensions follow. First, power: a "'politics of visibility,' together with the 'politics of multistability,' exemplifies the political hermeneutics of technology. By organizing perceptions and interpretations, technologies embody subtle forms of power" (p. 144), with Rosenberger's anti-homeless street furniture as the case (p. 143; Winner's overpasses are noted, p. 143 n. 1). Second, political action, via Arendt: "by exchanging ideas and concerns, in a public space that is demarcated from the private sphere of the home, human beings form a polis, where a community arises out of plurality" (p. 147), and since "the public realm actually requires a material space to exist," postphenomenology "can reveal another political dimension of technology" (p. 148). Here the platforms enter: "algorithms that determine the newsfeed of people on social media, can contribute to segregation rather than the formation of a plural community" (p. 149), and the design of "public spaces – from smart cities to digital environments – implicitly gives shape to what the demos, the people, can be in a democracy. Technologies organize the process of polis-making, in the Arendtian sense of the plural" (p. 149). Third, issue formation: "Deliberately distributed disinformation frustrates both the formation of issues and the gathering of publics" (p. 152), which Wellner reports as "the political hermeneutics of technology" proper (p. 152, via wellner2024, p. 11). The reference list cites Kudina and Verbeek (2018) (p. 154).

## Relation to the argument

RQ1, structural axis. This is the one Verbeek text in the library where a technology sits between people rather than between a person and the world, and the way he takes it is instructive. The newsfeed algorithm (p. 149) mediates a plurality, and its effect is on "community" and "the demos": the unit is the public, not the pair, so the relation is one human to a polis through a platform, scaled up from the dyad without passing through the triad. Nothing in the snippets has the platform reading two identified people and committing a determination that binds both; "segregation" is an aggregate outcome. The objective axis is nearer: Wellner's gloss (p. 11) that platforms "enact the rules, execute them, judge the users" is her addition (citing Shadmy 2019), not Verbeek's text as far as I saw. Adaptivity is unaddressed; "algorithms that determine the newsfeed" is the only algorithmic phrase the search returned and it says nothing about learning. For the paper: cite this as the point where Verbeek's own schema meets a platform and chooses Arendt over the dyad, which is a remedy-locus finding in itself, since polis-making is repaired by public space, not by anything between the two people the feed separates.
