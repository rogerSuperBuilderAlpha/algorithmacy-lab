## What it argues

Against Heidegger's Gestell, Latour builds mediation from the NRA slogan. The materialist says the gun acts, the NRA says it is "a tool, a medium, a neutral carrier of will" (p. 31); both "start with essences" (p. 33). The first meaning, translation, is drawn from the gun in hand (Fig. 1, p. 32): Agent 1's goal is interrupted, it enlists Agent 2, "a third agent emerges from a fusion of the other two," and "the question now becomes which goal the new composite agent will pursue. If it returns, after its detour, to Goal 1, then the NRA story obtains. The gun is a tool, merely an intermediary." If the gun's script supersedes, the materialist story obtains. "But a third possibility is more commonly realized: the creation of a new goal that corresponds to neither agent's program of action. (You had wanted only to hurt but, with a gun now in hand, you want to kill.) I call this uncertainty about goals translation" (p. 32). The second meaning, composition: "Action is a property of associated entities ... The chimp plus the sharp stick reach (and not reaches) the banana"; "B-52s do not fly, the U.S. Air Force flies. Action is simply not a property of humans but of an association of actants, and this is the second sense of what I intend by technical mediation" (p. 35). The third, reversible blackboxing, uses a broken overhead projector: while it works it is "a silent and mute intermediary"; when it fails, "our 'projector' grew from being composed of zero parts to one to many. How many actants are really there? The philosophy of technology has little use for arithmetic" (p. 36), and the seven-step figure shows the count varying from step to step (p. 37). The fourth, delegation, is the speed bump: the driver's goal is translated "from 'slow down so as not to endanger students' into 'slow down and protect my car's suspension'," and "from an observer's point of view, it does not matter through which channel a given behavior is attained. From her window, the chancellor sees that cars are slowing down and, for her, that is enough" (p. 38). The engineers' programme "is now inscribed in concrete" (p. 38); the "sleeping policeman" is an actorial, spatial and temporal shift, and "the enunciator of this technical act has disappeared from the scene" (p. 39). "An object stands in for an actor and creates an asymmetry between absent makers and occasional users" (p. 40). The essay's summary: "Technical action is a form of delegation that allows us to mobilize, during interactions, moves made elsewhere, earlier, by other actants" (p. 52), and "techniques are not fetishes, they are unpredictable, not means but mediators, means and ends at the same time" (p. 53). Its last words relocate humanity to "the possibility of mediating between mediators" (p. 64): "They mediate our actions? No, they are us."

## Relation to the argument

RQ1: this is the formal account to set against Φ, and it agrees and disagrees in specific places. It agrees on a threshold: the intermediary is the composite that returns to Goal 1, the mediator is the one that does not, so a channel exists in Latour where it does not in Verbeek. It agrees on the objective axis almost verbatim: translation is "the creation of a new goal that corresponds to neither agent's program of action" (p. 32), which is the construct's "pursues an objective neither of them set" stated for a two-agent composite thirty years earlier. Composition (p. 35) is the reading closest to irreducibility: action belongs to the association and cannot be assigned to a part. It disagrees on counting. Latour refuses arithmetic (p. 36) and makes the number of actants relative to the step of blackboxing (p. 37), which cuts against a fixed Φ value but supports the Φ essay's stated limit that the verdict holds relative to a model; reversible blackboxing is that limit in Latour's words. The speed bump (p. 38) is the best two-human case in the cluster: engineers and drivers coordinate through concrete, the chancellor does not care through which channel, and the delegate carries the absent maker's objective into every driver's decision. What Latour's delegation lacks is the run-time third objective: the bump does what the engineers wanted, and translation's "neither agent's" goal is symmetric and non-adversarial. The construct's intermediary is a delegate that also translates, and the paper can say so with page numbers. For RQ2, p. 40's "asymmetry between absent makers and occasional users" is what a seam discloses; in the triad, the absent maker is the platform.

## Caution

A philosophical essay with myths (Daedalus, Kubrick's monolith, baboons) as its cases; the gun, projector, speed bump and pipette are thought experiments, not studies. The OCR is poor and I have quoted only passages whose page text was unambiguous; the paper should re-check each quotation against a clean copy before it prints. Translation is defined for a human plus an artifact, not for two humans plus an artifact, so the "neither agent's goal" reading has to be extended, not cited, to reach the triad. Latour's symmetry is exactly what Verbeek (2014, p. 86) rejects, so the paper cannot borrow both without noting the disagreement.
