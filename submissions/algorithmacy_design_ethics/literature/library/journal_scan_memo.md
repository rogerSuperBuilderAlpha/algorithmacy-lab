# Journal scan memo — algorithmacy paper

Scan date: 2026-09-18. Method: Crossref records for every article each journal registered from 2025-01-01 to date (by ISSN), filtered by title for AI / algorithm / automation / trust / mediation / platform / design-ethics vocabulary; then the journals' own collections, calls-for-papers and author-guideline pages, read through the browser where the publisher walls automated fetches. Every title, author, date and deadline below comes from one of those two sources. Where I could not verify something I say so.

Three caveats on coverage. AI & Society registered 1,191 items since January 2025; I read the most recent 400 (March–September 2026) plus four earlier items I verified by DOI. IJHCS's call-for-papers page sits behind a CAPTCHA, so its live special issues are unverified. Philosophy & Technology's guideline page states no word cap, and I did not capture the Human Factors review-article cap.

## 1. Ethics and Information Technology (Springer, hybrid)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, centrally.** About 100 articles a year, four issues (v27 2025, v28 2026). The 2025–26 run includes Malone, Afroogh & D'Cruz (2025), "When trust is zero sum: automation's threat to epistemic agency" (27/2); Christiaens (2025), "Trust and power in Airbnb's digital rating and reputation system" (27/2), a two-sided-platform case; Figueroa, Marin & Jaff (2026), "Translating the value of well-being into design features of social media platforms: a value sensitive design approach" (28/1); Igaya (2026), "Marketing to machines: the fiduciary illusion in anticipatory companion AI" (28/3); Bitton & Sadler (2026), "Scrolling alone: platformization and the infrastructure of social life" (28/3); Wood (2025), "Autonomous and AI-enabled systems: extensions or replacements of human will and control?"; and Muralidharan & Savulescu (2026), "All you need is… justification: algorithmic justifiability trumps transparency" (28/2). A responsibility-gap thread runs through several issues (Kasar 2025; Gong 2025; Kuilman 2026).

**Format.** "Original Research Paper," approx. 5,000–8,000 words of content, hard ceiling 8,000; double-blind. Review papers are accepted on the same terms if they reinterpret a field. Book reviews 2,000–3,000 words.

**Fit.** Strong for a design-ethics argument that leans on human-factors evidence (T2) and workable for the appropriate-reliance argument if it is cast as ethics of epistemic agency rather than as ergonomics (T3; Malone et al. 2025 is the direct interlocutor). The 8,000-word ceiling is the constraint: an IIT-based structural section has to be compact and the formalism relegated to an appendix or a companion paper. All fourteen listed collections, including "Socially Disruptive Technologies and Conceptual Engineering," "AI, Judgment and Rules," and "Between the Ethics and Epistemology of Explainable AI," show status Closed. A search snippet claimed the conceptual-engineering issue was still accepting; the journal's own page says otherwise.

## 2. Philosophy & Technology (Springer, hybrid)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, at volume.** 359 registered items since January 2025, quarterly issues (v38 2025, v39 2026), with an active commentary-and-reply ecology (Sahebi & Montefiore ↔ Metcalf on undisclosed LLM use; Deller ↔ McKenna on super-persuasive AI; Wieczorek ↔ Dekker & Hermans on AI in education). The closest neighbours to the algorithmacy argument: Jovchevski, Buijsman & Neerincx (2026), "What is Wrong With Automation Bias?" (39/2); Largo (2026), "Human-AI Interaction, Moral Responsibility and Epistemic Credit" (39/2); Martino & Possati (2026), "Technological Intentionality and the Concept of Flesh: A Merleau-Pontian Perspective on Technological Mediation" (39/3); Zelny (2025), "Offloading Wisdom: Four Technological Relations that Mediate Phronesis" (38/2), which works Ihde and Verbeek against virtue; Torrecilla-Pinero (2026), "Judgment Cannot Be Delegated: A Subject-Preserving Framework for AI Governance" (39/3); Donahue, Stone & Jayaram (2026), "Platform Legitimacy Between Rights and Rule" (39/2); Hansen (2026), "Analysing Structures of Silence in AI-mediated Public Spheres" (39/4); Zeilstra (2026), "The Not So Innocent Nudge" (39/3) on design and autonomy.

**Format.** Research articles plus short commentaries and replies. The guideline page I read states no word limit; the recent research articles run in the 8,000–12,000 range by inspection. Hybrid OA.

**Fit.** The best home for the mediation-theory thesis (T1): mediation theory, Verbeek, and virtue-of-technology arguments appear in current issues, and Jovchevski et al. (2026) means a reliance-focused paper (T3) would land in an existing conversation rather than start one. A formal instrument (IIT) is unusual here but not alien; the journal ran a "Machine Learning and Society" and a "Trust and Opacity in AI" collection (both closed). Two collections are open: "Artificial Intelligence, Hyperpersuasion and Democratic Backsliding," deadline **30 September 2026**, and "The ethics of medical artificial intelligence: Trust and adoption of AI in healthcare," deadline **1 December 2026**; "Editorial Critical Perspectives" is ongoing. The hyperpersuasion call is about belief formation and civic participation; algorithmacy would need reframing as a persuasion-vulnerability argument to qualify, and the deadline is twelve days out.

## 3. Design Issues (MIT Press)

**Publishes in this territory: no.** Sixty-three items across v41 (2025) and v42 (2026). The content is design history, design studies and design education: a themed section "Design Studies Beyond Design" (42/1, Gruendel & Mareis), "Design and Articulations of Pasts" (41/4), "Notes on Culture and Design" (41/3), plus pieces on Clara Porset, colonial-era Korean commercial art, Argentine furniture, type design and playful systems. One title touches ethics — Song & Jun (2025), "Rethinking Design Ethics from the Perspective of Spinoza" (41/1) — and none touches AI, algorithms, automation, trust, HCI, or value-sensitive design.

**Format.** Approximately 5,500 words maximum excluding footnotes and captions, anonymized, via Scholastica; the journal asks for a paragraph explaining fit with its editorial scope.

**Fit.** Poor. This is the clearest disagreement with the planner's list. Even the design-methodology thesis (T4) would be foreign to the current register, and the length cap rules out a formal section. Do not target.

## 4. Science and Engineering Ethics (Springer, fully open access)

**Status.** Active and undisturbed. Ninety-eight registered items since January 2025, issues numbered through 32/5 (2026), editors-in-chief Dena Plemmons and Behnam Taebi. I found no report of editorial resignation or disruption at this journal; the resignation story in circulation concerns Elsevier's Journal of Human Evolution. One fact the planner did not flag: the journal's publishing model is now fully open access, so an APC applies.

**Publishes in this territory: yes, with a strong VSD strand.** Herrera (2026), "Habituation and the Human Will: How Passive Reliance on Human-AI Collaboration Erodes Ethical Agency to Prevent It"; Lara & del Valle (2026), "Are You Sure This is What You Really Want? Conversational Recommender Systems for Enhancing Human Autonomy"; Guler & Zhu (2026), "Navigating Access: Opportunities and Risks of AI-Mediated Hiring for People with Disabilities"; Cociancig & Heuer (2026), "Toward a Clearer Process for Value Sensitive Artificial Intelligence" (32/2); Calvert (2025), "Principles and Framework for the Operationalisation of Meaningful Human Control Over Autonomous Systems" (31/5); Lim, Nicolaides & Yang (2025), a postphenomenological study of AI nudges (31/3); Jenkins (2025), "Threads and Needles: A Value-Sensitive Design Approach to Online Toxicity" (31/3); Constantinescu & Kaptein (2025), "Responsibility Gaps, LLMs & Organisations: Many Agents, Many Levels, and Many Interactions" (31/6).

**Format.** Research articles; no stated cap captured. Two collections are open: "Ethics of AI" (ongoing, no deadline) and "Towards a Hybrid Ethics of Technology. Bridging Ethics of Technology and Intercultural-Comparative Ethics" (with ESDiT), deadline **31 January 2027** — intercultural in focus, so not a match unless the paper acquires that angle.

**Fit.** Good for T4: three VSD-process papers in eighteen months, and Calvert's meaningful-human-control framework is the kind of operationalisation argument the comparison with adversarial design would sit beside. Also viable for T2. The APC is the cost.

## 5. Big Data & Society (SAGE, open access)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, in a critical-data-studies register.** 256 items since January 2025 (v12–13). Closest to the paper: Kawakami, Taylor & Fox (2026), "AI failure loops in devalued work: The confluence of overconfidence in AI and underconfidence in worker expertise" (13/1); Zhao (2026), "Algorithmic inequality by design: Human resistance and its limits in chatbot-based customer service" (13/2); Hong, Cheng & Liu (2026), "Working the algorithm: Contextual skills of on-demand gig workers" (13/2), which is two-sided platform coordination by another name; Dingelstad, Grommé & Taal (2026), "Reasoning with algorithms: Partiality and uncertainty in neonatal care" (13/3); Cullen, Ellison & Pasquetto (2026), "Seeing like an API: Platform-mediated research and the politics of access" (13/3); Li, Kim & Lou (2026), "Into the black box: Laypeople's folk theories about generative AI chatbots" (13/2).

**Format.** Original research up to 10,000 words including references, triple-reviewed and double-blind; commentaries up to 3,000 words; APC $1,500 with waivers on request. The 2026 call for special-theme proposals closed 31 August 2026 (that call is for guest editors, not papers; manuscripts for selected themes are due 28 February 2027).

**Fit.** Moderate and register-dependent. The gig-work and failure-loop papers show the phenomenon is live here, but the journal wants empirical or critical social science; an argument built on Verbeek, Vallor and an IIT instrument would read as philosophy submitted to the wrong desk. A T3 variant framed as a sociology of "appropriate reliance" in gig platforms could work; the other three theses would not.

## 6. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies (Elsevier)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, mostly empirically.** 396 items since January 2025. Trust and reliance are a steady stream: Anthis & Kyriakidou-Zacharoudiou (2026), "Trust by design: Human factors and trust calibration in ML-based educational systems" (v216); Hunsicker, Duhl & Haubert (2026), "Trust the Explanation or my Expectation? Effects of Output Accuracy and Explanations on Expectation Violations and Trust in AI-Supported Decisions" (v211); Campagner, Fregosi & Natali (2026), "Under what influence: Measuring AI influence to fit user profiles in decision-making" (v210); Yuan & Rau (2027), "XAI in the human-agent-environment loop: how do task, explanation strategy, and AI literacy shape user engagement?" (v218). Design ethics appears too, and as theory: Öz, Dindler & Lindberg (2026), "The turn to practice in design ethics: Characteristics and future research directions for HCI research" (v210); Han, Coghlan & Buchanan (2026), "Ethical gaps and power dynamics in decision-making about AI adoption" (v212); Fabbri, Jia & Jerez Arnau (2026), "Feeding the (short-video) feed: a design proposal for user control of social media recommender systems under the Digital Services Act" (v214). Recent special issues: child-centred AI (2026) and "Re-centering the user in recommender system research" (preface 2027).

**Format.** Full-length empirical articles dominate; conceptual and review pieces are a minority but present (Öz et al. 2026 is a review; Fabbri et al. 2026 is a design proposal). Live calls: unverified (CAPTCHA).

**Fit.** Better than the planner allowed for T2, provided the human-factors evidence is foregrounded and the paper offers a design proposal or a review-style synthesis rather than a purely philosophical argument. T3 would need at least a re-analysis of existing reliance data to pass here.

## 7. Theoretical Issues in Ergonomics Science (Taylor & Francis)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, as the theory venue of human factors, though thinly.** Sixty-three items since January 2025 (v26–27), much of it occupational ergonomics and safety. The theoretical strand is real: Hoffman & Brézillon (2026), "What is context?: a synthesis of views and its implications for human-AI work systems"; Lintern (2026), "An evolutionary model of sociotechnical system safety"; Gill (2026), "Beyond SHELL: the SSHELL framework for advancing Human Factors theory" (27/5); Patriarca (2026), "Work system patterns for complex adaptive systems" (27/4); Karwowski, Salvendy & Endsley (2025), "Grand challenges for human factors and ergonomics" (26/4); Khan, Laeeque & Samdani (2026), "Algorithmic dehumanization and safety behaviors in ride-hailing" (27/5); Bjurling, Arvola & Alfredson (2025), "Trajectories of attention and control in human-machine interactions: the case of swarms" (26/6).

**Format.** Stated aim: "to advance the science and philosophy of human factors and ergonomics… theoretical issues, methodology, and philosophical dialogues." Typical paper no more than 10,000 words inclusive. No live special-issue call found.

**Fit.** The strongest venue for T3 as stated. A paper arguing that "appropriate reliance" (Lee & See 2004) is undefined for triadic automation is a definitional, theoretical contribution, which is exactly what TIES exists to publish and what Human Factors' format now resists (see 8). Hoffman is a reviewer-pool name on both trust and context. Reach is smaller than Human Factors; the trade is a reader who will accept a structural argument without a new experiment.

## 8. Human Factors (SAGE / HFES)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, and the reliance conversation is here.** 132 items since January 2025, monthly. Chung & Yang (2026), "System-Wide Trust (SWT) Versus Component-Specific Trust (CST) in Multi-Agent Human–Agent Teams"; Renata & Lee (2026), "Modeling Automation Trust Diffusion in Teams: Linking Micro- and Macro-Trust Dynamics"; Kerstan, Grote & Schmutz (2026), "Collaborative Decision-Making in Human-AI Versus Human-Human Teams"; Ebinger, Neuhuber & Kubicek (2025), "How Can I Trust You? The Effect of Risk and Automation Failures on Trust and Reliance Behavior" (68/4); Saad & Meyer (2025), "Context-Based Human Influence and Causal Responsibility for Assisted Decision-Making" (67/8); Jackson, Sato & Glassman (2026), "Automation Error Bias, Trust, and Dependence Behaviors in a Simulated Drone Collision Avoidance Task" (68/7). Multi-agent trust is now an explicit topic, which is the nearest thing to the paper's triadic structure.

**Format.** Research Article: 4,500 words excluding abstract, key points, tables and references; "experimental, methodological, or theoretical," but expected to include statistical analysis where appropriate. Review Article: must declare a review methodology (narrative, systematic, meta-analytic) and "extract new knowledge" rather than identify a gap. Structured abstract; single-anonymized by default. The special issue "Investigating the Human Factors of AI Use" (Biondi, Vasta, Wiegmann, McDonnell, Castro) closed 1 October 2025.

**Fit.** Narrower than the planner assumed. The 4,500-word cap and the empirical norm leave no room for an IIT section plus a design-ethics section. The only viable shape is T3 as a Review Article with a stated methodology — a structured review of the reliance literature showing that its constructs presuppose a dyad — and even that must show the review method. Treat as a secondary target for a stripped-down T3.

## 9. Techné: Research in Philosophy and Technology (SPT / Philosophy Documentation Center)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, at small scale.** Thirty-one items in 2025–26 (v29–30), two issues a year, a new editorial team in 2026 (Irwin, "Thank You and Welcome," 30/1). Mediation and postphenomenology are current: Bohlmann (2026), "Distal and Proximal Human-Technology Relations" (30/2); Tafdrup (2026), "The Epistemic and Symbolic Mediation of Drones in the Russo-Ukrainian War" (30/2); Edin (2026), "Algorithmic Personalization and Datafication of Being" (30/2); Oie (2026), "Human Autonomy in a Cyborg Agent" (30/1); Bosschaert & Blok (2026), "Towards an Integrated Framework in the Philosophy of Technology" (30/1); Checketts (2026), "The Impossibility of Non-Repressive Technical Codes" (30/1). Issue 29/2 was a special issue on maintenance.

**Format.** Up to 8,500 words, double-anonymized, abstract 150 words. A special issue on Virtue Ethics (guest editor Marc Steen, TNO) closed **31 March 2026**, anticipated publication 2027; that call asked for "the added value of virtue ethics in the context of technology, design, engineering" — the Vallor strand of this paper, missed by six months.

**Fit.** Strong for T1 if Philosophy & Technology declines: this is mediation theory's home society journal. Small and slow (about fifteen papers a year), so a paper waits longer for an issue but faces a reader who knows Ihde and Verbeek cold.

## 10. AI & Society (Springer, hybrid)

**Publishes in this territory: yes, more than any other journal on the list, and that is the problem.** 1,191 registered items since January 2025 — roughly a hundred a month, most of them online-first with no volume assigned. The vocabulary of the algorithmacy paper is everywhere: Bohr (2026), "Coordination transparency: governing distributed agency in AI systems" (41/5); Ji & Yang (2026), "When AI starts hiring humans: social role inversion and the diffusion of responsibility"; Josifović & Noller (2026), "Agency and alignment: toward a normative architecture for human–AI interaction" (41/6); Vidal (2025), "Productive power in social networks: challenges for post-phenomenological mediation theory" (40/6); Mühlhoff (2026), "From delegation to moral abdication: classifying large language model uses by judgment and epistemic control"; Ibitoye, Ajenifuja & Abdul Kareem (2026), "Decision substitution and the reorganisation of judgement in AI-mediated society"; Kringen, Gallucci & Hildt (2026), "Using LLMs without trust: the adoption–trust paradox"; Nakagawa (2026), "Governing the AI-mediated corporation."

**Format.** Original articles, Open Forum, Curmudgeon Corner; no cap captured. All listed special-issue collections are closed; the last to close was "Responsible use of AI in Art Creation and Archival Practice" (31 July 2026).

**Fit.** Topically the closest match on the list for any of the four theses, and the venue where the specific phrase "AI-mediated" is a live term of art. The costs are dilution (a paper is one of a thousand a year) and a long online-first queue. I would hold it as the fallback for whichever thesis is chosen, not the first target.

## Where the scan disagrees with the planner

1. **Design Issues is not a candidate.** Zero AI, HCI or human-factors titles in 2025–26; a 5,500-word cap; a design-history and design-studies register.
2. **Human Factors is narrower than assumed.** A 4,500-word research-article cap and a methodology requirement for reviews leave room only for a stripped-down T3 as a declared review.
3. **Science and Engineering Ethics is fine, and fully OA.** No editorial disruption; issues through 32/5 in 2026; an APC now applies. Its VSD strand makes it the best T4 venue, which the planner did not single out.
4. **IJHCS carries more design-ethics theory than expected** (Öz et al. 2026; Fabbri et al. 2026), so T2 with a design proposal is viable there, not only in the ethics journals.
5. **AI & Society is the tightest topical match and the worst signal-to-noise**: hold as fallback.
6. **Techné just closed a virtue-ethics issue** the Vallor strand would have fitted; the journal remains the natural second home for T1.

## Ranked recommendation

**1. Philosophy & Technology — first target for T1, and the only journal where T3 already has a named interlocutor.** Mediation theory (Martino & Possati 2026; Zelny 2025), platform legitimacy (Donahue et al. 2026) and automation bias (Jovchevski et al. 2026) are all in the last two volumes; no stated word cap, so the IIT instrument can be shown rather than gestured at; a commentary-and-reply culture gives the paper a second life after publication.

**2. Ethics and Information Technology — first target for T2.** Trust and epistemic agency (Malone et al. 2025), VSD applied to platforms (Figueroa et al. 2026), and a platform-mediation case (Christiaens 2025) are in print; 8,000-word ceiling forces the formalism into an appendix, which for a design-ethics argument is the right place for it.

**3. Theoretical Issues in Ergonomics Science — first target for T3 as written.** It is the theory journal of the field whose construct the thesis attacks, it allows 10,000 words, and Hoffman & Brézillon (2026) on context in human–AI work systems is the neighbouring argument. Human Factors is the higher-reach alternative only if the paper is rebuilt as a 4,500-word declared review.

**T4** has no first-tier home on the list; Science and Engineering Ethics (Cociancig & Heuer 2026; Jenkins 2025) is the best fit, with the APC as the cost, and AI & Society as the fallback.
