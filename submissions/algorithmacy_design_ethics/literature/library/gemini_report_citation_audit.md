# Citation audit — sample of the Gemini report's Works Cited list

**Report audited:** "The Invisible Scripts of the Machine: Historical Parallels Between the Print Revolution and the Age of Opaque Artificial Intelligence" (Gemini-drafted; 193-entry Works Cited). **Sample:** the 25 entries handed to this pass (the brief called it 24; the list as supplied contains 25, and I audited all of them). **Method:** each entry was searched by title and author, the URL or a venue record fetched where reachable, and the real bibliographic identity recorded; three parallel search agents did the lookups and I checked their tables against the classification rules below. **Checked:** 2026-09-18.

## Summary: quality and fabrication rate

Nine of 25 sampled entries (36%) are real and citable as they stand, though three of those nine need re-citing from a web page to the version of record (Dover 2021 rather than "Cambridge Core"; Bushey et al. 2025 to the IEEE proceedings rather than a workshop PDF; the *Transformer Circuits Thread* index to a dated article). Fourteen of 25 (56%) exist but are low-tier hosts or secondary summaries standing in for a primary work the report should have cited: Wikipedia, a Scribd upload, two ResearchGate mirrors, a WordPress blog review, an OER textbook chapter, a travel-magazine feature, an EBSCO "Research Starter," an exhibition blog post, a consultancy "technology radar" blip, an unpublished undergraduate thesis marked "do not quote without permission," and a conference-CFP aggregator page. Two of 25 (8%) could not be verified: entry 86, "1 The AI of Ethics," which no search locates under any repository, and entry 119, a Verbeek paper attributed to "JAAFR," a pay-to-publish outlet with predatory markers, where no paper of that title can be found. I could confirm no entry as an outright fabrication, but 119 sits on the boundary between unverifiable and non-existent. Rate as a fraction: **9/25 citable as-is, 14/25 real-but-low-tier, 2/25 unverifiable, 0/25 confirmed fabricated.**

The pattern that matters more than the rate is where the low-tier entries fall. The report's four named theoretical anchors — Winner, Vallor, Verbeek, and Martela & Steger — are reached, in this sample, through a ResearchGate mirror of a commentary on Winner (78), a pseudonymous blog review and a community-college OER chapter on Vallor (103, 107), an unlocatable paper at a predatory-pattern journal on Verbeek (119), and a Scribd upload of Martela & Steger with the uploader's relabelled title (140). Not one of the four is cited to the primary work. A second finding bears directly on the harness's central question: entry 57, the "1st Global Algorithmacy Conference" CFP, is the user's own conference (convener Roger Hunt) mirrored on UPenn's CFP aggregator, and the UPenn page contains no definition of the term. The definition lives on algorithmacy.org — "the competency through which a worker coordinates with another human through an algorithmic third party" — which is a triadic definition. The Gemini report thus cites the user's triadic construct as its source for the word and then deploys the word dyadically.

## What this means for the paper

Nothing from the Gemini report's Works Cited list may enter the article on the strength of that list. Where the report reached a real primary work through a mirror or a summary (46, 58, 78, 140), the fix is mechanical: cite the version of record. Where it reached a summary of a primary work it never cited (15, 32, 36, 42, 103, 107), the primary work has to be read, not swapped in. Entries 86 and 119 should be dropped unless the report's author produces a locatable URL, and 119 should be dropped regardless of whether the paper exists, since the outlet fails any venue standard. Entry 57 should be recognized for what it is: the report's source for the term "algorithmacy" is the user's own conference.
