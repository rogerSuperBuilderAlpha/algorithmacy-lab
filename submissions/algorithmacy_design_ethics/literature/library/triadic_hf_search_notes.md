# Triadic human-factors search notes (RQ9)

Pass C(ii), 2026-09-18. The question was whether the human-factors literature contains a real, citable case in which an automated intermediary sits between two people, interprets both, and commits determinations that bind both — and in particular whether the controller–pilot datalink "party line" literature is such a case. Short answer: yes for the party line, with two citations the planner had not pinned down now verified; a weaker yes for clinical decision support between prescriber and pharmacist; in both cases the structural axis is present and the objective axis is empty.

## 1. CPDLC / "party line" — verified

**What I searched.** Web search for Midkiff & Hansman 1993 and for Pritchett & Hansman + "party line" + datalink; the Crossref works API with a bibliographic query; the NTRS citations API (records 20020032693 and 20020030133) and their PDF downloads; MIT DSpace for Midkiff's thesis; the SAE Mobilus page for paper 922023; the AIAA ARC table of contents for ATCQ 1(1) (403); IEEE Xplore for the DASC paper (page fetched empty); the DASC '98 page on Kuchar's MIT site, whose reference list confirms the ASL report numbers.

**What I found, with the citation status of each.**

- Midkiff, A. H., & Hansman, R. J. (1993). Identification of important "party line" information elements and implications for situational awareness in the datalink environment. *Air Traffic Control Quarterly*, 1(1), 5–30. doi:10.2514/atcq.1.1.5. **Crossref-verified.** The article itself is behind AIAA ARC; I read instead the full report it condenses (Midkiff's June 1992 MIT S.M. thesis, MIT DSpace, 105 pp.; also issued as MIT ASL-92-2, May 1992) and the SAE 922023 abstract (doi:10.4271/922023, Aerospace Technology Conference, Long Beach, Oct 1992, same title). Card: `midkiff1993.md`.
- Pritchett, A. R., & Hansman, R. J. (1994). *Variations in party line information requirements for flight crew situation awareness in the datalink environment.* MIT Aeronautical Systems Laboratory report ASL-94-5, 18 May 1994. NTRS 20020030133. **Full text read** (124 pp.). This is the "Pritchett & Hansman on party-line information requirements" the planner was unsure of. Card: `pritchett1994.md`.
- Pritchett, A. R., & Hansman, R. J. (1996). Variations among pilots from different flight operations in party line information requirements for situation awareness. *Air Traffic Control Quarterly*, 4(1), 29–50. doi:10.2514/atcq.4.1.29. **Crossref-verified, not opened.** The journal version of ASL-94-5.
- Pritchett, A. R., & Hansman, R. J. (1995). Variations in "party line" information importance between pilots of different characteristics. *Eighth International Symposium on Aviation Psychology*, Columbus OH, April 1995. **Secondary only** — cited in the 1995 preprint's reference list; not located.
- Hansman, R. J., Pritchett, A., & Midkiff, A. (1995). "Party line" information use studies and implications for ATC datalink communications. Preprint for the *Fifth International Conference on Human-Machine Interaction and Artificial Intelligence in Aerospace*, Toulouse, 27–29 Sept 1995. NTRS 20020032693, 9 pp. **Full text read.** Crossref also lists Pritchett, A., Midkiff, A., & Hansman, R. J. (1995), same title, *Proc. 14th Digital Avionics Systems Conference*, 26–31, doi:10.1109/DASC.1995.482801 — **verified, not opened**; almost certainly the same paper with a different author order. Card: `hansman1995.md`.
- Hansman, R. J. (2001). The effect of shared information on pilot/controller and controller/controller interactions. In L. Bianco, P. Dell'Olmo & A. R. Odoni (Eds.), *New Concepts and Methods in Air Traffic Management* (pp. 143–159). Springer. doi:10.1007/978-3-662-04632-6_9. **Abstract only** (Springer landing page). Broader "shared information" framing including dispatchers; a possible follow-up read, not carded.

**Assessment.** The party line is a literal instance of "suppress the direct channel": under shared VHF voice every pilot on the frequency is a free party to every clearance; under discretely addressed datalink the same coordination becomes point-to-point, the overhearer is cut out, and whatever the third party needs must be re-supplied by the mediator by design (the "compensation" problem that organizes all three MIT reports). The 1995 simulation adds a behavioural result that maps directly onto the contestation limb of algorithmacy: crews who overheard evidence of a controller error mostly noticed and did not act, from "reticence to insinuate that a controller error had taken place based upon PLI alone." Two limits. The mediator (Mode S datalink, ACARS) has no objective of its own, so the case supports the structural axis only. And the party who loses is the overhearer beside the coordination, not one of the two inside it; the essay's construct places the third party inside. Both limits should be stated when the case is used.

## 2. Clinical decision support between two clinicians — found, weaker

**What I searched.** Web search for clinical decision support + interruptive alerts + physician + pharmacist, and for DDI alerts presented to both prescribers and pharmacists (the second search hit the session's search budget); PubMed for the Russ record; ScienceDirect for a related RSAP paper (403); PMC (CAPTCHA) and Springer (cookie redirect) for the Van De Sijpe article; Europe PMC's REST API for its abstract.

**What I found.**

- Russ, A. L., Chen, S., Melton, B. L., Saleem, J. J., Weiner, M., Spina, J. R., Daggy, J. K., & Zillich, A. J. (2015). Design and evaluation of an electronic override mechanism for medication alerts to facilitate communication between prescribers and pharmacists. *Annals of Pharmacotherapy*, 49(7), 761–769. doi:10.1177/1060028015581678. PMID 25855701. **Abstract only.** Card: `russ2015.md`. A redesign of the alert-override field explicitly as a channel between prescriber and pharmacist; 20 prescribers, simulated crossover.
- Van De Sijpe, G., Quintens, C., Walgraeve, K., et al. (2022). Overall performance of a drug–drug interaction clinical decision support system: quantitative evaluation and end-user survey. *BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making*, 22. doi:10.1186/s12911-022-01783-z. **Abstract only** (Europe PMC). 38,409 very severe DDI alerts in a teaching hospital, 2019–2021, 88.2% overridden by prescribers; pharmacist review of overrides produced recommendations in 3.2% of reviewed cases, 79.2% accepted. Not carded; noted inside `russ2015.md`.
- Search-result titles not opened: "The role of clinical decision support in pharmacist response to drug-interaction alerts" (*Research in Social and Administrative Pharmacy*, 2015; ScienceDirect 403); Felisberto et al. (2024) meta-analysis of DDI override rates; a 2022 systematic review of DDI alert outcomes (PMC 12451929).

**Assessment.** The structure is real: a vendor rule base fires a determination neither clinician wrote; the prescriber must answer it to proceed; the pharmacist reads the answer through the system and may contest it. The Van De Sijpe loop (override → pharmacist review → recommendation → acceptance) is a triad with a contestation channel measured. But the direct channel is not suppressed — a pharmacist can still phone — and the Russ study's whole motivation is that the mediated channel is *worse* than the direct one, which is evidence for the essay's structural point but not an instance of a closed channel. No full text was reachable, so the cards are abstract-only and the numbers should be re-checked before use.

## 3. What was not found

No human-factors study in which the intermediary between two human parties pursues an objective of its own — nothing in this pass supports the objective axis with a triadic case. The reliance literature (nine cards) is uniformly dyadic; the nearest admissions of a second human are Jacovi et al.'s loan-officer/applicant example, Green & Chen's due-process sentence, and the Lai et al. survey's closing recommendation, each of which names the second party and does not model it.
