## What it argues

Per Kiem: DiSalvo draws "principally on the political theory of Chantal Mouffe," and "the central thesis ... is that adversarial design represents a means to generate the kind of antagonistic relations deemed essential to the experience of 'the political' and the condition of democracy" (Kiem p. 31). "Politics" is administrative governance; "the political" is "the experience of antagonism that emerges as a contested line of interest between two groups" (p. 32). DiSalvo: "For democracy to flourish, spaces of confrontation must exist, and contestation must occur. Perhaps the most basic purpose of adversarial design is to make these spaces of confrontation and provide resources and opportunities for others to participate in contestation" (DiSalvo p. 5, at Kiem p. 32). The book limits itself to computational design's "medium particularity" and gives one tactic per medium. **Revealing hegemony** (information design; Josh On's *They Rule* and Greenpeace's *Exxon Secrets*, which map board interlocks and funding ties) is "exposing and documenting the forces of influence in society and the mean[s] by which social manipulation occurs" (DiSalvo p. 35, at Kiem p. 33). **Reconfiguring the remainder** (social robots; Kelly Dobson's blender that responds to growling and her breathing object, Marc Böhlen's two robots that argue) takes Bonnie Honig's "remainder" — what a political order excludes — and privileges "characteristics that are generally excluded in the design of robots" (Kiem p. 33). **Articulating collectives** (ubicomp; Usman Haque's *Natural Fuse*, Mark Shepard's *Ad-hoc Dark (roast) Network Travel Mug* and *CCD-Me Not Umbrella*) builds "linkages between objects, people, and actions" to create "an open space of contest in which the elements gathered together are able to act out a plurality of conflicting practices, values, and beliefs" (DiSalvo p. 96, at Kiem pp. 33–34). Kiem's own critique: DiSalvo "does not pose the politics of the already designed as a political problem," so "political design ... can (by design) only play out within the bounds of a liberal democratic order" (Kiem pp. 35–36); most examples are "aestheticised props," and only "the informational designing involved in revealing hegemony" has purchase, though it "still operates within a politics of appeal (to the current order), rather than building political alternatives" (Kiem p. 38).

## Relation to the argument

The brief asked whether FeedVis fits "revealing hegemony" too easily, and the secondary account says it does, for a reason worth stating. DiSalvo's revealing-hegemony examples are *third-party representations* — a designer maps public data about a hegemon (Exxon, interlocked boards) for a public that then confronts it. FeedVis reveals *the artifact's own operation to one of its users*. Three differences follow. The revealer is not independent of the revealed (Eslami et al. built FeedVis against Facebook, but a designed seam, Ehsan's affordance, is authored by the intermediary's own designers). The audience is one party, not a public: the driver's seam is not the customer's, so the "space of confrontation" DiSalvo wants is not opened between the two humans, only between one of them and the mechanism. And the hegemon in DiSalvo is a *social force* revealed by mapping its ties, while in the triad the "hegemony" is the intermediary's *objective*, which is not a tie that can be mapped but a target that must be inferred — which is what the fourth affordance and the construct's "infer what the intermediary is doing" are for. So the fit is real at the level of *intent* (expose the forces of influence) and false at the level of *form* (who reveals, to whom, and what kind of thing is revealed). On the tertius problem: DiSalvo's agonism, per Mouffe, needs an "us" and a "them"; Kiem's complaint that he ignores "the politics of the already designed" is the de Certeau point — the contestation happens inside the adversary's proper. In the triad the "us" is split; but Simmel (simmel1950.md) says the split is the third's condition, so an adversarial design *for the triad* would be one that articulates the two humans as a collective — DiSalvo's *third* tactic, not his first. That relocates the construct's design politics from "revealing hegemony" to "articulating collectives," and Shepard's *CCD-Me Not Umbrella* (a camera-defeating device) shows that DiSalvo's third tactic already contains user-side obfuscation, connecting to the possible fifth affordance (see bruntonnissenbaum2015.md). CUTS AGAINST: DiSalvo's own examples are exhibition pieces, and Kiem's judgment that they yield "far more exhibitions, images, catalogues and books than actual political agency" applies with more force to a competence that must work at the point of a ride request.
