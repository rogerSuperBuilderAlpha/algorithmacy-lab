## What it argues

Nothing verified from the text. The book is a collection of Finnegan's essays, including her earlier "Literacy versus non-literacy: the great divide?" (1973), and is the standard citation for the anthropological objection that oral and literate are not two kinds of society but ends of a continuum in which every society mixes media, and that writing is not an autonomous technology with fixed effects. One adjacent fact is verified: Ong 1982 already argued with Finnegan's *Oral Poetry* (1977) over verbatim memorization, granting her only "'close similarity, in places amounting to word-for-word repetition' (1977, p. 76)" and calling her instances "ambiguous, at best" (Ong pp. 61, 63), so the Ong–Finnegan disagreement predates this book.

## Relation to the argument

This paper's use. Citation for the concession sentence on the contested great divide, alongside Street and Halverson. Her "continuum" position, if accurately reported, actually suits the paper: a competence/institution account does not need a divide between kinds of mind, only a change in what an order requires of its members, and a continuum of mixed media is compatible with that.

The scaffolding essay's use. The continuum position cuts against any clean "before Gutenberg / after Gutenberg" break; Johns 2002 makes the same point for print from the history of the book (p. 120 n. 34).
