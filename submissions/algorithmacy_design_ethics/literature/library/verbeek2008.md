# Verbeek, P.-P. (2008). Cyborg intentionality: Rethinking the phenomenology of human–technology relations. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*, 7(3): 387–395.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1007/s11097-008-9099-x · **Read depth:** abstract_only (abstract from Springer and the University of Twente record; the article is closed access, Semantic Scholar lists no open copy, and the Scribd upload does not expose text) plus secondary through Redaelli 2022 (direct_read, quotes p. 392) and Verbeek's own summaries in verbeek2008b (p. 96) and verbeek2015 (pp. 29–30) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed journal, Springer; the field's standard reference for composite intentionality · **Evidence basis:** secondary · **Parties modeled:** human–technology–world · **Relation last checked:** 2026-09-18

## What it argues

From the abstract: the article "investigates the types of intentionality involved in human–technology relations" to "augment Don Ihde's analysis" "by analyzing a number of concrete examples at the limits of Ihde's analysis," and distinguishes three types of "cyborg intentionality": "Technologically mediated intentionality occurs when human intentionality takes place 'through' technological artifacts; hybrid intentionality occurs when the technological actually merges with the human; and composite intentionality is the addition of human intentionality and the intentionality of technological artifacts." Redaelli quotes the article for "technological intentionality understood as a directedness (Verbeek 2008, p. 392)" and states that Verbeek there finds Ihde's four relations lacking "a careful analysis of some forms of intentionality" (Redaelli 2022, p. 162, n. 4). Verbeek's 2008 chapter gives the same idea as "'composite intentions' with intentionality distributed over the human and the nonhuman elements in human-technology-world relationships ... 'hybrid intentionality', or 'distributed intentionality'" (verbeek2008b, p. 96), and the 2015 piece cites this article as the source for the cyborg and immersion relations (p. 29, ref. 11). I did not see the article's own examples, its schemas, or its pages other than 392 as reported.

## Relation to the argument

RQ1, objective axis: composite intentionality is the plan's candidate for a mediator with its own directedness, and the abstract confirms the shape: human intentionality plus a technological intentionality, added. Two things follow even at this read depth. The technology's directedness is toward the world, so the composite is human → (technology → world), still one intentional human. And "addition" is the operative word; Redaelli's gloss that in AI the composite is "a result, not merely a summation" (redaelli2022, p. 164) is his extension, not Verbeek's text as far as I can see. The construct's intermediary is not an added directedness but a party that reads two humans and pursues a third objective, so this article supplies the vocabulary for the technology's side of the relation and nothing for the second human. Whether Verbeek's examples "at the limits of Ihde's analysis" include a technology that reads a person I cannot say without the text.
