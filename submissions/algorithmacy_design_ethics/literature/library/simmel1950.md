## What it argues

Simmel's claim is that the step from two to three changes the group in kind. In the dyad "each must actually perform something, and ... when he refuses to do this, only the other remains, without any superindividual energy" (I: 45); in the triad "each pair of elements are now joined by a broken line" through the third as well as directly (I: 45), and "a third and fourth member of the alliance would produce no further essential variation after the principal change had once occurred" (II). Three positions for the third follow. The **nonpartisan** is either a *mediator*, who "seeks to eliminate himself, and only to bring to pass that the two disunited or ununited parties may unite directly," acting as "a sort of central station which, in whatever form the controverted material may come in from one side, may give it out to the other side only in objective form," or an *arbitrator*, in whom the parties "have put this ultimate decision out of their own hands. ... It has become a person in the arbitrator" (II); between them "very many intermediate grades are produced." The ***tertius gaudens*** is the third who profits from the other two's division: "the advantage of nonpartisanship ... consists in the fact that he can set his own conditions for the decision" (II: 177), and his leverage "is determined exclusively by the relationship which the energies of the parties exhibit toward each other. ... When ... the quantities of force are practically equal, a minimum of addition often suffices in order to give a final decision to one of the sides" (II); "it is merely necessary that the energies of two antagonistic elements paralyze each other, in order that the never so weak position of the unattached third party may attain to unlimited strength" (II). ***Divide et impera*** is the active form: "the third instigates the difference intentionally, in order to gain a controlling situation" (II: 187). The lab's Boolean rendering (probe `gaudens_{regime}`) found the balanced-weights *gaudens* form irreducible with the third and outcome in the core, and classified *divide et impera* and the *gaudens*'s dependence on the parties' non-combination as *contingent* irreducibility (q214 probe #368).

## Relation to the argument

Simmel is the oldest formal answer to RQ8's question — adversarial design *for whom*, when the two "victims" of the mediator are also each other's opponents — and his answer reverses the worry. The parties' antagonism is not a complication for the *tertius gaudens*; it is his *condition*: "the energies of two antagonistic elements paralyze each other" is what gives the third his strength, and a driver and a customer who could combine against the platform would dissolve its position. So the three Simmelian positions map onto the construct's gradient with unusual precision. The **mediator** who "seeks to eliminate himself" so the parties "unite directly" is the channel: successful mediation ends with a direct tie and Φ = 0. The **arbitrator** in whom "the decision ... has become a person" is the party that commits a determination binding both — the structural axis. The ***tertius gaudens*** who "can set his own conditions for the decision" and profits from the parties' failure to combine is the objective axis. And ***divide et impera*** — instigating the difference to keep the controlling position — is the objective axis in its active, design-time form: a platform that suppresses the direct channel (no phone numbers, no off-platform contracting) is doing what Simmel's third does on purpose. Two consequences for the paper. First, the *gaudens*'s advantage lasts only while the parties do not deal directly, which is why the lab classifies it as *contingent* irreducibility; that gives adversarial design its target — not confronting the mediator, but restoring the parties' ability to combine — and makes the two-human antagonism something design can address rather than a reason agonism fails. Second, algorithmacy as a *competence in the coordinated party* has a Simmelian reading Simmel himself does not give: the parties who understand the third's dependence on their division can price their own non-combination. CUTS AGAINST: Simmel's third is a person with a will, "the never so weak position of the unattached third party." His analysis needs the third to *choose* his conditions; a mechanism that optimizes an objective set by its operator is a *gaudens* by proxy, and the paper should say whether the operator or the mechanism is the third. He also treats the parties' balance of force as fixed and the third's intervention as a small addition; the platform's determinations are not marginal.

## Caution

Read in the 1902 Small translation, not the 1950 Wolff translation the paper will cite; Wolff's wording differs and his pagination (pp. 145–169 for "The Triad," per the Google Books record) could not be matched to the quotations here. Verify each quotation against Wolff before it enters a draft, or cite the AJS text. Simmel's cases are human triads — marriages, coalitions, states — and his *tertius* has motives; nothing in him addresses an automated third.
