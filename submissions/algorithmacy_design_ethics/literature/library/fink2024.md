## What it argues

"Data cooperatives are a type of data intermediary using long established cooperative models and global regulatory affordances to manage data with or on behalf of members (data producers) for the benefit of members or for trade with external queriers (data users)" (Abstract). They "represent a user- or worker-owned alternative that suggests that data, and the economic and social gains emerging from it, be owned by users or workers — a decentralised form of data ownership that promotes collective power alongside individual or group owner-autonomy over data" (Introduction). The form is legally specific: many entities calling themselves cooperatives "may more accurately be another kind of data intermediary... because they are not managed through cooperative enterprises, which are a specific legal entity in most countries globally"; the ICA definition and seven principles apply, and "Cooperative managers or boards have a fiduciary responsibility to members, including duties to manage..., care..., loyalty (first allegiance is to the cooperative), and obedience" ("Definition and origin"). The socio-technical roles: members "store personal data and make decisions about its use and sharing, including giving legal consent... managing sharing at variously granular levels"; queriers are "users trading for access to data owned by the cooperative"; the cooperative "must manage user needs for cooperative members and queriers" ("Socio-technical considerations"). Consent is continuous — "systems like MIDATA allow users to change access permissions at any time and to understand which groups and for what purpose their data are being used" — and the MIT Open Algorithms approach "requir[es] queriers to submit algorithms and queries to the cooperative to be run, preventing queriers from taking, owning, or linking cooperative data." The EU Data Governance Act is "The best existing example" of supportive policy. Limits: "Thus far, little information exists on the economic viability of data cooperatives"; "meaningfully large datasets are difficult to create without successfully incentivising participation"; "Governance and sharing models are relatively untested"; "data cooperatives in many industries will face significant competition from corporate firms with more significant capital" ("Economic considerations"; "Discussion and conclusion").

## Relation to the argument

RQ8. Sorted: **collective standing — an instrument**. Fink gives the paper a citable definition of the cooperative variant and a clean contrast with the trust: the trust interposes an independent fiduciary (delacroix2019.md), the cooperative makes the members the owners, and both leave the coordinating platform in place. The entry is more useful for Principle I than its glossary form suggests because of the Open Algorithms mechanism: queriers "submit algorithms and queries to the cooperative to be run" — the direction of computation is reversed, so that the party with the data, not the party with the model, hosts the determination. That is a structural instance of parity in the construct's sense, and the paper can name it as the one existing design where the intermediary comes to the parties rather than the reverse. Two cautions for the argument. Fink's examples (MIDATA, Salus, SAOS, GISC) are health, farming and research cooperatives; none is a cooperative of workers or users of a matching platform, so the leap to the construct's cases is the paper's. And the entry is candid that the form is unproven, under-capitalised and dependent on critical mass — the same constraint sigg2025.md derives formally. CUTS AGAINST nothing directly; it grounds the *collective* pole and marks its economic fragility.

## Caution

A glossary entry: definitional, synthetic, and drawn from a small literature (Cheney et al., Hardjono and Pentland, Bietti et al.). No case is examined in depth and the author's own field is social work. The entry does not model any coordination through the cooperative; its counterpart to the member is a "querier" who buys access, not a second human whose outcomes the same determination binds.
