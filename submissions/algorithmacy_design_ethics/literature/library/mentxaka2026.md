## What it argues

A "dual taxonomy" sorts AI's democratic effects into seven risks (AIRD: subversion of autonomy, unfairness, power asymmetries, authoritarianism, undermining public discourse, undermining democratic principles, erosion of trust) and seven conditional contributions (AIPD), then maps each risk onto the seven requirements of the EU High-Level Expert Group's 2019 *Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI* in the form "X can be palliated by [ _ ] so requirement Z is fulfilled" (Fig. 5, image, p. 13). Transparency is the requirement mapped most often, to six of seven risks (p. 14). The authors grant that "explaining a model's output does not establish how an intervention would affect democratic outcomes" and that opacity "is not only technical, but also institutional, strategic, expert, and emergent" (p. 4). Their second lesson holds that trust "depends on power relations, perceived legitimacy, and the capacity for democratic oversight" (p. 15).

## Relation to the argument

A 2026 instance of the paradigm the census (`../../RESEARCH_DOSSIER.md` §1) tests: its remedies sit with institutions (regulators, auditors, certification bodies) and the artifact (transparency, robustness), and its one remedy addressed to persons is explanation, "enabling individuals to make informed choices" (p. 11). It builds on Floridi and Cowls's principles ([`floridicowls2019.md`](floridicowls2019.md)) and on Coeckelbergh's account of AI and democracy. The affected person appears as someone protected, never as a party who holds a control: Table I (image, p. 13) lists only institutions as "Responsible actors". It is not one of the six critiqued accounts and does not change the census; it is a further example of the same shape from the trustworthy-AI establishment, usable where the paper needs a current one.

## Caution

The authors call it "conceptual" and say it "has not yet been empirically validated" (p. 1), and that the mappings "do not establish the causal effectiveness of the proposed measures" (p. 2). Its subject is democracy and public institutions, not two people coordinated through a mediator, so the relation point carries over by analogy. Credit the public-service example's "appeals" (Table III, image, p. 14). Its paraphrase of the HLEG principles (p. 3) is not the guidelines' own wording; cite the guidelines directly.
