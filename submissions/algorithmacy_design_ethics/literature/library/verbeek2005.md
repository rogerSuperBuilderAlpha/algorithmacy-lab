## What it argues

By its chapter structure the book moves from a critique of classical philosophy of technology (Jaspers, Heidegger) through postphenomenology to two forms of mediation, hermeneutic ("A Material Hermeneutic," on perception) and pragmatic ("The Acts of Artifacts," on action), and ends with Borgmann's device paradigm and a design chapter. Redaelli quotes the book's placement of Latour: Latour is credited with showing that "technologies themselves coshape the interactions" (p. 103) but faulted for not bringing out how technology "coshapes the access human beings have to reality" (p. 104); Latour "argues not from the standpoint of human beings who are concretely situated in the world, but from the standpoint of an analyst who describes configurations equally from the perspective of humans and non-humans. [...] What postphenomenology contributes to actor-network theory is the situated perspective, the perspective 'from inside out'" (p. 168), and Verbeek's aim is "forging from these two approaches a fruitful way to analyze technological mediation" (p. 168) (all as quoted in redaelli2022, pp. 160–161). Verbeek's 2014 chapter cites pp. 166–168 for the claim that humans and things should be distinguished but not separated (verbeek2014, p. 86). Everything else the lab attributes to this book, including "constitutive mediator," I could not check against a page.
