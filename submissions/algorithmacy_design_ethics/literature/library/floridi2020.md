## What it argues

"AI4SG =def. the design, development, and deployment of AI systems in ways that (i) prevent, mitigate or resolve problems adversely affecting human life and/or the wellbeing of the natural world, and/or (ii) enable socially preferable and/or environmentally sustainable developments" (§Methodology). Of the five principles, "The principle of beneficence is of particular relevance. It states that the use of AI should provide benefit to people and the natural world, and indeed AI4SG projects should not just comply with but reify this principle … Beneficence is thus a necessary condition of AI4SG, yet it is insufficient, not least because the beneficent impact of an AI4SG project may be 'offset' by the creation or amplification of other risks or harms" (§Methodology). The seven factors are falsifiability and incremental deployment; safeguards against the manipulation of predictors; receiver-contextualised intervention; receiver-contextualised explanation and transparent purposes; privacy protection and data subject consent; situational fairness; and human-friendly semanticisation. The Conclusion maps each to a principle in Table 1 and states the rule of the mapping: "To reiterate, the principle of beneficence is assumed as a precondition for an AI4SG, so the factors relate to one or more of the other four principles: nonmaleficence, autonomy, justice and explicability." Table 1's column reads: nonmaleficence (falsifiability; safeguards), autonomy (receiver-contextualised intervention; semanticisation), explicability (explanation and transparent purposes), "Nonmaleficence; autonomy" (privacy and consent), justice (situational fairness). The third factor's best practice asks designers to build "with understanding of users' characteristics, the methods of coordination, the purposes and effects of an intervention; and with respect for users' right to ignore or modify interventions"; the fourth asks that "the goal (the system's purpose) for which an AI4SG system is developed and deployed is knowable to receivers of its outputs by default." The authors warn the seven are not "a 'checklist' which, if merely complied with, guarantees socially good outcomes."

## Relation to the argument

The protect/promote pattern stated by the school itself, at its most promotive: the paper exists to say what "AI for social good" requires, half of its own definition of social good is harm-shaped ("prevent, mitigate or resolve problems"), and when it turns to design every instrument is filed under a protective principle while beneficence is "assumed as a precondition." That is the sentence "Protection Without Promotion" needs from this school in its own words, and it is stronger than anything in `floridi2018.md`, where the promotive work is at least assigned somewhere (to funders and the market). Here it is assigned nowhere in the design: the good is presupposed by the project's aim and the designer's job is to keep the project from doing harm while pursuing it. Two smaller transfers. "Transparent purposes … knowable to receivers of its outputs by default" is the objective-disclosure norm `umbrello2021.md` already carries as the process-level warrant for Principle III; and "the methods of coordination" appears in the third best practice as something designers should understand about users — the word is there, the pair of coordinated people is not, since the "receiver" is one person facing an "intervention." The library already holds the seven factors via `umbrello2021.md`; cite this card only for the precondition sentence and the definition.

## Caution

A conceptual paper with illustrative cases, not an evaluation; the 27 projects were selected from a literature search and the seven "representative" cases are the authors' choice. The factor sections were not read in full here. The paper's cases are benevolent deployers (hospitals, conservation, public health); the operator with an objective of its own never appears, so the assignment of beneficence to a "precondition" is an assumption about who is designing, and the draft should say the pattern is one the school adopts for its best case, not one it was forced into.
