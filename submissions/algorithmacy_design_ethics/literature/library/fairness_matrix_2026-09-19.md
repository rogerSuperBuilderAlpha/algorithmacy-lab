# Fairness matrix — protect or promote, and who delivers the promotive part (F3 pass, 2026-09-19)

The restructured "Prevailing Frameworks" section wants to say, of Winner, Vallor, Verbeek, Value Sensitive Design and Contextual Integrity, that design ethics is built to protect people and underweights promoting their flourishing. `RESEARCH_PLAN_FLOURISHING.md` §6 predicted that this claim would not survive in its plain form and offered a reformulation: "whatever the account's telos, none of the five assigns the promotive work to the design of the relation between the two coordinated people." This matrix tests both forms against the texts, account by account, and records for each the telos, the design instrument, the party who delivers whatever promotion the account contains, and the sentence that shows it. Every quotation below is on a card in this library with its read depth and route; the Nissenbaum row is pending because F1's cards do not yet exist (the library holds only `bruntonnissenbaum2015.md`).

Read depth in one line per row: Winner, full text (1980) and partial plus an OCR census (1977); Vallor, full text (2015) and page-located snippets from the publisher's scans (2016, 2024); Verbeek, full text of the 2006 preprint and of the 2009 chapter that became *Moralizing Technology* chs. 2 and 4, plus snippets of the 2011 book; VSD, full text of 1996, 2006 (preprint), 2012 (preprint), 2011 and 2021, page images of 2015, publisher's description only of 2019.

## Verdict on §6

**The plain claim fails; the reformulated claim holds for all four accounts read, and no exception needs naming.** "All five are protective" is false of Verbeek on his first page and of Vallor on hers, and Umbrello & van de Poel's promoted/respected split means it is false of AI-era VSD as well. What survives, on every text read at full depth, is the locus finding: the promotive work — where an account has any — goes to the cultivated subject (Vallor), to the designer–user–artefact alliance serving one user (Verbeek), to the designer with borrowed ends (VSD), or to no one (Winner). None of the four assigns it to the design of a relation between two people whom a third party mediates. The census fact that carried the category-error section ("no second party on the page") carries this one too, and that is why the section can call it a second diagnosis of one error rather than a separate complaint.

**Verbeek** is the account the plan feared, and the fear was well placed as far as the plain claim goes. The 2009 chapter — the published form of *Moralizing Technology* chs. 2 and 4, located today after every route to the book failed again — is promotive in telos and in both instruments, and the paper must not list him as protective. But the chapter also answers the §6 question directly. The thing his subject gives "a desirable shape" is "the technological mediation of *their* subjectivity" (p. 260); his agents are designer, user and artefact (p. 256); his one two-person case treats the parents as one decision-maker and the unborn as the technology's construct (p. 253). The verbeek2011.md paraphrase "one's relation to other people acquires a desirable shape" is supported by neither the 2009 text nor today's p. 84 snippet ("the distance needed to gain a free relation to technology") and is flagged on that card as probably wrong. So the paper says "all five," reformulated — not "four of five." One correction to the plan's own wording: Verbeek denies that the promoted good is "the designer's conception" ("never the work of a 'prime mover'," p. 256), and his CTA augmentation democratizes the decision; the accurate phrase is "one user's good, decided by the designer–user–artefact alliance and materialized once."

**Vallor** is the partial exception §1.4 predicted, and she is more than that by one sentence. The telos is eudaimonist in her own words (2016, pp. 19, 160). Her design instruments are the protective ones (2015, p. 122; 2024, p. 196). But the p. 196 passage, now read continuously from the publisher's scan, has her draw the protect/promote line herself: the protective programme is "the low-hanging fruit," and "we also need to govern AI systems in more ambitious ways that don't just seek to mitigate their current harms, but that redirect their power to new and better ends." The section should quote this rather than say her demands are protective only. Where she puts the promotive part is the finding: in the cultivated person and in "a new collective agreement on what technologies are for" — a settlement, not a design, and not the relation.

**VSD** confirms the plan's reading and sharpens it. The founding text is protective without remainder; the 2006 heuristic list carries "Human Welfare" as its first entry; Borning & Muller's flourishing conditional is located (preprint p. 3) and is a hypothetical-imperative form, not a commitment; and Umbrello & van de Poel say in their own words that a promotive orientation "is still missing" and import it from the SDGs, with the protective values as "boundary conditions" that make a design "(minimally) acceptable." The section should cite this as VSD's own admission that promotion is borrowed, not as evidence that VSD lacks a promotive term — a reviewer who knows the 2021 paper will not accept the latter.

**Winner** is the null row and needs no more than the two sentences already in the draft.

**Nissenbaum** waits on F1. If F1's cards report what the planner read in the 2011 essay — that the heuristic's promotive term is the sphere's ends, adjudicated by institutions — the row slots in beside VSD as promotion delegated to a source outside the relation, and the "all five" holds with the same shape. If F1 finds the 2010 book gives the data subject an instrument inside the flow, the row changes and this file should be updated before the section is written.

## What contradicts §1 of the plan

1. §1.4: "the design demands are protective" is true of the enumerated instruments and false of the p. 196 governance sentence, which is promotive and has a collective, non-design locus. Rewrite the Vallor paragraph around that sentence.
2. §6: "the designer's conception of the good, materialized once" — the first half is what Verbeek explicitly denies (2009, p. 256; 2006, pp. 22–26). Keep "materialized once" (the adaptivity finding), drop "the designer's."
3. §5: the Borning & Muller conditional is not in manders2011.md's source and is in Borning & Muller at preprint p. 3, in the weak form described above.
4. verbeek2011.md's "relation to other people" paraphrase of p. 84: unverified and contradicted by the 2009 text; flagged on the card.
