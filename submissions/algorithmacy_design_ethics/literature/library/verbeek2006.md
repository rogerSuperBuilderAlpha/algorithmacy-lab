# Verbeek, P.-P. (2006). Materializing morality: Design ethics and technological mediation. *Science, Technology, & Human Values*, 31(3): 361–380.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1177/0162243905285847 · **Read depth:** abstract_only (abstract read from the University of Twente research record; the deposited PDF at ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/6843106/materializing_morality.pdf sits behind a Cloudflare challenge and the SAGE page returned 403) plus secondary through Verbeek's own later restatements (verbeek2008b, verbeek2015, both direct_read) · **Source-tier:** peer-reviewed, top STS journal · **Evidence basis:** secondary · **Parties modeled:** human–technology–world; designer–user in the method · **Relation last checked:** 2026-09-18

## What it argues

From the abstract: the paper deploys "the 'script' concept, indicating how technologies prescribe human actions," in "a normative setting" after a decade of descriptive use; when technologies co-shape action "they give material answers to the ethical question of how to act," so engineers are "doing 'ethics by other means': they materialize morality." It "first augments the script concept by developing the notion of technological mediation," then "investigates how the concept of mediation could be made fruitful for design ethics," and "elaborates two methods for anticipating technological mediation in the design process: performing mediation analyses and using an augmented version of constructive technology assessment." The 2008 chapter (verbeek2008b) restates the augmentation as mediation in two directions, pragmatic (Latour, Akrich) and hermeneutic (Ihde), and cites this paper as the home of "mediation analysis" (p. 100) and of "materialize morality" and "doing ethics by other means" (p. 99). The 2015 magazine piece cites *Moralizing Technology* rather than this paper for "designers materialize morality" (p. 31). I could not verify any page number inside 361–380, the paper's treatment of Ihde's embodiment and hermeneutic relations, or the CTA proposal in its own words.

## Relation to the argument

RQ2: this is, by the plan's account and by Verbeek's own citations, the source that tells designers to anticipate mediations, and so the licence for the seam as a designed mediation. What I can support from the abstract is narrower: two named methods, mediation analysis and augmented CTA, for anticipating mediations before deployment. That matches Ehsan et al.'s three-step seam procedure in its timing (design-time anticipation, not interaction-time surfacing) and its authorship (designers, not users), which is the concession the ehsan2024 card already records. Whether the paper says anything about disclosing a mediation to the user, as opposed to shaping it, I cannot say. The plan's claim that the paper "tells designers to anticipate mediations and design them on purpose" is consistent with the abstract; the second half, designing mediations in deliberately, is explicit in verbeek2008b (p. 101) and verbeek2015 (p. 31) and should be cited from those until this paper is read.
