# European Parliament and Council. (2022). Regulation (EU) 2022/868 of 30 May 2022 on European data governance and amending Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 (Data Governance Act). *Official Journal of the European Union*, L 152, 3 June 2022, 1–44.

## What it argues

Article 10 lists the services subject to Article 12 and to notification: "(a) intermediation services between data holders and potential data users"; "(b) intermediation services between data subjects that seek to make their personal data available... and potential data users, including making available the technical or other means to enable such services, and in particular enabling the exercise of the data subjects' rights provided in Regulation (EU) 2016/679"; "(c) services of data cooperatives." Article 11 sets a notification regime with a public register and a label, "data intermediation services provider recognised in the Union," and a common logo (Art. 11(9)–(10)). Article 12 states the conditions, and they are conditions of non-interest: "(a) the data intermediation services provider shall not use the data for which it provides data intermediation services for purposes other than to put them at the disposal of data users and shall provide data intermediation services through a separate legal person"; "(b) the commercial terms, including pricing... shall not be dependent upon whether the data holder or data user uses other services provided by the same data intermediation services provider"; "(c) the data collected with respect to any activity of a natural or legal person for the purpose of the provision of the data intermediation service... shall be used only for the development of that data intermediation service"; "(f) the... procedure for access to its service is fair, transparent and non-discriminatory for both data subjects and data holders, as well as for data users"; "(i) the data intermediation services provider shall take appropriate measures to ensure interoperability with other data intermediation services"; "(m) the data intermediation services provider offering services to data subjects shall act in the data subjects' best interest where it facilitates the exercise of their rights, in particular by informing and, where appropriate, advising data subjects... about intended data uses by data users and standard terms and conditions attached to such uses before data subjects give consent"; "(n)... provide data subjects with tools to both give and withdraw consent"; "(o)... maintain a log record of the data intermediation activity."

## Relation to the argument

RQ8. Sorted: **collective standing (legal form) — grounds the how of standing, not a counter-agent**. The DGA is the statute that gives Delacroix and Lawrence's trusts and Fink's cooperatives a registered category, and it is more useful to the paper than the sovereignty card saw, because Article 12 is a legislative definition of the construct's *moderator*. Read against the construct: an intermediary that may not use the data "for purposes other than to put them at the disposal of data users" (a), may not tie terms to its other services (b), may not repurpose the metadata of the mediation itself (c), and must act "in the data subjects' best interest" (m) is an intermediary the law forbids from "pursuing an objective neither set." The DGA's answer to Φ > 0 is to legislate Φ = 0 for a class of intermediaries — to require them to carry signals without interest — and the paper can cite it as the regulatory version of the moderator pole. That is also its limit for Principle I: the Act regulates the intermediary the user *chooses*, not the platform the user *faces*; nothing in Chapter III binds a labour or matching platform, and a recognised intermediary sits beside the coordination as a data-rights fiduciary, not inside it as a party. The interoperability condition (i) is a small precondition for counter-agents among intermediaries. CUTS AGAINST the paper if it claims that no existing instrument distinguishes interested from disinterested intermediaries; the DGA does, and does so by structural separation and purpose limitation rather than by any competence in the user.

## Caution

Only Chapter III was read; the recitals bearing on it (26–35) and the enforcement provisions (Articles 13–15) were not. Whether any recognised intermediary has delivered standing to data subjects is not addressed by the text and, per the sovereignty library's gaps file, no empirical work has shown it. The November 2025 Digital Omnibus proposal to fold these rules into the Data Act (recorded in the sovereignty card) was not checked this round; cite the Act as in force at the OJ date and flag the pending amendment.
