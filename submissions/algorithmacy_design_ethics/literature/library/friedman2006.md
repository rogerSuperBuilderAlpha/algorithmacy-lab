## What it argues

VSD is "a theoretically grounded approach to the design of technology that accounts for human values in a principled and comprehensive manner throughout the design process" (p. 1), run as iterated conceptual, empirical and technical investigations. Three cases carry it: a Mozilla cookie manager built to the informed-consent criteria of disclosure, comprehension, voluntariness, competence and agreement, whose formative evaluation added a sixth, "minimal distraction" (p. 6); Room with a View, a plasma display showing a live plaza scene to an interior office, where the researchers realised that passers-by "unknowingly had their images displayed" and surveyed 750 of them and interviewed 30 as indirect stakeholders, finding that more women than men objected (p. 8); and UrbanSim, which separates "explicitly supported values" (fairness, accountability, democracy) from "stakeholder values" that are "important to some but not necessarily all," and whose model "should not a priori favor or rule out any given set of stakeholder values" (pp. 10–11). The eight-feature "constellation" defines the stakeholder classes: "Direct stakeholders refer to parties – individuals or organizations – who interact directly with the computer system or its output. Indirect stakeholders refer to all other parties who are affected by the use of the system," with medical-records systems as the example — insurers, hospitals and doctors direct, patients "a rather important group of indirect stakeholders" (p. 13). The practical section adds that "an organizational power structure is often orthogonal to the distinction between direct and indirect stakeholders" (p. 15), that priority goes to "indirect stakeholders who are strongly affected, or to large groups that are somewhat affected" (p. 15), and, under "Integrate Value Considerations Into One's Organizational Structure," that "ideally, Value Sensitive Design will work in concert with organizational objectives ... In the real world, of course, human values (especially those with ethical import) may collide with economic objectives, power, and other factors. However, even in such situations, Value Sensitive Design should be able to make positive contributions, by showing alternate designs that better support enduring human values" (pp. 16–17). Among the technical heuristics: "Unanticipated values and value conflicts often emerge after a system is developed and deployed. Thus, when possible, design flexibility into the underlying technical architecture" (p. 19). Trust is glossed as "fundamentally a relationship between people (sometimes mediated by machines)" (p. 3).

## Relation to the argument

The definitional text for RQ5's stakeholder question, and it settles two points. First, the inversion the RQ posits does not arise at the level of definition: a counterpart who receives the intermediary's output is "direct" because direct includes interacting with the system's output; what the medical-records example shows is that in practice VSD classes the party the system is *about* as indirect, and the construct's counterpart is both — output recipient and object. Second, the power line is decisive: the direct/indirect distinction carries no information about who binds whom, by the authors' own admission, so it cannot do the work that the structural axis (co-determination) does; VSD would need to import that from elsewhere, and §6.2 says elsewhere is Participatory Design. On adjudication, §6.7 is the consensual assumption in the authors' words — VSD works "in concert with organizational objectives" and, in conflict, persuades by "showing alternate designs." That is advocacy toward the operator, not a rule against it. Room with a View is worth flagging as VSD's own two-party-plus-mediator case: the display binds the Watched without their knowledge, and the method's response was to survey them, which is exactly the process contribution — deciding whom the seam serves — that the four affordances lack. The paper does not cut against any affordance; the "design flexibility" heuristic anticipates "adapt as it changes" from the designer's side.

## Caution

A methods chapter whose evidence is three of the authors' own research prototypes, one with results "in progress"; the stakeholder definitions are stipulative and the power remark is a single sentence. The preprint may differ in wording from the Sharpe and Wiley printings, which I did not open. The list of thirteen values (Table 1) is presented as "a heuristic," the framing that Le Dantec et al. (2009) and Borning and Muller (2012) later dispute.
