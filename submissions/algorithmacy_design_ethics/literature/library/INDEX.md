# Algorithmacy and Design Ethics — Contextual Bibliography (index)

99 sources, one file per entry, built by 8 parallel Fable research passes and adversarially
re-verified by 3 parallel Fable verification passes (S2) that re-opened sources, checked every
quotation and page cite, and corrected errors in place. Grouped by research cluster (matching
`../../RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §3), not alphabetically. Two working files sit alongside the cards:
`gemini_report_citation_audit.md` (a 25-entry fabrication/quality audit of the report that
prompted this arm) and `journal_scan_memo.md` (a live scan of nine candidate journals'
2024–2026 tables of contents); `triadic_hf_search_notes.md` documents the search for the clinical
decision-support case.

**Status flags** (from each card's own S2 adversarial-verification section): ✓ confirmed, no
issues found on re-check. ✓* corrected — S2 found and fixed a real error (wrong quote, wrong
page, misattribution); the card is now accurate but carries the history in its S2 note. ⚠ could
not verify — the source could not be re-opened this round; use with the caution the card already
states.

**Parties-modeled census** (`grep -l "Parties modeled:\*\* two humans + mediator" *.md`, exact
string match): **12 of 99** cards carry that phrase — Corti 2023, Friedman & Nissenbaum 1996
(NRMP case), Hansman/Midkiff/Pritchett 1993–95 (CPDLC party line, 3 cards), Hirsch et al. 2017,
Russ 2015 (prescriber–pharmacist alert), Suchman 1994/1995 (2 cards), Vallor 2010, Winograd 1994,
and Winograd & Flores 1986 — against 87 that model a dyad (human–technology,
human–technology–world, designer–stakeholders) or an artifact-level relation with no second human
at all. Two of the twelve are qualified on inspection: Vallor 2010's own card notes its mediator
"is treated as a medium, not a party" (fails the objective axis by the card's own account), and
Hirsch et al. 2017 carries an S2 "could not verify" flag. The stricter count — sources that model
a mediator actually pursuing something of its own — is smaller still. That gradient, not just the
raw 12/99, is the empirical answer to RQ9.

## Cluster A — Postphenomenology and mediation (RQ1, RQ2)

  (chapter inaccessible; read via Hertz's notes and Latour's citations of it). Source of "script."
- ✓ [Corti, L., et al. (2023). Technological grandparents.](corti2023.md) — the one Cluster A

  mediator's side.

  hermeneutic relation is the field's closest existing analogue to an interpreting intermediary.

  presented as the abstract's wording.
- ✓ [Verbeek, P.-P. (2008). Cyborg intentionality.](verbeek2008.md) — composite intentionality,
  the nearest thing to a mediator with its own directedness.
- ✓ [Verbeek, P.-P. (2008b). Morality in design.](verbeek2008b.md) — states mediation is no
  intrinsic property of the artifact; Φ must be stated on the coordination form, not the device.
- ⚠ [Verbeek, P.-P. (2011). *Moralizing Technology*.](verbeek2011.md) — could not verify this
  round.
- ⚠ [Verbeek, P.-P. (2014). Some misunderstandings.](verbeek2014.md) — could not verify this
  round; only the abstract and a partial repository record confirmed.
- ✓ [Verbeek, P.-P. (2015). Beyond interaction.](verbeek2015.md) — the ACM/HCI-readable
  introduction to mediation theory.

**Central finding for RQ1:** Verbeek's framework has no threshold — "technologies always
mediate" is explicitly the "extensionist" view his theory rejects. The channel/party split has no
standing inside postphenomenology as it stands; Φ's threshold must be argued as an *extension* of
the theory, not a reconciliation with it. Corti et al. is the only canonical source with a
two-human schema, and even it has no independent-objective mediator.

**Central finding for RQ9 (this cluster's contribution):** the CPDLC party-line literature is a
real, citable field case of "suppress the direct channel" — pilots who could overhear every
clearance on a shared voice frequency lose that access under point-to-point datalink. One
documented behavioral finding: crews who overheard a controller's error mostly stayed silent
rather than contest it. Limit: the channel itself has no independent objective — structural axis
only, no objective axis. See `triadic_hf_search_notes.md` for the full search record, including
the clinical decision-support case (Russ 2015), which is real but has a *degraded*, not fully
closed, direct channel.

**Central finding for RQ5:** VSD's stakeholder model cannot adjudicate a cooptation case, from
three independent directions (Manders-Huits, Borning & Muller, JafariNaimi). The direct/indirect
distinction is defined by who receives the system's output, not by co-determination, so it
"carries no information about co-determination, which is the work Φ does." Every contestability
source inherits a controller/subject dyad; the co-determined counterpart appears in none of them.

## Cluster G — The hypothesis map's own proposal (RQ9 test case)

- ✓ [Mechanistic interpretability — method card.](mechanistic_interpretability_method.md) —
  verdict: discloses a model's internals to whoever holds the weights, i.e., the party who already
  set the objective. No representation of a second human party anywhere in the founding texts.
- ✓* [C2PA Technical Specification.](c2pa_content_provenance_standard.md) — verdict: discloses an
  asset's signed origin to a downstream validator; the consumer is "not specified in the trust
  model." No coordination between two humans appears anywhere in the document.
- [Gemini report citation audit.](gemini_report_citation_audit.md) — 9/25 sampled sources real and
  citable as-is; 14/25 real but reached through low-tier stand-ins (Scribd, ResearchGate mirrors,
  blogs); 2/25 unverifiable; 0/25 confirmed fabrications. All four of the report's named
  theoretical anchors (Winner, Vallor, Verbeek, Martela & Steger) were reached only through
  low-tier hosts. Entry 57 — the report's own source for the word "algorithmacy" — is the user's
  Algorithmacy Conference CFP; its actual definition is triadic, and the report uses the term
  dyadically anyway.

**Central verdict for RQ9 (the paper's central audit):** neither of the Gemini report's two
proposed mechanisms addresses the triadic problem. The "AI Context Engine" is a dyadic model-audit
remedy welded to an object-centered authenticity remedy, pointed at a problem that is neither.
