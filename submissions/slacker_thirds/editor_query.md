Dear [editor],

Four questions about the *Slacker* chapter, in descending order of how much they affect the draft.

**1. The word ceiling.** The chapter now has roughly 5,600 words of body text, plus 49 endnotes and a
bibliography. That is over the 3,000–5,000 range, by about 600 words, and I would rather ask than cut
blind. The overage comes from three sections added after the abstract was accepted: a short
acknowledgement of the existing criticism that sets the film against the internet, a section dating the
platform logic the chapter describes to the 1960s and 1970s rather than to the internet, and a reading
of the characters' attempts to be heard once leaving is closed to them. If the ceiling is firm I will
bring the body under 5,000; if there is room for a chapter of this length, I would prefer to keep the
three. Please also let me know whether notes count toward the limit.

**2. Title, editor of record, and deadline.** I have none of these on file and would like them for the
manuscript's front matter.

**3. Citation style.** The chapter is currently in Chicago notes-and-bibliography, with 49 notes. If
the collection uses author-date or MLA, the conversion is mechanical and I would prefer to do it
once.

**4. A change from the accepted abstract.** The accepted abstract distinguishes a "hosting" third
from an "engineering" third, compares the camera directly with Uber, and closes with a century-of-
cinema training claim, streaming enclosure, and questions about platform interpretive skills. The
current chapter argues that *Slacker* shows how a modern platform works: an
indifferent machine that packs people into the same space and picks who meets whom, so that
connection, shared belief, and a yes are not required. The city supplies proximity; the camera
supplies selection. The chapter no longer develops the century-of-cinema or streaming-enclosure
claims, and it does not define algorithmacy. I am flagging this substantive revision before
submission and can restore the accepted abstract's frame if the collection requires closer
correspondence.

One further note, in case it matters for the collection's apparatus: the chapter corrects several
small factual claims that circulate widely about the film. The Dobie Theatre run was eleven weeks,
not a year. Linklater's end credit reads "Should have stayed at bus station," with no definite
article, as the print and the AFI Catalog both have it, though the Criterion website inserts one.

With thanks,
Roger Hunt
