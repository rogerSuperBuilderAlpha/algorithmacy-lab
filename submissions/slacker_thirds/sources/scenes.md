## What the chapter is missing, ranked by fit to its argument

1. **The PixelVision handoff (seg. 30).** The film literalizes its own device: a camera physically
   passed from stranger to stranger, "shoot whatever you want," ending with "there ain't no film
   in that shit." A diegetic, democratized engineering third — the relay put in the characters'
   hands. Direct §4 material, and it strengthens the ending's Super-8 reading.
2. **The Video Backpacker (seg. 24).** The film's own media theorist: the video image "more
   powerful and useful than an actual event," reality as the degraded copy (can't rewind, can't
   fix the hue). Plus a working open distribution chain — dub, contacts, pirate TV — that
   prefigures §7's reachable-thirds argument. Strongest single addition for §6.
3. **The Ulysses passage (seg. 15).** "The last term of a preceding series… a series originating
   in and repeated to infinity" — the film reading aloud a description of its own serial form.
   One sentence in §4 buys a lot.
4. **"The underlying order is chaos" (seg. 25).** The breakthrough-day monologue voices exactly
   the self-organization reading Poulaki gives the film. The chapter can let the film itself state
   the rival account, then answer it — sharper than citing Poulaki alone.
5. **Small legible thirds at the club (segs. 28–29, 31).** The guest list that fails, the
   three-dollar cover, the transferable stamp, the 2 a.m. eviction. Street-level coordination
   infrastructure that is visible, arguable, and gameable — the exact contrast §5 draws with the
   platform, played out in miniature.
6. **"You just saw it on TV" (seg. 10) and the Scooby-Doo/Smurfs riff (seg. 26).** Folk media
   theory inside the film — characters doing amateur versions of Benjamin's training claim. §6.
7. **The camera inside the film (seg. 20).** The "true call" street interview: an on-screen
   interviewer with a camera, a second diegetic third. Pairs with the PixelVision scene.
8. **The Juan Apagato postcards (seg. 7).** A character who exists only as narration on numbered
   cards, with a serialized "next episode" — the film containing its own character-card system.
9. **The Old Anarchist (segs. 21–22).** The daughter's debunking makes him the film's monument to
   unreliable self-narration; the Whitman admiration is the dark side of Austin's hosting (the
   tower as the anti-host). Also the only scene where one character deliberately recruits another.
10. **The one-to-many thirds at dawn (segs. 33–34).** Tape recorder and loudspeaker car:
    broadcasting into public space, address without a addressee — another face of the third.
