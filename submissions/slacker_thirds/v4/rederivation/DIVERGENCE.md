# DIVERGENCE — adjudicating A, B and C

Written 2026-08-10 against `CANDIDATE_A/B/C.md`, `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md`, `OBJECTIONS.md`,
`chapter/chapter_v3.md`, `abstract.md`, `v4/factbase/CLAIMS.md`, `v4/factbase/scene_ledger.md`,
`v4/ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and `research/slacker_transcript.md`.

This memo does not recommend a design. It prices three of them and marks the places where a choice
would be expensive to unmake.

---

## 1. Verdict on derivability

**The existing frame is not derivable from the evidence, and the agreement that looks like
derivation is the brief's own handwriting coming back.** Neither blind designer reaches two named
kinds of third with the city on one side and the camera on the other: A explicitly discards it — "It
gives up the tidiness of two named kinds… the title terms stop doing the sorting" — and B puts the
camera and Austin on the *same* side of its primary question, so that the abstract's headline
contrast ("Austin hosts while the camera engineers") survives only as a difference of degree on a
secondary axis. What A and B do converge on — a two-question architecture with governing capacities
claimed separately — was written into `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §§1–2 as the stated satisfier, in
wording lifted from the reviewer's own proposed repair, so the convergence is transcription, not
independent arrival. One thing the brief did not ask for shows up in all three: a count of how many
of the film's segment-to-segment joins are audible in dialogue, promoted from bookkeeping to the
chapter's spine. That is the only structural move in the exercise that behaves like a finding, and
even it was pre-computed for all three designers by the scene ledger's handoff column. The honest
summary is that the incumbent frame is inherited, the repair is seeded, and the count is the one
place the film pushed back.

## 3. Where they agree

### 3a. Seeded agreements — not evidence

Each of these is traceable to a line in `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` (which A and B worked from), to
Reviewer 2's own proposed fix in `OBJECTIONS.md` (which C worked from), or to the shared factbase.
None of them tells the author anything about which frame the film supports.

1. **Splitting "which kind" from "what governing capacities it holds."** Seeded twice over.
   `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §2: "Satisfies it: treating 'which kind' and 'what governing capacities
   it holds' as separate claims, acknowledging non-selecting environments can hold governing
   capacities too." C got the same instruction from Reviewer 2 directly: "Demand: separate
   'selection' from 'regime.'" A's "capacity register," B's "holdings," and C's "regime" are three
   names for one dictated move.

2. **The two-question architecture itself, and the phrase "point opposite ways."** This is the
   seeding the brief did not advertise and is worth stating plainly. `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §1
   reads: "Satisfies it: different questions sorting different kinds — some separating cases where
   an **in-reach** environment does the work, others where a specific **pairing-in-sequence** does —
   with an account of why they **point opposite ways**." That sentence is a paraphrase of Reviewer
   2's repair in `OBJECTIONS.md` §1 ("attentional selectors are settings *in reach* and selectors
   *in sequence*… they point opposite ways"). A returns it nearly verbatim — "**Reach.** Does it hold
   people where they can summon each other" / "**Handoff.** Does it decide which specific party comes
   next… in sequence" / "The two questions point opposite ways." B takes the first question and
   replaces the second. Two independent designers landing on a two-question test proves only that
   both read §1.

3. **A counterexample that prices and excludes without choosing pairs.** `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §3
   specifies the shape ("a counterexample that doesn't choose pairs yet still prices a transaction
   and can exclude someone") and even suggests the object ("a door can select and exclude"). A and C
   both use the club door; B uses Austin. Fully dictated.

4. **The critical option in the training section, in the brief's own words.** `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md`
   §5 supplies the sentence: "the competence is one of following an arrangement someone else made,
   not of contesting it, which darkens the ending." A: "the competence to follow an arrangement
   someone else made." B: "following an arrangement, not contesting one." C reaches the same place
   through critical theory's vocabulary already in v3. That all three "darken" §6 is worth nothing
   as evidence.

5. **Both falsifier statistics.** `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §6 asks for "one falsifying case plus one
   specific thing the claim buys." A and B return the identical pair — Nilforoshan et al.'s 67% and
   Fan et al.'s 26%-against-44% — because both are sitting in `CLAIMS.md` at class A (S3-028,
   S3-031). Shared factbase, not convergence.

6. **The prior art.** `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §7 names the literatures without naming the citations;
   `CLAIMS.md` supplies the citations (S2-080 Fuhse, S2-082 Meyer et al., S2-083 O'Reilly–Strauss–
   Mazzucato). All three cite all three and all three mark them second-hand, which is correct — all
   three rows are class C.

7. **Withholding a reason is a fourth capability, with the cut as the boundary case.** §8 of the
   brief asks for "an explicit yes-or-no… and if yes, an account of where a boundary case (a device
   that also never explains an individual choice, like a cut in a film) sits relative to it." All
   three say yes and all three discuss the cut. The boundary case was handed over.

8. **Conceding the real-Austin / film-Austin slide, with Gaughen's three-mile jump and Linklater's
   "if you didn't know Austin, the film makes sense."** §4 dictates the concession; `CLAIMS.md`
   supplies both facts at class A (S3-055, S1-022).

### 3b. Unseeded agreements — the real evidence

1. **Counting the joins, and making the count the chapter's spine.** *Strength: moderate, with one
   large qualification.* Nothing in `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` mentions counting anything, and
   `chapter_v3.md` contains no count — its footnote 2 says only that "'About three dozen' handoffs
   matches the project's inventory of thirty-five dialogue segments." So the count is genuinely
   absent from both the brief and the incumbent draft, and three designers working from three
   different starting points all made it load-bearing: A's "the chapter's spine is the film's own
   finding," B's "Count the joins" as §1, C's "§3 is the section the frame stands on." The
   qualification: `scene_ledger.md` has a "The handoff out of it" column that adjudicates every
   transition segment by segment. Any designer reading the ledger has the count handed to them in
   pieces. What the ledger does *not* do is promote it from coverage bookkeeping to an argument, and
   that promotion is the shared move. Call it independent in judgment, dependent in data.

2. **Refusing the abstract's strong camera-to-Uber equation.** *Strength: weak-to-moderate.* All
   three narrow it — A to "same handoff question, different capacities," B to "the close reading
   arrives around the match… not inside it," C to "one shared power out of five." Partly forced by
   `OBJECTIONS_NEUTRAL.md` §3, which forbids resting a payoff on capacities the classification does
   not predict. What is *not* forced is how far they go: C is much the most severe about it, and
   volunteers that the result is "less than the abstract's 'formal and specific' promised."

3. **Locating the chapter's third at Simmel's own deferred fourth kind, and quoting the deferral as
   a deferral.** *Strength: low as evidence, high as reassurance.* The neutral brief never mentions
   Simmel. But the abstract does, `CLAIMS.md` S2-001 carries the exact passage at class A ("the
   group-forming, mediating function of a third element will be discussed in a later context"), and
   v3 §2 already makes the move. Three designers with the same row in front of them using the same
   row is not convergence. It does confirm the move is safe: the claim is class A and the deferral
   is verbatim.

4. **Refusing to say anything the image would have to settle.** *Strength: nil as evidence.* Forced
   by `ASSUMPTIONS.md` A3 and by the absence of any disc pass. B states it as a design choice ("no
   shot, format or take asserted"); C states it as a limit ("Nothing from segment 35, which is
   wordless, and no format named for any camera"). This is the instrument, not the finding.

## 4. Where they genuinely differ

### Fork 1 — the sorting machinery

- **A: a four-cell grid.** Reach and handoff are "independent, so there are four cells, not two
  kinds." Buys: the strongest answer to the "test does not sort" objection, because handoff "comes
  in a degree the transcript measures," so the test does *not* return the same verdict for every
  film. Costs: the paradigm cases do not spread across the grid. On A's own list, the camera
  (no/yes), Uber (no/yes) and Prime Video Slate (no/yes) share a cell; only Austin (yes/no) and the
  door policy (no/no) sit apart. The camera-versus-Uber distinction — the chapter's whole subject —
  is then carried by the capacity register, which by construction is not part of the sorting.
- **B: two questions, deliberately uncrossed.** B keeps the brief's first question and invents the
  second: "Can the joined parties check the join?" Buys: five paradigm cases sort to five different
  places, and the second axis is the one that connects to the closing questions about
  contestability — "the club door's rule was sayable and its stamp copyable." Costs: on question
  one, "a camera: no, of the people filmed" puts the camera beside Austin, the door and the
  submissions page. The abstract's central contrast is not what the primary question detects.
- **C: a single decidable question.** "Does the order of encounters arise inside the field or
  outside it?" Buys: the least machinery and a stated falsifier the transcript can decide. Costs:
  see §5 of the objection check below — the question answers *outside* for every representation,
  which is verbatim the failure mode Reviewer 2 named.

### Fork 2 — is the film's structure static or changing?

Only A treats it as changing, and this is the sharpest genuine difference in the exercise. A: "the
joining migrates… Through segment 16 the cut joins almost alone; from 17 to 31 the characters take
over, offering rides, addresses, guest lists and a licked wrist stamp; at dawn the cut takes it back."
B and C both read one ratio over the whole runtime — B, "fifteen of the thirty-four joins… are made
in speech a viewer can hear"; C, "fewer than a third of the handoffs are produced by something a
character says or does." **The migration claim survives the recount below** (see §5): roughly 3 of
the first 15 transitions carry a spoken bridge, roughly 9 of the next 15, and 0 of the last 4. If the
author wants an original empirical finding rather than an original number, this is where it is. It
is also the claim with the most to lose from a disc pass, because it depends on the segmentation
being right in two places rather than one.

### Fork 3 — the training section

- **A** takes the critical option and keeps a narrow positive case beside it: measured cut-reading is
  "partial (55% of experienced viewers on the gravestone sequence, 0% on the child sequence)," the
  one transfer test found nothing, and the narrow win is *Slacker*'s own — "1990 Austin viewers
  audited the handoffs and told Linklater the geography was wrong. They could contest a handoff's
  content; nothing in the measured record shows they could contest its order." That is the only
  version of §6 in the three that ends on an *ability* rather than a deficit, and it is built from
  the film's own reception.
- **B** takes the critical option and spends the section on labour: "Heiland's couriers — who
  theorised that speed drove their allocations, were wrong on an A/B test, and were never corrected
  — show what that competence looks like when it is all a worker has." Biggest section in B's budget
  after §5 (800 words). Moves §6 out of film history and into platform sociology.
- **C** takes the critical option and keeps the section inside cinema: "cinema trained a competence
  at receiving an arrangement and none at answering back, and *Slacker* is where that training
  becomes visible because no plot is there to absorb the credit." This is the version already in v3
  and already blessed by Reviewer 2 at panel 3 ("This is the right argument").

### Fork 4 — enclosure

A and B both take the earn path with the dated fact (Smits 2017 "unfiltered, with no gatekeeping
restrictions" against the page retrieved 1 August 2026, "Access to self-service submissions is by
invitation only"), and both confront the counter-evidence. C drops it: "the Smits fact that would
earn it was never entered." **C's ground is stale.** The fact was never entered *into v3*; it is in
the v4 factbase at class A twice over (S7-050 Smits, S7-041 Amazon). C is answering the state of the
evidence as of panel 2, not as of the rebuild. This is cheap to fix in C and should not weigh against
it, but it should not be read as a considered judgment either.

### Fork 5 — what the chapter is a chapter *about*

A ends up a chapter about the film's construction, with Austin demoted to background ("It gives up
Austin"). B ends up a chapter about audibility and checkability, with the image ruled out by design.
C ends up, by its own account, "a film chapter with a borrowed vocabulary, one original count, and a
concession on every page." A reader deciding on ambition should read those three self-assessments
side by side; they are the most honest paragraphs in the three documents.

## 5. The evidence check: is the count sound?

**Verdict: roughly sound as a band, not sound as a number, and blocked as a class.**

### The recount

Working the transcript against the ledger's own segment boundaries, adjudicating each transition at
the seam rather than accepting the ledger's summary:

| | transitions |
|---|---|
| **Firm spoken bridge at the seam** (12) | 4→5, 6→7, 7→8, 17→18, 18→19, 19→20, 21→22, 23→24, 27→28, 28→29, 29→30, 30→31 |
| **Equivocal** (5) | 5→6, 11→12, 13→14, 20→21, 26→27 |
| **No bridge** (17) | the rest, including 34→35, which is wordless and can never be audible |

So the defensible band is 12–17 of 34. A's "fifteen… seventeen carry none, two are equivocal" sits
inside that band; A's exact split does not reproduce — the recount finds three more equivocal cases
and two fewer firm ones. B's "fifteen of the thirty-four" sits inside the band too.

### Four specific problems, in order of severity

1. **The whole count is class D and the pipeline's own rule blocks it.** `ASSUMPTIONS.md` A3 lists,
   under "What is blocked until then," first item: "**The count and classification of the film's
   handoffs.**" The same section says "**Nothing in this category may be guessed, inferred from
   subtitles, or written around with a hedge.** The transcript is subtitle-derived and the project
   rule is that it is a pointer to the disc, not a source." `CLAIMS.md` bears this out: the factbase
   contains exactly one row sourced to the transcript (S4-079, the *Ulysses* passage) and classes it
   **D**, noting "film-content/dialogue fact; verified only against the project's own transcript."
   All three designs treat "subtitle-derived" as a sufficient hedge. Under the rule as written, it is
   not — D "may not be asserted at all until the author supplies it." **This is disqualifying for all
   three equally, and it is the finding that has to be settled before any of them is drafted, not at
   drafting.** Either the author watches the disc, or the author relaxes A3 in writing for this one
   class of claim. It is not a defect in any candidate; it is a rule none of them can satisfy.
2. **Two of the named bridges do not survive the transcript.** B's "a line repeated across the cut
   ('What a day, what a day,' 5→6)" — the string occurs once in that region, at line 281, which the
   ledger assigns to segment 6's opening. Whether it is a *repeat* requires knowing who says it and
   where the cut falls, which subtitles cannot show. The ledger's claim for 25→26 (the "shooee,
   howdy, shucks" catchphrase "carrying across the cut into segment 26") fails on line numbers: the
   catchphrase is at 1180–1181, fifteen lines before the boundary at 1195/1196, and the actual seam
   runs "Repetition is a form of change." into "It's all a whole bunch of, like, horrible values."
   The verbatim repeat that *does* hold is the cigarette line at 866/867 (19→20), which A and B both
   have.
3. **A and C are not counting the same thing, and neither says so.** A and B count transitions
   bridged by *audible dialogue*. C counts handoffs "produced by something a character says or
   does," explicitly excluding "a rhyme nobody in the film could hear" — which is why C gets "fewer
   than a third" (≤11) where A and B get 15. Both are defensible; they are different statistics.
   Whichever design is chosen, the criterion has to be stated in the chapter or a referee will read
   the number as wrong rather than as differently defined.
4. **The denominator is unstable and partly an artifact of the segmentation.** The ledger's own
   segments overlap in at least three places (6/7 at 308–318, 17/18 at 809–815, 18/19 at 848–851),
   so at those points "the transition" is not a point and a "bridge" is partly a consequence of
   where the boundary was drawn. Separately, B writes "the film's thirty-five dialogue segments" —
   segment 35 has no dialogue, so there are 34 dialogue segments, and the 34th transition is a
   guaranteed no in the denominator. A's "the thirty-four transitions the transcript covers" has the
   same problem: the transcript does not cover 34→35.

### One internal inconsistency worth catching now

B §2 says nineteen joins "are made by something dialogue cannot account for" — more than half. B §4
then says the camera "by the film's own dialogue… makes fewer than half the joins." Those cannot both
be true. On B's own count the camera makes 19 of 34.

### What would confirm it

One afternoon with the Criterion disc, taking notes against `scene_ledger.md`, recording for each of
the 34 transitions: (a) where the cut actually falls, (b) whether the bridging line is spoken before
or after it, (c) whether it is one speaker or two, and (d) whether the two segments share a location
or a shot. That settles the numerator, the denominator, the two failed bridges above, and A's
migration claim in a single pass. Nothing short of it upgrades the count out of class D. A second,
cheaper check that does not settle the class: the St. Martin's 1992 screenplay book, which carries
scene headings the subtitle transcript lacks and would at least fix the segment boundaries.

## 8. Three questions for the author

1. **Does A3 hold?** If the transcript stays "a pointer to the disc, not a source," none of the three
   can be drafted until an afternoon with the Criterion disc happens — so: disc first, or A3 relaxed
   in writing for the handoff count and dialogue quotations?
2. **Is the abstract's Austin-hosts / camera-engineers contrast a commitment to *keep* or a
   commitment to *answer*?** A demotes Austin to background, B puts Austin and the camera on the
   same side of its primary question, and C keeps the contrast at the cost of a sorting question
   that does not sort.
3. **One number or one shape?** B and C sell a ratio; A sells a migration across the runtime. The
   migration survives the recount and is the only claim in the three that no other account of the
   film contains — but it is also the claim a disc pass could break in two places instead of one.
