# Objections against the chapter's architecture, hedged rather than settled

Compiled from four review rounds (2026-07-03, 2026-07-22, 2026-08-02 panel 2, 2026-08-02 panel 3,
2026-08-02-full) plus `chapter/PANEL3_STATUS.md` and `chapter/AUTHOR_TASKS.md`. Scope: objections to the
chapter's *architecture* — its central distinction, its sorting test, its claim to novelty, and its
account of what the chapter's own evidence supports — not prose, citation hygiene, or single-fact
corrections. Every quotation below is verbatim, with file path and section. Where I could not find a
quotation for an item on the requesting brief, I say so rather than paraphrase it into existence.

This file states the objections. It does not resolve them. Where a synthesis or status file marks an
item "closed," I quote that claim and quote what changed; I do not adjudicate whether the change answers
the objection's substance. That judgment belongs to whoever rebuilds the chapter.

## 1. The test does not sort

### Quoted, verbatim

Reviewer 2, panel 2 (2026-08-02), `reviews/2026-08-02/08_reviewer2.md`, §1.2 ("FATAL. The three
questions misclassify the camera and the landing page — the chapter's own two exhibits"):

> "So the score sheet, run honestly: Austin 0/3, the prohibition 1/3, the camera 2/3, the landing page
> 2/3, Uber 3/3. The only object the test sorts cleanly is the one whose classification was never in
> doubt. **This is the load-bearing failure of the piece,** and it is worse than the novelty problem
> because the novelty problem has a repair that costs one sentence and this one costs a redesign."

Same section, on question 2:

> "Question 2 is vacuous for the camera. 'Could the encounter have happened without it?' A viewer and a
> character cannot meet without a medium, ever, by the nature of the case. So question 2 returns
> *selector* for every film, every novel, every broadcast, every painting. A test that classifies all
> representation as selection is not discriminating anything in the domain where the chapter's title
> object lives."

Panel synthesis, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/SYNTHESIS.md`, §8 ("THE ONE ARCHITECTURAL DECISION"):

> "Run honestly, the test splits on the chapter's own paradigm cases. **Austin** answers setting three
> times. **Uber** answers selector three times. **The camera** — the chapter's paradigm selector —
> answers selector, selector, *setting*, because it 'knows nothing about anybody,' a phrase §4 repeats
> as 'knows nothing whatever about you.' **Simmel's prohibition** splits two-to-one, and the chapter
> says so out loud. **§7's landing page** answers the first and third like a setting... No threshold is
> ever stated. A reader who tallies gets a different answer from the chapter's in three of five cases."

Panel synthesis, panel 3 (2026-08-07), `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/SYNTHESIS.md`, §3 C1:

> "**The three questions still do not sort the chapter's own cases.** Reviewer 2, general reader,
> network-theory. The current hedge ('diagnostics and not a decision procedure') sits four lines after
> 'Three questions tell them apart.' Both cannot stand. The camera still scores selector / selector /
> setting; the landing page repeats the pattern... Author still chooses among A/B/C; silence is no
> longer an option."

Reviewer 2, panel 3, `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/08_reviewer2.md`, Part 1, finding 1:

> "Both cannot be true in the form printed. If the questions 'tell them apart,' they must classify the
> camera and the landing page. If they only diagnose cases already classified by the definitions, the
> definitions are doing the real work and the questions should be demoted openly."

### Who raised it, and when

Reviewer 2 (hostile referee), panel 2, 2026-08-02. Restated by the panel-2 synthesis the same day, naming
network-theory and general-reader as independent arrivals at the same crack (§8: "Three reviewers reached
it independently through three different lenses"). Reviewer 2 raised it again, in a slightly modified
form, at panel 3, 2026-08-07; the panel-3 synthesis names general reader and network-theory as concurring.

### What the project did about it

`chapter/PANEL3_STATUS.md`, §C, audited 2026-08-09 against the file text:

> "C1 the three questions do not sort the cases | **closed, Option A** | 'Three questions help locate
> the difference, but the definitions carry it.' Q3 now grades rather than classifies, and the camera
> is named an **attentional selector** against Uber's **transactional selector**"

`reviews/2026-08-02-full/01_argument.md`, §2:

> "C1 (what the three questions are for). Applied, Option A. §2 now reads 'Three questions help locate
> the difference, but the definitions carry it,' and the definitions are bolded and do the classifying.
> The camera is sorted by definition ('the mixed case that matters here… an attentional selector')..."

The wording contradiction Reviewer 2 flagged in panel 3 (the test both "tells them apart" and is "not a
decision procedure") was removed. But the fix does not make the three-question score sheet classify the
camera 3/3 or the landing page 3/3 — it stops asking the score sheet to classify them at all, and
classifies them by definition instead. Panel 2's own synthesis, when it first laid out Option A, priced
this cost: "Q3 stops being a criterion and becomes a severity scale, which weakens the Uber column where
the chapter most wants force" (`reviews/2026-08-02/SYNTHESIS.md`, §8). Whether redefining the test's job
counts as making it sort, or as conceding it never could, is not decided in any file read for this
report.

### What would count as answering it

In Reviewer 2's own words (panel 2, `08_reviewer2.md`, §1.2): "the honest repair is more interesting than
the current version. It is roughly this: question 2 does the sorting for transactional thirds, questions
1 and 3 do the sorting for attentional thirds, and they point opposite ways because attentional selectors
are settings *in reach* and selectors *in sequence*. The chapter half-knows this... and never lets the
knowledge back into the test."

## 2. Distinction or relabelling

### Quoted, verbatim

Reviewer 2, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/08_reviewer2.md`, §1.4 ("SERIOUS. §5's three powers dissolve the
distinction they were supposed to complete"):

> "Which brings the hostile question directly: **is this a distinction or a relabelling?** My answer,
> and I want the author to answer it in the text rather than around it: it is a real distinction that
> does not earn its place, because it is doing no explanatory work at either end. At the Austin end it
> renames Feld's focus. At the Uber end it is idle, because the powers that matter are not selection
> powers. It does its only genuine work in the middle, on the camera — and that is precisely the case
> where the test that defines it returns the wrong answer (1.2)."

The same objection recurs, restated, at panel 3. Reviewer 2, `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/08_reviewer2.md`,
Part 1, finding 4:

> "The problem remains that two of the three powers are not entailed by selection. Pricing a match and
> removing a party are powers of platform governance, market position, contract, and law. A selector may
> hold them. A setting may hold versions of them too. A market can charge stall rent and expel a vendor.
> A nightclub door can select and exclude. A bulletin board can charge and ban."

### Who raised it, and when

Reviewer 2, panel 2 (2026-08-02) and again, independently worded, panel 3 (2026-08-07).

### What the project did about it

Not adjudicated anywhere in the record examined. It is absent from `chapter/PANEL3_STATUS.md`'s theory
section (C1–C4 cover the three questions, the camera-coordinates-the-viewer point, the novelty universal
negative, and enclosure — not this). It is absent from `reviews/2026-08-02-full/SYNTHESIS.md`'s "Applied
this pass" list. The objection is raised in nearly identical substance in two separate rounds five days
apart with no synthesis file marking it accepted, declined, or closed in between.

### What would count as answering it

Reviewer 2's own demand, panel 3, `08_reviewer2.md`, Part 1, finding 4: "Demand: separate 'selection' from
'regime.' The selector chooses a path or pair. The regime can price, read, remove, and withhold reasons.
Uber is both. The camera is only the first. That is the sharper contrast."

## 3. The powers do not follow from selecting

### Quoted, verbatim

Reviewer 2, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/08_reviewer2.md`, §1.4:

> "Second, and this is the real damage: neither surviving power follows from selecting. Pricing a
> transaction and expelling a party are things a firm with market power does. A pure setting can do
> both. A bulletin board can charge for listings and ban a user; Craigslist deactivates accounts; a
> landlord prices a market square. Nothing in 'chooses which two parties come together' entails, implies
> or predicts either. So the payoff the whole chapter builds toward — what platforms can do to you that
> cameras cannot — is carried entirely by properties the setting/selector distinction does not track."

Reviewer 2's paste-ready concession sentence, same file, Part 3, item 3 (replacing "The questions tell you
what kind of third you face. What it can do to you is a separate matter."):

> "'The questions tell you what kind of third you face. What it can do to you turns out to be a separate
> matter, and separate in a way that costs me something: pricing a passage and expelling a party are
> powers a setting can hold too. A market square can charge rent and call the police.'
>
> This is the honest version of 1.4 and it is better for the chapter than leaving a referee to find it."

### Who raised it, and when

Reviewer 2, panel 2, 2026-08-02.

### What the project did about it

The proposed sentence — "A market square can charge rent and call the police" — does not appear in any
later review file or status audit; it was not adopted. `chapter/PANEL3_STATUS.md` does not track this as
an item at all. Panel 3's Reviewer 2 raises the same underlying problem again (Objection 2 above, finding
4) without reference to the earlier paste-ready line, which suggests the concession was never entered
into the chapter and the objection simply resurfaced in new wording five days later.

### What would count as answering it

Reviewer 2's own framing, `08_reviewer2.md` §1.4: "My answer, and I want the author to answer it in the
text rather than around it" — i.e., the concession has to be stated in the body, not conceded only in a
review response.

## 4. Which Austin

### Quoted, verbatim, in full

Reviewer 2, July 3 round, `reviews/08_reviewer2.md`, §3.2:

> "**3.2 The real-Austin / fictional-Austin equivocation.** Relatedly, §3 runs two Austins together. The
> census (465,622), the oil crash, rent falling $515→$350, agglomeration, the Live Music Capital
> timing — all describe the *real 1990 city* and its economic base. The coherence argument needs the
> *film's* Austin, the diegetic construct that hosts the strangers. §3 slides between them as if one.
> The economic material is vivid and I want to keep it, but it is establishing the *conditions of
> production*, not the *hosting third that binds the film* — and the chapter treats the two as a single
> 'Austin hosts.' **To satisfy me (3.1 and 3.2 together):** name the equivocation openly — the real city
> is the *material base*, the film's stitched city is the *hosting third* — and concede that because the
> co-presence is constructed, 'the engineering rides on the hosting' is a claim about the film's *fiction
> of a city*, not a documentary constraint. That concession is survivable and more honest; leaving it
> implicit lets a hostile reader say the whole host/engineer split is one man's editing bench."

### Who raised it, and when

Reviewer 2, July 3, 2026 (the first, ten-reviewer extensive panel), `reviews/08_reviewer2.md`, §3.2.

### What the project did about it

Never adjudicated. `reviews/SYNTHESIS.md` (the July 3 synthesis) contains no entry for this finding under
Accepted, Declined, or Author-only — a full read of that file, and a targeted search for "equivocation,"
"falsif," "material base," and "diegetic construct," returns no adjudication of this specific charge in
any synthesis file across all four rounds (2026-07-03, 2026-07-22, 2026-08-02 panel 2, panel 3, or the
2026-08-02-full pass). The July 3 synthesis's "equivocation" entries all concern a different, later-arising
problem (the camera-coordinates-the-viewer / feed-vs-engineer crack — see the note below), not this one.

One coincidence worth recording: by the 2026-08-02 round, §3 of the chapter opens its economic paragraph
with the phrase "The material base was slack" (`reviews/2026-08-02/02_urban-sociology.md`, quoting the
chapter; also `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/02_urban-sociology.md`) — the exact term Reviewer 2 proposed for
naming the real city. No synthesis file states that this vocabulary was adopted in answer to the
equivocation charge, and the charge's other half — naming the film's stitched city as a *constructed*
hosting third, distinct from the documentary city — is not traceable as addressed in any file read for
this report. This is the objection with the strongest textual case that it was never engaged, let alone
settled: it was raised once, in the first round, and does not recur — not as an open item, not as a
declined one — in any subsequent panel file.

### What would count as answering it

Reviewer 2's own words, `reviews/08_reviewer2.md` §3.2: "name the equivocation openly — the real city is
the *material base*, the film's stitched city is the *hosting third* — and concede that... 'the
engineering rides on the hosting' is a claim about the film's *fiction of a city*, not a documentary
constraint."

## 5. Is §6 self-defeating

### Quoted, verbatim

Reviewer 2, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/08_reviewer2.md`, §1.6 ("SERIOUS. §7 converts 'nobody ran the
study' into a positive finding"):

> "And the larger structural question a hostile referee will ask: **what is §6 for?** It establishes
> that reading cuts is learned, then establishes that the learning probably does not transfer to the
> thing the chapter cares about. As the chapter stands, §6's conclusion is that its own premise is
> inert. The abstract the editor accepted promises the opposite ('cinema has trained viewers to read
> engineering thirds for a century'). Either §6 argues for a narrower connection it can defend — that
> *Slacker* trained viewers to notice the choosing, because it removed the plot that usually absorbs the
> credit, which is a claim about this film and not about transfer — or it is a 660-word detour that ends
> in a shrug."

Critical theory, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/06_critical-theory.md`, "The one thing only the author can
supply":

> "Whether cinematic training produced *spectators* rather than *parties* — and whether the chapter is
> willing to say so. Everything §6 measures is a competence at receiving an arrangement: following a
> cut, attributing intent to a hidden arranger, not noticing the choosing. Nothing in it is a competence
> at answering back. If that is right, the century of teaching is not a resource for contesting
> selectors but part of what makes them uncontested, which turns the chapter's closing from hopeful into
> something sharper and truer to its own evidence."

### Who raised it, and when

Reviewer 2 and critical theory, independently, both panel 2, 2026-08-02.

### What the project did about it

Substantively adopted, on the record. Reviewer 2, panel 3, `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/08_reviewer2.md`,
finding 6, quotes the chapter as it then stood:

> "'What that century taught is a competence at receiving an arrangement... and none of it is a
> competence at answering back.' This is the right argument."

Reviewer 2 panel 3 asks for one further sentence ("Cinema supplies a habit of reception, not a politics of
reply") whose adoption is not confirmed in any later file read for this report. This is the one item on
this list where the record shows the chapter took the darker reading directly from critical theory's
formulation, in critical theory's own vocabulary ("receiving an arrangement," "answering back"). See also
the final section, below.

### What would count as answering it

Reviewer 2's own words, `08_reviewer2.md` §1.6: "Either §6 argues for a narrower connection it can
defend... or it is a 660-word detour that ends in a shrug."

## 6. "Host" is unfalsifiable

### Quoted, verbatim, in full

> "**2.1 Is 'host' a concept or a relabeling of agglomeration + Lofland? What would falsify 'Austin
> coordinates'?** This is the section's central exposure. The host is defined as a Feldian focus:
> 'brokers no pair and reads no one; it furnishes an environment and lets encounters fall out of it'
> (lines 69–70). But 'furnishes an environment and improving the odds without proposing the match'
> (lines 128–129) is true of *any* populated, legible place. There is no state of the world you would
> accept as 'Austin does *not* coordinate.' A concept that nothing falsifies is a lens, not a claim —
> and calling an affordance 'coordination' inflates the verb. You half-concede the exposure yourself:
> 'she names the code and the economists name the odds, and what I'm after is the seat' (lines 90–91).
> So the honest question is: what does *host* predict or explain that Lofland's locational order plus
> Duranton–Puga agglomeration do not already give you? If the answer is 'nothing new is explained; the
> value is placing the focus *in Simmel's triad* as a seat' — then the contribution is a *taxonomic*
> relocation, and it should be sold as exactly that, not as a mechanism. **To satisfy me:** state one
> falsifier for 'a host coordinates' (e.g., a place with density and co-presence where encounters
> demonstrably do *not* fall out — and say why *Slacker*'s Austin is on the other side of that line), and
> name one thing 'host' buys you over 'legible dense city.'"

## 8. Is "declines to explain" a fourth power?

*(Of the same kind as the items above: an architectural fork the panel synthesis itself named as a
decision, never made.)*

### Quoted, verbatim

Panel synthesis, panel 2, `reviews/2026-08-02/SYNTHESIS.md`, §8:

> "**Is 'declines to explain' a fourth power?** §7 needs it to be; §5's enumeration does not contain it.
> The two strongest pieces of evidence in the chapter's second half — the Amsterdam **disclosure**
> remedy and Amazon's 'may not be resubmitted or appealed' — are both about explanation rather than
> price, matching or removal. If the answer is yes, §5's enumeration changes and the chapter gains a
> power that has nothing to do with money, and which **a camera arguably shares**, since a cut never
> explains itself either. A4 has to be fixed regardless; this decides in which direction."

Same file, §7, task T10: "Decide whether 'declines to explain' is a fourth power. Feeds §8."

Reviewer 2, panel 3, `reviews/2026-08-02-panel3/08_reviewer2.md`, Part 3, item 7:

> "**7. §5, define the fourth power if §7 keeps it.** Add one sentence before the platform examples:
>
> > A fourth power matters because it is the one contested parties most often need first: the power to
> > withhold the reason for the decision.
>
> If this is added, the camera comparison must acknowledge the difference: a cut does not explain
> itself, but the person cut from the frame has no claim on the explanation."

### Who raised it, and when

Panel synthesis, panel 2 (2026-08-02), naming it as one of two subordinate decisions riding on the
architectural fork in §8. Reviewer 2, panel 3 (2026-08-07), restates the demand independently.

### What the project did about it

Only the sentence's grammar was patched, not the underlying decision. `reviews/2026-08-02-full/
01_argument.md`, finding AR1:

> "**AR1 (§7, antecedent). 'What it adds instead is a fourth thing the three questions do not reach.'**
> ...the sentence pivots to §2's three *questions*. The questions never tested for pricing, reading, or
> removal either — they classify kinds of third — so the sentence implies a contrast that does not
> exist... The fourth thing belongs to the powers. Surgical: 'a fourth power the three in §5 do not
> include' or 'a fourth thing the three powers do not include.'"

This fixes the sentence's antecedent (which noun the "fourth thing" refers back to) without deciding
whether a fourth power exists. No file read for this report states that §5's enumeration was changed to
three-or-four powers as a settled matter.

### What would count as answering it

The panel synthesis's own framing, `reviews/2026-08-02/SYNTHESIS.md` §8: "If the answer is yes, §5's
enumeration changes... this decides in which direction" — i.e., an explicit yes/no, stated in the text,
with §5's count revised to match.
