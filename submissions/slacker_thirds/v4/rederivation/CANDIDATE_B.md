# CANDIDATE_B — the joins you can hear

## 1. What the chapter is for

It gives a Linklater reader a countable account of how *Slacker* holds together, built from what characters say, and usable on Uber and a streaming submissions page without pretending the film predicted either.

## 2. The frame

Two questions, kept apart, and a list that cross-cuts both.

**Does the third name the pair?** A **room** changes who is within reach of whom and names no pair. A **dispatcher** decides, on an occasion, that *these two* transact. Simmel's own deferred case is the room: elements "unified by their common relation to a phenomenon which lies outside of them," set aside because the third is too distant for "properly sociological interactions" (Wolff 1950, 145). He says he will return to it; he does not abandon it.

**Can the joined parties check the join?** In *Slacker* the joining is mostly audible. Counting from the subtitle transcript, fifteen of the thirty-four joins between the film's thirty-five dialogue segments are made in speech a viewer can hear — an overheard question ("Did I overhear you say you got a friend that's missing?", seg. 4→5), a line repeated across the cut ("What a day, what a day," 5→6), a spoken invitation ("Why don't you come look?", 6→7), a transaction ("Five-dollar cover." / "I got a stamp," 29→30). The count is mine, subtitle-derived, owed a disc check. The other nineteen are made by something dialogue cannot account for.

**Holdings** — pricing, rating, removal, and declining to say why — follow from market position, contract and law, not from which kind of third this is. They cross-cut both questions and are claimed separately every time.

## 3. The nine constraints, one sentence each

1. **Sorting.** Question one sorts by whether a pair is named (Austin: no; a door policy: no; a camera: no, of the people filmed; Uber: yes; a submissions page: no); question two sorts by whether the join is checkable, which is where films differ from each other and where the club door differs from Amazon's "we cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected."
2. **Both ends.** The city end is not a relabel because the room claim is stated against a falsifier and loses on it in most large cities, and the platform end is not inert because I never derive pricing or removal from "names the pair" — I assert them separately, from Binns et al. and the Amsterdam ruling.
3. **Entailment.** In running text: naming the pair entails none of the holdings, and Austin is the counterexample — it names no pair yet prices a room, rates a dress, and removes a person from a sidewalk (*Papachristou*, pin cites owed a re-check against the U.S. Reports PDF).
4. **Which city.** Concede outright: Gaughen documents a three-mile jump the film presents as continuous, and Linklater's own account is that "if you didn't know Austin, the film makes sense… syntactically, it works" — so the room is a constructed one, and the census figures are conditions the production drew on, not evidence about the fiction's mechanism.
5. **Training.** I choose the critical option and say so: the competence *Slacker* exercises is following an arrangement, not contesting one, and Heiland's couriers — who theorised that speed drove their allocations, were wrong on an A/B test, and were never corrected — show what that competence looks like when it is all a worker has.
6. **Falsifier.** Density plus legibility does not produce mixing: exposure segregation is 67% higher in the ten largest US metros than in small ones (Nilforoshan et al.), street-level mixing is only 26% explained by visitor concentration against 44% by adjacent amenities and residential diversity (Fan et al.), and density's effect on friendship-oriented interaction runs negative (Brueckner and Largey) — what the room claim buys beyond agglomeration is the checkability criterion, not the fact of encounter.
7. **Prior art.** I engage Lazar directly, since he already sorts intermediaries by what they select (Signal between users, Tinder matching users) and already argues algorithmic power is structural *and* agential — my two questions refine his split rather than discover it — and I engage the German *konkreter/abstrakter Dritter* debate and the unified matching-plus-attention line (Meyer et al.; O'Reilly, Strauss and Mazzucato), all three marked second-hand.
8. **Withholding.** Yes, it is a distinct capability and it goes on the list, because it can be held without any of the others; a cut is the boundary case and sits outside it, since a cut explains nothing and binds nobody, whereas a deactivation explains nothing and you must live with it.
9. **Closure.** I have the dated fact: Smits found Amazon submission in 2017 "unfiltered, with no gatekeeping restrictions" (209), and the same channel now reads "Access to self-service submissions is by invitation only" (retrieved 1 Aug 2026) — scoped to that one channel, with Tzioumakis's refilled middle tier and Waldfogel's growth in titles (second-hand) conceded on the page.

## 4. The abstract's commitments

- **Simmel's third** → the room is Simmel's deferred group-forming third, quoted from Wolff and the 1908 German, with the deferral stated as deferral.
- **Austin / camera** → kept and graded: the camera is the film's only candidate dispatcher, and by the film's own dialogue it makes fewer than half the joins.
- **Uber** → kept and made formal: it names the pair, prices per trip, and bars the driver from asking the fare; the close reading arrives around the match — pricing, rating, removal — not inside it.
- **Training** → kept, narrowed, and reversed in tone (constraint 5).
- **Contestable, not extractive** → answered by checkability: the club door's rule was sayable and its stamp copyable; Art. 9(1)(b)(iii) and Art. 10(5) of Directive 2024/2831, and Seattle's deactivation-reporting schema, are what that looks like written down.

## 6. What the frame gives up

It gives up the moral punchline. Rooms and dispatchers do not sort into good and bad — the room prices, rates and removes, and a matchmaking friend lands in Uber's cell on question one. It gives up the picture: the argument runs on dialogue, so everything the film does that only the image does, including its wordless ending, lies outside the chapter's reach and is admitted as such. And it gives up prediction — checkability explains why a join can be argued with, never who wins.
