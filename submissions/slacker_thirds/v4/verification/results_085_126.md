**Locator-wrong: V-099 and V-100.** Both cite Meyer et al., *SMJ* 45(9) 2024, "abstract" as the locator. The abstract does distinguish a "matching mechanism" from an "attention mechanism," but the specific finding quoted and interpreted — that the two mechanisms "coexist on most platforms" — appears only in the article's body text, on p. 1733, not in the abstract. The chapter's own framing ("I cite their published abstract, no more") is therefore inaccurate for this specific claim.

**What Simmel actually defers (V-092–V-096).** In *Soziologie* (1908), chapter 2, section "Der Unparteiische und der Vermittler," Simmel states that isolated elements sharing a common relation to something outside themselves become unified by that relation, gives two examples — an alliance formed against a common enemy, and the invisible church, which unites all believers through their shared relation to one God — and then explicitly stops: "Diese gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung eines dritten Elementes ist indes in späterem Zusammenhang zu behandeln" ("This society-forming mediation of a third element is, however, to be treated in a later context"). His stated reason is that in this configuration the third element stands at too great a distance from the other two for genuine three-way sociological interaction to occur; what looks like a triad here is really a pair of two-way relations (the allied/faithful parties as a unit, and that unit's relation to the outside power). So: yes, he sets the question aside, in the middle of introducing it, immediately after stating it and giving both examples; and yes, he explicitly promises to return to it ("zu behandeln" = still to be treated), rather than dismissing it. This was confirmed against the full German original (socio.ch, matching Simmel's own 1908 pagination context), not against Wolff's 1950 English translation, which could not be accessed (Internet Archive copy is lending-restricted; no snippet or full-text access found anywhere else online, including zero indexed hits for the row's exact English wording). The two QUOTATION rows resting on Wolff's specific English phrasing (V-093, V-095) are therefore marked `unretrievable`, not `verbatim-confirmed` — the content matches, the exact translated wording is unverified.
