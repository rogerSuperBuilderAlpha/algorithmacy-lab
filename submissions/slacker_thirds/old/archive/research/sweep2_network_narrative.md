## 1. David Bordwell

**Blog, "Lessons from BABEL" (Nov 27, 2006)** — fetched: https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2006/11/27/lessons-from-babel/
- Formal definition, verbatim: "The central formal principle is that several protagonists are given more or less the same weight as they participate in intertwining plotlines. Usually these lines affect one another to some degree."
- Anti-zeitgeist argument, verbatim: "I found over a hundred such films, some going back quite far (e.g., Grand Hotel) but most made since the 1980s. Do they reflect some social Zeitgeist? Are we seeking connections with one another? Nope, I don't think so. The most proximate and pertinent causes lie elsewhere."
- Slacker not mentioned in this post (verified).

**Blog, "Telling the big story: Network narratives at Venice 2019" (Sep 11, 2019)** — fetched: https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2019/09/11/telling-the-big-story-network-narratives-at-venice-2019/
- The network film "demotes individual protagonists, in favor of revealing x degrees of separation among several individuals"; reveals "remote connections and unexpected convergences." No Slacker/Linklater mention (verified).

**"Mutual Friends and Chronologies of Chance" (Poetics of Cinema, 2008)** — full text inaccessible ([T&F chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9780203941898-10/mutual-friends-chronologies-chance-david-bordwell)). Coherence = conventions (chance meetings, accidents, deadlines, shared locales), degrees of connection, protagonist scaling. **UNVERIFIED whether Slacker appears in the chapter or its filmography — library check required.** Jeong (below), working from Bordwell's framework, treats Slacker as a case Bordwell's categories strain to hold.

## 2. María del Mar Azcona, The Multi-Protagonist Film (Wiley-Blackwell, 2010)

Reached: publisher preview ([e-bookshelf PDF](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-725394-3ab4179d53.pdf)); Oria review ([Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7643501.pdf)); [Wiley page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Multi+Protagonist+Film-p-9781444333930).
- Genre account: conventions of "characters..., storylines, narration, point of view and endings... chance, group dynamics and coincidence"; theme of "the contingent nature of human experience."
- Canon: Grand Hotel, Short Cuts, American Pie, Love Actually, Syriana, Gosford Park, Singles, Magnolia, Traffic, Crash, Babel.
- Slacker: no mention found in preview or reviews. UNVERIFIED as definitive negative (index uncheckable), but her canon is the converging ensemble film.

## 3. Maria Poulaki, "Network films and complex causality," Screen 55:3 (2014): 379ff.

Fetched via author copy: https://www.academia.edu/8491437/Network_Films_and_Complex_Causality ([OUP page](https://academic.oup.com/screen/article-abstract/55/3/379/1624541)).
- "Causality in network films is subordinated to the systemic organizing principle of emergence"; "feedback circuits that connect agencies across different diegetic levels."
- Slacker does not appear (verified against fetched text; also absent from her 2011 dissertation bibliography, [UvA PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1194700/99264_19.pdf)). Her apparatus requires feedback, which Slacker structurally forbids.

## 4. Amanda Ciafone, "The Magical Neoliberalism of Network Films," IJoC 8 (2014): 2680–2704

Fetched: https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/2452/1213 ([article page](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/2452)).
- Network films "rely on risk and randomness to map a fantastic network of interrelations, resulting in the magical meeting of multiple and divergent characters" (p. 2680); "contingency... maintains the existing capitalist system by attributing any deviations from the social equilibrium to chance and accident rather than immanent social antagonisms" (p. 2687).
- Three levels (verbatim): "network narrative forms (multilinear, interconnected plotlines), themes of network social relations..., and networked industrial production."
- "Magical neoliberalism" borrowed from Alberto Fuguet (2001).
- **Slacker appears exactly once**, in a list of network narratives "emergent in art house films" (p. 2683), between Lóng hǔ fēng yún (1987) and Reservoir Dogs (1992). No analysis.
- Difference/extension: her diagnosis runs through convergence (the magical meeting); Slacker has no collisions. The deferred third names the coordinating instance itself where Ciafone names only what coordination conceals; her essay ends at capitalism's representation, the deferred third points at its automation.

## 5. Everett and Ramírez Berg

**Wendy Everett, "Fractal films and the architecture of complexity," Studies in European Cinema 2:3 (2005): 159–171** — text via https://vdoc.pub/documents/fractal-films-and-the-architecture-of-complexity-22hpfv29fdq0; [Bath record](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/fractal-films-and-the-architecture-of-complexity/).
- Unity via butterfly-effect consequence ("no single action, however random or trivial, is immune from wider consequences"). Slacker not mentioned (verified).

**Charles Ramírez Berg, "A Taxonomy of Alternative Plots in Recent Films," Film Criticism 31:1–2 (2006)** — verified via [Berg's UT CV](https://rtf.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/2025-04/Charles%20Ramirez%20Berg%20Vita-4-13-25-3.pdf); [JSTOR issue](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i40166769).
- **His term: the "Daisy Chain Plot."** Per [Screenplayology digest](https://www.screenplayology.com/content-sections/screenplay-form-content/3-2/): "The story jumps from one protagonist to the next, never to revisit a previous protagonist. E.G. Slacker." First family: plots diverging by number of protagonists (alongside Ensemble/Polyphonic, Parallel, Branching).
- Daisy-chain section at pp. 24–26, verified indirectly via Jeong's citation "(Berg 2006, 24–26)". **UNVERIFIED: sentence-level definition and full example list** — library check. Caution: a popular writing-craft "daisy chain" (object passed hand to hand, Twenty Bucks/The Red Violin) is NOT Berg's protagonist-handoff definition.
- Berg is the only canonical taxonomist for whom Slacker is the defining example of its category. He classifies; he does not explain coherence.

## 6. Tröhler and the relay comparisons

**Margrit Tröhler, Offene Welten ohne Helden (Schüren, 2007)** — partial OA fetch (https://www.schueren-verlag.de/oa-publikationen/titel/offene-welten-ohne-helden-plurale-figurenkonstellationen-im-film.html); [mediarep review](https://mediarep.org/handle/doc/836).
- Typology: Gruppenfigur, Figurenensemble, Figurenmosaik. Plural films "funktionieren... nicht axiologisch, sondern topologisch"; cohesion is "assoziative Kohäsion" carried by space, bodies, montage. The literature's most Slacker-compatible answer (coherence without convergence). Slacker not found in accessible portions — UNVERIFIED as full negative.

**Relay/La Ronde/Phantom of Liberty comparisons in print (verified verbatim):**
- Michael Barker, "Slacker: Looking Back," Criterion — "a string of stories à la Arthur Schnitzler's La ronde and an ironic surprise-a-minute twist reminiscent of Luis Buñuel's The Phantom of Liberty." https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/1060-slacker-looking-back
- Scott Tobias, A.V. Club, Sep 3, 2009 — "the camera acting as an eavesdropper drifting to whatever conversation happens to command its attention." https://www.avclub.com/slacker-1798217569
- Budd Wilkins, Slant, Sep 22, 2013 — "round-robin experimental narrative structure"; Linklater's character "figuratively passing the baton to another." https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/slacker/
- Linklater's own "relay-race style" design language: [Wikipedia production account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slacker_(film)), sourced to Macor 2010.

**Seung-hoon Jeong, "Network Narratives in Global Cinema," Panoptikum 26 (2021)** — fetched: https://www.academia.edu/70241784/Network_Narratives_in_Global_Cinema_The_Shift_from_Community_to_Network_and_their_Narrative_Logics
- Verbatim: "Bunuel's Phantom of Liberty shifts among characters only through the contingent movement of the camera"; "we sometimes feel even the camera's hesitation about whom to follow"; "a remarkable precursor of the 'daisy chain plot' (Berg 2006, 24–26)." Calls Waking Life parallel to "his other network narrative film Slacker (1991)"; "networking as process rather than map or figure."
- The one scholar theorizing the Slacker structure head-on; frame is Lacanian/Deleuzian; names the camera-agent but psychologizes it. Must cite.

## The open seam

| Account | What holds the film together | Survives Slacker? |
|---|---|---|
| Bordwell | conventions + degrees of connection + intertwining | No — no lines re-cross |
| Azcona | genre conventions; chance binding an ensemble | No — no ensemble, no coincidence payoff |
| Poulaki | emergence via feedback circuits | No — strands never feed back |
| Everett | butterfly-effect consequence | No — consequences do not propagate |
| Ciafone | the "magical meeting" | No — no meetings, only adjacencies |
| Berg | daisy chain (taxonomy) | Names it; doesn't explain it |
| Tröhler | topological, associative cohesion | Closest — corpus apparently without Slacker |
| Jeong | the camera-agent | Sees it — then sublimates it into a truth-seeking soul |

The literature poses but does not answer: when every intra-diegetic binding agent is removed, what remaining instance makes the film one thing, and what kind of social relation is that instance? Bordwell's anti-zeitgeist reasoning clears the ground rather than blocking: if the coordinating office is older than the technology, the platform did not create the arrangement — it staffed a vacancy. Nobody asks what happens when the selector of who comes next, visible in Slacker as a camera one could in principle argue with, is reimplemented as a recommender with no address.
