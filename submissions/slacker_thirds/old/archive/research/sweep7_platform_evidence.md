## 3. EU law, exact provisions

P2B, Reg. (EU) 2019/1150, Art. 4 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150):
- 4(1): statement of reasons on a durable medium for restriction/suspension, prior to or at the time.
- 4(2): "at least 30 days prior to the termination taking effect, with a statement of reasons."
- 4(3): opportunity to clarify facts via the Art. 11 internal complaint-handling process.
- 4(5): statement "shall contain a reference to the specific facts or circumstances ... as well as a reference to the applicable grounds."

DSA, Reg. (EU) 2022/2065 (EUR-Lex truncated; verbatim via eu-digital-services-act.com mirror, matches OJ):
- Art. 17(1): "a clear and specific statement of reasons to any affected recipients ... for any of the following restrictions ... (d) suspension or termination of the recipient of the service's account."
- Art. 20(1): "access to an effective internal complaint-handling system."
- Art. 20(6): decisions "taken under the supervision of appropriately qualified staff, and not solely on the basis of automated means."

Platform Work Directive, Dir. (EU) 2024/2831 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024L2831). Art. 9 transparency; Art. 10 human oversight; Art. 11 human review.
- Art. 10(5), THE LAW RE-INSTALLING THE DOORMAN: "Any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate the contractual relationship or the account of a person performing platform work or any other decision of equivalent detriment shall be taken by a human being." [double-pass]
- Art. 11(1): right to "an oral or written explanation ... without undue delay" and "access to a contact person designated by the digital labour platform to discuss and to clarify the facts, circumstances and reasons having led to the decision."
- Art. 11(2): reply "within two weeks of receipt of the request."

## 7. Post-2020 corroboration

- Tarafdar, Page & Marabelli, "Algorithms as co-workers," Information Systems Journal 33(2) (2023): 232–267, DOI 10.1111/isj.12389: driver asking for specifics received only "Your account has been suspended for activity that violates our terms and conditions"; coded under an "Unexplained" theme (25% of interviews, 53% of forum posts); "even upon requesting clarification, the driver was left in the dark." [NB: Marabelli is a co-author — same Marabelli as Möhlmann/Salge/Marabelli 2023.]
- Lu et al., "Dark side of algorithmic management on platform worker behaviors," Human Resource Management 63(3) (2024): 477–498, DOI 10.1002/hrm.22211: "All 21 participants reported that their appeals are mainly automatically handled by algorithms."
- Fired by an App (ALC & RDU 2023, https://www.asianlawcaucus.org/news-resources/guides-reports/fired-by-an-app-report): deactivation "can happen suddenly without explanation or meaningful recourse"; "30% of deactivated drivers were not given any explanation"; 40% of deactivated Uber drivers not given enough information on how to appeal. NBC coverage carries Uber's rebuttal that reviews are "run by human representatives" (useful for fairness).

## Recommended substitution

Replace the Prime Video exhibit with UBER DRIVER DEACTIVATION anchored in the two Uber-authored pages. All four door-acts map onto a person in the platform's own words: price/commission (fare and service fee set unilaterally; Rosenblat & Stark p. 3759), record (the ratings ledger: "minimum average ratings"), refusal ("loss of access ... at our sole discretion"), withheld reason (the carve-out: reasons communicated "except when doing so poses a risk to other users"). The channel exists (Review Center) and is scripted/asynchronous (R&S p. 3771; Tarafdar et al. 2023) — which is exactly the recast claim: an address without an answerer of the doorman's kind. The EU/Seattle/Washington provisions then enter as the counter-movement: attempts to legislate the reason-giving human answerer back into the doorway (PWD Art. 10(5) is the single best sentence for this). Amazon BSA §3's withheld-reason clause is the best secondary exhibit.
