## Where a hosting/selecting distinction would land

The claim lands most naturally in the cinematic-urbanism conversation as a correction to its governing metaphor: that literature has exactly two registers for the city (setting/symptom; character/protagonist) and no register for the city as an interaction order — infrastructure keeping unacquainted people co-present and available without convening them. Ramirez is the ideal published foil: he sees the "seamless urban tissue" and the walk-away handoffs but collapses holding and selecting into the dérive. The network-narrative literature (Bordwell; Jeong, who names Slacker) receives the structural half: chance is under-described — the city hosts and the narration selects, and Slacker is the limit case because selection is the film's only connective device. The algorithmic-spectatorship literature (Anikina; Frey; Alexander; Beller behind them) receives the historical half: it lacks a pre-digital formal example of audiences pleasurably submitting to a third party's selection among co-present strangers; Slacker in 1991, endorsed by Linklater's channel-surfing gloss, is that example. The composite claim is unoccupied: Hansen gives training, Beller the economy, Anikina the automated cut, Conrath the cut as relation-maker — the hosting/selecting distinction that assembles them is not in print.
