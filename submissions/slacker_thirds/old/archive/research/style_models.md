# Style models for the human rewrite (2026-07-02)

Sampled real text from the bibliography's most-cited / best-styled authors and distilled the register for
the rewrite. Samples: Bordwell, *Poetics of Cinema* introduction (preview PDF, ~2,000 words read); Hansen,
"The Mass Production of the Senses" (full PDF, pp. 60–64 read); plus the accepted abstract itself, which is
the voice the paper must match.

## What the models actually do

**Hansen (humanities argument at full power):**
- Navigates in the first person: "I begin with an example," "What interests me in this account is less X
  than Y," "It is not my intention to contest…," "I am using 'classical cinema' here as a technical term."
  The "I" is not confession; it is the steering wheel of the argument.
- Opens with a concrete scene (the 1996 Pordenone festival program) before any thesis.
- Concedes in stride, mid-paragraph — "To be sure, Soviet montage aesthetics did not emerge fullblown…" —
  instead of quarantining concessions in numbered clarifications.
- Long, clause-rich sentences are fine when anchored by specifics (dates, names, places).
- Asks a question and answers it; invites the reader in: "(add your own examples)."

**Bordwell (readable film scholarship):**
- Contractions throughout ("there's," "doesn't," "it's harder to patch up an old piece").
- Wit in parentheticals: "(and he had a capacious imagination)."
- Rhetorical question, then his own answer: "Who offers the compelling evidence…? Not professors of
  literature but sociologists…"
- Plain connectives: "Still," "In any event," "Undoubtedly." No throat-clearing signposts.
- Opinionated and direct; willing to say a thing is wrong.

**The accepted abstract (the author's own voice — the target):**
- First person with contractions: "I want to use this framing," "I'll argue."
- Short declarative runs: "Cities do this. Matchmakers do this. Algorithms do this."
- Wry asides in parentheses: "(cult, if one must!)".

## The spec applied in the rewrite

1. First person singular throughout — the biggest single change; the current chapter has zero "I" while
   the accepted abstract runs on it.
2. Contractions where speech would use them.
3. Sections open on scenes, films, dates — not theses.
4. Concessions and qualifications delivered in stride ("To be sure…", "I should be careful here"),
   not as armored clarification blocks.
5. Questions asked and answered; occasional direct address.
6. Paragraph shapes vary; sections may just stop; no landing line per section (aphorism budget ~3 total).
7. All 50 citation keys, all verified quotes, References/Filmography byte-identical.
