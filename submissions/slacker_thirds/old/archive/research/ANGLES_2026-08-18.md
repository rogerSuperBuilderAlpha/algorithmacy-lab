# Angle memo — where the deferred third expands the Slacker literature

Deep-research synthesis, 2026-08-18. Four sweeps: Slacker/Linklater scholarship, network narrative theory, sociology of the third, urban sociology and spectator training. Full sweep files accompany this memo. Every citation below was verified by a research agent against a fetched page unless marked UNVERIFIED; the sweep files carry the URLs.

## The finding in one paragraph

The Slacker literature has produced nine distinct published answers to the question of what holds the film together, and every one of them locates the glue either inside the storyworld (relay, talk, ethos, time, drift), in the container (Austin, the 24-hour frame), or in the audience (Gen X spectatorship, indie viewing competence). One faction answers that incoherence is the point (Marlovits 2013; Bizarro 2025). No published account treats holding and selecting as two functions of one arrangement, and no published work connects Slacker to Simmel, platforms, algorithms, or coordination of any kind. The argument does not need to manufacture a gap. It needs to enter through a debate that already exists, and the strongest available entry is the anti-narrativity position, because the collection's own co-editor holds it.

## Ranked angles

### 1. Answer the house position: anti-narrativity (strongest)

Sara Bizarro, co-editor of the collection, published "Slackers Against Narrativity" (Blog of the APA, 23 October 2025), reading the film through Galen Strawson's Episodic/Diachronic distinction: no protagonist, no story, no thread, and non-narrativity as protection against prevailing narratives. Marlovits (2013) holds the academic version: viewer frustration at the missing plot is the point; the slacker refuses the action hero's job of rearticulating narrative coherence. This is the debate the chapter can enter and expand. Concede the whole anti-narrativity case at the level of plot, then pose the question it cannot answer: a film that refuses every narrative binding agent is still experienced as one thing rather than ninety fragments, and something is doing that work. The deferred third names the instance the anti-narrativity reading has no vocabulary for. This positions the chapter as completing the editors' own line rather than contradicting it, and the CFP's declared interests (narrative structures, urban space) receive it directly.

### 2. Supply the mechanism for Berg's daisy chain

Charles Ramírez Berg's taxonomy ("A Taxonomy of Alternative Plots in Recent Films," Film Criticism 31, 2006, pp. 24–26) names the Slacker structure the Daisy Chain Plot: the story jumps from one protagonist to the next, never to revisit, and Slacker is his defining example. A taxonomy names without explaining. The chapter supplies what the category lacks: an account of who or what performs the jump and on what authority. This is the cleanest film-studies hook because Berg is canonical, Slacker is his paradigm case, and nobody has theorized the category since. Every other network-narrative theory fails on Slacker by design: Bordwell needs intertwining, Poulaki needs feedback, Everett needs consequence propagation, Ciafone needs the magical meeting. Slacker is their degenerate case, and the sweep file documents each failure with quotations.

### 3. Split the dérive

The closest existing accounts all describe the camera's path and miss its office. Soldani (2017) gives the camera a Debordian dérive that reveals; Boom (2022) gives it swerves and lacunae; Stone gives it flânerie as philosophy in action; Price (2003) has it follow "whatever person happens to walk into" the conversation; Ramirez (Places Journal, 2011) sees the "seamless urban tissue" and the walk-away handoffs but collapses everything into drift. Drift is aimless and first-person. Slacker's selection is neither: the characters do not choose whom we see next, and the cut is not wandering, it is deciding. The angle: the dérive reading is half right, and splitting it yields the two jobs, the city holds (hosting) and the camera selects. Ramirez is the ideal published foil because he already observed both halves without distinguishing them.

### 4. Complete Erickson: Austin from myth to infrastructure

Erickson (ReFocus ch. 1, 2022, pp. 15–33) explains the film's cultural coherence, how it congealed into an emblem of a generation, a city, and a director, and treats Austin as a mythologized object of nostalgia. She does not explain its formal coherence. The chapter completes her: Austin is not backdrop or myth but working infrastructure, a third that holds strangers in mutual reach without introducing them. Macor (2010) supplies the production-history evidence that the film was materially made by the network of acquaintance it depicts. Bonus: the Goffman/Lofland apparatus (civil inattention, world of strangers) has verifiably never been imported into film analysis, so the equipment arrives new rather than as a repeated move.

### 5. Correct Jeong's camera-agent

Seung-hoon Jeong ("Network Narratives in Global Cinema," Panoptikum 26, 2021) is the one scholar who theorizes the selecting camera head-on: he reads Phantom of Liberty as precursor of Berg's daisy chain and writes of "the camera's hesitation about whom to follow," naming Slacker as a parallel case. But he psychologizes the camera-agent (hesitation, liberation, truth-seeking) where the chapter socially specifies it: the camera performs an office, selection among co-present strangers, that platforms later staff. Jeong must be cited; he currently owns the nearest formulation.

## The theory side is clear

The Simmel deferral has no commentator. No one in Simmel Studies, TCS, the Journal of Classical Sociology, or the German sociology-of-the-third revival remarks on the postponement itself, and the 2021 Routledge Handbook of Simmel Studies contains no triad chapter at all. A useful find: the deferral sentence exists in English in Simmel's own 1902 AJS serialization ("This society-constructing mediation of a third element is, however, to be treated in a later connection"), which predates Wolff and strengthens the philological footing. The German revival's thirds are all in-between figures (mediator, broker, translator, parasite) or constitutive-inside figures (Lindemann's virtual observer); the distant third is exampled everywhere and theorized nowhere.

Nearest occupants, in order: Neuberger (KZfSS 2022, platforms as Simmelian mediating thirds of the public sphere, in German, journalism-framed); Vallas and Schor (Annual Review of Sociology 2020, one sentence calling platforms tertius gaudens); Stark and Pais 2020 and Stark and Vanden Broeck 2024 (the exact geometry, an apex co-opting a relation it never enters, with zero Simmel and zero third-vocabulary); Kostrova (AVANT 2018, the only close reading of the external-unification passage, exported to phenomenology); Ajunwa (2020, tertius bifrons, a two-faced broker, still between). The defensible positioning sentence: others have put platforms in Simmel's triad as brokers between parties or as mediating thirds, and others have described the platform's outside-the-relation apex without Simmel; no one has recovered the third Simmel deferred, and no one has read it through a film.

Vallas and Schor 2020 is now a must-cite: they invoke Simmel's tertius gaudens for platforms by name, and the chapter cannot appear ignorant of the one prior Simmel-platform sentence in a major review journal.

## Corrections the sweeps force on current claims

The claim that Slacker is often called a city-symphony descendant did not survive verification; no printed instance was found. If the chapter wants the lineage it must argue it as its own genealogical claim, framed by MacDonald (Film Quarterly 1997–98) and Jacobs, Hielscher and Kinik (2019), or drop it. Civil inattention belongs to Goffman 1963, not Lofland 1973, as flagged previously. Bordwell's anti-zeitgeist argument works for the chapter, not against it: if the coordinating office is older than the technology, the platform did not create the arrangement, it staffed a vacancy. Oake's and Newman's Slacker-specific formal claims are unverified at page level; do not attribute specific readings without checking the texts.

## Recommendation

Lead with angle 1 (the anti-narrativity debate, which the volume's co-editor has a published stake in), enter film studies through angle 2 (Berg's named-but-unexplained category), and use angle 3 (the dérive split) as the local foil when introducing the two jobs. Angles 4 and 5 supply the citations that show command of the newest scholarship (Erickson 2022, Jeong 2021). This is targeted repositioning of the existing opening and section transitions, not a rewrite of v14's body: the Simmel, Royce, and Reich apparatus and the scene analyses stand; what changes is whom the chapter is talking to when it states the coherence question.
