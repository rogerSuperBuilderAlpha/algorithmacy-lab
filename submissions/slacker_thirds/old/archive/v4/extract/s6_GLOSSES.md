## Finding 2 — Benjamin's apparatus already tests and removes people

- Verdict: this "complicates" the chapter's claimed boundary between §4/§5 (platform powers) and §6
  (cinema); if a removal/testing power already exists in Benjamin's account of cinema's apparatus, the
  platform's three added powers cannot all be introduced as purely post-cinematic. (lines 60, 64–66)
- Interpretive synthesis: two lenses judged that Benjamin's examiner-selector (Finding 1) and Benjamin's
  excluding apparatus (Finding 2) are "one thought rather than two," i.e., the same passage carries both.
  (lines 85–86)
- Corrected/narrowed framing (offered by the dissenting third lens and adopted as the finding's final
  wording): removal is not wholly post-cinematic because Benjamin's apparatus already tests and excludes —
  but the tested party in Benjamin is the performer/actor, not a spectator, and pricing is absent from
  Benjamin's account entirely. This narrower claim is presented as "worth having" despite refuting the
  original framing. (lines 88–96)

## Finding 4 — Kuleshov effect

  134–135, 691–692)

  137–138, 694–696)
- Interpretive inference (not itself a result of the Cao et al. study): "That study measured no
  film-experience variable, so it says nothing about whether the reading is learned." The measurement
  absence is a fact (S6-033); the conclusion that the study therefore "says nothing about whether the
  reading is learned" is the dossier's inference from that absence. (lines 138–139, 181–182, 696–697)
- Editorial instruction: describe the Cao et al. sample as "a Chinese university sample," not
  "undergraduates" (the paper never uses that word), and do not write "only experienced participants."
  (lines 180–182)

## Finding 6 — first-time-viewer deficit and diegetic sound

- Verdict/title framing: "the first-time-viewer deficit is substantially an artifact of silent stimuli" —
  this causal framing was itself flagged by the dissenting third lens as too strong (see next bullet), so
  the finding's own headline is in tension with its recorded dissent. (line 219)
- Dissent, explicitly marked by the dossier as "the useful part": the causal claim that silence explains
  the earlier deficit is judged an overstatement, since there is no true silent condition in the 2018
  audio study (only levels of audio support), and the cross-study comparison to 2010/2015 mixes different
  stimuli, films, and partially overlapping participants. The phrase "the same research team demonstrated
  this" is judged "too strong." (lines 258–264)
- Editorial recommendation: the chapter should cite the team's fourth (audio) study alongside the three
  silent-stimulus studies "or be seen to have cherry-picked," and should narrow its claim to "bridging
  shots without an audio scaffold," noting continued first-time-viewer failure on unfamiliar environments
  even with sound. (lines 714–717)

## Finding 9 — the closing move: Möhlmann et al. vs. Heiland

- Verdict: the chapter's closing claim that platform workers are "building the second skill now, the hard
  way" (footnoted to Möhlmann et al.) is judged unsupported by that paper, which theorizes only that
  workers "keep up with" algorithmic instructions, not that they arrive at correct models. "Systematic
  keeping-up is not accuracy, and nothing in the sentence supports contestation." (lines 369–371, 410–411)
- Verdict/synthesis: "Pooled sensemaking converges unevenly" — an interpretive summary drawn by combining
  Heiland's two findings (wrong-but-disciplining belief about speed; correct-and-exploited belief about
  proximity). (line 374)
- Dissent noted: the third verification lens refuted an overreaching claim that "the riders' shared theory
  was flatly wrong" (since it was only wrong on the speed dimension, not proximity); this correction is
  described as carried into the finding's final wording. (lines 421–422)
- Recommendation for the chapter's argument: reframe the closing move so that "what workers build is
  partly folklore the platform lets stand," which is said to "sharpen §7's enclosure argument instead of
  softening it." (lines 743–744)
