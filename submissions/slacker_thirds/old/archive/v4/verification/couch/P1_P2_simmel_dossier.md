# P1/P2 dossier — Simmel's deferred third, German Tertiarität, and brokerage

Run 2026-08-12. This dossier supplements `research/deep/2026-08-01_s2.md`; it does not
re-adjudicate the primary Simmel passage. Searches targeted the exact German phrase, `Distanz`,
`Zweierkonfigurationen`, `Figur des Dritten`, `Theorien des Dritten`, `Tertiarität`, `abstrakte
Dritte`, `abwesende Dritte`, `Streit`, money, the stranger, and brokerage. Bibliographic metadata
for the journal items was checked against Crossref. Read depth and provenance class are stated for
every source.

This was a single research pass, not three independent adversarial lenses. Accordingly, findings
report `vote: not run` rather than manufacturing a vote. Convergence among independent retrieved
sources is stated in the confidence line.

## Executive result

The narrow non-return claim survives: no commentator located here says that Simmel later redeems
the exact promise of *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung eines dritten Elementes*, and no source
reads its *Distanz* as the third's ignorance of which particular pair will meet. The latter remains
the chapter's inference and should not be attributed to Simmel.

The broad novelty claim does not survive. A substantial German-language line explicitly theorizes
absent, abstract, generalized, institutional, medial, and platform thirds. Lindemann supplies the
cleanest conceptual rival: durable order requires an *abwesender Dritter*. Fuhse supplies the
cleanest boundary against network analysis: networks observe concrete thirds, while abstract thirds
remain in principle unobservable from that perspective. Neuberger directly calls journalism and
digital platforms mediating thirds and uses Simmel's phrase for mediation at the seams of functional
differentiation.

Brokerage remains different in kind. Its canonical definitions require an actor/node connected to
or influencing two alters. But the two literatures are not wholly separate: Fuhse explicitly stages
network research against *Theorien des Dritten*, even though he cites none of Burt, Obstfeld, Gould
and Fernandez, Kwon, or Halevy. The defensible citation-gap claim is therefore asymmetric and
document-level, not tradition-wide.

## Finding 1 — No secondary source found notices the unkept promise (moderate; vote: not run) [B]

No retrieved commentator independently records that Simmel never returns to the promised
*gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung*. The exact phrase does reappear in contemporary scholarship:
Neuberger uses it affirmatively for mediation across differentiated social subsystems, not as a
philological notice about an unfulfilled cross-reference. Thus the project may report a documented
search absence, but not “scholars agree that Simmel never returned.”

Neuberger's own thesis is that journalism and platforms should be analyzed as mediating thirds in a
digitally transformed public sphere. He does not reconstruct the local logic of Simmel's deferral.

**Evidence.** Neuberger, p. 166: “Die ‘gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung’ (Simmel 1992,
S. 125) muss daher vor allem an den Naht- und Bruchstellen der gesellschaftlichen Differenzierung
ansetzen.” This is a reuse of the phrase as a live concept, not a claim about its later treatment.

**Relation:** COMPLICATES B-S4 and SUPPORTS only the narrower absence statement. It gives no warrant
for converting a full-text project search into a scholarly consensus.

**Source:** Christoph Neuberger, “Journalismus und Plattformen als vermittelnde Dritte in der
digitalen Öffentlichkeit,” *Kölner Zeitschrift für Soziologie und Sozialpsychologie* 74,
suppl. 1 (2022): 159–181, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11577-022-00832-9. Full open text,
publisher/Crossref metadata verified. **Provenance A.**

## Finding 2 — Commentary reads a structural triad/dyad boundary, not ignorance of particulars (high; vote: not run) [A]

The retrieved Simmel commentary consistently treats the decisive change as objectification,
superpersonality, replaceability, publicity, or the presence/absence of a third. None treats
*Distanz* as the third's not knowing which two particulars will meet. Kostrova states the strongest
version: Simmel keeps dyad and triad separate and introduces the third “from the outside.” Bedorf
likewise says that the triad is governed by structural dominance while the dyad depends on the
particular individuals.

**Evidence.**

- Kostrova, pp. 190–91: “In Simmel, the dyad and the triad are strictly separated. Unlike the
  former, the latter is treated as a superpersonal structure that does not depend on the elements it
  is composed of”; and p. 193: “the transition from a dyad to a triad is carried out, so to speak,
  by simple summation: the third element is added, introduced from the outside.”
- Bedorf, pp. 16–17: “Die Triade basiert auf der Strukturdominanz, während die Dyade auf einem
  Individualitätspriori beruht”; an element can be replaced without destroying the triad, unlike
  the dyad.

**Relation:** REFUTES the attribution in A-S1 and COMPLICATES A-S3/B-S2. “No-known-pair” may remain
an explicit authorial operationalization, but it is not a retrieved reading of Simmel's *Distanz*.

**Sources:** Elizaveta Kostrova, “The Dyad and the Third Party: The Traces of Simmel’s Distinction in
Phenomenology and Family Studies,” *Avant* 9, no. 2 (2018): 187–202,
https://doi.org/10.26913/avant.2018.02.12. Full open text and Crossref verified.
**Provenance A.** Thomas Bedorf, “Stabilisierung und/oder Irritation: Voraussetzungen und
Konsequenzen einer triadischen Sozialphilosophie,” in Thomas Bedorf, Joachim Fischer, and Gesa
Lindemann, eds., *Theorien des Dritten: Innovationen in Soziologie und Sozialphilosophie*
(Munich: Fink, 2010), 13–32. Full author-deposited chapter. **Provenance A.**

## Finding 3 — The standing rivals are absent, abstract, generalized, and institutional thirds (high; vote: not run) [A]

The German third-theory line does occupy much of the chapter's proposed cell, though not under the
name “deferred third.”

Lindemann's thesis is that theories repeatedly discover and then forget the third because they
identify the third actor with the order whose emergence it enabled. She distinguishes Simmel's
typology of concrete third positions from a systematic “emergence function,” then argues that
durable normative order requires an absent third. Fuhse's thesis is that network research can model
concrete thirds as configurations of dyads but cannot observe abstract/generalized thirds without
another perspective. Bedorf adds an “imagined third” and institutions/normative orders as stabilized
third positions.

**Evidence.**

- Lindemann, p. 494: “Der Dritte, der die Bedingung für die Etablierung einer dauerhaften Ordnung
  darstelle, müsse nämlich als abwesender Dritter gedacht werden.”
- Lindemann, p. 495: “Tertius verschwindet gleichsam in der Ordnung, als deren Entstehungsbedingung
  er gegolten hat.”
- Fuhse, p. 1: “Weitgehend ausgeklammert bleiben dabei ‘generalisierte Andere’ bzw. ‘abstrakte
  Dritte’ ... Sie bleiben jedoch aus der Perspektive prinzipiell unbeobachtet.”
- Fuhse, p. 3: “In der Netzwerkforschung geht es dagegen immer um konkrete Dritte.”
- Bedorf, pp. 20–21: the “eingebildete Dritte” acts as an ideal legal actor and stable normative
  reference; institutions and orders become visible through the third.

**Relation:** SUPPORTS the need for a non-broker category but REFUTES the strong novelty wording in
A-S5 and A-S7. The chapter can coin “deferred third,” but must distinguish it from these established
figures and explain what its no-known-pair criterion adds.

**Sources:** Gesa Lindemann, “Die Emergenzfunktion des Dritten – ihre Bedeutung für die Analyse der
Ordnung einer funktional differenzierten Gesellschaft,” *Zeitschrift für Soziologie* 39, no. 6
(2010): 493–511, https://doi.org/10.1515/zfsoz-2010-0604. Full text and Crossref verified.
**Provenance A.** Jan Fuhse, “Dyaden und Dritte in Netzwerken,” discussion paper for “Die Triade als
Basiskonstellation des Sozialen,” University of Oldenburg, June 2013. Full author-hosted text; not
peer-reviewed. **Provenance A for wording, C for field-wide generalization.** Bedorf 2010, above.
**Provenance A.**

## Finding 4 — Money and the stranger are systematic rivals; *Streit* is family, not demonstrated fulfilment (high on the first clause, moderate on non-fulfilment; vote: not run) [A/B]

Lindemann explicitly places money and the stranger within a typology of temporally absent thirds.
Money presupposes a presently absent but future-present accepting third; the stranger was absent,
is now present, and remains. These are genuine alternatives to “deferred third,” not merely loose
analogies.

The conflict reception strongly supports the chapter's “same family” clause. Coser explicitly
distills his propositions from Simmel and treats conflict as capable of maintaining group boundaries.
Bedorf says Simmel's third typology makes the associative function of conflict analyzable. But no
retrieved source says that *Der Streit* is Simmel's promised later treatment of the p. 103 case.

**Evidence.**

- Lindemann, p. 501: “Im ersten Fall ist der Dritte gegenwärtig abwesend, aber zukünftig anwesend.
  Diese Struktur sei die Bedingung für die Funktionsweise des Geldes”; the next sentences contrast
  the stranger, “gestern noch nicht da ... gegenwärtig anwesend und bleibt.”
- Coser, preface pp. 7–8: “This book deals mainly with a number of basic propositions which have
  been distilled from theories of social conflict, in particular from the theories of Georg
  Simmel”; conflict may “contribute to the maintenance of group boundaries.”
- Bedorf, p. 26: “Die Typologie des Dritten dient dazu, die vergesellschaftende Funktion des Streits
  analysierbar zu machen.”

**Relation:** BOTH. SUPPORTS B-S3 and the “same family” half of A-S4; COMPLICATES A-S6 by supplying
money and the stranger as rival distant/temporal thirds; leaves the fulfilment/non-fulfilment
verdict as the chapter's own.

**Sources:** Lindemann 2010 and Bedorf 2010, above. **Provenance A.** Lewis A. Coser,
*The Functions of Social Conflict* (New York: Free Press, 1956; Routledge reprint 1998), esp. preface
7–8 and ch. 2. Publisher preview/full searchable text retrieved; original pagination visible.
**Provenance A.**

## Finding 5 — Waldenfels/Pyyhtinen make the third co-present, not distant (moderate-high; vote: not run) [A/C]

The phenomenological line is not a claimant to Simmel's deferred outside potency. It is a rival that
undoes the strict separation of two and three. Kostrova's full-text reconstruction of Waldenfels says
the other already meets me as a third and that the third's presence opens law, order, rights, and
justice. Pyyhtinen's article abstract similarly claims that a third is always included in the dyad,
even as an excluded third.

**Evidence.**

- Kostrova, p. 195: “the other always meets me already as the third, as well as I always turn out to
  be the third”; the third “corresponds to the social dimension, to the community, and to their
  exposure to general rules.”
- Kostrova, p. 196: Waldenfels “insists that the dyadic and the triadic dimensions do not exist
  separately ... their crossing is ... continual.”
- Pyyhtinen abstract: “a third element is always included also in the dyad, be it only as an
  excluded third.”

**Relation:** COMPLICATES A-S5/A-S6. Waldenfels and Pyyhtinen are standing names for thirdness, but
not support for a third at such a distance that only dyadic configurations remain.

**Sources:** Kostrova 2018, above. **Provenance A.** Olli Pyyhtinen, “Being-with,” *Theory, Culture
& Society* 26, no. 5 (2009): 108–128, https://doi.org/10.1177/0263276409106353.
Abstract and Crossref metadata only in this pass. **Provenance C; do not page-pin or quote beyond
the abstract.**

## Finding 6 — An established platform/media-third line directly occupies the impersonal cell (high; vote: not run) [A]

Neuberger explicitly extends Simmel to institutions, journalism, techniques, algorithms, and digital
platforms. His thesis is not merely that platforms are objects within human triads. Platforms enable
participation and interaction, select and aggregate algorithmically, and serve as mediating thirds
in public communication. This is the strongest falsification of “contemporary sociology followed
only the thirds Simmel kept.”

**Evidence.**

- p. 161: “Neben personalen Dritten sind Institutionen ‘auf Dauer gestellte und zum abstrakten
  Prinzip erhobene Figuren des Dritten’.”
- p. 159: “Besonders Plattformen ermöglichen anderen Akteuren Partizipation und Interaktion” while
  their operators or third parties steer communication algorithmically.
- p. 170: platforms mediate “direkt durch eigene algorithmische Selektion und Aggregation” or
  indirectly by giving third parties a production/distribution environment.
- p. 174: professional mediation is “durch partizipative und algorithmische Formen ergänzt.”

**Relation:** REFUTES the broad A-S7 claim and COMPLICATES A-S2/A-S6. SUPPORTS couch for A-S8/A-S9
only at the level of an impersonal platform/media third with enabling and selecting functions; it
does not make the chapter's film/city/camera join.

**Source:** Neuberger 2022, above. Full OA publisher text. **Provenance A.**

## Finding 7 — Brokerage is actor/node-and-alters scoped; it does not match *Distanz* (high; vote: not run) [A]

The brokerage literature's own definitions support the exclusion, but the reason must be precise.
Brokerage is a position or behavior of an actor relative to alters. It does not require the alters to
be initially disconnected in every process-oriented definition, but it always targets identifiable
others' relationships. Obstfeld's *tertius iungens* explicitly connects people in one's network.
Stovel and Shaw allow “individuals (or groups),” yet say brokerage is built from informal personal
relations. None of these sources admits a non-node third that does not know which pair it joins.

**Evidence.**

- Kwon et al., accepted manuscript p. 6: “Brokerage occurs when one actor (the broker) is connected
  to two other actors (alters) who are not themselves connected.”
- Kwon et al., manuscript pp. 4–5: the review excludes networks outside work/professional contexts;
  criterion 5 concerns non-human *networks* and simulations, not non-human brokers.
- Halevy et al., p. 216: “brokerage” denotes “a particular structural position in a social network,”
  and “brokering” the behaviors “individuals pursue when acting as brokers.”
- Halevy et al., p. 217: brokering targets alters' pre-existing relationship or lack thereof and
  includes intermediation and modification.
- Obstfeld's abstract defines *tertius iungens* as connecting people in one's social network “by
  either introducing disconnected individuals or facilitating new coordination between connected
  individuals.”
- Stovel and Shaw's abstract: brokerage lets disconnected or isolated “individuals (or groups)”
  interact, but “is built from informal, personal relationships.”

**Relation:** SUPPORTS A-S7 and A-S11 in their narrow comparative form. COMPLICATES any wording that
says all brokerage requires an initially unconnected pair, because process brokering also modifies
existing ties. It does not prove a psychological “knowledge” condition; it proves actor/alter scope.

**Sources:** Seok-Woo Kwon et al., “Network Brokerage: An Integrative Review and Future Research
Agenda,” *Journal of Management* 46, no. 6 (2020): 1092–1120,
https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206320914694. Full accepted manuscript. **Provenance A.**
Nir Halevy, Eliran Halali, and Julian J. Zlatev, “Brokerage and Brokering: An Integrative Review and
Organizing Framework for Third Party Influence,” *Academy of Management Annals* 13, no. 1 (2019):
215–239, https://doi.org/10.5465/annals.2017.0024. Full author manuscript. **Provenance A.**
David Obstfeld, “Social Networks, the Tertius Iungens Orientation, and Involvement in Innovation,”
*Administrative Science Quarterly* 50, no. 1 (2005): 100–130,
https://doi.org/10.2189/asqu.2005.50.1.100. Abstract only in this pass; exact definition converges
with Halevy's full-text quotation. **Provenance B/C.** Katherine Stovel and Lynette Shaw,
“Brokerage,” *Annual Review of Sociology* 38 (2012): 139–158,
https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-081309-150054. Publisher abstract/metadata only.
**Provenance C.**

## Finding 8 — The citation gap is real but asymmetric and must be stated at document level (high; vote: not run) [A]

Three canonical brokerage documents contain no named German third-theory author or vocabulary from
the tested list. Bedorf and Lindemann contain no canonical brokerage name or *broker* terminology.
Fuhse is the bridge: he explicitly positions relational/network sociology against the 2010 German
collection and calls *brokerage* a triadic network mechanism, but his references omit Burt,
Obstfeld, Gould and Fernandez, Kwon, and Halevy.

The traditions therefore have touched conceptually, and Fuhse explicitly stages the comparison.
What remains true is that the canonical brokerage reviews do not cite the German line, while the
retrieved German core texts do not engage canonical brokerage authors.

**Evidence.**

- Fuhse, p. 1: the paper reconstructs network research's position in the “gegenwärtigen
  sozialtheoretischen Diskussion zum ‘Dritten’ (Bedorf et al. 2010).”
- Fuhse, p. 2 lists “brokerage” among mechanisms visible only from at least triadic networks.
- Reproducible full-text/reference-list regex results: Kwon 0, Halevy 0, Gould–Fernandez 0 for the
  named German-author/concept list; Lindemann 0 and Bedorf 0 for the named brokerage list; Fuhse 2
  lexical *brokerage* hits and 0 named canonical brokerage authors.

**Relation:** BOTH for A-S7. SUPPORTS a precise citation-gap sentence; REFUTES “the traditions do
not cite each other” as an unqualified reciprocal/tradition-wide claim.

## Finding 9 — The ordinary-role reception is secure, but it also breaks “known pair” (high; vote: not run) [A]

Contemporary secondary sources consistently reproduce Simmel's mediator, *tertius gaudens*, and
*divide et impera* typology. Yet they do not license the statement that every ordinary third acts
on a known pair. Bedorf emphasizes functions that change over a situation and discusses the
abstention of a niche party that has not precommitted to either coalition. Lindemann and Neuberger
place generalized, anonymous, and institutional thirds beside personal ones.

**Evidence.**

- Neuberger, p. 161: Simmel distinguishes “vermittelnde (Unparteiische, Vermittler), trennende
  (etwa Herrschende, die dem Motto ‘divide et impera’ folgen) und begehrte (lachende) Dritte.”
- Bedorf, p. 17: the third may be mediator, beneficiary, ruler, coalition target, or intervener, and
  the functions can replace one another during a situation.
- Bedorf, p. 25: the niche party can support either large party “ohne sich vorab auf eine bestimmte
  Koalitionsoption festlegen zu müssen.”

**Relation:** SUPPORTS B-S1; REFUTES the universal rationale in A-S2.
