2. **Web-search summaries are leads, never sources.** Open the page or PDF. Abstract-only = class C;
   do not assert page-level claims from abstracts.
3. **Film locations:** prefer Austin Film Commission, Criterion materials, *Austin Chronicle*, Macor,
   AFI Catalog, named newspaper reviews. Fan blogs are C unless corroborated.

5. **Reuse prior dossiers** (`P1_P2` … `P5_P6`) as bibliographic leads for Reich/platform joins;

## P7 — Critical consensus that the camera joins (and the “hundred people” figure)

*Covers S-A1, S-A2. Blocking: §1 opener.*

> The chapter opens by saying published accounts of why *Slacker* holds together usually stop at one
> claim—the camera joins scenes the characters do not—and that the film follows roughly a hundred
> people across one day in Austin.
>
> **Find citable statements of both claims.**
>
> 1. **Camera-as-joiner critical consensus.** Locate at least two independent published accounts
> (newspaper review, magazine, scholarly essay, Criterion liner, or book chapter) that explain the
> film’s coherence by the camera / editing / “baton pass” linking strangers who do not form a plot.
> Quote verbatim with publication, date, and page or stable URL+access date. Prefer Canby, Ebert,
> Turan, Kael if she wrote, Criterion essayists, Macor, or peer-reviewed film scholarship.
> 2. **“Hundred people” (or nearest figure).** Find the earliest or most authoritative count
> (~100 cast, speaking parts, credited roles). Credits, press kit, Criterion, or a review that states
> a number. If the number is folklore, say so and recommend a hedge (“dozens,” “a large cast”).
>
> **What counts as a hit:** named publication + verbatim sentence the chapter can footnote.
> **What would falsify the “usually stop” gloss:** a dominant published account that *does* give a
> structural/coordinating theory of the camera beyond style—report it; the chapter must then argue past it.
