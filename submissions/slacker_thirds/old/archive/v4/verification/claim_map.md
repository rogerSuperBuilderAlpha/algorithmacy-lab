# Claim map — *Reach and Handoff: Slacker's Thirds*, chapter_v4.md

Every checkable assertion in the chapter body, Notes, and Bibliography, one row per claim. Sentences that argue, define, or transition without asserting an external fact are not rowed — see the method note at the end of this preamble.

**Method for this pass:**
- A row is created wherever the chapter asserts something that could in principle be checked against a source: a quotation, a number, a claim about what a cited work argues, a claim about the film's content or construction, or a claim that something exists (or doesn't) in a record.
- Purely argumentative or definitional sentences — the chapter's own thesis-building prose ("Reach is my name for...", "Four cells, not two kinds of third.") — are excluded as not independently checkable against an external source. This exclusion is a judgment call, not a verification; if a reviewer disagrees that a given sentence is "mere argument," it should be added.
- Where one sentence bundles a factual claim with argument, only the factual clause is rowed, and the row reproduces that clause verbatim (not the full sentence) when the clause is separable; the full sentence is rowed verbatim when the claim isn't cleanly separable.
- A "Note" value of a footnote number means the marker `[^n]` appears at or immediately after that clause in the chapter text. Many sentences in a footnoted paragraph carry no marker of their own — those are marked NONE even when a nearby footnote plausibly covers the general topic. This is deliberate: the point of the map is to show exactly which clauses ride on a marker and which don't.
- `[DISC:...]` markers are treated as the rule specifies: the sentence gets a row, Retrievable = `author`.
- Retrievability of film dialogue and film-content claims is marked `author` throughout, because Note 1 states plainly that "No quotation has yet been confirmed against the Criterion disc" — the transcript itself is the project's working instrument, not a confirmed source.
- Retrievability of census statistics (segment/transition counts, kappa, coder agreement, "the six deliberate handoffs," etc.) is marked `internal`, per the task's own definition, since these rest on the project's own coding tables (`v4/factbase/handoff_census.md` and the coder files), not on anything a web agent or a library visit could confirm.

## 1. Unsupported assertions

Every sentence rowed above with Note = NONE is, by definition, an assertion carried on no footnote. That is roughly half the table — most of the FILM and QUOTATION rows, because the chapter attaches per-sentence footnotes only to secondary-literature claims and a handful of disc-flagged film moments, not to ordinary film narration or dialogue quotation. Note 1's blanket disclaimer ("Dialogue throughout this chapter is quoted from the film's subtitle track...") covers *all* of these as a class, but it is a one-time methodological note, not a per-sentence citation, and it explicitly admits no quotation has been checked against the disc. So the "no note" rows fall into two very different buckets:

**(a) Covered by the blanket transcript disclaimer (Note 1) — legitimate, low-risk.** Nearly all FILM and QUOTATION rows describing what happens or is said on screen (V-001–V-017, V-023–V-026, V-038–V-041, V-050–V-053, V-056–V-064, V-066–V-068, V-072, V-077–V-091, V-103–V-113, V-123–V-125, V-133–V-135, V-144–V-151, V-159–V-164, V-169–V-170, V-173–V-188). These are common-knowledge-in-context claims about *this specific film*, and the disclaimer already flags the whole class as unconfirmed-against-disc. I would not treat these as suspicious individually — they're the expected shape of a film-close-reading chapter — but collectively they are exactly what a disc check is for.

**(b) Not obviously covered by anything — genuinely bare.** These are the ones worth flagging as unsupported in a stronger sense, because they assert something beyond "the film shows/says X":
