## The argument

1. *Slacker* has about a hundred characters, no protagonist, and almost no character who meets another
   twice. Audiences followed it. Something other than story held it together.
2. Two things coordinate the people in this film, and they work differently. Austin is a **setting**:
   it puts strangers within reach of each other and supplies the conventions that let one address
   another, while arranging no particular meeting. The camera is a **selector**: it chooses which
   person the viewer sees next.
3. A platform is a selector too, and it holds three powers the camera has no use for. It reads the
   parties, it prices each match, and it can remove a party.
4. A 1991 audience could follow a film built entirely out of such choices because reading a cut is a
   learned skill and a century of filmmaking taught it. That much is measured. Whether the same skill
   helps anyone read an algorithm is not measured, and nobody has run the study.
5. The chain that carried *Slacker* to viewers was made of people who could be addressed and whose
   refusals were attributable. What replaced it narrows twice — once at licensing, once at
   prominence — and explains neither.

## Terminology

| v3 term | Definition, stated on first use |
|---|---|
| **setting** | A setting puts people within reach of one another and supplies the conventions that let one approach another. It arranges no particular meeting. |
| **selector** | A selector chooses: which particular parties come together, or which particular one an audience meets next. |
| two kinds of selector | Some selectors choose what an audience sees — the camera, the streaming landing page. Some choose who does business with whom — the taxi dispatcher, Uber. |
| third party | Someone who coordinates two others who never deal with each other directly. Simmel's term; gloss it, don't lean on it. |
| structural hole | Two people who would each gain from what the other knows, and who are not connected. |
| brokerage | Standing between two parties and doing something to their relationship. |

Retired: hosting third, engineering third, the two faces, the seat, thick market, apparatus.

## The three questions, and what they do not do

1. Does it act on everyone at once, or on two particular people?
2. Could the encounter have happened without it?
3. Does it know who these particular people are, and change what it does accordingly?

**A repair the chapter must make, from §2's research.** Simmel's own *divide et impera* case is a
superior who forbids by general prohibition associations that "are merely possible and very often do
not exist even as wishes of the elements separated by the prohibition." That third acts on everyone
and knows nothing about particulars, so on questions 1 and 3 it scores as a setting — while
destroying reachability rather than supplying it. The three questions sort by scope, counterfactual
and knowledge. They do not sort by whether a third adds or subtracts possible encounters, and they
are not tests of harm. §2 says that in one sentence rather than hoping no reader tries the case.

That limit is load-bearing rather than embarrassing: it is why §5 needs the three powers. The
questions tell you what kind of third you are looking at. The powers tell you what it can do to you.

# §1 — The coherence puzzle (780 / 600)

**Claim.** *Slacker* refuses every device that normally makes a film followable, the people who saw
it followed it, and the readings on record put the linking work on the camera without asking what
kind of coordinator a camera is.

**Moves.**

1. *(180)* The taxi monologue, and the film abandoning Linklater a block later. The credit.
2. *(160)* The refusal, with the production and reception record: 16mm, summer 1989, about $23,000,
   eleven weeks at the Dobie, Orion Classics in July 1991, $1.2 million.
3. *(220)* The premise, narrowed and enumerated. No critic in the retrievable reviews reports losing
   track of who was on screen or why the film moved on; the complaints are boredom, tedium, monotony
   and pointlessness. The Sundance thirds go here, in the body, and say what they show — people left
   because the film seemed to mean nothing, which is a different failure from losing the thread.
4. *(120)* Linklater designed the handoffs to carry the viewer and said so in 1991. This caps the
   novelty rather than raising it.
5. *(100)* Three published readings already put the linking on the camera — Macor, Boom, Ramirez.
   The chapter's question is what kind of coordinator does the linking, and whether it knows who
   these two particular people are.

**Structural change from v2.** The Macor/Rosenbaum hinge collapses and must be rebuilt. "The least
auteur film ever made" is Lee Daniel's quip about credit at p. 112, not Macor's thesis; her section
heading is "Chaos by Design," and she has Linklater replacing *La Ronde*'s narrator with "the fluid
camera and a kind of 'baton-passing'." She is not the collaborative-sourcing pole against Rosenbaum's
tight authorship — she is already on the camera-as-linker side, which is where move 5 puts her.

# §2 — Two ways to coordinate strangers (980 / 760)

**Claim.** A third party can coordinate two others who never deal with each other directly, and it can
do so in two ways that three questions tell apart.

**Moves.**

1. *(200)* Simmel: adding a third position changes the form. His three roles. And on the first page
   of "The Triad" he names a third of another kind — a power outside the parties, to which all stand
   in the same relation — calls that mediation group-forming, and defers it.
2. *(200)* Brokerage after Simmel: Burt, Obstfeld, and the shared assumption that a broker works on a
   pair. Structural hole defined in plain words.
3. *(160)* The other way. Feld named the mechanism in 1981. Define the setting.
4. *(240)* The three questions, stated plainly and run visibly on Austin, the camera, and Uber. Then
   the scope sentence: they sort by reach, counterfactual and knowledge, not by whether the third adds
   or subtracts encounters. Simmel's own *divide et impera* is the case that shows why.
5. *(180)* What is new, stated at the right grain, with the warrant.

**Novelty, at the grain the research supports.** Nobody has set a setting beside a broker and held
both to one test. Not: nobody has distinguished the two. Simmel drew a version of the line himself;
the German *Theorien des Dritten* debate names it as *konkreter* versus *abstrakter Dritter*; Feld
named the mechanism; Small folds settings into brokerage. The defensible warrant is documentary and
falsifiable by anyone with three PDFs — see Halevy, Kalmijn and Finkel below.
