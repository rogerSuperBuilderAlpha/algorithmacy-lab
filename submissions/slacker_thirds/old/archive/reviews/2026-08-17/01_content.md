# Content review — *Slacker* chapter v9

**Lens:** Film-and-source accuracy (content seat only)  
**Manuscript:** `'You seen Gary around?': The Deferred Third of *Slacker*` (`chapter_v9.md`)  
**Venue:** Bloomsbury collection *Slacker: Answering the True Call — Essays on Linklater's Cult Classic*, eds. Sara Bizarro and Melissa Remark  
**Standpoint:** Hostile fact-check of scenes, credits, quoted lines, numbers, dates, and load-bearing uses of Simmel, Royce, Reich, Feld, Stark & Pais, Soldani, Bordwell, Viljoen, the Amazon licensing page, and the Rideshare Drivers United report. Project files were used as leads only. Every proposed correction is marked **verified** (source retrieved this session) or left in VERIFY.

## Step 0 — Register and bar

The chapter is a first-person film-studies collection essay. I am not judging architecture, cohesion, or style except where a content error is the cause. I am not converting the prose to Annals register. Quoted dialogue, first person, contractions, and the signature Gary line are off-limits as prose defects.

What I judge hard: whether a reader who knows *Slacker*, or who opens the cited page, will catch the chapter misdescribing a scene, a credit, a quotation, a number, or a source. Disc-dependent claims stay in VERIFY. A banned fact that has walked back in is a finding even if the surrounding sentence is otherwise careful.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 — The chapter fuses two hitchhikers into one person

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'` (labour paragraph); the error is repeated in `Reach, Selection` and the Conclusion.

**Claim.** One man, fresh from his stepfather's funeral, stands outside Les Amis and tells a camera crew he will work only when he hears the true call.

**Quote.**

> Outside Les Amis a hitchhiker fresh from his stepfather's funeral tells a camera crew that he will take a job only "when I hear the true call," and then refuses to say what the true call is.

The same fused figure returns as "the hitchhiker" who will not name the true call (Reach, Selection; Conclusion).

**Why it damages this lens.** These are two different people in two adjacent segments. **Verified** against the subtitle-derived transcript (`research/slacker_transcript.md`, lines 848–893) and against published credits and location pages this session:

- Segment 19: a hitchhiker accepts a lift "back into town" from a stepfather's funeral ("Should've let him rot"; "He was a serious fuck-up"). He asks for a cigarette and is let out. No camera crew. No job talk. No "true call."
- Segment 20: a different man, credited in AFI as **Hitchhiker awaiting "true call"** (Charles Gunning), is stopped by a crew "for a project we're working on," refuses voting and wage-work, and says he will get a job "when I hear the true call." Asked what that is, he answers "You know, the true call. I know when I hear it," then ends the interview.

**Verified** (Austin Film Commission, *Slacker* locations page, retrieved this session): Les Amis is the true-call interview — "the angry hitchhiker (Gunning) provides some choice quotes for a camera crew." The Daily Texan (9 July 2026) places Gunning "in front of Les Amis Café." Neither source attaches the funeral to Gunning.

The fusion is not colour. The paragraph uses this one body to carry (a) contribution without a contract, (b) a withheld reason parallel to Steve's door, and (c) one of the three questions the film "leaves hanging." Two of those jobs belong to Gunning; the funeral belongs to someone else. A *Slacker* editor will notice.

A smaller overclaim sits on the same line. He does not "refuse to say" what the true call is. He gives a tautology. The refusal is to specify, not to speak.

**Fix.** Split the two men. Keep Les Amis, the crew, and the true call on Gunning. If the funeral hitchhiker is wanted, give him his own sentence (the lift, the withheld eulogy). Do not let "the hitchhiker" in later sections mean both.

### Finding 2 — The opening "echo" and "no character bridges" cannot stand as settled

**Section:** Philosophical Origins (closing of the Simmel stretch); `'You seen Gary around?'` (opening two paragraphs).

**Claim.** The film opens twice on a street; no character bridges taxi and hit-and-run; they share a city and a cut and nothing else; the taxi man's "always someone getting run over" is an echo "no one on screen is in a position to notice."

**Quotes.**

> The film opens twice on a street within four minutes, and both times the deferred third is already there.

> Then the film is on a different street, where a woman lies struck

> No character bridges those beats, and nothing in the story connects them. Austin contains them both, and the cut decides which of them we watch. In the taxi the man had said there is always someone getting run over, and the next thing we see is a son who has run over his mother, an echo that no one on screen is in a position to notice.

> The cut from the taxi to the hit-and-run inherits that omission: two parties and a city, never three terms in one field.

**Why it damages this lens.** The deferred-third reading of the opening is the chapter's first film proof. It needs the two beats to be unbridged. Published location writing says otherwise.

**Verified** (Austin Film Commission, same page): "A taxi drops Richard Linklater … off on 24th Street between San Antonio & Nueces Streets near the Castilian dorm, **where he witnesses a hit and run.**" IMDb locations independently list "Nueces Street and 24th Street … (Woman gets hit by car)." If that is right, the second opening is not "a different street," a character *does* bridge the beats, and the passenger who just theorised "always someone getting run over" is on the sidewalk when a woman is struck. The echo would then be available to someone on screen — the man who said it.

The dialogue half of the echo is real. **Verified** (transcript line 21): "It's like there's always someone getting run over or something really weird." The chapter's compression ("there is always someone getting run over") is fair. The hit-and-run son is also in the transcript ("Looks like some guy, uh, ran over his mother"). What is not settled is the *unnoticed* and *unbridged* half.

"Within four minutes" is disc-dependent. "Ninety minutes" later in the Simmel paragraph is a rounding of a 97-minute film (**verified**, AFI Catalog 59046). Neither is the main problem. The main problem is that the chapter asserts, as fact, a discontinuity the city's own film-commission page denies.

This stays partly in VERIFY: nobody on this project has watched the Criterion disc, and Pierson's "there's not even a cut between scenes" (same Savlov interview the chapter cites) is itself too strong — the film is full of cuts. The Film Commission page is still a published source an editor can open. The chapter cannot treat "no character bridges those beats" as known.

**Fix.** Until the disc is watched, write only what dialogue and published location notes will carry: the taxi monologue, the accident, the card, the son's house, the rhyme in the lines. Do not assert "different street," "no character bridges," or "no one on screen is in a position to notice." If the disc shows Linklater walking into the accident, the deferred-third example has to move, or the claim has to become: the *city* holds both, and the *passenger* is the one person who could notice the rhyme and does not comment.

### Finding 3 — The Amazon sentence reprints a banned cut

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'` (platform paragraph); note 18.

**Claim.** Removed titles "may not be resubmitted or appealed"; selection is sole discretion; Amazon "cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected."

**Quote.**

> Amazon's Prime Video licensing documentation states that removed titles "may not be resubmitted or appealed," that selection sits at the company's sole discretion, and that it "cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected."

**Why it damages this lens.** **Verified** this session against the live page ("Prime Video Direct content providers: Licensing consideration and title review," last updated 5 August 2025):

> We understand you invest a lot in your content and will provide you with visibility in your Slate portal if your title isn't selected for licensing or removed from our catalog. **Unless otherwise indicated,** removed titles (or titles not selected for licensing) may not be resubmitted or appealed. All content is selected at the sole discretion of Prime Video, and we cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected for licensing.

The two cuts that earlier panels banned have walked back in: the clause is not unconditional, and the sentence immediately before it promises portal visibility. BANNED_FACTS Part 1 #28 exists for this exact flattening. The rest of the page-use (sole discretion; withheld reason) is good. The quotation as printed is not.

**Fix.** Restore "Unless otherwise indicated." If a short body sentence is needed, say Amazon gives the verdict, withholds the reason, and — unless it indicates otherwise — bars resubmission and appeal.

### Finding 4 — Soldani is made to say more, and from the wrong pages, than she writes

**Section:** Opening; note 2.

**Claim.** Soldani never mentions Instagram. She makes *Slacker* a twenty-four-hour urban narration whose camera drifts from character to character and from place to place "without splitting those two jobs." Note 2 pins this to 72–75.

**Quote.**

> The film scholar Maria Teresa Soldani never mentions Instagram. She makes the resemblance a matter of form. *Slacker*, she writes, is a twenty-four-hour urban narration whose camera drifts from character to character and from place to place without splitting those two jobs.

**Why it damages this lens.** **Verified** against the *Imaginations* 7.2 (2017) PDF this session.

- Instagram: zero hits in the article. The negative is good.
- "24-hour narration of city life": pp. 73–74. Inside the 72–75 pin.
- "The camera drifts along the street space, from character to character and from place to place": **p. 82**, after the 72–75 range. The note's pin does not cover the drift sentence the body welds to her.
- "without splitting those two jobs": **not in Soldani**. She describes a unified dérive (Debord, long takes, limited cuts). She does not theorise reach versus selection, and she does not say the camera refuses to split those jobs. That clause is the chapter's, and it is the opposite of the later thesis that the film "keeps a strict division of labour between reach and selection."

"The inside of that experience is doomscrolling" is also the chapter's, not hers. Fine as a turn if it is not hung on her name.

**Fix.** Give her the 24-hour urban narration at 73–74. If the drift sentence is kept, cite 82 and quote her, not the split-jobs gloss. Own "without splitting" as this chapter's later problem, or drop it. The Instagram negative can stay.

### Finding 6 — LSEG "oblige written reasons within fourteen days" overstates the rule

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'`; note 20.

**Claim.** The LSEG FX SEF rulebook obliges written reasons within fourteen days and provides for reconsideration.

**Why it damages this lens.** **Verified** this session against *LSEG FX SEF Rulebook* v4.1, effective 11 August 2026 (the edition the note cites). Rule 302(g)–(h):

- If the SEF declines, conditions, or terminates, it notifies the Affected Person.
- That person **may, within seven calendar days, request in writing** the reasons.
- **Then** the SEF has fourteen days to send written reasons.
- Reconsideration by the Board is available after that.

The chapter's "oblige written reasons within fourteen days" reads as automatic. The obligation is triggered by a written request. The contrast with Amazon (no reasons, no appeal) is still real. The rule as stated is not.

The UK MTF "sole discretion" / prior-notice endeavour (Rules 3.1.5 / 5.1.4) was not opened this session. VERIFY.

**Fix.** "An excluded party may demand written reasons; the SEF then has fourteen days to send them, and the Board will reconsider."

### Finding 7 — "Steve is refused without a reason" is not what the door says

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'` (Blue Bayou).

**Quotes.**

> Steve is refused without a reason, and the refusal has a shape that Reich had described twenty-six years earlier.

> It sets the price, keeps the record, decides the case in its own forum and owes nobody an explanation.

**Why it damages this lens.** **Verified** (transcript 1354–1360): the doorman charges three dollars, hears "S-T-E-V-E" / "Steve plus three or four," and answers "Sorry, Steve. Don't see you" and "You're not on the list." That is a reason. What is withheld is why a list Steve believes he owns does not have him, and why the door will not take his word. The four-power taxonomy can still fit if the withheld item is the *further* reason. "Refused without a reason" as a report of the scene is false.

The rest of the door is good. Credit **S-T-E-V-E with a van** (AFI; chapter's "With A Van" is only title-case). Location **Blue Bayou on South Congress**: **verified** (Film Commission; Chronicle map). Cover three dollars: **verified**. Block quote: the three lines are in the transcript, in that order, with other talk between them; "Figures" is not clearly the doorman. Note 6 already warns that the subtitle track compresses. The stamp scene is in the right place; "wrist to wrist" underspecifies the lick-transfer the transcript and the Film Commission lore both have — keep the quoted objection, name the lick.

**Fix.** "Steve is refused, and the only reason the door will give is that it does not see him on its list."

### Finding 8 — Body and note disagree on whether Reich covers the four powers

**Section:** Blue Bayou / Reich paragraph; notes 16–17.

**Quotes.**

> The Blue Bayou's door does that work in miniature. It sets the price, keeps the record, decides the case in its own forum and owes nobody an explanation. Reich's diagnosis covers all four.

Note 16 (correctly):

> The four powers named in the text — price, evaluation, removal, and the withholding of reasons — are this chapter's taxonomy and not Reich's; his own scheme at 740 enumerates four *judicial theories* licensing grantor power … and includes neither pricing nor evaluation.

**Why it damages this lens.** The note is the accurate one. **Verified** in substance this session: the short-cuts sentence the chapter quotes is Reich's ("procedures which, in varying degrees, represent short-cuts that tend to augment the power of the grantor at the expense of the recipient"); the closing demand "The denial of any form of privilege or benefit on the basis of undisclosed reasons should no longer be tolerated" is Reich's. "Reich's diagnosis covers all four" cannot stand next to a note that says pricing and evaluation are not his. An editor who checks 740 / 751 will believe the note and distrust the body.

Van Alstyne on the closing pages as "tentative suggestion" and "mild conjecture" was **not retrieved** this session (the Cornell PDF 403'd; a W&M URL returned the wrong article). VERIFY. Do not treat the reception claim as cleared.

**Fix.** Keep the note. In the body: "The door does, in miniature, the dependence Reich described. The four powers are this chapter's list, not his."
