# Style / Register — *'You seen Gary around?': The Deferred Third of* Slacker (v9)

**Venue:** Bloomsbury collection *Slacker: Answering the True Call — Essays on Linklater's Cult Classic*, eds. Sara Bizarro and Melissa Remark. Chicago endnotes, US English (Merriam-Webster 11th), 3,000–5,000 words, final draft 2026-11-01.

**Standpoint:** Style / register seat only. I am judging whether this first-person film chapter is better at being itself — Hansen/Bordwell manner, scenes before theses, contractions allowed, pointed concessions allowed — and whether the prose, slop, jargon, and word-budget choices would survive a film-collection editor who can also hear a sociology seminar when one starts. I am not rebuilding the argument, not census-ing citations, and not reading other seats.

**Manuscript counted this session:** body ≈ 4,034 words without headings (4,074 with), against a firm 3,000–5,000 range. About 1,000 words of headroom remain.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

**Register identified.** This is a first-person film-studies collection chapter. "I" steers; the film section opens on streets and credited names; the deferred third and "You seen Gary around?" are signature lines the piece is built around. First person, contractions, scene-first openings, and pointed concessions are off-limits as defects. Quoted dialogue and quoted scholarship are off-limits as defects. I will not convert this into Annals diction, and I will not apply the dissertation's no-first-person rule.

**Bar I will hold it to.** House *invariants* only: named agents and active voice; claim-first; every abstraction touches a case; rhythm variation; no self-narrating rigor; no antithesis machine run as a tic; no inflation vocabulary; no agentless passive that hides who did the thing. Plus this seat's own job: slop as uniformity of optimization; unexplained jargon for a cross-disciplinary / film-collection reader; metaphor doing argumentative work with no literal nearby; the four prior-panel bans if they survive (throat-clearing; meta-commentary / roadmaps; unexplained jargon; metaphor-as-argument); collection fit / film-forwardness; word budget. Proposed rewrites stay in the author's diction, shorter or the same length, more concrete. If a rewrite comes out more nominalised than the original, it is deleted.

The piece is already trying to be the right thing. Most of the damage is local: a cheap contemporary open, a Simmel stretch that talks German to a film reader, a landing-line habit in the philosophy pages, and a couple of performed-care ticks. The film pages are the chapter.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### 1. The Instagram / doomscrolling open cheapens a form claim the chapter already has

**Section:** untitled opening, first paragraph.

**Claim.** The first paragraph spends its contemporary charge on a named app and a 2020s vice-word, then has to be walked back two paragraphs later by Bordwell. That is metaphor doing the "why this chapter now" work, and it is the first thing a film-collection editor reads.

**Quote.**

> The film now feels like a feed. In 2011 Richard Linklater told Marc Savlov he saw the film as "either channel surfing or … going in and out of different movies," a "primitive version of what now would be exponentially more complex."[^1] The film scholar Maria Teresa Soldani never mentions Instagram. She makes the resemblance a matter of form. *Slacker*, she writes, is a twenty-four-hour urban narration whose camera drifts from character to character and from place to place without splitting those two jobs.[^2] A face talks for a stretch and then it is gone. Another face arrives. The inside of that experience is doomscrolling.

**Why it damages this lens.** "Never mentions Instagram" is a gotcha dressed as scholarship. Soldani's form claim does not need an app she declined to name; the sentence makes the chapter sound as if it had been waiting to say Instagram and then congratulated itself for not quite saying it. "Doomscrolling" then does the argumentative work the form claim had already done. It is not a scene. It is not Linklater's word. It is a platform-discourse landing that dates the piece to the feed-essay moment and forces the Bordwell paragraph ("warned against treating multi-protagonist films as symptoms of the internet") to clean up after the open. The four-ban that survives here is metaphor-as-argument: film = feed, inside = doomscrolling, with the literal statement (Soldani: drift without splitting the two jobs) sandwiched between the two metaphors rather than leading them.

The face-arrives rhythm in the middle of the paragraph is the chapter being itself. The frame around it is the chapter auditioning for a different magazine.

**What would fix it.** Open on Linklater's own channel-surfing line, or on the face that talks and is gone. Keep Soldani's form. Land on the viewer's lack of choice, which is the argument, not on an app or a vice-word. Instagram can stay in the note if the Anikina/Kendall contrast still earns its keep. See Part 3, rewrite 1.

This finding is not "don't be contemporary." Platforms belong in the chapter. They belong after a form has been seen, which is what paragraphs 2–4 already do.

### 2. The Simmel pages talk past a film-collection reader, then land the same epigram until the film is allowed to start

**Section:** "Philosophical Origins in Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe" (≈1,116 words of a 4,074-word body).

**Claim.** This section is where the chapter stops being a *Slacker* essay and becomes a close reading of *Soziologie* with a film used as illustration. The style damage is not that Simmel is present — the deferred third has to be earned — but that German terms, a reception parade, and a closer-formula do the earning in a register the collection did not buy.

**Quotes, in the order a reader hits them.**

Unexplained or half-explained German in the body:

> The third here stands at such a *Distanz* from the other two that no properly sociological interaction encompasses all three alike. What remains are *Zweierkonfigurationen* – pairs –

> Simmel indexed the passage himself, under *Distanz des vereinigenden Momentes*

> Christoph Neuberger has taken *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung* to journalism and to platforms.[^10]

*Tertius gaudens* is glossed ("the laughing third"); *divide et impera* is glossed ("divide and rule"); *Zweierkonfigurationen* is glossed after the German ("pairs"). *Distanz* is just the German for the English word in the same sentence. *Distanz des vereinigenden Momentes* is not glossed. *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung* was translated two paragraphs earlier as "The group-forming, mediating function of a third element," then returns as a bare German string a film reader has to re-look-up. *Tertius iungens* is never glossed at all:

> Ronald Burt's structural-hole broker and David Obstfeld's *tertius iungens* each know which two parties they stand between.

A film-collection reader who has Simmel in one undergraduate week will get "laughing third." She will not get "the third who joins" unless someone says so. Note 12 already says the coinage is Obstfeld's, not Simmel's. The body still withholds the English.

Then the reception parade, with a doubled name:

> Gesa Lindemann treats the absent third as the precondition of any durable dyadic order, not an addition to it. For Jan Fuhse the problem is observational: network research can see a concrete third and cannot see an abstract one. Christoph Neuberger has taken *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung* to journalism and to platforms.[^10] Albrecht Albrecht Koschorke, with Michel Serres behind him, reverses the chapter's order.

Four scholars and a typo in five sentences, then the one sentence the section was for: "Simmel omitted the ground. The taxi is already moving before anyone in *Slacker* has met anyone." That last pair is the chapter. Everything above it is a literature review the notes already hold.

**Why it damages this lens.** Unexplained jargon is one of the four bans, and it survives here. So does a milder form of throat-clearing: the section heading itself ("Philosophical Origins in Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe") tells a Bloomsbury film editor that the seminar is about to begin. Named agents are a house *virtue* — Lindemann and Koschorke doing things is better than "the literature has established" — but a film chapter can name two people and put the rest in the note. The parade is uniformity of optimization: every adjacent theory gets a sentence and a closer. The film waits.

The same section also runs the landing-line habit that the mechanical locator was pointing at (see Finding 3). By the time Gary's café is allowed to fail Royce's test, the reader has been told five times that two parties share a city and no relation.

**What would fix it.** Keep the Wolff quotation, the postponement, the naming of the deferred third, and the taxi/hit-and-run weld. Move *Distanz*, *Zweierkonfigurationen*, and *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung* to the notes, where they already live. Gloss *tertius iungens* in passing ("the third who joins"). Cut Fuhse from the body; keep Lindemann or Koschorke, not both plus Neuberger. Fix "Albrecht Albrecht." See Part 3, rewrites 2 and 3.

Do not "fix" this by adding more Simmel. That is how the section got here.

### 3. Short sentences in the philosophy pages are a metronome of closers; in the film pages they are earned

**Sections:** opening through Simmel/Royce; contrast with "'You seen Gary around?'" and the hitchhiker beat.

**Claim.** The mechanical report's 24.6% of sentences under ten words (venue mean 8.2%) is a locator, not a fail. I split the body at ~20% under ten, depending on dialogue. The question is whether those shorts are variation after a dense build, or a drumbeat of thesis-epigrams. They are both, and the split is sectional.

**Earned shorts — do not touch.**

> Kids hawk Cokes. A man sells T-shirts.

> Gary never appears.

> Somebody goes for help.

> The doorman charges him three dollars.

> Linklater refuses to resolve.

These are scene. They puncture a long sentence the way the house rule wants. "Gary never appears" is a signature-adjacent landing and it has the film in it.

**Metronome shorts — the philosophy pages, and a few joins.**

> The inside of that experience is doomscrolling.

> Platforms have a history.

> The chapter reads the sidewalk as an organisation.

> What never returns is the third *as a third*.

> The film has the postponed third.

> Sharing a city is not sharing a past.

> The film keeps a third you can argue with. The platform removes it.

The last couplet is close to a signature, and it occurs once, at the close. I would not rewrite it. I flag the *habit* that produces it: almost every paragraph in the Simmel stretch, and several in the opening, exits on a polished restatement of "two parties, a city, no relation / no yes / no face / postponed third." That is the landing-line drumbeat. It is also, from the other side of the wall, the flow seat's semantic restatement. From this seat it is uniformity of optimization: every paragraph auditions to be quoted.

"What never returns is the third *as a third*" is a good sentence. It is a bad thing to have a cousin of it at the end of the next three paragraphs ("The taxi is already moving…"; "The film has the postponed third"; "Neither the taxi nor the woman struck in the street is a node in anybody's network"; "the room he is asking fails every part of it"). One of those landings should stay. The others should stop on the case.

**What would fix it.** Keep the shorts that name a body, a door, a Coke, Gary. In the philosophy pages, end two of the five deferred-third restatements on the next thing the reader must see (Wolff's postponement; the conflict-chapter return; Royce's canoe) rather than on the thesis again. Do not lengthen sentences for the mean. The venue mean is not a target.

### 4. Two stretches perform care instead of enacting it

**Sections:** end of the opening; Reich paragraph in the film section.

**Claim.** Self-narrating rigor is rare in this draft — that is a genuine clean — but two moments still tell the reader how carefully the author is leaning.

**Quotes.**

> I come to this film from organisation theory. The chapter reads the sidewalk as an organisation.

> Reich's diagnosis covers all four. I lean on the diagnosis rather than on his remedy deliberately.

**Why it damages this lens.** The first is throat-clearing, the first of the four bans, and it survives at the exact spot an opening should stop: after "Platforms inherited a third that was already on the sidewalk." The identity card does not make the next section more readable. It tells a film editor that a management paper has sat down. "The chapter reads" also makes the chapter the agent of the reading, which is the one agent-substitution this register does not need; "I" is already licensed.

The second is performed rigor in the house sense: the adverb *deliberately* narrates the virtue of the lean. The lean is already visible in "Reich's diagnosis covers all four" and in the note that the four powers are this chapter's taxonomy. What follows — Van Alstyne, "tentative suggestion and mild conjecture," "the demand was a hope appended to that description" — is a law-review reception paragraph inserted between Steve's refusal and the stamp. The film had a face. The prose changed rooms.

Pointed concessions are off-limits as defects. "I lean on the diagnosis rather than on his remedy" is a concession and can stay. *Deliberately* cannot. The Van Alstyne characterisation can live in note 17, where it already lives.

**What would fix it.** Cut the organisation-theory two-liner, or turn it into a sentence that does work ("I am reading the sidewalk as an organization: a third that holds people in reach and does not introduce them"). Cut *deliberately*. Move the reception sentence to the note. No rewrite in Part 3 for these; they are cuts, not recasts.

### 5. The film section breaks its own register when the platforms arrive as documentation

**Section:** "'You seen Gary around?' – The Deferred Third of *Slacker*," from "The same powers, once they drop the face, are written down" through Viljoen / Rideshare Drivers United.

**Claim.** The film section is the chapter's stylistic home until Amazon, the 810 drivers, LSEG, Reuters Dealing 2000-2, and Viljoen arrive in two paragraphs and the diction goes law-review.

**Quote.**

> The same powers, once they drop the face, are written down. Amazon's Prime Video licensing documentation states that removed titles "may not be resubmitted or appealed," that selection sits at the company's sole discretion, and that it "cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected."[^18] Rideshare Drivers United and the Asian Law Caucus, surveying 810 California drivers, found two-thirds reporting deactivation and thirty per cent of those given no reason at all.[^19]

Then, still in the same run: the four powers "now attach by rule," the Video Backpacker, "Reuters Dealing 2000-2 and EBS," "Calling it one would spend his term twice and in opposite senses," Viljoen's "horizontal, population-based data relation."

**Why it damages this lens.** This is not an argument finding about whether the empirics are true. It is a register break. The preceding pages had Steve spelling S-T-E-V-E, a licked stamp, "You seen Gary around?", kids hawking Cokes. The reader was in Austin. Then the chapter starts quoting licensing documentation. The Video Backpacker is the one film object in the pile, and he is used as a gloss on a queue. A collection reader who came for *Slacker* will feel the room change, and she will not be wrong.

"Spend his term twice" is a metaphor with a literal next to it ("the app's assignment is not membership"), so it is not the metaphor-as-argument ban. It is slightly cute. It can stay.

**What would fix it.** Keep one written-down third (Amazon *or* the drivers, not both plus LSEG plus EBS in the body). Let the notes carry the rest — they already do. Give the Video Backpacker his own two sentences as a *film* beat, not as a simile for deactivation. If the 1,000-word headroom is spent anywhere in this section, spend it on Paul, the PixelVision, or the hitchhiker, which currently share a list, not on another rulebook.

**Opening, paragraphs 2–4 (after the first).** Clean enough for this seat once Finding 1 is granted. Scene-first, credited names (S-T-E-V-E With A Van; the man who should have stayed at bus station), dialogue that is allowed to be dialogue, abstractions that touch a door and a stamp and a café. The third uses the film/platform couplet once and returns to Gary. "I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset" is a light meta-pointer; I would not die on it. ## Part 2 — Seat-specific audit

### Slop, as this house defines it

**Inflation vocabulary.** I searched the body for tapestry, delve, nuance, testament, crucial, foster, landscape, realm, navigate, furthermore, moreover, additionally, "it is important," "stated plainly," "as we can see." None. This is a real clean. Say so and move on.

**Antithesis machine.** The house ban is "not X; it's Y" more than once a paragraph. I do not find that tic. What I find are single, load-bearing contrasts, which the house *allows* when they carry the argument:

- "The promise returns. What never returns is the third *as a third*."
- "He permits aloofness, enmity, and the absence of any meeting. What he does not permit is a community of people who have not each, individually, claimed the same past."
- "Austin looks at first like a focus of that kind… But the people on screen share sidewalks and form no cluster."
- "The film keeps a third you can argue with. The platform removes it."

Once each, not stacked. Do not "fix" these into softer prose. They are the piece being itself.

**Anadiplosis across sections.** Not a pattern. "Hold / holding / held" is a thread entity renamed, which is cohesion, not a tic. "The same drift is how a platform works. A platform holds…" happens once.

**Nominalization / jargon-substitution.** The body prefers verbs more often than not: Linklater films, Soldani writes, Simmel postpones, Royce waits, Reich describes, the doorman charges, the cut selects. The jargon that *does* sit in the noun slot is mostly the chapter's own toolkit (deferred third, reach, selection, algorithmacy, grant) and is defined. The failures are the untranslated German and *tertius iungens* (Finding 2), not "effects a coordination." I am not going to invent a nominalization crisis.

**Performed rigor.** Finding 4. Otherwise absent. "I will call what he set aside the deferred third" is a naming move, not a virtue-narration. "Whether that agent is a person or an algorithm I leave open" is a pointed concession; off-limits as a defect.

**Agentless passives that hide agents.** Rare. The one I would change:

> The line is quoted often as a licence for membership without meeting.

Who quotes it? "Readers often take the line as a license for membership without meeting" names a vague agent, which is still better. "Acceptance is written into the definition itself" is Royce's definition doing the writing; acceptable. "The same powers… are written down" is a setup whose next sentences name Amazon and the drivers. I will not "activate" sentences that already have agents.

**Filler transitions.** None of the banned list. "Meanwhile" at the Blue Bayou is ordinary. Keep it.

### Em-dash / en-dash — actual glyphs

The mechanical report's 6.50/1k "em-dash" is a misread of the file. Counted this session, on the live `chapter_v9.md`:

| Glyph | Code | Body | Notes + bibliography | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| em-dash — | U+2014 | **0** | 13 | Notes only: paired asides and one German quotation in note 8 |
| en-dash – | U+2013 | **27** (6.69/1k body words) | 51 | Body: used *as* em-dashes. Notes: mostly Chicago page ranges (correct), plus a few title en-dashes |

There are no ASCII `--` sequences. The body never uses a true em-dash. It uses the en-dash for every em-dash job: gloss (*tertius gaudens* – the laughing third –), list (sidewalk – a city that unifies…), and the heading "'You seen Gary around?' – The Deferred Third of *Slacker*."

I do not demand zero dashes. House style wants em-dashes in the good use (weld a gloss, stage a qualification), and does not want them rationed below roughly 3/1k. 6.69/1k of en-as-em is inside a human range. The *problem* is the paired-dash crutch, three times in the body:

- `The *tertius gaudens* – the laughing third – profits`
- `*Zweierkonfigurationen* – pairs – either`
- `not as property but as largess – a grant – whose granting`

Those are the house "dash pair bolting an aside into mid-sentence." Convert to commas or a parenthesis, in the author's voice, without losing the gloss. The single-dash landings can stay. If the production style is Chicago em-dashes, convert the body en-dashes that are doing em work to U+2014, and leave the note page-ranges as en-dashes. That is a typesetting pass, not a prose pass.

### Collection fit / film-forwardness

This sits in a *Slacker* essay collection. The editors accepted a chapter about a film that follows about a hundred characters through a day in Austin. v9 still has that film, and when it is on screen the prose belongs in the book.

The philosophy pages crowd it. Word counts this session: opening 439; Simmel/Royce 1,116; film section 1,749; reach/selection 449; conclusion 321. The named film section is the longest, which is the right shape. But 1,116 words of Simmel–Royce–Lindemann–Fuhse–Neuberger–Koschorke–Serres–Burt–Obstfeld, plus a 449-word reach section that spends its first 300 words on Feld, Barns, Warner, and Duranton & Puga, plus two platform-documentation paragraphs inside the film section, is more seminar than sidewalk. A reader who bought *Essays on Linklater's Cult Classic* can do Simmel. She should not have to do *Zweierkonfigurationen* and an integrative review in *Journal of Management* before the taxi finishes talking.

The opening Instagram / doomscrolling frame does not help that reader. It cheapens. It dates. It makes the Bordwell caution look like damage control. Platforms can come in with Stark and Pais in paragraph 3, where they already have a mechanism (co-opt without delegated authority) and a face still on the street. That is the contemporary move the collection can use. The app-name in paragraph 1 is the one it cannot.

Heading style is part of fit. "Philosophical Origins in Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe" is a seminar title. "'You seen Gary around?'" is a collection title. The chapter already knows the difference; the first heading forgot.

### Word budget

Body ≈ 4,034 against 3,000–5,000, stated as firm, no overage. About 1,000 words of headroom.

**Spend some of it. Do not spend it all. Do not spend it on more theory.**

Spend 400–600 words, and spend them on film-forwardness:

- Weld one more *visible* beat into the Simmel pages so the deferred third is seen before it is named a third time. The taxi and the struck woman are already there; they need to stay in the shot while Wolff is talking, not return as a closer.
- Give one of the list-items in "the people on screen have taken the joins over" a scene: Paul, or the PixelVision handed over with its terms, or the hitchhiker. Three or four concrete sentences, not a new construct.
- Gloss *tertius iungens* and drop the German from the body (that is a cut, which *creates* a little more room).

Hold the rest. Filling to 5,000 with another secondary, another rulebook, or another restatement of the deferred third would make Finding 2 and Finding 5 worse. The ceiling is a ceiling, not a target. v3 already taught this project what happens when the body goes over.

### *Algorithmacy* as a style object

The word is this chapter's, unpublished, defined on first use. For a film-collection reader it is the one English coinage that sounds like a TED portmanteau. The definition is plain ("fluency with determinations that arrive without reasons, and without anyone to answer them") and that sentence is better than the word. I would not kill the term — the conclusion needs a name for a competence the cited people do not have a word for — but I would not lean on the word in the last paragraph when the definition will do. "I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset" is the chapter reminding us it coined something. The people on screen asking after Gary is the better close, and it is already the close.

### First person and contractions

Twenty-one authorial "I"s in the body. That is the register working. Contractions in the author's own sentences are scarce; the film quotes carry *don't*, *can't*, *I'm*. I will not flag the scarcity as a defect. The register *allows* contractions; it does not require the author to pepper them. The voice is slightly more formal than Bordwell-chatty and well short of Annals-impersonal. That is a human position. Leave it.

## Part 3 — Paste-ready revisions

Highest-value fixes only, in the author's voice, ranked by how much each improves the piece. Each is shorter than or the same length as what it replaces. None is more nominalised than the original. Signature lines ("You seen Gary around?"; the deferred-third naming) are not rewritten.

### 1. Opening paragraph — drop the app, keep the faces

**Replaces** the first paragraph.

The film opens on a face that talks for a stretch and then is gone. Another face arrives. In 2011 Richard Linklater told Marc Savlov he saw that drift as "either channel surfing or … going in and out of different movies," a "primitive version of what now would be exponentially more complex."[^1] Maria Teresa Soldani makes the resemblance a matter of form. *Slacker*, she writes, is a twenty-four-hour urban narration whose camera drifts from character to character and from place to place without splitting those two jobs.[^2] You stay with a speaker until the film doesn't. **Why this is the largest gain.** First impression for a film editor; kills Instagram, doomscrolling, and "never mentions"; keeps Soldani, Linklater, and the face-arrives rhythm; lands on selection, which is the argument. The contraction is licensed. If the author wants a contemporary word in graf 1, "feed" can return in paragraph 2, where the platform is already the subject.

### 2. The distance paragraph — English in the body, German in the note

**Replaces** the paragraph that begins "He sets it aside because of distance."

He sets it aside because of distance. The third stands so far from the other two that no properly sociological interaction takes in all three alike. What remains are pairs: either the relation among those who join, or the relation between them as a unit and the outside center of interest. God and two believers do not constitute one three-way field. Two allies and a far enemy do not either.
