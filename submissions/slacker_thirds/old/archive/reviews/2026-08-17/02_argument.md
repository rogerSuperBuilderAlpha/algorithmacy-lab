# Argument review — hostile architecture

**Manuscript:** "'You seen Gary around?': The Deferred Third of *Slacker*" (chapter_v9.md)  
**Venue:** Bloomsbury, *Slacker: Answering the True Call — Essays on Linklater's Cult Classic*, eds. Sara Bizarro and Melissa Remark  
**Seat:** Argument. Hostile architectural referee. Not a copyedit. Not a scene census.  
**Standpoint:** I am trying to break the chapter's load-bearing joints. If a joint holds, I say so and move. I do not re-litigate the dropped hosting/engineering test, the three-question score sheet, or the Uber-dispatch architecture except where a piece of that construction has come back under a new name.

**Recommendation, up front:** major revisions. The film work at the opening cut, the Blue Bayou door, and the Gary ask is good enough to keep. The architecture that claims to organise that work — a deferred third inherited from Simmel's postponement, jointly described by Royce and Reich, sorted by reach and selection, crowned by algorithmacy, and lost when "the platform removes it" — does not hold as one argument. It is three adjacent lectures, a title term with five referents, a binary that the middle of the film violates, a coined competence the film cannot show, and a slogan standing on a mechanism the notes already complicate. An editor who accepted hosting/engineering thirds and a camera–Uber parallel will recognise the city/cut split and the platform sting if she squints. She will not recognise the chapter she accepted. That drift is major, not fatal, and not absorbable without a letter only the author can write.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

This is a first-person film-studies collection chapter. "I" steers; scenes before theses; contractions; pointed concessions. That register is the assignment. I will not flag first person, contractions, or the signature lines the piece is built around ("You seen Gary around?"; the deferred third as a phrase). I will not convert the chapter toward dissertation or Annals voice.

What I judge hard:

- Whether the title construct earns a chapter, or only a resemblance.
- Whether Simmel, Royce, and Reich join, or share a sidewalk.
- Whether reach/selection sorts the film's own cases, including the stretch where people on screen take the joins over.
- Whether algorithmacy does work the rest of the chapter has not already done.
- Whether a Bloomsbury editor can still see the accepted abstract in the live text.
- Whether the contribution survives once Neuberger, Warner, Feld, Duranton & Puga, Viljoen, and Stark & Pais are granted their due.
- Whether "the platform removes it" is a mechanism or a polemic.

House invariants I will use only as argumentative pressure, not as a style pass: named agents, claim-first, every abstraction touches a case. When a construct never touches a case, or touches the wrong case, that is an architectural failure.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 — Simmel, Royce, and Reich do not join

**Claim.** The chapter presents three patrons as one afternoon's description. They are three lectures. The sidewalk is the venue they share, not the argument that binds them.

**Quote.**

> Linklater films a day in which those problems remain on the sidewalk – a city that unifies without introducing, a shared day nobody claims as his own, access still spoken aloud to a person who can answer.

And in the conclusion:

> Simmel describes the structure, Royce the membership test, Reich the grant, Stark and Pais the co-optive enrollment.

**Why it damages this lens.** "Those problems" and the three-clause dash are parataxis. A day can contain a city, an unclaimed past, and a door. That does not make a postponement, a membership test, and a grant one mechanism. The conclusion's roll call makes the failure official: four names, four jobs, no derivation.

Test of a join: can you cut one patron and still have the chapter? Cut Royce and Gary still shows a city that will not produce a missing man. Cut Reich and the door is still a face that charges, lists, and refuses. Cut Simmel and reach/selection plus Warner, Feld, and Duranton & Puga still give you city versus cut. Independently detachable patrons are the definition of adjacent lectures.

A real join would look like one of these: a single mechanism with three necessary conditions; a derivation (the deferred third, once it has a face, is Reich's grantor; once it lacks a yes, it fails Royce); or a sort (cases that are Simmel-only, Simmel-plus-Reich, all three). The chapter performs none of those. It sequences opening-cut / door / Gary and calls the sequence a synthesis because the same afternoon holds them.

The heading of the origins section tells the truth the conclusion conceals. The section is "Philosophical Origins in Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe." Reich is not an origin. He arrives in the film section as a name for the door. If all three were co-equal descriptions, Reich would be in the origins. If Reich is only an application, the conclusion should not pair him with Simmel and Royce as the three descriptions "true of one afternoon."

**What would fix it.** Force a join or cut. The honest join, if the author wants all three, is that they describe three refusals of one arrangement — it does not enter the pair, it does not claim the day, it does not explain the refusal — while a person is still there to be asked. Write that as a claim, then let each scene earn one refusal. The cleaner cut: Simmel's distance (not his postponement) is the ancestor; Royce is a one-paragraph "this is not a community"; Reich is the door's legal name. Do not list them as three origins of one object.

### Finding 2 — "Deferred third" does not earn the chapter

**Claim.** Simmel postponed a third that *unifies* from a distance. The film shows a city that *holds* without unifying. The bibliographic drama of the postponement is not the film's form. The title term then picks up four more referents and cannot bear a chapter.

**Quote.**

> I will call what he set aside the deferred third: a third that unifies by standing so far off that it never comes between an identified pair. Austin holds that shape for ninety minutes. The man in from the bus station and the people standing around a woman struck in the street occupy one city and no relation whatever to one another.

Later, with Feld:

> the people on screen share sidewalks and form no cluster. The shared activity that produces Feld's ties is missing, and what remains is the bare holding I have been calling reach.

And in Reach/Selection:

> The deferred third adds the selection half.

**Why it damages this lens.** Simmel's examples on the page the chapter cites are an alliance against a common enemy and the invisible church. Those thirds unify. Distance is "an especially favourable constellation for the bond." The chapter's Austin does the opposite, and the chapter says so: no relation, no cluster, bare holding. A third that unifies by standing far off and a city that holds strangers who form no bond are different operations. Mapping them is a suggestive analogy, not an identity of form.

The postponement itself is a fact about Simmel's book: he promised to treat the group-forming mediation later, and when the theme returned in the conflict chapter it returned as a fact about pairs. That is a philological finding. The chapter then says the cut from the taxi to the hit-and-run "inherits that omission." A scholarly postponement is not a cinematic form. The form worth keeping is: two parties and a city, never three terms in one field. You can get that from Simmel's *distance* argument without the story of what he failed to restore two hundred pages later. The postponement-as-form is the chapter trying to make a reading of *Soziologie* into a reading of *Slacker*.

Then the name spreads. Across the chapter "deferred third" is:

1. what Simmel set aside (the omitted triad-member);
2. Austin, which "holds that shape";
3. the arrangement that "holds strangers available… and arranges not one of their meetings";
4. Warner's public plus selection ("adds the selection half");
5. pluralised as "its two thirds" — reach and selection.

A title construct that means the genus, one species, the pair of species, a bibliographic omission, and a Warner-upgrade cannot organise a chapter. The old hosting/engineering pair at least named two. This name wants to be both.

Does anything survive? Yes: a third present as a condition (city, God, enemy) that never enters the interaction field as a party. That parallel is real and smaller than the chapter. It does not require the postponement narrative, and it does not license calling Austin a unifier.

**What would fix it.** Use "deferred third" once, for Simmel's omitted object, and stop. Call Austin reach. Call the camera selection. Call the pair the arrangement. Do not say the deferred third adds the selection half. If the film's form is holding-without-introducing, say that, and let Simmel be an ancestor of the distance, not the form itself.

### Finding 3 — Reach/selection does not sort the film's own cases

**Claim.** The chapter asserts a strict division of labour the film, on the chapter's own account, does not keep. The doorman is a selector the binary cannot absorb. The middle stretch is not the same mechanism changing hands. This is the heir of the old "test does not sort" fatal, in new clothes. I am not reopening the three questions. I am saying a new binary has the same failure mode.

**Quote.**

> The film keeps a strict division of labour between reach and selection, its two thirds.

> *Slacker* never lets the city do the cut's job, or the cut do the city's.

> Through the rest of the day that holding keeps working, while the mechanism of the joins changes hands. … By the middle of the day the people on screen have taken the joins over, and they do it with things they can hand across and sentences they say out loud.

**Why it damages this lens.** If the division is strict, the middle stretch cannot be the mechanism of the joins changing hands. Either the cut is always the selector (and the people close meetings the city already made possible, which is reach being used, not selection changing owner), or the people are selecting, and the binary has a third agent. The chapter says both.

Two different joins are being treated as one. The taxi-to-body cut is extra-diegetic: two parties share a city and no story-world meeting; the cut places them in sequence for the viewer. The lift, the address, Steve's van, the stamp, the PixelVision camera are diegetic: people meet, hand things, say terms. Dawn's "Wake up, America" is extra-diegetic again. "The mechanism of the joins changes hands" claims one mechanism. It is two, alternating. The opening and the dawn look like the cut joining because nothing is handed and nobody is addressed. The middle looks like people joining because things are handed and sentences are said. That is a change of *kind*, not of *hands*.

The door is worse for the binary. Reich's doorman charges, keeps a list, decides the case, withholds a reason. That is selection in the story world. It is not the city and it is not the cut. The chapter's own best scene produces a third selector the Reach/Selection section never mentions. A strict division of two thirds that cannot place the chapter's exhibit is not a strict division.

Other cases the binary only sorts by charity: Gary is city-plus-cut plus a talker who latches on (participant closure). The hitchhiker faces a diegetic camera crew inside the film's camera. The Super-8 from Mount Bonnell is the cut again. The binary is clean for the extra-diegetic opening and close. It is not a sort of "the film's own cases."

Duranton and Puga are asked to underwrite more than they can. They split match quality from match probability as two things *cities* do. The chapter takes the split as "a precedent for treating reach and matching as separable," then assigns the two functions to a city and a camera. Separability is not an assignment of agents. In the middle stretch, matching is done by people asking for lifts — closer to Duranton and Puga's agents in a dense city than to a camera choosing a face.

**What would fix it.** Write the paragraph the middle stretch is owed. The city never chooses the next face. The cut never puts two strangers in the same café. People, in the middle of the day, use the city's reach and close meetings themselves. That is not the cut's job changing hands. It is the arrangement working as advertised: hold, do not introduce, let them do it. Then say, in the same paragraph, that the doorman is a selector *in* the city — a face administering reach — and not a second camera. If that means the "two thirds" are reach and selection only at the level of city versus cut, and the door is a different object (a grant), own the remainder. Do not call the division strict and then narrate its exceptions as the same mechanism.

### Finding 4 — Algorithmacy is a label, and the exhibit contradicts the definition

**Claim.** The term is unpublished, coined here, and asked to carry the chapter's use-claim. The film cannot show what the definition names. What the film shows, the rest of the chapter has already said without the word.

**Quote.**

> I call fluency with determinations that arrive without reasons, and without anyone to answer them, algorithmacy. The film is where that competence can still be seen working, and that is its use for making the most of the world the platforms inherited.

And at the close:

> The competence that the day in Austin demands of its participants – knowing how to argue with a door, how to copy a stamp, how to say a join out loud – is the way to make the most of a third that has dropped its face. I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset.

**Why it damages this lens.** The definition is fluency with determinations that arrive *without* anyone to answer them. The exhibit is arguing with a door, copying a stamp, saying a join out loud. Those are skills for a third that still has a face. The conclusion then says those faced skills are "the way to make the most of" a faceless third. That is a transfer claim. No mechanism of transfer is offered. Knowing how to argue with a door does not obviously help when there is no door — that is the chapter's own diagnosis of the platform.

So algorithmacy is either a name for a competence the film does not depict (faceless fluency), or a mislabel for a competence the film does depict (faced argument). In the first case the film cannot be "where that competence can still be seen working." In the second case the word does no work the door, the stamp, and the spoken rule have not done. Note 4 ("the definition given here is the one this argument requires") does not close the gap. It announces that the gap is load-bearing.

Delete the word and the chapter still has: a third you can argue with; a platform that withdraws the person; a day that shows the leftover skills. The use-claim ("its use for making the most of the world the platforms inherited") is what needs the word, or a substitute. As written the substitute is an assertion.

**What would fix it.** The honest line: the film cannot show algorithmacy. It shows the competence algorithmacy replaces. That is its use — a record of what was dropped. If the unpublished construct needs a cameo, give it one sentence as a name under development and do not identify it with arguing, stamping, and saying a join. If the chapter needs a use-claim without the unpublished term, say the day is where you can still see a face administer a grant, and stop.

### Finding 5 — An editor will not recognise the accepted chapter

**Claim.** Vocabulary moved, and then the argument moved. Reach/selection is hosting/engineering renamed. The rest is a different chapter. Drift is major. Not fatal. Not absorbable without the author writing the editors.

**Quote.** The accepted abstract promised:

> Slacker contains two distinct kinds of third: "hosting" and "engineering." … Austin hosts while the camera engineers. … The parallel between Linklater's camera and Uber's algorithm is formal and specific. Both select, rank, and filter among parties that never negotiate directly with each other. … I'll argue that cinema has trained viewers to read engineering thirds for a century.

The live chapter promises a deferred third, a sidewalk that still has a face, Royce's failed yes, Reich's grant, and algorithmacy. Uber appears as deactivation statistics, not as the camera's formal twin. Lofland, civil inattention, and the pap-smear pitch are gone. The training subject has changed: the abstract trained *viewers* by letting them watch an engineering third; v9 trains nobody and shows *participants* arguing with a door. Those are different pedagogical claims.

**Why it damages this lens.** Collection terms already flag the terminology change as an author-only reply. v9 is not a terminology change. It is a new spine (Simmel's postponement + Royce + Reich) under a renamed split (reach/selection) with a new use-object (algorithmacy) and a new sting ("the platform removes it"). What remains visible if you squint: two kinds of third; Austin versus camera; platforms as the contemporary comparison; Stark and Pais; the film as making something about platforms visible; no return, no resolution.

An editor who assigned the slot for the camera–Uber / cinema-training / hosting-becomes-engineering piece will notice. The abstract's distinctive claims are not renamed. They are gone. The collection accepted the abstract *pending editorial review*. That is exactly the moment an unannounced rebuild gets sent back or treated as a different submission.

I am not saying the old architecture was better. I am saying the live chapter cannot pretend to be the accepted one. Prior panels closed hosting/engineering. Closing it in the manuscript without closing it with the editors is an argument problem because the chapter's warrant to exist in this book is that abstract.

**What would fix it.** Not a sentence I can write. The author tells the editors what moved and why the city/cut split plus the faced third is still the chapter they bought. If they wanted camera ≈ Uber and cinema-as-training, those claims have to come back or the slot has to be re-sold. This seat flags the drift. It does not draft the letter.

### Finding 6 — "The platform removes it" is a polemic standing on a thinner mechanism

**Claim.** The mechanism the chapter actually argues is: platforms keep charge, list, refusal, and withheld reason, and withdraw the person who had to say the rule out loud. "Removes" inflates that. The notes already refuse the inflation.

**Quote.**

> The film keeps a third you can argue with. The platform removes it.

Against the earlier, better sentence:

> The platforms that inherit this arrangement keep the charge, the list, the refusal and the withheld reason. They drop both the person who can state the rule and the stamp that strangers could copy among themselves.

**Why it damages this lens.** The door already withholds reasons. "Sorry, Steve. Don't see you." The withheld reason is not what platforms invent. What the chapter can claim is the withdrawal of a person at the curb and of a transferable stamp. Even that is cleaner than the evidence. Amazon's licensing page withholds details and bars appeal — a strong exhibit for one firm, one product. Rideshare Drivers United finds two-thirds reporting deactivation and thirty per cent of *those* given no reason, from a union-skewed sample the note itself qualifies. Seventy per cent of the deactivated got some reason. The LSEG notes show a venue obliged to give written reasons and reconsideration, and another that "endeavours" to give prior notice. Rideshare Drivers United "exists because drivers organised to compel an answer from a third that will not give one" — which is the chapter saying the third can still be forced to answer. That is withdrawal until pressure, not removal.

The Video Backpacker analogy (cannot rewind a stabbing / cannot replay an allocation) is a different loss wearing the same sentence. The FX matching venues of 1992–93 are a leftover from the dropped engineering-third architecture: matching without buying. They are not required by a deferred-third argument, and they reappear as a one-paragraph guest.

Polemic in the last two sentences of a film chapter is allowed. Polemic that contradicts the chapter's own mechanism and notes is not. "Removes" is the line a hostile referee quotes to say the ending was decided before the evidence.

**What would fix it.** Keep the better sentence. End on withdrawal behind a rule, not on disappearance. Let RDU be the political form that answers a withdrawn face, which is closer to the abstract's last question and closer to Stark and Pais than a slogan.

## Part 2 — Seat-specific audit

The brief's load-bearing questions, then the chapter in order.

### 2.1 Does "deferred third" earn the chapter? Is Simmel's postponement the film's form, or a suggestive analogy?

Analogy. See Finding 2. The postponement is a fact about a book. The film's form is a city that holds and a cut that sequences parties who share no relation. The strongest non-analogical remainder is: a condition that unifies or holds from outside the pair and never enters as a third party. Even that remainder splits. Simmel's distant third unifies (enemy, God). Austin holds without unifying (the Feld paragraph). The chapter needs the unifying sense to inherit Simmel and the non-unifying sense to describe the film. It cannot have both without saying it is using Simmel against his examples.

I grant, without re-checking the German (VERIFY), that the phrase occurs once and that the conflict-chapter return treats distance as a condition of a bond rather than as a triad-member. That reading can be right and still not be *Slacker's* form. A correct Simmel claim used as a film claim is still an analogy.

### 2.2 Do the three patrons join?

No. See Finding 1. Additional pressure from the film section's sequence: Simmel is applied to the opening cut, Reich to the door, Royce to Gary, Stark and Pais to the hitchhiker, Viljoen to the app. That is a tour of names across a tour of scenes. The join sentence the conclusion offers — "Linklater films the interval in which all three descriptions are true of one afternoon" — is the weakest join available. Many descriptions are true of one afternoon.

Royce is the most detachable and the most tautological. "A day in a city cannot meet that test" follows from the definition once acceptance is constitutive and strangers have not accepted. Gary is then an illustration of a default, not a near-miss. Two men in a café who do not take a missing third party as the same past event are not a failed canoe. They were never candidates. The chapter first says the room "fails every part" of Royce's test, then says the second condition (contemporaries in communication) holds on the street. Both cannot stand. The useful, unstated Royce job in this collection would be to block a reading of Austin as a scene-community (Soldani's "body of a scene"; the book's "true call"). That job is never assigned. As written, Royce proves that unclaimed strangers are not a community of memory. Yes.

Reich is more attached, because the door is a real exhibit. Note 16 already says the four powers are the chapter's taxonomy, not Reich's. The body still says "Reich's diagnosis covers all four." Content will own the source crack. Architecture owns this: the door carries the diagnosis; Reich is authority borrowed for a homemade list. The scene survives without him. He does not join Simmel or Royce except by being true on the same day.

### 2.3 Does reach/selection sort the film's cases, including the middle stretch?

No, not as a strict division. See Finding 3. The binary sorts the extra-diegetic opening and close. It does not sort the door (a story-world selector), the middle (participant closure), or the nested cameras without remainder. The old fatal was a three-question test that misclassified the camera. The new fatal is a two-term division that the chapter's own narration of "joins changing hands" contradicts. Different costume, same failure: the sorting instrument does not survive contact with the chapter's exhibits.

### 2.4 Does algorithmacy do work?

No. See Finding 4. Unpublished is not the problem. A coined term is allowed. The problem is load-bearing use plus a definition the film cannot instantiate. The rest of the chapter has already done the faced-third / withdrawn-face contrast. The word is a bow and a promissory note.

### 2.5 Can an editor recognise the accepted chapter?

Partly the split, not the argument. See Finding 5. Drift rating: **major**. Not fatal — the object is still *Slacker*, still two thirds, still platforms. Not absorbable — the abstract's load-bearing claims (camera ≈ Uber; cinema trained viewers; hosting becoming engineering; political economy of aesthetic versus extractive engineering) are absent, not renamed. Reach/selection is the one recognisable descendant of hosting/engineering. Algorithmacy is a cousin of the training claim with the wrong subject (participants, not viewers). The camera–Uber parallel is gone.

### 2.6 Prior art / novelty — does the contribution survive the neighbors?

Grant the neighbors what the chapter already gives them, and see what is left.

- **Neuberger** already took *gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung* to journalism and platforms. The chapter says so. Novelty cannot be "Simmel's mediating third, now on platforms." Leftover: the philological claim that the third-as-third never returns, and the claim that the film instantiates the postponed form. The first is a Simmel contribution sitting in a *Slacker* book. The second is Finding 2.
- **Warner** already has strangers constituted by a common relation to something outside them, no interaction required, membership by attention. The chapter says the deferred third adds selection. Then the Austin-half of the deferred third is Warner minus a circulating text, and the addition is the camera. Warner plus a camera essay does not need Simmel's postponement.
- **Feld** is used negatively: Austin looks like a focus and produces no cluster. "Reach" is the remainder. "A city holds people who do not form a cluster" is a city. The name is the chapter's; the fact is not.
- **Duranton and Puga** already split match quality from match probability. They do not assign matching to a camera. Note 23 correctly owns that no retrieved source pairs city-as-reach with cut-as-selection. That pairing is the chapter's, and it is the accepted abstract's pairing under new words.
- **Viljoen** already has enrollment in a grouping through no act of one's own. The chapter adds: that is not Roycean membership. Thin, and it spends Royce on a contrast Viljoen already marked (no claim).
- **Stark and Pais** already have co-optation without delegated authority. The chapter adds the face: on the sidewalk there is still somebody to ask. That contrast is real and is the chapter's.

**Remainder that survives:** (1) the city/cut division of labour, which is the accepted chapter's spine; (2) the faced grant at the door, and the stamp as transferable property against a list that is not; (3) the claim that platforms keep the four powers and withdraw the person. Those are enough for a film chapter. They do not require the postponement philology, Royce's canoe, or algorithmacy. The architecture over-equips a two-or-three-point contribution with patrons who do not need each other.

Koschorke / Serres, cited in passing ("the third comes first"), is another neighbor that can swallow the taxi-already-moving claim. I flag it; I do not build a finding on a clause.

### 2.9 Section by section

**Introduction (feed / doomscrolling versus Bordwell).**  
Five paragraphs, three warrants. The film feels like a feed; the inside is doomscrolling. Then a platform is a self-less third; Simmel, Royce, Reich; the film is "an observation and a reaction to how those older arrangements set us up for the triadic world of the platform." Then Stark and Pais, algorithmacy, use-claim. Then Bordwell: do not treat multi-protagonist films as internet symptoms; this arrangement is older; platforms inherited it. Then: I come from organisation theory; the sidewalk is an organisation.

The Bordwell paragraph is supposed to retract the feed as etiology. It does not retract the feed as experience, and it does not confess the distinction. "Set us up" is the abstract's training thesis smuggled back as inheritance. Note 5 restricts the claim to what the film makes visible and denies a causal relation to technical development. The intro's "set us up" and "inherited" are inheritance claims, which Bordwell does not forbid, but they are written in the register of preparation. A hostile reader hears: the sidewalk trained us, then the platforms came. That is the accepted abstract's argument, and it is the one v9 no longer makes in the body.

The org-theory badge is a second introduction. It does not follow from Bordwell. The deferred third is not yet named. For a film collection, the feed hook will play. For this seat: you opened on a resemblance you then disavow. Own it in one move — the feeling is mine and is not an explanation — or drop the doomscrolling sentence. Do not keep it as atmosphere and proceed as if Bordwell had cleared it.

The platform definition in paragraph 2 ("holds strangers available… arranges none of their meetings… charges, lists, refuses, withholds a reason") already fuses Simmel-hold, Reich-grant, and the faceless agent. That fusion is the chapter's promised object. The rest of the chapter never derives it. It restates it.

**Philosophical Origins.**  
This is the most carefully argued theoretical stretch, and it is overweight relative to the join it fails to produce. The Simmel philology (once at p. 103, return at p. 321 as dyad, index entry, brokerage literature has no instrument) is a paper of its own. In a 3,000–5,000 word *Slacker* chapter it crowds the film. I am not the flow or style seat; I am saying the architecture spends its capital on an ancestor the film does not instantiate as stated (unifies ≠ holds).

The brokerage exclusion (Kwon et al.: actors, work context, no non-humans) is used to say Austin and the cut fall outside before measurement begins. True, and cheap. A literature's scope condition is not a finding about a film.

Royce is set up with real care (cumulative conditions, acceptance written into the definition, aloofness permitted, unclaimed past not). The payoff — a day cannot meet the test; Gary's room fails it — is a tautology plus a preview of a scene. The canoe is vivid. It does not make the film's strangers a failed community. It makes them not a community. The section ends on Gary before we have seen Gary. That is a hook. It is also a tell: Royce exists here to arrive at a line the film section will repeat.

**The long film section.**  
This is where the chapter is itself, and where the architecture keeps interrupting.

The double opening is the best argument in the file. Two streets, no character-bridge, Austin contains both, the cut decides which we watch, the "always someone getting run over" echo is an arrangement no one on screen can notice. That is a deferred-third *scene* in the only sense I will grant: two parties, one city, never three terms in one field. If the chapter trusted this, it would need less Simmel.

"Meanwhile" at the Blue Bayou is a hard join. The door is a different object. The writing is good — list, refusal, stamp, closing time, face versus grant. This is the faced third the conclusion needs. It is not the opening's third. The section asks one stretch of film to illustrate distance-without-relation *and* grant-with-a-face *and* (a page later) failed membership. That is the three-lectures problem at the level of scenes.

Gary is the title scene and the Royce scene. As deferred-third material it works: the sidewalk held both men, the cut (or the walking) selected a meeting, nobody introduced them, Gary is never produced. As Royce material it is the tautology above. Keep Gary for the ask the city cannot answer. Do not make him a membership test.

The middle stretch is the chapter's most interesting film claim and the binary's undoing. ## Verdict

**Major revisions.**

The chapter has a film argument and a theoretical superstructure that does not carry it. The film argument is: a city holds strangers and introduces none of them; a cut sequences some of them for us; in the middle they close meetings themselves; a door still has to say the rule; platforms keep the powers and withdraw the person.
