# Review — media industries / indie distribution / streaming enclosure

**Verdict:** minor revisions
**Target:** chapter_v3.md (post-followup; panel 3)

## Step 0 — register

The first person, contractions, scene-first movement, and concessions belong to the chapter's chosen
register. I do not treat them as defects. The current §7 has also fixed the prior fatal count: §5 names
three powers, and §7 now says Amazon's supplier page shows two of them while pricing is absent. The
remaining work is narrower. The section must decide what "enclosure" means, trim one unsupported
present-tense comparison, and keep the VoD evidence inside what its sources actually measured.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and structure

### 1. Enclosure is still named more strongly than it is proved

Current text:

> What replaced that chain narrows what reaches a viewer twice over, and the two stages are separate.

and:

> The studios did close their specialty divisions, and Yannis Tzioumakis documents it. What his numbers
> refuse is the second half of the easy story, that the streamers then took over.

This is now much fairer to Tzioumakis. Verified: I retrieved the article in this session. His abstract
and conclusion repeatedly limit the study to American independent film theatrical distribution,
2008-2019. The conclusion says the late indies filled the gap, indiewood kept substantial theatrical
visibility, and Netflix and Amazon were involved in a "different and rather low-key manner." The chapter
now says "theatrical distribution changed shape more than it shrank," which is the right verb.

The section title, though, is still "Enclosure." The body proves replacement, narrowing, and opacity.
It does not yet prove enclosure unless the word is defined narrowly. Waldfogel is the live
counter-pressure here. Verified: I retrieved his 2017 *Journal of Economic Perspectives* article in this
session. He writes that digitization relaxed the distribution bottleneck, that 2010 releases available on
Netflix and Amazon Instant were roughly twice the number available in theaters, and that movies
"distributed to consumers through some familiar distribution channel" at least doubled between 2000 and
2010. A media-industries reader will know this literature. The chapter should not sound as if fewer films
can get to market in the broad sense.

The missing fact that earns the title is Amazon's submission door. Verified: I retrieved Amazon's current
provider pages and Smits's thesis in this session. Amazon now says, "Access to self-service submissions
is by invitation only," and, for Prime streaming, "We aren't accepting unsolicited submissions ... Rights
holders can't request consideration at this time." Smits described the earlier Amazon Prime Video route
as "unfiltered, with no gatekeeping restrictions." That is not a general fall in film availability. It is
the closure of a route from rights holder to dominant storefront.

Paste-ready insertion, directly before the Amazon paragraph:

> In 2017 Roderik Smits could still describe Amazon's Prime Video route for non-premium titles as
> "unfiltered, with no gatekeeping restrictions." The current provider page says the door now opens by
> invitation: "Access to self-service submissions is by invitation only." For Prime streaming it is
> flatter: "We aren't accepting unsolicited submissions ... Rights holders can't request consideration at
> this time." That is enclosure in the narrow sense this section can prove: a route from rights holder to
> dominant platform has been narrowed before the title reaches a viewer.

If that paragraph is too costly, retitle the section "Replacement" or "Opacity." The current evidence
supports those words without further repair.

### 2. One present-day acquisition claim outruns the source

Current text:

> Theatrical distribution changed shape more than it shrank — and *Slacker*'s own position in 1990, with
> no other bidder and one modest offer taken on a template, looks more like the present than like the boom
> in between.

The first clause is supported by Tzioumakis. The second clause is not. Tzioumakis ends in 2019 and is
about theatrical distribution, not the current acquisition market. The chapter supplies no live trade
evidence for the present comparison. It may be true. It is not proved here.

This matters because the sentence carries the enclosure-versus-replacement question. If the present is
like *Slacker*'s no-bidder position, the claim becomes a large market diagnosis. If the point is that
*Slacker* was never the boom, the claim can stay local and needs no new source.

Paste-ready replacement:

> Theatrical distribution changed shape more than it shrank. *Slacker*'s own position in 1990, with no
> other bidder and one modest offer taken on a template, was never the boom the specialty divisions later
> made imaginable.

Or cut the second sentence entirely. The paragraph works without it.

### 3. The power count is fixed, but the new "fourth thing" needs a cleaner name

Current text:

> Amazon publishes its own account of how titles are licensed for Prime Video, and two of the three
> powers are on the page. It reads: "we continuously review performance metrics and viewing behavior." It
> removes: titles may be taken down "at any time at the sole discretion of Prime Video." Pricing the page
> says nothing about. What it adds instead is a fourth thing the three questions do not reach.

This is no longer the old arithmetic error. Two of three powers is right. The missing pricing claim is
handled honestly. Verified: Amazon's live page contains the "sole discretion," "performance metrics and
viewing behavior," visibility-in-portal, no-resubmission, no-appeal, and no-additional-details language.

The phrase "a fourth thing" still feels undernamed. It sounds like a fourth power while avoiding the word.
The section can state the property directly: a platform can give a verdict and withhold reasons and
appeal. That phrasing also connects better to the earlier Amsterdam remedy in §5, where disclosure was
the remedy even when reinstatement was not.

Paste-ready replacement for one sentence:

> What it adds is procedural: a platform can give a verdict while withholding reasons and appeal.

Then the next sentence can keep its current contrast with Blodgett:

> That is the modern counterpart of the Orion vice-president who said no. She said no, and Linklater knew
> whom to ask.

### 4. Bengesser, Hilborn, and Steemers now mostly survive, but "editorial promotion" is too narrow

Current text:

> Their landing-page figures are about editorial promotion: they cleared cookies daily and worked from
> fresh accounts, though they are careful to add that "it is by no means certain that this can be
> excluded totally."

Verified: I retrieved the article in this session. The current chapter fixes the prior denominator error:
the landing-page share is now "the British share," not one title in ten from the whole catalogue. It also
keeps the authors' personalization hedge. Those were the two large faults in the earlier version, and
they are gone.

The remaining issue is "editorial." The article describes "content promotion and presentation," and its
conclusion asks for more evidence about "algorithmic filtering and promotional processes." It does not
separate editorial curation from algorithmic curation. Use a word the source owns.

Paste-ready replacement:

> Their landing-page figures are about prominence: they cleared cookies daily and worked from fresh
> accounts, though they are careful to add that "it is by no means certain that this can be excluded
> totally."

The next sentence can stay:

> A page that chooses what you see next, largely without knowing who you are, is the nearest thing
> streaming has to Linklater's camera.

"Largely" does the needed work.

### 5. The Pierson/Orion chain is stronger with the single-informant dependency named

Current text:

> Pierson, a producer's representative whose job was introducing films to buyers, took it to them. The
> Dobie booked it on its own judgment and held it eleven weeks.

and:

> Linklater and Pierson took the video release to Susan Blodgett, who ran Orion's home video division,
> and lost...

The naming of Susan Blodgett fixes the prior "named vice-president" problem. The section still does not
tell the reader that the 1991 distribution chain rests heavily on Pierson, with Macor's account also
using him as a source. That does not weaken the argument if it is disclosed. It may strengthen it,
because the point is precisely that this was an addressable chain of people rather than a measurable
system exposed in many datasets.

Paste-ready addition after the first sentence quoted above:

> Nobody has written the sociology of that job, so Pierson's memoir is not one account among several. It
> is the account, and Macor's account also runs through him.

If the addition costs too much, move the dependency into note 67.

## Part 2 — The four bans, and AI-slop audit

**Ban 1, metaphor doing argumentative work.** One live hit: "Enclosure" in the heading. The body now
needs one literal sentence defining the enclosure claim, preferably through the Smits-to-Amazon closure
above. "Every link in the chain" is fine because the literal claim follows at once: a person could be
telephoned.

**Ban 2, throat-clearing.** Clean in §7. The section mostly opens on facts and lets the comparison follow.

**Ban 3, meta-commentary / forward-backward reference.** One small hit remains:

> ... sixteen theatres at its widest — not a national audience reached but sixteen bookings argued for
> one at a time, which is the scale to hold in mind before comparing any of this with a recommender.

The last clause tells the reader how to use the fact. State the comparison instead.

Paste-ready replacement:

> ... sixteen theatres at its widest: sixteen bookings argued for one at a time, not a reach delivered by
> an interface.

**Ban 4, unexplained jargon.** "Prominence" is defined by the sentence "Prominence decides what is
found." That is enough. "Lowest tier" and "tier above it" are understandable in context but a little
compressed for readers outside film-industry studies. If one clause is available, name the frame:
"Tzioumakis's lowest distributor tier..." No other §7 jargon needs emergency repair.

**AI-slop audit.** The old count problem is gone. The surviving decorative antithesis is the unsupported
"more like the present than like the boom in between." Cut or replace it under Part 1. The sentence
"not a national audience reached but..." earns its contrast because a reader will otherwise overread
"nationally released." Keep the contrast, but remove the instruction to "hold in mind."
