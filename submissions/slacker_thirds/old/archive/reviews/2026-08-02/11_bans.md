# Review 11 — the four bans

**Verdict: minor revisions.** Thirty-five hits, none structural. Every fix below is a line, a clause or
a word, and the chapter can absorb all of them in an afternoon without losing a paragraph.

## Step 0 — register

The first person, the contractions, the scenes-before-theses and the concessions taken in stride are the
chapter's method, and I have not counted any of them against it; where I propose a rewrite it is shorter,
more concrete and more active than what it replaces, or I have dropped it.
The prose is genuinely good, and the bans are being kept most of the time — this review is the residue
after a draft that already survived one ban purge, not a report of a draft that ignored them.

## Part 1 — rigor and structure, as the bans reach them

Four items where a ban violation and a substantive problem are the same problem.

**1. §7 miscounts §5's powers, and does it in the banned form.** §5 enumerates three powers and numbers
them: "The first is that it prices each match," "The second thing is that it reads the parties," "The
third thing is that it can remove a party." §7 then says: "Amazon publishes its own account of how titles
are licensed for Prime Video, and three of the powers described above are in it." It lists *reads*,
*removes*, and *declines to explain* — and then calls pricing "the fourth power." Declining to explain
was never one of the three. So the sentence both refers backward in the form ban 3 forbids ("described
above") and refers backward *incorrectly*. The refusal to explain is a real and well-evidenced fourth
thing — it is what the Amsterdam court's disclosure remedy was about — but §5 never names it as a power,
so §7 cannot cash a cheque §5 did not write. Either name four powers in §5, or fix the count in §7.

**2. §2 defines a Feldian focus in a way that destroys its own *PNAS* argument.** The chapter's gloss:
"A focus, in his term, is a person, place or activity that people organise around." Eight paragraphs
later, the whole force of the *PNAS* critique rests on: "Feld's foci are not people." Both sentences are
on the page. As written, Rosenfeld, Thomas and Hausen are entitled to call friends and family "the
essential network foci," because the chapter has just told the reader that a focus can be a person. The
project's own research file (`research/deep/2026-08-01_s2.md`, lines 445–46, 838) has Feld's foci as
"entities around which joint activities are organized, not persons," which makes "a person" a slip in
the gloss rather than a defensible reading. One word.

**3. §3 asserts cheapness and then reports the opposite ranking.** "Cheap space buys unstructured time"
is followed within two sentences by "Dallas that year sat at 15.3 percent vacancy and Houston at 15.8,
so Austin was the tighter market of the three." The concession is honest and I would not cut it, but as
it stands the paragraph's own comparison undercuts its topic sentence and the reader is left to reconcile
them. The reconciliation the numbers support is *room*, not *price* — 48 percent housing growth against
34.6 percent population growth, $346 median rent — and the paragraph says so at the end ("what it had was
a great deal of room"). Move that clause up and the tension goes away.

**4. §7 applies one of the three questions to streaming and drops the other two.** §2 builds an apparatus
of three questions and the chapter is disciplined about running it — until the streaming case, where only
question three appears: "A page that chooses what you see next, without knowing who you are, is the
nearest thing streaming has to Linklater's camera." Reach and counterfactual go unrun. Given that the
same paragraph has just established that licensing removes 92 percent of titles before prominence gets a
vote, the counterfactual answer is right there and would be the strongest sentence in the section.

**Asymmetry worth naming.** The chapter glosses its sociology and assumes its cinema. *Civil inattention*,
*nonterminal*, *tertius gaudens*, *disintermediation*, *brokerage*, *dérive*, *focus*, *producer's
representative* — all defined in a plain clause at first use, and well done. *Diegetic*, *the 180-degree
rule*, *a top of sixteen screens*, *the second window*, *the third tier* — none defined. If the collection's
reader is a film reader, the second list is fine and the first list is over-explained; the chapter clearly
believes the reader is more general than that, and the bans as written ("every term a reader might not
own") commit it to the wider standard. Pick one and apply it evenly. My fixes below assume the wider one.

## Part 2 — the four bans

### Ban 1 — metaphor doing argumentative work

**Cleared first, because the brief named them.** These are illustrations of claims stated literally, and
should stay:

- **"a bare chain"** — glossed in the same breath: "each one tied only to whoever shares their sidewalk
  for a few minutes."
- **"an ordered walk"** — glossed in the same breath: "scene one, then scene two, in that sequence,
  chosen." Both graph words are earned and neither floats.
- **"*Slacker* takes away the alibi"** — cashed out in the next sentence: "In an ordinary film the cut
  serves the plot, so a viewer credits the plot. Remove the plot and the choosing is the only thing left
  to notice." The literal statement arrives one sentence *after* the figure rather than before it, which
  is the only quarrel available, and it is not worth having. Keep.
- **"Every link in the chain … was a person who could be telephoned"** — the figure and the literal fact
  are in the same sentence.
- **"A camera can pass you over. It cannot price your passage and it cannot take you off the street."** —
  a summary of three powers just stated at length with citations. This is what the ban permits.
- **"took nothing from them except a day, and gave the day back watchable"** — closing figure, resting on
  §5's literal statement of what a selector can do that a camera cannot. Keep.
- **"hand-holding"** — Linklater's own word from the quotation two lines above. Keep, but see H31.

**Hits.**

**H1. "Her own floor is the answer" (§4).** *Floor* is unexplained and it is carrying the connective work
of the paragraph — why Poulaki's own criterion defeats the rebuttal. The quotation that follows states
the criterion; the word that introduces it does not.

**H2. "One gate refused them, and it had a name. The earlier gate had responded" (§7).** The first *gate*
sits on a literal fact stated one sentence earlier (a named vice-president said no) and survives. The
second does not: "the earlier gate" is asked to refer to a stage of the acquisition process the chapter
has never called a gate, and the metaphor is doing the referring.

**H3. "When its choices stop being one route across a field the parties could cross by themselves, and
start being the only route" (§7).** This is the chapter's climactic definition, the place where a literal
sentence matters most, and it arrives as a field with routes across it. §2 has the literal version already
— "Could the encounter have happened without it?" — so the figure adds nothing but a picture.

**H4. "Taking a stranger's run of choices for a world went invisible as convention" (§6).** Not a metaphor
exactly; a compression so tight the claim has to be reverse-engineered from it. Subject is a gerund phrase,
verb is *went invisible*, and the mechanism (habituation) is inside the preposition.

### Ban 2 — throat-clearing

**H5. "Watch what happens after the taxi." (§4).** The audit of the previous draft killed "Watch the first
handoff closely" in this exact form and for this exact reason (`00_breakdown.md` line 308: "Directive/
throat-clear | Open on the scene"). The construction came back.

**H6. "What I am claiming is narrow." (§2).** Named in the brief, and it earns the naming: the claim
itself is the next sentence, and it is narrow on its own without being pre-labelled.

**H7. "The best evidence for this power is not where a reader would expect it" (§5).** The cut ledger
records this as the replacement for "It is worth saying where this power is best evidenced…". The
self-narration went; the announcement stayed. The finding underneath is excellent and can start the
sentence.

**H8. "What is actually measured is this." (§6).** Its only job is to say a measurement is coming. There
is a real claim hiding in it — Münsterberg, Benjamin and Hansen measured nothing — and stating that claim
costs the same number of words.

**H9. "Economists have a name for what a place like that does." (§3).** Announces that a term is coming,
then the term arrives unglossed anyway (see H16). The audit gave the remedy for the previous draft's
version of this: "Lead with the gloss."

**H10. "Two qualifications, both real." (§2).** Announces two concessions rather than making them. The
concessions themselves are strong and do not need a warm-up; "both real" is the author vouching for their
seriousness instead of letting them be serious.

**H11. "Two further things in her account are worth having." (§4).** Same shape, and the two things follow
immediately.

**H12. "The warrant is documentary and anyone with three PDFs can check it." (§2).** The second clause is
excellent and belongs. The first announces the character of evidence that the next three sentences
demonstrate.

### Ban 3 — meta-commentary

**H13. "and three of the powers described above are in it" (§7).** The banned form, plus the miscount.
See Part 1, item 1.

**H14. "The camera's linking work is not a discovery and I am not claiming it as one." (§1).** The first
clause concedes priority — keep it. The second narrates the author's care about credit, and the brief
names this construction.

**H15. "and saying why sharpens the distinction" (§5).** Tells the reader that the next three sentences
will pay off, then they pay off.

**H16. "which makes the point sharper" (§7).** Same move, same section as the next one. The clause is
attached to a genuinely sharp methodological fact (cookies cleared daily), which does the sharpening by
itself.

**H17. "which is the scale to hold in mind before comparing any of this with a recommender" (§7).**
Instructs the reader in how to use a fact, and gestures at a comparison the section has not made yet.

**H18. The "worth" tic — three instances.** "a complication worth keeping" (§3), "it is worth keeping"
(§4), "are worth having" (§4). Each announces the author's judgment that something is valuable in place
of the thing being valuable. Three in a 5,400-word chapter is a pattern, and the brief lists this family
("worth keeping in mind") explicitly.

**H19. "I want to draw that difference in the right place, though" (§5).** Narrates precision. The
paragraph's last two sentences already draw the difference in the right place: "So the contrast is not
between a cut and a match. It is between a cut and a regime that prices and removes."

**H20. "so I want one instance rather than a pattern" (§3).** The limitation is real and must stay. Its
current form makes it a fact about the author's wants rather than about Gaughen's survey.

**H21. "All I can say is that surge has not been shown to know who the two parties are" (§5).** Two
problems in nine words: the author narrating the boundary of his own claim, and an agentless passive
("has not been shown") in a chapter that otherwise names its agents relentlessly.

**H22. The attribution-marking habit, borderline and worth a decision.** "That the cost falls harder on
the person who wants out is my claim, not his" (§3); "I will assert the differential pay and leave the
personalisation where she leaves it" (§5); "The authors propose this rather than show it" (§6). Each is
doing honest work and none is throat-clearing. Three in one chapter starts to read as a mannerism. The
§3 one is the most droppable — note 26 could carry it — and the §5 and §6 ones should stay, because they
mark contested evidence rather than authorial modesty.

### Ban 4 — unexplained terms

**H23. "in co-presence a member cannot simply decline to answer, since the excusing inference is
unavailable and cold-shouldering gets read in instead" (§3).** The least readable sentence in the chapter,
and the only one where a general reader will simply stop. Three unowned terms — *co-presence*, *a member*
in the ethnomethodological sense, *the excusing inference* — plus a passive with no agent. Everything in
it can be said in one plain sentence.

**H24. "The formal apparatus is built on an ordered triple, and every measure that came out of it is a
property of a node." (§2).** Three technical terms in the sentence that carries §2's central claim about
the broker literature. *Ordered triple*, *measure*, *node* — a film reader owns none of them, and the
point (the whole tradition scores individuals) is plain in ordinary words.

**H25. "on a top of sixteen screens" (§1, and again in §7).** Trade usage. Outside distribution it reads
as a typo, and it is load-bearing in both places — §1's scale of success and §7's contrast with a
recommender.

**H26. "Adding diegetic sound to the clips … including cuts violating the 180-degree rule" (§6).** Two
film-studies terms, unglossed, in a paragraph whose entire subject is viewers who had never seen a film.
The irony is sharp enough to be worth avoiding.

**H27. "no measured elasticity can be assigned to matching alone" (§3), and "assortative matching runs
*negatively* with local market size."** *Elasticity*, *observationally equivalent* and *assortative
matching* in four lines. The Mion and Naticchioni finding is a good complication and it dies unread.

**H28. "Address was possible, and refusal was attributable." (§7).** Two abstract nouns and a term used in
an unusual sense — a reader hits *address* and thinks of a street. This is §7's thesis sentence and the
plainest sentence in the section by rights.

**H29. "The third tier's fall from 10.22 to 2.11 percent … the middle tier refilled" (§7).** Tzioumakis's
tiers, borrowed without their definitions. The paragraph is already doing careful work refusing an easy
story; the reader cannot follow the refusal without knowing what fell.

**H30. Smaller ones, same fix pattern:** "on the Drag" (§3 — Austin-local, not national); "the second
window" (§7 — release-sequence jargon, and "the video release" is right there in the previous sentence);
"a participatory audit" (§5 — note 48 already says what it was); "an A/B test" (§6); "contestable rather
than proprietary" (§7 — the pair are not opposites, and neither is glossed); "the second skill" (§6 —
never numbered as the second of anything); "Convergence is uneven" (§6 — convergence of what with what).

**H31. "Three published readings have already located that hand-holding in the camera." (§1).** Not a
ban-1 hit, because Linklater's quotation is two lines up. But the chapter adopts his figure as its own
noun and then makes it the referent of what three scholars found. Quotation marks fix it at zero cost.

**H32. The film's own answer is alluded to and never quoted (§7).** "the film headed the charge off itself
on one of the oblique-strategies cards a passerby hands out in it." What the card says is the evidence,
and it is missing. The line is in the project's own transcript at `research/slacker_transcript.md` line
1133 — "Withdrawing in disgust is not the same thing as apathy" — it was in `chapter.md` (lines 352–54),
and the cut ledger does not record dropping it. This looks like an unlogged compression casualty rather
than a decision. Note the wording trap the outline records at line 854: Rosenbaum has "not the same thing
as apathy," four other books drop "thing"; the subtitle track has "thing."

### AI-slop audit (register-aware)

Clean, and I am not going to invent problems. Antithesis constructions run 22 in 5,413 body words, about
4.1 per thousand, under the house target of 5. No filler transitions, no empty closers, no "it is worth
noting," no uniform paragraph shapes — the rhythm alternates long evidential sentences with two- and
three-word verdicts and does not metronome. Two small things:

- **"rather than" carries 14 of the 22** and clusters four times in §7 (lines 365, 374, 382, 399). Most
  earn it because the wrong alternative is one a reader would reach for ("a preliminary posture rather
  than a final judgment" — right). Two are decorative: "the market changed shape rather than shrinking"
  and "contestable rather than proprietary."
- **"She said no, and Linklater knew whom to ask"** immediately after "the Orion vice-president who said
  no." Verbatim repetition three words apart, and the second one is the chapter's closing beat for the
  section, so it should not be an echo.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions.** The bans are being kept in the places where keeping them is hardest: the two
definitions in §2, the Bordwell/Chatman passage, the three powers in §5, and the closing question-and-answer
in §7 are all stated literally and would survive a much harsher reading than mine. What is left is
residue — five announcements, seven meta-clauses, two metaphors carrying weight they should not, and a
dozen terms that need a clause each. Nothing here requires a paragraph to be rethought.

## Biggest genuine strength

The chapter refuses to let a figure do the definitional work at the two points where the temptation is
overwhelming. §2 could have defined setting and selector by analogy and instead gives two bolded literal
sentences and three literal questions, then immediately runs the questions against a case that *fails*
them (Simmel's *divide et impera* scoring as a setting while destroying reachability) rather than against
a case that flatters them. §4 does the same with the camera: it states Bordwell's objection in his own

## The one thing only the author can supply

Whether "declines to explain" is a fourth power of a selector, or an aspect of the three. §7 needs it to
be a power and §5 does not have it. The Amsterdam judgment (disclosure ordered, reinstatement refused) and
Amazon's "may not be resubmitted or appealed" are the two strongest pieces of evidence in the chapter's
second half, and both are about explanation rather than about price, matching or removal. If the answer
is yes, §5's enumeration changes and the chapter gains a power that has nothing to do with money — which
is also the one power a camera arguably shares, since a cut never explains itself either. That is an
argumentative decision, not a line edit, and it is the author's.
