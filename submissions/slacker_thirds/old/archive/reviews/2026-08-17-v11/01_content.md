## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### Finding 1 — The opening is still treated as an unbridged selection after the chapter's own location notes say the passenger witnesses

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'` (film-section open; later "two kinds of join" paragraph). Voice-pass residue against v10.

**Claim.** The cut after the taxi is selection. Early on, the cut places parties who never meet — the taxi, then the body — in one sequence for us.

**Quotes.**

> The film opens on a street within the first five minutes, and the deferred third is already the holding. The cut that follows is selection.

> Location notes put the drop on 24th Street, between San Antonio and Nueces, near the Castilian, and say the passenger witnesses a hit-and-run there. What the stretch still shows, even if he walks into the next street, is that Austin holds both beats and introduces nobody.

> Early on the cut places parties who never meet — the taxi, then the body — in one sequence for us.

v10 had, in the body, "I have not settled that continuity from the disc" and "Whether anyone on screen is in a position to notice it is a fact the disc still owes." v11 dropped both from the body (note 14 still withholds disc confirmation) and re-added "The cut that follows is selection."

**Why it damages this lens.** The deferred-third reading of the opening is still the chapter's first film proof. It needs the first join to be a cut that selects a new face, and the taxi and the body to be parties who never meet. The chapter's own note now prints the published claim that contradicts both.

**Verified** this session (Austin Film Commission, *Slacker* filming locations, retrieved): "A taxi drops Richard Linklater … off on 24th Street between San Antonio & Nueces Streets near the Castilian dorm, **where he witnesses a hit and run.**" **Verified** (Brendan Gaughen, "*Slacker* Geography, 25 Years Later," *The End of Austin*, 2014, retrieved): the drop is timestamped at **4:43** on 24th between San Antonio and Nueces; the next outdoor pin is **6:47** at 2405 Nueces, "Linklater's residence," the house the camera enters after the body. "Within the first five minutes" is therefore fair as a published timestamp. What Gaughen does not settle, and what the Film Commission asserts, is whether the passenger is still in the accident frame.

If the passenger walks into that street — the possibility the chapter itself now names — "the cut that follows" is following a man, not selecting a stranger, and "parties who never meet" is false of the taxi and the body. Witnessing is not introducing. The city can still hold both beats without introducing them. That is all the stretch still shows. "Never meet" and "the cut that follows is selection" go past it. They are the old "no character bridges" claim in new clothes.

The dialogue rhyme is still real. **Verified** (subtitle transcript, line 21): "It's like there's always someone getting run over or something really weird." The chapter's compression is fair. The son's house and the card are in the transcript. Note 14 is the accurate sentence in the file. The body has not kept up with it.

Closed item 2 banned three phrases. Those three are gone. The item also required a disc hedge, or a claim limited to what the stretch still shows. The hedge is now only "even if he walks into the next street," and two later sentences spend it.

**Fix.** Keep the location-note sentence and note 14. Write only the taxi monologue, the accident, the card, the son's house, and the rhyme in the lines. Do not call the first join selection, and do not say the taxi and the body never meet, until the disc says so.

### Finding 2 — "Outside the same door" fuses the Blue Bayou list with the Space stamp

**Section:** `'You seen Gary around?'` (Blue Bayou / stamp). Morning panel missed this.

**Claim.** After Steve is refused at the Blue Bayou, a man offers a hand stamp outside the same door.

**Quote.**

> Down at the Blue Bayou on South Congress a man credited as S-T-E-V-E with a van … The doorman charges him three dollars. … Steve is refused, and the only reason the door will give is that it does not see him on its list. … Outside the same door, a man offers a hand stamp — "I got a stamp. I think I can, you know, get you in"

**Why it damages this lens.** These are two admission events. **Verified** against the subtitle transcript this session:

- Blue Bayou door (lines 1354–1360): "What's up, dude? I'm on the guest list." — "Three dollars." — "S-T-E-V-E." / "Steve plus three or four." — "Sorry, Steve. Don't see you." / "You're not on the list."
- After the refusal, someone says they are going "up to the Space" for a performance (1379).
- Stamp door (1388–1404): "What's the cover?" — "Uh, I think it's five bucks." — "I got a stamp. I think I can, you know, get you in or something." Then the lick-transfer and "Five-dollar cover. — I got a stamp."

Three dollars, then five. A named second destination. The project's own scene ledger already treats the stamp as "the very next door." **Verified** this session (Film Commission; Gaughen item 21): the guest-list refusal is the Blue Bayou at 2008 S. Congress (1:22:54 in Gaughen). Neither published location page attaches the stamp to that door. Gaughen's next outdoor pin is the Congress/4th archway at 1:30:36. The Space may be indoors; the disc still owes the building. What the soundtrack will not carry is "the same door."

This is not colour. The paragraph uses one door to carry grant (list) versus property (stamp). That contrast still works as two moments in one afternoon. It does not work as two methods at one curb. A *Slacker* editor who has heard the covers will notice.

The rest of the door is good. Credit **S-T-E-V-E with a van**: **verified** (Gaughen; Film Commission, Steve Jacobson). Cover three dollars: **verified**. Block quote: both lines are in the transcript, in that order; "Figures" is still not set as the door's, correctly. The lick and the quoted objection are in the transcript. "Wrist to wrist" remains a compression of a lick-transfer the disc still owes as an image.

**Fix.** Keep Blue Bayou for Steve. Move the stamp to the next door, or to "the Space," and let the $5 cover do the work of splitting them.
