# Sentence audit — chapter v16 → v17 against the Poulaki model

Model: Poulaki, "Network Films and Complex Causality," Screen 55.3 (2014), read in full from the uploaded PDF. Every body sentence of v16 (191, split by script) was judged against the model's sentence habits and its own context in paragraph and section. Verdicts: KEEP or REWRITE. 27 rewrites applied; v17 is the result.

## The model's sentence profile (the standard applied)

Poulaki writes medium-to-long sentences with medial subordination; she links sentences with explicit connectives (however, thus, therefore, indeed); she attributes claims to named agents ("As Bordwell notes," "According to this model"); she rations the first person to thesis and section-turning claims ("I describe ... and argue," "I would argue"); she uses the rhetorical question sparingly, as a pivot, twice or three times in seventeen pages; she almost never writes a sentence under eight words, never writes fragments, never writes staccato pairs, never closes a paragraph on an aphorism. The AI-slop patterns hunted were therefore: fragments, staccato antithesis pairs, aphoristic snap-closers, anaphora, cleft one-liners, chiasm, and stock idioms ("runs deeper than," "is a history of exactly that," "the one that matters here," "X is one thing and Y is another").

## Model-structure usage ledger (guarding against over-borrowing)

Opening template ("aside from being ... can also be considered ... as its first reviewer wrote"): 1 use, sentence 1. "In this chapter I use X as an illustration": 1 use. "I describe ... and argue": 1 use. "I would argue": 1 use. Two-part question pivot ("does X play any role ... and if so what form does it take"): 1 use. "The viewer soon learns to anticipate": 1 use. Medial "however": 3 uses (sentences 23, 26-rewrite, 175), at the model's own density. "Thus": 1 use (R3). "Whereas": 1 use (R11). "Nonetheless": 1 use (R16). "Meanwhile": 2 uses (R1, sentence 142). Attribution verbs varied across five instances: wrote, adds, argued, describe, describes. Not used at all, held in reserve: "Indeed," "At the same time," "In this sense," "arguably," "seems to," "may be better conceived," second rhetorical question. No model sentence was copied; only shapes were borrowed, each at most once except "however" and "meanwhile."

## Residual deliberate deviations from the model

The model uses dashes and free passives; house rules exclude both, so colons, commas, and active constructions do that work. Sentences 47 to 50 (the thesis run) and 130 (the pivot) stay shorter than the model's average deliberately: the model itself drops to plain declaratives at thesis statements, and these are the thesis. "Sharing a city is not sharing a past" (87) and "somebody is always standing there to be asked" (149) are the chapter's two licensed refrains, each now occurring once.
