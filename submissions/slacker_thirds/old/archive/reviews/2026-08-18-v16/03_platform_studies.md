# Reviewer 3 — platform studies / law and political economy

## Findings

1. MAJOR — The no-address thesis is stated without the qualifications an informed reader will supply. "The counterparty that removes them has removed anyone who could be argued with"; conclusion: "Now that no one has to answer." Platforms have addresses: appeal flows, support channels, human-review escalations; EU P2B Regulation 2019/1150 art. 4 requires a statement of reasons for restricting business users; the DSA requires statements of reasons and complaint handling; the Platform Work Directive (2024) mandates human oversight of automated decisions; Washington State (2022) and Seattle have deactivation-review regimes. Note 25 itself concedes Amazon promises "visibility in the Slate portal." As written, falsifiable by a screenshot of Uber's appeal page. Fix: recast from no address to an address without an answerer of the doorman's kind — scripted, asynchronous, unarguable in real time (Rosenblat and Stark 2016 on templated support responses is the standard citation and is absent). One body sentence on the EU explanation rights and why they do not restore the doorman's kind of answerability (the reason arrives after the queue has moved; the rewind image already does this work).

2. MAJOR — The Amazon example is a category slip, and two examples cannot carry "the platforms that later operated this same arrangement." The doorman refuses a person access to a place; Prime Video Direct declines to license a title from a supplier — a merchandising decision about a commodity; the parallel act is account deactivation, not title non-selection. "In their own words" is true only of Amazon; the RDU/ALC report is a union's words. Fix: replace Amazon with a person-access example (Amazon Seller Central suspension language; Uber's deactivation page), or mark the case as the grant operating on things where RDU shows it on persons, and argue the continuity. Soften "operated this same arrangement" to "assembled a comparable arrangement"; map holding and selecting onto the platform side (Barns does the matching half), not only the grant.

3. MAJOR — The co-optation analogy inverts Stark and Pais. Their co-optation enrolls behavior in practices of algorithmic management; contribution is captured, ordered, valorized. The true-call hitchhiker contributes nothing the city captures, is subject to no management practice, and refuses enrollment. Calling Austin's holding co-optation strips the concept of capture and management. Fix: invert — the city holds WITHOUT co-opting; co-optation is what platforms added to the holding. The PixelVision party (contribution passed along under stated terms) is the legitimate proto-co-optation image; move the concept there and cut "hires him for nothing." Also revise the conclusion's "Stark and Pais describe the enrollment that later withdrew anyone who could be asked" — they describe no withdrawal of a person.

4. MAJOR — The chapter romanticizes the doorman's addressability into a quasi-accountability it never was. Club-door discretion is the paradigm of arbitrary, frequently discriminatory exclusion (dress codes as racial proxies; no forum beyond the bouncer); arguing achieved nothing and risked worse. Reich's procedural demands were aimed at human grantors — the spoken grant was his problem, not his baseline. Fix: a body passage severing addressability from accountability: Steve's ability to answer the door gets him nothing; addressability is a distinct property whose loss is a distinct loss. One sentence conceding a doorman's grounds could be no less undisclosed and considerably less contestable than an algorithm's.

5. MINOR — The body's Reich claim outruns note 21's disclaimer. "Performs that account in miniature" asserts Reich's account; two of the four acts (pricing, record-keeping) are not in Reich's account of largess, and Reich 1964 concerned government largess; the private-door extension is licensed only by note 22's Reich 1990. Fix: "performs, in miniature and in private, the structure Reich described in public law," and lift the Reich-1990 point into a body clause.

6. MINOR — Survey limitations disclosed in note 26 but not carried into the body. "Two thirds reporting deactivation" pools suspension with removal; the sample is self-selected via union networks. Fix: "a self-selected sample of 810 California drivers... reporting suspension or deactivation"; pair with a peer-reviewed corroborant (Rosenblat & Stark 2016; Rosenblat, Uberland 2018).

7. QUERY — Viljoen used accurately but selectively; "enrollment" is the chapter's word, not hers, and her own answer to "what it takes when nothing pronounces it" is democratic collective data governance, which cuts against the individual-address frame. One sentence acknowledging her remedy is collective, and that RDU is that remedy in embryo, would close the gap and strengthen the ending.

8. QUERY — "The four acts at Steve's door are all present" shows only two on the platform side (refusal, withheld reason). Deactivation supplies all four (commission taken, rating file, deactivation, null reason); map them, or claim only two.

## Verdict

The conceptual architecture — deferred third, Royce test, reach/selection split — is novel and well-documented, and the notes are unusually honest. But the platform half carries the payoff and is the weakest part: the no-address thesis ignores appeal channels and EU explanation rights; one of two platform exhibits is a category slip; the co-optation analogy inverts Stark and Pais; the doorman's addressability is upgraded into an accountability it never possessed. All repairable by promoting the notes' caution into the body, recasting no-address as no-answerer-of-the-doorman's-kind, and swapping or reframing the Amazon example. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS with a clear path to acceptance; do not touch the Simmel and Royce material.
