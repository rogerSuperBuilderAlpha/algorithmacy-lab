# Reviewer 1 — film studies (network narrative, American indie, film facts)

Method: every quoted line checked against sources/transcript.md and sources/scenes.md; literature checked against sweeps 1–2.

## Findings

1. MAJOR — The "borderline case" claim suppresses the one network theorist who accounts for non-converging films. "Theories of the network narrative ... require materials this film withholds. Their accounts of multi-character coherence run through convergence." Tröhler's *Offene Welten ohne Helden* offers "assoziative Kohäsion," topological rather than axiological, coherence without convergence, and the author's own sweep calls it "the literature's most Slacker-compatible answer." Bordwell's definition is hedged ("Usually these lines affect one another to some degree"), which the flat "require" ignores. Fix: cite and confront Tröhler in body or note 12; either show associative cohesion still fails to name the selecting instance, or restrict the borderline claim to convergence-based theories.

2. MAJOR — The relay/baton answer, the published position closest to the thesis, is never stated or critiqued in the body. Małecka credits "the fluid camera and a kind of 'baton-passing'"; Walters credits "fluid transitions and long takes"; sweep1 lists relay as answer #1 of nine. The chapter cites Małecka only for ethos and buries baton-passing in a note aside. Selective citation shading into straw man. Fix: state the relay position in the scholarship paragraph and distinguish the two-jobs thesis from it explicitly (who holds the baton between hand-offs; who selects).

3. MAJOR — The Steve scene is misreported in three respects. (a) Transcript: "I made him put me on the guest list, and I'm on the guest list" — Steve made the roommate put him ON a venue-kept list; nobody says the roommate wrote it. "A list he believes he owns" overstates; he believes he is ON it, which is exactly the grant-vs-property distinction the section wants, so the error dulls the chapter's own point. (b) "Spent the afternoon" and both later "the same afternoon" are wrong: the guest-list talk, van ride, and door scene sit in the night stretch (the venue empties "past 2:00"); likewise "By the middle of the day" lists night scenes (van, address, stamp, PixelVision). (c) The credit "S-T-E-V-E with a van" and "Blue Bayou on South Congress" appear in neither transcript nor scene inventory; note 3 concedes the disc check is open. Fix: correct wording, fix the day-arc, verify credit and venue or cut them.

4. MAJOR — "A selection no character makes" is contradicted by the film and by the chapter's own fourth section. The conspiracy talker singles out the Gary-asker ("Say, pal!") and a second stranger; the Old Anarchist deliberately recruits the burglar (scene notes: "the only scene where one character deliberately recruits another"); the stamp-holder selects whom to admit. The chapter itself concedes "by the middle of the day the people on screen close meetings themselves." Fix: reformulate as a claim about hand-offs specifically, and address the shadowing scene as the hard case.

5. MAJOR — "The cut" misdescribes the film's most characteristic hand-offs, which occur inside single takes. Many transfers are pans and tracking drifts within continuous takes; the Gary sequence runs continuously from café to sidewalk. Attributing selection to "the cut" hands hostile readers an easy formal refutation. Fix: use "the camera" (or "cut and camera movement") consistently; note that intra-take selection, unmarked by editing, is the strongest evidence for the selection thesis.

6. MINOR — The Gary scene's stranger-framing is undercut by the transcript. Reply is "I told you. No one's seen him for months" — the asker is a returning acquaintance and does receive an answer; what he leaves without is Gary. Fix: drop "passerby breaks in," acknowledge the prior asking, and rest the stranger claim on the sidewalk pairing, which the transcript fully supports.

7. MINOR — "Roughly one hundred unacquainted characters" overstates: long stretches pass among acquaintances (Paul's housemates, couples, the anarchist and daughter, Steve and the women). Fix: "most of them unacquainted with most of the others."

8. MINOR — "Nothing propagates" is falsified by the film's opening, as narrated two paragraphs earlier (hit-and-run → death, call, arrest across the scene boundary). Fix: "nothing propagates past a single hand-off" or "after the opening accident."

9. MINOR — Quotation trims without ellipsis. (a) "get you in" — transcript ends "get you in or something." (b) "You're in a hurry..." — transcript reads "I mean, you're in a hurry..." (c) "the two of them walk in free" — staged for a group ("And Rachael… you need to get a stamp also"; "We can get in free"). Fix: exact wording or marked elisions; "the group."

10. MINOR — The run-over remark is about his dreams: "Do you ever have those dreams… It's like there's always someone getting run over or something really weird." The irony (dream leaks into street) is stronger than the flattened version. Fix: "in his dreams there is always someone getting run over."

11. MINOR — The Paul hand-off conflates two speakers: the ranter is interrupted BY a second man who says "Uh, Paul's moved, man" and delivers "Why don't you come look? It's really weird." Fix: distinguish the roles.

12. MINOR — Drift critique fair to Boom, not to Soldani or Stone. Soldani has four positive mechanisms; the dérive names attraction by terrain, not destinationlessness; Stone's flânerie has positive content. The sharper fair critique: flânerie and dérive are first-person (the walker chooses); in Slacker the camera chooses over the walkers' heads. Fix: replace "mostly negatively defined" with the first-person/third-person contrast. Also confirm the Soldani quotation ("drifts along the street space...") at Imaginations 7.2, p. 82.

13. QUERY — Berg attribution rests on a tertiary digest and note 9 contains a leftover memo ("wording to be checked"). Check Film Criticism 31, pp. 24–26; confirm "never to revisit" is Berg's criterion; rewrite the note.

14. QUERY — Unverified: (a) Walters continuation "just fleeting glimpses of bohemia in its twilight phase" (only the first clause is verified); (b) "network filmographies" (plural) — verified only for Ciafone's list; Bordwell's filmography unverified, Poulaki verified absent; soften to singular or verify; (c) the Linklater channel-surfing quote and Marlovits "emphasis in the original" unconfirmed in the research files.

## Verdict

The central distinction is genuinely novel and the Simmel/Reich apparatus careful. But the survey omits its two nearest rivals (Tröhler; the relay), the strong formulation ("a selection no character makes," "the cut") is contradicted by the film and the chapter's own fourth section, and the centerpiece scene is misdescribed in wording, time of day, and the ownership claim the section turns on. All repairable; most fixes sharpen the thesis. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS, with re-review of the scholarship section and all film description against print sources and the disc.
