## Part 1 — Fit, pacing, film-forwardness, the six insertions

### Does it fit the collection and love the movie?

Yes, and more than the last draft did. The chapter now touches eleven distinct scenes and quotes the
film's own dialogue six times; the argument keeps returning to bodies on the Drag rather than to the
literature. Two moments read like an author who has watched the film many times and noticed things:
the taxi-dispatcher callback in §5 ("The very first scene of *Slacker* rides in a taxi, and somewhere a
dispatcher is picking fares off a radio") and the Video Backpacker who "can't forgive the scene its
missing rewind and wrong hue." A crossover reader who loves the film will feel loved back. The
sociology is carried lightly — Simmel, Burt, and Feld each get a sentence of plain English before any
term of art — and the three-question test in §2 is the kind of portable idea a collection wants one
chapter to hand the others.

### The six new insertions, one by one

I audited each for the assigned question: author seeing the film, or list being serviced? I also
checked every new film detail against `research/slacker_transcript.md` (subtitle-derived; the standing
disc-verification protocol still applies before print).

1. **§4 Ulysses (creek bridge).** Keep. One sentence that buys a lot: the film reciting "the chain's
   grammar once, out loud" is the author's frame, not the inventory's. Transcript confirms the quoted
   phrases (lines 643 ff.). Two notes: (a) the quotation currently carries no footnote — a film-studies
   volume will want a note identifying the passage (Joyce, *Ulysses*, "Ithaca") and the film as the
   source; (b) the disc check should confirm the reading against the subtitle paraphrase.

2. **§4 PixelVision.** Keep — this is the strongest of the six. "The device goes over to the crowd" and
   "A film that ran on a hidden selector ends by handing the selection out" are argument, not coverage.
   One precision error: "The movie's last image comes from **another such camera**" — the ending camera
   is a Super-8, not another PixelVision (scene inventory, seg. 35). A film-studies reader will catch
   the apparatus swap. Fix in Part 3 (R4). Optional, word-neutral upgrade: the film's own line "there
   ain't no film in that shit" (transcript line 1436) is better than the paraphrase "until somebody
   notices it holds no film," and a crossover readership will grin at it.

3. **§4 chaos monologue (Poulaki).** Keep, unchanged. "But somebody chose where his scene goes" is the
   whole rebuttal in seven words — the best new sentence in the draft. Letting the film voice the rival
   account before answering it is exactly what the July 3 inventory hoped for.

4. **§5 club trio (guest list, stamp, 2 a.m.).** Keep; it plays the §5 contrast in miniature and the
   licked wrist is a seen detail. "Steve's name" is transcript-confirmed ("Sorry, Steve. Don't see
   you," line 1356). But "**two get gamed on screen**" is loose: the transcript shows the stamp trick
   *succeeding* and the guest-list claim *failing* — Steve argues; the cousin games. Sharpened rewrite
   in Part 3 (R5) that is also more film-forward.

5. **§6 Video Backpacker + TV traveler.** Keep. Both quotations check against the transcript (lines
   1067, 502), and the traveler's framing ("when you get back, you can't tell whether it really
   happened to you") supports the chapter's "comes back from traveling." "Benjamin's claim, restated by
   the trained" is a closer worth its place in the aphorism budget.

6. **§7 pirate TV.** Keep the content, move the splice. The sentence lands between the real
   distribution chain and its summary line, so "Every link could be argued with, and was" momentarily
   reads as a claim about the *pirate* chain. One-slot move fixes it (Part 3, R3). "Kansas City" and
   the dub chain are transcript-confirmed (line 1119).

**Should any insertion be cut entirely rather than polished? No.** None reads as list-servicing; each
attaches to an argument the section was already making. If the editors force a cut in §4, the only
candidate is the hyperlink-cinema paragraph (not an insertion): its point — curation taken for
navigation — is half-made by the two-faces paragraph above it and remade by §7. I would still keep it;
"a hyperlink waits for you to click, and nobody clicks here" earns the 69 words.

### Is §4 now crowded?

Crowded but load-bearing — it holds. Ten paragraphs, roughly 1,450 words, but the section is the
chapter's heart and the three insertions all attach to existing paragraphs rather than opening new
fronts. The one pacing cost: the middle run (two-faces → Poulaki → hyperlink) is three consecutive
analytic paragraphs without a scene, the longest such stretch in the chapter; the chaos-monologue quote
inside the Poulaki paragraph is what saves it. The PixelVision paragraph then re-grounds the section
before the Linklater architecture quote closes it. Sequence is right; no reordering needed.

### Structural findings (ranked)

1. **The §7 three-questions callback is broken.** "So the three questions I've been circling aren't
   rhetorical" presupposes an enumeration that is not in the text. July 3 accepted item 14 recorded the
   questions as "seeded at the end of §1 now"; the current §1 ends on the thesis sentence with no
   questions. Worse, §2 *does* announce "three questions" — the host/engineer test (act on / path /
   read) — so the definite article sends the reader to the wrong triad. Cheapest true repair without
   re-seeding §1: rename §2's triad "three tests" (one word) and open the §7 paragraph "Three questions
   are left standing, and none of them is rhetorical." (R1.)
2. **Consecutive-closer refrain in §7.** Paragraph 1 ends "every link in it was a person you could
   name"; paragraph 2 ends "still a person you could name and fight." The same formula-phrase, twice
   running — house style's verbatim-repetition tell. The second instance is the sharper; rewrite the
   first (R2), which also removes a triple statement (that paragraph already opens on "human and
   reachable").
3. **§5 exits on five consecutive epigrams** — "watching one work" / "so does the next ride" / "One
   face composes; the other governs" / "meter running" / "in the open." Each is good alone; in
   sequence they are the landing-line drumbeat the July 3 cadence pass thinned elsewhere. Cheapest
   fix, zero words: merge the two-sentence "parallel is exact" mini-paragraph into the "symmetry
   breaks" paragraph that follows, which retires one exit by making it a mid-paragraph beat (R6).
4. **Forward reference in §2:** "Lofland names the method and the economists name the odds" — the
   economists (Duranton–Puga, market thickening) arrive only in §3. A reader hits "the economists"
   with no referent. Either accept the cataphora as a tease or trim to "Lofland names the method, and
   what I'm after is the seat itself." I lean trim; the odds-naming is §3's reveal to make.
5. **Prior-art and load-bearing claims:** sound. The Feld move ("sociology has a name for the mechanism
   if not for the seat") is stated at the defensible grain the July 3 panel negotiated; Poulaki is
   engaged at her level and now answered by the film itself; the equivocation (viewer as coordinated
   party) has its dedicated paragraph. A hostile referee's first target remains the §6 training claim,
   and the chapter already fences it ("That last step is a guess… only a real study could"). No
   specialist wince from this chair.

## Part 2 — Sentence-by-sentence slop audit (the editor's charge)

Method: every paragraph of the seven-section body, read aloud, against house style + the 2026
checklist. Flags are F-numbered with exact quote, tell, and rewrite in the piece's register; close
calls I cleared are C-numbered, because knowing what I did *not* flag is half the audit. Mechanical
baseline (confirmed): antithesis family 13 (~2.6/1k — under the <5/1k budget); em-dashes 58; 37
sentences ≤6 words; sentence mean 18.7, stdev 11.5 (healthy variance — no metronome).

### Whole-piece scan results (2026 checklist)

- **Copulative avoidance (#2):** zero hits ("serves as / stands as / marks / represents / boasts").
  Clean.
- **Inflation vocabulary (#3, #11):** zero hits (no pivotal/underscores/testament/leverage/etc.).
  Clean.
- **Trailing participial codas (#6):** one hit — "making sense of the algorithm together" (§6) —
  cleared: it names concrete agents doing a concrete thing, not unearned synthesis.
- **Fake balance (#7), elegant variation (#8), vague attribution (#9), canned closers (#10), boosters
  (#12):** clean. The piece repeats its load-bearing terms (host, engineer, third) instead of cycling
  synonyms, and every claim has a named agent — Speed, Rosenbaum, Pierson, Macor, Simmel, Burt,
  Obstfeld, Feld, Lofland, Goffman, Milgram, Long, Gaughen, Bordwell, Ramírez Berg, Poulaki, Stark and
  Pais, Benjamin, Hansen, Kuleshov, Beller, Perren, Smits, Frey. This is the house style's highest-
  leverage rule, kept everywhere.
- **The two tells this piece is actually prone to** (and where my flags concentrate): the anadiplosis
  pivot (word repeated across a clause or paragraph boundary as the joining device) and the epigram
  exit. Neither is a banned construction; both are budget items, and the budget is overspent.

**The anadiplosis census** (the uniformity tell only a whole-read surfaces). July 3 recorded this
figure "reduced to the one §3 instance." The current draft runs it seven times: *position…a position*
(§1), *plausible/plausibility* (§3, mild), *join/joining* (§3→§4 hinge), *balks/balk* (§4),
*carries/carrying* (§4), *carried/carriage* (§4), *breaks/breaks* (§5), *handed/handing* (§6). Partly
regression, partly new material. The move is good — that is why it multiplied — but at seven it reads
as the machine's one join. Keep the three best (*balks/balk*, which is genuinely evidentiary;
*join/joining*, which is the chapter's structural hinge; *handed/handing*, which enacts the handoff it
describes) and vary the rest. Note *carries/carrying* and *carried/carriage* are the SAME root twice
in one section — the §4 double is the must-fix (F5).
