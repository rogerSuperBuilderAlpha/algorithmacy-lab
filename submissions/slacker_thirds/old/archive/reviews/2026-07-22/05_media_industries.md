# Review 05 — Media Industries / Distribution History

Reviewer lens: indie distribution history and streaming economics (Pierson, Perren, Tzioumakis, Smits,
Lobato, Frey; *Slacker*'s own release record). Target read in full: `chapter/chapter.md`, ~4,998-word body.
July 3 triage honored: accepted items are treated as settled; declined items are not re-flagged.

**Verdict: minor revisions.** The distribution history is now accurate at every point I can check, and the
Frey/Lobato deployment in §7 is the best use of that literature I have seen in a film-studies chapter. The
single most important fix: the new §7 pirate-TV sentence asserts as pictured fact a route the scene only
promises — restate it at the one-link grain the film actually shows, and put the scene on the disc-check
list.

## Part 1 — Distribution history and industrial argument

- Dobie run from July 1990, roughly a year: correct.
- Orion Classics national release July 1991; the August 29, 1991 "Live Music Capital" resolution as "eight

- Gross "over a million dollars" (§1) and "$1.2 million" (§5, §7): consistent with the ~$1.23M reported

- Orion Classics' $100,000 advance, cited to Pierson: matches Pierson's own account.
- Orion bankruptcy "five months after the national release": Orion Pictures filed Chapter 11 in December
  1991; July 5 → December is five months. Correct.
- "its Classics team left to found Sony Pictures Classics": historically right (Barker, Bernard, Bloom,
  January 1992) — **but note this exact claim sits on the July 3 author checklist as "verified by reviewers
  but not by us," and it now appears in the body without a footnote.** The fact is safe; the sourcing is
  not yet yours. Barker's own Criterion piece (already in the bibliography, note [^6]/Barker 2004) may carry
  it; if it does, hang note 49's neighborhood on it; if not, this is a two-minute trade-press cite. Do not
  ship an unsourced founding claim next to a footnoted advance figure — a referee will notice the asymmetry.
- Filmography entry (Detour, 1990; Orion Classics, 1991; Criterion spine #247): correct.

### 1.2 The new pirate-TV sentence (§7) — overreaches the scene; the flag of this review

> "The film pictured such a chain — the Backpacker's contraband tape headed for Kansas City pirate TV by
> dub and acquaintance."

What the scene shows is one handoff: a dub handed to one man, who mentions Kansas City contacts. The
sentence converts that into a completed itinerary ("headed for") over a multi-link route ("by dub and
acquaintance" — plural links) to a stated destination the film reaches only in dialogue. Three separate
inflations of one on-screen event. This is exactly the failure mode the July 3 panel policed everywhere
else (cf. the "every transfer happens on screen" fix): claim the grain the film shows, report the rest as
what a character says.

And here is the thing the overreach obscures: the accurate version is *better for your argument*. The
chapter's point in this paragraph is that *Slacker*'s distribution chain was a sequence of reachable human
thirds — and that chain, too, existed at any given moment as one handoff plus promises down a line. Pierson
didn't hand Orion a finished national release; he handed them a print and a pitch. The film picturing one
link and a promise is the precise mirror.

Rewrite (ready to paste, +4 words):

> "The film pictured such a chain's first link — the Backpacker's contraband dub handed to an acquaintance
> who promises it to Kansas City pirate TV."

Disc-check items to add to the standing author checklist: (a) that the dialogue actually says "pirate TV"
and "Kansas City"; (b) whose tape it is (the sentence assigns ownership to the Backpacker); (c) that the
dub/handoff is on screen rather than reported. Per the July 3 rule, unverified film-content details are
author options, not assertions.

### 1.3 The compressed Miramax closer (§7) — fair to Perren, with two footnote-level cautions

> "…absorbed into a studio niche-market strategy, indie inside Disney, booming harder for the capture."

This is Perren's actual thesis at the right grain — the accepted "absorbed and accelerated" periodization,
compressed without distortion. "Booming harder for the capture" is true to *Indie, Inc.*'s Disney-era
chapters (the boom's peak years are the post-acquisition years). Keep it.

> "Miramax recut what it bought and shelved some of it outright, and a film it buried had no route around
> the third either — but that third was still a person you could name and fight."

Two cautions, neither fatal:

1. **Sourcing grain.** Note [^50] is a bare cite to Perren. The recutting is in Perren; the shelving/burying
   of acquisitions is documented more fully in the journalistic literature (Biskind's *Down and Dirty
   Pictures*, not in your bibliography) than in Perren's institutional account. Either confirm Perren's
   pages carry both claims (library check — add to the checklist alongside the existing Perren-adjacent
   items) or soften "shelved some of it outright" to "sat on some of it." The claim is true; make sure the
   note under it says it.
2. **The resonance of "name and fight."** Personalizing Miramax's gatekeeping is fair — Perren's
   institution had a notoriously personal bottleneck, and the July 3 panel already accepted the "Harvey
   Scissorhands" asymmetry concession. But a 2027 film-studies audience will hear "a person you could name
   and fight" through the post-2017 history of exactly that person being named and fought, in another
   register entirely. I think the echo *strengthens* the sentence — the nameable third was eventually held
   to account precisely because he was nameable, which is the chapter's contestability point — but the
   author should decide deliberately whether to let it ring unmarked. No text change required; this is a
   flag for intent, not error.

The compression itself is an improvement on longform: it keeps Perren's "reachable ≠ accountable"
distinction (which is the honest hedge the July 3 panel asked for) while landing the political contrast
with the algorithm. As distribution history, fair.

### 1.4 Streaming §7 — at the defensible grain throughout

- **Smits**: "sales agents and distributors persist, adapting to Netflix and Amazon as the powerful new
  buyers they now broker toward" — this is Smits's thesis stated correctly (gatekeepers persist and
  reorient). The July 3 reversal fix held; do not touch.
- **Specialty divisions**: "Most of the studio specialty divisions closed in a wave around 2008–2010" —
  the accepted "Most of" hedge held; matches Tzioumakis's pullback periodization; SPC/Searchlight/Focus
  survive the sentence intact.
- **Festival market**: *CODA* to Apple for a record $25M (Sundance 2021) and *Fair Play* to Netflix for
  $20M in "the chillier market after the 2022 correction" (Sundance 2023) — both figures correct and both
  inside the cited Bloom piece; the Neon/A24 outbidding clause keeps the market plural, which is what the
  July 3 fix demanded. Dated and defensible.
- **Frey**: "Viewers, it turns out, neither trust nor much use the recommendations. But the enclosure
  doesn't run through the recommendation." This is the chapter's quiet triumph from my lens. Most
  humanities uses of the algorithmic-curation story would have leaned on the recommendation engine and been
  wrong; this paragraph absorbs Frey's counter-evidence and *relocates* the power claim upstream to
  acquisition, licensing, and surfacing — which is Lobato's infrastructure argument used correctly.
  "It works in the plumbing, not at the interface" is the right grain. No overreach anywhere in the
  paragraph. (One micro-repetition flagged in Part 2.)
- **"The second enclosure, and the more complete one"**: defensible as written — the paragraph earns
  "more complete" through proprietariness ("Nobody is allowed to film the matching engine" in §5 sets it
  up) rather than asserting streaming monopoly. A hostile referee could note that classical Hollywood's
  vertical integration was the zeroth enclosure, but "that chain" scopes the claim to the indie chain of
  §7's first paragraph, so the sentence survives.

### 1.5 One small industrial wince (§7, optional)

> "Twenty-three thousand dollars to $1.2 million is what distribution looks like when its thirds broker in
> the open."

The $23,000 is the 16mm shooting cost; the film that grossed $1.2M had Orion's blow-up, sound work, and
P&A on top of it, and gross is not net to anybody in the chain. The sentence is rhetorical, the paragraph
has already shown the $100,000 advance (so the reader knows more money entered), and the essay register
tolerates it — I would let it stand, but flag it so the choice is conscious. If you want it bulletproof at
zero cost: "Twenty-three thousand dollars of production money to $1.2 million…" (+3 words against a 2-word
budget headroom; probably not worth it).

### 1.6 Load-bearing weak points, from this lens

1. The pirate-TV sentence (1.2) — a referee with the disc attacks it first. Fixed by the rewrite above.
2. The §6→§7 hinge — "cinema as training ground" → "the attention face now brokers what the trained are
   shown" — survives because §7 concedes the recommendation-skepticism evidence before making the claim.
   As written, it holds.

No polemic-for-mechanism failures in the industrial sections: every political claim (scoring, deactivation,
surge, proprietariness) is grounded in a cited mechanism. The prior-art posture is also clean — the chapter
claims no novelty about distribution history, only arrangement.

### Flags

**F1. §7, para 1 — the pirate-TV sentence.** Tell: unearned synthesis (the participle-coda family in
disguise — a claim inflated past its evidence). Full treatment and rewrite in Part 1.2. This is the
review's top flag in both parts.

**F2. §7, paras 1–2 — verbatim repetition at consecutive paragraph exits.** New since July 3: para 1 now
ends "…but every link in it was a person you could name," and para 2 ends "— but that third was still a
person you could name and fight." Two consecutive paragraphs exiting on the same phrase is the house
verbatim-repetition tell plus the landing-line drumbeat in miniature, and the second (the new Miramax
closer, which is the stronger line) is the one worth keeping intact. If the echo-and-escalation is
deliberate, say so in the margin and keep it; my recommendation is to vary the first, which also lets you
dissolve that sentence's paired-dash crutch (the Orion aside bolted in with two dashes). Rewrite of the
para-1 sentence (net −1 word, kills one dash pair):

> "The chain was fragile as well as reachable: Orion filed for bankruptcy five months after the national
> release, and its Classics team left to found Sony Pictures Classics. But every link in it had a name and
> a phone number."

"A name and a phone number" keeps the concreteness, hands "name and fight" exclusively to the Miramax
closer, and the colon-plus-clean-split is more in the Bordwell register than the dash sandwich.

**F3. §6, final para — double agentless passive.** "And where the second skill is being built now, it's
being built the hard way — platform workers pooling theories…" Tell: the house style's number-one tic,
`is being built` twice, with the agent deferred to an apposition. The agent is right there in the sentence;
promote it. Rewrite (net −1 word):

> "And where the second skill is coming into being, platform workers are building it the hard way —
> pooling theories of a dispatcher they can't see, making sense of the algorithm together."

(The trailing ", making sense…" coda is redundant with "pooling theories" except for "together," which
carries the collective point — kept.)

**F4. §6, Video Backpacker paragraph (new) — uniqueness claim and unverified scene detail.** "Its one
shut-in, the Video Backpacker…" asserts the film has exactly one shut-in; the credit list has at least one
adjacent recluse figure, and the uniqueness does no argumentative work. Tell: unearned precision. Rewrite:
"Its great shut-in, the Video Backpacker…" (net 0). Separately, "can't forgive the scene its missing rewind
and wrong hue" compresses the monologue into two specific complaints — the "much more powerful and useful
than an actual event" quote is safe (widely reproduced and presumably in the verified-quote set), but
*rewind* and *hue* are shot-level dialogue details of the kind the July 3 panel routed to the disc. Add to
the author checklist; if the disc gives only one of the two complaints, cut the other. The compression
itself is excellent prose — this flag is about verification, not style.

**F5. §4, §7 — vague attribution where the footnote knows the name.** Three instances of the
Wikipedia-guide "observers argue" pattern, each with a named scholar sitting in the note:

- §4: "Others answered that a movie still puts something in charge of what we see and when…" [^31] is
  Chatman. Rewrite: "Seymour Chatman answered that a movie still puts something in charge of what we see
  and when…" (net +1). This one matters most because Bordwell is named and his opponent isn't — the debate
  reads lopsided.
- §4: "Critics in the 2000s called films like this 'hyperlink cinema.'" [^35] is Quart. Rewrite: "Alissa
  Quart gave films like this their 2005 name: 'hyperlink cinema.'" (net 0). Coinage deserves a name.
- §5: "Scholars have spotted Simmel here before…" [^39] leads with Ajunwa. Rewrite: "Ifeoma Ajunwa has
  spotted Simmel here before, reading the platform straight out of his three-party form." (keep the
  footnote's second cite as is; net 0).

(§1's "Others credit the city itself…" [^7] is a survey-of-received-opinion sentence inside a paragraph
that names four critics; acceptable as variation — cleared.)

**F6. §7 — paired-dash crutch.** "The engineering third now standing over cinema itself — the training
ground — is the camera's own face…" The dash pair bolts an aside mid-sentence (the crutch pattern, not the
construct-naming pattern). Rewrite: commas — "The engineering third now standing over cinema itself, the
training ground, is the camera's own face, the attention face, grown a memory and a market, and it brokers
what the trained are shown." (net 0). Low priority; the sentence otherwise earns its density.

**F7. §7 — adjacent-sentence word repeat.** "By the time you reach the interface, the platform has already
chosen what got acquired, licensed, and surfaced. It works in the plumbing, not at the interface." Tell:
unintended verbatim repeat blunting the plumbing/interface antithesis. Rewrite first sentence: "By the time
you reach the home screen, the platform has already chosen what got acquired, licensed, and surfaced."
(net +1; "home screen" is also more concrete, which the house style prefers.)

**F8. §1.1 sourcing asymmetry** — the uncited SPC-founding clause; treated in Part 1.1. Not a prose flag;
a citation flag.

## Closing note

The chapter's biggest genuine strength, from this lens: §7 gets the streaming economy *right* — it absorbs
the evidence against the naive algorithm-power story (Frey) and still lands the enclosure claim by moving
it upstream to acquisition and licensing (Lobato, Smits, Tzioumakis, all deployed at their actual grain),
while the *Slacker* release chain is rendered with every figure correct. Almost nobody writing about
platforms and cinema does both halves honestly.

The one thing only the author can supply: the disc. Specifically the Video Backpacker scenes — whether the
dialogue says "pirate TV" and "Kansas City," whose tape changes hands and on screen or off, and whether the
rewind and hue complaints are both in the monologue. Those four details are the only places in the chapter
where the prose currently outruns what any of us has verified, and twenty minutes with the Criterion
edition closes all of them.
