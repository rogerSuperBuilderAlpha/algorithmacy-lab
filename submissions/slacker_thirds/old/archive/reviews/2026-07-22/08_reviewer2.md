## Part 1 — The claims I would reject the chapter over

Ranked most damaging first.

### 1.1 The PixelVision close is false at the level it claims, and factually wrong one level down (§4, "Late in the film the device goes over to the crowd…")

The paragraph's landing line: **"A film that ran on a hidden selector ends by handing the selection out."**

It doesn't. The party camera and the cliff camera are both *inside Linklater's film*. Every frame they
"shoot" reaches the viewer because the hidden selector cut it in; the handing-out is depicted, staged, and
placed — it is one more of the selector's picks. The selection was never handed out at all. The sentence
survives only as sound.

And the correct reading is *stronger* for the chapter's thesis, not weaker. An engineering third that stages
the devolution of its own device while retaining every pick is precisely the platform gesture §7 needs — the
feed that lets you "customize" what it still curates. As written, the paragraph concedes that the film
escapes its own mechanism at the end. Correctly read, the ending is the mechanism at its most complete: even
the image of your camera, flung free, comes to you selected.

Two factual problems underneath, both in material added since the July 3 triage explicitly declined to
assert unverified ending specifics ("Super-8 ending specifics… noted as author options rather than
asserted"):

- **"The movie's last image comes from another such camera"** — it doesn't. The ending camera is a Super 8,
  a different device from the PixelVision handed around earlier; multiple public accounts confirm the Super 8
  at Mount Bonnell and PixelVision in a club scene, not the ending
  ([Austin Film Commission](https://www.austintexas.org/film-commission/film-tourism-guide/slacker/),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slacker_(film))). "Another such camera" is checkably false.
- **"somebody notices it holds no film"** and **"at a party"** (vs. the documented club scene) — unverified
  diegetic details, entered against the triage's own stated policy. PixelVision records to audio cassette,
  which makes "holds no film" doubly suspect as phrased. Disc check required before any of this is asserted.

Rewrite supplied in Part 3 (R1). This is the finding I'd reject over: a new signature line that inverts the
argument, resting on an error the panel's own process was built to catch.

### 1.2 The club trio dilutes "engineering third" until it means any gatekeeper (§5, "So are the film's small engineering thirds…")

"A doorman holding a guest list that's missing Steve's name, a paid-entry stamp pressed onto a cousin's
licked wrist, a barman emptying the club at two" — run these through the chapter's own three-question test
(§2). What do they act on? The crowd at the door, not a pair. Could the encounter have happened without
them? They control *entry to the setting*, not any match within it. What do they read? A list, a stamp, a
clock — credentials and types, never parties. By the chapter's own rubric they score as *hosts* — the host's
boundary being enforced. Call them engineering thirds and the construct swallows every bouncer, tollbooth,
and ticket window in the world; the Uber parallel then loses exactly the specificity the abstract promised
("select, rank, and filter among parties that never negotiate directly").

The visibility contrast the paragraph actually wants — Austin's coordination machinery is filmable and
arguable, the platform's is proprietary — survives relabeling completely. It may even sharpen: *even the
city's gatekeeping* is done in the open. Fix is R2. Secondary: "two get gamed on screen" never says which
two, and all three shot-level details are unverified film content of the class the triage routed to the
disc-check author task. Same policy breach as 1.1.

### 1.3 The Poulaki paragraph refutes a stoner, not a scholar (§4, "the film even voices her account…")

Poulaki's account is not "there is no structure." It is that structure *self-organizes* from local
interactions and resonances — a formal claim about where coherence comes from, not a denial that it exists.
Quoting the breakthrough-day talker ("the underlying order is chaos") and then observing "But somebody chose
where his scene goes" is ventriloquism followed by a non-answer: somebody chose where every scene goes in
every edited film, including every film in Poulaki's corpus. She knows films have editors. The riposte lands
on the character and leaves her untouched.

The paragraph already contains the real answer, and it's good: "a theme rhymes across the cuts but doesn't
cause them… her account has no seat for the relayer." Rest on that alone (R3).

But the hostile version of her rejoinder should be faced, because the chapter arms it: the relay rule —
leave with whoever crosses the frame — is *local, content-blind, and iterated*, which is exactly the kind of
rule self-organization theory models. And §4's own viewer paragraph concedes the camera "can do its job
blind, reading nobody." Run the camera through the §2 test and it fails the third question — the pure
engineer "reads the parties themselves," and this one reads no one. The chapter never runs its own test on
its own two thirds. It should, explicitly, and own the miss: the camera is an engineer on obligatoriness and
pair-picking, a host on reading — and that asymmetry is precisely what §4 calls the attention face. What
would settle it against Poulaki for good is a disc example of the camera *breaking its own rule* — leaving
without a crossing body, lingering past a handoff — because a rule that can be broken has a holder, and a
holder is a seat. Only the author, with the disc, can supply that shot. (This folds into the standing
close-reading task; I am not adding a new one.)

### 1.4 The novelty claim in §2 dies against Mario Small (prior-art trap)

"What the broker literature never did was set the plain old setting beside the broker and hold the two to
the same test." Nearest rival: Mario Luis Small, *Unanticipated Gains: Origins of Network Inequality in
Everyday Life* (Oxford, 2009) — a book-length study of routine organizations (childcare centers) as
*brokers* of social ties, under an "organizational embeddedness perspective": settings whose routines make
ties among mothers as a by-product and which also actively connect people to people and to resources
([OUP](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/unanticipated-gains-9780195384352),
[author's page](https://www.mariosmall.com/unanticipated-gains)). That is a setting and the brokerage
apparatus held together in one framework, sixteen years ago. The "never" is dead.

The claim survives *narrowed*, and the narrowing is friendly: Small's move absorbs the setting into the
broker — the center is read as one more (organizational) broker. The chapter's move runs the other way:
keep the two apart, because a setting that brokers nothing is its own way of holding the seat. Cite Small,
say that, and the §2 contribution is actually cleaner than it is now (R4). The author should verify my
characterization of Small against the book itself before adopting — I verified the book's thesis from
publisher and author materials, not page-level text, and the harness rule (never adopt an unverified
correction) binds me too.

### 1.5 The Video Backpacker proves thematization, not training (§6)

"The film keeps this training on screen… Benjamin's claim, restated by the trained." The Backpacker is a
fictional grotesque the film frames as pathological. A character *about* mediated perception is evidence
that Linklater thematized the training thesis — it is zero evidence that 1991 audiences possessed the
skill. "The trained" equivocates between characters and audiences in exactly the way my accepted §4 finding
policed for viewer vs. character; the same discipline applies here. The chapter's actual audience evidence
is in §1 (box office, critics not lost) and §6's own testable-version paragraph, which is the most honest
paragraph in the chapter. Reframe the Backpacker as the filmmaker's self-consciousness (R5).

### 1.6 The pirate-TV sentence inverts the thesis it decorates (§7)

"The film pictured such a chain — the Backpacker's contraband tape headed for Kansas City pirate TV by dub
and acquaintance." The paragraph's thesis is a chain of *reachable, accountable* commercial thirds — rep,
theater, distributor, video store. A contraband dub headed for pirate TV is the opposite: distribution with
no accountable broker anywhere, closer to hosting (informal network, no gatekeeper) than to open brokerage.
One line of reported diegetic dialogue (itself unverified against the disc) cannot carry a distribution
thesis, and this one carries it in the wrong direction. Either cut it or invert it honestly — the film
*imagines the broker-free version* of the chain while riding the broker-full one (R6). Inverted, it earns
its place.

### 1.7 Internal contradictions (smaller, but a referee will find them)

- **"and all three are the same animal"** (§4) — three sentences after the paragraph establishes "a real
  difference" between the attention face and the labor face, and calls it "the key to the comparison." If
  the difference is the key, they are not the same animal. The kinship *ordering* (feed nearest, dispatcher
  farther) is what the triage accepted; keep the ordering, drop the zoology (R7).
- **"carry a whole film with nothing underneath it"** (§6) — §3 spent four paragraphs putting something
  underneath it: cheap rent, the crash, the census, the thick market. The sentence means "no plot"; it says
  "nothing." Also "the proof": one film is a demonstration, not a proof (R8).
- **"Lofland names the method and the economists name the odds"** (§2) — "the economists" haven't appeared
  yet; Duranton and Puga arrive a section later. Vague attribution at the exact moment the house style
  demands a name, and a forward reference the reader can't cash.
- **"Scholars have spotted Simmel here before"** (§5) — vague attribution (2026 tell #9); the names are in
  the note. Put Ajunwa in the sentence (R9).
