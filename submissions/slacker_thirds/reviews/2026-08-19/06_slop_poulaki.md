## 3. Part 1 — findings, ranked by damage

**F1. [103] (R16) says the opposite of what the paragraph needs — the one semantic regression of the
audit.** The rewrite reads: "The grant nonetheless passes from one person to another, and Steve can
talk back to the door even though the door owes him nothing." "Passes from one person to another" is
transfer language, and transferability is exactly the property the chapter reserves for the hand stamp
*against* the guest list five sentences later: "A stamp is property, showable and transferable between
strangers on their own initiative. The guest list was a grant, and only the door's own record could
confirm it" ([108–109]). The v16 original — "The grant is spoken to a person, by a person" — carried
the right idea (both ends of the *pronouncement* are human) in a shape the audit disliked. The merge
kept the shape-fix and lost the idea. Fix at P5. This is the finding that shows why applied rewrites
need re-reading: it passes every mechanical check and undermines the section's controlled comparison.

**F2. The zero-dash regime rests on a misquoted house rule, and the deficit is real.** The audit's own
deviations note says "house rules exclude both" dashes and free passives. The governing file
(`~/.claude/writing-style.md`) says the opposite: em-dashes are "allowed and load-bearing," explicitly
not an AI tell, not to be rationed "below roughly 3 per 1,000 words"; only the lazy parenthetical-aside
dash is cut. The brief repeats the correction. So the body's 0.00/1k — below every one of the 42
exemplars, band 0.35–10.88, mean 4.12 — is not a house requirement but an inherited error the audit
codified. The zero predates the audit (v16 was also at zero), but the audit turned an accident into
policy and then compensated: colons, comma chains, and semicolons were made to do the dash's work, and
the felt cost is a slightly airless, comma-laden texture exactly where a gloss should weld onto a term.
Where a reader feels it: [81] (a double colon — see F4), [163] (a "so that the person is grouped"
scaffold holding up what a dash appositive would carry), [5] ("takes the form of a grant, priced,
recorded, refused" — a list that lost its staging colon in R1 and got a copula-avoider in exchange).
Restorations at P1–P4 (P12 optional): four dashes, each also fixing an independent wart, bringing the
rate to 0.90/1k — out of DEFICIT and inside the corpus band. I deliberately do not chase the house
~3/1k figure: inserting dashes for the rate's own sake would be its own uniformity of optimization.

**F3. [161] (R23) cures an aphorism with an agentless passive — the house style's worst tic, installed
by a slop-removal pass.** "That such remedies were thought necessary is itself evidence of the vacancy
they address." Thought necessary by whom? The v16 original ("The remedies exist because the vacancy is
real") was an aphorism, but it was active and concrete. The rewrite trades the lesser defect for the
greater. Fix at P6.

**F4. [81] (R13) fused two sentences into one period with two colons.** "It is strict at a single
point: Royce writes acceptance into the definition itself, and he sets three conditions … and which
come to this: each self can stretch…" The second colon (introducing the three conditions) is doing
proper work; the first is the fusion's seam showing. Fix at P3, which also restores a short sentence
to a stretch the audit lengthened.

**F5. v17 is more uniform than v16 — the full argument is Part 2(c).** Summary of the numbers: same-
method sentence-length CV fell 0.695 → 0.589 and the under-10-word share fell 16.7% → 11.4%; semicolons
rose 24 → 31 (5.5 → 6.9/1k against a corpus mean of 2.39, max 8.62); ten of the 27 rewrites merged
sentence boundaries and none split one; three rewrites added or sharpened concede-then-gap clauses, and
the move now runs on all ten of the chapter's rivals. Fixes at P7, P8, P11 claw back the worst of it.

**F6. Small entity-drumming and verb losses in disturbed paragraphs.** [26–27]: "The drift readings
describe… What the drift readings mistake, however…" — v16's pronoun was right; the rewrite drummed
the noun (fix P9). [14]: R3 traded the live verb "leaked" for "moved" (fix P10). "Takes the form of"
now appears twice ([5], [94]); P1 removes the un-Reich-anchored one.

### (c) The over-borrowing verdict

**Ruling: real defect, moderate severity, and specifically repairable — but not where the question
expected it.** At the lexical layer the chapter has *not* become more Poulaki than Poulaki: the ledger
is disciplined, every borrowed shape sits at its claimed count, the reserve list is empty, and no model
sentence was copied. The rationing worked. The over-optimization is structural, and it shows in three
places a reader can feel.

**First, the one-way ratchet.** Ten of the 27 rewrites merged sentence boundaries; not one split a
sentence or created a new short one. Every repair pushed the same direction — longer, merged,
connective-linked — because the model profile ("never writes fragments, never staccato pairs") only
penalizes the short end. Measured by the same splitter on both versions: sentence-length CV fell 0.695
→ 0.589, and sentences under ten words fell from 16.7% to 11.4% of the body. (The official report's
v17 CV of 0.796 uses different quote handling; the *delta* here is method-consistent.) The prior panel
ruled the short-sentence profile genre, not defect, and warned that lengthening would damage the
chapter. The audit lengthened anyway, 27 times, and v16 → v17 gained 83 words in the process. What
survives of the short end — "Gary never appears." [123], "Neither man on the Drag claims anything."
[128], "A driver cannot rewind an allocation." [159], the [48–50] thesis run — is the author's voice,
and it survived by being marked KEEP, not by the method favoring it.

**Second, the punctuation compensation.** One repair move — merge on a semicolon — was applied in R8,
R21, R22, and R25 (and R1's [4]), driving semicolons from 24 to 31 (5.5 → 6.9/1k, corpus mean 2.39,
z = +2.85, near the corpus max of 8.62) while em-dashes sat at 0.00 against a band whose *floor* is
0.35. The harness is right that the ecosystem gets the dash rule backwards, and the audit's own note
shows the mechanism: it believed the house rules exclude dashes, and they do not (F2). A chapter with
the highest semicolon rate and the lowest dash rate in its register is wearing one mark as a
prosthesis for the other. Would a reader feel it? Not as a count — as texture: the gloss-bearing
sentences ([81], [163], [5]) read comma-laden and airless where a dash appositive is the genre's
native move. Verdict: real, fix by exchange, not addition — P1–P4 restore four dashes at sites where
each also removes an independent wart, and P7 returns one semicolon to a period. Both marks move
toward band without a single word added.

**Third, the exit drumbeat.** Concede-then-gap exits — rival stated at its strongest, then the gap
named — counted across the body: the relay [17–18], the shared ethic and the talk reading [20–23], the
drift readings [26–30], the formal literature [31], the refusal reading [34–39], the network theories
[43–44], Tröhler [45–46], the platform-in-triad literature [72–73], Feld [170–172], and the myth
reading [174]. **Ten exits; every named rival leaves the chapter through the same door.** Poulaki runs
the move on one principal rival (Bordwell, p. 384) in seventeen pages; the audit's Part C adopted it
as "the standard way of leaving every source," and standardization is precisely what a signature
cannot survive. The felt case is the intro, paragraphs 3–5: five exits in roughly 600 words, three of
them on the same hinge (concession sentence, medial "however," gap clause). The rewrites made this
marginally worse: R5 added a gap clause, R12 converted an aphorism into a gap sentence, R14 extended a
gap. The surface wording varies well ("says nothing about," "cannot join," "proves too much," "does
not arise," "never materializes," "has remained where Simmel left it") — which is why this stays
moderate rather than severe — but the geometry never does, with one exception: [174] leaves the myth
reading by *absorption* ("the arrangement described here is the thing the myth mythologizes"), not by
gap, and it is the best exit in the chapter. I propose no rewiring of exits (word-costly, and seat 07's
altitude); I flag the shape so the next revision varies one intro exit toward the [174] pattern, and P8
trims the flabbiest added gap clause.

**Is v17 more uniform than v16? Yes, measurably:** CV down, short-sentence share down, semicolons up,
ten merges and zero splits, three exits sharpened, and two thesis statements that v16 kept in
different shapes ([3–5] staccato, [47–51] staccato) now differ only because the audit flattened one of
them — its own residual-deviations note concedes "the model itself drops to plain declaratives at
thesis statements," a license it granted at [47–51] and revoked at [3–5]. By the project's definition
— uniformity of optimization — the audit made the chapter modestly more slop-like at the structural
layer while making it cleaner at the lexical one. The repair is P1–P11: every proposal either restores
variance or removes a defect the optimization installed, at a total of −23 words.

One residual shape the Poulaki pass never touched, logged as a locator only: the wh-cleft reveal
("What the scene preserves is…" [99], "What the platform withdrew is…" [153], "What the network
theories lacked was…" [180]) opens three paragraphs or sections with identical grammar, plus three
more clefts mid-paragraph ([27], [139], [173]). Each is load-bearing; six in 186 sentences, three as
openers, is a house shape worth an eye in the next pass, not a change now.

## 6. Verdict

- **The one thing only the author can supply:** the read-aloud pass the house file calls the last
  gate — specifically over the ten merged sentences, since four of those merges were made to satisfy
  a profile, not an ear, and only his ear can say which periods he wants back beyond the two I have
  argued for.
