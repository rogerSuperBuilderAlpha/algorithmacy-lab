## 3. Part 1 — findings, ranked by damage

### F1. The "same gesture" sentence cross-wires the chapter's own columns

The sentence, closing the network-narrative paragraph of the introduction:

> "The film asks after a man named Gary and declines to produce him, and I argue that this refusal and the platform's withheld reason are the same gesture seen at forty years' distance."

The chapter builds a three-column table and argues it well: the city holds, the camera selects, the door grants. The platform section then proves one identity act for act — the doorman's four acts recur "in the documents of a platform he could not have named." That proven identity runs door-to-door: the doorman's unexplained list and Uber's unexplained deactivation. It is the chapter's best stretch of argument.

The introduction claims a different identity. Gary's non-production is not a grant-act; by the chapter's own assignments it belongs to the other side of the scheme — the conclusion glosses it as a holding fact ("producing him would supply the one thing the arrangement never supplies," i.e., an introduction), and the agent who declines to produce him is the selector, the film itself. So the sentence pairs a selection-side refusal with a grant-side refusal, and the chapter's own vocabulary forbids the pairing: the doorman "administers a grant, which belongs to neither job." The equivocation has a second layer. The film's refusal is an authorial act on the telling, exercised over viewers, costing them nothing; the platform's withheld reason is a worldly act exercised over a livelihood. The chapter itself supplies the instrument for that disanalogy — "Addressability and accountability are distinct properties" — and never applies it to its own thesis sentence. Nothing between the introduction and the conclusion argues the identity; the conclusion simply juxtaposes the two refusals again, and its final image, "a city that will not tell them," quietly personifies the city to keep the symmetry standing — a city has no reason to withhold; the platform has one and withholds it. "Will not" is doing covert work that "cannot" would refuse to do.

The deep cause is that the conclusion already claims the right thing: "The platform keeps the charge, the list, the refusal, and the withheld reason, and withdraws the person who had to say them out loud." That is arrangement-identity, which the chapter earns and CLAIM.md licenses. Gesture-identity is an escalation the chapter never pays for. **Fix:** bring the introduction down to the conclusion's claim — assert the door-to-door identity the chapter proves, and let Gary emblematize the holding rather than the grant (R1). The locked thesis contains "keep" and "withdraw"; it does not contain "same gesture." The fix is inside the lock.

### F2. Half the coordination is textual, and one sentence claims otherwise

The introduction:

> "I would argue that the film's coordination is real, external to its story, and lodged in the world the story shows."

Two sentences earlier the chapter has dismissed Tröhler on exactly this ground: "All of these joining agents are textual, and the question of a worldly arrangement … does not arise." But the chapter's own second agent is textual. Austin is in the depicted world; the camera is not — it is the telling's instrument, the same ontological kind as Tröhler's Tyche. The chapter knows this at its best moment ("what in the depicted city keeps the next stranger available *for the montage to find*" — the worldly half serves the textual half), and the complaint against Tröhler is properly that she lacks the worldly half, not that textual agents are inadmissible. The sentence quoted above erases the seam, and the seam matters at the exact joint the thesis leans on: the platform's selecting is worldly — an algorithm assigning actual counterparties — while the film's selecting is an aesthetic act over viewers. Only the holding is shared across the forty years without remainder. Left unmarked, the seam is what lets F1's equivocation through.

The repair converts the seam into the payoff, which the chapter is one sentence away from: the film could only do in the telling what the platform now does in the world. **Fix:** R4 rewrites the overclaiming sentence as a division ("real and divided: the holding lodged in the world the story shows, the selecting in the telling of it"), and R3/R3b states the fusion in the platform section. Both are in the author's diction and both serve the locked thesis, which itself pairs a diegetic holder with an extradiegetic selector.

### F3. The Simmel–Royce joint is asserted, not built — and p. 321 is a standing counter-instance

The bridge between the two theory sections is one clause: "Royce supplies the exact test for it." Exactness is asserted, never shown. Why is a community of memory — Royce's thick notion, requiring each member to claim identical events — the right operationalization of Simmel's thin *Vereinigung*, a common relation to an outside point? On Simmel's own p. 145 sentence ("isolated elements are unified by their common relation to a phenomenon which lies outside of them"), the taxi passenger and the struck woman have a common relation to Austin — they are held by it — and by that sentence alone the unification the chapter denies would come for free. The chapter needs a criterion that blocks the trivial reading, and it has one on the shelf: claiming. Simmel's two examples pass Royce's test — each ally claims the standing threat as his own future (a community of expectation), each believer claims the same sacred history as his own past — and Austin's residents claim nothing. The chapter draws the contrast by example ("Whereas an enemy unifies those who league against it, and a God unifies those who worship, Austin … introduces none of them") but never states the principle, and without the principle the three sections read as recruited in sequence: a geometry, then a membership test, then a theory of grants, joined by the author's ordering. One sentence welds the first two (R2); F4 below welds the third.

The same weld disarms a counter-instance the chapter cites and never notices. The conflict-chapter thesis it reports — "a certain distance between the elements to be united and the point that unites them is an especially favorable condition of the bond" — predicts that distant Austin should *favor* union, and Austin produces none. As it stands the chapter hands a hostile reader Simmel's own sentence as a rebuttal. With the claiming criterion in place the answer is available: Simmel's distant points are objects of common orientation, faced and claimed; Austin is inhabited and claimed by nobody. Say it or be asked it.

Two verified findings ride along. First, note 19's German quotation silently truncates the sentence: the 1908 text continues ", insbesondere wo es sich um ausgedehnte Kreise handelt" — "especially where extended circles are concerned." I verified this against the first edition (see VERIFY): the passage sits on p. 321 as cited, the quoted portion is accurate, and the trim is unmarked. Chicago wants the ellipsis; the argument wants the clause, because Simmel restricts the distance thesis to *extended circles* — precisely city-scale collectives — which sharpens the counter-instance above rather than softening it (R8). Second, the "distance" the chapter attributes to Austin deserves one hard look: Simmel's third is far (a God, an enemy over the horizon); Austin contains its residents. The stipulation paragraph calls it "the far point over against the people it holds in one field," and a point that holds you in its field is not far in Simmel's sense — the deferral ground (no properly sociological interaction takes in all three) is satisfied by Austin only trivially, because a city cannot interact with anything. The chapter is candid that Austin departs from Simmel "at the instructive point"; it should be equally candid that what survives of Simmel in the construct is the outside position and the philology of the deferral, and that the work is done by the chapter's own reach-and-claiming apparatus. The weld sentence makes that apparatus visibly one machine, which is the honest answer to the borrowed-authority objection.

### F4. The grant is homeless in the scheme that needs it most

Twice the chapter parks its pivotal object outside its own architecture: "The doorman, meanwhile, administers a grant, which belongs to neither job," and, opening the penultimate section, "The city and the camera do not swap jobs, and the film's doors mark the boundary between them" — a sentence that never explains how a door marks a boundary between a holding and a selecting. A two-job scheme that needs a third category for the scene carrying the entire platform half of the argument reads as patched. The materials for the repair are again already present: in the film the three functions are distributed across three agents — a city, a camera, a doorman — and the platform is the same arrangement fused, one entity performing all three with no person fronting any of them. Stated once (R3), the doorman's "neither job" status becomes the point rather than the leak: distribution is what the film shows, fusion is what the platform did, and "two jobs and a third act" stops sounding like a scheme plus an exception.

### F5. The novelty note survived my attack, but it is defended by nobody

Note 36: "Reach and selection are this chapter's terms; no retrieved source pairs the city as reach with the cut as selection." I tried to falsify it. The nearest rival I could retrieve is Kracauer: *Theory of Film* names the street — extended to stations, halls, lobbies — as the medium's home ground of "haphazard contingencies," "a region where the accidental prevails over the providential" (pp. 62–63; VERIFIED via Elsaesser, see VERIFY). Kracauer pairs the city-as-reservoir with the camera — but his camera *records* the street's contingency and administers nothing; the office of selection is not in him. The city-symphony genre (Ruttmann's one-day Berlin) enacts the pairing without theorizing it, and its montage follows nobody — no hand-off, no reach between characters. Criticism of *The Phantom of Liberty* has the selection half in relay-and-abandonment vocabulary, which the chapter already engages through Macor's baton and answers ("a baton names the hand-off and says nothing about who holds the film between hands"). So the pairing claim stands after an honest attempt to break it. What does not stand is leaving it undefended: a reader in this venue will reach for Kracauer within a page of the abstract, and an absence claim that has visibly never met its nearest neighbor reads as unsearched. Name him in the note and mark the difference in one clause (R9). The same note also says "the cut as selection," which the chapter's body has renounced — closed item 20 made the camera, not the cut, the selector. The note regressed; make it "the camera as selection."

### F6. "This chapter ends by supplying it with a complement" — it doesn't

The promise is in the fourth paragraph, about the Strawsonian reading. The complement arrives three sentences later, in the same paragraph ("The characters can afford to be Episodic because something else is doing the joining"), and the conclusion never mentions Episodics, Strawson, or Bizarro again. A hostile reader checks promised endings. Either weaken the verb ("supplies," R6, word-negative) or actually deliver at the end (R6b: "The characters can afford to be Episodic; the arrangement cannot." — ten words before the final paragraph, and it closes the loop with the paper's other half). The first is safe; the second is better.

### F7. The legibility premise is an assertion wearing an observation's clothes

"…a genuine refusal of coordination would play as shuffle, and *Slacker* is legible at every hand-off; viewers who cannot say what the film is about can always say where they are." This carries the whole case against Marlovits, and it is a claim about viewers, offered without a viewer, a study, or a scene. The defensible ground is a film-fact the chapter already holds in note 9 — Ramirez: in each transition "the character who walks away participates in the next scene." Spatial continuity at the hand-off is what defeats shuffle; viewer psychology is a corollary, not a premise. R7 moves the ground into the body, but its universal form ("every hand-off") is checkable only against the disc — hence the AUTHOR-ONLY gate. Until then the sentence is rhetoric standing where evidence should stand, in the paragraph that dispatches a named scholar.

### F8. Two lesser items

The "hard case" defense — "What no character ever chooses is which use of the city's reach the film follows next" — is the only place where the chapter answers an objection with an abstraction instead of a scene, and the asymmetry shows. The concrete cash-out exists at the security check: the talker chose his listener; he did not choose that the film would then leave him — but whom the camera leaves with is a visual fact the subtitle transcript cannot supply (AUTHOR-ONLY; see VERIFY). And "the film's doors mark the boundary between them" should either say what boundary (the edge of the city's free reach, where holding becomes granting) or go.

Sections clean for this lens: the Royce exposition itself (the Tai-Nui material and the three conditions are accurately deployed); the Steve's-door scene reading; the Uber documentation, descent vocabulary aside (see Part 2, line 5).

## 4. Part 2 — seat-specific audit: the six lines of attack

**1. One construct or three adjacent lectures?** Three, as drafted — at the joints, not in the members. The Simmel–Royce joint is one asserted clause (F3); the Reich joint is a scheme-external "third act" (F4). Both welds exist in the chapter's own materials and cost under fifty words combined. The removal test: cutting Royce would lose the negative half of the argument — holding is not belonging (the Gary sidewalk analysis), enrollment is not membership (the Viljoen paragraph), and the licensed refrain "sharing a city is not sharing a past." Royce is load-bearing; the remedy is welding, not amputation. Verdict: the construct is one machine that reads as three lectures because two joints were never tightened.

**2. The most attackable sentence.** It does not survive as written, and the chapter never closes the gap — see F1. The equivocation is measurable in the chapter's own vocabulary (a selection-side refusal equated with a grant-side refusal), the disanalogy is armed by the chapter's own distinction (addressability vs. accountability, to which add: an aesthetic withholding over viewers vs. an economic one over workers), and the conclusion's "a city that will not tell them" personifies to cover it. The saving fact: the chapter *proves* a nearby identity — doorman's withheld reason to platform's withheld reason, act for act — and CLAIM.md's thesis licenses arrangement-identity, not gesture-identity. R1 retreats to the proven claim and loses nothing the lock protects. This is the single most important fix in the review.

**3. Do the two jobs stay apart?** The talker concession is not fatal, because the meta-selection defense genuinely works: characters select interlocutors inside the story; no character selects what the film follows, and in a plotless film that following is the only connective there is. But the defense is under-cashed (one abstract sentence where the chapter elsewhere gives scenes — F8), and the day-tour paragraph concedes that at night the joining is delegated to hand-able objects and merely ratified, so the camera's job is cleanly its own only part of the day. The scheme survives on the retained meta-choice. The doorman is the real pressure point: "belongs to neither job" is a patch as written and a feature only once the distribution/fusion sentence exists (F4/R3). Press released — conditional on R3.

**4. Novelty.** I searched to break note 36 and failed to break it: no retrieved source pairs the city as reach with the camera as selection. Nearest rivals, named and cited: Kracauer's street (*Theory of Film*, 62–63 — reservoir without the selecting office; VERIFIED), the city-symphony genre (the pairing enacted, never theorized, and without hand-offs), and the relay criticism of Buñuel (the selecting office without the worldly reservoir, already answered via the baton). The absence claim is falsifiable and currently naked; R9 dresses it.

**5. The Bordwell fence.** The fence itself holds: nothing in the Uber section claims the technologies explain the film's form, and "a platform he could not have named" actively blocks the reading. What the section spends beyond the fence is descent vocabulary: "ancestor" (the PixelVision camera), "what came next," "what happened once the enrollment was added and the person … removed" (a process narrated as history), and the introduction's "forty years' distance." CLAIM.md licenses priority ("the arrangement is older than the technology, which is the point") and locks "keep"/"withdraw"; it does not license inheritance, and no evidence of descent is offered — the platforms may have converged on the arrangement independently, which is all the thesis needs. The comparison's strength is synchronic ("act for act, in the documents"); R5 makes the Stark–Pais sentence structural at a two-word saving, R1 removes the "same gesture" escalation, and with R3's fusion sentence anchoring the frame, "ancestor" can stand as figure. The fence is honored; the section's verbs occasionally lean over it.

**6. Does the film cooperate?** On the transcript record, largely yes: the dialogue quoted at every hand-off resolves (the mechanical pack confirms all twenty), the café-to-sidewalk drift is continuous as described, and the security-check dialogue is as quoted. The two universal claims are the exposure: "legible at *every* hand-off" and viewers who "can *always* say where they are" are asserted, grounded at best in Ramirez's note-9 observation, and checkable only against the disc (F7). The film cooperates scene by scene; the universals are on credit, and the author already owes the disc pass that would settle them.

## 6. Verdict

- **Biggest genuine strength:** the Steve's-door/Uber homology, argued act for act from primary documents — price, list, refusal, withheld reason — with the philology of Simmel's deferral (p. 103/p. 321, verified this session) a close second. Both survive hostile reading intact.
