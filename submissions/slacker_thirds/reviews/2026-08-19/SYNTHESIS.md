## 2. Mandatory — these three cannot ship as written

**M1 — The "same gesture" sentence (introduction).** Seat 05's central finding, and it holds.
The chapter writes: "I argue that this refusal and the platform's withheld reason are the same gesture
seen at forty years' distance." Gary's non-production is a *holding/selecting*-side refusal; the withheld
reason is a *grant*-side act. The chapter's own three-column scheme forbids equating them, and its own
addressability/accountability concession arms the disanalogy. The chapter nevertheless *proves* a nearby
identity act for act — the doorman's unexplained list against Uber's unexplained deactivation — and
`CLAIM.md` and the conclusion already state that defensible version. Retreat to what is proved:

> The film asks after a man named Gary and declines to produce him — the one introduction the
> arrangement never makes — and I argue that the doorman's unexplained list and the platform's
> unexplained deactivation are one act, thirty-five years apart, with nobody left to pronounce it.

(+12, and the body's first em-dash pair. Seat 05's text with C5's date correction folded in.)

**M2 — The Steve door scene is misreported, three times.** Seat 01, verified against the transcript.
The chapter says the doorman "charges him three dollars," that he is "charged, checked, and turned away,
and arguing leaves him three dollars poorer," and that the doorman "charges him, checks a list, turns him
away." The transcript (lines 1354–1371) shows the door quoting "Three dollars" as the **cover price**,
because the guest list does not cover him; **no payment appears**; his own party goes in; and Steve says
"Couldn't get in." The grant was refused while priced entry stayed open. The correction is *better* for
the argument: it sharpens the stamp-versus-grant contrast the same section draws, because the commodity
stayed available exactly where the grant failed. Whether money changes hands off-dialogue is a disc
question and stays in VERIFY.

**M3 — Prior art sits on a page the chapter already cites.** Seat 03. Rosenblat & Stark, *IJoC* 10
(2016), at **3771** — the page note 33 already uses — reads: "there is no managerial correspondent
empowered with the role of 'foreman' to mediate drivers' frustrations." That is the chapter's vacancy,
named, on a page it cites for something else. Left alone it is the worst exposure in the piece; cited in
the note, it becomes corroboration at **zero body words**. Seat 03's wider sweep found the vacancy
half-named repeatedly (Gray & Suri's "no one at the helm," Tarafdar's absent dialogue, Almada, Bovens,
Van Loo) and **nobody** pairing it with a door, with Simmel, or with an act-for-act mapping. The novelty
survives; it needs to be defended rather than asserted.

## 3. Collisions, adjudicated

The two model axes were set at different altitudes precisely so their advice could be told apart. They
collided twice, and both collisions are informative rather than a wash.

**X1 — ¶1's verb. Seat 06 (Poulaki, sentence) vs seat 07 (McLoughlin, paragraph).**
Both rewrite "access takes the form of a grant." Seat 06 proposes "access **arrives as** a grant —
priced, recorded, refused…"; seat 07 proposes "access **is** a grant, priced, recorded…".
**Seat 07 wins the verb, seat 06 wins the punctuation.** "Takes the form of" is copula avoidance, a named
tell on the project's own 2026 checklist — and "arrives as" is *also* copula avoidance, so seat 06's fix
does not clear its own rule. But seat 06 is right that the sentence wants a dash, and restoring one here
starts clearing the Tier 3 deficit. Merged:

> At the film's doors, meanwhile, access **is** a grant — priced, recorded, refused, and left
> unexplained by a person who can still be argued with.

The same ruling applies to ¶1's opening: "can also be considered a coordination puzzle" is the same tell,
and seat 07's "**is** a founding document … **and it is also** a coordination puzzle" fixes it.

**X2 — ¶6. Seat 05 (argument) vs seat 07 (structure).** Seat 07 compresses the paragraph to kill the
duplicate thesis landing and name the deferred third; seat 05 rewrites its first sentence because
"lodged in the world the story shows" overclaims — the camera is textual, which is the exact ground on
which the chapter fences Tröhler. **Both are right and they compose.** Take seat 05's correction as the
opening sentence and seat 07's compression for the rest:

> I would argue that the film's coordination is real and divided: the holding lodged in the world the
> story shows, the selecting in the telling of it — the deferred third of my title. Austin does the
> holding, the camera does the selecting, and at the doors a person still pronounces the grant.

Net −19, and it fixes a duplicate landing, a missing title concept, and a diegetic overclaim at once.

**X3 — Not a collision: the em-dash deficit vs the semicolon spike.** Seat 06 owns this and the ruling
stands. Zero em-dashes in 4,470 words is below all 42 corpus exemplars; semicolons run 7.01/1k against a
mean of 2.39. The cause is documented — the v17 audit wrote "house rules exclude" dashes, which
**misreads `~/.claude/writing-style.md`**, a file that calls em-dashes load-bearing, explicitly not an AI
tell, and says not to ration them below ~3/1k. Restoring four dashes at sites that each independently
need repair takes the chapter out of DEFICIT at a net −23 words.

## 7. What the panel did not find

No fabricated quotation: all 20 dialogue quotations resolve against the transcript. No apparatus fault:
39 markers, 39 notes, no duplicates, no orphans either direction, sequential first use. No verbatim ban,
no rate tell over budget. No reviewer questioned the thesis, and the register held under eight readers.

**The strength, on which seats 01, 04, 05 and 07 independently agree:** Steve's door as the middle term.
"The four acts at Steve's door recur, act for act, in the documents of a platform he could not have
named" is the most publishable move in the chapter, it is better than the camera–algorithm analogy the
editors accepted, and — with M2's correction and M3's citation — it survives hostile reading intact.

**The honest summary of the slop question.** Sentence by sentence, v17 is clean. What the two slop seats
found is that the *repair* made it more uniform: ten merges and zero splits, sentence-length CV
0.695 → 0.589, short sentences 16.7% → 11.4%, semicolons 24 → 31, em-dashes to zero, the thesis landing
whole twice, one clause landing seven times, and a fix that installed the only agentless passive in the
chapter. Uniformity of optimization is this project's own definition of slop. The chapter did not get
sloppier; it got more evenly optimized, which is the same thing wearing better clothes.
