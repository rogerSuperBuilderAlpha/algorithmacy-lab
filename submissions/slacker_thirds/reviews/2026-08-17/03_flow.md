# Flow / cohesion — *'You seen Gary around?': The Deferred Third of* Slacker (v9)

**Venue:** Bloomsbury, *Slacker: Answering the True Call — Essays on Linklater's Cult Classic*  
**Standpoint:** cohesion seat only. Tail-head, end-focus, paragraph hooks, section handoffs, drumbeat nouns, list-versus-walk, and semantic restatement. Not a synonym hunt. Not an argument review except where a join failure hides a claim. Mechanical locators from the uncalibrated `slacker` register (2026-08-17) are treated as locators, not verdicts.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

The register is a first-person film-studies collection chapter in the Hansen/Bordwell manner. "I" steers; scenes before theses; contractions; pointed concessions. Scene-first openings are legitimate. A paragraph that cuts with the film — taxi line, then a different street — is a scene-cut, not a buried lead. I will not propose turning those cuts into topic sentences.

What I judge hard:

- Whether sentence N leaves a handle and sentence N+1 picks it up, or whether the reader has to start again.
- Whether a paragraph close is a forward hook or an epigram that shuts the door on the next stretch.
- Whether Simmel → Royce, Origins → film, film → reach/selection, and reach → conclusion are handoffs or resets.
- Whether Reich/door, Gary/Royce, reach/selection, and algorithmacy are introduced once and carried, or re-taught.
- Whether the same conclusion re-lands in different words (intro, Origins close, film-section close, reach/selection, conclusion). Lexical checkers miss this; it is the chief job.

What I will not treat as defects: first person; contractions; quoted dialogue; a short sentence after a dense build; 0% citation-final sentences in an endnote chapter; the film's own cuts rendered as paragraph cuts; Annals diction applied to a film essay.

The locators (tail-head 39.1%; 81.2% of paragraph joins share no entity; 24.6% of sentences under 10 words) told me where to look. I read the joins. Most of the 81% is real. A few of those entity-less joins are the film working. I say which.

## Part 1 — Findings, ranked by damage

### 1. The umbrella sentence re-lands four times

**Claim.** The chapter's portable conclusion — a third that holds strangers available and arranges none of their meetings; a face you can still argue with; the platform drops it — is delivered in the opening, mid-film, at the reach-section close, and again in the conclusion, with only the nouns swapped. That is semantic restatement, not reformulation. Reformulation compresses a finding so later sentences can carry the compression. Restatement re-argues the finding so each section can have its own landing.

**Quote — three landings of the same sentence.**

Opening:

> A platform holds strangers available to one another and arranges none of their meetings.

Film section, after the taxi / hit-and-run:

> The echo is the arrangement at work: a third that holds strangers available to one another and arranges not one of their meetings.

Conclusion:

> The opening cut, the club door, and the day walked underneath them show one third holding strangers within reach and arranging none of their meetings.

The face-variant is the same conclusion in a second diction:

> *Slacker* already has contribution without a contract on the street, and a third that still has a face. The day lets us watch, and practice, how to argue with that third. (opening)

> The film adds the face to Reich's diagnosis. … They drop both the person who can state the rule and the stamp that strangers could copy among themselves. (film)

> The film keeps a third you can argue with. The platform removes it. The competence that the day in Austin demands of its participants … is the way to make the most of a third that has dropped its face. I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset. (conclusion)

**Why it damages cohesion.** A reader who has accepted the opening already has the finding. Each later landing spends the end-focus of a paragraph on a sentence the reader can recite. The local predicates that *are* new — the unnoticed echo; Royce's yes failing in the café; the four powers at the door; reach ≠ selection; the joins changing hands — get used as evidence for the umbrella instead of being allowed to close their own stretches. The conclusion then restates the opening rather than advancing it. The one sentence in the conclusion that is not a restatement is the role-assignment ("Simmel describes the structure, Royce the membership test, Reich the grant, Stark and Pais the co-optive enrollment"). Everything around it is the opening again.

**What would fix it.** Keep the umbrella in the opening. Let the film-section close on the unnoticed echo, or on the joins changing hands, or on "the inclusion waits for nobody's yes" — those are this section's findings. Let the conclusion keep the role-assignment and the Gary return, and cut the third recitation of "holding / arranging none." Algorithmacy can be used as a subject once in the film section so the conclusion does not have to re-introduce it with "I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset."

### 2. Origins lands on the film, then yanks the reader back to the literature — three times — and Simmel → Royce is a reset

**Claim.** The Simmel stretch is the chapter's best internal chain. The paragraph joins around it are the chapter's worst habit: a paragraph earns a film landing, and the next paragraph opens on a scholar, a page number, or a new problem as if the landing had not happened. The Simmel → Royce turn is the largest of those resets.

**Quote — the three abandoned landings, then the Royce open.**

P3 close → P4 open:

> The man in from the bus station and the people standing around a woman struck in the street occupy one city and no relation whatever to one another.
>
> The phrase occurs exactly once in the 1908 *Soziologie*, at the postponement.

P4 close → P5 open:

> The cut from the taxi to the hit-and-run inherits that omission: two parties and a city, never three terms in one field.
>
> Gesa Lindemann treats the absent third as the precondition of any durable dyadic order, not an addition to it.

P5 close → P6 open:

> The taxi is already moving before anyone in *Slacker* has met anyone.
>
> Network-brokerage research took the pair-brokering thirds from the triad chapter.

P6 close → Royce:

> Neither the taxi nor the woman struck in the street is a node in anybody's network.
>
> Royce's problem is how strangers become members.

**Why it damages cohesion.** Each of those closes puts the reader on the sidewalk. Each of those opens starts a new lecture. The heading ("Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe") told the reader Royce was coming, so the reset is not a surprise. It is still a reset. Nothing in the Royce opener names the taxi, the woman, the deferred third, or the failed network reading. The two problems are adjacent: strangers who share a city and no relation; strangers who have not each claimed a past. The prose never says they are adjacent. The reader has to do that join.

The Lindemann / Fuhse / Neuberger / Koschorke paragraph is a one-finding-per-sentence run that ends by snapping back to the taxi. That last sentence is a good hook. The next paragraph ignores it.

**What would fix it.** Do not land on the film unless the next paragraph stays there or names the landing as it leaves. The philological paragraph ("The phrase occurs exactly once") should open on the postponement, not follow a film close; move the Austin sentence to the end of the conflict-chapter paragraph, where the cut already inherits the omission. Open Royce on the same two parties, or on the fact that a city that holds them is not yet a community. If the literature parade stays, subordinate it: one sentence for the reception, then the taxi. Do not emit four scholars and a film snap.

Simmel → Royce is a reset, not a join. The heading does some work. It is not enough.

### 3. Origins closes on Gary; the film section is titled for Gary; the film section then opens on the taxi

**Claim.** The most expensive section-to-section handoff in the chapter. Origins earns Gary as the Royce test. The heading quotes him. The first sentence of the section sends the reader back to a scene Origins has already used twice.

**Quote.**

Origins close:

> When a stranger walks into a café on the Drag and asks whether anyone has seen Gary, the room he is asking fails every part of it.

Heading:

> 'You seen Gary around?' – The Deferred Third of *Slacker*

Film-section open:

> The film opens twice on a street within four minutes, and both times the deferred third is already there.

**Why it damages cohesion.** The reader was just put in the café. The heading confirms that is where we are going. Then we are at the bus station, which Origins P1 and P3 already described. Gary does not return for five paragraphs, and when he does he arrives as a hard cut from the platform sentence (Finding 4). The film-section open is a good sentence. It is the wrong sentence for this join. Either Origins should not close on the café, or the film section should open by saying the café comes later and the deferred third was already in the first four minutes.

This is not a scene-cut. A scene-cut would be the film's own cut, which the chapter handles well two paragraphs later ("Then the film is on a different street"). This is a handoff that drops its own hook.

**What would fix it.** Two clean options, not both. (A) Close Origins on the test, not the café: a day cannot meet it; the film will put a name to the failure; before it does, it opens twice on a street. Then the current film-section open is the right next sentence. (B) Keep the Gary close and open the film section on the delay: the café question comes late; the film opens twice first. Either weld is one sentence. The present join costs more than that sentence would.

### 4. Constructs are carried unevenly: Reich and Royce-applied are carried; algorithmacy and reach/selection are re-taught or dropped; Gary arrives from a platform sentence

**Claim.** The chapter knows how to carry a term. It does it with Royce's yes and with Reich's diagnosis. It does not do it with algorithmacy, with reach/selection, or with the Gary entrance inside the film section.

**Quote — the good carry, then the failures.**

Royce, carried (not re-taught):

> They are contemporaries, and they are in communication, so Royce's second condition holds on the street … The third condition fails, and the acceptance built into it.

and later:

> An app assigns a queue position whether or not the driver assents to it, and the inclusion waits for nobody's yes.
>
> Because Royce makes acceptance constitutive, the app's assignment is not membership.

Reich, introduced at the door (right place, gloss welded to the scene):

> Steve is refused without a reason, and the refusal has a shape that Reich had described twenty-six years earlier.

Then carried:

> The film adds the face to Reich's diagnosis.

Algorithmacy, welded in the opening, then absent until the conclusion re-cites the opening:

> I call fluency with determinations that arrive without reasons, and without anyone to answer them, algorithmacy.

> I called that competence algorithmacy at the outset.

Reach/selection: used as verbs in the Gary paragraph ("held both men within reach" / "the cut selected"), then announced as a strict pair in a one-sentence section open, then taught from Feld, then taught again from Warner and from Duranton & Puga. The gloss of "reach" arrives mid-paragraph, after the heading and the claim have already spent the word:

> …what remains is the bare holding I have been calling reach.

Gary's entrance inside the film section, after a platform close:

> They drop both the person who can state the rule and the stamp that strangers could copy among themselves.
>
> Gary is missing, and nobody in the room can answer for him.

**Why it damages cohesion.** Algorithmacy is the chapter's coined term. Gloss-welding it in paragraph 3 and then not using it as a subject until the last paragraph means the conclusion has to point back ("at the outset") instead of using the word. That is a term-then-thread failure. Reach and selection are this chapter's other coined pair (note 23 says so). They arrive as if already strict, get a late gloss, and are then re-explained by two more literatures in the next paragraph. The conclusion never uses "selection" as a noun. Gary, already earned at the Origins close, is treated as a new scene with no weld from the door/platform paragraph that precedes him. "Nobody in the room can answer" faintly echoes "the person who can state the rule," but Gary is the Royce case, not the Reich case, and the prose does not say so until two paragraphs later.

The conclusion then re-teaches Royce and Reich from scratch:

> Royce made acceptance part of the definition of community, so membership waits for each member's yes. Reich described the legal form of a dependence that the Blue Bayou's doorman was exercising in person.

Those sentences were already in the chapter. They are not reformulations. They are the introductions again.

**What would fix it.** Use algorithmacy once as a subject in the film section — after the door, or after the joins said out loud — so the conclusion can keep the word without the pointing-back clause. Gloss-weld reach and selection in the section's first sentence, and let Feld do one job (reach is not a focus) rather than also running Warner and Duranton & Puga as a second teaching of the same split. Open the Gary block on the fact that the film still has a room that can be asked. In the conclusion, keep the role-assignment; cut the re-definitions.

### 5. The long film section is a walk, except for one list; Reach/Selection is a list

**Claim.** The brief asked whether the long film section is a walk or a list. It is a walk. The taxi, the body, the echo, Steve, the stamp, Gary, the joins changing hands — those paragraphs move. One paragraph in that section is a list, and the whole Reach/Selection section is a list.

**Quote — the walk that works.**

> Through the rest of the day that holding keeps working, while the mechanism of the joins changes hands. Early on the cut does the joining … By the middle of the day the people on screen have taken the joins over, and they do it with things they can hand across and sentences they say out loud – a lift … an address … Steve's van … the stamp … a PixelVision camera … By dawn the cut has resumed … When the cut is doing the joining there is nothing handed over and nobody addressed.

Three temporal stations (early / middle / dawn), the middle grouped under "things they can hand across and sentences they say out loud." That is list-handling done right. The opener ("that holding keeps working, while the mechanism of the joins changes hands") is a forward hook the paragraph then walks. This is the chapter's best cohesion.

**Quote — the list that does not.**

> The same powers, once they drop the face, are written down. Amazon's Prime Video licensing documentation states … Rideshare Drivers United … found two-thirds reporting deactivation … The four powers that the Blue Bayou's doorman exercised in person … now attach by rule. The Video Backpacker gets at the loss from the other side. … In the three years after the film's release the same form appeared in finance, where Reuters Dealing 2000-2 and EBS …

Amazon and the drivers are one point (the powers written down). The Backpacker is another (you cannot rewind an allocation). The FX venues are a third (the form follows the film by a year or three). Three findings stacked as if the first sentence covered them. "The same powers" also reaches back past the labour paragraph to the door, so the opener is a long-distance hook the reader has to retrieve.

Reach/Selection, after a one-sentence claim, is scholar-first twice:

> The film keeps a strict division of labour between reach and selection, its two thirds.
>
> In 1981 Scott Feld asked how people come to know one another without a matchmaker.

Then, after the city/cut epigram:

> *Slacker* never lets the city do the cut's job, or the cut do the city's.
>
> Michael Warner's account of a public constitutes strangers as a collectivity precisely through their common relation to something outside them …

Then:

> The film shows those functions performed by visibly different agents, a city and a camera.
>
> Linklater refuses to resolve.

**Why it damages cohesion.** Feld, Barns, Warner, Duranton & Puga are four sources for one split the film has already shown. The section's job, for this seat, is to name the split and keep walking the film. Instead it teaches the split, then teaches it again, then epigram-closes, then starts a new paragraph on "Linklater refuses to resolve" with no pickup from "a city and a camera." "Resolve" is in the heading, not in the previous close. The heading is doing the join. The paragraphs are not.

The film-section walk is not the problem. Do not "fix" it into a tighter thesis sequence. The list paragraph and the Reach section are the problem.

**What would fix it.** In the powers paragraph, keep Amazon and the drivers under "written down"; give the Backpacker and the FX venues their own sentence of purpose, or cut one. In Reach/Selection, open on the split with the gloss welded on; let Feld mark what Austin is not; then go to the film's refusal. Warner and Duranton & Puga can live in a note. "Linklater refuses to resolve" should open on the split it is refusing to collapse, not on the director's name as a reset.

## Part 2 — Seat-specific audit

Section by section. I mark which locator-flagged joins cost the reader and which are legitimate scene-cuts. I do not manufacture a problem where a section is clean.

### Opening (untitled)

Five paragraphs. The first locator was right to point at the first join and wrong to treat it as empty.

**P1 → P2.** Close: "The inside of that experience is doomscrolling." Open: "The same drift is how a platform works."

"The same drift" is a backward hook. It does not pick up the end-focus. P1 ends on *doomscrolling* (the interior of the experience). P2 opens on *drift*, which was Soldani's verb two sentences earlier. The reader has to step back over the last sentence to find the handle. That costs a beat. It is not a scene-cut: it is a claim that the spectator's interior and the platform's mechanism are the same movement. The claim may be right; the join still skips the word it just landed on. Moderate cost. Do not rewrite P2 as a thesis about doomscrolling unless you mean to keep doomscrolling as a thread, which the chapter does not.

**P2 → P3.** Close: "the triadic world of the platform." Open: "In 2020 David Stark and Ivana Pais described platform owners…" Year-first, name-first, no pickup. The platform is in the sentence but not in the head. Mild cost. Ordinary scholarly insert. The paragraph then welds Slacker's street to their co-optation and gloss-welds algorithmacy — that weld is clean.

**P3 → P4.** Close: "that is its use for making the most of the world the platforms inherited." Open: "David Bordwell has warned against treating multi-protagonist films as symptoms of the internet."

This one costs. The reader just received algorithmacy and its use. Bordwell arrives as a new agent with a new warning. No entity shared. After the warning, P4 restates P2 ("Platforms inherited a third that was already on the sidewalk"). The Bordwell paragraph is a concession that breaks the thread between the coined term and the organisation-theory landing. If the concession stays, it needs a pickup: the film is one of those older constructions, and still the sidewalk is the case. Right now the first half of that is there and the join is not.

**P4 → P5.** Close: "a third that was already on the sidewalk." Open: "I come to this film from organisation theory. The chapter reads the sidewalk as an organisation."

Clean. "Sidewalk" → "sidewalk as an organisation." Backward hook plus rename. The best join in the opening.

**P5 → Origins.** Close: "The chapter reads the sidewalk as an organisation." Heading: "Philosophical Origins in Simmel's Postponement and Royce's Canoe." Open: "Georg Simmel named a third that unifies people who never meet…"

The organisation claim is a dangling hook. Nothing in Origins, the film section, Reach, or the conclusion picks up "organisation." Simmel, Royce, and Reich were already named in P2; the heading could have been hooked from there. P5 reads as a signature line that the chapter then forgets. Either carry "sidewalk as organisation" into Origins (the deferred third is how this organisation holds) or cut the sentence. Leaving it as a closer that the next section ignores is the opening's real handoff failure. The doomscrolling join is smaller.

Short sentences in this stretch ("Another face arrives." / "Platforms have a history." / "The chapter reads the sidewalk as an organisation.") are rhythm after density, which the house bar allows. The last of them is the one that costs, because it is a paragraph-final verdict with no next-paragraph pickup.

**Opening, for this seat:** not clean. Two real costs (Bordwell insert; organisation dangling). One moderate skip (doomscrolling → drift). One strong join (sidewalk). Algorithmacy is gloss-welded and then left on the table.

### Reach, Selection, and the Refusal to Resolve

Handoff: "a third that will not give one" → "The film keeps a strict division of labour between reach and selection, its two thirds."

Reset. New heading, two terms used as verbs once and never gloss-welded as the chapter's pair, announced as already strict. "Its two thirds" tries to attach them to the deferred-third apparatus. The attachment is asserted, not walked.

The one-sentence opener is claim-first, which the house bar wants. The next paragraph does not open on reach, or selection, or the division. It opens "In 1981 Scott Feld asked…" Year-first reset, same shape as Stark and Bordwell. Feld then does useful work: Austin looks like a focus and forms no cluster; what remains is "the bare holding I have been calling reach." The gloss is late. Barns is nearer to selection. "The city supplies the one and the cut supplies the other" is a real reformulation. "Slacker never lets the city do the cut's job, or the cut do the city's" is an epigram closer. It shuts the door.

Warner opens on a public, not on that door. Duranton & Puga split the same two functions again. The film sentence at the end ("a city and a camera") is the landing the section already had. Then "Linklater refuses to resolve" shares no entity with "a city and a camera." The heading's third term is doing the join.

The refusal paragraph is a miniature of the whole chapter: do not produce Gary, do not name the true call, do not bring the bus-station man back; the recurring question is one the city is not built to answer; a door still has to say the rule; a camera still has to choose. That is semantic restatement in a fourth location (Finding 1), and it is also a forward hook the conclusion then recites rather than answers.

**Reach/Selection, for this seat:** the weakest stretch. Late glosses, scholar-first opens, the split taught twice, an epigram, a reset onto "Linklater refuses," and a close that is the chapter's finding again. The film has already shown the division. This section should have named it and moved.

### Conclusion

Handoff: "A camera still has to choose the next face without telling anyone why." / "The opening cut, the club door, and the day walked underneath them show one third holding strangers within reach and arranging none of their meetings."

The camera/why close is not picked up. The conclusion opens on a three-part recap. Legitimate as a conclusion's first move only if what follows advances. What follows is:

- P1: the umbrella sentence, then the taxi, Steve, Gary, the hitchhiker — the exhibits again.
- P2: Simmel postponed; the theme returned as pairs; Royce made acceptance constitutive; Reich described the grant; Linklater films the interval. Origins plus the door, compressed. Same landing as opening P2 ("Linklater films a day in which those problems remain on the sidewalk").
- P3: "The film keeps a third you can argue with. The platform removes it." Then algorithmacy pointed-back, then the role-assignment (the one advance), then Gary's question as a title-return.

**Restatement or advance?** Restatement, with one advance. The role-assignment is new portable form: who owns which predicate. The Gary closer is circular, which a film essay may want. Circularity is not an advance. The opening promised that the film is where the competence can still be seen working, and that that is its use. The conclusion says the competence is the way to make the most of a third that has dropped its face. Those are the same sentence.

A conclusion that advanced would have used the local predicates the body earned — the unnoticed echo; the joins changing hands; acceptance constitutive, so the app is not membership; reach is not a focus; the door still has to say the rule out loud — and then said what the opening could not yet say. It does not. It says the opening again, more neatly.

**Conclusion, for this seat:** not clean. The Gary return is earned as a sound. The argument of the close is a recitation.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions.**

The chapter coheres when it walks a scene into a claim. It fails when it lands on the film and opens on a scholar, when it titles a section for Gary and starts at the bus station, and when it re-delivers the same conclusion in the opening, the film middle, the reach close, and the last page.
