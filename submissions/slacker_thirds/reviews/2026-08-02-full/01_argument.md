## 1. The spine, end to end

The chapter's argument runs: a coherence puzzle no story-world device explains (§1); two ways a third
coordinates strangers, defined so that the definitions classify (§2); Austin as the setting that made
the encounters possible without arranging any (§3); the camera as the selector that arranged them,
and the sense in which that is a pre-digital platform form (§4); what a transactional selector adds —
pricing, reading, removal (§5); why a 1991 audience could receive a film made of selections, and the
measured limit of that competence (§6); what changes when the selecting position becomes the only
route, in the platform's own published words (§7).

Each section needs the previous one. §3's setting is required before §4 can say the camera picks only
from whom the city has assembled. §4's attentional/transactional split is required before §5 can
enumerate what the transactional kind adds. §6's training claim depends on §4's fixed-order selector,
and §6's transfer limit is what makes §7's closing sentence ("It taught nothing about answering back")
earned rather than asserted. No section is decorative and none can be cut whole.

The thesis lands where the abstract promised, in the new vocabulary: the film's resemblance to a
platform begins with selection; platform power begins when the selector can read and govern the
people whose activity it orders. That is the abstract's "cinema engineers for aesthetic ends,
platforms for extraction," restated at a defensible strength — resemblance of position, difference of
powers — without the extraction language the chapter could not evidence.

## 4. Findings

**AR1 (§7, antecedent). "What it adds instead is a fourth thing the three questions do not reach."**
The paragraph enumerates the §5 powers: "two of the three powers are on the page… It reads… It
removes… Pricing the page says nothing about." The "fourth thing" (a verdict with the reasons
withheld) continues that enumeration of powers, but the sentence pivots to §2's three *questions*.
The questions never tested for pricing, reading, or removal either — they classify kinds of third —
so the sentence implies a contrast that does not exist and revives the wrong-antecedent bug panel 2
fixed once already ("three questions I've been circling"). The fourth thing belongs to the powers.
Surgical: "a fourth power the three in §5 do not include" or "a fourth thing the three powers do not
include."

**AR2 (§2, close). "The narrower opening is visible across three canonical statements that do not
cite the other tradition."** Two problems. "The narrower opening" has no plain referent on first
read — the opening is the chapter's own question, named one sentence earlier, but "opening" reads
spatially. And the three canonical statements live only in note 20a (Halevy; Kalmijn & Flap; Finkel),
while the body's next three sentences discuss Small & Gose, Finkel, and Rosenfeld — a reader will
map "three canonical statements" onto those three sentences and be wrong twice (Small & Gose and
Rosenfeld are not in the audit; Finkel is in both lists). Authorial: the fix needs a sentence that
either names the three documents or detaches the Small/Finkel/Rosenfeld exhibits from the count.
Proposal for the author: "Three field-defining reviews never cite the other tradition.[^20a] The
conflation shows up in the field's best work: Small and Gose call routine organisations brokers…"

**AR3 (§4, dangling sentence). "The film also ends after a fixed sequence."** The paragraph's work is
done by "Linklater fixed the order once, and every viewer since has received the same order" and the
finished/public/identical contrast. The "also ends" sentence repeats the fixity point, connects to
nothing downstream (no later argument uses the film's ending against a platform's endlessness), and
blurs an otherwise clean paragraph. Surgical cut, or authorial expansion if the endlessness contrast
is wanted — as written it is neither.

**AR4 (§5, compressed transition). "The selector here also acts sideways across a pair it assembled.
Three powers mark what that addition costs."** The first sentence is doing real work (Kellogg's six
functions are vertical, employer-to-worker; the platform also acts across the matched pair). The
second is compressed past clarity: "that addition" reaches back to "acts sideways," and "costs" has
no stated payer. The three powers are what the sideways position lets the platform do to the pair.
Authorial; proposal: "Three powers come with that sideways position."

**AR5 (§1, unclear predicate). "Of the ninety-eight credited roles, almost none meet twice."** Roles
do not meet; characters do, and "meet twice" is ambiguous between "appear in more than one scene" and
"encounter the same person again." The abstract's version was "Most characters never meet." The claim
is also on the T1 disc-pending list, so the wording should not be settled before the disc pass — but
whatever the disc shows, the predicate needs a subject that can meet. Authorial, disc-pending.
