## What it does for the argument
ROUND-2 HEADLINE FINDING (F1). Jeong names the camera as the selecting agent, and the chapter
cites him at note 10 for Berg's page range only. At 144: "the camera autonomously shifts from
one person to another the hero encounters, without cutting and often without his presence...
which renders palpable the camera's own agency unlike the cross-cutting made under the
director's full control. This 'camera with or without a man' is the real agent of networking on
the ground." And, turning to Bunuel: "such a camera-agent... we sometimes feel even the camera's
hesitation about whom to follow between different characters whose paths cross" - attached
directly to Berg's daisy-chain plot. At 143 he names Slacker as Linklater's "other network
narrative film."

## Bearing on novelty
This is the panel's M3 in the chapter's own field: prior art on a page the chapter already
cites. Two claims are now false as written - the drift paragraph's "the drift concepts have no
name for it," and note 36's "no retrieved source pairs the city as reach with the cut as
selection." WHAT SURVIVES: Jeong's camera-agent connects; the chapter's camera chooses one and
never returns. He has no withheld introduction, no reach, no door, no grant, no person who could
be asked - and his framework holds that "nothing precedes and exists outside ever-changing
networks of relationship," which is a rival ontology, not a duplicate. Cite him for the camera-
agent, credit the naming, then draw the line: his camera connects, this one chooses and
declines. Roughly +25 words.
