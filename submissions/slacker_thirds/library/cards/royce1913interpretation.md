## What it does for the argument
SWEEP RANK 1, high. Royce's own theory of the third, and his positive answer to the chapter's
own question. Following Peirce he requires a "mediating idea, or... 'a third'" for any complete
comparison, and "Comparison must be triadic in order to be both explicit and complete"
(2:172-73). Then the sociology: "I mean to interpret you to somebody else, to some other
neighbor, who is neither yourself nor myself. Three of us, then, I seek to bring into the
desired unity of interpretation" (2:209); "we three constitute a Community... a Community of
Interpretation" (2:211). The offices are interchangeable at will (2:212). Placement: §'Royce's
test for membership', after the three conditions, one note.

## Bearing on novelty
THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL CARD IN THIS DOMAIN. Royce's answer to 'how do you end up next to someone
you were never introduced to' is: somebody wills to interpret you to a stranger, and the willing
is what makes the three of you one. That interpreter is addressable by construction. Austin does
none of it; the camera selects without addressing; the platform keeps the selecting and
withdraws the addressee. The chapter's central distinction gets a pragmatist name and a 1913
date - and the Royce section stops being a single-purpose test. It is also, unhandled, a prior-
art exposure: Royce wrote a theory of the third and the chapter cites him only for a membership
test.
