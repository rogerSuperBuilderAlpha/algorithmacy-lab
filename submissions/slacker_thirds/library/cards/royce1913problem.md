## What it does for the argument
The membership test that does the chapter's hardest work: a community exists when each member
accepts the same past or future event as part of his own individual life and self (2:50-51). Two
Maori who say "I came over in the canoe Tai-Nui" are one community even though "Each may keep
aloof from the other. They may be enemies" (2:46). Thin at the emotional end, strict at one
point only - ACCEPTANCE - which is what lets the chapter say that holding is not membership.

## Loci
- 2:50-51 - the definition and the community of expectation
- 2:45-46 - the canoe
- 2:60-61, 2:67, 2:68 - the three conditions, verbatim in n. 24
- 2:55 - not every social group meets the conditions

## Bearing on novelty
The sweep confirms the CUMULATIVE reading is Royce's own, not merely his commentators' - 2:55
says flatly that not every social group meets all the conditions. That closes a live objection.
But see royce1913interpretation: the half of Royce the chapter does not use is itself a theory
of the third.
