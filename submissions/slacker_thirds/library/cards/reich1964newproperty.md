## What it does for the argument
The chapter's central legal borrowing, and it borrows the DESCRIPTIVE half exactly: "the
granting, regulation, and revocation of government largess is carried on by procedures which, in
varying degrees, represent short-cuts that tend to augment the power of the grantor at the
expense of the recipient" (751). The prescriptive half is at 783: "the denial of any form of
privilege or benefit on the basis of undisclosed reasons should no longer be tolerated."

## Loci
- 751 - the largess description
- 783 - the demand

## Bearing on novelty
SWEEP CORRECTION: note 28 says the demand "remains a hope appended to the description." It does
not. Brennan wrote it into constitutional law six years later in Goldberg v. Kelly. The chapter
is wrong about its own source's afterlife, and the correction is better for the argument - the
demand was met for public largess and never for the private door.
