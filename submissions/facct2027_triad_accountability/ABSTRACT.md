**Title:** Accountability in the Triad: Who Answers When an Algorithm Judges Two People at Once

## Abstract
Algorithmic accountability research models a two-place relation: a system on one side and an affected person on the other. Many systems do not sit in that position. A marketplace ranking, a booking platform's review score or a hiring screen stands between two people who depend on each other. It evaluates both of them and binds both to its verdict. We call this arrangement a triad, and we argue that two-place accountability cannot assign responsibility inside it. Following Bovens, accountability is a relation in which an actor explains its conduct to a forum that can judge it. In a triad, each party's outcome depends on the counterpart and on the rule at once. The evaluated party cannot tell how much of a verdict each one produced, and the operator can deflect responsibility onto either party. A forum that hears only the affected person therefore hears half of the evidence. We draw on published studies of seller ratings, hotel rankings and host evaluations on home-sharing platforms to specify what a three-place forum requires. It must hear both parties together. It must also support three competencies that let participants attribute responsibility: interpreting an ambiguous result, specifying intent so the counterpart can read it through the system, and tracking rule changes the operator does not announce. We close with design and audit implications. Audits should test whether a system lets the two parties jointly contest one verdict. They should also test whether its explanations separate the counterpart's contribution from the rule's.

**Keywords:** algorithmic accountability; platforms; contestability; triads; organizational factors
