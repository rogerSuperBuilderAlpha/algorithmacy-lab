# Argument: competence and standing in the triad

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Angle sharpened against the OT paper (see FLAGS 2).]

**P1.** On platforms and in AI-mediated work, the operator acts through the agency of the parties it evaluates. It benefits from their agency without making them its agents, and accountability becomes distributed, deflected and denied. AI is better understood as a capability of a system of relations than as an agent.
- *Sources:* `lima_pdw/literature/cards/starkvandenbroeck2024.md` (p. 14); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/stelmaszak2026.md` (pp. 336, 341); `coordinative_sovereignty/literature/library/cards/lazar2025governing.md` (power between mediatees; section I).

**P2.** A co-opted person's agency then has two parts that vary independently. The first is competence: the practical ability to interpret the system's verdicts, address the other party through it, and notice when it changes. The second is standing: a recognised right to contest a verdict before someone obliged to answer.
- *Sources:*
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/bovens2010.md` (virtue, a property of one party, versus mechanism, a relation between parties)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/kellogg2020.md` (p. 387: often "no procedure in place" to contest)
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/cameron2024.md` (choice-based consent)

**C.** Competence without standing turns the competent person into the moral crumple zone: they absorb blame for verdicts they could read but not contest. Standing without competence goes unused. Organization theory should therefore locate agency in the triad as the joint product of the two, and ask how organizations distribute each to the parties they evaluate.
- *Source:* `dxd_foundation/literature/library/elish2019moral.md` (pp. 40, 49).
