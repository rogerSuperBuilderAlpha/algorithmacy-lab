**Title:** Competence and Standing: The Agency of the Co-opted in AI-Mediated Organizing

## Abstract
When an AI system evaluates two people and binds both to its verdict, who acts? Platforms and AI-mediated workplaces answer by co-optation. A booking platform ranks a hotel on guests' reviews, a marketplace demotes a seller on buyers' ratings, and an AI tool can draft the appraisal a manager gives an employee. In each case the operator benefits from the parties' agency without treating them as its agents, and accountability is distributed, deflected and denied. Recent theory treats AI not as an agent but as a capability of a system of relations among people and algorithms. We ask what agency remains to the people inside such a system. We argue that the co-opted person's agency has two components that vary independently. Competence is the practical ability to interpret a verdict, to address the other party through the system, and to notice when the system's rule changes. Standing is a recognized right to contest a verdict before someone obliged to answer. The two combine into four positions. With both, agency is effective. With competence alone, the person becomes a moral crumple zone, absorbing blame for verdicts they can read but cannot contest. With standing alone, rights go unused. With neither, the person is simply managed. We develop propositions about how organizations distribute competence and standing among the parties they evaluate, why operators prefer to supply competence (training) over standing (contest), and how asymmetric distributions between the two parties shape whom the system blames. The account relocates the production of action from the person or the machine to the configuration of competence and standing in the triad.

**Keywords:** agency; co-optation; algorithmic management; accountability; standing
