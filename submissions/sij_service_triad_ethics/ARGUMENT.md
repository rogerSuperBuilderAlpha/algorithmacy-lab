# Argument: where accountability breaks in the service triad

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Angle sharpened against the OT paper (see FLAGS 2).]

**P1.** Service work has long been a triangle of worker, customer and firm. Platforms make the third corner an evaluating system that turns customers' judgements into verdicts on workers and properties. The evaluated side often cannot see how the verdict was formed.
- *Sources:*
  - `hospitality_phygital/library/cards/scottorlikowski2012accountability.md`
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/jhaver2018.md` (hosts' double negotiation)
  - `dxd_foundation/literature/library/cruz2023digital.md` (p. 2: platform–client–worker as a triangular service relation)

**P2.** Accountability breaks at identifiable points in that triad. Score aggregation hides each party's contribution. Access rules set by the intermediary sort guests before any service begins. Ratings do not travel between platforms. Oversight given to staff who cannot override the system makes them absorb responsibility without control.
- *Sources:*
  - `lima_pdw/literature/cards/rahman2021.md` (p. 976, paraphrase)
  - `hospitality_phygital/library/cards/edelman2017discrimination.md` (the harm located in the intermediary's design decision)
  - `hospitality_phygital/library/cards/zervas2021reputation.md`
  - `hospitality_phygital/library/cards/fink2025oversight.md` (liability sponge; working paper)

**C.** Mechanisms that restore accountability differ in which party they return the verdict to. Appeals return it to the evaluated worker or property. Reciprocal reveal returns it to both parties at once. Managerial adjustment returns it to the firm. Guest-visible attribution returns it to the customer. Service firms and regulators should choose mechanisms by where the break occurs and to whom the verdict should return.
