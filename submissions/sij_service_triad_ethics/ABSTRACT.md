**Title:** Where Accountability Breaks in the Service Triad, and to Whom It Can Be Returned

## Abstract
Service work has always involved three parties: the worker, the customer and the firm. On booking and home-sharing platforms, and increasingly inside hotels, an evaluating system now occupies the third corner. It turns guests' judgements into verdicts on staff, hosts and properties, and it sets the rules under which guests and hosts meet. This paper asks where accountability breaks in that arrangement and which mechanisms can restore it. Drawing on published studies of hotel reviews, home-sharing hosts, discrimination in guest acceptance and portability of reputation, we identify four break points. Score aggregation hides how much of a verdict each party produced. Access rules set by the intermediary sort guests before any service begins. Ratings do not travel between platforms, so a poor verdict cannot be escaped. Oversight assigned to frontline staff who cannot override the system leaves them absorbing responsibility without control. We then compare four restoring mechanisms by the party to which each returns the verdict. Appeals return it to the evaluated worker or property. Reciprocal reveal returns it to both parties at once. Managerial adjustment returns it to the firm. Guest-visible attribution returns it to the customer. The resulting framework tells service managers and regulators which mechanism fits which break. It also shows why transparency alone, which returns the verdict to no one, does not restore accountability in the service triad.

**Keywords:** service triad; algorithmic accountability; hospitality platforms; opacity; human oversight
