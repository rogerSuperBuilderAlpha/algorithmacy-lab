## Acquisition

## What the cards turned up

Three findings change how sources are cited, not only what is on file.

1. **Network competence is a firm-level construct.** Ritter and Gemünden define "a
   company's degree of network competence" and survey companies. §4's table asks what
   each form demands of the *coordinated party* and answers "Yes, for three decades"
   in the network row. The precedent transfers as a form of argument, not as a unit of
   analysis, and §8 then commits algorithmacy to the individual.
2. **§4 cites a capability that §8 excludes.** Schreiner, Kale and Corsten's alliance
   management capability is firm-held, which is exactly the class §8 rules out on
   possession grounds.
3. **Spitzberg and Cupach (1984) is the one source in the competency lineage that is
   cited but unread.** If the relational model locates competence in the partner's
   judgement rather than in the speaker, §8's warrant needs restating — a competency
   construct for a form whose counterpart is absent would have a problem the paper has
   not addressed.
