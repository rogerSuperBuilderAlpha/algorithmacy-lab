## What it argues

Barney builds the resource-based view on two assumptions — that strategic resources are heterogeneously distributed across firms, and that the differences are stable over time — and derives from them the conditions under which a firm resource can generate sustained competitive advantage. Four empirical indicators are set out: value, rareness, imitability and substitutability. The model is then applied by examining the potential of several firm resources to generate sustained advantage, and the paper closes on what this resource-based account implies for other business disciplines.

## Relation to the argument

§8 cites this one clause behind Teece, as the resource-based predecessor to dynamic capabilities, and it earns the mention for the same reason: the whole apparatus is about what a *firm* holds. Barney's four indicators are tests of a resource's protection from the market, and protection presupposes ownership. That is the point §8 needs. When the paper says a capability is held by the party that owns the routine and that the platform worker owns nothing, Barney supplies the clearest statement of why ownership is not incidental to the concept but constitutive of it — a resource that can be traded away or imitated is, on this account, not a source of sustained advantage at all. Verdict: supporting citation for §8's exclusion of the capability family; one clause is the right weight.

## Caution

Abstract-grounded only. Barney is cited in a compound with Teece, Pisano and Shuen as if the two were a single position; see the note on `teece1997`. More substantively, the resource-based view has a live internal debate about whether resources are held by firms or by individuals within them — the human-capital and micro-foundations strands — and a reviewer working in strategy may read §8's flat "works at the firm level" as passing over it. The sentence survives that objection, because even the individual-level strands treat the individual's resource as appropriable by the firm, which is exactly the possession structure the platform worker lacks. Worth having the answer ready rather than the claim unqualified.
