## Tier A — load-bearing, and currently unwarranted

**A1. Relational coordination has no source, and it guards the paper's flank.**
`# Extant Constructs` opens by defending why it reviews only algorithm-centred constructs: relational
coordination already includes the counterpart and presupposes what the arrangement removes. That
sentence closes the cheapest relabelling attack available — *"algorithmacy is relational coordination
performed under opacity."* The manuscript cites Gittell (2002). **No Gittell card and no
relational-coordination card exists in the Lima library.** The dissertation shelf holds
`gittell2004`, abstract-only, and about a different paper. Two things are needed: the correct primary
citation with the construct's definition, and a search for anyone who has already carried relational
coordination into algorithmically mediated settings — which would open the flank rather than close
it. *Round dispatched.*

**A2. The Trinidad argument asserts a methodological position and cites nothing.**
The 26 August revision made formation in the GauntleTT cohort constitutive rather than biographical —
that the platform-work literature forms constructs at sites the arrangement has already sorted, where
differences arrive pre-explained by skill, task mix or hours, and that the Trinidad cohort held none
of those explanations. That is a real position about theorizing from a context, it is what the
workshop's distinctive aim asks for, and it stands on the author's say-so. Needs the
contextual-theorizing literature behind it. *Round dispatched.*

**A3. Spitzberg and Cupach (1984) is load-bearing twice and the book has never been opened.**
`extended_preview`, reconstructed from the 1989 restatement and Spitzberg (2006). The manuscript now
uses it for the competence anchor repair (competence as a quality of a relationship judged by both
parties) **and** for §6's level-shift concession. Both are argument-carrying. *Round dispatched.*

## Three corrections applied to `PAPER.md`

1. **Spitzberg's outcome criteria were wrong, and the error was introduced by this revision.** The
   draft said four (appropriateness, effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction) with "the first and last"
   administered by the partner. The article's working typology (p. 648) has **five**, with efficiency
   folded inside effectiveness: appropriateness, effectiveness, coorientation, satisfaction, and
   relationship development. With five members the "first and last" pair also picked out the wrong two.
2. **Spitzberg and Cupach's level claim was anchored to the wrong text.** "Measurement at one level
   licenses no inference at another" is later Spitzberg (2015/1989), not verified 1984 wording. What the
   1984 book verifiably holds is that competence is at once *molar and molecular*. The concession now
   says that. The relational-competence claim in §4 is confirmed and stands.
3. **Laupichler, one word.** SNAIL's reported validation is an exploratory factor analysis with no
   external criterion, so course efficacy is its declared *use*, not its validation criterion.
