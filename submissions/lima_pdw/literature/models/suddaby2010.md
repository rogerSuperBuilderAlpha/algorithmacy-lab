## The four elements (his p. 347)

“The essence of construct clarity comprises four basic elements.”

1. **Definitions.** Precise, parsimonious categorical distinctions. A good
   definition captures essential properties, avoids tautology and circularity,
   and does not smuggle in antecedents or outcomes (pp. 347–348).
   “A leader who transforms organizations” is the empty pattern to avoid.
2. **Scope conditions.** Space, time, and values — when the construct does and
   does not apply (pp. 348–349, following Bacharach).
3. **Semantic relations.** “No construct is an island.” Show the construct on
   the horizon of its neighbours so it is not old wine in a new bottle
   (pp. 349–350).
4. **Coherence.** Logical consistency among definition, scope, relations, and
   the larger theoretical argument (pp. 350–351). Diagrams are not a substitute.

He also warns: construct clarity is not construct *validity*. Clarity is
conceptual. Validity is empirical. Do not mix the two in the algorithmacy
section.
