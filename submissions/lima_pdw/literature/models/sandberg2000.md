# Model — Sandberg (2000), *AMJ* 43(1), 9–25

Read 2026-08-19 from `../pdfs/sandberg2000.pdf` (OCR; quote carefully).

## The arc

Observation: the same job (engine optimizer, Volvo) is done at very different
levels. Existing construct: rationalistic competence as a list of attributes
(KSAs) identified independently of how the work is conceived. That construct
cannot explain the variation. New construct: competence **is** the worker’s
**way of conceiving the work**. Method: phenomenographic interviews (plus
observation) with **20** optimizers drawn from a department of **50**. Findings:
three conceptions, hierarchical.

| Conception | What the work is | N |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Optimizing **separate** qualities | 6 |
| 2 | Optimizing **interacting** qualities | 10 |
| 3 | Optimizing from the **customer’s** perspective | 4 |

Attributes do not distinguish the groups; the conception organizes which
attributes matter. Peers and superiors treat conception 3 as the most competent.
He presented the results back to the 20 and then to all 50 (pp. 15–16 of the
article’s own count in the OCR).

## Architecture to steal

1. Open on a managerial problem (what constitutes competence), not on a gap
   slogan.
2. Give the existing approach a real hearing (worker-oriented, work-oriented,
   multimethod — all still attribute lists).
3. State the alternative in one sentence: meaning work takes on in experience,
   not the attribute set, constitutes competence.
4. Methods: site, why this job, how 20 were chosen, interview stem (“What does
   optimization mean?” / “What is a competent optimizer for you?”), how
   transcripts were sorted, member check.
5. Findings organized as **named conceptions**, each with incident talk, then
   a table that shows how attributes rearrange under each conception.
6. Close on what a competency model built from attributes would miss.

## What not to copy

Phenomenography. Volvo engineers. The claim that competence cannot be scaled —
we have a later quantitative arm and must not pretend Sandberg licenses it.
Use him for what competence *is*, not for how it is measured.

## What it does to our algorithmacy section

The locked definition already says what the person can *do*. Sandberg says
competence is also how she **conceives** the work. The repair the old card
flagged still stands: do not define competency as a KSAO bundle and then cite
him. Point at the locked definition, then say: that competence is a way of
taking part in a coordination, and the way of taking part is a way of
conceiving what the system and the counterpart are doing.

Conception 3 includes a customer. That is not our counterpart-through-system.
It is a conception of engine work that orients to the driver. Steelman it in
one sentence so a reviewer cannot say Sandberg already put the other human
inside competence. He put the customer inside a *conception of the job*. He
did not specify a competence for coordination through an opaque, adaptive,
binding intermediary.
