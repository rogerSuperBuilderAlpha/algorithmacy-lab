# Model — Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen & Neysen (2020), *ASQ* 65(3), 644–676

Read 2026-08-19 from `dissertation/research/library/pdfs/curchod2020.txt` — the Warwick WRAP
author-accepted manuscript (R4). **Do not cite page numbers from that file.** "Working for an
algorithm: Power asymmetries and agency in online work settings."

## The arc

Power in workplaces has been theorised for hierarchies, where a manager observes a worker. Online
evaluation settings are not that. Seventy-seven high-performing eBay business sellers in France and
Belgium describe acute sensitivity to rare negative reviews. Their accounts expose asymmetry at two
levels — transactional (seller to customer) and governance (seller to eBay) — through three mechanisms:
a **visibility gap**, an implicit **coalition** between anonymous buyers and a distant platform owner,
and algorithms that mediate and objectify the relation. Sellers respond by exploiting practical
knowledge of the algorithm.

## What to steal

1. **"This theory does not reach" done with a named theory.** The paper does not say the literature has
   a gap; it says the monitoring literature presumes an observer who is present, and then shows a
   setting where the observer is a crowd and an algorithm. Our §3 asks a version of this of each
   construct, and the model for saying it about a *literature* rather than a construct is here.
2. **Findings split by level, not by theme.** Transactional then governance. Our arrangement has the
   same two levels — what happens between the participant and the counterpart, and what happens between
   the participant and the gate — and separating them would keep §5 from collapsing the two.
3. **The agency half in its own section.** They establish the asymmetry completely before reporting what
   sellers do about it, which is Rahman's condition-then-conduct order arriving independently. Two ASQ
   qualitative papers, same order. That is a genre convention worth obeying.
4. **The interview protocol in an appendix.** This settles a question the plan raised: reviewers in
   this venue expect the instrument to be inspectable. Ours are in the repository at
   `interview/protocols/`, and §5 points at them rather than reprinting. An appendix carrying the block
   structure — task, unexplained outcome, interpretation and next act — would cost half a page and match
   the genre.
5. **A sample described by what it is good for and what it is not.** They interviewed high performers
   and say so, and the ceiling that creates is stated rather than defended.

## What not to steal

- **N = 77 interview-only, and the two-level power frame.** Power and agency are not competence, and
  their dependent variable is a structural relation rather than an ability.
- **The sociomateriality vocabulary.** It is theirs, it is load-bearing for them, and importing it would
  put our construct in a conversation it does not join.

## The finding this memo turned up

**Curchod and colleagues named the triad in *ASQ* in 2020, in one sentence, and `PAPER.md` cites them
for something else.**

> First, in place of traditional dyadic exchanges, customer reviews enact triadic relationships among
> the platform operator, buyers, and sellers that generate multiple accountabilities.

And their central mechanism is our asymmetry, in their words: buyers keep "their track record hidden,
their identity private, and their direct e-mail confidential," while "the track records and details of
sellers were visible to all." They call it a **visibility gap**, and they add a second mechanism our
paper has not named — an implicit **coalition of interests between a mass of invisible buyers and a
distant platform owner**.

`PAPER.md` §2 cites them once, for "ratings that cannot be carried to another site." That is a true
thing they show and the least of what they show.

Three consequences, all for the author:

- **The §3 close needs its scope stated**, for the same reason [`cameron2024.md`](cameron2024.md) gives.
  Curchod is prior art on the position. Scoped to competence constructs the claim survives; unscoped it
  does not.
- **The visibility gap is an antecedent for asymmetric interpretation**, and citing it that way turns a
  rival into a foundation. Their sellers cannot see who judged them or on what basis, and the platform
  and the crowd are aligned against that seeing. Our first facet is what a person supplies when that is
  true.
- **The coalition mechanism is worth borrowing outright.** It names something the co-optation account
  gestures at and does not specify: the counterpart's interest and the platform's interest are joined in
  the evaluation procedure, which is why the counterpart is not available as an ally. That is a sharper
  statement of why the position is empty than "the algorithm sits between them."
