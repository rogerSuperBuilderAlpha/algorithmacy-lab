# Model — Rahman (2021), *ASQ* 66(4), 945–988

Read 2026-08-19 from `dissertation/research/library/pdfs/rahman2021.pdf` (author-deposited accepted
manuscript, paginated 1–44). **Do not cite page numbers from that file.** The construct hearing is
[`../steelmans/rahman2021.md`](../steelmans/rahman2021.md); this memo is about how the paper is built.

## The arc, in one line each

Third-party evaluations are known to work as control **because people can align to their criteria**.
Algorithmic evaluations increasingly do not disclose criteria. What happens to alignment then? Answer:
a form of control in which criteria and their changes are unpredictable — the invisible cage — and two
opposed forms of reactivity, sorted by dependence and setback.

The theory section does one job and does it in about four pages: it establishes that the existing
literature's mechanism (align to visible criteria) has a **precondition**, then shows the precondition
failing. He does not open with a gap slogan. He opens with what is known.

## The skeleton

| Section | What it does |

| **Third-Party Evaluations, Reactivity, and Opacity** | The known mechanism, then two subsections that break it: *Labor Platforms' Increased Use of Opaque Third-Party Evaluations*, *Increasing Focus on Opacity* |
| **Methods** | Site, the user agreement and arm's-length relation, Data Collection, Data Analysis. Table 1 is a data-source inventory |
| **Findings: Inside the Invisible Cage** | Two movements: what the cage *is*, then what workers *do* |
| — movement 1 | Differentiating Freelancers and Preventing Gaming through Opaque Evaluation → Opaque Inputs and Processing Contribute to Output Unpredictability → Difficulty in Learning → Lack of Recourse → Paranoia |
| — movement 2 | Experimental and Constrained Reactivity, then one subsection per cell of a 2×2 (high/low performer × dependence and setback), with a figure of "reactivity pathways" |
| **Discussion** | Contributions to reactivity; contributions to evaluations; implications of the rise of algorithmic evaluations; **Boundary Conditions, Limitations, and Future Work** |

## What to steal

1. **A theory section that names a precondition rather than a gap.** Our §2 wants exactly this move.
   Paper 1's forms each say how a person takes her place; coordinative co-optation does not. That is a
   precondition failing, not a hole in a literature.
2. **Findings in two movements: the condition, then the conduct.** He spends the first half establishing
   that the cage is real and closed — five components, and even cracking one leaves the others — and only
   then reports what workers do inside it. Our §5 has the same shape available: what the arrangement
   withholds, then what the participant did about it.
3. **Table 1 as a data-source inventory, not a sample table.** Each row is a source (interviews,
   discussion board, platform communications, registered-user observation) with columns for description,
   sampling strategy, and *use in analysis*. That third column is the one reviewers want and the one
   most methods sections omit. At our scale the table has three or four rows and still earns its place.
4. **A named mechanism for the variation, stated before the cells.** Platform dependence and evaluation
   setbacks are announced, then each cell is worked. He does not let the typology arrive as a surprise.
5. **Boundary conditions as a heading, not a paragraph of hedging.** Ours are already drafted in the
   research plan's Suddaby table; give them the heading.

## What not to steal

- **His evidence base.** Three years, 98 interviews, 2,053 board messages. We have early qualitative
  work from a harness instrument. Borrowing his *display* at our N would read as a completed study, and
  §6's title exists to prevent exactly that.
- **The 2×2 by performer level.** His cut is structural position; ours is not, and a typology of cells
  is not available from a handful of incidents. Organise by the interview's own blocks, per the plan.
- **His construct's dependent variable.** Reactivity is exposure to an evaluation. Ours is a competence
  toward a counterpart. Copying his findings architecture wholesale drags the paper back onto his axis.

## Page budget

Roughly 44 manuscript pages: theory ~9, methods ~7, findings ~18, discussion ~8. The findings are
**forty percent of the paper**, and the theory is a fifth. A PDW manuscript of ours at 6,200 words
cannot hold that ratio, and should not pretend to — but the *order* is the transferable part: condition
before conduct, and the discussion earning its length from the findings rather than from the framing.
