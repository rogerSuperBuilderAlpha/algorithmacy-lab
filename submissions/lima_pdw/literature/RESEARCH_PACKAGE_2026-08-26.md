### Tier 1 — closes a known exposure or converts a claim into evidence

**1. The field's own stocktakings — [`steelmans/field_stocktakings.md`](steelmans/field_stocktakings.md)
(cards: [`oeldorfhirsch2025`](cards/oeldorfhirsch2025.md), [`iyamu2026`](cards/iyamu2026.md),

one side and a system on the other." As written that is a claim about seven readings. The
stocktakings make it a documented property of a field: Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum reviewed **50**

## 2. What the library contradicts in the draft

Every quoted phrase, page cite and specific number in the manuscript was checked against the
steelman, card, or PDF that covers it (PDF text extracted for sutherland2020, guzmanlewis2020,
zhou (working paper), suddaby2010; sandberg2000 OCR'd today). Verified-and-holding items are
listed at the end.

**C1. L128 — the competency anchor cites a book written to reject the citation's claim.**
Manuscript: "Spitzberg and Cupach (1984) treat communication competence as what a party is able to
do in a coordination, as against what she did on any one occasion." The card
([`cards/spitzberg1984.md`](cards/spitzberg1984.md), `extended_preview` via the authors' 1989
restatement) records the opposite: competence is "**not a property of a person** but a quality of
a relationship, produced jointly, **judged by both parties**, and specific to the episode."
[`steelmans/spitzberg2006.md`](steelmans/spitzberg2006.md) confirms the direction from the 2006
full text: "competent interactants can facilitate the competence of cointeractants."
*Consequence:* the review's finding 1 replacement stands, and it is a gift — the arrangement does
not lower the relational score, it removes the instrument, because the counterpart never reaches a
position from which she could judge. Note the shelf holds two cards for the book:
[`cards/spitzbergcupach1984.md`](cards/spitzbergcupach1984.md) (`metadata_only`) says attribute
nothing to the book; `spitzberg1984.md` documents the relational model from the 1989 restatement.
Ground the repair on the 1989 restatement and Spitzberg (2006); the book itself stays unopened
(see §4 below). The same card adds the level-shift concession §6 owes: measurement at one level
(incidents) licenses no inference at another (a scale).

**C2. L110 — a spliced quotation, running in the argument's favour.** Manuscript: clients were
used "to provide some verification as to which literacies or skills were apparent" to them
(p. 463). The PDF (verified today, `pdfs/sutherland2020.pdf`, p. 463) reads: *"Clients' narratives
conversely provided some verification as to which literacies or skills **were in fact valued** in
gig workers."* "Were apparent" is lifted from the preceding sentence (about workers' own
accounts), and "to them" is appended outside the marks. "Valued" makes the client an *evaluator*
of competence — a stronger counterpart presence than "apparent," so the splice softens the
paper's nearest rival. *Consequence:* use the review's finding 4 replacement verbatim. Note the
same misquote lives in [`steelmans/sutherland2020.md`](steelmans/sutherland2020.md) ("Hunt for the
counterpart" table row and following sentence) — the steelman should be corrected too, or the next
drafter will re-import the error.

**C3. L78 — wrong page.** "within a communication context in which at least one of the interaction
partners is a machine" is cited (p. 73); it sits on **p. 74** (verified today against
`pdfs/guzmanlewis2020.pdf` text: the quote falls in the second half of the span between the p. 73
and p. 75 running heads; the review's finding 5 agrees). The p. 71 cite for "communicative
subjects, instead of mere interactive objects" is correct. *Consequence:* one character.

**C4. L90 — an absolute the library has already falsified.** Manuscript: "Every competency is a
relation between a person and a technology, and **none names another person**." Long & Magerko's
competency 10 is titled *Human Role in AI*: "Recognize that humans play an important role in
programming, choosing models, and fine-tuning AI systems"
([`steelmans/longmagerko2020.md`](steelmans/longmagerko2020.md), all seventeen verbatim from the
camera-ready; [`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md) §"One claim in the draft is falsifiable as written";
[`cards/long2020.md`](cards/long2020.md), narrowed 2026-08-19). A reviewer with the table finds
the title in seconds. *Consequence:* narrow to the checkable form, which is stronger: *no
competency names another person as a party to the interaction; the one competency that names
humans at all names them as the system's makers* (cite by competency number). The library did
this narrowing on 19 August; the manuscript has not absorbed it.

**C5. L30 and L146 — "several responses" against the arm's own intake.** The locked abstract says
"several responses obtained to date"; L146 says "We have obtained several responses."
[`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md) ("Checked while the memos were being written") records the intake
bucket holding **a single object** as of 2026-08-19; `../AGENDA.md` item 7 carries the full note.
*Consequence:* the review's finding 6 sentence ("We have obtained *n* responses…") needs the real
number; the abstract correction requires an unlock, which only the author can order. This package
did not re-count the bucket; if responses arrived since 19 August the number may have moved —
verify before writing *n*.

**C6. L92 — programmability is left under the wrong question.** Manuscript: "…ethics under the
fourth; programmability among them as well." The grouping is 4 / 2 / 9 / 1 / 1: ethics is the sole
competency under *How should AI be used?* and programmability (#17) is the sole competency under

sentence reads programmability into the fourth question and silently drops the fifth.
*Consequence:* half a sentence; a reviewer with the table will notice, and the paper's own "five
questions" setup at L90 promises the fifth.

**C7 (precision, not error). L72 — "The outcomes that matter are appropriateness and
effectiveness."** Spitzberg's 2006 model carries four outcome criteria — appropriateness,
effectiveness, **efficiency, satisfaction** (steelman, "Their claim" section). "That matter" makes
the sentence defensible as emphasis; it will read to a Spitzberg reader as an undercount, and
satisfaction (the partner's affect) actually helps the paper's removal-of-the-instrument point.

**C8 (reference-list form). L240 — Zhou cited as "63(2)".** The journal version is article-numbered:

[`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md)). Cite the article number, not an issue-with-no-pages.

## 5. The counterpart claim, tested across the whole shelf

**The claim (L42, locked; L116):** every existing competence construct places a person on one side
and a system on the other, and leaves the human counterpart outside the competence it specifies.

**Verdict: it survives, on four scope conditions — and the shelf now lets it be stated as a
documented negative result rather than an absence of evidence.**

I searched the 387 cards for anything that puts a second human party inside a competence,
literacy, or skill construct, reached through a system that reads both sides. The candidates that
came closest, and where each stops:

Nothing on the shelf falsifies the claim. Four scope conditions are load-bearing, and the paper
should state all four:

1. **Competence constructs only.** The *position* has been named repeatedly — fourth party (2001),
   Curchod's triad (2020), Cameron's triangle (2024), Healy & Pekarek (2024). Unscoped, "the
   missing piece is a position" is false four times over. Scoped to constructs of a party's
   capacity, it holds everywhere.
2. **Counterpart as a party to the interaction.** Not humans as the system's makers (Long &
   Magerko competency 10), not peers (Hong, Zhou's WeChat crews, Design Consideration 11), not
   downstream beneficiaries (UNESCO's student, Nutbeam's patient).
3. **Through a binding intermediary that reads both parties.** Not a channel (Spitzberg, Abidin,
   DigComp area 2), not a tool or delegate (Mahy, Hancock), not a disclosed fourth party (ODR),
   not an app that schedules a face-to-face encounter (Scolari).
4. **A claim about a position, not a name.** Gagrčin's card: three new named constructs since the
   abstract was accepted; names are cheap. The claim survives only as "no construct gives the
   counterpart a position," never as "nobody has named a competence for platform work."

**Stating it as evidence.** The negative result now has a citable form: two systematic reviews and
a scoping review — 50, 169, and 12 studies, across four and seven databases
(`steelmans/field_stocktakings.md`) — audit the field and find the person–system line everywhere;
Iyamu and colleagues looked for the counterpart-facing competence with a framework that had a slot
for it and report it "largely absent from both conceptual frameworks and measurement instruments";
Ng et al.'s four-cell compression is the transmission mechanism that keeps every later instrument
two-term (`cards/ng2021.md`); Rahman and Sutherland each had the counterpart *in the sample* and
still produced two-term constructs (their steelmans, "Hunt" sections); the EU's decade-long DigComp
and UNESCO's framework are the policy-scale instances; and Abidin closes the pincer from the other
side — the one construct with a real counterpart deliberately excludes the algorithm. Every
construct that centres the algorithm has no counterpart; the one construct with a counterpart has
no algorithm; and the literatures that drew the three-party geometry never wrote a competence for
it. That is the strongest form of the paper's contribution, and every piece of it is on this shelf.
