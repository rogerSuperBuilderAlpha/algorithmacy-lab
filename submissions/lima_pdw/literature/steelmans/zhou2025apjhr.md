# Steelman — Zhou, Lei, Liu, Huang & Hou (2025)

Read 2026-08-19 from the **journal version**, `dissertation/research/sources/pdfs/zhou2025_algorithmic_competency.pdf`
(*Asia Pacific Journal of Human Resources* 63: e70004, open access; received 7 April 2024,
accepted 10 February 2025). This closes item 1 of the research plan's "still to acquire."

**Verification result first, because the plan asked for it.** Items 5, 8 and 11 in
[`../ZHOU_2025_INSTRUMENT.md`](../ZHOU_2025_INSTRUMENT.md) were transcribed from the Rutgers working
paper. All twelve items, their numbering, their dimension assignment and both sets of loadings match
the journal's Table 2 exactly. Two typographic differences only: the journal prints "customers–workers
matching" with an en dash, and item 5's parenthetical reads "(i.e., imprecise navigation)". **The
instrument file may now be cited as final.**

## Their claim, in their words

> we define AC of on-demand labor platform workers as their understanding of platform algorithms that
> assign and evaluate their work and their ability to adapt to and navigate those algorithms

Four dimensions, defined in their §3.1:

> Understanding AM refers to platform workers having a sophisticated comprehension of how AM operates
> and the ability to navigate its dynamic nature. Embracing AM pertains to platform workers'
> willingness to trust in the efficiency and accuracy of AM for matching and other functionalities.
> Leveraging AM denotes platform workers' capacity to utilize AM strategically or advantageously for
> their benefit. Remediating AM involves platform workers' ability to address or supplement the
> deficiencies of AM.

Method: 99 semi-structured interviews with Chinese ride-hailing drivers and food-delivery couriers
(Sample 1; 98.99% male, 79.80% platform work as main income, mean tenure 2.54 years, ~30 min each) →
14 items → expert content validation → EFA at N = 275 → CFA at N = 213, second-order four-factor model
preferred → validity at N = 230 → three-wave antecedents and outcomes at N = 225. Twelve items survive,
three per dimension, overall α = .85.

The construct's ancestry is stated and matters: the first three dimensions come from **Jarrahi and
Sutherland (2019)** — sensemaking, circumventing, manipulating — which is the same lineage as our
gig-literacies neighbour. Zhou and colleagues re-ran that inventory on an Asia-Pacific dispatch
population and got a fourth dimension out of it.

## Best version

A sympathetic reviewer says this: they did the thing our paper says nobody has done. They named a
competency for platform workers under algorithmic management, generated it inductively from ninety-nine
workers rather than from theory, validated it across five independent samples, discriminated it against
the two nearest measures, and placed it in a nomological net with antecedents (peer social support,
cognitive job crafting) and consequences (customer-oriented service behaviour, identification with gig
work). Their discriminant work is more careful than most: digital competence at r = .37 overall but
r = .04, ns, with *Embracing* — a null they predicted and explained, because digital-competence measures
omit attitude. Competence need satisfaction at r = .54 is convergent and they say so. Perceived
algorithmic management at r = .17 is discriminant and they predicted that too.

Three of the four dimensions are not knowledge. Any hearing that files this as "what a worker
understands about how the system works" has read the label and not the items.

## What they make the algorithm

An **object of navigation**, in their own verb. Also an object of repair: *Remediating* treats AM as a
defective apparatus the worker patches. Never a party, never an evaluator whose judgement must be
answered — an apparatus with rules that can be learned, exploited and worked around.

## Hunt for the counterpart

The customer appears thirty-five times in the article, and in four distinct roles. None of them is a
party to the competency.

1. **As a rating source, inside an item.** Item 8, *Leveraging*: "I proactively explore AM rules to
   minimize negative customer feedback." The customer here is an input to the score, and the object of
   the verb is the *rules*, not the customer.
2. **As a matching function, inside a belief.** Item 11, *Embracing*: "I think the platform AM is
   highly efficient, such as in customers–workers matching." This names the triad's actual mechanism
   inside a validated algorithm-competence instrument, and a reviewer who finds it first will present
   it as the counterpart appearing in the rival scale. It is not. The item measures a belief about the
   platform's efficiency; it sits in the dimension that correlates weakest with the rest (.22–.25); it
   asks nothing about any capacity of the worker toward the person matched.
3. **As an outcome variable.** Customer-oriented service behaviour, Peccei and Rosenthal's (1997)
   measure — "the extent to which workers engage in continuous improvement and exert effort on the job
   for the benefit of customers." **This is the sharpest discrimination available.** In their own model
   the counterpart matters empirically, is measured, and still sits outside the construct.
4. **As a source of unfair ratings to be appealed — and the appeal runs to the machine.** This is new
   and the arm had not noticed it. *Remediating* item 6 reads "I can use platform APP functions (i.e.,
   reporting exceptions and appealing) to resolve vulnerabilities in AM," and the interview quotation
   behind it is explicit: "N22: When receiving unfair ratings from customers, I can use evidence like
   recordings to appeal to the AM." A worker disputes the customer's rating **to the algorithm**. The
   counterpart is the source of the grievance and never the addressee of it.

Item 6 is worth more to §3 than item 11 is. It is the only place in the rival scale where a worker
contests an outcome, and the contest is routed to the apparatus — which is our claim about the form,
appearing as a validated item in someone else's instrument. It also lands on §5: our first respondent
never contested an outcome because no channel read as the place for it. Zhou's workers have that
channel, and it still does not reach the customer.

## The test

A worker can score at ceiling on all twelve items — she understands AM, trusts its efficiency, exploits
its rules for high-quality orders, and repairs its failures through WeChat groups and the appeals
function — and have no capacity whatever to work out what the person on the other side of the match
wanted, or to make herself legible to that person through a system that reads them both. Nothing in the
scale would detect the failure, because the counterpart enters their design only after the construct is
finished, as a dependent variable. **Person–system, measured well.**

## What we inherit

Their derivation discipline, and their honesty about a weak dimension. Also their finding that the
antecedents of algorithmic competency are social — peer support, moderated by collectivism — which is
the empirical anchor our *implicit acquisition* property has been missing and which we should cite
rather than assert.

## What adding items will not get

Add a thirteenth item about the customer and you get another item about the customer *as an input to,
or output of, the apparatus*, because that is the only position the construct's definition gives her:
AC is defined as understanding of, and ability to navigate, "platform algorithms that assign and
evaluate their work." The relation named in the definition has two terms, worker and algorithm. A third
term cannot be added by measurement.
