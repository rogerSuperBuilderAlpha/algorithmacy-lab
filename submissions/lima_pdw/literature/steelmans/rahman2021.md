Read 2026-08-19 from `dissertation/research/library/pdfs/rahman2021.pdf` — the author-deposited
accepted manuscript, textually the *ASQ* article (66(4), 945–988), paginated 1–44.
**Do not cite page numbers from this file.** Quotations below are verbatim; page anchors need the
published version.

## Their claim, in their words

The construct, from the abstract:

> These workers experienced what I call an "invisible cage": a form of control in which the criteria
> for success and changes to those criteria are unpredictable.

The five components, in one sentence of his:

> These components include evaluation criteria, because they can shift and are largely opaque;
> execution of the evaluation, because the intervals at which evaluation changes occur are uncertain;
> magnitude of the evaluation, because the extent to which the evaluation changes cannot be identified;
> impact of the evaluation, because workers are unsure how changes in their scores affect their short-
> and long-term reputation and ability to secure further work; and who influences the evaluation,
> because the extent to which each party (i.e., client vs. platform) affects the evaluation score is
> unspecified.

The two forms of reactivity, as he codes them:

> Experimental reactivity was characterized by freelancers increasing their platform activity to try
> different tactics to increase their scores, such as testing different contract lengths or how
> contracts were closed. Constrained reactivity involved freelancers trying to limit their exposure to
> the evaluation.

Two mechanisms sort the forms: **platform dependence** and **evaluation setbacks**. Method: over three
years; 98 semi-structured interviews (80 freelancers — 36 scoring ≥ 90, 26 between 33 and 89, 18 new
and unscored — plus **18 clients**); the platform's discussion board; archival documents; registered-user
observation. The site is "TalentFinder," which in 2015 replaced a transparent five-star system with an
opaque 100-point score. He declined to bid on work, on stated ethical grounds.

## Best version

The strongest reading is that Rahman has already described the *situation* our construct answers, with
more evidence than we will have, and described it as a problem of control rather than of competence.
The five components are not a list; they are a proof of closure. Even a worker who cracks one component
is left with the others: *"even if a freelancer makes headway in uncovering one or a few components of
the evaluation algorithm, other facets will remain opaque."* And his outcome is bleak in a way that
should discipline our claims — reactivity **entrenches** the platform's hold rather than loosening it,
and the paranoia persists years after the change.

He also has the mechanism our paper needs and does not yet cite. The client's decisive input is
private by design. TalentFinder told clients, verbatim: *"This feedback will be kept anonymous and never
shared directly with the freelancer."* Workers could not see it and did not understand how it moved
their scores. That is the counterpart's judgement, routed through the system and withheld from the
person it judges — our structural claim, documented as a platform's interface decision in 2015.

## What they make the algorithm

An **object of interpretation**: a score whose criteria are hidden and shift without notice. He sets it
against the Weberian iron cage, whose rules are fixed and knowable. The algorithm is a controller and a
puzzle, not a party.

## Hunt for the counterpart

This is the construct where the hunt pays best, because **the counterpart is in the sample and is still
outside the construct.**

- **Eighteen clients interviewed**, recruited through freelancer referral because the platform blocked
  the direct route: *"Registered freelancers could see a client's full profile only after applying for
  their job, and even then, TalentFinder withheld their contact information."* The platform withholds
  access to the counterpart even from the researcher.
- He states his purpose for them precisely: *"to understand their perspectives on providing freelancers
  feedback on the platform and to triangulate what freelancers said about their experiences working
  with clients."* Clients are corroboration and context, not a term in the construct.
- Online Appendix E records, per client, *"whether they indicated they were aware of how their actions
  affected freelancers' evaluation scores."* He measured the counterpart's own ignorance of her effect —
  and did not build it into the theory, because his theory is about control.
- **"Who influences the evaluation"** is the fifth component, and it is the counterpart appearing as a
  failure of *interpretation*: the worker cannot tell whether the client or the platform moved the
  score. That is the closest any neighbour comes to our position, and it stops one step short. Not
  knowing which party moved the score is a problem about the score. Ours is a problem about the party.
- The one place workers act toward the counterpart is constrained reactivity — taking platform-met
  clients off-platform, against a user agreement barring off-platform payment for two years on pain of
  deactivation. Reaching the counterpart requires **leaving the arrangement**, at risk of the account.
  That is the same shape as Sutherland's P39 and it should be said once, across both.

## The test

Yes, and the evidence is in his own findings. A freelancer can hold a fully serviceable theory of the
evaluation — its criteria shift, its execution is irregular, its magnitude is unknowable — and act on it
competently by experimenting or by withdrawing, and still have no way to work out what the client
wanted, no way to answer a private rating she cannot see, and no way to tell whether the client or the
platform closed the door. Rahman's own workers demonstrate this: they read every available article
about the algorithm and remained paranoid. **A perfect reading of the cage is not a coordination with
the person on the other side of it.**

## What we inherit

Three things, and we should say so rather than treat him only as a boundary.

1. **Shifting criteria as an environmental condition**, which is our third operation stated as the world
   rather than as a competence.
2. **The five components as a decomposition of what is withheld.** Criteria maps to interpreting;
   execution and magnitude to keeping track; impact maps to nothing a worker can supply; who-influences
   maps to the counterpart problem and names a failure mode our three operations do not cover — not
   knowing *which party to interpret*.
3. **Dependence and setback as rival explanations.** His divergence is explained by structural position
   and by score history, not by competence. Any variance claim of ours has to survive both. This is the
   same discipline the review demanded for Brynjolfsson and Dell'Acqua, arriving from inside the
   organizational literature.

## What adding items will not get

Reactivity is cut by **engagement level** — experiment more, or expose yourself less. Ours is cut by
**direction and time**. Items added to a reactivity measure would refine the engagement cut; they would
not produce a second axis, because the construct's dependent variable is the worker's exposure to the
evaluation, and the client is not on that axis at any value. He proves this against himself: he had
eighteen clients in the field and the construct still came out with two terms.
