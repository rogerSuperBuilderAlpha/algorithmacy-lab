## Their claim, in their words

The definition, verified across all three sources and quoted identically in every one:

> a set of competencies that enables individuals to critically evaluate AI technologies; communicate and
> collaborate effectively with AI; and use AI as a tool online, at home, and in the workplace

Method: an interdisciplinary literature synthesis across education, HCI and AI, yielding **seventeen
competencies** organised under five questions — *What is AI? What can AI do? How does AI work? How
should AI be used? How do people perceive AI?* — plus fifteen design considerations for teaching them.
A framework, not a measure: nothing here is factor-analysed, and no items exist.

Four competencies verbatim (from the secondary that quotes them):

- **1, Recognizing AI:** *"Distinguish between technological artifacts that use and do not use AI."*
- **2, Understanding Intelligence:** *"Critically analyze and discuss features that make an entity
  'intelligent', including discussing differences between human, animal, and machine intelligence."*
- **16, Ethics:** *"Identify and describe different perspectives on the key ethical issues surrounding
  AI (i.e. privacy, employment, misinformation, the singularity, ethical decision making, diversity,
  bias, transparency, accountability)."*
- **17, Programmability:** *"Understand that agents are programmable."*

The remaining thirteen names, as grouped in the integrative review and **not yet verified against ACM**:
Interdisciplinarity and General vs. Narrow AI (under *What is AI?*); AI's Strengths & Weaknesses and
Imagine Future AI (under *What can AI do?*); Representations, Decision-Making, ML Steps, Human Role in
AI, Data Literacy, Learning from Data, Critically Interpreting Data, Action & Reaction, and Sensors
(under *How does AI work?*).

## Best version

The framework is more than a knowledge inventory, and two of its clauses are the ones a reviewer will
press.

**The definition's second clause names collaboration:** "communicate and collaborate effectively with
AI." A construct that already claims communication and collaboration as competencies cannot be waved
past by a paper whose contribution is a communication competency. §3's answer is the right one — the
collaboration is *with AI*, so the machine occupies the chair the counterpart would sit in — and it has
to be made in their own wording rather than by characterisation.

**And the framework is aimed at a general population, not at workers**, which is a scope difference
rather than a defect. Long and Magerko write for learners and designers of learning; their fifteen design
considerations are pedagogical. Judging a curriculum framework by whether it supports a validated
individual-difference measure is judging it by a standard it never set.

## What they make the algorithm

An **object of knowledge**. Every competency is a relation between a person and a technology: recognise
it, understand how it represents, know the steps by which it learns, interpret its data critically,
identify its ethical stakes.

## Hunt for the counterpart

**The card's current formulation is falsifiable and must be narrowed.** `cards/long2020.md` says "No
competency in the list mentions another person," and §3 says "none names another person." Competency 10
is called **Human Role in AI**. A reviewer holding the table will find it in ten seconds.

The distinction survives, and it is worth more stated precisely than asserted broadly. *Human Role in AI*
concerns humans as the makers and supervisors of the system — the people who choose models, curate
training data, and stay in the loop — which the secondary source confirms in its own behavioural
anchors, running from "describes the value of humans-in-loop" to model-tuning conduct. Humans appear in
the framework as **producers of the AI**, never as a party the learner is coordinating with through it.
Nothing in the seventeen asks whether the person on the other side of a match received what was meant.

The safe form of the sentence, for §3 and for the card: *no competency names another person as a party
to the interaction*; the one competency that names humans at all names them as the system's designers.
Both halves are checkable, and conceding the second is what makes the first survive.

## The test

Yes, and the gap is wide. A person can satisfy every one of the seventeen — she recognises AI, explains
what makes an entity intelligent, walks through the steps of machine learning, interprets a dataset
critically, articulates the ethical stakes, and understands that agents are programmable — and have no
capacity whatever to make herself legible to a human counterpart through a system that reads them both
and commits a decision. The framework would score her at ceiling. Its five questions are all questions
*about the system*; not one of them is a question about a person reached through it.

## What we inherit

Two things.

**The derivation contrast, which is our sharpest illustration of the admission rule.** Their seventeen
come from synthesising what others have said AI literacy involves. There is no rule in the framework
that would exclude an eighteenth, and none that explains why these seventeen and not twenty. Our facets
are derived from what the form withholds, and the contrast lands here more cleanly than against any
other neighbour.

**A design constraint on our own study, not a boundary claim.** Long and Magerko's setting is teaching,
and AI literacy is what a teaching context is likeliest to be measuring by default. The Hult course
teaches something in this space. If the instrument measures algorithmacy and the curriculum teaches AI
literacy, discriminant validity stops being an abstract requirement and becomes a design problem for
§5.

## What adding items will not get

Ng, Leung, Chu and Qiao (2021) already compressed the seventeen into four cells — know and understand,
use and apply, evaluate and create, ethics — and later instruments inherit those cells. Every one of
them is a relation between a person and a technology. Items multiply inside cells that were drawn
without a counterpart, which is the clearest case in §3 of a boundary that more measurement cannot move.

## Outstanding, and the only real gap in Phase 1

not the absolute one.
