# Steelman — Spitzberg (2006)

Read 2026-08-19 from the publisher's full text, *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* 11(2),
629–666, doi:10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00030.x (bronze open access at Oxford Academic). Quotations are
verbatim; page anchors still need the PDF, which OUP serves behind Cloudflare — one library session
closes that.

## Their claim, in their words

CMC, for him, is *"any human symbolic text-based interaction conducted or facilitated through
digitally-based technologies."* Competence in it carries the relational architecture into a mediated
setting: motivation, knowledge, skills, context, outcomes.

- **Motivation:** *"the ratio of approach to avoidance attitudes, beliefs, and values in a given CMC
  context."*
- **Knowledge:** *"the cognitive comprehension of content and procedural processes involved in
  conducting appropriate and effective interaction in the computer-mediated context."*
- **Skills:** four clusters — attentiveness, composure, coordination, expressiveness.
- **Context:** five facets — cultural, chronological, relational, environmental, functional.

The outcome criteria are where the model does its evaluative work:

- **Appropriateness:** *"perceived legitimacy or fit of a message to the context."*
- **Effectiveness:** *"degree to which preferred objectives are optimized."*
- **Efficiency:** *"relative economy with which preferred outcomes are achieved."*
- **Satisfaction:** *"positive affect associated with fulfillment of positively valenced expectancies."*

The empirical arm is a set of pilot studies, and he says so repeatedly — *"research on the CMC
competence model is nascent."* The measurement outcome is candid: negatively worded items attenuated
subscale reliability, *"the various items designed to measure context, message, and media factors were
not as multidimensionally complex as originally anticipated,"* and when all items are factor-analysed,
*"four reliable factors emerge that roughly parallel motivation, knowledge, skills, and outcomes."* The
hypothesized structure did not appear as drawn. §3's sentence to that effect is correct.

## Best version

Two things a sympathetic reviewer will press, and the second one is the important one.

**First, he is the genuine predecessor.** This is the only member of the competence lineage proper that
takes mediation as its subject, and the paper says so in §4. The move he makes — mediation changes the
*parameters* of competence without changing its structure, because the parties on either end remain the
coordinators — is exactly the move our paper has to refuse, and refusing it well requires stating it
this way rather than as a channel metaphor.

**Second, his model is not a person's-property model, and the counterpart is inside it.** He writes that
*"competent interactants can facilitate the competence of cointeractants"* and that *"an incompetent
interactant can diminish a normally competent cointeractant's performance."* Competence in this
tradition is produced between people and judged by the partner: messages *"are filtered through the
receivers' expectations for messages in those media."* Anyone who reads Spitzberg as measuring a
faculty that sits in one head has read the instrument and not the model.

That reading bears directly on the live review's first finding. §4 currently cites Spitzberg and Cupach
(1984) for a distinction between what a party is able to do and what she did on an occasion. The 2006
article — which we have, in full, and which the 1984 book stands behind — runs in the other direction:
competence is a judgement rendered in an interaction, with the co-interactant able to raise or lower it.
The repair the review proposes is not a concession. **It is the strongest version of our claim**, and
Spitzberg (2006) supplies it without waiting on a library copy of the book.

## What they make the algorithm

A **channel with properties**. Media *"provide different sensory information"* producing *"different
effects"*; interactivity facilitates *"socioemotionally and relationally focused functions."* The medium
constrains, filters and imposes conventions. It has no stake, renders no judgement, and produces no
output of its own.

## Hunt for the counterpart

Present, central, and unavoidable — which is why this construct has to be handled as the near case
rather than as an early one. The partner appears in the definition of knowledge (*"appropriate and
effective interaction"*), in every outcome criterion (appropriateness is perceived by someone;
satisfaction is someone's affect), and in the co-interactant claims quoted above. The receiver's
expectations are the filter through which competence is assessed.

What the counterpart never does in Spitzberg's arrangement is get **displaced**. She is on the far end
of the channel, reachable, and her judgement is the measure. Our arrangement removes her from the
position where she could render it.

## The test

Yes, but the failure is instructive rather than flat. A person can be fully CMC-competent — motivated,
media-knowledgeable, attentive, composed, coordinated, expressive, and rated appropriate and effective
by her partner — and still be unable to coordinate through a system that reads both parties and commits
a decision neither controls. The reason is that his outcome criteria have no administrator here. If
appropriateness is *perceived legitimacy* and satisfaction is the partner's affect, then a system that
prevents the partner from perceiving, judging or replying has not lowered the score. **It has removed
the instrument.** That is a sharper result than "the model stops at the channel," and it is available
from his own definitions.

## What we inherit

The evaluative axis, which our three operations do not supply. Appropriateness and effectiveness state
what counts as performing the operations *well*, and any later instrument of ours needs that pair or
something like it. We also inherit his honesty about a preliminary measure: he published a model whose
factor structure did not replicate and said so in the article that proposed it.

## What adding items will not get

His items are written for a dyad through a medium. Adding items about an algorithm produces items about
the medium's properties — its affordances, its conventions, its constraints — because that is the only
role the model has for a technology. The missing element is not a media parameter. It is a third
occupant who evaluates and commits, and the model has no chair for one.
