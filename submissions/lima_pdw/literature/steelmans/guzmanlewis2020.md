# Steelman — Guzman & Lewis (2020)

Read 2026-08-19 from `../pdfs/guzmanlewis2020.pdf` (*New Media & Society* 22(1),
70–86; online 2019).

## Their claim, in their words

Communicative AI (conversational agents, social robots, automated writing) is
“designed as communicative subjects, instead of mere interactive objects” (p. 71).
Human–machine communication “focuses on questions of communication as they
relate to technologies designed to fulfill the communicator role” (p. 73):
meaning-making “within a communication context in which at least one of the
interaction partners is a machine” (p. 74; corrected 2026-08-26 — an earlier
version of this memo carried p. 73, and the quotation sits on p. 74 of the PDF).

The agenda has three parts (p. 74): **functional** (how people make sense of
devices as communicators), **relational** (AI’s place in social worlds; self in
relation to the machine), **metaphysical** (what then counts as human, machine,
and communication).

This is an agenda, not a finding and not a competence measure.

## Best version

They are precise about the shift from CMC. CMC research “theorizes technology as
a mediator of human interaction” (p. 72). HMC theorizes the machine as a
**partner**. They say the anthropocentric definition of communication is the
obstacle, and they want theory that can treat a device as a communicator without
reducing it to a channel. That is a real and useful move for voice assistants,
bots, and social robots.

## What they make the algorithm

A communicator. A partner. One pole of a dyad.

## Hunt for the counterpart

The “other” in this article is the machine. Humans appear as the people who talk
to AI, or as the historical occupants of the communicator role the machine now
takes. A second human who is being reached *through* the system is the CMC
picture they are leaving. Relational dynamics are person–AI, not person–person
via AI (that is Hancock et al.’s AI-MC).

## The test

A person can be fully competent at HMC — she treats the system as a partner,
reads its cues, maintains the relation — and never have coordinated with another
human through a system that reads both and decides. The competence HMC would
specify, if it specified one, is competence *with the machine*.

## What we inherit

The cleanest statement that CMC’s channel and HMC’s partner are different
boundaries. We use that arrangement; we do not borrow their ontology.

## What adding items will not get

Items about “communicating with AI” stay inside the dyad they opened. The
missing position is the human counterpart on the other side of a binding
intermediary.
