# Steelman — Sutherland, Jarrahi, Dunn & Nelson (2020)

Read 2026-08-19 from `../pdfs/sutherland2020.pdf` (WES 34(3), 457–475).

## Their claim, in their words

Gig literacies are “critical literacies… emerging around online freelancing.” Workers
“must adapt their skills and work strategies in order to leverage platforms creatively
and productively, and as a component of their ‘personal holding environment’. This
involves not only using the resources provided by the platform effectively, but also
negotiating or working around its imposed structures and control mechanisms”
(p. 457). Later they name the thing a **platform literacy**: “the know-how required
to leverage platform resources in order to minimize the precarity of independent
work, while retaining as much autonomy as possible from those platform structures
themselves” (p. 470).

Method: 20 freelancer interviews, **19 client interviews**, walkthrough of Upwork
(one author as client, one as freelancer). Thematic analysis; clients used to check
which literacies were visible to the other side (pp. 461–463).

## Best version

This is not a worker–system cartoon. They interviewed the counterpart. Their own
findings headings are five literacies (pp. 463–469):

1. **Building reputation** — Job Success Score, badges, private client feedback,
   refunds to block bad reviews.
2. **Self-presentation** — profiles, chat that bumps a bid, tests clients set.
3. **Maintaining productivity** — routines, third-party tools, avoiding the diary.
4. **Mitigating transaction risk** — escrow, logging chat, vetting clients, “gut
   feeling” for who is trustworthy.
5. **Building relationships** — rapport, repeat business, often **off-platform**;
   also peer networks for belonging.

“Building relationships” is their heading, not a reviewer’s gloss. P10: engage the
client “to build a rapport and a little longer lasting relationship” for repeat
business (p. 468). They contrast this with Uber: on Upwork the worker can choose
and talk to the client; on Uber the algorithm assigns (p. 470).

## What they make the algorithm

An obstacle and a resource inside a marketplace. Ratings and ranking are things
to learn and work around. The algorithm does not commit a decision that binds
both parties in the Stark sense; Upwork matches and scores; the client still hires
and can take the relation off-site.

## Hunt for the counterpart

| Place | What appears |
|---|---|
| Sample | 19 clients, about the same platform. |
| Definition | Freelancer skills for using and working around the platform. The client is not in the definition. |
| Findings | Client is in reputation (ratings, refunds), self-presentation (who notices you), risk (scams, gut), and **relationship building as a named literacy**. |
| Discussion | Relationship building is “the foundation of longer-term engagements with clients” and is essential *because* Upwork leaves more agency than Uber (p. 470). |

Clients' narratives “provided some verification as to which literacies or skills
were in fact valued in gig workers” (p. 463) — corroboration, not a second pole of
the construct. (Corrected 2026-08-26 against the PDF: an earlier version of this
memo spliced “were apparent” from the preceding sentence, which is about workers'
own accounts.)

## The test

A person can fully satisfy gig literacies — good score, good profile, productive,
safe transactions, a stable off-platform client list — and still never have
coordinated *through* an intermediary that reads both sides and commits a
decision neither controls. Their most counterpart-heavy literacy is a strategy for
**escaping** mediation (repeat work off-platform after a bad score; P39, p. 468).
That is the opposite of the competence we name.

## What we inherit

Enrollment without standing as a competence burden. Situated learning of an
opaque rating. The five-aspect list, now verified as their headings.

## What adding items will not get

Putting “relationship building” on a scale still measures a literacy *beside* the
platform. Algorithmacy is defined on coordination that runs *through* an
intermediary neither party can bypass. Upwork is a hard neighbour and the
wrong form.
