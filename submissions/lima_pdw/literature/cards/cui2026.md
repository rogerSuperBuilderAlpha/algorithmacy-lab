## What it argues

Three randomised controlled trials, run **"by the companies as part of their ordinary course of business"** at Microsoft, Accenture and an unnamed Fortune 100 firm, gave a random subset of developers access to an AI code-completion assistant. The authors are candid that the individual trials are underpowered: **"each experiment is noisy and results vary across experiments."** Pooled across **4,867 developers**, they report **"a 26.08% increase (standard error: 10.3%) in completed tasks among developers using the AI tool."**

On heterogeneity, one sentence, and it points the same way as Brynjolfsson et al.: **"Notably, less experienced developers had higher adoption rates and greater productivity gains."** Two distinct channels are bundled there — differential *adoption* and differential *return conditional on adoption* — and the abstract does not separate them.

## Relation to the argument

The fourth and largest independent replication of divergence-by-experience, and the only one in this cluster where variation across sites is reported rather than hidden: the same instrument produced results that "vary across experiments" at three different firms. That within-technology, across-context variation is closer to what §1 wants than anything in the worker-level results, but the paper attributes it to noise rather than to organisational difference, and offers no test.

The adoption channel deserves separate attention in §6. If less experienced developers adopt more, then measured "intensity of use" is itself endogenous to position, which complicates §1's claim that outcomes do not track "the tools they use." The manuscript should not treat tool volume as an exogenous covariate.

## Caution

Vendor-run experiments at the firms that make and sell the product, with two co-authors at Microsoft; the trials were not designed as research. The 26.08% estimate carries a standard error of 10.3% — a wide interval that the abstract does not hide and citations routinely do. No volume or page numbers exist yet: cite as advance online publication with the DOI, and re-verify before the Lima manuscript is fixed. Outcome is completed tasks, not earnings or retention. Do not cite for unexplained heterogeneity; the reported heterogeneity is explained by experience.
