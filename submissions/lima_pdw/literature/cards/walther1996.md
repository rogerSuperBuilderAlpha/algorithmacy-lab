## What it argues

The hyperpersonal model. Walther argues CMC can be not merely as personal as face-to-face interaction but *more* so, and specifies four components that jointly produce the effect: **selective self-presentation** by the sender, who exploits the channel's editability to present an optimised self; **idealisation** by the receiver, who fills the missing cues with favourable inference; **channel management**, the asynchrony and editability that make composition strategic; and a **behavioural confirmation** feedback loop in which the idealised partner is treated as ideal and behaves accordingly.

The 2007 extension (doi:10.1016/j.chb.2006.05.002) elaborates selective self-presentation as a cognitive and linguistic process; the 25-year retrospective with Whitty (doi:10.1177/0261927X20967703) reviews what survived.

## Relation to the argument

Selective self-presentation is the most fully specified antecedent of **intent specification** anywhere in communication theory. Walther's sender exploits editability and asynchrony to compress a self into what the channel will carry, choosing what to include, what to omit, and what to let the receiver infer — which is §7's facet exactly, absent the compression-loss framing.

The receiver side is where the paper's arrangement diverges instructively. Walther's mechanism depends on the receiver **over-attributing** from sparse cues, and the loop closes because the sender then experiences the idealised treatment. On a platform the receiving position is occupied by a system that does not idealise and does not confirm; it scores. The hyperpersonal effect and the algorithmic gate are the same structure with different receivers, and one produces intimacy while the other produces the invisible cage. That is a sharp illustration of why §9's third position matters, and it is drawn from the CMC literature's own best-known model rather than asserted against it.

## Caution

The model's components have received uneven empirical support; selective self-presentation is well evidenced, behavioural confirmation much less so. 1996 CMC, and the retrospective concedes much of the model was built for a media environment that no longer exists. Do not treat the four components as established mechanisms.
