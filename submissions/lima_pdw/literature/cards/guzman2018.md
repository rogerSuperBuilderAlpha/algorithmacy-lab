## What it argues

The volume that named the field. Guzman's introduction traces the assumption HMC contests — that communication technology is a channel between humans, an assumption she shows was built into the discipline's founding models and survived every revision to them — and argues that machines designed to occupy the role of communicator break it. The chapters work through voice assistants, robots, chatbots, and algorithmic curation, and the recurring analytic question is the **ontological** one: what kind of entity is the machine taken to be by the person interacting with it, and what follows.

Guzman's own later article, "Ontological boundaries between humans and computers" (doi:10.30658/hmc.1.3), develops this into the field's central theoretical problem.

## Relation to the argument

Useful to §9 mainly as confirmation that the "partner" characterisation is the field's self-description rather than the paper's imposition. Guzman's framing move is explicitly to relocate the machine *from* the channel position *to* the communicator position — one seat vacated, another occupied — and that is a substitution within a two-position model, not the addition of a third position.

The ontological question is also a live one for the paper. §7's construct depends on the worker treating the system as an opaque adjudicator rather than as a partner or a tool, and HMC's evidence is that people's ontological classification of a machine varies and shapes how they interact with it. If workers differ in what they take the gate to *be*, that is a candidate explanation for §10's stratified fluency competing with the competency account, and it comes from the literature §9 is dismissing.

## Caution

An edited collection: coherent framing, uneven chapters, and no single authoritative statement of the field's theory. Crossref lists it as an edited-book with no author field; Guzman is the editor and should be cited as such. For a citable statement of HMC's programme, Guzman & Lewis (2020) or Fortunati & Edwards (2020) are cleaner.
