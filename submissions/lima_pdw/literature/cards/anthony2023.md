## What it argues

Anthony, Bechky and Fayard ask what it means to work "with" AI, and they answer by sorting the technology-in-organizations literature into lenses. Two are inherited: technology as a **tool** and technology as a **medium**. They then propose a third, which treats technology as a **counterpart in a system of work** — a system that includes the technology's design, its implementation and its use. Because that perspective needs a grounded account of how AI meets work, they argue for ethnography, and specifically for relational ethnographic approaches, and they set out strategies for doing it. The paper is a perspective piece that frames directions for research; the abstract reports no data.

## Relation to the argument

**Gives the new introduction its "counterpart" and, with it, the tool → medium → counterpart sequence that the paper's own oracy → literacy → algorithmacy sequence can be set beside.** The author's introduction cites it for generative AI "functioning more as - but not quite - "counterparts" in a system, implementation, and use". The abstract supports the word "counterpart" and the three-part system, but its list is "design, implementation, and use"; the draft's "a system, implementation, and use" drops *design* and promotes *system* into the list. "But not quite" is the author's own qualification and the abstract neither supports nor contradicts it. The abstract speaks of AI in general, not generative AI.

The paper matters to the triad argument for a second reason that the draft does not yet use. Its "medium" lens is the position section 2 assigns to computer-mediated communication (the technology as channel between two people), and its "counterpart" lens is the position section 2 assigns to human–machine communication (the technology as the other party). If the body bears that out, Anthony et al. have already named, inside organization theory, the two dyadic readings the paper says miss the triad — and the question becomes whether their *system of work* already contains the third position. That needs the full text.
