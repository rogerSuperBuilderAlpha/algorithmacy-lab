## What it argues

Xiao and colleagues ran roughly 600 participants through either a standard Qualtrics survey or an AI-powered chatbot that asked the same open-ended questions, interpreted free-text answers, and probed when an answer was thin. Analysing over 5,200 free-text responses against Gricean maxims, they found the chatbot condition produced significantly higher engagement and better-quality responses on informativeness, relevance, specificity and clarity.

## Relation to the argument

The strongest empirical warrant available for the study's collection instrument, and the one that answers the reviewer's first question — does an agent-conducted interview produce worse data? Here it produced better data than the text-box alternative, and the mechanism was probing: the agent did not accept a thin answer. That is precisely the "knock twice" rule in `AGENT.md`, arrived at independently after the first pilot run took five closing answers at face value.

## Caution

The comparison is chatbot versus *online survey*, not chatbot versus human interviewer — it establishes that agent probing beats a text box, not that it matches a skilled interviewer. The system predates current LLMs, the questions were survey-length rather than 45-minute semi-structured, and the quality measure is Gricean rather than interpretive depth. Cite it for the probing effect and nothing wider.
