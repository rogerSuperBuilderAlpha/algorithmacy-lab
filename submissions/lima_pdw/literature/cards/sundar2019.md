# Sundar, S. S., & Kim, J. (2019). Machine heuristic: When we trust computers more than humans with our personal information. In *Proceedings of the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (pp. 1–9). ACM.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1145/3290605.3300768  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** ACM Digital Library

**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Sundar and Kim test the **machine heuristic**: the mental shortcut by which people infer that a machine, being mechanical, is objective, unbiased, and secure — and therefore safer to disclose to than a human. In their experiment participants disclosed more personal information and expressed more trust when they believed a machine rather than a person was handling it, and the effect was mediated by endorsement of the heuristic. The finding inverts the usual automation-distrust story: for some tasks, machine attribution is a trust *advantage*.

## Relation to the argument

Directly relevant to §10's stratified fluency and to §7's asymmetric interpretation, because it names an individual difference in how a worker construes the system. A worker who endorses the machine heuristic reads a gate's determination as objective and therefore as information about her work; a worker who does not reads it as an artefact of a rule with interests behind it. **Those two workers will do completely different interpretive work on the same outcome, and only the second is doing what §7 calls asymmetric interpretation.**

That is a candidate mechanism for variance — and a rival to the competency account, since heuristic endorsement is a belief rather than a capacity and could be measured directly. §11 should include it, either as a control or as a component: if algorithmacy scores collapse once machine-heuristic endorsement is partialled out, the construct is measuring a disposition, not a competency.

## Caution

A short CHI paper, online experiment, privacy-disclosure task, single context. The machine heuristic is Sundar's construct from the MAIN model and its measurement is a short self-report scale. Do not extend the trust finding to evaluative gates without saying it is an extrapolation. Kim is second author.
