## What it argues

Brannick and Coghlan take the standard objection to insider research — that being native disqualifies you — and dismantle it paradigm by paradigm. Working through positivism, hermeneutics and action research, they find that none of the three contains an inherent reason why complete membership invalidates research. They then name the four dynamics that insider research actually has to manage: **access**, **preunderstanding**, **role duality**, and **organizational politics**, and they conclude that the value of insider research is worth reaffirming rather than defending apologetically.

## Relation to the argument

This is the strongest single answer to the objection an OS/OT reviewer will raise first, and it is in *Organizational Research Methods*, which is where that reviewer lives. Their four dynamics map almost exactly onto §9 of the protocol: access is the reason the study is possible at all; preunderstanding is what §10 concedes; role duality is the instructor-investigator conflict and is handled by removing the vote and the consent record; organizational politics is why the third-party consent holder receives withdrawals before grades are final. ## Caution

It is a defence, and a reviewer who has already decided will read it as special pleading unless the structural handling is shown to be real. Note also that the authors write primarily from action research, where the insider is *meant* to intervene; this study's investigator is trying to do the opposite, so the role-duality argument transfers but the action-research warrant does not.
