## What it argues

Ng and colleagues take Long and Magerko's seventeen competencies and compress them into four aspects that have since become the field's working ontology: **know and understand AI**, **use and apply AI**, **evaluate and create AI**, and **ethical issues**. The compression is deliberate and analogical — they adapt the structure of "classic literacies" (reading, digital, media) to AI, which is why the aspects arrange themselves as a Bloom-style ladder from comprehension to creation with ethics bolted on the end. The evidence base is thirty peer-reviewed articles, reviewed exploratorily rather than systematically, and the stated purpose is to give the emerging field "a sound theoretical foundation to define, teach and evaluate AI literacy."

The paper's influence exceeds its evidence. Carolus et al. built MAILS directly on this four-aspect scheme, generating sixty items to populate it; Markus et al.'s AICOS retains the same sub-competencies with Detect AI and Generative AI added; Ng's own group later turned it into a 25-item questionnaire for secondary students. When a 2023 or 2025 AI literacy instrument reports factors called "Use & Apply AI" or "Know & Understand AI," it is reporting Ng et al.'s categories.

The review also flags what it does not do: it establishes "the groundwork for future research such as competency development and assessment criteria," conceding that competency development is downstream work. Teaching and ethical concern are the two applications the authors name. Work is not among them.

## Relation to the argument

Neighbour, and the one that explains why the whole downstream scale literature has the same blind spot.

The four aspects are all one-place predicates on a knower. "Know and understand AI" takes AI as object. "Use and apply AI" takes AI as instrument. "Evaluate and create AI" takes AI as artefact under assessment or construction. "Ethical issues" takes AI as a bearer of moral properties — bias, privacy, fairness — that the learner should be able to identify. None of the four has a second human argument place. There is no aspect for *coordinating with someone through* AI, and no aspect in which the AI commits anything to anyone. The paper's own framing sentence about AI spreading "to enhance user experience, improve work efficiency, and create many future job opportunities" tells you where the counterparty went: dissolved into "user experience," which is one person and a screen.

This matters more than the Long and Magerko card does, because Ng et al. is the *transmission mechanism*. The paper's §9 claim that adding items to AI literacy will never produce algorithmacy gains its force here: every subsequent instrument inherits a four-cell scheme with no relational position in it, so item addition happens inside cells that are already defined without a counterpart. The structural deficiency is upstream of measurement.

On derivation: this is inventory in its purest form — thirty papers, coded, compressed by analogy to prior literacies. On competency versus skill: Ng et al. describe something explicitly designed to be taught and evaluated in classrooms, which is skill by the paper's criterion. On the two properties: implicit acquisition is not merely absent, it is contrary to the paper's whole purpose, which is to define what should be taught.

Cite it in §9 as the moment when AI literacy acquired the shape it still has, and as evidence that the missing position was never lost — it was never there.

## Caution

Exploratory review, thirty articles, no stated inclusion protocol or coding reliability; the authors call it exploratory and it should not be cited as a systematic review. Read at abstract depth — the four aspects are firmly established from the abstract and from downstream instruments that name them, but the article's own definitional sentence and its treatment of the teaching literature should be checked in the open-access PDF before quotation. Nothing here is empirical: no sample, no items, no psychometrics. Do not cite it for anything about workers, workplaces, or platform labour; the corpus is education and general-public AI understanding.
