# Bachman, L. F. (1990). *Fundamental considerations in language testing*. Oxford University Press.

**Identifier:** ISBN 9780194370035 (0194370038)  ·  **Read depth:** extended_preview  ·  **Source read:** Oxford Applied Linguistics edition, chs. 4 and 6; McNamara's *Language Testing* review essay (doi:10.1191/0265532203lt268xx)
**Source-tier:** book
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Bachman reorganises Canale and Swain into a model built for measurement rather than for teaching. **Communicative language ability** has three parts: language competence, strategic competence, and psychophysiological mechanisms. Language competence splits into *organizational* competence (grammatical and textual) and *pragmatic* competence (illocutionary and sociolinguistic), each further partitioned. Strategic competence is promoted out of the residual-repair role Canale and Swain gave it and made a general executive — assessment, planning, and execution — that mediates between language competence, the language user's knowledge structures, and the context of situation.

The book's real contribution is the insistence that a competence model and a measurement model are different objects and must be joined explicitly. Half of it is about test method facets, reliability, validity, and the ways a test's own format contaminates the inference from performance to competence.

## Relation to the argument

Directly useful to §11 and to any scale the paper licenses, in two ways. First, Bachman's promotion of strategic competence to an executive that assesses a situation, plans, and executes is the nearest existing description of what §7's three facets do *together*: reading the situation from outcomes, compressing intent to fit the channel, revising when the situation moves. A reviewer who knows this literature will see the resemblance, and the paper is better off naming it than being caught by it.

Second, Bachman's test-method-facets argument is the discipline §7's "convergence disclosure" already half-applies. He shows that a trait score is always confounded with the format that elicited it, which is exactly the risk when algorithmacy is measured by self-report items about a platform the respondent is currently subject to. The construct-validation caution the paper owes Zhou et al. (2025) has a methodological form here.

## Caution

The domain is language testing; the architecture is heavily componential and was criticised at the time for proliferating boxes faster than evidence. Do not adopt the taxonomy. Note the edition tangle: OpenLibrary carries a 1988 Addison-Wesley record (ISBN 9780194370028) alongside the 1990 Oxford imprint — cite 1990, Oxford. Bachman & Palmer (1996), *Language testing in practice*, is the applied successor; I did not verify a publisher-side ISBN for it and have not carded it.
