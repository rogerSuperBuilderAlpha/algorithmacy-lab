# Obstfeld, D. (2005). Social networks, the *tertius iungens* orientation, and involvement in innovation. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 50(1), 100–130.

**Identifier:** doi:10.2189/asqu.2005.50.1.100  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** SAGE Journals
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Obstfeld proposes the counterpart to Burt's tertius gaudens: **tertius iungens**, "the third who joins," a strategic orientation in which a broker introduces disconnected parties to each other or facilitates coordination between already-connected ones. He measures it as an individual **orientation** — a disposition, with a validated scale — and shows in an automotive-engineering division that people high on it are more involved in innovation, net of network structure. His central move is that brokerage is not a position alone; it is a position plus a behavioural orientation, and the same structural location can be worked either way.

## Relation to the argument

Two contributions. First, Obstfeld demonstrates that a **third-party orientation can be operationalised as an individual difference and validated** — which is the closest methodological precedent in organisation studies for what §11 proposes. He derived a small scale for a disposition about relations among three parties and showed it predicted outcomes net of structure. That is the shape of the algorithmacy instrument, and it comes from a top journal in the paper's own field.

Second, the gaudens/iungens contrast is a diagnostic for the platform. Obstfeld's insight is that occupying a hole does not determine how it is worked; the same third party can separate or join. Platforms claim iungens (they connect) and are structured as gaudens (they profit from non-connection), and §5's withholdings are the evidence. That is a compact way to state the paper's critique in a vocabulary *Administrative Science Quarterly* readers already hold — and this paper's nearest neighbours in §9, Rahman (2021) and Curchod et al. (2020), are ASQ papers.

## Caution

A single organisational site, cross-sectional network data, and an orientation measured by self-report. The iungens construct describes a *person* choosing to connect others; the platform is not choosing in that sense, so the diagnostic is analogical. Do not attribute intention to the system on Obstfeld's authority.
