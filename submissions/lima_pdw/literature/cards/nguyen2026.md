## What it argues

Nguyen and Welch step back from the enthusiasm and ask what kind of technological artifact a generative model is before asking whether it belongs in qualitative analysis. Their technically informed evaluation finds significant shortcomings and concludes that uncritical adoption by management researchers would introduce unacceptable epistemic risks, because the essence of qualitative data analysis lies in the interpretation of meaning, which they hold to be an inherently human capability.

## Relation to the argument

The most important citation in this sub-cluster, and the paper should cite it against itself. It is in *Organizational Research Methods*, it is recent, it is sceptical, and any reviewer in this space will know it. Citing it lets the paper draw the line it needs: the agent collects, humans interpret. §7's analysis is entirely human — open in-vivo coding, a Paper-2-blind second coder, memos, a discard log — and Nguyen and Welch's objection lands on the analysis stage, which this design does not automate. Engaging them explicitly is far stronger than hoping the reviewer does not raise it.

## Caution

Their argument is about analysis, so quoting it as an objection to AI *collection* would misrepresent them; be precise about what they claim. Their position is also strong enough that a reviewer may extend it to collection whether or not the authors do — the paper needs its own argument for the collection stage, and Wuttke et al.
