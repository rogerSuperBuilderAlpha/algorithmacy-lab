## What it argues

The state-of-the-area review of CMC competence a decade after Spitzberg's model. Bunz and Montez survey the constructs that have accumulated around competent technology-mediated interaction — computer literacy, computer-mediated communication competence, computer-email-web fluency, digital literacy, media literacy, netiquette — and show they are a terminological pile rather than a research programme, measuring overlapping things with incompatible instruments. They organise the area around the medium's affordances and the user's fluency with them, and conclude that the field lacks both an agreed construct and a measure that survives a change of platform.

## Relation to the argument

Two direct uses. First, this is the **evidence** for §9's channel characterisation, extended from Spitzberg's model to the whole area: across every construct Bunz and Montez survey, the technology is a thing the user is fluent *with*, and the communication partner remains a human on the far side. The section's claim rests on one citation; here is a survey that makes it about a literature.

Second, their "fails when the platform changes" complaint is §8's dating argument stated by the CMC literature about itself. §8 argues a competency must survive its mediator changing and that skills do not; Bunz and Montez document twenty years of measures that did not survive. That is a real precedent for the paper's demanding prediction and a warning about the cost of failing it.

## Caution

A review, not an original study, and the 2015 vintage predates the constructs §9 actually contends with — HMC, AI-MC, algorithmic competency. Crossref's record for this chapter omits the volume ISBN; the volume is 9783110317053. Montez is second author.
