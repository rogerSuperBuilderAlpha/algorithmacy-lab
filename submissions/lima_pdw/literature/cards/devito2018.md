## What it argues

An interview study with 28 participants on how folk theories get built and what they are for. Two claims. First, people draw on diverse sources when forming theories — not only their own observation but talk, press coverage, platform statements, inference from others' outcomes — and the resulting theories are more complex, multifaceted and malleable than the earlier literature assumed. Second, and this is the paper's framing move, folk theorisation is put in service of **self-presentation**: algorithmic feeds obscure information about one's audience and environment, which complicates self-presentation, and the theory exists to let the user decide how to behave.

## Relation to the argument

This is the closest published statement of §7's inbound-to-outbound structure, and the manuscript has to engage it directly rather than cite it in passing. DeVito et al. already link the two facets in a single mechanism: you theorise the opaque system *in order to* present yourself through it. That is asymmetric interpretation feeding intent specification, named, evidenced, and published seven years before this paper's derivation.

What it does not do is what §7 claims. The link is a single process — theory formation for the sake of presentation — not a partition into facets, and nothing in it derives the structure from a withheld condition. There is no separate change-detection element (DeVito adds that in 2021), no accountability position, and no counterpart: self-presentation on a feed is directed at a diffuse audience, not at a party whose wants must be reconstructed and to whom something must be conveyed. Section 7's honest position is that the *pairing* of inbound and outbound is established prior art, the *derivation* of why exactly these and no others is not, and the paper should concede the first to strengthen the second.

## Caution

Twenty-eight interviews, consumer social platforms, no economic stakes, no evaluative gate committing binding determinations. Self-presentation is a Goffmanian frame carrying its own commitments, and mapping it onto intent specification loses the transactional element §7 cares about — a submission that must pass a gate is not a performance for an audience. Read at abstract depth; the folk-theory-formation findings and the self-presentation integration should be read in full before the manuscript positions itself against them, because this is the source that most narrows §7's novelty claim.
