## What it argues

**"US productivity growth accelerated after 1995 (unlike Europe's), particularly in sectors that intensively use information technologies (IT)."** Using two micro panels the authors show **"US multinationals operating in Europe also experienced a 'productivity miracle'"** — that is, the same firms, in the same European countries, facing the same institutions and labour markets, got more out of IT than their non-US peers. **"US multinationals obtained higher productivity from IT than non-US multinationals, particularly in the same sectors responsible for the US productivity acceleration. Furthermore, establishments taken over by US multinationals (but not by non-US multinationals) increased the productivity of their IT."** The explanation: **"the US IT related productivity advantage is primarily due to its tougher 'people management' practices."**

## Relation to the argument

The cleanest natural experiment in the pre-AI literature for "same technology, same environment, different returns." Holding country, sector and institutional context fixed, the return to IT varies by who owns the establishment, and the takeover result gives the variation a plausible causal reading. If §1 wants an authority for the proposition that technology's effects are not a property of the technology, this is the strongest one available.

And, again, the answer it finds is management practice — organisational, not individual, and specifically about how people are supervised and rewarded. For the manuscript that is a double-edged citation: it establishes the phenomenon and it locates the cause where §1 does not want it.

## Caution

Firms and establishments, not workers; productivity, not earnings or exit; 1990s–2000s IT. "Tougher people management practices" is measured by the authors' management survey instrument, which has its own well-known critics. Nothing here is about platforms, algorithms or individual competency, and the inferential distance to a London driver is very large — cite for the general proposition only.
