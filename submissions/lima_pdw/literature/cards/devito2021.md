## What it argues

DeVito routes folk theorisation into literacy, explicitly, and this is the paper that does it. She adopts a definition of algorithmic literacy built on Gurstein's "effective use": "the capacity and opportunity to be aware of both the presence and impact of algorithmically-driven systems on self- or collaboratively-identified goals, and the capacity and opportunity to crystalize this understanding into a strategic use of these systems to accomplish said goals." Then she argues that no static, checklist, skills-at-one-moment treatment can serve, because the platforms change under the user; what is needed is a New Literacy, extensible by design, and folk theorisation is the mechanism that makes it extensible.

The empirical base is a Facebook-based Asynchronous Remote Community study with 25 LGBTQ+ platform users, chosen for heightened self-presentation concerns. Two constructs come out of it. **Theorization Complexity Level (TCL)** — "the level of system complexity a user is aware of, takes into account, and employs to pursue their own goals when folk theorizing" — resolves into four additive levels grouped in two families: functional theorists (basic awareness, causal powers) attend to the presence and effects of the system; structural theorists (mechanistic fragments, mechanistic ordering) attend to its causes. **Perceived platform spirit** — the user's sense of what a platform is and what it is for — governs the adaptation decision when the platform changes: adapt, limit, or leave.

The discussion states the literacy target precisely. Structural theorisation "appears to give the user agency"; functional theorists "are left to simply react to effects." The threshold for literacy is not full mechanistic knowledge but knowing that multiple mechanistic fragments feed the decision, which is enough to tell the user to ramp up information gathering when something changes. DeVito then proposes two routes for lifting functional theorists to structural: scaffolding causal understanding by interrogating mechanistic detail, and supplying trusted experts.

## Relation to the argument

The most dangerous single source in this sub-cluster, and the manuscript must confront it in §7 and §9 rather than list it. DeVito already holds three of the four things the paper claims for algorithmacy. Her literacy is acquired through participation, not instruction — folk theorisation is what users do because the rule is undisclosed, which is §10's implicit acquisition, named and evidenced. Her TCL findings are a stratified-fluency result: users of equivalent standing on the same platform theorise at four measurably different levels. And her definition is not an inventory of reported practices but a functional derivation from what changing opaque platforms deny the user, which is uncomfortably close to §6's derivation claim.

Two discriminations survive, and the manuscript should lead with them. First and decisively, **the counterpart is absent**. DeVito's user pursues "self- or collaboratively-identified goals" through a platform toward an audience — self-presentation to a diffuse, invisible public. Algorithmacy is defined on a specific other party whose intent must be reconstructed and to whom something must be conveyed, through a system that then *commits a determination binding on both*. TikTok deciding not to show your video binds you; it does not bind a named counterpart who wanted something from you. That is the positional difference, and it is not a matter of degree: DeVito's relation has two positions plus an audience, not three positions with a shared, committing intermediary.

Second, DeVito's literacy is **explicitly teachable and she designs for teaching it**. The whole of §5.1.1 asks how to boost users to structural theorisation — scaffolding, expert provision, informal multiliteracies pedagogy. That is the skill/competency line in §8 falling on the other side: a capacity that instruction can raise on demand is a skill in the manuscript's vocabulary, however implicitly it was first acquired. Algorithmacy's claim is that the competency dates with the form and cannot be conferred by a training module, and DeVito is the strongest published case against that claim. The manuscript should say so, and should answer it by distinguishing raising a user's model of *how the system works* (teachable, DeVito's target) from raising a worker's ability to *coordinate with a party the system stands between* (not reducible to a model of the system, because the other party is also adapting).

## Caution

Twenty-five participants, one recruitment community, consumer social platforms, self-presentation stakes rather than income. TCL is an inductive classification from a small qualitative corpus, not a validated measure — no instrument, no reliabilities, no independent replication; do not cite it as if it were a scale. Perceived platform spirit borrows adaptive structuration theory's "spirit" and carries that theory's commitments. Article 339 in the CSCW2 issue; ACM paginates it 1–38, so cite the article number alongside the page range. My read was extended rather than complete — I did not work through the full platform-spirit results or the appendix protocol, and anyone quoting from those sections should read them directly.
