## What it argues

The published abstract makes one claim and reports one method. The claim is that reciprocity in feedback giving distorts the production and content of reputation information in a market, and the distortion hampers trust and trade efficiency. The method starts from feedback patterns observed on eBay and other platforms and then runs laboratory experiments to test whether reciprocity can be managed by changing the way feedback information flows through the system. The abstract reports that the changed flow produces more accurate reputation information, more trust and more efficient trade, and closes on implications for theory building and for the design of market trust systems.

What the discussion-paper card adds, and what this card cannot confirm for the article, is the content of those patterns and treatments: a two-sided feedback system in which each side can read the other's rating before posting its own, a "blind" alternative in which neither can, and a one-sided detailed seller rating that only the buyer gives. The 2009 paper reports eBay field percentages for mutual feedback, seller feedback conditional on the buyer's, and the timing of problematic pairs, and laboratory percentages for feedback frequency and realised efficiency under each treatment. Its field section is called "a first look." Which of those numbers survived into the 2013 article, and in what form, is not known from the abstract. Between the 2009 paper and the article there were at least two further versions, a 2010 NBER market-design conference paper and a 25 March 2012 draft on Ockenfels's Cologne site, and the sentence in the published abstract about "more accurate reputation information, more trust, and more efficient trade" is stated more flatly than the 2009 paper's results, where the efficiency gain from the blind treatment was not significant.

## Relation to the argument

For `egos2027_st64_rules_of_play`, this is the source for reviews read as compliments: when both sides rate and each can see the other's rating before giving its own, a rating becomes a move in an exchange between the two parties rather than a report to the platform, and the platform's record of the trade is the residue of that exchange. The rule that governs the order of posting is the platform's, and the authors' word for changing it is engineering. The arm can cite the abstract for the claim that reciprocity distorts the record and that a change in information flow repairs it; it should cite the discussion-paper card, labelled as such, for anything more specific.

For `jms_organizing_truth_arbiter` §3, the abstract supplies the third condition of truth by arbitration, the anticipatory report, in its lab form: a party shapes its report in expectation of the other's, and the platform can remove the expectation by withholding the other's report. The field test of that removal is Fradkin, Grewal and Holtz (2021), carded at `fradkin2021.md`, whose abstract reports that the withheld-reveal rule lowered ratings and did not reduce adverse selection. Read together, the two abstracts say that the record changes when the rule changes, which is the arm's point, and they do not say that the record then becomes true.
