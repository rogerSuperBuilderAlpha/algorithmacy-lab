# Hohenstein, J., & Jung, M. (2020). AI as a moral crumple zone: The effects of AI-mediated communication on attribution and trust. *Computers in Human Behavior*, 106, 106190.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1016/j.chb.2019.106190  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** Elsevier ScienceDirect
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Hohenstein and Jung ask what an AI in the conversation does to *blame*. Pairs communicated through a messaging interface with or without smart-reply suggestions; the presence of the AI changed how participants attributed responsibility when the conversation went badly. Trust in the partner was higher in the AI-mediated condition, and negative attributions shifted toward the system. Borrowing Elish's term, they call the AI a **moral crumple zone**: a component positioned to absorb blame that properly belongs to the humans or to the system's designers.

## Relation to the argument

**A genuine three-party finding in the AI-MC literature, and the closest empirical work to what §7 describes.** Two humans coordinating, an intelligent system between them, and a measured redistribution of responsibility across the three positions — the arrangement §9 says the field never assembles. The paper needs to know this exists.

It also marks the boundary cleanly. Their AI absorbs attribution; it does not **commit determinations binding on both parties**. Nothing the smart-reply system does settles anything about either participant's standing, pay, or continued access. The crumple zone deflects blame after the fact; the gate in §7 decides the outcome in the first place. That is the difference between a third *presence* and a third *authority*, and it is the distinction §9's structural claim actually depends on.

There is a further use in §5–§6. The withheld condition the paper says no worker can supply individually is accountability. Hohenstein and Jung show experimentally that inserting an AI into a communication redistributes accountability away from the parties — which is an empirical mechanism for exactly that withholding, and stronger than an assertion.

## Caution

Laboratory study, dyadic chat task, smart replies of 2018 vintage, modest sample. The moral-crumple-zone concept is Elish's (doi:10.17351/ests2019.260) and describes automation-blame in high-stakes systems; Hohenstein and Jung adapt it. Jung is second author.
