## What it argues

Gig workers facing an algorithmic manager they cannot see or argue with practise **anticipatory compliance**: they pre-emptively conform to what they believe the system wants, going beyond what is asked, in order to pacify an evaluator whose response they cannot predict or contest. Compliance runs ahead of instruction because the sanction is unpredictable and the appeal is absent.

## Relation to the argument

The behavioural signature of the accountability withholding, and the reason §5's third claim needs §6's distributive paragraph. Where a worker cannot contest a determination, she over-complies in advance — which is not a facet in §7's sense, because it answers no condition she can supply, but is exactly the conduct the missing forum produces. The manuscript's guard against reading the paper as an argument for training workers to absorb a withheld standing gets sharper if it can point to what absorption actually looks like: not skill, but pre-emptive surrender of latitude.

Anticipatory compliance is also a rival reading of intent specification that §7 should distinguish. Packaging a submission so a gate reads it correctly and pre-emptively conforming to a feared evaluator are behaviourally similar and motivationally opposite — one is an attempt to convey meaning, the other an attempt to avoid attention. An instrument that cannot tell them apart will mismeasure the facet.
