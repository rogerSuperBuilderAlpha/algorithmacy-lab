## What it argues

A PRISMA-guided review of over 500 English-language articles yielding 62 algorithm-audit studies, organised primarily by the **behaviour** audited — discrimination, distortion, exploitation, misjudgement — with secondary codes for domain, organisation and audit method (sock puppet, direct scrape, crowdsourcing, and so on). The review reports what audits have exposed and where the gaps are, and closes with the common ingredients of successful audits.

## Relation to the argument

The evidential floor under §5's claim that the withholding is real and consequential. Sixty-two studies, all of them establishing something about a system's behaviour that its operator did not disclose, is the strongest available demonstration that these systems act on people in ways that are neither announced nor discoverable without effort.

The four behaviour categories are also worth §7's attention as a contrast case. They classify what a system *does wrong*; §7 classifies what a worker *does about* what a system withholds. A reader who knows this literature may expect the facets to be indexed to harms; they are not, and the paper should say why — a harm typology cannot generate a competency structure, because most of what a worker copes with is not a harm but an absence.

## Caution

A review of research studies, not of worker practice; the audits are performed by researchers with instruments and comparison conditions, so nothing in it transfers to what an individual can infer. English-language only, and the sample is of published audits, so it inherits publication bias toward audits that found something. Read at abstract depth.
