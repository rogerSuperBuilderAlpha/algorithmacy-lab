## What it argues

Schatzki gives practice theory its ontology. Social life, he argues, is constituted in a **site**: a mesh of **practices** and **material arrangements** in which human coexistence inherently transpires. A practice is an organized nexus of doings and sayings, held together by four things — practical understandings (knowing how to do, identify, and prompt the relevant actions), explicit rules, a teleoaffective structure (an ordered range of ends, projects, and acceptable emotions), and general understandings. His position is a **flat ontology**: there is no social level above practices from which structures act down on them, so large phenomena are large meshes rather than higher-order entities, and change is always change in the mesh.

## Relation to the argument

Two things here bear on §8 and §10. First, **practical understanding** is the ontological category the paper's competency belongs to — knowing how to go on in a practice, prior to and irreducible to explicit rules — and it is the most precise available answer to a reviewer who asks what kind of thing algorithmacy is if it is neither a trait nor a stock of knowledge. Second, and more usefully, Schatzki's **teleoaffective structure** names exactly what an opaque gate withholds. A practice is organized by an ordered range of ends and acceptable ways of pursuing them, and the whole difficulty §5 describes is that the worker is enrolled in a practice whose teleoaffective structure is set elsewhere and not disclosed, so she must reconstruct the ends from outcomes. That is a sharper statement of the paper's central condition than "the rule is undisclosed," and it comes from an ontology that is already standard in the venue. The flat ontology is also a caution to §8's table, which stacks individual, role, firm, and form as levels; Schatzki denies there are levels, and the paper should either take the point or say why it is using a levels vocabulary anyway.

## Caution

A dense philosophical monograph, not an organizational study; it supplies ontology and vocabulary and no findings, and every application to platform coordination is the paper's own. Schatzki's flat ontology is a strong commitment that sits awkwardly beside the level-of-analysis argument §8 uses against dynamic capabilities — borrow the categories, or take the ontology, but do not do both silently.
