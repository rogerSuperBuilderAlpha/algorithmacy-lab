## What it argues

A qualitative study of ride-hailing drivers in Lima, Peru, based on 40 in-depth interviews and more than 35 informal conversations with drivers, plus a systematic review of Latin American media coverage of the platform economy. The paper argues that Lima's drivers value ride-hailing platforms not primarily for formal job security but for their role in mitigating physical risk in a context of high informality and urban violence: platforms enable cashless transactions and, per the abstract, provide "data-driven oversight of passengers and routes." The study frames this through workers' "spatial agency" and their "reworking" of platform technologies, reconceptualizing platform labor through the lens of urban security rather than conventional labor-security frames, and situates the finding within debates on platform labor in the Global South.

## Relation to the argument (Lima PDW manuscript)

Cited three times as the empirical anchor for the paper's motivating claim that a counterpart-directed, safety-relevant interpretive capacity already shows up in naturalistic fieldwork, ahead of the paper's own formal construct (algorithmacy): line 19 ("Manky (2025) watches ride-hailing drivers in Lima read opaque platform metrics to judge whether a passenger is safe to accept"), line 29 ("Lima's drivers supply the sharper version: reading a black-box metric is how a driver decides whether a stranger is safe to pick up"), and line 169 ("Manky (2025) demonstrates that ride-hailing drivers in Lima (N=40) interpret opaque platform metrics to infer passenger trustworthiness and ensure physical safety"). The N=40 figure is exact. The general thrust — platforms functioning as a data-driven safety/trust-production mechanism for drivers rather than as employment security — is confirmed at abstract level. The specific mechanism the manuscript attributes to the study — reading an opaque/black-box *metric* to decide, in the moment, whether to *accept* a given passenger — is a plausible and abstract-consistent reading of "data-driven oversight of passengers and routes," but is not itself the abstract's own phrasing, and the article body could not be obtained to confirm it precisely (it is closed access with no OA copy on record). Also worth the site's obvious relevance: this is the paper's home-city empirical touchstone (Lima), likely part of why it was selected.
