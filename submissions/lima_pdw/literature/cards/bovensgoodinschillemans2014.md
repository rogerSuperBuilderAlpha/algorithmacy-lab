## What it argues
The handbook consolidates public accountability as a field and organises it around the actor–forum relation the editors' own work established. Its parts run from conceptual foundations, through the varieties of accountability — political, legal, administrative, professional, social — and the mechanisms that carry them, to accountability's effects and its pathologies: deficits where no forum exists, overloads where too many do, and rituals where a forum exists but does nothing. The editors' introduction insists on the point that matters here. Accountability is an institutional achievement, built and maintained by arrangements, and it does not arise from the good intentions of the party being held to account. The volume also collects the field's self-criticism, including work on the costs of multiple forums and on the gap between a formal arrangement and its felt effect on conduct.

## Relation to the argument
The handbook makes §5's definitional move look like standing scholarship rather than one article's framing: a field has organised itself around the forum requirement for over a decade. It matters more for §6 than for §5. §6's institutional route — statutory review, designed appeal, worker forums — is the handbook's own subject, and the volume supplies vocabulary for *grading* such routes instead of merely naming them: whether a forum is demarcated, whether it can pass judgement, whether consequences follow, and whether the arrangement produces answerability or a ritual of account-giving. The pathology material guards §6 against a naive reading. An appeal process can exist and still fail Bovens's test, so "institutionally supplied" is not the same as "supplied," and the division in §6 should say so — otherwise a reviewer will point out that platforms already run appeal queues.

## Caution
Read at abstract depth only. Do not quote the handbook or attribute specific arguments to named chapters without pulling them. It is an edited volume whose chapters disagree, so citing "the handbook" for a proposition is weaker than citing the chapter that makes it. Its subject is public accountability; private and platform accountability appear only at the margins, and the paper's application remains an extension.
