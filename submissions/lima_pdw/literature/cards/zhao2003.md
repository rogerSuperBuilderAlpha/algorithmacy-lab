## What it argues

Zhao builds a taxonomy of copresence along two axes: the **mode of presence** (physical or electronic proximity) and the **mode of the other** (human, human-representation, or artificial). Crossing them yields six forms, from corporeal copresence with a physically present human to hypervirtual copresence with an electronically present artificial entity. His analytic point is that "being with another" is not one relation: coordinating with a person, with a person's electronic proxy, and with an artificial agent are structurally distinct conditions, and lumping them is why the presence literature keeps producing incompatible findings.

## Relation to the argument

The nearest thing in this literature to a **positional** analysis, and it demonstrates both the value and the limit of the move §9 makes. Zhao gets the right kind of answer — categories defined by who or what occupies the other position — and his taxonomy still has only two positions in it. Every cell is self-and-other. There is no cell for *self, absent other, and present artificial third*, which is the arrangement §7 describes, and Zhao's grid is precisely the sort of exhaustive typology that would have contained one had anyone been looking for it.

That is usable evidence for §9. Rather than asserting that the constructs inherit a dyad, the paper can point to the field's most systematic attempt at enumerating mediated social configurations and observe that the enumeration is two-place throughout.

## Caution

A conceptual taxonomy with no empirical component, oriented to virtual reality and telepresence research. "Copresence" in Zhao's sense is about the phenomenology of being-with, not about coordination or task outcomes. It is a supporting citation for the positional argument, not a load-bearing one.
