## What it argues

The canonical statement of the resolution to the productivity paradox. **"To understand the economic value of computers, one must broaden the traditional definition of both the technology and its effects."** Case studies and firm-level econometrics support two propositions: **"1) organizational 'investments' have a large influence on the value of IT investments; and 2) the benefits of IT investment are often intangible and disproportionately difficult to measure."** The productivity payoff appeared **"well before the recent surge in the aggregate productivity statistics,"** and the later measured revival partly reflects **"intangible capital accumulated in the past."**

The organising claim is that the technology is not the unit of analysis. What a computer is worth to a firm depends on what else that firm did — work organisation, decision rights, skills, process redesign — none of which is on the invoice.

## Relation to the argument

The oldest and most durable finding in this cluster, and it is the general form of §1's assertion: identical technology, dispersed outcomes, and the dispersion tracks complementary organisational investment rather than the technology itself. Twenty-five years of IT-productivity research says the volume of technology deployed predicts very little on its own. That is the empirical genealogy §1's second sentence needs, and it is far better grounded than the generative-AI literature that gets cited for the same point.

It carries a warning too, and the manuscript should absorb it rather than dodge it. The productivity-paradox literature located the missing variable at the level of the **organisation** — practices, structures, complementarities — not the individual. §1's move is to push the residual down to the worker. That is a further step, and Brynjolfsson and Hitt are evidence that the previous generation of this argument found its answer one level up. §6 already concedes the descent is "motivated, and not entailed"; this is the source that makes the concession bite.

## Caution

Firms, not workers; a review essay in a perspectives journal, not new estimation; twentieth-century IT, not generative AI or algorithmic management. It measures business performance, not worker earnings or exit. Do not stretch "organizational investments matter" into "individual competency matters" — that is the inference the paper does not make and the manuscript must argue for separately.
