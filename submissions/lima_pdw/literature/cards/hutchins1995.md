## What it argues

Hutchins studies navigation aboard a US Navy ship and argues that the cognitive system doing the navigating is not any individual's head. It is the whole arrangement of people, charts, alidades, hoeys, and procedures, with representational state propagating across media, and the ship's fix is computed by that system rather than by a navigator. His analytic unit is therefore the **socially distributed cognitive system**, and his method is to trace how a representation moves and is transformed as it passes between people and artifacts. Two consequences matter beyond the case. Learning is the propagation of a person into a position in the system, so becoming a navigator is acquiring a relation to the instruments and to the others, not internalizing a body of knowledge. And robustness is a property of the arrangement — the famous account of the ship recovering from an engineering casualty shows the system reorganizing to keep computing the fix when individuals could not.

## Relation to the argument

The strongest ontological warrant for taking the interview's tool seriously, and a challenge to §8's unit claim that the paper should meet rather than sidestep. If cognition is a property of arrangements of people and artifacts, then a participant working out a gate through a language model is a distributed cognitive system, and the competence being exercised has no clean location in the person. §8 says the unit of measurement is the person and the unit of comparison is the form; Hutchins gives the reason a reviewer will have for doubting the first half. The answer available to the paper is a good one, and it is the one Salomon and colleagues gesture at: the person is the right unit of measurement not because competence resides there but because the person is the unit that outcomes accrue to, which is a claim about the arrangement's distributive structure rather than about cognition. Hutchins also supplies the vocabulary for §11's third question — whether noticing travels — since propagation of representational state across a group is exactly what that question asks about, and it is observable in a repository record.

## Caution

A cognitive-anthropological monograph on one setting, a naval vessel with codified procedures, formal training, and a stable task, and its theoretical apparatus (representational state, propagation across media) is heavier than most organization-theory arguments need. Distributed cognition is a research programme with its own commitments, and adopting it wholesale would put the paper at odds with the individual-level construct it is building; borrow the observation, not the programme.
