## What it argues

Lomborg and Kapsch borrow Hall's **decoding** as a sensitising device for how people come to know algorithms. The move is deliberate: decoding foregrounds a gap that must be filled by mobilising semiotic and socio-cultural knowledge before communication becomes meaningful, which is what an opaque algorithm forces. If the box cannot be opened, the relationship people experience with it can still be studied. An exploratory study across walks of life yields **three prototypical modes** — preferred, negotiated, oppositional — of engagement with algorithms in daily life.

## Relation to the argument

The most direct precedent for treating opacity as a *communication* problem rather than a knowledge problem, which is the move §9 says the five constructs fail to make. Hall's encoding/decoding already contains a sender, a message and a receiver, so the vocabulary is triadic in a way AI literacy is not — and this is the one place in the cluster where a third position is structurally present. Section 9's diagnosis would be sharper for engaging it: decoding gets closer than the five constructs because it keeps a sender in the frame, but the sender it keeps is the *platform*, not the unmet counterpart, so the triad still collapses to two positions with the algorithm occupying one of them.

The three modes are a typology of stance, not of practice — preferred, negotiated and oppositional describe how a person relates to the system's project, not what they do to read it, signal through it, or notice it changing. It is therefore not a competitor to §7's structure, and saying so explicitly is worth a sentence in the manuscript, since a reader who knows this literature will wonder.

## Caution

Exploratory and small; the abstract reports no sample size. The Hall apparatus carries strong commitments from cultural studies about dominant ideology and resistance that this paper does not need and should not import wholesale. "Decoding" as used here is a sensitising concept, not an operationalisation. Read at abstract depth.
