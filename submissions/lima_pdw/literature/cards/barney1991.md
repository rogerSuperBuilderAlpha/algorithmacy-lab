## What it argues

Barney's statement of the resource-based view. Building on two assumptions — that strategic resources are heterogeneously distributed across firms, and that the differences are stable over time — he asks what makes a resource a source of *sustained* competitive advantage, and gives four empirical indicators: the resource must be **valuable**, **rare**, **imperfectly imitable**, and **non-substitutable**. Imperfect imitability is where the interesting work happens, and Barney grounds it in unique historical conditions, causal ambiguity, and social complexity. He applies the model to several classes of firm resource and closes on implications for other business disciplines.

## Relation to the argument

§8 invokes Barney as the possession-based predecessor of dynamic capabilities, and the reading is correct: the resource-based view is explicitly about what a firm **has** and can keep others from getting, which is the cleanest possible statement of the property §8 says the coordinated party lacks. The card's value is that Barney also supplies the sharpest way to state what is odd about the paper's case. On the VRIN criteria, algorithmacy is valuable, rare, and causally ambiguous — imperfectly imitable in exactly Barney's sense, since nobody can say precisely what the competent worker is doing — and yet it generates no sustained advantage for the person who has it, because she cannot appropriate the returns. That is not a failure of the construct; it is §6's distributive claim restated in the vocabulary of the field the paper is writing for, and it is a more powerful way of making the point than the level-of-analysis argument §8 currently leads with. A capacity with every property of a strategic resource, held by a party with no standing to bargain over it, is a good one-sentence statement of what the paper is about.

## Caution

A conceptual article, and the most-cited one in strategy; the framework has been criticized for tautology (resources are identified as valuable by the advantage they explain), for its static assumptions, and for offering no account of how resources are acquired. The VRIN criteria are defined at the firm level throughout, so applying them to an individual worker's capacity is an analogy the paper makes and must flag as one. Abstract-level read; the four indicators are verified from the abstract, and the imitability mechanisms are attested in the reception rather than in it.
