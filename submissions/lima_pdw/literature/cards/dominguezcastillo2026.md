## What it argues

The title states the position and the record supplies nothing further. Dominguez Castillo joins **critical AI literacy** to the ethics of algorithmic management and to *obligations owed to workers* — the third term is the one that matters, because it points the argument at what employers and platforms owe rather than at what workers should acquire. The surrounding literature in the same journal issue runs in that direction: transparency that algorithmically managed workers cannot in practice reach, contestability mechanisms that are rare and largely performative, and calls for targeted AI literacy support directed at unions and works councils rather than at individuals.

## Relation to the argument

**Potentially the sharpest normative challenge to the manuscript, and it is unread.** Section 8 already concedes the point in its own voice: "A rise in average algorithmacy is not a good outcome on its own. If a cohort's scores rise while the form's withholdings stay fixed, what has risen is the amount of unpaid coordination work the population absorbs." An article titled "critical AI literacy and the obligations owed to workers" is very likely making exactly that argument at length, and possibly making it *against* the project of naming and measuring worker competence at all — on the ground that a competence construct individualises a burden that belongs to the party that created it.

That is the strongest available objection to the whole paper, and it is better met than avoided. Section 6's division is the meeting-place: two of the three withheld integrating conditions a worker can supply privately, and one she cannot, which is precisely a statement of where obligation cannot be discharged by literacy. The manuscript should cite this article at that point, concede the force of the obligation argument, and show that its own structural limit is the same finding reached from the other direction. Done well, an apparent opponent becomes corroboration.

If the article instead argues that workers *should* be equipped with critical AI literacy, it becomes a resource and the risk evaporates. Either way the manuscript needs to know which.
