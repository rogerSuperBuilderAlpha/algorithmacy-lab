## What it argues

The standard introduction to autoethnography: research that connects the personal to the cultural, written by the three scholars most associated with the form. Ellis, Adams and Bochner set out its varieties — evocative and analytic, layered accounts, co-constructed narrative — and defend it against the two standard charges, that it is insufficiently rigorous as science and insufficiently artful as story, by arguing that it answers to different criteria than either.

## Relation to the argument

Cite it to mark a boundary rather than to claim membership. The paper's design is *not* autoethnography — the participants are the subject, the investigator is not — and saying so with a citation forecloses a misreading that a reviewer will otherwise reach for the moment he learns the author built the site. The one place it becomes live is `protocols/SELF.md`, the self-interview branch of the collection instrument, which does produce first-person data about the author's own experience of running the programme.

## Caution

FQS is open-access and lightly weighted in management. The article is an overview, not an argument, and its criteria (evocativeness, verisimilitude) are not the criteria this study asks to be judged by — invoking it loosely risks importing standards the design does not meet and does not want.
