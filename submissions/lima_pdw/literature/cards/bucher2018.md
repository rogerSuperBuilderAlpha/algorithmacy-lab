## What it argues

The book-length development of Bucher's position. Algorithms are socio-technical rather than computational objects, and the organising idea is **programmed sociality**: social relations on algorithmic media take shape through the systems that select, rank and route, and those systems are shaped in turn by the social worlds they operate in. Two threads matter for this cluster. The **technicity of algorithms** insists the specific material and computational form is load-bearing for the power exercised. The **algorithmic imaginary**, developed from the 2017 article, holds that people's sense of what algorithms are feeds back into their conduct and into the systems themselves.

## Relation to the argument

The most developed statement that the mediator's form is constitutive of the ordering rather than incidental to it, which is what §7's definition of algorithmacy assumes when it makes "opaque, adaptive intermediary that commits determinations binding on both parties" the thing the competency is indexed to. It also supplies the reason §7 can reject machine orchestration as a facet: an affordance of one mediator is not a property of a coordination form, and Bucher's technicity argument is the general version of that distinction — the form matters, but which form matters is a question about the specific system.

## Caution

Media and communication theory across algorithmic media broadly; no worker population, no coordination form, no measurement. The book's arguments apply equally to a firm's own employees and to ordinary users, so it individuates nothing about the arrangement §7 is about. Read at publisher-summary depth only.
