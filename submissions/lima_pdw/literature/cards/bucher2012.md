## What it argues

Bucher inverts the panoptic reading of platform power. The disciplinary force on Facebook is not the threat of constant visibility but the **threat of invisibility** — the possibility of not appearing in others' feeds at all. EdgeRank rewards participation with exposure, so users regulate themselves toward whatever they believe the ranking favours, and the regulation happens without any instruction being issued.

## Relation to the argument

The mechanism that makes the outbound facet worth having. Section 7 says intent specification is compressing what one means into the signals a system will carry, but says little about *why* a worker bothers. Bucher's answer is that the alternative to being carried is not being carried at all — invisibility is the sanction, and it is administered silently. That supplies the motivational premise §7 leaves implicit and connects to §5's accountability point: a party who disappears has not been told she disappeared, so there is nothing to appeal.

For §10's stratified fluency, Bucher is also the source of the inequality mechanism. If exposure is the reward for correctly inferred participation, then differences in inference convert directly into differences in exposure, and the form reports the result as performance — which is §6's distributive consequence stated a decade earlier for a consumer platform.

## Caution

A 2012 analysis of EdgeRank, a ranking system that no longer exists in the form described; the specific mechanics are dated even if the argument is not. Theoretical and interpretive, drawing on Foucault; no users interviewed and no practice documented, so it cannot evidence a facet. Consumer social media.
