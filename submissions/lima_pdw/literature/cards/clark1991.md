## What it argues

Clark and Brennan model communication as the joint construction of **common ground**. A contribution is not complete when it is uttered; it is complete when the parties have mutual evidence that it was understood, and reaching that state is *grounding*, a collaborative process with its own costs. They enumerate the constraints a medium imposes — copresence, visibility, audibility, cotemporality, simultaneity, sequentiality, reviewability, revisability — and the **grounding costs** each affects: formulation, production, reception, understanding, start-up, delay, asynchrony, speaker change, display, fault, and repair. Parties minimise total cost, so a medium's constraints determine which grounding techniques are used, not whether grounding is attempted.

The **least collaborative effort** principle is the model's engine: parties do the least work jointly sufficient to establish mutual understanding.

## Relation to the argument

**The best theoretical machinery available for §5's "common understanding, withheld," and the paper does not have it.** Clark and Brennan make grounding a *joint* achievement requiring evidence of uptake from the other party. What §5 describes is an arrangement in which uptake evidence is structurally unavailable: the counterpart never signals understanding, and the system's outcome is not evidence of understanding but a verdict about it. **Grounding cannot occur.** That is a much more precise statement of the withholding than "common understanding" alone, and it explains why the two facets split as §7 says: with no grounding loop, inbound and outbound understanding cease to be two halves of one process and become two separate, unaided inferences.

Their constraint list is also a ready instrument for characterising a platform arrangement — which constraints hold, which grounding costs rise — and their repair cost is directly relevant to §7's example of the driver who cannot get a complaint recorded: repair is the grounding technique the arrangement makes unavailable.

## Caution

Psycholinguistics, oriented to real-time conversation and small collaborative tasks; the media constraints were written for 1991 telephony and email. The model assumes both parties want to ground and are cooperating, which does not describe a platform's position. The volume is a 1991 APA book with a 10-digit ISBN only. Brennan is second author.
