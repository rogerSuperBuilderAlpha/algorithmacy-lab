## What it argues

Alvesson attacks the neopositivist reading of interview data — that a well-conducted interview yields the interviewee's true beliefs — and the romantic reading that it yields authentic experience. An interview is a social situation with its own politics: the respondent is doing identity work, following local scripts, managing the interviewer's impression, and improvising within a genre. Alvesson offers eight metaphors for what an interview *is* and argues that the analyst's first task is deciding which one applies before treating any answer as evidence of anything.

## Relation to the argument

This is the citation for §10's concession that interviews collect accounts, and it upgrades that concession from a limitation into an analytic stance. It is also the most useful frame for the study's sharpest risk: a participant talking to an instrument her instructor built is unmistakably in a situation with politics, and Alvesson gives the vocabulary for reading her answers as situated performance rather than discounting them.
