## What it argues

A synthesis of the authors' steel-finishing-line studies and the wider complementarity literature, arguing that human-resource practices deliver their returns as a **system** rather than singly. Incentive pay alone does little; incentive pay bundled with problem-solving teams, extensive screening, training, communication and employment security moves productivity substantially. The comparison across near-identical production lines is what licenses the claim — the technology and the product are held constant and the practice bundle varies.

## Relation to the argument

The general principle behind the whole complementarity strand, stated compactly and with a DOI, and the reason "same technology, different outcomes" has been a settled empirical fact for a quarter-century. Where the manuscript needs a single citation for the productivity-paradox point without a paragraph of exposition, this and Brynjolfsson & Hitt (2000) are the two to use.

Substituted here for Ichniowski, Shaw & Prennushi (1997), *American Economic Review* 87(3), 291–313, which is the more frequently cited primary study but which the AEA has never deposited with Crossref — it has no DOI and could not be machine-verified for this pass. ## Caution

A perspectives-journal synthesis, not new estimation; the primary evidence lives in the underlying steel-line papers. Manufacturing, US, twentieth-century. Practices at the level of the line, not competencies at the level of the person — the whole tradition explains dispersion by what management did, which is one level above where §1 wants to put it.
