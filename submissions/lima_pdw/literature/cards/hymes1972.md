## What it argues

Hymes takes Chomsky's competence and refuses its idealisation. A child who acquired only grammatical competence, he argues, would be socially unintelligible — knowing which sentences are well formed says nothing about which are *appropriate*, *feasible*, or in fact *performed* in a community. Competence must therefore include tacit knowledge of the rules of use, and Hymes proposes four judgements a member makes about any utterance: whether it is formally possible, feasible given psycholinguistic limits, appropriate to context, and actually done. Communicative competence is the ability to make those judgements and act on them.

The reframing carries a political charge Hymes states plainly. The deficit accounts of working-class and minority children's language were artefacts of measuring performance against an idealised grammar rather than against the communicative norms of the child's own speech community. Competence is relative to a community's ways of speaking, which is why the ethnography of speaking has to precede the measurement.

## Relation to the argument

The paper's warrant sentence in §8 names Hymes, and the citation does more work than a name-check. Two things transfer. First, **the competency is indexed to a setting**: Hymes's competence is competence-in-a-speech-community, algorithmacy is competence-in-a-coordination-form. That is precisely §8's "should travel across mediators and fail across forms" prediction, and Hymes is its ancestor. Second, **appropriateness is a judgement made by others**, not a property of the utterance — which is the structural reason a competency needs a counterpart in the account, and so an argumentative resource for §9's triad claim rather than mere pedigree.

A third transfer the paper does not make and should: Hymes's target was a *deficit attribution*, performance shortfalls read as incapacity because the measuring frame was wrong. §10's stratified fluency risks the reading Hymes warned against — a distribution of scores taken for a distribution of worth — and §8's closing caution ("a rise in average algorithmacy is not a good outcome on its own") repeats his move. Saying so grounds the paper's normative caution in the lineage it claims.

## Caution

Hymes's frame is dyadic-plus-community, not triadic: the third term is a normative order, not an agent that commits determinations. Do not read the community into the algorithm's position. The 1972 Penguin chapter condenses longer 1971 and 1966 versions and pagination differs across reprints — keep the 269–293 the reference list already uses. Do not attribute to it the fuller SPEAKING taxonomy, which belongs to "Models of the interaction of language and social life" (also 1972, in Gumperz & Hymes).
