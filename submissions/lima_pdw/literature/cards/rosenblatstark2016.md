## What it argues

A study of Uber's driver-facing apparatus built from a nine-month observation of driver forums plus interviews, arguing that the platform's power over drivers runs on **information asymmetries**. Uber holds the data, sets the rules, changes them unilaterally and communicates selectively; drivers hold formal independence and almost no information. The paper documents the asymmetries in detail — surge maps whose relationship to actual demand drivers cannot verify, blind acceptance of ride requests without destination, ratings that function as automated managerial discipline, and unilateral rate changes announced through blog posts and emails.

## Relation to the argument

The founding empirical statement of the withholding §5 partitions, and the source that establishes it as a *design* rather than a byproduct. Rosenblat and Stark show the platform deciding what drivers may see, case by case, which is what makes §5's "the apparatus forecasts her with precision and updates its own rule without announcing it" a documented arrangement rather than an inference.

The forum material is also the earliest documentation of the collective interpretive work `bishop2019` and `lee2015` later name. Drivers pooled evidence about surge behaviour and rate changes because no individual could see enough, which is the same constraint on temporal tracking noted elsewhere in this cluster.
