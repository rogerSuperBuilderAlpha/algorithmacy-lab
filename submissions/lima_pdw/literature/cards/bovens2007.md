## What it argues
Bovens cuts an inflated evaluative catchword back to something an analyst can use. He separates two senses and works in one. Accountability as a *virtue* is a normative quality predicated of actors — transparency, responsiveness, a willingness to answer — and it is essentially contested. Accountability as a *mechanism* is an institutional arrangement, and that is what he defines: "a relationship between an actor and a forum, in which the actor has an obligation to explain and to justify his or her conduct, the forum can pose questions and pass judgement, and the actor may face consequences." Three stages follow: the actor informs, the forum interrogates and the actor justifies, the forum passes judgement, which may carry consequences. Bovens treats the *possibility* of consequences as constitutive rather than their imposition, so ombudsmen and forums that reward rather than punish still qualify. He then polices the boundary hard. Transparency, responsiveness, participation and control each fall outside the definition, because each lacks a specific forum, a phase of justification, a judgement, or a consequence. Four sorting questions produce the typology. To whom account is rendered yields five forum types — political, legal, administrative, professional, social — and the "problem of many eyes." Who renders it yields corporate, hierarchical, collective and individual accountability, his strategies for the "problem of many hands." About what yields financial, procedural and product. Why yields vertical, horizontal and diagonal. A closing part turns evaluative, offering democratic, constitutional and learning perspectives for judging whether an arrangement is adequate.

## Relation to the argument
This is the definitional spine under §5's decisive move. When the paper says accountability is "a forum with standing to demand justification," it is using Bovens's structure, and that structure is what makes the claim in §5 structural rather than rhetorical. A relation needs a second party, an obligation, and a phase in which that party passes judgement; a worker acting alone generates none of the three. The virtue/mechanism split does further work the paper has not yet claimed. What an individually skilled worker can cultivate is accountability *as a virtue* — her own answerability, her own diligence in rendering account — and Bovens has already argued that this is a different concept from the mechanism rather than a partial supply of it. §5's sentence about the worker who "becomes expert at rendering account under a schema she did not set" is that distinction restated in platform terms. His boundary exclusions discriminate directly for §5: the dashboards, ratings and metrics a platform emits are *transparency*, which he rules out for want of a scrutinising forum, and the steering the platform performs is *control*, which he rules out for operating without justification. His own caveat on social accountability — that interest groups and online panels may fall short because "the possibility of judgement and sanctioning often are lacking" and the forums are not "clearly demarcated, coherent and authoritative" — hands the paper the platform case in the source's own vocabulary. "Many hands" then sharpens §6: a worker seeking a forum in an algorithmic arrangement finds conduct dispersed across users, ranking rules and unannounced updates, so even locating the actor to call to account is work.

## Caution
Bovens built this for European public governance. His actors are officials and public organisations; his forums are parliaments, courts, ombudsmen, audit offices and professional tribunals. Applying it to a platform is an extension by analogy and should be flagged as one. The framework is retrospective and *ex post* by design, so it says nothing directly about the anticipatory steering that dominates algorithmic management — the point of contact is the missing backward-looking forum, not a claim that platforms fail at deliberation. The five forum types are public-law categories, and mapping platform relations onto them takes argument rather than transcription, since the platform occupies a position no public-governance forum was built to describe. Finally, the analytical half of the framework is deliberately value-neutral; the normative virtue sense Bovens sets aside should not be reintroduced through the back door.
