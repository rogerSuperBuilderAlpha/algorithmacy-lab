## What it argues

Fifteen interviews with US TikTok users about how the For You Page algorithm interacts with identity. Participants believed the algorithm actively suppresses content tied to marginalised social identities — race and ethnicity, body size and appearance, disability, class, LGBTQ identity, political affiliation — and the authors name a new folk theory for this belief, the **Identity Strainer Theory**. Two further constructs follow: **algorithmic privilege**, held by users positioned to benefit on the basis of their identities, and **algorithmic representational harm**, the harm of algorithmic symbolic annihilation. The behavioural half is the part this cluster needs: participants **changed their behaviour to shape their algorithmic identities**, both to align the algorithm's model of them with how they understood themselves and to resist suppression, through individual action, collective action, and altered performance.

## Relation to the argument

"Changed their behaviour to shape their algorithmic identities to align with how they understood themselves" is intent specification stated as an empirical finding, and it is the version closest to §7's phrasing — the worker compresses what she means into the signals the system accepts, and the compression is deliberate about what is lost. Unlike the influencer literature, where the object is visibility as such, here the users have a specific content they are trying to make the system carry: who they are. That is nearer to §7's "make intent legible" than a reach-maximisation account is.

The individual/collective/performance split in the resistance repertoire is also a small typology worth noting against §7's structure. It divides outbound work by *channel* rather than by direction, which is a different cut from the paper's and does not compete with it.

## Caution

Fifteen interviews, one platform, US-based, on a topic where the belief in suppression is contested and unverifiable from outside — the authors study the theory, not whether TikTok in fact suppresses. Consumer content creation rather than work with a counterpart. The identity and harm framing is normatively committed, and this paper's use should stay at the level of the documented practice. Read at abstract depth; the resistance taxonomy is worth reading in full if §7's outbound facet is developed further.
