## What it argues

In-depth interviews combined with the walkthrough method and think-aloud protocols with 22 people aged 16–26. Swart organises the account as understanding, feeling and engaging, and reports three findings that matter here. Sense-making strategies are **context-specific**, not general. They are **triggered by expectancy violations and explicit personalisation cues**. And the two links one would hope for do not hold: intuitive, experience-based insight into personalisation does not automatically let young people verbalise it, and knowing about algorithms does not automatically prompt them to intervene in algorithmic decisions.

## Relation to the argument

Two of Swart's negative findings are load-bearing for §11 and neither is comfortable. The verbalisation gap says a competency acquired through participation may be inaccessible to self-report, which is a direct methodological problem for an instrument built from self-report scales — §7 discloses that the lab instrument's three scales measure what the derivation predicts, and Swart says the measurement channel may not reach the thing. The knowledge-to-action gap says algorithmic knowledge and algorithmic practice come apart, which supports §9's diagnosis that AI literacy measures the wrong object, and simultaneously warns that a competency measured as knowledge will not predict conduct.

The expectancy-violation trigger corroborates `devito2017` and `bucher2017` on how temporal tracking starts.

## Caution

Twenty-two young people in one country, on news consumption rather than work; the outcome of interest is civic and informational, not economic. Walkthrough and think-aloud protocols are strong for surfacing reasoning and weak for establishing that the reasoning is the reasoning used when nobody is watching. Read at abstract depth; the verbalisation-gap finding should be read in full before it is used against the instrument, since it is the most consequential item on this card.
