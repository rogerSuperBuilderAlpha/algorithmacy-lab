# Mieczkowski, H., Hancock, J. T., Naaman, M., Jung, M., & Hohenstein, J. (2021). AI-mediated communication: Language use and interpersonal effects in a referential communication task. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 5(CSCW1), 1–14.

## What it argues

An experiment on what AI assistance does to the *language* two people use to coordinate. Pairs performed a referential communication task — the classic paradigm in which partners must converge on shared descriptions of ambiguous figures — with and without smart replies. The AI-assisted condition changed language use (shorter, more positive, more conventional messages) without degrading task success, and interpersonal perceptions of the partner were largely preserved.

The referential task matters: it is designed to measure **grounding**, the process by which two parties build the common ground that lets a reference succeed.

## Relation to the argument

The most methodologically relevant AI-MC study for §11. It measures two humans coordinating through a system, on a task whose outcome depends on whether they establish shared reference, and it does so with a paradigm with fifty years of validation behind it. If the paper wants a behavioural rather than self-report measure of **intent specification** — did the worker's meaning arrive — the referential communication paradigm is the obvious instrument, and Mieczkowski et al. show it survives the insertion of an AI.

The substantive finding also bears on §7. AI mediation made messages more conventional without hurting task success, which is a compressed-signal result: the channel narrowed and coordination held. §7 says of intent specification that "something is always lost in the compression, and part of the competency is knowing what can afford to be lost." Here is a study measuring precisely what was lost and finding it did not matter for that task — a useful calibration and a caution against assuming compression always costs.

## Caution

Laboratory dyads, a constrained referential task, smart replies rather than generative assistance, and both partners knew the conditions. Crucially, **both parties are present and in contact**, which is exactly the condition §7's arrangement removes; the paradigm would have to be modified to withhold the counterpart before it measured algorithmacy. Note the year and venue: PACM HCI 5(CSCW1), 2021 — often miscited as CSCW '21 proceedings.
