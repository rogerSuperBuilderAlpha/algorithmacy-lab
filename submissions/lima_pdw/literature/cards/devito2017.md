## What it argues

A content analysis of 102,827 tweets from #RIPTwitter, the 2016 backlash to rumours that Twitter would replace its reverse-chronological timeline with algorithmic curation. Resistance turns out to run on **expectation violation**: users objected less to algorithmic curation as such than to the breach of an assumed arrangement about how the feed behaves. Folk theories act as frames for reactions, with a graded coupling the authors state directly — more detailed folk theories are expressed through more specific reactions to algorithmic change.

## Relation to the argument

The best evidence in the cluster that **temporal tracking** is a real and gradable capacity rather than a residue of interpretation. The theory-specificity/reaction-specificity coupling means a worker's model of the system can be read off the structure of her response to a change, which is what §11 needs if it is going to measure the facet at all. Section 7 says the worker who detects the shift early acts on a different model than the worker still operating on last month's; DeVito et al. supply the observable form of that difference.

The expectation-violation trigger also matters for how §7 words the facet. Detecting a rule shift is not surveillance of the rule; it is the failure of a prediction the worker was already making. That makes temporal tracking parasitic on asymmetric interpretation — you can only notice the rule moved if you had a rule — and the paper should say so, because it is a prediction: workers scoring low on interpretation should score low on tracking, while the reverse need not hold.

## Caution

One hashtag, one platform, one episode, English-language Twitter in 2016, and a corpus that self-selects for the vocally mobilised — it says nothing about the majority who adapted quietly or did not care. Reconstructed from the abstract at both this card's depth and the lab card's; the specific folk-theory taxonomy and coding scheme are unverified here and must be checked before any claim rests on a particular category or count. It is also a *rumoured* change: the reactions are to an announced prospect, not to a detected unannounced shift, which is the harder case §7 describes.
