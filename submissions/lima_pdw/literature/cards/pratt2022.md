## What it argues

Templates made qualitative research popular and, misused, make it worse. Pratt, Sonenshein and Feldman propose methodological bricolage instead: the researcher combines analytic moves chosen to solve the problems of *this* project, and communicates trustworthiness by showing why that arrangement fits, rather than by demonstrating conformity to a named recipe.

## Relation to the argument

This is the citation that lets the paper decline to be a Gioia study. The protocol assembles pieces — abductive analysis, sensitizing concepts, in-vivo first-cycle coding, a blind second coder, a documented discard log, negative case analysis, a ledger for triangulating one specific research question — and bricolage is the published name for doing that on purpose. It converts "which template is this?" from an unanswered question into an answered one.

## Caution

Bricolage is easier to claim than to demonstrate, and the paper's own authors warn that it is not a licence to improvise. The design still has to say *why* each borrowed move solves a stated problem, which costs words.
