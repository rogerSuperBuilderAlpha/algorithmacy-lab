## What it argues

Cotter isolates a way of knowing algorithms that the field had been using without naming. **Practical knowledge of algorithms** captures "knowledge located at the intersection of practice and discourse": not knowing *that* an algorithm is or does X, but knowing *how* to accomplish X within algorithmically mediated spaces, as guided by the discursive features of one's social world. She develops it against prior work on algorithmic knowledge and against theories of knowing, and grounds it in a case study of BreadTube, a leftist YouTube community.

The two-part structure of the definition is what matters. Practical knowledge is procedural — a knowing-how, not a knowing-that — and it is *socially situated*: what counts as knowing how to act is set by the discourse of a particular community, not by correspondence to the system's actual mechanism. Cotter is thereby explaining how competence can exist and be recognised in a domain where, as Hargittai and colleagues insist, no ground truth is available: the community supplies the standard the system withholds.

## Relation to the argument

The most sophisticated rival on the epistemology, and it takes the manuscript's easiest argument away. Section 8 wants to say the rival literature measures a knowledge state where algorithmacy is a competency — knowing-that versus knowing-how, propositional versus enacted. Cotter has already drawn that line, in those terms, inside this literature. The manuscript cannot present the knowing-that/knowing-how distinction as its own contribution and must cite her when making it.

What survives is the triad and the derivation. Cotter's practical knowledge is knowledge of how to act *in* an algorithmically mediated space — how BreadTube creators get videos recommended, how they read the platform through their community's discourse. There is no counterpart: the space is populated by an audience and by fellow community members who supply interpretive resources, not by a party on the other side of a determination. Nothing in her account has the system issuing an output that binds a second person alongside the first. And the construct is derived from theories of knowing applied to an observed case, which is a strong derivation of a *kind* of knowledge but not a derivation of *which facets* a coordination form demands.

There is also a genuine adjacency worth naming rather than hiding. Cotter's "discursive features of one's social world" is a community-of-practice claim, and the manuscript's own implicit-acquisition property leans on participation in a community. If algorithmacy is acquired through participation and calibrated by what other workers say, then Cotter has the acquisition mechanism and the manuscript has the position structure; the honest statement is that these are complementary, and that algorithmacy adds the third position rather than a better account of learning.

Her earlier "visibility game" work (Cotter, 2019, *New Media & Society* 21(4), 895–913, doi:10.1177/1461444818815684, already in the lab library) describes influencers playing the same one-sided visibility problem; this article is the one that names a competence construct, so this is the card to cite for that purpose.

## Caution

**Which year to cite:** the version of record is 26(4), 2131–2150, April 2024; the online-first date is March 2022 and most of the citing literature, Oeldorf-Hirsch and Neubaum included, writes "Cotter (2022)." Cite 2024 with the full page range and note the online-first year if the manuscript quotes a page number from the early-access PDF, whose pagination differs.

Single case study of one online community, conceptual rather than measurement work — there is no instrument, no items, no distribution, and nothing here can support a claim about how practical knowledge varies across a population. Read at abstract depth: SAGE blocks automated retrieval, so the BreadTube analysis, the theoretical sources Cotter builds on, and everything beyond the abstract and the p. 2 definition are unverified on this card. Do not attribute the knowing-that/knowing-how distinction to Ryle through Cotter without checking whose account she actually adopts.
