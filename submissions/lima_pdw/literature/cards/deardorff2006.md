# Deardorff, D. K. (2006). Identification and assessment of intercultural competence as a student outcome of internationalization. *Journal of Studies in International Education*, 10(3), 241–266.

**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Deardorff runs a Delphi study with intercultural scholars to settle what intercultural competence is and how it could be assessed. The consensus definition — the ability to communicate effectively and appropriately in intercultural situations based on one's intercultural knowledge, skills, and attitudes — reproduces Spitzberg's appropriateness/effectiveness pair. Her process model orders the components: attitudes (respect, openness, curiosity) enable knowledge and comprehension plus skills, which produce an internal outcome (adaptability, an ethnorelative frame of reference) and then an external outcome (effective and appropriate behaviour). The panel also converged on a methodological point: intercultural competence cannot be assessed by a single instrument and requires mixed evidence.

## Relation to the argument

Useful as the field's most explicit statement that a competence construct is **process-ordered rather than a flat list** — attitudes precede knowledge, knowledge precedes internal reframing, internal reframing precedes competent behaviour. §7 presents three facets as coordinate. It is worth asking whether they are: asymmetric interpretation plausibly precedes intent specification, since you cannot compress intent into signals a system will carry until you have some model of what it carries. If the facets are ordered, §11's measurement model changes, and Deardorff's is the precedent for saying so.

Also relevant to §8's "acquired through participation" row. Deardorff's model makes the internal outcome — a reframed way of construing the situation — the hinge, which is close to Sandberg's (2000) competence-as-conception that §8 already leans on.

## Caution

Higher-education assessment, Delphi consensus rather than empirical test, and a construct whose scholars had not agreed on a definition for thirty years. Do not import the pyramid. Cite for the process-ordering point and for the mixed-methods assessment consensus.
