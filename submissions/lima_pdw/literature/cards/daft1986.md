## What it argues

Daft and Lengel argue that organisations face two distinct information problems — **uncertainty**, the absence of information, and **equivocality**, the presence of multiple conflicting interpretations — and that the two require different remedies. Uncertainty is reduced by *more* information. Equivocality is reduced only by *richer* media, because it requires parties to negotiate a shared interpretation rather than to acquire facts. Media are then ranked by richness on four criteria: capacity for immediate feedback, multiplicity of cues, language variety, and personal focus. Face-to-face is richest, unaddressed formal documents poorest. Structural design follows: an organisation facing high equivocality must build rich-media mechanisms into its structure.

## Relation to the argument

The uncertainty/equivocality distinction is the sharpest available diagnostic for what §5 says the platform withholds, and the paper is not using it. The worker's problem under an opaque gate is **not** primarily uncertainty — she is not short of data, she has ratings, outcomes, and a rejection history. It is equivocality: the outcomes admit multiple incompatible readings and there is no way to settle which is right. Daft and Lengel's theorem is that equivocality is resolved only by rich, feedback-bearing, personally focused exchange, which is precisely what the arrangement withholds. **Asymmetric interpretation is what a worker does when equivocality is high and the rich channel that would resolve it has been removed.**

That gives §7's first facet a forty-year-old theoretical warrant from the organisation-theory side, which for this paper's audience is more persuasive than a communication-discipline citation.

The feedback criterion also grounds §8's dating argument: richness is a property of the *channel*, and the paper's mediator is not a channel, which is why richness alone will not describe it.

## Caution

Media richness as a ranking of channels has been contested for decades — Markus, Fulk, Dennis and Valacich all showed richness is socially constructed and task-contingent rather than intrinsic to a medium, and Dennis, Fuller & Valacich (2008) replaced it. Use the uncertainty/equivocality distinction, which has survived; treat the richness hierarchy as superseded. Daft, Lengel & Trevino (1987, doi:10.2307/248682) is the empirical test.
