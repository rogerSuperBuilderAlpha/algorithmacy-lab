## What it argues

Andrew Wilkinson, at the University of Birmingham's Institute of Education (home of *Educational Review*), names "oracy" as a general ability in the oral skills of speaking and listening, arguing it deserves the same standing in schooling as literacy and numeracy. Secondary sources report Wilkinson himself framed the contribution modestly: the construct had "merely been given a name," with the work of defining it, teaching it, and relating it to literacy and numeracy still to come.

Note for the record: a second 1965 item under the identical title "The Concept of Oracy," also by Wilkinson, is indexed by Crossref in a different venue (DOI 10.1111/j.1754-8845.1965.tb01326.x; container-title given retrospectively as *English in Education*, vol. 2, issue A2, pp. 3–5, dated June 1965 — three months before the *Educational Review* piece). This is very likely the same argument delivered first to a NATE-affiliated outlet and then placed in Wilkinson's home institution's journal; both are genuine, Crossref-verified DOIs, not a fabrication. Secondary literature overwhelmingly cites the *Educational Review* 17(4) version as the source of the term, which is why it is the one carried forward here.

## Relation to the argument

The paper builds "algorithmacy" on the lineage of literacy, numeracy and oracy; crediting Wilkinson in one clause at first use of "oracy" matches how literacy's and numeracy's own disciplinary histories are left implicit rather than re-argued.

## Resolved 2026-08-30 — venue ambiguity closed

The apparent second venue is a metadata artifact, not a genuine competing original publication. *English in Education* did not exist as a journal title in 1965: NATE's academic journal under that name launched in January 1967, and NATE's publication before that (from 1964) was the *NATE Bulletin*. Wiley, the journal's current publisher, evidently back-assigns continuous volume/DOI metadata to the title's lineage predecessor, producing a Crossref record that anachronistically labels a 1965 item with a journal name that didn't yet exist. *Educational Review* 17(4), pp. 11–15 (Sept. 1965) is independently confirmed by Taylor & Francis' own DOI page, Wikipedia's "Oracy" entry, Cambridge International's education brief, and multiple secondary sources tying the coinage to Wilkinson's post at the University of Birmingham's School/Institute of Education, home of *Educational Review* — this is the venue essentially all secondary literature cites for the coining. PAPER.md's citation is correct as printed; no further library check is needed.
