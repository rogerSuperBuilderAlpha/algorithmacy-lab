## What it argues

Nicolini surveys the traditions that feed practice theory into organization studies — praxeology, Giddens and Bourdieu, activity theory, ethnomethodology and conversation analysis, Schatzki's site ontology, and actor-network and material-semiotic work — and reads each for what it offers the study of work. His synthesizing proposal is a method rather than a theory: **zooming in** on the real-time accomplishment of a practice, its situated doings and sayings, bodies, tools, and talk, and **zooming out** to follow how that practice connects to others across space and time and persists as a texture of organizational life. No single tradition does both, so he argues for a deliberate toolkit that switches lenses.

## Relation to the argument

For this cluster the value is methodological, and it lands on §11 rather than on §8. §11's first study question — how a working theory of the gate gets built and revised, from which incidents, on what evidence, with what abandoned versions — is a zoom-in question, and the third — whether noticing travels through the cohort — is a zoom-out question. Nicolini gives the design a vocabulary for saying that these are two different observational stances on one object rather than two topics, which strengthens the claim that the qualitative study is doing something the panel instrument cannot. His treatment of tools and artifacts as participants rather than backdrop is also the cleanest licence for taking the Lima interview's AI assistant seriously as part of the practice rather than as an outside aid.
