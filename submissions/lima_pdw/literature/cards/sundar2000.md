**Identifier:** doi:10.1177/009365000027006001  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** SAGE Journals
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Sundar and Nass ask **who the user thinks is talking**. Participants received identical content from a computer while being told the source was the computer itself, the programmer behind it, the network it drew from, or another person using it. Source attribution changed evaluations of the content substantially — the same message was judged differently depending on which entity the user oriented to — and the computer-as-independent-source orientation produced distinct effects from the human-source orientations.

The finding is that source orientation is a variable the user supplies, not a fact the interface determines.

## Relation to the argument

**The single most transferable finding in this cluster for §9's third position.** Sundar and Nass show that a user coordinating through a machine is always making an implicit attribution about who is behind the message, and that the attribution has consequences. The arrangement in §7 is exactly a situation of contested source orientation: when a rating arrives, the worker must decide whether she is being told something by a rider, by a platform, by an algorithm, or by nobody — and §7's asymmetric interpretation is partly the work of settling that question.

This gives the paper something better than a structural assertion. **The triad is not just a description of the arrangement; it is a problem the worker has to solve.** Which of the three positions issued this outcome, and on whose behalf, is undetermined from the worker's side, and answering it is competence. That formulation makes the triad do explanatory work in §7 rather than sitting in §9 as a taxonomic complaint, and Sundar and Nass supply the experimental warrant.

Their machine-heuristic work (Sundar & Kim, 2019, doi:10.1145/3290605.3300768) extends the argument to trust in automated systems.

## Caution

Laboratory study, 2000-era web interfaces, student participants, and the manipulation is a stated cover story rather than an ambiguity the participant resolves for herself. The Computers Are Social Actors programme it belongs to has faced replication questions. Cite for the source-orientation construct, not for effect sizes.
