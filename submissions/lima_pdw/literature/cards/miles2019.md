## What it argues

The standard sourcebook for qualitative analysis as a set of displayable operations: coding methods catalogued and compared, matrix and network displays for moving from codes to claims, jotting and memoing protocols, and explicit tactics for testing conclusions — checking for representativeness, weighing evidence, following up surprises, looking for negative evidence, ruling out spurious relations, and getting feedback from informants.

## Relation to the argument

The tactics chapter is where §7's negative case analysis and member checking come from, and the citation makes them recognised procedures rather than local inventions. Its display methods are also the practical answer to how a two-wave, four-strata, ledger-triangulated corpus gets assembled into an argument that fits a paper.

## Caution

Huberman died in 2001 and Saldaña carried the 3rd and 4th editions; author list and edition year both need care. The book's realist streak sits awkwardly with the constructivist and abductive commitments elsewhere in the design — take the tactics, not the epistemology.
