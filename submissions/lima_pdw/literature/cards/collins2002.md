## What it argues

Collins and Evans propose a normative theory of expertise for a field that had spent two decades dissolving the boundary between experts and the public. Their central distinction is between **contributory expertise**, the ability to contribute to a domain's practice, and **interactional expertise**, the ability to converse fluently and indistinguishably about a domain acquired through linguistic immersion in its community without ever practising it. The sociologist who has spent years among gravitational-wave physicists can hold up his end of a technical conversation and pass a version of a Turing test set by practitioners, without having built a detector. Around these they place lay expertise, referred expertise, and the ability to judge experts, and they argue that expertise so understood can be disentangled from political rights in technical decision-making.

## Relation to the argument

The card that speaks most directly to the Lima interview's wrinkle. The participant acquired her working understanding of the review gate not from the cohort but by pasting the bot's responses into an AI assistant and having them explained — she was doing linguistic immersion in a practice she was not participating in, which is interactional expertise in exactly Collins and Evans's sense, with a language model standing in for the community of talk. That is a real theoretical fit rather than a metaphor, and it gives the paper a name for a route to competence that the situated-learning literature does not have, since Lave and Wenger's newcomer must participate to learn. It also supplies a discriminant test with teeth for §11: interactional expertise predicts a participant who can talk accurately about what the gate wants and whose submissions do not improve correspondingly, while contributory expertise predicts the two moving together. And it sharpens §10's falsifier — a cohort "taught the system explicitly" that scores well might be demonstrating interactional expertise rather than refuting implicit acquisition, and the protocol should be able to tell those apart.

## Caution

A programmatic and heavily contested article; the "third wave" proposal drew sustained objection from science studies for reinstating an expert/lay boundary the field had worked to dissolve, and the paper should not import that normative project. Interactional expertise has been tested mainly through imitation-game experiments in small scientific communities, not in workplaces or with machine interlocutors, so applying it to a participant conversing with a language model is an extension the paper makes and must own. Abstract-level read; the contributory/interactional pair is verified from the abstract, but the finer taxonomy runs through a long article — verify before citing specific categories.
