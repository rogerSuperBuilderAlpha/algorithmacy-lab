## What it argues

Eraut takes the loose vocabulary of workplace learning and disciplines it. He distinguishes three kinds of non-formal learning by the learner's intention: **implicit learning**, which happens with no intention to learn and no awareness at the time that anything was learned; **reactive on-the-spot learning**, which is near-spontaneous and opportunistic, noticed but unplanned; and **deliberative learning**, in which time is set aside for the purpose. His claim is that the significance of the third is commonly overstated, because most professional learning is of the first two kinds. He then treats tacit knowledge as a problem of detection and representation rather than as a mystery, and distinguishes three types: tacit understanding of people and situations, routinized actions, and the tacit rules underpinning intuitive decision-making. These converge when professional performance runs under time pressure.

## Caution

A theoretical analysis of problems arising from Eraut's own empirical work in professional education, not a report of new findings, and its cases are nursing, medicine, engineering, and accountancy — regulated professions with defined bodies of knowledge and identifiable experts, none of which the paper's setting has. The typologies are analytic proposals that Eraut himself treats as provisional.
