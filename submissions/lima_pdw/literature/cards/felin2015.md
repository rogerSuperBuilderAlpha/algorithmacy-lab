## What it argues

Felin, Foss, and Ployhart review the movement that pushed strategy and organization theory to ground collective constructs — routines, capabilities, absorptive capacity — in individuals, their interactions, and the structures that aggregate them. Their organizing claim is that collective-level explanations that stop at the collective level are incomplete, and that a microfoundational account has to specify three things: individuals and their attributes, the processes and interactions among them, and the mechanism by which the individual level aggregates to the collective outcome. They survey how the movement developed across strategy, organizational behaviour, and human-resource management, note the danger of methodological individualism collapsing the collective into a sum of persons, and set an agenda around emergence and multilevel research design.

## Relation to the argument

§8 makes one empirical claim about the strategy literature — that the microfoundations of dynamic capabilities "treat the individual as a component of a firm-level capacity" — and this is the source that either supports it or corrects it. The characterization is broadly fair for the movement's dominant strand, where individual attributes are inputs to a firm-level explanandum, and citing the review makes the claim checkable rather than assertive. The review also supplies the argument for why the paper's move is not simply the same move. Microfoundations run *downward* from a collective outcome that is already the object of interest; the paper runs the other way, taking the individual capacity as the explanandum in its own right and the coordination form as the constant against which it varies. Saying so in a clause would answer the strategy reviewer who reads §8 and thinks the paper has rediscovered microfoundations with a different label. The aggregation question is also a live one for §10's second property, since a distribution of algorithmacy across a cohort is precisely a collective-level fact the paper describes but does not aggregate to any outcome.

## Caution

A long review article rather than a theory or a study; it maps a movement and does not settle the debates within it, and the movement itself is contested by scholars who hold that collective phenomena have genuinely emergent properties.
