# Dennis, A. R., & Valacich, J. S. (1999). Rethinking media richness: Towards a theory of media synchronicity. In *Proceedings of the 32nd Annual Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences* (10 pp.). IEEE Computer Society.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1109/hicss.1999.772701  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** IEEE Xplore
**Source-tier:** conference
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

The paper that broke media richness theory in public. Dennis and Valacich assemble the accumulated disconfirmations — studies where lean media outperformed rich ones on equivocal tasks, where the richness ranking failed to predict choice, where new media refused to sit on the hierarchy — and argue the theory's error is treating richness as an intrinsic property of a medium rather than a relation between a medium's capabilities and a communication process. They propose synchronicity as the replacement construct and sketch the five capabilities the 2008 paper formalises. Their companion HICSS paper (doi:10.1109/hicss.1998.653082) reports the empirical test.

## Relation to the argument

Kept mainly as the record of *how* a mediated-communication construct failed, which is a live risk for algorithmacy. Media richness failed because it attributed to a technology a property that turned out to be a relation between technology, task, and community of use. §8 promises algorithmacy "should travel across mediators and fail across forms" — a strong claim about where the property lives — and Dennis and Valacich are the cautionary case of a field that made an analogous claim, defended it for a decade, and lost.

The specific failure mode transfers. Richness died because researchers kept finding communities that used a lean medium richly, having built up conventions the theory could not see. §10's implicit acquisition predicts exactly such communities on platforms — workers who have collectively worked out what the gate rewards — and if the resulting capacity is a property of the cohort rather than the person, §8's "unit of measurement is the person" is wrong in the same way richness was wrong.
