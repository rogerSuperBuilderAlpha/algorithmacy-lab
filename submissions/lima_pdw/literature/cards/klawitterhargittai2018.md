## What it argues

Interviews with creative entrepreneurs who sell through algorithmically mediated marketplaces, on the skills they need to make their goods findable. The informant phrase in the title — it is like learning a whole other language — frames the argument: algorithmic skill is treated as a distinct competence, unevenly held, acquired informally, and consequential for whether creative work reaches buyers.

## Relation to the argument

The earliest labelling of the thing §7 is about as a *skill* rather than a knowledge, in a population that has a real counterpart (buyers) and a real economic outcome (sales). That combination is rare in this cluster and it is why the card is here despite the thin verification. The language metaphor is also worth §8's attention: a language is learned through use, is not reducible to rules that could be stated, and is held in degrees — three of the properties §8 and §10 claim for competency, expressed by an informant rather than by a theorist.

The paper is nonetheless on the far side of §9's line. Findability is the object, so what is studied is outbound signalling to a marketplace ranker; the buyer's wants are not reconstructed and no determination binding on both parties is at issue.
