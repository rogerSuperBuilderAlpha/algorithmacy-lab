## What it argues

Fifteen Airbnb host interviews on what it is like to be evaluated by a partially transparent algorithm. The paper names **algorithmic anxiety** — the condition produced when uncertainty about how the algorithms work combines with a perceived lack of control — and organises host experience along those two dimensions, perceived control and perceived uncertainty. Hosts face a **double negotiation**: they must attract guests, the visible task, and they must also appeal to evaluative algorithms disclosed only in hints, kept opaque both to protect intellectual property and to prevent gaming. Anxiety concentrates where uncertainty meets low control, which is the algorithmic side rather than the guest-facing side. The coping repertoire is the second half: hosts reverse-engineer the search algorithm, one reading Airbnb's engineering blog posts to work out how it is implemented; they build folk theories from platform tips, host forums, superstition and trial; and they use social comparison against other hosts to calibrate.

## Relation to the argument

The **double negotiation** is §9's triad seen from the worker's chair, and it is the single closest empirical statement in the cluster to the paper's core structure. A host orients two audiences at once — the counterpart she is trying to reach and the intermediary that decides whether she reaches them — and the two orientations have different epistemics, different affect and different tactics. Section 9 argues that the five constructs each collapse a three-position arrangement into two; Jhaver et al. show a population living in the three positions and reporting the two negotiations separately. The manuscript should cite it there, not only in the opacity discussion.

The coping repertoire maps onto §7 unevenly, which is informative. Reverse-engineering and folk-theory construction are asymmetric interpretation; social comparison is a change-detection instrument of the kind Bishop documents. There is nothing in the repertoire that is outbound. Hosts appeal to the algorithm by adjusting photos, pricing and responsiveness, but the paper frames those as guest-facing actions, and the compression problem §7's second facet describes does not appear.

## Caution

Fifteen interviews on one platform, co-authored by two Airbnb employees at the time, so the framing carries the platform's vocabulary and the design-solutions conclusion tilts toward legibility rather than standing. Airbnb hosts are asset-owning micro-entrepreneurs, so the dependence that sharpens the anxiety in ride-hail or freelancing is muted. "Algorithmic anxiety" is the authors' coinage and should be attributed.
