## What it argues

The book behind the CASA programme. Reeves and Nass run a long series of experiments replacing "person" with "computer" in classic social-psychology designs, and find the results largely hold: people are polite to computers, reciprocate to them, respond to their flattery, apply personality and gender stereotypes to them, and treat media as places. Their thesis — media equals real life — is that human social and perceptual responses evolved for a world without media and are triggered by media cues automatically.

## Relation to the argument

The foundational text for §9's "partner" row, and worth carding because it establishes *when* the machine acquired a party position in communication research: 1996, well before HMC named itself. The paper's genealogy in §9 implies HMC created the move; Reeves and Nass made it, and HMC institutionalised it.

More usefully, the book marks the limit of that move for the paper's purposes. Every media-equation experiment puts a human and a machine in a room and measures the human's response. The machine is a social actor **toward the participant**, never a party positioned between the participant and someone else. Two hundred experiments and the design is two-place throughout — which is a stronger form of §9's evidence than the field's programmatic statements, because it is a demonstrated research design rather than a stated commitment.

## Caution

A trade-facing book reporting the authors' own experiments; many are small, student-sampled, and pre-registration-era, and the media-equation thesis has faced substantial replication and boundary-condition criticism. Multiple imprints with different ISBNs (CSLI and Cambridge, 1996 and later paperbacks) — cite CSLI 1996. For the citable academic statement, use Nass & Moon (2000).
