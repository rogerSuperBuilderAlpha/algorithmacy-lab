## What it argues

Wolff's translation is the standard English source for the triad material, and it is the version communication and organisation scholars actually cite. Part II reproduces "The Isolated Individual and the Dyad," "The Triad," and the discussions of the non-partisan and mediator, the tertius gaudens, and divide et impera, in the terminology that entered the English-language literature. The mediator section is the most developed: Simmel distinguishes the third who mediates by *reconciling positions* from the third who mediates by *deciding between them* — the arbitrator — and argues that the second form requires the two parties to have surrendered something the first does not.

## Relation to the argument

**The arbitrator/mediator distinction is the single most useful thing in this cluster for §7's definition.** §7 defines algorithmacy as competency to coordinate through "an opaque, adaptive intermediary that commits determinations binding on both parties." Simmel already separates the intermediary who helps two parties reach agreement from the one who settles the matter for them, and observes that the second requires a prior submission by both. The paper's arrangement is the second — with the twist that the submission was never given: the worker did not agree to arbitration, she signed terms of service.

That is a precise, citable statement of the paper's novelty. **Simmel's arbitrator holds authority the parties conferred; the platform holds authority neither party conferred, and neither can withdraw.** The triad is old; this variant of it is not, and stating the difference in Simmel's terms makes §9's structural claim survive contact with the sociology of the triad instead of being blindsided by it.

## Caution

A 1950 translation of pre-1908 material, with all the interpretive contest that implies; Wolff's renderings of *Wechselwirkung* and *der Unparteiische* are debated. The 1950 first printing has no ISBN — the verified 9780029289204 is the Free Press edition catalogued at 1964. Cite the 1950 Wolff translation with an original-work note, and prefer the AJS articles (doi:10.1086/211115, doi:10.1086/211128) where an identifier is required.
