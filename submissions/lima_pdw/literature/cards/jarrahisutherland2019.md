## What it argues

The paper's frame, so far as it is verifiable at this depth: data-driven algorithms let digital labour platforms manage transactions between gig workers and service recipients automatically, and the resulting **information asymmetries** make it hard for workers to gain control over their work, because they do not understand how algorithms make consequential decisions such as assigning work and evaluating them. Against that, the authors propose **algorithmic competencies** — the capacities through which gig workers understand and appropriate the algorithms managing them.

## Relation to the argument

This is the closest prior use of the paper's own noun, and §8 should engage it directly rather than leave "competency" looking like a fresh coinage. Jarrahi and Sutherland put *algorithmic competencies* in print in 2019 for the same population, motivated by the same asymmetry, and paired with the gig-literacies work §9 already treats as the nearest precedent.

**The competencies are enumerated, and the enumeration is inductive.** Converging secondary reports — including the Semantic Scholar summary and the citing literature — give three activities identified through qualitative inquiry among Upwork users: **sensemaking**, the time and effort workers invest in building a nuanced understanding of algorithmic management; **circumventing**, bypassing or supplementing algorithmic processes with tools outside the platform; and **manipulating**, altering algorithmic outputs by changing the inputs the system collects. They map recognisably onto three of Zhou et al.'s (2025) four validated dimensions — Understanding, Remediating and Leveraging — which is unsurprising, since Zhou and colleagues descend from this chapter.

That settles the §7 question in the manuscript's favour, provisionally. Three activities collected from what workers were observed doing is an inventory, not a partition: nothing in the account says why three rather than five, and nothing connects the list to a structural property of the arrangement. The collected-versus-derived defence in §9 therefore covers this source as it covers `sutherland2020`. Two further cuts hold. All three activities are worker-to-system — understand the algorithm, route around it, feed it — and the client on the other side of the Upwork match appears in the setting and not in the competence. And circumventing and manipulating are tool-indexed practices that date with the platform, which is precisely the skill/competency line §8 draws.

The safe posture is descent, not displacement. The manuscript already claims descent from gig literacies; it should claim descent from this chapter too, name it in §4 rather than burying it in §9, and say plainly that algorithmacy adds the position the lineage never had. **A full-text read remains owed** — the three activities above rest on secondary reports, not on the chapter.
