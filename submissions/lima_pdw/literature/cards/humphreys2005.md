## What it argues

Humphreys advocates the autoethnographic vignette as a bounded device: not a whole first-person study, but short, dated, personal passages inserted into an otherwise conventional research account to expose the researcher's own taken-for-granted understandings of the world under study. He illustrates with a personal story of career change and argues the technique raises reflexivity generally, not only in explicitly autobiographical work.

## Relation to the argument

The most economical answer to "where does the author's position appear in the text?" A vignette or two — the moment the gate's private vote was designed, the first time a participant asked who objected — would locate the investigator in the account without turning the paper into a memoir, and the word budget for that is small. It also gives the analytic memos of §7 a publishable form.

## Caution

*Qualitative Inquiry* is outside the OS/OT canon and the vignette technique can read as self-indulgent to a reviewer who did not ask for it. Use it sparingly and never as a substitute for the structural handling in §9.
