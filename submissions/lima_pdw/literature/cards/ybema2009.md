## What it argues

The volume that consolidated organizational ethnography as a named field, collecting chapters on the practical and epistemological problems of studying organizations up close — the shift from participant observation to observant participation (Moeran), at-home ethnography (Alvesson), and the ethics of what fieldworkers conceal and disclose (Fine and Shulman).

## Relation to the argument

Cite the volume when the paper needs to place its design in a tradition rather than to lean on a single argument; cite the Alvesson chapter when it needs the at-home claim specifically. Fine and Shulman's chapter on ethical issues in organizational ethnography is worth reading before the IRB application in §12 goes in.

## Caution

An edited collection — it argues nothing as a whole, and citing it as though it did is a tell. The title collision with Neyland (2008) is real and has produced mis-citations in published work; check the DOI, not the title.
