## What it argues

A two-year inductive ethnography of four groups in an investment bank, asking how knowledge workers establish that an analysis produced by an analytical technology can be trusted. Anthony's finding is that they engage in **validating practices** — actions that confirm whether a produced analysis is trustworthy — and, surprisingly, that engaging in those practices does not require understanding what the technology actually computes. Workers **black box** their tools: they trust them without understanding them. She identifies two distinct paths. In one, validating practices are **partitioned** across roles, with junior bankers assembling analyses through the tools while only senior bankers evaluate the outputs, so juniors never develop the judgement that would let them validate; in the other, the practices are shared, and understanding develops. The consequence is a claim about expertise formation: the partitioned path produces workers who can operate the technology and cannot judge it.

## Relation to the argument

The card that speaks most directly to the interview wrinkle and to the paper's most uncomfortable question. §10 says algorithmacy develops through participation, and Anthony shows a case where participation develops *operation* without developing *understanding*, because the division of validating labour keeps the junior worker on the assembling side. Applied to the Lima interview, the participant validated her way to a working relation with the gate — she read ninety percent of the tool's output and flagged things before submitting — while explicitly reporting that she could not fully understand the requirements alone. That is validation without understanding in Anthony's precise sense, and the paper needs a position on whether it counts as algorithmacy or as its simulacrum. §7's asymmetric-interpretation facet says the worker's competence *is* her conception of what the system is doing, which makes understanding constitutive; Anthony's evidence says competent practice can proceed without it. Resolving that is worth a paragraph in §9 or §12, and the resolution likely runs through Collins & Evans: what the worker has is interactional rather than contributory. Anthony's partitioning finding is also a second candidate mechanism for §10's stratification that does not appeal to individual difference.

## Caution

An inductive ethnography of one investment bank, in a setting with clear seniority structure, formal accountability for analyses, and colleagues who do understand the tools — none of which the paper's site has. "Black boxing" here means workers' practical treatment of a tool they could in principle open, which is a weaker opacity than a rule the platform withholds; do not run the two senses together. Abstract-level read; the two paths and the partitioning finding are verified from the abstract, and the validating practices themselves are described only in the article.
