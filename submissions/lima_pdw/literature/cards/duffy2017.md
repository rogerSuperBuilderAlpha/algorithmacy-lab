## What it argues

Duffy's study of fashion bloggers, beauty vloggers and designers develops **aspirational labour**: work performed now, largely unpaid, in the hope of future compensation and recognition, sustained by a promise of doing what you love. The promise is unevenly redeemed, and the book's central finding is that the labour is gendered, that visibility work is a large and unacknowledged part of it, and that the structural conditions of the creative economy make the aspiration a mechanism of extraction as much as of opportunity.

## Relation to the argument

Duffy supplies the population and the stakes for the visibility-work literature this cluster leans on, and one caution §10 should absorb. Aspirational labour predicts that people will invest heavily in reading and signalling to a platform *whether or not the investment pays*, which means observed effort is a poor proxy for competency and a poor predictor of outcome. Section 11's design needs to separate how much interpretive work a participant does from how well she does it, and Duffy is the reason.
