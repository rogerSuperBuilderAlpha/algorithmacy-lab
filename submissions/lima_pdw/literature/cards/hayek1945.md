## What it argues

Hayek's rebuttal to Oskar Lange's case for central economic planning. The knowledge a rational economic order requires is never available to any single mind — it exists dispersed across society as fragmentary, often tacit, local knowledge (what fertilizer this field needs, which crate a shipper has spare capacity for) that changes constantly and never in principle "enters into statistics." A central authority working from statistical aggregates has already thrown away the very information that makes the local decision efficient, because aggregation abstracts from exactly the small differences of time, place, and circumstance that matter. The price system solves this without anyone aggregating anything centrally: "It is more than a metaphor to describe the price system as a kind of machinery for registering change, or a system of telecommunications which enables individual producers to watch merely the movement of a few pointers... in order to adjust their activities to changes of which they may never know more than is reflected in the price movement." Its virtue is "the economy of knowledge with which it operates, or how little the individual participants need to know in order to be able to take the right action" — an index number, a symbol, condensing "its significance in view of the whole means-end structure" into one signal, without anyone — including the price system itself — compiling a statistic.

## Relation to the argument

Five uses, all defining the Market coordination form's mechanism: "the price mechanism as an aggregated public statistic" (line 38); "a posted price accompanied by an unrestricted right of refusal defines the classical market archetype... where price signals coordinate decentralized actors without intermediary evaluation" (line 181); the Appendix A synthesis paragraph, "the price mechanism bears the primary coordinative burden because it functions as an aggregated public statistic no participant needs to reconstruct privately... that public statistic delivers predictability and common understanding at once" (line 509); Table A1's mechanism cell, "a posted term backed by an unconstrained right of refusal" (line 513) and deliverables cell, "via public price statistics" (line 514); and the closing synthesis, "the evaluation of posted prices backed by unconstrained exit rights" (line 522).

The substantive claim is accurate — Hayek does argue that the price mechanism condenses dispersed knowledge into a single publicly observable signal that no participant needs to reconstruct privately, and that this signal delivers exactly the economy-of-knowledge benefit the manuscript credits it with. But the specific word **"statistic"** (lines 38, 509 ×2, and 514) is Hayek's own term for the thing he says the price mechanism succeeds *by not being*: "the sort of knowledge with which I have been concerned is knowledge of the kind which by its nature cannot enter into statistics and therefore cannot be conveyed to any central authority in statistical form." Calling the price mechanism "an aggregated public statistic" collides with — reads as nearly the inverse of — Hayek's explicit distinction between the price signal (which works because it need not be a statistic) and a statistical aggregate (which he holds inadequate for exactly this job).

**DRIFTED — exact fix.** Four locations use "statistic(s)" for what Hayek calls an index, symbol, or signal. Recommended replacement swaps "statistic" for Hayek's own vocabulary throughout:

- Line 38 — before: "relying on the price mechanism as an aggregated public statistic (Hayek, 1945)." — after: "relying on the price mechanism as a condensed public signal (Hayek, 1945)."
- Line 509 — before: "it functions as an aggregated public statistic no participant needs to reconstruct privately. In modern coordination terms, that public statistic delivers predictability and common understanding at once" — after: "it functions as a condensed public signal — Hayek's own figure is a system of telecommunications — that no participant needs to reconstruct privately. In modern coordination terms, that public signal delivers predictability and common understanding at once"
- Line 514 (Table A1, Market/"What it must deliver" cell) — before: "Predictability and common understanding via public price statistics" — after: "Predictability and common understanding via a condensed public price signal"

Line 513's "a posted term backed by an unconstrained right of refusal" needs no change.

## Caution

Full text obtained and quoted directly, so this is a primary-text verification, not a secondary paraphrase — the drift finding above rests on Hayek's own sentences, not on a citing source's reading of him. One thing outside this card's scope: Hayek's essay does not itself discuss "an unrestricted right of refusal" or "posted price schedules" as a formal feature — that framing (used at lines 38, 181, 513, 522) is the manuscript's own generalization about market exchange, not something drawn from this specific essay, though it is not inconsistent with Hayek's argument and is standard textbook market theory. Treat the "right of refusal" language as the manuscript's synthesis rather than Hayek's, even though the citation attached to it is otherwise doing real work (the knowledge-aggregation claim, which is his).
