## What it argues

The abstract asks two questions: how valuations change when consumers produce them online, and what outcomes those valuations produce for the organizations being evaluated. It answers with a comparison of two hotel valuation schemes at work in the hospitality industry, the AA's inspection and star scheme, which rests on specialised expertise and institutional criteria, and TripAdvisor, which rests on consumer content and emergent criteria. The authors treat each scheme as an apparatus constituted through particular material-discursive practices, report significant differences between the two schemes' valuation practices and outcomes, and offer a practice-based lens on the materiality of valuations that explains the differences in terms of performativity.

That is the whole of what was read. The abstract says the two apparatuses differ in practice and in outcome; it does not say what hotels did in response to either, and so this card cannot confirm or refute any sentence about hotel conduct. The question the arm needs answered, how hotels respond to TripAdvisor, lives in the findings section, which the LSE and MIT copies hold.
