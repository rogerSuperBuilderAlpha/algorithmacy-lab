## What it argues

Chapter 1 lays out the book's whole apparatus, all of it directly usable:

**The action research cycle.** A pre-step (context and purpose) followed by a spiral of four steps: **constructing** (a dialogic, collaborative step naming the working issue — not the researcher deciding alone), **planning action**, **taking action**, and **evaluating action**, which feeds the next cycle's constructing (Figure 1.2–1.3). The Lewinian root is a "spiral of steps, each of which is composed of a circle of planning, action and fact-finding about the result of the action."

**Concurrent cycles at different timescales.** Every AR project runs several cycles at once, at different grains — the authors' own image is a clock: the hour hand is the whole project (can take years), the minute hand is a phase or section of it, the second hand is a specific action within a phase (a meeting, an interview). The three hands' revolutions are nested and mutually enabling (Figure 1.4).

**The meta-cycle, for dissertation work specifically.** Two AR cycles run in parallel when the AR project is also someone's dissertation: the **core** cycle (the project/organizational change itself) and the **thesis** cycle (a reflection cycle *about* the core cycle, per Zuber-Skerritt and Perry, 2002) — the dissertation is an inquiry into the project, and the two are "integrally interlinked" but "not identical." Meta-learning is assessed through Mezirow's (1991) three reflection types: **content** (what is happening), **process** (how it is being done), **premise** (the assumptions being challenged).

**Three voices/audiences.** First-person (inquiry into one's own action and assumptions), second-person (collaborative, face-to-face work with others on shared concern), third-person (impersonal, disseminated to those not directly involved) — quoting Reason and Marshall (1987): "All good research is for *me*, for *us*, and for *them*." The authors state second-person practice as primary: it is through collaborative engagement that first-person learning happens and third-person actionable knowledge eventually emerges.

**What an action researcher must show, for rigor.** Four items: (1) how the researcher engaged the cycles (constructing, planning, taking, evaluating) and how these were recorded as a true representation; (2) how the researcher challenged and tested their own assumptions continuously, through content/process/premise reflection; (3) how the researcher accessed different views, producing both confirming and contradicting interpretations; (4) how interpretations are grounded in and challenged against scholarly theory. Reason's (2006) five quality questions (relational praxis, reflexive concern for outcomes, plurality of knowing, significant work, sustainable new capability) are offered as the field's own criteria, explicitly *not* positivist ones.

**The methodological stance on role duality.** Quoting Riordan (1995): action research "require[s] a practitioner of science who is not only an engaged participant, but also incorporates the perspective of the critical and analytical observer, not as a validating instance but as integral to the practice." Duality is not a bias to manage down — it is constitutive of the method.

**On borrowing analysis techniques from other qualitative traditions.** The chapter explicitly licenses this: "Techniques from qualitative research approaches through the way in which you formulate the issue, collect and analyse data and report results... Techniques from grounded theory approaches may be useful once the core compatibilities and incompatibilities between the two approaches are recognized" (p. 15).

## Relation to the argument

This is now the design's governing methodology, not a supporting citation for role duality. Four direct uses: (1) **"The Program"** — the five institutional configurations (Trinidad secondary schools, La Brea, Boston, Hult preliminary, Hult formal) are AR cycles in the book's own sense: each ran constructing → planning action → taking action → evaluating action, and what was learned evaluating one cycle changed the constructing of the next (the gate mechanics changed, the curriculum changed). The concurrent-cycles clock is the literature for why this multi-grain structure is not just a chronology: the whole two-year program is the hour hand, each cohort is the minute hand, each interview or evaluated submission is the second hand. (2) The **meta-cycle** distinction is the literature for the paper's own two-level structure: the *core* cycle is the GauntleTT/Hult program itself (which continues and evolves regardless of the dissertation), and the *thesis* cycle is this paper's inquiry into what the program reveals about coordination — they are related but must not be conflated, which is a clean answer to a reviewer who asks why the "empirical section" reads as still in progress. (3) **Researcher positionality** should stop presenting role duality defensively (a "challenge to manage") and instead state it via Riordan's quote as the method's own premise. (4) **Analysis technique layering is licensed explicitly**: the chapter itself says a qualitative technique (here, abductive analysis, Timmermans & Tavory 2012) can be adopted inside an AR design once compatibilities are checked — so citing both is not redundant, it is exactly the two-layer structure the source recommends: AR governs the design and practitioner relationship, abductive analysis governs how the harness transcripts are coded.

The first/second/third-person triad is best used for the *study's* audiences (Reason and Marshall's own formula), not forced onto the three interview protocols one-to-one — the self-protocol is closest to first-person material, but "second-person" in this book means collaborative planning and action with others, not merely interviewing them, and the paper should not claim more structural parallel than that.
