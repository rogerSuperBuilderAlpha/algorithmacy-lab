## What it argues

Seven years of ride-hail fieldwork — participant observation as driver and rider, 136 longitudinal interviews, media and company materials — asking why workers stay in, and often like, a job the literature calls the epitome of the bad job. Algorithmic management segments the work into a stream of small decisions, which allows more frequent and narrower choice. Workers navigate it with two opposite tactic sets that produce the same result: **engagement tactics**, following the nudges and staying inside the boundaries, and **deviance tactics**, manipulating the input through geo-spoofing, declining rides and gaming surge. Both elicit consent and both let workers see themselves as skilful agents. The construct is the **constant and confined choice**. Control lands through the score, sanctions run to permanent deactivation, and the paper is precise about the withheld standing: drivers cannot organise, may not negotiate personally with managers, lack access to official voice channels, have minimal recourse against customer malfeasance, and hold limited knowledge of the code managing them. Deviance works only because leniency is programmed in; genuine deviance — geo-spoofing — brings sudden permanent deactivation.

## Relation to the argument

The strongest field evidence for §5's third claim, the one §5 says a worker cannot do anything about. "No means of recourse," "cannot organise," "lack access to official voice channels" is the withheld forum documented as an operating condition in a seven-year study, and §5 should quote it. Cameron also supplies the mechanism that makes the withholding survivable, which is what makes §6's distributive point land: workers experience the arrangement as choiceful, and the consent masks the structural terms.

For §7 the deviance tactics matter as a limit case of intent specification. Geo-spoofing is outbound signalling in its purest form — the driver manufactures the signal the system reads — and it is the one that gets you deactivated. That is the second-order judgement `petreduffyhund2019` names, met here with a hard boundary and a stated penalty. Section 7's clause about knowing what can afford to be lost should be paired with knowing how far the signal can be shaped.

## Caution

Single-sector inductive ethnography of ride-hail; sampling favoured longer-tenured drivers, which biases toward those who coped. The construct is consent, not competency, and the manuscript's frame is read onto it. The independent-contractor "cannot organise" language is a documented condition in this setting, not a universal legal claim — classification is contested and jurisdiction-dependent, and the paper itself notes the AB-5 challenges. Worker harms foregrounded; the manuscript stays descriptive.
