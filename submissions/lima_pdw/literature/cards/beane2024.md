## What it argues

Beane generalizes the shadow-learning finding into a trade argument about skill under intelligent machines. His claim is that expert-novice skill transfer has always depended on three conditions, which he abbreviates as challenge, complexity, and connection: novices need work that stretches them beyond current ability, exposure to the full messy context rather than a sanitized slice, and a bond of mutual respect and trust with an expert who is genuinely on the line alongside them. Intelligent systems reliably remove all three — they take the challenging portion of the task, they abstract away the context, and they separate the novice from the expert — and organizations mistake the resulting productivity gain for progress while the pipeline of expertise quietly stops. He argues the erosion is not inevitable and draws design and management implications from cases where the three conditions were rebuilt.

## Relation to the argument

The most current statement of the problem the paper's site sits inside, and useful to §11 for a reason the paper has not yet used. The Hult cohort builds production software through an AI coding tool and submits to a gate reviewed in part by a bot; on Beane's account that arrangement subtracts challenge, complexity, and connection at once, which predicts that participants acquire the coordination competency the paper is after while acquiring less of the technical competence the programme nominally teaches. The Lima interview reads exactly that way — the participant grew confident about what the gate wanted and simultaneously concluded she could not complete successful builds without the assistant. That is a finding the paper can claim, and it also gives §12 a limit to state: algorithmacy may develop most sharply in settings where other competences are eroding, so a rise in it is not evidence of a well-functioning programme. The connection condition is also the sharpest gloss on the interview's closing remark that discussion with other participants was missed and remains an invaluable part of learning.

## Caution

A trade book for a general audience, not a peer-reviewed contribution; its evidence is the author's own field research (principally the surgical work) plus interviews and cases reported without apparatus, and the three-condition scheme is a framing device rather than a tested model. Cite Beane (2019) for evidence and this book for the generalization, and never the other way round.
