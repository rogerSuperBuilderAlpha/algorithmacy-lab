## What it argues

Chapter 1 opens by stipulating the object of linguistic theory: an ideal speaker-listener in a completely homogeneous speech community, knowing the language perfectly and unaffected by memory limits, distractions, shifts of attention, or errors. On that stipulation Chomsky draws the distinction the whole later lineage runs on — **competence**, the speaker-hearer's tacit knowledge of the language, against **performance**, the actual use of language in concrete situations. Performance is evidence for competence and a poor mirror of it, because performance is degraded by everything the idealisation brackets. The grammar models competence; the corpus records performance.

Two features matter downstream. The distinction separates a *capacity* from its *occasions*, not one capacity from another. And it is deliberately asocial: the homogeneous speech community is a methodological device that buys a clean object by discarding the social conditions of use.

## Relation to the argument

This is the source of §8's grammar. When the paper writes "what a party is able to do in a coordination, as against what she did on any occasion," it is using Chomsky's cut, inherited through Hymes. Citing Hymes (1972) and Spitzberg & Cupach (1984) without naming Chomsky leaves the distinction floating; a half-sentence naming its origin costs nothing, because the competence/performance cut is exactly what licenses measuring algorithmacy on a worker rather than on her outcomes.

The idealisation is also the paper's warrant in reverse. Chomsky bought competence by abstracting from the social situation; Hymes bought it back by re-admitting the situation; algorithmacy is defined *by* a situation — what the form withholds — and so sits on the Hymes side of the family. Saying so pre-empts a linguist's objection that "competence" is being used loosely.

## Caution

Chomsky's competence is knowledge of a grammar, held by an idealised individual, and is not a graded quantity: you either have the grammar or are still acquiring it. Algorithmacy is graded, socially acquired, and defined against a specific institutional arrangement. Cite Chomsky only for the competence/performance *distinction*, never as warrant for the construct.
