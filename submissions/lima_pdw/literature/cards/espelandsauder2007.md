## What it argues

The abstract opens on the growing use of public measures to evaluate individuals and organizations and takes media rankings of law schools as its case. Its claim is that reactivity, the methodological concept for people changing their behaviour when they are measured, is a useful lens for seeing how such measures effect change. Two mechanisms produce reactivity: self-fulfilling prophecy and commensuration. The abstract does not gloss the second; in Espeland's earlier work the word names the transformation of different qualities into a common metric, and that gloss is mine, not the article's. The article then distinguishes the patterns of effects that reactivity produces and proposes a framework for investigating the consequences of public measures, intended and unintended.

The empirical base, the catalogue of effects, and the discussion of gaming were not read. Secondary summaries list several effects by name; they are not recorded here because they were not read in the source.
