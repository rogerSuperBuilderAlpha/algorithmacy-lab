## What it argues

Mees-Buss, Welch and Piekkari argue that the Gioia methodology has hardened from a set of moves into a template, and that the template's demands — the data structure, the first-order/second-order ladder — can crowd out interpretation and produce studies that display rigour rather than achieve it. They propose heuristics in place of the template, and they write from the experience of trying to follow it.

## Relation to the argument

The published permission not to produce a data structure, in the same journal that published Gioia, Corley and Hamilton. Paired with Pratt et al. (2022) on bricolage, it lets the paper decline the template in one sentence and with two ORM citations, which is a stronger position than declining it silently.

## Caution

It is a critique with its own axe; a Gioia-sympathetic reviewer will not accept it as neutral. Declining a template also raises the bar on saying what the analysis actually did — the protocol has to carry that weight, and §7 mostly does.
