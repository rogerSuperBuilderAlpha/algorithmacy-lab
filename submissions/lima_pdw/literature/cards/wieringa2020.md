## What it argues
Wieringa reviews 242 English-language works on algorithmic accountability and finds a literature using the word without the concept. Her corrective is to take Bovens's definition — the actor–forum relation, with its obligation to explain and justify, the forum's capacity to question and judge, and the possibility of consequences — and use it as the analytic grid against which the field's contributions are sorted. Read that way, most of what is filed under algorithmic accountability turns out to address only one element, usually the actor's disclosure, and to leave the forum, the judgement and the consequence unspecified. She extends Bovens's framework for the algorithmic case along a sociotechnical axis, insisting that the accountable object is not the model but the whole assemblage — the design decisions, the data, the deployment context, the organisational actors — distributed across ex ante, in medias res and ex post phases. Her conclusion is that the field has produced an extensive literature on transparency and an underdeveloped one on accountability proper, and that the gap is precisely the forum.

## Relation to the argument
Wieringa is the citation that shows the paper's §5 definition is the settled one in the algorithmic case, not an import the author chose because it suited the argument. She surveyed the field, adopted Bovens, and found the forum missing across the corpus. For a reviewer who suspects the accountability claim is engineered, this is the strongest single answer: the review of 242 works reaches the same diagnosis about the same missing element.

She also supplies §6's framing at the right level of generality. Her sociotechnical assemblage and her three temporal phases say that accountability has to be arranged at design and deployment as well as after the fact, which is why §6's institutional route names statutory review and designed appeal rather than only redress. And her finding that the literature over-invests in transparency is the general form of the paper's own warning that explaining a system to a worker leaves the relation untouched.

## Caution
Read at abstract depth; the corpus size and the specific coding scheme should be verified against the original before citation. It is a review of the *literature*, not evidence about systems in the world — it establishes what scholars have written, not what platforms do. Its normative programme is directed at designers and regulators, and it does not address worker competency at all, so the connection to §5's individual-supply claim is the paper's inference from her framework rather than a claim she makes.
