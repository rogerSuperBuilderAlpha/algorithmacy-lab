## What it argues

Two business-school academics write dual autoethnographic accounts of academic conferences, structured around a sense of doubleness borrowed from *Jekyll and Hyde*, and analyse them through iterated writing and re-reading. They claim two contributions: autoethnographic accounts furnish a genuinely new kind of empirical material — case studies in identity work — and unorthodox sources, including novels, can be legitimate analytical resources in organizational research.

## Relation to the argument

Its use here is as evidence that a top-tier organization-studies journal publishes first-person work by authors writing about institutions they belong to. The doubleness they describe is also a fair description of the investigator's position: instructor and researcher in the same room, with participants who cannot see which one is speaking.
