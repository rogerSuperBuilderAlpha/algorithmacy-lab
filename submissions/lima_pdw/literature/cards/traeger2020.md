# Traeger, M. L., Strohkorb Sebo, S., Jung, M., Scassellati, B., & Christakis, N. A. (2020). Vulnerable robots positively shape human conversational dynamics in a human–robot team. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 117(12), 6370–6375.

## What it argues

The best-powered demonstration that a machine in a group reshapes the humans' relations with each other. Across 51 groups of three humans and one robot playing a collaborative game, robots that made vulnerable statements — admitting error, expressing uncertainty — produced markedly more equal and more responsive conversation *among the human members*, who spoke more evenly, listened more, and reported better group experience. The robot's own contribution to the task was held constant; only its self-presentation varied.

## Relation to the argument

For §9, the decisive feature is the unit of effect. The outcome is human-to-human conversational dynamics, and the manipulation is the machine's conduct. **A machine's behaviour changed how two humans coordinated with each other**, measured directly, in PNAS. Any version of §9 that implies the field cannot see past the human-machine dyad has to answer this study.

For the paper's positive argument, the finding is more useful than threatening. What the robot varied was the *legibility of its own uncertainty*, and legibility improved the humans' coordination. The platform gate does the opposite: it presents determinations with no admitted uncertainty, no error, no account of itself. If Traeger et al. are right that a machine's disclosure of its own fallibility improves the humans' coordination around it, then the gate's opacity is not merely withholding information — it is actively degrading the coordination it mediates. That is an evidence-backed mechanism for §5 and a stronger claim than the paper currently makes.

## Caution

Laboratory groups, a collaborative game, an embodied robot with scripted speech, 51 groups. The robot is a teammate with a disclosed role and no authority over outcomes — not an adjudicator. Note the author-name variant (Strohkorb Sebo / Sebo). Five authors; Christakis last.
