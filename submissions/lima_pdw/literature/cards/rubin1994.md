## What it argues

Rubin and Martin build the Interpersonal Communication Competence Scale, a thirty-item self-report measure across ten dimensions — self-disclosure, empathy, social relaxation, assertiveness, interaction management, altercentrism, expressiveness, supportiveness, immediacy, environmental control. It is the compact, widely reused instrument that made competence measurable in survey research, and its dimensions are drawn by synthesis from the prior scales rather than derived from a theory of what competence must contain.

## Relation to the argument

The clearest illustration of the derivation problem §9 uses to distinguish algorithmacy from gig literacies. Rubin and Martin's ten dimensions are *collected* — assembled from what earlier instruments measured — with no rule saying why ten and not eight. §7's admission rule is a direct answer to that pattern, and the ICCS is the canonical case of the pattern in this literature. The paper claims descent from Sutherland et al. (2020) while distinguishing itself on derivation; the same contrast runs cleanly against the field's own most-used competence scale, and stating it against a communication instrument rather than only against a gig-work study makes the methodological claim look general.

Also useful in the other direction: the ICCS's dimensions are almost entirely about the *presence* of a partner — disclosure to, empathy with, support of, immediacy toward. Ten dimensions and not one survives the removal of the counterpart from contact. That is §9's diagnosis, demonstrable item by item, and it is a stronger demonstration than the abstract argument the section currently runs.

## Caution

Self-report, undergraduate development samples, no behavioural criterion. Widely used and widely criticised for exactly that. Do not model an algorithmacy instrument on it; use it as the negative example.
