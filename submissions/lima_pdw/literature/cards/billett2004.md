## What it argues

Billett argues against treating learning as something that happens only in explicitly educational settings, and proposes reading the workplace itself as a learning environment constituted by an interaction between two things: the **affordances and constraints** the social setting makes available — what a workplace invites or blocks a person from doing — and the **agency and biography** of the individual participant, who chooses how to engage according to her own preferences and goals. Workplaces impose expectations and norms in the interest of their own continuity and of particular participants, so the affordances are never neutrally distributed. Learning at work is therefore a complex negotiation about knowledge-use, roles, and processes, and is best understood as a question of the learner's participation in situated work activities.

## Relation to the argument

The mechanism §10's stratified-fluency property needs and does not yet have. §10 asserts that algorithmacy is unevenly distributed across equivalently placed workers, and a reviewer will immediately ask what produces the unevenness if the structural conditions are identical. Billett's answer is a two-sided one that avoids reducing the variance to individual difference: workplaces **afford** participation differentially, and individuals **elect** to engage differentially, and learning outcomes are the product of the two. Applied to the Hult gate, the affordance side covers who gets a peer reviewer who reads carefully and who gets one who does not, and the agency side covers the participant who read "about 90% of the output" before submitting. That is a far better account of stratification than a bare appeal to unequal private capacity, and it protects §6's distributive argument from the charge that it merely relabels talent. Billett is also the standard citation for the claim that workplace learning is not a lesser form of education but a different one — useful in §8 when the paper says what participation supplies that instruction cannot.

## Caution

A conceptual article in a practitioner-facing vocational-education journal, synthesizing Billett's own prior studies rather than reporting new data; the affordance/agency pairing is a framework, not a tested model, and "affordance" here is used loosely relative to its technology-studies sense. The workplaces Billett studies are conventional employment settings with co-present colleagues, and the paper's site has neither a stable employer nor a knowable evaluator. Abstract-level read; the affordance/agency formulation is verified from the abstract, but verify Billett's own terms before quoting.
