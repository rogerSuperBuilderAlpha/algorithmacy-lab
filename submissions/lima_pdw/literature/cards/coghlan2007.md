## What it argues

Coghlan sets out what an insider action researcher must confront — preunderstanding, role duality and organizational politics — and then does something less common: he supplies an epistemology for it. Drawing on Bernard Lonergan's critical realism, he argues that the insider transcends his own subjectivity not by pretending to detachment but by being attentive to the data, intelligent in understanding, reasonable in judgement and responsible in action, and that these furnish the rigour criteria.

## Relation to the argument

Worth holding in reserve rather than citing in the body. Its value is that it answers the deepest version of the objection — not "you are biased" but "on what account of knowledge could an insider's judgement be trusted at all?" If a reviewer pushes that far, this is the reply. It also names role duality, which is exactly what the instructor-investigator occupies.
