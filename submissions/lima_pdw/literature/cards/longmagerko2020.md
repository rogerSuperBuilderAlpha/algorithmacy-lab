## What it argues

An interdisciplinary synthesis defining **AI literacy** as a set of competencies that enable people to critically evaluate AI technologies, communicate and collaborate effectively with AI, and use AI as a tool. From a review across education, HCI and AI, the authors present a set of competencies and accompanying design considerations for AI-literacy interventions.

## Relation to the argument

Section 9 cites this as the fifth construct, the one that makes the algorithm an object of knowledge, and the card's job is to record why the diagnosis holds even though Long and Magerko use the word "competencies" and even include collaboration with AI. Their framework is built around what a person should understand and be able to do **with respect to AI systems**. Every competency is a relation between a person and a technology. Nothing in it requires a second human party whose wants must be reconstructed, and nothing in it concerns a determination binding on two parties. Section 9's claim that the missing element is a position rather than a variable is exactly right here: one could add competencies indefinitely without producing algorithmacy, because the counterpart has no place to go.

## Caution

A synthesis and design framework, not a validated instrument; the competencies are proposed from a literature review, not measured or factor-analysed. The setting is AI education for general audiences, mostly learners rather than workers. Read at abstract depth; the competency list should be inspected before §9's comparison is stated in final form, since the argument turns on what is in it.
