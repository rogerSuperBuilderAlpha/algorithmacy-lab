## What it argues

Krämer builds a media philosophy around the figure of the **messenger** rather than around the channel. Her messenger model has three positions by construction — sender, messenger, addressee — and the messenger is defined by a paradox: he must be personally present to carry the message and personally absent from its content, a "heteronomous" figure who transmits what is not his. Her cases run from the angel and the herald to the translator, the witness, the analyst, and the virus, and her structural claim is that transmission is never neutral, because the messenger's own materiality and interests inflect what passes. The medium is not a space between two parties; it is a *third party* with a peculiar mode of being.

## Relation to the argument

**The philosophical answer to the critical question, and it is triadic by construction.** Krämer's central objection to twentieth-century communication theory is exactly §9's: the field theorised the medium as a between rather than as a position, and thereby lost the figure who actually does the transmitting. Her messenger model is a three-position account of mediated communication published in German in 2008 and in English in 2015.

What it does not do is what the paper does. Krämer's messenger is heteronomous — defined by *not* being the author of what he carries, and by having no stake in it. The platform gate is autonomous in exactly that respect: it commits content neither party authored, on criteria that serve neither, and it is a stakeholder. **The paper's third party is a messenger who has become a principal**, and Krämer's framework is the sharpest available way to say what has changed. That is a better use of her than as a competitor.

Her insistence that transmission always inflects also supports §7's intent-specification facet from the philosophy side: something is always lost, and the loss is structural rather than a defect to be engineered away.

## Caution

Continental media philosophy, historically and figuratively argued, with no empirical content and no engagement with organisation or platform studies. It is a demanding read whose payoff for this paper is one structural distinction. Two Crossref records for the same book (AUP monograph and De Gruyter edited-book) — cite the AUP 2015 English translation. Nothing in it concerns competence.
