## What it argues

Small attacks the habit of justifying qualitative sample sizes by analogy to survey sampling, where more is better and representativeness is the goal. Field-based studies, he argues, follow a different logic: case selection is sequential and theoretically driven, each case is chosen for what the previous ones left unresolved, and the study reaches its limit at saturation rather than at a target N. He calls this logical rather than statistical inference and shows why "small sample" is the wrong criticism.

## Relation to the argument

The best answer to the reviewer who reads "12–16 participants at each of two waves" and stops. §5's stratified purposive sampling with a saturation rule and an exhausted-unsaturated report is Small's logic implemented, and citing him converts a defensive paragraph into a design claim. His argument also supports why the gate-outcome stratum matters more than the headcount — a study of only those who passed would recover a survivor's account.

## Caution

Written for ethnography, and reviewers trained on cases may still want an Eisenhardt-style replication argument alongside it. It also does not license *any* small N; the design still has to show the sequential, theory-driven selection Small requires, which two fixed waves only partly do.
