## What it argues

Contu and Willmott track what organization studies did to situated learning theory after 1991 and find a systematic loss. Lave and Wenger's account treats power relations as a medium of learning — access to participation is granted or withheld, and who gets to become competent is a political question. In the organizational reception, they argue, that appreciation of power is displaced by a managerial preoccupation with harnessing reified "communities of practice" to the fulfilment of reified corporate objectives. Their demonstration case is Orr's photocopier technicians, cited everywhere as the exemplar of the new situated view and, they argue, systematically stripped of the conflict, the labour-process conditions, and the political economy that Orr documented. What is left is a story about informal community as a resource for the firm. They call for a revitalized situated learning theory in which learning practices are read as enabled and constrained by relations of power.

## Relation to the argument

The bridge between this cluster and §6's distributive edge, and the source that keeps the paper from being read as another management appropriation of Lave and Wenger. §10's second property — stratified fluency, with §6's observation that a form converting unequal private capacity into unequal outcomes has made a competency do an institution's work — is precisely the political reading Contu and Willmott say the field abandoned. Citing them lets the paper claim the harder half of the situated-learning inheritance rather than the manageable half, and it pre-empts the reviewer who says the paper has rediscovered communities of practice and made them a competency. It also supplies the warrant for §8's most exposed sentence, that a rise in average algorithmacy is not a good outcome on its own: on their account, treating a distribution of learning access as a distribution of individual capacity is the characteristic error of the managerialized reading. The paper should cite Lave & Wenger (1991) *through* this article, not around it.

## Caution

A critical-theory intervention with a polemical edge; it argues about how a literature was read rather than presenting evidence about learning, and its reconstruction of Orr is contested. Adopting its frame commits the paper to a critical register that §6 explicitly tries to hold at arm's length by keeping the descriptive and distributive questions apart, so cite it for the diagnosis of the reception and for the power-relations point, not for the labour-process politics wholesale.
