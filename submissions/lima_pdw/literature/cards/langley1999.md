## What it argues

Langley confronts the specific difficulty of process data — events, sequences, incidents, told at different levels and over different time spans — and lays out seven sense-making strategies for theorizing from it: narrative, quantification, alternate templates, grounded theory, visual mapping, temporal bracketing, and synthetic strategy. Each trades accuracy, simplicity and generality differently, and her point is that the choice should be made and defended rather than backed into.

## Relation to the argument

RQ1 and RQ3 are process questions in exactly her sense. "How does a working theory of the gate get built and revised, from which incidents, with what abandoned versions" is a call for temporal bracketing or visual mapping; "who notices a rule change and how does noticing travel" is a diffusion sequence that the dated ledger makes datable.
