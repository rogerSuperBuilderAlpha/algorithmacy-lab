## What it argues

Langley confronts the specific difficulty of process data — events, activities and choices ordered over time, on ambiguous units of analysis, variably embedded temporally, eclectic in kind (interpretations, emotions, cognitions alongside discrete events) — and lays out seven generic "sensemaking" strategies for theorizing from it, each scored on Weick's (1979) accuracy/generality/simplicity tradeoff (Table 1): **narrative** (a detailed story built from raw data, high accuracy, low generality/simplicity — the mode ethnographers and contextualists default to); **quantification** (coding incidents into a matrix for statistical pattern-detection, high simplicity/generality, low accuracy — the Van de Ven/Minnesota Innovation Research Program style); **alternate templates** (running the same events through several a priori theoretical lenses in parallel, as Allison's 1971 Cuban Missile Crisis study did with rational-actor, organizational-process and political models); **grounded theory** (Glaser & Strauss/Strauss & Corbin's hierarchical coding of incidents into categories, dimensions, and core categories — high accuracy, low-to-moderate simplicity); **visual mapping** (flowchart-style diagrams of events/decisions/activities against a time axis, an intermediary step rather than a finished theory); **temporal bracketing** (decomposing a continuous process into successive periods separated by discontinuities, using each period as a unit for replicating an emerging pattern — moderate-to-high accuracy, moderate generality, and explicitly the strategy of choice "if there is some likelihood that feedback mechanisms, mutual shaping, or multidirectional causality will be incorporated into the theorization"); and **synthetic strategy** (compressing the whole process into global summary variables for comparison across cases, sacrificing process detail for predictive power). None is claimed superior; strategies can combine (three groups — grounding, organizing, replicating), and Langley closes by insisting theorizing always requires an "uncodifiable creative leap" no strategy alone supplies.

## Relation to the argument

**Temporal bracketing is the directly usable strategy, and it names something the current draft's "Program" section does without naming it.** The section already narrates the two-year trajectory — Trinidad secondary schools (Jul–Aug 2025) → La Brea → Boston public cohort (Apr–May 2026) → Hult preliminary cohorts (Jul–Aug 2026) → the formal Hult spring 2027 course — as a sequence of discrete institutional configurations. Langley's own worked example (Figure 2, a firm's technology adoption traced across three periods separated by discontinuities: a strike, a change in leadership) is structurally the same move: successive periods, not claimed to have theoretical significance in themselves, used to structure comparison and to let discontinuities at the boundaries do analytic work rather than being smoothed into a single continuous narrative. Citing Langley converts the Program section from an unstructured chronology into a named analytic choice, and licenses treating the boundary between cohorts (a change of site, of gate mechanics, of population) as itself informative — which is what the section's own claim about three different withholding conditions across cohorts already needs. Her caution that grounded-theory-style analysis "makes more sense" for some process data than others, and less well for macroscopic, long-period phenomena like this one, is a reason to prefer bracketing over grounded-theory coding specifically for the *developmental trajectory*, while abductive analysis (Timmermans & Tavory) still governs the *within-interview* coding of critical incidents — the two operate at different grains and are not competing claims.

## Caution

Verify the author every time — the library's existing `langleyleyshon2021.md` is Paul Langley and Andrew Leyshon on FinTech political economy, and conflating them would be an unusually visible error in a paper about method. The article predates the "process studies" special-issue consolidation; Langley, Smallman, Tsoukas & Van de Ven (2013) is the contemporary companion citation if the paper needs the field's later standing rather than the original strategy typology. Do not present temporal bracketing as replacing the abductive frame for coding transcripts — Langley's strategies are about organizing *process* data (the trajectory across cohorts), not about how a single critical-incident interview is analyzed.
