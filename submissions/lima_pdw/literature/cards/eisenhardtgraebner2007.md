## What it argues

An AMJ "From the Editors" commentary revisiting the 1989 roadmap (Eisenhardt, 1989, *AMR*) from the editors' side, addressing the objections case researchers actually meet in review. Five challenges, each with a stated antidote: (1) **justifying theory building at all** — either theory-driven (extend an existing theory a research question is tightly scoped within) or phenomenon-driven (broadly scoped, justified by the phenomenon's importance and the absence of viable theory); (2) **theoretical sampling** — cases are chosen for the likelihood they will offer theoretical insight, not to be representative of a population, and single-case sampling in particular is "straightforward: they are chosen because they are unusually revelatory, extreme exemplars, or opportunities for unusual research access" (quoting Yin, 1994) — the article's own worked examples are Weick's (1993) Mann Gulch fire and Dutton & Dukerich's (1991) Port Authority study, both single, extreme cases; (3) **interview-data bias** — mitigated by numerous, diversely positioned informants and by combining retrospective with real-time data collection; (4) **presenting evidence** without either losing the theory in a full narrative (feasible for a single case) or losing the empirical grounding in tables (the multi-case problem); (5) **writing emergent theory** without a fixed template. On terminology, the article explicitly advises **avoiding the phrase "qualitative research"** and **"grounded theory building"** except when using Glaser and Strauss's precise sense, because both labels cover epistemologically incompatible research strategies and readers who expect one will be confused or disappointed by another — the theory-building-from-cases strategy the article describes is stated to be "objective" and "positivist" in stance, explicitly contrasted with naturalistic/interpretive qualitative traditions.

## Relation to the argument

The single-case justification is exactly as advertised and directly usable in "The Trinidad Field Setting as a Strategic Research Site": a single case is warranted when it is "unusually revelatory, extreme... or an opportunity for unusual research access," and the section's own three-condition argument (an unsorted labor market, an identical curriculum administered on a synchronized timeline, three structurally distinct opacity conditions across cohorts) is precisely a revelatory-case argument in the article's terms — pair it with Merton's strategic-research-site language already in the draft rather than replacing it. Use the citation **narrowly, for the site-justification move only**. The article's own terminology warning is a reason not to reach further: it explicitly distinguishes "theory building from cases" as an objective, positivist, replication-logic strategy from the naturalistic/interpretive traditions this paper actually uses (harness interviews coded abductively, not multiple bounded cases compared by replication logic). Citing Eisenhardt & Graebner for the site argument and Timmermans & Tavory for the analytic method is coherent; citing Eisenhardt & Graebner as a second governing methodology alongside abductive analysis is not — the article says as much about avoiding label conflation.

## Caution

Do not let this citation drift into standing for the paper's whole methodology. The design here is a single evolving field site with critical-incident interviews coded abductively, not a multiple-case, replication-logic study in Eisenhardt's sense — the article is explicit that its own strategy is a distinct "positivist" one from naturalistic qualitative inquiry, so citing it for anything beyond the single-case justification risks exactly the label confusion the article itself warns against.
