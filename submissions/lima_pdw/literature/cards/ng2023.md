## What it argues

Ng's group operationalises its own 2021 four-aspect scheme into a 25-item, five-point Likert questionnaire and tests it on 363 secondary school students across two Hong Kong schools. Confirmatory factor analysis returns six factors: intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, behavioural intention, behavioural engagement, know and understand AI, and use and apply AI. Reliability is weak in places — Cronbach's alpha ranges from .58 to .88 across the six factors, and .58 is below any conventional floor. The authors then propose a less parsimonious model organised on an affective/behavioural/cognitive/ethical (ABCE) framework, which fits acceptably and reports alphas of .91 to .94, and concede that "further studies are needed to confirm the factor structure."

The instrument's centre of gravity has moved since 2021. Four of the six factors are motivational or behavioural rather than competence-bearing: intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, behavioural intention, behavioural engagement. Only two — know and understand, use and apply — carry AI content, and the 2021 review's third and fourth aspects (evaluate and create, ethical issues) do not survive as separate factors in the parsimonious model. The construct being measured is closer to learner disposition toward an AI curriculum than to any capacity exercised outside a classroom.

## Relation to the argument

Neighbour, and a weak one; useful mainly as evidence for the paper's structural claim rather than as a threat to it.

No human counterpart appears. The items measure a student's motivation to learn about AI, her confidence that she can, her intention to keep going, her actual engagement, and two knowledge domains. There is no second party anywhere in the instrument, no determination, and no coordination. The relational content is student↔curriculum, mediated by AI as topic.

The card earns its place in §9 for a different reason: it shows what happens when you try to *measure* the AI-literacy construct in situ. The AI-specific content thins and generic learning-psychology factors expand to fill the instrument. Ng et al.'s six factors are four parts educational psychology to two parts AI. That is the predictable consequence of a construct built as an inventory of knowable things: once you demand psychometric behaviour from it, the knowable things do not cohere, and what coheres is motivation. The paper can use this to press the derivation discrimination — a construct derived from what a coordination form withholds would not decompose into intrinsic motivation and behavioural intention, because those are not what the form withholds.

Also usable against uniformity: the study reports no analysis of *why* equivalently placed students differ, treating variance as measurement noise around a teachable mean. Stratified fluency requires a construct that predicts and explains unevenness. This one absorbs it.

## Caution

A three-page ICALT short paper, not a full validation study; 363 students in two schools in one city, with no invariance testing and no external criterion. The .58 alpha is disqualifying for the weakest factor and the authors say so. Do not cite this as "the Ng et al. AI literacy scale" as though it were an established instrument — it is a pilot the authors describe as needing confirmation, and the group's more-cited product remains the 2021 conceptual review. Read at abstract depth; the item wordings are not in the abstract and would need the IEEE version of record, which matters if the manuscript wants to quote an item to make the no-counterpart point. For that purpose Pinski and Benlian's fully reproduced item set is the better exhibit.
