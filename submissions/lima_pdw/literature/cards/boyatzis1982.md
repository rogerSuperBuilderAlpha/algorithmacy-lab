## What it argues

Boyatzis operationalized McClelland's programme and gave the field its standard definition: a competency is **an underlying characteristic of a person** that results in effective or superior performance in a job — a motive, trait, aspect of self-image or social role, skill, or body of knowledge. The book reports a study of over two thousand managers across a dozen organizations, sorts competencies into clusters (goal and action management, leadership, human resource management, directing subordinates, focus on others, specialized knowledge), and distinguishes threshold competencies, which are needed to hold the job at all, from those that distinguish superior performers. The organizing device is a model of effective performance as the fit between three things: the individual's competencies, the job's demands, and the organizational environment.

## Relation to the argument

This is the source of the definition §8 actually uses, and citing it would make the section honest about its lineage. §8's "combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, behaviours and attributes enabling effective performance in a role or context" is the Boyatzis definition with a broader noun list; it is not Sandberg's, and §8 currently attributes the spirit of it to Sandberg. The card's second use is more constructive. Boyatzis's three-way fit model — person, job demands, environment — is a close structural analogue of what §8's table is trying to do, since "the competency a form demands" is a claim that the environment sets the demand and the person meets it. If the paper is going to use a competency vocabulary, this is the version of it that most nearly supports the form-indexed claim, and it comes with a warning: on Boyatzis's model the competency is still an underlying characteristic *of the person*, dispositional and possessed, which is the property §8 objects to in "capability" and does not disown in "competency."

## Caution

A management monograph presenting proprietary consulting research; the sample, instruments, and analysis are not reproducible from the book, the behavioural-event-interview method it relies on has been criticized for circularity, and the competency clusters have not held up well in independent replication. It is nonetheless the citation of record for the definition, and the paper should cite it for that reason rather than as evidence. Metadata-level read; verify the definition's exact wording against a copy before quoting, since it is quoted in several slightly different forms across the literature.
