## What it argues

The inaugural editorial of the field's journal, and more argumentative than the genre requires. Fortunati and Edwards define HMC as the study of meaning-making between humans and machines, insist the machine be treated as a communicative *subject* rather than an instrument, and lay out the field's open problems: what counts as a machine, whether machine communication is communication at all, how to study a partner that has no interiority, and what methodological apparatus survives the move. Their 2021 follow-up, "Moving ahead with human-machine communication" (doi:10.30658/hmc.2.1), reviews the first years of output.

## Relation to the argument

Two contributions to §9. It confirms the field's constitutive unit in the field's own founding words — meaning-making *between humans and machines* — which is the dyad, stated as a definition rather than inferred from practice. And Fortunati's own background makes the second contribution more interesting: she is the theorist of unwaged reproductive labour (*The Arcane of Reproduction*, 1995, already carded in the library), and the editorial's concern with what machine communicators do to human labour is a thread the paper's co-optation frame could pick up. HMC's founding editor is not naive about labour; she simply is not asking the coordination question.

That matters for tone. §9's diagnosis is better delivered as "this field asks a different question well" than as "this field missed something," and Fortunati & Edwards make the different question explicit enough to quote.

## Caution

An editorial in a new open-access journal; it sets an agenda and cites broadly rather than establishing findings. Both authors are the journal's founding editors, so the definition is a stipulation by interested parties. Edwards here is Autumn Edwards, not Chad Edwards — the pair publish together and are frequently confused.
