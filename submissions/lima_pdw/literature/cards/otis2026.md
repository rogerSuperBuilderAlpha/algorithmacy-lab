## What it argues

A field experiment with Kenyan entrepreneurs randomised access to a GPT-4-powered business assistant and measured revenues and profits. There is no average effect: **"we are unable to reject the null hypothesis of no average treatment effect on firm revenues and profits."** There is a large interaction. **"The effect for entrepreneurs who were low performing at baseline is over 0.20-standard-deviations lower than for initial high performers. Subsample analyses show that low performers did nearly 10% worse because of the AI assistant, whereas high performers may have benefited by over 15%."**

The mechanism sentence is the one this cluster needs, and it is unusual: **"This differential impact does not appear to result from differences in the questions posed to the AI or the advice that it provided but rather, from the advice that entrepreneurs chose to implement."** Input to the system was equivalent across arms. Output from the system was equivalent. What diverged was *what the recipient did with it* — selection and implementation of advice.

## Relation to the argument

The most valuable non-obvious source in this cluster, and it cuts two ways at once.

Against §1: the moderator is baseline performance, so this is divergence by position, and it runs opposite to the novice-levelling studies — here the strong pull further ahead and the weak are actively harmed. Any claim that the AI literature shows a single directional heterogeneity is wrong, and §6's "novice-levelling" sentence needs the qualifier that the field-experimental record is split.

For §1: the authors ruled out the two channels a technology explanation would use. Same questions asked, same advice returned, different outcomes. What remains is a difference in how the recipient reads and takes up an opaque adviser's output — which is close to the construal mechanism §1 needs and, in this design, is *measured as a residual after the technology channel is closed*. It is not competency in the manuscript's sense, and the authors do not name it, but it is the peer-reviewed result that comes nearest to locating variance at the level of the worker's engagement with the system rather than the system's behaviour toward the worker.

## Caution

Entrepreneurs running their own firms, not workers under algorithmic management; there is no platform, no dispatcher and no employer, so the co-optation frame does not transfer without argument. The interaction is with **baseline performance**, a position variable, so this does not establish divergence net of skill — it establishes that the *direction* of the skill interaction is not stable across settings. "May have benefited by over 15%" is hedged in the original; keep the hedge. Subsample analyses on a null average effect invite multiple-comparisons scepticism.
