## What it argues

Three years of fieldwork on a large high-skill freelancing platform — 98 interviews, 2,053 discussion-board messages, archival documents, registered-user observation — on what workers do when the platform's rating is both opaque and moving. The **invisible cage** is Rahman's construct: control in which the criteria are hidden and shift without notice, so workers experience the rating as control yet cannot decipher or learn from the criteria or from changes to them. He sets it against the Weberian iron cage, whose rules are fixed and knowable, and delineates five components that make the cage invisible — the evaluation **criteria** (opaque, shifting), its **execution** (uncertain intervals), its **magnitude** (extent of change unidentifiable), its **impact** (effect on reputation uncertain), and **who influences** it (client or platform, unspecified). In 2015 the platform replaced a transparent five-star system with an opaque 100-point Success Score, without notice.

The findings are a two-form typology of **reactivity**. *Experimental reactivity* is testing tactics to raise the score — different project types and contract lengths, category switching, customised pitches. *Constrained reactivity* is limiting engagement to protect it — taking platform-met clients off-platform, refusing new clients, withdrawing. Which one a worker enacts turns on **platform dependence** and **evaluation setbacks**. The opacity is deliberate and defended: adopted to prevent gaming and preserve differentiation, backed by a two-year ban on off-platform payment and an algorithm that detects contact exchange. The reactivity entrenches rather than relaxes the platform's hold, and paranoia persists years on.

## Relation to the argument

Already the paper's anchor for §5's account of what co-optation withholds, and the five components deserve more weight than the manuscript currently gives them. They are a **structured decomposition of the withholding**, and three of them line up with §7's facets while two do not. Criteria maps to asymmetric interpretation, execution and magnitude to temporal tracking, impact to nothing the worker can act on, and who-influences to the counterpart problem — a worker who cannot tell whether the client or the platform moved the score cannot address either. That last one is worth a sentence in §7, because it names a failure mode the three facets do not cover: not knowing which party to interpret.

The reactivity typology is the sharpest challenge to §7's structure available in the organisational literature, and §7 should meet it. Rahman's two forms are cut by *engagement level* — experiment more, or expose yourself less — while §7's three facets are cut by *direction and time*. The cuts are orthogonal rather than competing, and saying so protects the derivation. Where Rahman does anticipate a facet is temporal tracking: the whole construct rests on criteria that shift without notice, which is §7's third facet stated as the environment rather than as a competence.

## Caution

A single-platform inductive study of high-skill freelancing; the construct is worker reactivity, and the coordination-form frame is this paper's. The algorithm's criteria are by design unknown to the author too. Worker harms are foregrounded and the manuscript's use stays descriptive. Rahman documents reactivity but does not measure competence or outcome, so he cannot evidence §10's stratified fluency — he shows workers respond differently and explains the difference by dependence and setback, which are *rival* explanations to competency and are the ones §11 must separate.
