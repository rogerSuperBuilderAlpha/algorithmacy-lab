**Identifier:** doi:10.1177/1461444819858691  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** SAGE Journals (online first 2019; issue 2020)
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read

## What it argues

Guzman and Lewis argue that AI technologies are not media through which people communicate but **communicators in their own right**, and that communication theory built on the assumption of a human sender and a human receiver cannot accommodate them. They propose Human–Machine Communication as the area that takes the machine as a communicative subject, and set three research agendas: the **functional** (what these technologies do, and what roles they take in communication), the **relational** (how people relate to them, and how those relations reshape relations among people), and the **metaphysical** (how AI destabilises the categories of human, machine, and communication themselves).

The relational agenda is broader than the article's own emphasis and includes, explicitly, the effect of machine communicators on human-to-human relationships.

## Relation to the argument

§9's "partner" row, and the characterisation is fair for the article's centre of gravity: the functional and relational agendas are worked through the human-machine pair, and the theoretical payoff is described as revising the sender-receiver model by admitting a machine to one of the two seats. That is the dyad §9 diagnoses, and Guzman and Lewis do inherit it.

**But the diagnosis is not airtight, and the paper should know where the gap is.** The relational agenda explicitly asks how communication with machines alters communication *among humans*, which is a three-party question posed rather than answered. A reviewer from HMC will point at that sentence. The defensible response is that posing the question is not theorising the position: nothing in the agenda gives the machine a seat that *adjudicates* between two humans, and the field's subsequent output has overwhelmingly gone to the human-machine dyad. §9 should say "the field has not taken this up" rather than "the frame excludes it," because the former is true and the latter is checkable and false.

## Caution

An agenda-setting essay, not an empirical study or a formal theory; it programmatically declines to specify mechanisms. Note the year: online-first 2019, issue 22(1) 2020 — cite 2020 as the paper already does. Lewis is second author.
