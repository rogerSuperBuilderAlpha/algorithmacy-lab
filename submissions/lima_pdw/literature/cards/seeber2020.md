## What it argues

Twelve authors set an agenda for AI as a **team member** rather than a tool. They distinguish machines that support a team's work from machines that take a role in it — contributing ideas, facilitating, moderating, evaluating — and argue that the second changes team processes, member relations, and the distribution of authority within the team. Their agenda organises questions by team function: idea generation, decision making, coordination, and the social-emotional work of holding a team together.

The interesting cases for this cluster are the **facilitation and evaluation** roles, where the machine acts on the relations *among* the human members rather than alongside them.

## Relation to the argument

A genuine multi-party framework with a machine in it, from the information-systems literature, and therefore something §9 has to place. The machine-as-facilitator role in particular puts a system in a position that shapes what passes between two humans, which is the arrangement §9 says is missing from the constructs it sorts.

Where the paper's ground remains: a teammate is **inside a group with a shared task and a shared fate**, whose members know one another and whose machine member's contributions are visible and discussable. §7's arrangement has no team, no shared task, and no visibility — the counterpart is unmet and the system's contribution is a verdict, not a proposal a member can argue with. The distinction the paper needs is between a machine that **participates** in a group's deliberation and one that **adjudicates** between parties who never deliberate. That is the same distinction the ODR literature draws between the fourth party as support and the fourth party as decider, and it is the paper's real boundary.

## Caution

An agenda paper by a large author group, programmatic and without data. The twelve-author list is verified against Crossref and is frequently truncated to four names in citing literature; keep it intact or use "Seeber et al." The HICSS 2018 predecessor has a different, overlapping author list.
