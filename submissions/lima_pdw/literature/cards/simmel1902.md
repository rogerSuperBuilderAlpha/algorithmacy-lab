## What it argues

Simmel's founding argument that **the number of parties is itself a sociological form**. The dyad is unique: it has no life independent of its two members, each faces the other directly, and either one's withdrawal ends it. Adding a third member changes the structure qualitatively, not quantitatively. The triad admits forms the dyad cannot contain — the **mediator** (*der Unparteiische*, the impartial third who reconciles), the **tertius gaudens** (the third who profits from the other two's opposition), and **divide et impera** (the third who creates the opposition in order to profit). In every case the third party's presence means that relations between the other two are no longer immediate: they are routed, and the router has interests.

Simmel's deeper point is that in the triad, for the first time, a *supra-individual* structure appears — something that outlasts any member and to which members are subordinated.

## Relation to the argument

**The classical warrant for §9's structural claim, and the paper should cite it.** Simmel establishes that dyad and triad are different forms rather than the same form with different populations, which is precisely what §9 asserts when it says "the missing element is a *position*, not a variable." A reviewer will find the assertion more credible with a hundred-and-twenty-year-old sociological argument behind it than without.

Three of Simmel's terms map onto the paper's arrangement with unsettling directness. The platform is *der Unparteiische* by self-description — the neutral intermediary — and *tertius gaudens* by structure, since it is the party that profits from the other two never meeting. §5–§6's account of what co-optation withholds is a description of divide-et-impera conditions: the worker and the counterpart cannot reach each other except through the third, and the third's revenue depends on that.

Simmel also supplies what the paper's triad currently lacks — a reason the third position is *stable*. His supra-individual structure is exactly the "commits determinations neither endorsed" clause: the triad's characteristic feature is that its members are subordinated to something none of them individually is.

## Caution

1902, translated from Simmel's *Soziologie*, and the AJS serialisation splits across two issues that are routinely cited as one. Simmel's mediator is a *person* with interests, not a system with a rule, and his triad presumes all three parties are aware of one another — the paper's worker often is not aware of the counterpart at all. This is a structural analogy and a strong one; it is not a theory of algorithmic mediation, and it says nothing about competence.
