# Canale, M., & Swain, M. (1980). Theoretical bases of communicative approaches to second language teaching and testing. *Applied Linguistics*, 1(1), 1–47.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1093/applin/1.1.1  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** Oxford Academic
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Canale and Swain do what Hymes did not: they decompose communicative competence into components a test could address. Three here — **grammatical competence** (the code), **sociolinguistic competence** (appropriateness of meaning and of form to context), and **strategic competence** (the verbal and non-verbal repair strategies deployed when the other two fall short or when performance conditions degrade). Canale's 1983 revision adds **discourse competence**, cohesion and coherence across extended text. They also insist on separating communicative competence from communicative *performance*, the realisation of those competences in actual production and comprehension, and warn that a test measures the latter and infers the former.

The paper is a design document, and its argument for decomposition is blunt: a competence you cannot partition is a competence you cannot teach or assess.

## Relation to the argument

The methodological precedent for §7's three facets. Canale and Swain show that moving from a competence *name* to a competence *construct* means naming a small set of components and a principle of division, and that components are defensible only if you can say why there are that many. §9's boast — algorithmacy's facets are derived from what the form withholds, which "is what lets the account say why there are three" — is the same discipline, and citing them makes it visible as a standard rather than an idiosyncrasy.

**Strategic competence is the sharper borrowing.** It names the capacity to keep a communication going when the enabling conditions are absent: a missing word, a broken channel, an unshared code. That is structurally what algorithmacy asks of a worker — sustaining coordination when the arrangement has withheld what coordination normally runs on. If a reviewer asks whether any prior competence construct anticipated "competency as compensation for withheld conditions," strategic competence is the honest answer, and it is a component rather than a whole construct, which is a difference the paper can defend.

## Caution

The domain is second-language teaching and testing, and the model has been contested inside applied linguistics for forty years (Bachman's reorganisation, Celce-Murcia's revisions). Do not import the component scheme as a template. Crossref carries a duplicate record, 10.1093/applin/i.1.1, a roman-numeral volume artefact listing Canale alone; use 10.1093/applin/1.1.1. The paper is routinely miscited as Canale (1980) without Swain.
