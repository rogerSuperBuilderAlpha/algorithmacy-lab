## What it argues

Jarrahi proposes turning qualitative interviewing on the machine: treating an AI system as an interviewee whose characteristics and behaviours can be probed with the methods developed for human respondents, in order to characterise systems that resist inspection by other means.

## Relation to the argument

Included as a **disambiguation card**. The title is close enough to this study's concern that it will surface in any search for AI and interviewing, and it is about the opposite arrangement — a human interviewing a machine, not a machine interviewing humans. Citing it as support for the instrument would be a visible error. Its one genuine adjacency is to Christin (2020) and Seaver (2017): it is another tactic for studying systems that will not open.

## Caution

**Not about AI-conducted interviews of human participants.** Do not cite it for the instrument under any circumstance. Its own methodological status is also contested — whether a model's outputs constitute an interviewee's answers is precisely what Kapania et al. (2025) dispute.
