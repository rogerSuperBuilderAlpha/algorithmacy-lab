## What it argues

A comparative ethnography of an open high-skill freelancing market and a closed ride-hail platform, on how algorithmically mediated customer control changes the relationship between control and resistance. Because customers rate workers and algorithms tally those ratings to control future opportunity, the service encounter **expands**: control and resistance now operate before, during and after a task rather than only during execution. The paper tracks how latitude for covert resistance narrows across the three stages — most before the task, when the customer cannot yet monitor; least after it, when the worker is reduced to influencing customers to protect a rating and to Hail Mary actions such as filing disputes with little chance of succeeding. The staged aims are **Inflate, Safeguard, Salvage**. A second finding cuts the other way: because control runs through customer ratings, workers can influence and manipulate the algorithms in ways platforms cannot always observe.

## Relation to the argument

The three-stage structure is the best available account of **when** each facet of §7 operates, and the manuscript would gain from adopting it in §11's design. Inbound interpretation and outbound specification are before-task and during-task operations; the after-task stage is where the worker discovers whether her reconstruction was right, and where §5's accountability withholding bites — Hail Mary dispute-filing with little chance of succeeding is what §5 predicts when a worker tries to supply a forum privately.

The blind-spot finding is also a live threat to §5's asymmetry claim and §11 is designed to look for exactly this. If workers can manipulate ratings in ways the platform cannot observe, and if that manipulation reliably reverses outcomes, then §5's "she cannot cause the appeal to be received" is under pressure. What Cameron and Rahman actually show is influence over the *input* to the rating, not over the platform's response to a challenge, so §5 survives — but the distinction is fine and worth stating in the manuscript rather than leaving to a reader.

## Caution

Two pseudonymised platforms; the study foregrounds covert resistance, so it is an account of pushing back within the form rather than a taxonomy of it. The version underlying the lab card is the accepted manuscript, pre-typesetting; cite *Organization Science* 33(1):38–58 for page numbers. The authors' sympathy is with worker agency and the manuscript takes the structural finding without the advocacy.
