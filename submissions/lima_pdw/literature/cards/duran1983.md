## What it argues

Duran isolates one component of competence and argues it is the component that matters: **adaptability**, the ability to perceive socio-interpersonal relationships and adjust one's goals and behaviours accordingly. The Communicative Adaptability Scale operationalises six dimensions — social composure, social confirmation, social experience, appropriate disclosure, articulation, wit. Duran's 1992 review (doi:10.1080/01463379209369840) revisits the conceptualisation and concedes the measure had drifted from the construct.

The claim underneath is that competence is not a fixed repertoire but a capacity to *revise* behaviour as the read of the situation changes.

## Relation to the argument

The nearest antecedent in this literature to **temporal tracking**, §7's third facet. Duran's adaptability is the capacity to notice that the situation has changed and to alter behaviour accordingly; temporal tracking is the capacity to notice that an undisclosed rule has moved and to alter behaviour accordingly. The difference is what supplies the signal. Duran's actor reads a partner and a social setting that are visible to her; §7's worker reads only outcomes from a system that does not announce its revisions. That is a real difference and it is worth stating, because a reviewer from communication will otherwise ask why temporal tracking is not just adaptability under a new name.

The 1992 self-critique is also a useful caution for §7. Duran found that a scale built to measure a capacity ended up measuring social ease, because the items that survive psychometric filtering are the ones respondents can answer about themselves comfortably. Temporal-tracking items risk the same fate.

## Caution

Self-report, undergraduate samples, dimensions that on inspection mix capacity with personality (wit, social composure). Duran himself flagged the construct-measure drift. Cite for the adaptability concept and the drift warning, not the scale.
