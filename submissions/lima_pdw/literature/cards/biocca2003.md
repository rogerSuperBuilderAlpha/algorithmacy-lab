## What it argues

Biocca, Harms and Burgoon audit twenty-five years of social presence research and find the construct incoherent: definitions range from co-presence to psychological involvement to behavioural engagement, measures do not converge, and the field has treated a medium property, a perception, and an interaction outcome as one thing. They propose a three-level reconstruction — **co-presence** (awareness of the other), **psychological involvement** (attention, empathy, mutual understanding), and **behavioural engagement** (interaction and mutual assistance) — with mutual awareness and, critically, **perceived mutual understanding** as the hinge, and they lay out validity criteria any measure must meet.

## Relation to the argument

The most useful item here is the middle level. Their psychological-involvement dimension includes *perceived* mutual understanding — the sense that the other party grasps what one means — and their argument is that a measure must distinguish perceived from actual, because the two dissociate and the dissociation is where mediated communication goes wrong.

That is the paper's arrangement stated as a measurement problem. Under an opaque gate the worker has no route from perceived to actual: she can believe her intent registered and have no way to check. §7's asymmetric interpretation is the labour of estimating actual understanding from outcomes alone, and Biocca et al.'s framework gives §11 a construct-validity structure for measuring it — the gap between a worker's perceived mutual understanding and the outcome she actually received.

Their audit is also a caution the paper should read as a mirror. A construct proliferates definitions when its early statements are evocative and its measures are not disciplined by a derivation rule. §7 has a derivation rule; §11 must keep it.

## Caution

A review and criteria paper, not a validated instrument; the Networked Minds measure the authors developed has had mixed uptake. Presence research is oriented to virtual environments and telerobotics, not work platforms. Three authors; Burgoon third.
