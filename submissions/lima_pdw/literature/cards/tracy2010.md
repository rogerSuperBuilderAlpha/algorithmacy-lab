## What it argues

Tracy proposes eight criteria that cut across paradigms — worthy topic, rich rigor, sincerity, credibility, resonance, significant contribution, ethics, and meaningful coherence — and distinguishes each criterion (the end) from the practices that achieve it (the means), so that studies in different traditions can be judged without forcing one tradition's methods on another.

## Relation to the argument

Useful precisely because it is paradigm-neutral: a bricolage design that is neither Gioia nor grounded theory still has to say by what standard it should be judged, and Tracy's eight give an answerable list. Sincerity, in her sense — transparency about the researcher's biases and about the methods' shortcomings — is what §9 and §10 of the protocol already do, and meaningful coherence is the criterion the theory–method fit argument satisfies.

## Caution

*Qualitative Inquiry* sits outside the OS/OT canon and the eight criteria are frequently cited as a checklist, which is the misuse Tracy warns against. Lincoln and Guba (1985) remain the more familiar reference for an organizational reviewer.
