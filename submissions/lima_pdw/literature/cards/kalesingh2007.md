## What it argues

Kale and Singh ask how the capacity to work a network form gets built. Their answer is a learning process with four moments — articulation, codification, sharing and internalization of alliance management know-how — and they show it is positively related to a firm's overall alliance success. Prior work had established that firms with a dedicated alliance function do better; this paper shows the alliance function also drives the learning process, and that the process partly mediates the function's effect. The claim is that the learning process is one of the main mechanisms through which the dedicated function produces success — an attempt to open the black box between structure and outcome. Evidence is survey data from a large sample of U.S.-based firms and their alliances.

## Relation to the argument

This is the acquisition half of the network precedent, and §10 needs it. The paper asks how algorithmacy is acquired and how it is distributed; the network literature's answer for its own competency is that the capacity is manufactured by a deliberate internal process of articulating and codifying tacit know-how, and that the manufacturing can be organized. Anyone who says the seventh row invites a deficit reading of the coordinated party can be pointed here: the network column's competency was theorized from the start as an organizational achievement, not a personal endowment.

The contrast with the fourth column is where the argument bites. A firm builds alliance capability through a dedicated function that articulates, codifies and shares what it has learned. A platform worker reconstructing an unstated rule has no function, no codification, and — where the platform's terms forbid or discourage it — limited sharing. The same learning process, stripped of every institutional support, is roughly what §5 says co-optation withholds.

## Caution

Abstract only. The mediation claim is cross-sectional and self-reported; "partly mediates" is doing real work in the abstract and the manuscript should not upgrade it. The construct is firm-level throughout, and "alliance success" is a managerial judgment measure in this literature. Note also that Emerald's *Strategic Direction* and *Development and Learning in Organizations* both published short digests of this paper under near-identical titles with H. Singh as sole listed author; cite the *Strategic Management Journal* article (10.1002/smj.616), not the digests.
