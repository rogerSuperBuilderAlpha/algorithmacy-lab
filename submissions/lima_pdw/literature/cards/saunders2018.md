## What it argues

Saturation means different things in different traditions, and Saunders and colleagues separate them: theoretical saturation in grounded theory, inductive thematic saturation, a priori thematic saturation, and data saturation as simple informational redundancy. They then press on operationalisation — what a study must actually specify for a saturation claim to be checkable, and why "saturation was reached" without a rule is unfalsifiable.

## Relation to the argument

§5 of the protocol states a rule — a stratum saturates when three consecutive interviews add no new code and move no existing code's boundary — and this article is what makes that rule recognisable as good practice rather than arbitrary. It also supports the more unusual commitment: reporting a stratum as **exhausted-unsaturated** when the cohort runs out first, which is precisely the honest reporting these authors argue for.

## Caution

Health-research provenance and a conceptual paper rather than an empirical one. Note the type of saturation being claimed — with facets held as sensitizing concepts, the study is closer to inductive thematic saturation than to theoretical saturation in the grounded-theory sense, and conflating them invites a question.
