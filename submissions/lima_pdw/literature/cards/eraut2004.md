## What it argues

Eraut's most-cited statement of what informal learning at work consists of and what makes it happen. He argues that "informal learning" is best treated not as a residual category but as the main channel of professional development, and he builds a typology of **work activities** that give rise to it — participating in group processes, working alongside others, tackling challenging tasks, working with clients, consultation, problem-solving — alongside **learning processes** such as asking questions, listening and observing, reflecting, trying things out, and learning from mistakes. Two sets of factors govern the outcome: **learning factors** (the challenge and value of the work, feedback and support, confidence and commitment) and **context factors** (the allocation and structuring of work, encounters and relationships at work, individual expectations of performance and progress). His recurring finding is that feedback and support determine whether challenging work produces learning or produces stress.

## Relation to the argument

Directly relevant to §11's protocol design and to the falsifier in §10. Eraut's finding that challenge without feedback does not reliably produce learning is a warning about the paper's own site: the Hult gate supplies challenge and withholds reasoning, which is Eraut's predicted worst case, and yet the paper claims competency develops there. Confronting that is worth a paragraph, and the honest answer is probably that what develops under challenge-without-feedback is not uniform learning but a stratified distribution — which is exactly §10's second property, and would connect the two properties by a mechanism instead of by assertion. Eraut's list of learning processes also gives the interview protocol its prompts: asking questions, observing others, trying things out, and learning from mistakes are each observable in a cohort, and the Lima interview shows three of the four. The list of context factors — how work is allocated, what relationships exist at work, what performance is expected — is a ready-made specification of the "affordances" §10 needs for stratification.

## Caution

Crossref carries no abstract for this article, so this card is reconstructed from the reception and from Eraut's 2000 companion piece rather than from any part of the text; verify the typologies and the feedback finding against the article before citing specifics. The empirical base is UK professional and mid-career workplaces studied through interview, and the categories are inductive rather than validated. Eraut is a continuing-education scholar writing for that field; his vocabulary does not map cleanly onto organization-theory usage, and "informal learning" in his sense is broader than §10's "participation."
