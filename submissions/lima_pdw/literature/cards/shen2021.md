## What it argues

Formal audits have blind spots, and critical issues often surface only in everyday use after deployment. Shen and colleagues propose **everyday algorithm auditing**: a process in which users detect, understand and interrogate problematic machine behaviours through their day-to-day interactions with a system. Their argument is that everyday users are powerful at surfacing behaviours that centrally organised audits miss, **regardless of users' knowledge about the underlying algorithms**, and they analyse real-world cases to draw design lessons for platforms and tools that would support such auditing.

## Relation to the argument

Two services and one warning. It is the bridge between the audit literature and the folk-theory literature, and it makes the case that a worker's outside-in inference is not merely a degraded audit but a differently powerful one — she has the position and the volume of ordinary interaction that a sock-puppet study cannot buy. That is a defence §7 can use against the `metaxa2021` comparison.

The warning is the "regardless of users' knowledge" clause, and it is aimed directly at §10. If detection does not depend on knowing how the algorithm works, then what §7 calls asymmetric interpretation may be less about understanding and more about exposure and volume — a worker who does a lot of transactions sees more anomalies. Section 6 already lists task mix and hours worked as live rival explanations, and this paper supplies the mechanism by which they would work.

The everyday-auditing frame is also the collective version of temporal tracking: what individuals surface, a community aggregates into a claim that the system changed or is behaving badly.

## Caution

A conceptual proposal with case analysis rather than a study of a population; the cases are drawn from public episodes of user-surfaced algorithmic harm, which self-select for the visible and the scandalous. Consumer platforms, harm detection rather than rule inference — noticing that a system behaves badly is not the same operation as inferring what it rewards. Read at abstract depth.
