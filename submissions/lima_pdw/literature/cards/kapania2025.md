## What it argues

Nineteen qualitative researchers were interviewed about proposals to replace human participants with LLM-generated synthetic data. Initially sceptical, several were unsettled to see plausible narratives emerge; over successive conversational turns they identified the limits — LLM "participants" foreclose consent and agency, produce responses lacking palpability and contextual depth, and risk delegitimising qualitative methods by making the cheap substitute look adequate.

## Relation to the argument

Cite it to draw a bright line the paper must not be suspected of crossing. This study uses an LLM to *conduct* interviews with real people who consent, review their own transcripts, and set `reviewed_by_human: true` themselves; it does not simulate participants. Stating that distinction with a citation that documents why simulation is unacceptable is cheap insurance, because "AI interviews" will read to some reviewers as "AI-generated data" unless the paper forecloses it.

## Caution

It is about synthetic participants, not AI interviewers, so it is a boundary citation rather than a supporting one — do not let it stand in for evidence about the instrument. Its own evidence is 19 researchers' opinions, which is a study of professional attitudes rather than of data quality.
