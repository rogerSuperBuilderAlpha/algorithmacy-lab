## What it argues

The standard craft text on research interviewing, organised around the seven stages of an interview inquiry — thematizing, designing, interviewing, transcribing, analysing, verifying, reporting — and around a sustained argument that interviewing is a craft with epistemological and ethical commitments rather than a technique for extracting pre-existing opinions. Their two metaphors, the interviewer as miner (digging out buried knowledge) and as traveller (constructing understanding through the journey), frame the epistemological choice.

## Relation to the argument

The reference behind the interview guides in `instruments/`, and the source of the vocabulary a method section needs: semi-structured design, probing, the ethics of the interview relationship, and verification. The miner/traveller distinction is also directly useful for defending the LLM-agent instrument — an agent instructed to introduce no vocabulary of its own and to knock twice on a "no" is built on the traveller premise, and saying so situates the instrument in a tradition rather than in a tool.

## Caution

Edition and author order both shift across printings (Kvale 1996 sole-authored; Kvale & Brinkmann 2009; Brinkmann & Kvale 2015) and mis-citation is common. Nothing in it anticipates an interview conducted by a machine, so it warrants the interview design and not the delivery mechanism.
