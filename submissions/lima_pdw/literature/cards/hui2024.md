## What it argues

A difference-in-differences study of freelancers on a large online labour platform around the releases of ChatGPT, DALL·E 2 and Midjourney. **"Freelancers in highly affected occupations suffer from the introduction of generative AI, experiencing reductions in both employment and earnings,"** with the pattern replicated on the separate release of image models.

The heterogeneity result is the one that matters here, and it is a null in the place every other paper in this cluster finds a coefficient: **"Exploring the heterogeneity by freelancers' employment history, we do not find evidence that high-quality service, measured by their past performance and employment, moderates the adverse effects on employment. In fact, we find suggestive evidence that top freelancers are disproportionately affected by AI."**

## Relation to the argument

The one peer-reviewed study in this cluster where measured prior quality **fails** to explain who is hurt. Everything else here — Brynjolfsson et al., Noy and Zhang, Cui et al., Choi and Schwarcz, Otis et al. — finds a skill moderator and reports its sign. Hui, Reshef and Zhou looked for one on platform earnings and employment, using the platform's own performance history, and did not find it.

That is the closest thing the literature offers to §1's premise, and it is worth stating precisely what it does and does not deliver. It delivers: outcome divergence on a platform, in earnings and work volume, not explained by the workers' recorded prior performance. It does not deliver: a positive account of what does explain it. The paper's own explanation is demand-side — occupational exposure to the models — so the variance it documents is largely *between* occupations, not between two drivers doing the same work. The manuscript can honestly say that where prior skill was tested against platform earnings it did not moderate; it cannot say this paper found within-occupation divergence.

## Caution

Occupation-level exposure is the treatment, so the study identifies across occupations; within-occupation dispersion is not its estimand. "Suggestive evidence" is the authors' own hedge on the top-freelancer result and must be carried over verbatim. Short-term window around 2022–23 releases. Online freelancing, not dispatch-based platform work — no algorithmic assignment of jobs, and the worker chooses and bids. Do not present the null moderator as evidence that skill is irrelevant; it is evidence that this measure of skill did not moderate this effect.
