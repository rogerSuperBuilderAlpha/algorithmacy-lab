## What it argues

Roulston, one of the most-cited methodologists of qualitative interviewing, makes the case for retaining human interviewers and human interviewees against the drift toward automated collection. The argument, as reported, is that an AI interviewer can generate data but not meaning: what the interview relationship produces — the negotiated, situated understanding between two people — is not a by-product of the transcript but the thing the method is for.

## Relation to the argument

The best statement of the objection this design must answer, and the paper is stronger for naming it. The honest reply is not that Roulston is wrong but that the study accepts a specific trade: it gives up the interviewer's in-the-moment interpretive presence in order to remove the instructor from the room, which is the coercion problem §8 and §9 exist to solve. Stating the trade explicitly, with Roulston cited as the cost side and Chopra & Haaland's preference finding as the benefit side, is a defensible position; pretending there is no cost is not.
