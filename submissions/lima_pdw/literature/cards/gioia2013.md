## What it argues

Gioia, Corley and Hamilton codify the systematic inductive approach that carries their name: first-order codes in informant terms ("staying close" to how informants describe their own experience, treating them as "knowledgeable agents"), second-order themes in researcher/theoretical terms, aggregate dimensions, and the data structure diagram (their guiding mantra: "no data structure, know nothing") that displays the whole ladder so a reader can audit the climb from participant language to construct. Their stated purpose is to make interpretive, inductive work legible to reviewers trained on quantitative standards without surrendering its interpretive character, and the origin story (Gioia & Chittipeddi, 1991) is explicitly a response to a skeptical editor who found rich ethnographic prose "unconvincing" absent a demonstrated evidentiary trail.

The article's most consequential move for a paper doing abductive work: the authors describe their *own* process, once literature-cycling begins after initial 1st-order/2nd-order coding, as a shift "from 'inductive' to a form of 'abductive' research, in that data and existing theory are now considered in tandem (Alvesson & Kärreman, 2007)" (p. 21). They call the earlier stage of the process "enforced ignorance" or "willing suspension of belief" about the literature — deliberately delayed, not absent, engagement with theory — precisely to avoid confirmation bias, then explicitly license returning to theory once first- and second-order categories are in hand. They also warn against their own method being adopted as a "cookbook" or "formula," insisting it is a flexible orientation, not a template, and that treating it as one is a live editorial concern (two "moderate concerns" in their own Assessing Others' Use section).

## Relation to the argument

Two corrections to the prior card. First, Gioia et al. do **not** make claims about the categories' origin that are incompatible with abductive analysis — the opposite: their own text names the shift into abduction as the point where literature and data are held "in tandem," which is the same move Timmermans and Tavory (2012) formalize as a cultivated theoretical position (see [[timmermans2012]]). The two are not competing traditions needing reconciliation; Gioia's coding discipline is a *technique* that operates inside an abductive frame, and the paper can say so in one clause rather than leaving the two citations to imply rival epistemologies.

Second, on template use: the most likely reviewer default is still "why is there no data structure diagram?" and the paper needs an answer whether or not it builds one. The honest answer is that the full ladder (1st-order codes → 2nd-order themes → aggregate dimensions → dynamic model) is built for concepts that emerge open-endedly from informant language across a large interview set; this design starts from three operations derived in advance from Paper 1 and is testing them against transcripts, which is a different job. What transfers regardless is the *discipline*: first-cycle coding must stay in the participant's own terms before any facet label is applied, exactly as Gioia's "1st-order, informant-centric" step requires, and the paper can cite that discipline directly rather than gesturing at "in vivo terminology" unattributed. Decline the full data-structure template using the authors' own warning against "cookbook" use (p. 26) rather than only Mees-Buss et al. (2022) — the objection then comes from inside the method's own source rather than from a critique of it, which reads as more secure.

## Caution

Templating is the known pathology, and an OS/OT reviewer is as likely to be tired of the Gioia method as to demand it — cite the discipline, not the full apparatus, and say so explicitly. Do not repeat the prior card's claim that Gioia's method and abductive analysis make incompatible claims about where categories come from; the source itself says otherwise.
