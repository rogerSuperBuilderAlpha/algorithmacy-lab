## What it argues

Gioia, Corley and Hamilton codify the systematic inductive approach that carries their name: first-order codes in informant terms, second-order themes in theoretical terms, aggregate dimensions, and the data structure diagram that displays the whole ladder so a reader can audit the climb from participant language to construct. Their stated purpose is to make interpretive work legible to reviewers trained on quantitative standards without surrendering its interpretive character.

## Relation to the argument

The most likely reviewer default — "why is there no data structure?" — and the paper needs an answer whether or not it adopts the method. The honest answer is that the Gioia ladder is built for constructs emerging from informant language, and this study starts from three derived facets it is trying to expose to refutation; the first-order/second-order distinction is nonetheless exactly the discipline §7 imposes when it requires first-cycle coding in vivo before any facet term is applied. Cite it for that discipline, and cite Mees-Buss et al. (2022) or Pratt et al. (2022) for declining the full template.

## Caution

Templating is the known pathology, and an OS/OT reviewer is as likely to be tired of the Gioia method as to demand it. Do not claim the method while running abductive analysis — the two make incompatible claims about where the categories come from.
