## What it argues

Bucher studies what ordinary users think the Facebook algorithm is doing, and argues that these folk understandings — the "algorithmic imaginary" — are consequential in their own right: they shape how people post, hide, and interpret what they see, and they therefore feed back into the system's behaviour. The imaginary is not a failed approximation of the real algorithm; it is a real object with effects.

## Relation to the argument

RQ1 asks how a working theory of the gate gets built and revised, which is an algorithmic-imaginary question in all but name. Bucher establishes that such theories are worth collecting even when — especially when — they are wrong about the mechanism, which pre-empts the objection that interviewing participants about a gate they cannot see yields only misconceptions. Her attention to affect also bears on RQ4, where what a participant does after an outcome that strikes her as wrong is not a purely cognitive matter.
