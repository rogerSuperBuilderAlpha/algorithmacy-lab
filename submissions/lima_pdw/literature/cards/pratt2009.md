## What it argues

Pratt's premise is that qualitative research has no boilerplate — no standard method paragraph that signals competence the way a regression table does — and that authors and reviewers both suffer for it. He gives practical guidance from the editor's chair: show the data, justify the sampling in terms of the theoretical question, make the analytic process visible, and stop apologising for not being quantitative.

## Relation to the argument

The canonical "how to write this up so an AOM-trained reviewer trusts it" citation, and the one that most directly protects a design switching from a psychometric to a qualitative arm. His insistence that sampling be justified theoretically rather than by size is exactly what §5 of the protocol does with its four strata and its exhausted-unsaturated rule.

## Caution

An editorial, not a study; it prescribes and does not test. Its advice reflects AMJ norms circa 2009, and the field has moved — Pratt, Kaplan & Whittington (2020) is where his own position develops, so citing the 2009 piece alone can date the paper.
