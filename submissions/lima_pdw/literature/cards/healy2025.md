## What it argues

Healy and Pekarek argue that platform gig work is misdescribed when analysed as a worker-platform relation, because a third party — the **consumer** — is constitutive of it. Consumers rate, tip, complain, and cancel, and platforms convert consumer behaviour into managerial consequence for the worker while disclaiming the employer relation that would make them answerable for it. They call the resulting configuration a triangular relationship, drawing on the labour-law literature on triangular employment (agency work, labour hire), and their central claim is that worker vulnerability arises specifically from the triangle: the party with power over the worker's income is not the party with obligations to her, and the party whose judgement bites has no obligations at all.

Their companion work on consumer views of gig work (doi:10.1111/ntwe.12157) supplies the empirical base.

## Relation to the argument

**The paper's closest structural competitor, in its own literature, and it is not cited.** Healy and Pekarek assert precisely §9's structural claim — that the arrangement has three positions, that a two-party analysis is a projection, and that the third position is where the trouble comes from — for platform gig work, in a mainstream work-and-employment journal.

The differences are real and they are the paper's contribution. Their third party is the **consumer**, a human whose judgement is mediated by the system; the paper's third party is the **system**, and its counterpart is the human. So the two accounts have the same three positions with the algorithm and the human in opposite roles, which is worth stating precisely because it shows the triad can be carved two ways and the paper has chosen one. And Healy and Pekarek's explanandum is **vulnerability**, an outcome of the arrangement; the paper's is **variance**, a distribution across workers within it. They ask what the triangle does to workers; the paper asks what workers do in it, and what differs among them.

That is a defensible, complementary position. It is not available if the paper claims nobody has noticed the third position.

## Caution

A conceptual and review article, not an original empirical study; the triangular-employment literature it draws on is legal and its transposition to platform consumers is argued rather than tested. This card sits at the edge of the communication-competence cluster and properly belongs to the platform-work cluster too.
