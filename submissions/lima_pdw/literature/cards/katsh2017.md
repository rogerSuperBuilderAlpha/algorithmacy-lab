## What it argues

Katsh and Rabinovich-Einy update the fourth-party thesis for platform-scale dispute volume. Their argument: networked commerce generates disputes faster than any human process can absorb, so resolution has been automated, and the automation has moved upstream from resolution into **prevention** — systems that detect and pre-empt disputes before parties articulate them. Their case is eBay's resolution centre, which handles tens of millions of disputes a year with almost no human involvement. The book's normative worry is that automated resolution produces outcomes without producing justice: parties get a determination, no reasons, and no route to contest, and the accumulated data belongs to the platform.

They also observe that ODR has migrated from courts into the platforms whose transactions generate the disputes, so the neutral is now owned by one of the interested parties.

## Relation to the argument

**The closest existing description of the paper's arrangement, from a field the paper has not read.** Determinations issued at scale by a system, binding on two parties who do not meet, without reasons, with no contest route, by an entity that is not neutral because it owns the platform — that is §5's withholdings and §7's definition, described in 2017 by dispute-resolution scholars.

It supplies the paper two things it lacks. A **legitimacy vocabulary**: their reasons/contestability/data-ownership triad is a sharper articulation of the accountability condition §7 says no worker can supply individually, and it explains why that condition is structurally unsupplyable rather than merely hard. And a **scale mechanism**: their volume argument explains why platforms automate adjudication in the first place, which the paper currently treats as given.

What the paper still owns: nobody in *Digital Justice* asks what capacity a party needs to do well under such a system. Their remedy is institutional design — better systems, oversight, standards — which is §6's second route. Algorithmacy is about the first. Stating the division that way lets the paper cite the strongest prior description of its own object without conceding the question.

## Caution

Law and dispute resolution; the analysis is institutional and prescriptive, with case illustrations rather than data, and no measurement of anything on the party side. Its "prevention" argument occasionally slides into approving what it describes. Rabinovich-Einy is second author; Susskind contributes to the introduction.
