## What it argues

Bowen addresses head-on the awkwardness of researchers who claim grounded theory and also begin with concepts. He opens by attributing the term itself to Blumer (1954), quoted directly: a definitive concept "refers precisely to what is common to a class of objects... by the aid of a clear definition," while a sensitizing concept "lacks such specification... it gives the user a general sense of reference and guidance in approaching empirical instances," suggesting "directions along which to look" rather than prescribing what to see. Bowen then works the distinction through his own study of community-based antipoverty projects in Jamaica (JSIF), where three literature-derived sensitizing concepts — community/citizen participation, social capital, empowerment — formed the study's conceptual framework going in. The outcome, stated plainly: **one concept (participation) survived into the emergent theory largely intact; the other two (social capital, empowerment) were discarded outright**, though Bowen credits them with sensitizing him to lines of inquiry that produced two different concepts (collaboration, enablement) that did make it into the theory. He is explicit that a sensitizing concept "can be tested, improved, and refined," and that its "ultimate survival... depends on where the data take us; emergent concepts may supplement or displace them altogether" (quoting Padgett, 2004). The trustworthiness apparatus he reports — member checks, negative case analysis, thick description, an audit trail — is drawn from Lincoln and Guba (1985).

## Relation to the argument

This is the applied citation that the Blumer warrant needs, and it is a stronger fit than the prior card credited: Bowen does not merely gesture at concepts surviving or not, he **reports a discard** — two of three sensitizing concepts dropped from the final theory, by name, with the finding stated as evidence of rigor rather than as a design failure. That is the precedent for the paper's own negative-case commitment: a design that specifies its facets in advance and then states plainly if one does not survive contact with the transcripts is following Bowen's own reported practice, not improvising a hedge. Between them, Blumer supplies the warrant (a sensitizing concept is a legitimate starting point, not a bias) and Bowen supplies both the applied precedent and the model for reporting what changed. The paper can cite Blumer (1954) directly for the definition — Bowen quotes it at length rather than paraphrasing — and cite Bowen for the demonstrated practice of discarding.

## Caution

Bowen's own theoretical frame is Glaser-and-Strauss grounded theory (constant comparative method, "grounded" in the classic sense), not Timmermans and Tavory's abductive analysis — he never uses the word "abduction." The paper is citing Bowen for the *applied precedent of concepts that can be discarded*, not for his grounded-theory epistemology; keep the citation narrow to that move and let Timmermans and Tavory (2012) carry the epistemological frame, since their own footnote 7 already routes "sensitizing concepts" through Blumer as compatible with abduction (see [[timmermans2012]]).
