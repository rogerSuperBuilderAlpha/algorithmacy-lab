## What it argues

Habermas borrows Chomsky's term and moves it from grammar to dialogue. Linguistic competence produces well-formed sentences; **communicative competence** produces situations of possible understanding — the speaker's mastery of the dialogue-constitutive universals that let an utterance be embedded in an intersubjective relation at all. He works the argument through its negative case, systematically distorted communication, where the surface of dialogue stays intact while the conditions of genuine understanding are absent and the parties cannot tell. The competence is therefore competence in a *form* of communication, and the form has conditions the arrangement may or may not satisfy. Where they are unsatisfied, no amount of individual skill recovers understanding. The companion paper in the same issue, "On systematically distorted communication" (doi:10.1080/00201747008601590), supplies the pathology this one makes legible.

## Relation to the argument

**This is the closest thing in the communication literature to "a competency a coordination form demands," and the paper should know it exists.** Habermas's move has the same shape as §8's: a competence defined not by what an individual can do in general but by what a particular form of communication requires of a participant, with the form's own conditions as the analytic prior. Claiming novelty for "the competency a form demands" means distinguishing the claim from Habermas, not ignoring him.

The distinction is available and clean. Habermas's competence answers to a *counterfactual ideal* — conditions any communication would have to meet to produce understanding — and is normative and universal. Algorithmacy answers to a *positive withholding* — what this arrangement in fact does not supply — and is descriptive and form-specific. Habermas asks what the form ought to supply and calls the shortfall distortion; §5–§6 ask what the form does not supply and ask what the worker must then furnish privately. Close enough that the difference is worth a paragraph and dangerous to leave unsaid.

## Caution

A programmatic sketch, superseded by the universal-pragmatics papers and by *The Theory of Communicative Action*; a Habermas specialist will not accept the 1970 piece as the mature statement. It is philosophy with no measurement apparatus and cannot be cited as precedent for a scale. Cite it for the *shape* of the claim, and be explicit that algorithmacy declines the counterfactual ideal.
