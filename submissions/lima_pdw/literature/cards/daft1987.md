## What it argues

The empirical follow-up to the 1986 theory. Surveying managers, the authors find that those who match media richness to message equivocality — rich channels for equivocal messages, lean channels for unequivocal ones — are rated higher performers than those who do not. They call the matching capacity **media sensitivity** and argue it is a distinguishing feature of effective managers.

## Relation to the argument

**This is a competence claim in everything but name, and it is the closest thing in the organisational literature to "the competency a mediated arrangement demands."** Media sensitivity is an individual capacity, unevenly distributed among equivalently placed people, that predicts performance under a communication constraint and is acquired through experience rather than instruction. Substitute the terms and it is §8's competency row and §10's stratified fluency, in 1987, about managers and memos.

The paper should treat this as a precedent rather than a rival, because the differences are real and statable. Media sensitivity is a *choosing* capacity — the manager selects among available channels — and it presupposes that channels are available to select among and that their properties are known. The worker in §7 chooses nothing: the mediator is imposed, its properties are undisclosed, and the counterpart is unreachable by any channel. Media sensitivity is competence in a **market of channels**; algorithmacy is competence under a **single unchosen one**. Stating that contrast does more for §8's originality claim than the skill/capability table does, because it engages the nearest neighbour instead of the easiest targets.

## Caution

Cross-sectional manager survey, self-reported media choice, performance ratings by superiors — the causal claim is weak and the sample is 1980s American middle management. Trevino is third author here and first author on the companion *Communication Research* paper (doi:10.1177/009365087014005006), which argues the symbolic rather than rational basis of media choice and partly undercuts this one.
