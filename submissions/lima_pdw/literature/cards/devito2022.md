## What it argues

A grounded-theory interview study with 17 transfeminine TikTok creators. DeVito's framing figure is the door that is also a trap: visibility is the route to recognition and normalisation for trans people, and simultaneously the route to harassment, moderation and harm, so creators whose goals require visibility must navigate doors and traps at once. Folk theorisation is how they do it.

The finding that advances the construct is the functional split in the theories themselves. Creators hold multiple overlapping folk theories of two kinds: **actionable theories**, which guide navigation toward a door worth opening, and **demotivational theories**, which mark a trap too risky to spring. DeVito introduces five novel folk theories spanning both the For You Page and TikTok's content moderation systems, and names two cross-cutting problems — perceived algorithmic paternalism and decontextualisation — as the structural failures creators are theorising around.

## Relation to the argument

This is the paper where an algorithm-competence construct comes closest to *binding determinations*, and the manuscript should use it for exactly that and no more. Content moderation is not curation. When TikTok suppresses or removes a transfeminine creator's video, it commits a determination that the creator did not endorse and cannot appeal, with consequences for reach, income and safety. Folk theories of moderation are therefore theories of a gate, not of a recommender — the closest published analogue to §3's committing intermediary.

The counterpart is still missing, and the gap is instructive. The determination binds one party. Nobody on the other side of the FYP has an interest that the creator must reconstruct and address; the audience is a distribution, not a counterpart. A creator who correctly theorises the moderation system gets her video seen — she does not thereby coordinate with a specific person whose own submission the same system is also adjudicating. Algorithmacy's triad requires that the system's determination be binding *on both parties*, and TikTok's is binding on one.

Two things this source is genuinely good for. The actionable/demotivational distinction is a competence structure — knowing when not to act is part of fluency, which the inbound/outbound framing in §7 does not currently accommodate. And the stratification here is not by demographic but by *what visibility costs you*, which is a better model for §10's stratified-fluency claim than the education-and-age gradients the survey literature reports: fluency concentrates where the stakes concentrate.

## Caution

Seventeen participants, one platform, one community, and a setting where the risks are identity-based rather than contractual. Read at abstract depth only — ACM blocks automated retrieval and I found no author deposit, so the five folk theories, the paternalism and decontextualisation analyses, and any participant counts beyond N = 17 are unverified here. Do not attribute a literacy *definition* to this paper; DeVito states the definition in the 2021 CSCW article (`devito2021.md`) and this one applies rather than revises it.
