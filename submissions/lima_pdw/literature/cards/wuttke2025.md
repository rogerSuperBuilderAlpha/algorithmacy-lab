## What it argues

The first randomised head-to-head the literature has: university students assigned at random to a conversational interview conducted either by an LLM or by a human interviewer, both working from identical questionnaires on political topics. Wuttke and colleagues report that AI conversational interviewing produced data of quality comparable to the human-led condition, with scalability as the added benefit, while flagging that critical implementation questions remain open.

## Relation to the argument

This is the citation the paper actually needs, because it is the only one that compares an LLM interviewer with a *human* interviewer rather than with a survey form. Where a reviewer asks whether the instrument degrades the data, this is the published evidence that it need not. The population — university students discussing a topic on which they hold positions — is also closer to this study's cohort than the economics or HCI alternatives.

## Caution

A workshop paper at a computational-linguistics venue, small-scale and described by its own authors as in-depth rather than powered; "comparable quality" is a null result on a small sample, not a demonstration of equivalence. Political attitudes are not opaque-gate experiences, and nothing in it addresses interviewing people about an institution the interview's commissioner controls.
