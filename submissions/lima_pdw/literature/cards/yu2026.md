## What it argues

Yu, Sang and Han develop a scale for the **digital competence of AI chatbot users** and place it in a nomological net with smart customer experience, trust, and reuse intention. Secondary reporting gives the structure as **four factors across 19 items — utilization of digital tools, search, communication, and digital literacy** — motivated by the argument that existing digital-competence measures are fragmented and insufficiently validated for AI-mediated settings. **The sample, country, reliabilities and fit statistics were not retrievable**, and the four factor names come from a search summary rather than the article.

The *communication* factor is the one to look at, and the one most likely to be misread. In a service-chatbot setting, communication competence means competence at communicating **with the bot** — phrasing a request the system can act on. It is the same object as prompt engineering under a different name, and it terminates at the machine.

What can be said from the title and venue is that this is a consumer-side competence construct — the user is a customer interacting with a service chatbot, and the outcomes are experience, trust and continued use. It belongs to the marketing and information-systems strand of the competence literature rather than the labour strand, and it measures competence in a commercial self-service encounter.

## Relation to the argument

**Neighbour, and worth a single line in a footnote at most.** Its value to the manuscript is as one more data point in the proliferation Gagrčin, Naab and Grub document: another named competence construct for interacting with an algorithmic system, in another discipline, with another outcome set, published in 2026. The manuscript's novelty claim should be framed so that this article's existence is irrelevant to it, and framing the claim on the *position* rather than the name accomplishes that.

The discrimination is straightforward. A customer talking to a service chatbot has no counterpart: the firm is on the other end, the chatbot is the interface, and the interaction terminates in a transaction rather than in a coordination with another person whose understanding matters. A *communication* factor in such a scale measures fluency at addressing the machine, not at reaching a person through it — which is the same collapse the manuscript's §9 diagnoses in prompt engineering, appearing here under the very word the manuscript needs. Say so if the scale is raised: the presence of "communication" in a factor list is not the presence of a counterpart.
