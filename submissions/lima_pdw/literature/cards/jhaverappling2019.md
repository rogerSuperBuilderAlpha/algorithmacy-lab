## What it argues

A survey of 907 Reddit users reflecting on a post removal a few hours after it happened. About a fifth (18%) accepted the removal as appropriate; over a third (37%) did not understand why the post was removed; 29% expressed frustration. The findings that carry: users who had **read community guidelines** or **received explanations** for the removal were more likely to perceive the removal as fair and to post again.

## Relation to the argument

The clean quantitative test of a prediction §6 makes and §10 assumes. Section 6 says the two conditions a worker can supply respond to training and selection, while accountability responds only to forums, designed appeal and statutory review. Jhaver et al. hold a moderation gate constant and vary disclosure — guidelines read, explanation received — and find both move perceived fairness and future participation. That is disclosure improving a *predictability* condition, exactly where §6 predicts institutional supply should land, and it does so without any worker becoming more skilled.

It is also a partial counterexample to §10's implicit-acquisition property in its strongest form. Reading the guidelines is explicit instruction, and it worked. Section 10's defence is available — Reddit's community guidelines are disclosed, so this is not the undisclosed-rule case the property is about — but the defence needs stating, because a reviewer will reach for this study.

## Caution

Reddit volunteers and volunteer moderation, not platform work with income at stake; the gate is human and community-run as much as algorithmic. Self-reported perceptions collected shortly after the event, with obvious affective loading. The correlation between reading guidelines and perceiving fairness is not identified as causal in the abstract. Read at abstract depth.
