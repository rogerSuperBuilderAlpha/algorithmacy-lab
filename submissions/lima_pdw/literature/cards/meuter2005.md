## What it argues

Self-service technologies — telephone banking, automated hotel checkout, online trading — let customers "produce services for themselves without assistance from firm employees." Meuter and colleagues ask why customers try them, in situations where the customer genuinely has a choice of delivery mode and where actual behavior, not stated intention, is observed. Their answer is a set of **consumer readiness** variables: **role clarity, motivation, and ability**. These three mediate between the established adoption predictors (innovation characteristics, individual differences) and the likelihood of trial. Adoption is not explained by the technology's properties directly; it runs through whether the customer knows what he is supposed to do, wants to do it, and can.

## Relation to the argument

The most precisely specified competence-demand construct in the service literature, and the most transferable. Role clarity is the customer-side analogue of the platform worker's central problem — knowing what the arrangement expects of you when nobody states it — and Meuter et al. show it is separable from motivation and from ability, measurable, and consequential for whether the coordination happens at all. If §7 derives three facets of algorithmacy, this trio is a live alternative decomposition that a reviewer may raise, and the paper is better placed to say why its own carving differs.

It also does something for the market column that Berg and Adkins & Ozanne do not: it shows a firm *shifting production work onto the coordinated party* and the shift succeeding or failing on that party's readiness. That is the mechanism behind §4's inversion claim, observed a decade before the platforms, in a market setting.

## Caution

Abstract depth only; the measurement of the three readiness variables and the mediation tests need direct reading. The construct is customer-side in a voluntary-trial setting with a real alternative — which is exactly what the co-optation column removes, and the disanalogy should be stated rather than smoothed over. And the readiness variables are proximate psychological states, not a durable competency; treating them as the market cell's "network competence" would overstate their standing in the field.
