## What it argues

Thematic analysis of influencer discussion in closed Facebook groups, blogs and marketing material, 2017–2018. Cotter names the **visibility game**: an assemblage in which influencers, algorithms and the platform-as-game-master negotiate exposure under rules encoded in code and Terms of Use. Learning the rules is called "research" — reading gurus, discussing in groups, running A/B or "beta" tests, all against a platform that discloses little and updates constantly, which breeds explicit scepticism about whether anyone knows anything. Playing then splits on a belief about what the algorithm can detect. **Relational** influencers think Instagram can discern genuine connection, so they build real relationships. **Simulation** influencers think connectivity can be faked undetectably, so they run engagement pods with rules specifying comment length so the comments "look legit," follow/unfollow to game ratios, and formerly bots. Cotter's analytic point is that knowing the rules does not dictate strategy: influencers play by the rules but not always by their spirit.

## Relation to the argument

The strongest documented case of the two facets dissociating on a belief, which is what §7 needs and currently argues from everyday observation. Relational and simulation influencers face the same gate and hold different theories of what it can read, and the *outbound* practice that follows differs completely while the inbound work — research, testing, group discussion — is common to both. That is a cleaner dissociation than §7's driver example because the population is the same and the difference is located.

The A/B testing is also the closest thing in the cluster to §7's checkability clause. Asymmetric interpretation is "checkable only by being tried again," and Cotter's informants have institutionalised the trying: they run tests, compare notes, and revise. Section 11 could take its behavioural indicators from here.

## Caution

Interpretive analysis of influencers' own discourse in semi-public and closed forums; quotes lightly altered for privacy; population self-selected professionals. Cotter does not verify either belief about detectability, so the material shows the strategic reorganisation is real and internally coherent, not that either theory is right. The "game" is an analytic metaphor with Bourdieu and Galloway behind it. The counterpart is a diffuse audience, not a party whose wants must be reconstructed.
