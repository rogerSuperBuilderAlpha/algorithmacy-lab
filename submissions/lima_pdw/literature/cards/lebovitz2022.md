## What it argues

A field study in a major US hospital where diagnostic radiologists in three departments used AI tools for breast cancer, lung cancer, and bone age determinations. The common experience across all three was **opacity**: the tool's result frequently diverged from the radiologist's initial judgement and offered no reasoning for the divergence, which raised rather than lowered uncertainty. What differed was the response. In only one of the three departments did professionals consistently incorporate the AI results into their final judgements, and the difference lay in whether they engaged with the opacity — interrogating the divergences, comparing the tool's outputs against their own reasoning over many cases, and building a working account of when it could be relied on — rather than avoiding it by dismissing or deferring to the tool.

## Relation to the argument

The closest published study of the exact behaviour §7's asymmetric-interpretation facet names: building a working theory of an opaque system from its outputs alone, because the reasoning is withheld. Lebovitz and colleagues show that the same opacity, faced by equivalently placed professionals in one hospital, produces different responses across groups, which is §10's stratified fluency observed in a professional setting and traced to engagement practices rather than to individual capacity. That is directly usable evidence for a property the paper currently asserts. It also gives §11's first study question a validated shape — the mechanism they document is repeated comparison of the system's output against one's own prior judgement across many cases, which is precisely what an interview about how a working theory of the gate was built and revised should probe for, and what the Lima participant did over four builds. The paper should note the difference too: radiologists have an independent criterion (the eventual diagnosis) against which to calibrate their theory, and a participant at a private-vote gate has only the outcome.

## Caution

A qualitative field study in one hospital, with three departments as the comparative units, so the between-group finding rests on a small number of cases and the mechanism is inferred rather than tested. The professionals are highly credentialed with strong prior domain judgement, which is what makes engagement with divergence possible at all; a novice with no independent judgement cannot interrogate an opaque output the same way, and that is the condition of most of the paper's population. Abstract-level read; the engagement finding is verified from the abstract, and the practices that constitute engagement are described only in the article.
