## What it argues
Wood, Graham, Lehdonvirta and Hjorth study remote gig work across Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa, combining interviews and survey data, and refuse the good-job/bad-job dichotomy. Algorithmic management, they argue, delivers genuine autonomy over task, schedule and location — workers do choose what to take and when — and simultaneously produces low pay, social isolation, overwork and irregular hours. The two are not in tension; they are produced by the same arrangement. Their mechanism is the rating and reputation system, which functions as a form of control precisely because it is *not* experienced as supervision: the score determines access to work, it accumulates from client evaluations the worker cannot contest, and it cannot be moved to another platform, so the worker disciplines herself to protect it. They also document the structural condition that makes it bite — an oversupplied global labour market in which the worker's alternative is not another platform but no work — and they show how that condition converts nominal autonomy into effective compulsion, with workers accepting unfavourable terms and unreasonable clients rather than risk a rating.

## Relation to the argument
This is the paper's evidence base for the withholding analysis in a *global South* population, which matters given that the construct was formed in Trinidad and Tobago. The autonomy/control combination is §4's inversion stated empirically: an arrangement that presents as market-like while charging the worker for what a market supplies free.

Its accountability material is what §5 should take. Ratings accumulate from evaluations the worker cannot contest, and the authors report workers absorbing unjustified negatives because contesting risks the client relationship and produces nothing — a finding that matches Curchod and colleagues in a completely different population and market. Two studies of different platforms on different continents both find skilled workers declining to dispute evaluations they consider wrong. That convergence is the paper's answer to a reviewer who suspects the live interview case is idiosyncratic.

The non-portable reputation appears here too, and it is the third independent documentation of the mechanism that forecloses Hirschman's exit at the same time as voice goes unreceived.

## Caution
Remote freelancing platforms specifically, in two regions, with a survey and interview design that establishes patterns rather than causal effects. The authors' frame is labour process and job quality; competency is not a construct they use, and the self-disciplining they document is read as a control effect rather than as variance across workers. Their structural explanation runs through *oversupply*, which is a market condition the paper's account does not model and which a reviewer could offer as a rival explanation for the outcome variance §1 opens with. That rival deserves naming in §6 alongside prior expertise and task mix.
