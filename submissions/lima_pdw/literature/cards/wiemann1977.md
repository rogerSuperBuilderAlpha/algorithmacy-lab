## What it argues

Wiemann brings Hymes's notion into the communication discipline as a measurable interpersonal construct and gives it the definition the field then used for two decades: communicative competence is the ability to choose among available communicative behaviours so that one's own goals are accomplished **while maintaining the face and line of one's fellow interactants** within the constraints of the situation. Five dimensions carry it — affiliation/support, social relaxation, empathy, behavioural flexibility, and interaction management — and interaction management (turn-taking, topic control, smooth initiation and termination) does most of the empirical work. He builds a 36-item Communicative Competence Scale, tests it against manipulated confederate behaviour, and shows observers' competence judgements track interaction-management skill above the others.

The definition is deliberately Goffmanian: competence is judged by the other party, on the criterion of whether the interaction was managed without damaging either participant's line.

## Relation to the argument

Wiemann is where "competence is other-attributed" becomes an operational commitment rather than a theoretical remark, and that is the load-bearing feature for §9. In his model the second party is not decorative — she is the judge, and the score is her judgement. **That makes the dyad constitutive of the construct rather than an inherited habit**, which is a stronger version of the diagnosis §9 delivers than §9 currently states. When the paper says the mediated-communication constructs "inherit a dyad," Wiemann shows the dyad was not inherited absent-mindedly; it was chosen, because appropriateness has to be adjudicated by somebody and the only available adjudicator was the partner.

That reframing helps the paper. The triad's novelty is not that a third body is present — Wiemann's model would happily accommodate a bystander — but that in the arrangement §7 describes **the adjudicator's seat has been taken by the system**, while the counterpart whose satisfaction the competence is supposedly about has been removed from contact. The paper should say that: the algorithm does not add a third position so much as occupy the position Wiemann reserved for the partner, leaving the partner unmet.

## Caution

The scale is old, observer-rated, and validated on undergraduate dyads in laboratory conversation; its psychometrics have been superseded. Interaction management as Wiemann measures it is turn-taking behaviour, which does not travel to asynchronous platform mediation. Cite for the definition and the other-attribution commitment, not for the instrument.
