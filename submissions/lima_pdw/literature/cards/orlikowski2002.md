## What it argues

Orlikowski studies a geographically dispersed high-tech software organization doing global product development and asks how the work gets done at all. Her answer refuses the two standard framings. Knowing is **not a static embedded capability** and **not a stable disposition of actors**; it is an ongoing social accomplishment, constituted and reconstituted as people engage the world in practice. The competence to do global product development is therefore both collective and distributed, grounded in the everyday practices of organizational members rather than located in any of them. She specifies the practices that enact it — sharing identity, interacting face to face, aligning effort, learning by doing, and supporting participation — and treats them as continuously performed rather than as a resource the firm has acquired.

## Relation to the argument

The most awkward and most valuable card in the cluster, because Orlikowski's sentence is a direct challenge to §8's table. §8 places competency in "the individual in a role" and rejects capability partly because a capability is possessed; Orlikowski shows a *competence* that is collective, distributed, and possessed by nobody, and she uses the word "capability" for it while denying it is embedded or dispositional. If knowing is an ongoing accomplishment rather than a possession, then the possession objection §8 makes to "capability" loses some of its force, and the individual-level claim §8 makes for "competency" needs an argument it does not currently give. The paper's best response is available and should be written: the unit of *measurement* is the person because that is where the variance to be explained appears (§8's first consequence), and that commitment is compatible with the capacity being enacted in practice rather than stored. Orlikowski also supports §11's third study question — a distributed competence predicts that noticing pools across the cohort, and a person-level competency predicts it does not, which is an empirical fork the design can actually observe. ## Caution

An interpretive field study of one firm's distributed development work, with no algorithmic mediation and no opaque evaluator; the setting is collaborative, not co-optative, and the practices she names presuppose the shared identity and face-to-face contact that a platform arrangement withholds. Her use of "capability" is deliberately against the resource-based grain, so quoting it into §8 requires care not to make it look like agreement with Teece. Abstract-level read; the five enabling practices are attested in the reception rather than in the abstract, so verify them before listing them.
