## What it argues

Salomon, Perkins, and Globerson draw a distinction that has organized thirty years of argument about learning with tools. **Effects with** a technology are the upgraded performance a person achieves while working in partnership with the machine — the joint system does more than the person alone. **Effects of** a technology are the cognitive residue that remains when the person works away from the machine, the lasting change in what she can do unaided. The two come apart: a partnership can produce large effects with and no effects of. Both, they argue, depend on the individual's **mindful engagement** in the partnership rather than on the technology's power; a person who offloads without engaging gets performance and no residue. They close by asking whether intelligence and ability are properties of the individual or of the joint person-machine system, and decline to settle it, offering the two views as complementary.

## Relation to the argument

The precise instrument for the interview wrinkle, and the source that turns it from an anecdote into a measurement problem the paper has to solve. The Lima participant's account is a clean statement of effects *with* and an open question about effects *of*: she read ninety percent of the tool's output, spotted things, flagged them, and submitted better work — and she concluded she could not understand the requirements alone or complete successful builds without the assistant. If algorithmacy is measured on a person while she works through an assistant, the instrument records the joint system's competence, and §8's claim that the unit of measurement is the person becomes ambiguous rather than false. That is a real threat to the three-wave scale §11 reports, and naming it is better than waiting for a reviewer to. The **mindful engagement** condition also gives the paper a mechanism for stratification with better provenance than any it currently uses: two participants with the same assistant and the same gate diverge according to how they engage the partnership, which is neither talent nor position, and it matches the participant who read almost everything the tool produced against one who would not.

## Caution

A theoretical article in educational psychology from before the web, with the "intelligent technologies" of the period (writing partners, tutoring systems, spreadsheets) as its cases; the with/of distinction is a conceptual proposal and the empirical literature testing it is mixed, particularly on whether residue reliably appears. Applying it to a large language model is an extension the paper makes. Abstract-level read; the distinction and the mindfulness condition are verified from the abstract. The related distributed-cognition arguments are in Pea (1985), *Beyond amplification*, DOI 10.1207/s15326985ep2004_2, and in Salomon's 1993 edited volume, neither of which this card covers.
