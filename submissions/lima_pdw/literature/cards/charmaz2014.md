## What it argues

Charmaz's constructivist revision of grounded theory abandons the claim that categories emerge from data independent of the analyst. Researcher and participants construct the data together; the analyst's position, prior knowledge and interactions shape what the study can see. The method survives — initial and focused coding, memo-writing, constant comparison, theoretical sampling — but the epistemology changes, and reflexivity becomes part of the procedure rather than a disclaimer attached to it.

## Relation to the argument

The constructivist premise is what makes the study's interview material intelligible as data rather than as contamination. §10 already concedes that interviews collect accounts and that participants know who built the course; Charmaz's position is that co-construction is the condition of interview data everywhere, not a defect of this one. Her coding vocabulary — initial coding, in-vivo codes, focused coding, memos — is also the vocabulary §7 uses.

## Caution

Grounded theory and abductive analysis are not the same programme, and Timmermans and Tavory wrote their 2012 article partly against grounded theory's inductive claim. Do not cite both as though they agreed; cite Charmaz for coding craft and reflexivity, and Timmermans and Tavory for the inferential stance. Editions differ substantially — the 2nd (2014) and the 3rd (2026 Sage reissue lineage) are not interchangeable for page cites.
