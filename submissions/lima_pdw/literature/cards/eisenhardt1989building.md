## What it argues

Eisenhardt sets out a roadmap for building theory inductively from cases: theoretical rather than statistical sampling, multiple data collection methods, within-case analysis before cross-case comparison, iterative tabulation against emerging constructs, and enfolding the resulting propositions in conflicting and similar literature. She is explicit that the researcher should begin with a research question and possibly a priori constructs, though without hypotheses to test.

## Relation to the argument

The a priori constructs clause is the useful part. Eisenhardt licenses starting with specified constructs in an inductive design — which is what the three facets are — provided they remain tentative. Her theoretical-sampling logic also underwrites §5's stratification: sampling on prior experience and gate outcome is a design choice about which comparisons the data must be able to make, not a bid for representativeness.

## Caution

This is a *cases* design and this study is a single site, so the cross-case machinery does not apply; the unit here is the participant account, not the case. Her positivist register also sits awkwardly beside the abductive and sensitizing-concept commitments — pick one epistemology and say so. And disambiguate the 1989 reference in every citation.
