## What it argues

Suchman's argument, first made in 1987 and extended here, is that plans are **resources for action** rather than determinants of it, and that human-machine interaction fails when designers assume the machine can read a user's purpose from her actions. Her expert-help-system study shows the asymmetry precisely: the user can see and interpret the machine's behaviour as meaningful conduct, while the machine has access only to a thin, pre-categorised trace of the user's. Interaction breaks down not because the machine lacks intelligence but because the resources available to each party for making sense of the other are radically unequal. The second edition's new chapters extend this to agents, robots, and the boundary work by which machines are made to look like actors.

## Relation to the argument

**The deepest available theoretical statement of asymmetric interpretation, and the paper does not cite it.** Suchman's asymmetry is exactly §7's: one party interprets richly from a thin trace, the other party's "interpretation" is a categorisation the first party cannot see. Her contribution is the diagnosis of *why* the repair burden lands where it does — the human is the only party capable of the interpretive work, so she does all of it, and the system's failures present to her as her own.

That is the mechanism behind §5's "what co-optation withholds" and behind §8's claim that participation rather than instruction is the acquisition route. Suchman's users learn the machine by running into it, because there is nothing else to learn it from.

Her boundary-work argument in the second edition also bears on §9's ontological question: what a machine *is* in an interaction is achieved rather than given, which is a reason to be careful about a construct that stipulates the system is an adjudicator rather than asking what workers take it to be.

## Caution

Anthropology of technology, not organisation theory or communication science; ethnographic and conversation-analytic evidence from a single 1980s photocopier study, extended by argument. Suchman is hostile to the very move HMC makes — granting machines a communicator position — which makes her useful for the paper's critique and awkward if the paper also wants the triad to have an agent in the third seat. Cite the second edition (2007); Cambridge Core dates the monograph record 2006.
