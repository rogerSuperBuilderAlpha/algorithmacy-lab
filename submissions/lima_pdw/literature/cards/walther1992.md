**Identifier:** doi:10.1177/009365092019001003  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** SAGE Journals

**Evidence basis:** direct_read

## What it argues

Walther dismantles the cues-filtered-out consensus. The reduced-cues theories — social presence, media richness, deindividuation — had predicted that text-based CMC would be impersonal because it strips nonverbal information. Walther argues they mistook a *rate* effect for a *capacity* effect: communicators adapt, encoding relational information into the cues the channel does carry (language choice, timing, message length), and given enough time they develop relationships as personal as face-to-face ones. **Social information processing theory**: the channel constrains the bandwidth of relational communication, not its ceiling.

The argument's engine is a review showing that the studies producing impersonal findings were nearly all one-shot laboratory sessions with no anticipated future interaction.

## Relation to the argument

Walther's adaptation thesis is the strongest available prior statement of the paper's core intuition, and it is the one §9 does not engage. His claim is that people develop, through experience and without instruction, the capacity to convey what a narrow channel was thought unable to carry. That is **intent specification** with a different name and thirty years of evidence, and §10's implicit acquisition is Walther's time-and-experience mechanism.

The difference the paper must state is the counterpart. Walther's adaptation works because the partner is there, adapting in return, and the loop closes: the sender learns what carries because the receiver responds. Remove the counterpart and put an unresponsive adjudicator in the loop, and the learning signal changes from a partner's reply to an outcome with no explanation — which is precisely why **asymmetric interpretation** has to be a separate facet. Walther's worker learns from a person; §7's worker learns from a verdict. That is a clean, defensible difference and it makes §7's two-facet split of common understanding look principled rather than convenient.

## Caution

1992, text-based CMC, and the theory's own empirical base was reviews and reanalysis rather than new experiments. The time-and-experience mechanism assumes a stable channel and a persisting partner, neither of which the paper's arrangement supplies. Do not cite Walther for hyperpersonal claims — that is the 1996 paper.
