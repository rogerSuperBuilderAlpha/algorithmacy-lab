## What it argues

A review written at the point where the construct fractured. Wiemann and Backlund sort the field into two incompatible traditions: a **cognitive** one, descending from Chomsky through Hymes, in which competence is knowledge and is not directly observable; and a **behavioural** one, in which competence is a repertoire of performed skills and is observable by definition. They show the disagreement is not terminological. The cognitive tradition makes competence a capacity that performance may fail to display; the behavioural tradition makes competence identical with successful performance and therefore unable to distinguish a competent person having a bad day from an incompetent one. They come down on the side of holding both — competence as knowledge, judged through performance, with appropriateness and effectiveness as the twin criteria.

## Relation to the argument

The single best short statement of the hazard §8 walks into. Algorithmacy is defined as a capacity ("what a party is able to do") and will be measured through what workers report doing and, in §11, through gate outcomes. Wiemann and Backlund show that when a competence construct is operationalised by its successful performances, the construct collapses into the outcome and explains nothing — the exact failure mode that would make §10's stratified-fluency claim circular, since a worker who fares well would score high by construction.

Useful, too, for the discrimination the paper owes Zhou et al. (2025). Their four dimensions sit on the behavioural side; algorithmacy's derivation from withheld conditions puts it on the cognitive side. That is a principled contrast with a fifty-year pedigree, and it is more defensible than the content comparison §9 currently attempts.

## Caution

A 1980 review of an education-facing literature; its coverage of the intervening forty years is obviously nil, and the two-tradition split has been complicated since (Spitzberg's knowledge/skill/motivation triad is one response). Two Crossref records exist for the same article — prefer the AERA DOI. Backlund is second author.
