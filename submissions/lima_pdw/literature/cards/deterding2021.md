## What it argues

Deterding and Waters propose "flexible coding" as an alternative to line-by-line grounded-theory coding for modern interview projects: index the transcripts broadly first, write analytic memos against theoretical questions, then code intensively only where the memos say the argument lives. The approach assumes — rather than denies — that the researcher came to the field with theoretical interests, and it treats software-assisted indexing as ordinary rather than as a threat to interpretation.

## Relation to the argument

The most practical citation available for the protocol's memo-and-negative-case machinery, and the one that makes the two-wave design tractable: wave-1 transcripts get indexed and memoed, and the intensive coding follows the four research questions. It also gives a defensible answer to the reviewer who assumes any interview study must be full open coding of everything.

## Caution

Written for large interview studies in sociology; a 12–16 participant, two-wave design is at the small end of what it assumes. It is also explicitly *not* grounded theory, so pairing it with Charmaz without comment would invite a consistency question.
