## What it argues

Ziewitz opens with the predicament, stated more precisely than anywhere else in this cluster: "When measures come to matter, those measured find themselves in a precarious situation. On the one hand, they have a strong incentive to respond to measurement so as to score a favourable rating. On the other hand, too much of an adjustment runs the risk of being flagged and penalized by system operators as an attempt to 'game the system'." Measures are held to be most useful when they depict those measured as they usually are and not as they intend to be. From ethnographic fieldwork with UK search-engine-optimisation consultants, he shows that maximising a website's visibility means navigating a shifting boundary between good and bad optimisation, and that the work consultants do is **ethical work** — artfully arranging themselves to cope with moral ambiguities the search engine's operators provoke and delegate. The ethicality of optimisation has itself become a site of governance and contestation.

## Relation to the argument

**The strongest existing account of outbound signalling to an opaque evaluator**, and the one §7 should build the intent-specification facet on. Three reasons it beats the alternatives. It is an occupational study of people who do nothing else, so the practice is visible in its developed form rather than as a sideline of content creation. Its opening formulation is §7's compression problem stated as a structural predicament rather than as a technique: you must adjust to the measure, and adjusting too much is itself punishable, so the competency is a judgement about *how much* adjustment the measure will tolerate — which is more precise than "knowing what can afford to be lost." And the delegation claim identifies where the ambiguity comes from: the operator declines to specify the boundary and leaves the optimiser to work it out, which is common understanding withheld outbound, named as an act of the withholding party.

Ziewitz also gives §7 something it lacks entirely: a vocabulary for the moral texture of the facet. Intent specification is not neutral packaging. It is work that its practitioners must continually justify to themselves and to others, under a label — gaming — supplied by the party that will not say what the rule is.

## Caution

Ethnography of a UK SEO consultancy world; the "counterpart" is a searcher the consultant never meets and whose wants are not reconstructed, so this is outbound only. Search optimisation is a professional service sold to clients, which makes the practitioners more like `bishop2020`'s algorithmic experts than like §7's worker — they optimise on behalf of others and their livelihood does not depend on their own rating. STS framing with commitments about ethics as practical accomplishment. Read at abstract depth; given the weight proposed above, full text should be acquired.
