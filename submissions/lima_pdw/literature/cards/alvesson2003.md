## What it argues

Alvesson names the double bind of researching a setting one already inhabits. Closeness gives access to what an outsider could not reach and simultaneously produces closure — the researcher stops seeing what everyone in the setting takes for granted. His response is not to eliminate closeness but to build in devices that break closure: deliberate estrangement, attention to the moments where one's own reactions are strongest, and a written account of the researcher's position in the setting rather than a claim of detachment.

## Relation to the argument

The best single framing of the author's problem: he is not merely inside the programme, he designed it, so his closure risk is maximal. Alvesson supplies the vocabulary to state that as a methodological condition with known handling rather than as an apology, and the protocol's structural devices — the blind second coder, the pre-committed refutation conditions, the ledger maintained by a third party — are exactly closure-breaking devices.
