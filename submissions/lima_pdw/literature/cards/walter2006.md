## What it argues

Walter, Auer and Ritter carry the network-competence programme out of established manufacturers and into new ventures. Network capability — a firm's ability to develop and use interorganizational relationships — is operationalized in four dimensions: coordination, relational skills, partner knowledge, and internal communication. Across 149 German university spin-offs they find that network capability raises organizational performance, and that it interacts with entrepreneurial orientation: entrepreneurial orientation pays off in competitive advantage, but the relationship between orientation and performance runs through the venture's capacity to build and work its relationships rather than around it.

## Relation to the argument

Two uses. First, it shows the network-competence construct is not a single team's single study: the same research programme, a different population, a different dependent variable, the same result that the capacity varies across parties and that the variance predicts outcomes. That is what §3's falsifiability condition asks of a seventh-row cell — some capacity of the coordinated party must vary with outcomes under the form — and the network cell meets it repeatedly.

Second, the four dimensions are the most individual-facing operationalization in the cluster. "Relational skills" and "partner knowledge" describe reading a counterpart and knowing what he can do, which is close to the language §4 uses for the network cell.
