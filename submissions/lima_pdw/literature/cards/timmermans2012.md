## What it argues

Grounded theory promised novel theory and has mostly not delivered it, and Timmermans and Tavory locate the failure in its commitment to letting categories emerge inductively. Following Peirce, they argue induction cannot yield a new theoretical idea — it only confirms a rule already implicit in the cases sampled — while abduction can, because it starts from a *surprising* fact and asks what would have to be true for it to make sense. They then refuse the opposite error, the researcher who arrives with a framework and simply finds it: abductive analysis works from a *cultivated* theoretical position rather than a blank slate or a fixed lens, built by reading broadly across theories before and during the research so that a finding can register as surprising against more than one candidate account (this is explicitly not "theoretical sensitivity" as Glaser used the term, nor Charmaz's constructivist grounded theory, which the article treats as abduction still subordinated to induction).

They then name three formal methodological steps, each traced to a named source, that discipline the cultivation of surprise: **revisiting** — returning to the same observation repeatedly as it is inscribed (field notes, transcripts, coding, memos), drawing on Jean-Luc Marion's (2002) phenomenology of the "saturated phenomenon," whose relevance changes with each return; **defamiliarization** — inscription itself (the act of writing observation into text) as a technique of estrangement, traced through Russian formalism (Shklovsky) and Berger's "estranging the familiar," so that what was taken for granted becomes a possible focal point; and **alternative casing** — deliberately working a data excerpt against multiple theoretical frameworks in turn ("How do the data look from Abbott's system theory of professions? From Heimer's organizational theory of responsibility? From Suchman's situated action?") to render the phenomenon a case of more than one thing before settling. A footnote (n.7) traces the underlying legitimacy of working from concepts-in-advance to Blumer's (1954) distinction between definitive and *sensitizing* concepts, and the article treats "theoretical coding"/analytical induction — testing and narrowing candidate abductions against negative cases — as the process that weeds out unproductive leads. The worked examples (Vaughan on the *Challenger*, Timmermans on ER resuscitation) show theoretical misfits, not confirmations, doing the generative work.

## Relation to the argument

This is the citation for the paper's methods section. The design's whole procedural apparatus — the three constitutive operations held as sensitizing concepts rather than definitive ones, first-cycle coding open and in the participant's own terms, a record of which candidate readings a transcript ruled out, the negative-case criterion already specified in Table 2 — is an operationalization of "cultivated position that stays breakable." Citing Timmermans and Tavory converts what could read as an unresolved tension (deriving three operations from Paper 1 and then looking for them in the transcripts) into a named and defended analytic stance, and it gives Table 2's pre-committed disconfirming-evidence criteria their epistemological warrant: abduction requires that the surprise be able to arrive. Two of the three named moves map onto design choices already in the manuscript and can be named as such rather than performed unnamed: **alternative casing** is the move behind treating a participant who reports full transparency as evidence *against* the construct rather than as noise, and **revisiting** is the warrant for treating the harness interview's own iterative reframing (asking again from a different angle when a first answer is thin) as an analytic technique, not just better elicitation. The Blumer citation (n.7) is the one to use for "sensitizing concepts" directly, ahead of Bowen (2006), which is the applied precedent rather than the source of the term — see [[bowen2006]].

## Caution

The article is explicit that abduction is *not* Charmaz's constructivist grounded theory with abductive moments added, and not Glaser's "theoretical sensitivity" — it is presented as a genuine break from the inductive premise both retain. A methods section that cites this article for its authority and also leans on Corbin & Strauss or Charmaz for coding vocabulary without noting the tension invites exactly the objection Timmermans and Tavory anticipate from "insiders" of the grounded theory community (p. 181).

## Caution

It is a *Sociological Theory* argument, not a procedure manual — it will not tell a reviewer how the coding was done, and pairing it with a coding-level citation (Deterding & Waters 2021, or Vila-Henninger et al. 2024) is what makes the claim checkable. Also note that abduction licenses prior theory only where the prior theory is genuinely revisable; an insider investigator studying his own construct at his own site has to show revisability rather than assert it.
