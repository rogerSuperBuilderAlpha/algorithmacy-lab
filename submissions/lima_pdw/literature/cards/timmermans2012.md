## What it argues

Grounded theory promised novel theory and has mostly not delivered it, and Timmermans and Tavory locate the failure in its commitment to letting categories emerge inductively. Induction, they argue after Peirce, cannot yield a new theoretical idea; abduction can, because it starts from a surprising observation and asks what would have to be true for it to make sense. They then refuse the opposite error — the researcher who arrives with a framework and finds it. Abductive analysis works from a *cultivated* theoretical position: the analyst reads widely beforehand so that surprise is possible, then applies three formal moves — revisiting, defamiliarization and alternative casing — to keep the data able to break the position.
