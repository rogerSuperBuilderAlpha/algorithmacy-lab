## What it argues

An empirical case study of Uber drivers, theorising **"a new form of sensemaking — algorithm sensemaking"** with three sub-elements: **"(1) focused enactment, (2) selection modes, and (3) retention sources."** The setup is the tension between transparency and opacity: **"algorithms often exhibit limited transparency and are perceived as highly opaque, which impedes workers' understanding of their inner workings. While algorithmic transparency can facilitate sensemaking, the algorithm's opaqueness may aggravate sensemaking."** The **"sophisticated, multistep process of algorithm sensemaking allows platform workers to keep up with algorithmic instructions systematically,"** and the theoretical contribution is to position it as **"a mediator linking workers' perceptions about tensions in their work environment and their behavioral responses."**

## Relation to the argument

The qualitative counterpart to Zhou et al. and, structurally, the more useful of the two for the manuscript's own construct. A mediator between the environment and the response is precisely the shape §6 needs: the withheld condition is constant across workers, the behavioural response varies, and something the worker does sits between them. Möhlmann and colleagues name that something, decompose it into three elements, and locate it in the same population as §1's opening scene.

It is also a source the paper already cites (it is in the accepted abstract's reference list), so promoting it into §1 costs nothing in bibliography.

**Added for the opacity-folk-theories cluster (§7 comparison).** This is the only published *structured* account of the sub-practices of coping with algorithmic opacity that decomposes into exactly three parts, so §7's derivation has to be read against it. The three parts do not match. Focused enactment, selection modes and retention sources are Weick's enactment-selection-retention sequence applied to an opaque manager, and all three are inbound: they describe how a worker converts algorithmic outputs into a working account. There is no outbound element, so nothing in the model corresponds to intent specification; there is no separate change-detection element, since re-theorisation is folded into retention; and there is no position for a condition that admits no private supply. Section 7 partitions by *what a worker can do about a withheld condition*; Mohlmann and colleagues partition by *stage of a sensemaking cycle*. The cuts are orthogonal, and saying so explicitly protects the derivation from the obvious reviewer objection that the three-part structure is borrowed.

The honest limit: sensemaking here is theorised, not measured, and no outcome is attached. The paper shows drivers do different sensemaking work; it does not show that the difference produces different earnings or different exit. §1 cannot borrow an outcome from it.

## Caution

Interpretive case study — no effect sizes, no sample statistics in the abstract, no comparison group and no outcome variable. "Sensemaking" is Weickian enactment theory, a different theoretical apparatus from competency; treating the three sub-elements as facets of algorithmacy would be an over-read and a reviewer familiar with this literature will say so. Uber-specific, single-platform. Do not cite as evidence of outcome variance; cite as evidence that a worker-level interpretive process stands between the algorithm and the response.
