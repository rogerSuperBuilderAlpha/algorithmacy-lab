# Long, D., & Magerko, B. (2020). What is AI literacy? Competencies and design considerations. In *Proceedings of the 2020 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (pp. 1–16). ACM.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1145/3313831.3376727  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** ACM Digital Library
**Source-tier:** conference
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

Long and Magerko synthesise an interdisciplinary literature into a definition — AI literacy is a set of competencies enabling people to critically evaluate AI technologies, communicate and collaborate effectively with AI, and use AI as a tool — and derive **seventeen competencies** organised under five questions (What is AI? What can AI do? How does AI work? How should AI be used? How do people perceive AI?), plus fifteen design considerations for teaching them. The competencies are overwhelmingly epistemic: recognising AI, understanding representations, knowing the steps of machine learning, understanding data's role, appreciating limitations.

## Relation to the argument

§9's "object of knowledge" row, and the characterisation is accurate. Two of the seventeen competencies — *interdisciplinarity* and *imagining future AI* — reach past pure knowledge, and one, "critically interpret data," is close to §7's asymmetric interpretation in form; but every one of them is about the worker's relation to the technology. **No competency in the list mentions another person.** That is a checkable, itemised demonstration of §9's "drop the counterpart" charge, and stating it as a count is stronger than stating it as a characterisation.

The derivation contrast also matters. Long and Magerko's seventeen come from a literature synthesis — what other people have said AI literacy involves — with no rule that would exclude an eighteenth. §7's admission rule would exclude most of them, and §9's claim to derive rather than collect has its clearest illustration here.

Worth noting for §11: the paper's cohorts are teaching contexts, and AI literacy is the construct a teaching context is most likely to be measuring by default. If GauntleTT's curriculum teaches AI literacy and the instrument measures algorithmacy, the discriminant validity question is not abstract — it is a design constraint.

## Caution

A conceptual synthesis with no instrument, no validation, and no empirical test; the seventeen competencies are proposals. Its audience is educators and interface designers. The construct has proliferated measures since 2020 (self-report AI literacy scales, most of them weak) and the paper should check whether any of them has been used on platform workers before claiming the space.
