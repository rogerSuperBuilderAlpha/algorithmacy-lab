## What it argues

Mahy and Li built the closest thing this literature has to a *human-AI collaboration* measure, and the name is the reason to check it. Two scales come out of the study: **Human-AI Collaboration Dynamics (HACD)** and **Generative AI-Research Augmentation (GAI-RA)**. Respondents were university staff, doctoral students, master's students and research-active undergraduates at sixteen English Medium Instruction universities in Mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau. Exploratory factor analysis on one subsample (N = 344) produced the dimensional structure; confirmatory analysis followed on two independent subsamples (N = 344 and N = 447).

HACD is not one scale but four nested versions, running from a core two-factor form to an expanded five-factor form — the authors call it a flexible measurement toolkit, which is another way of saying the factor structure did not settle. GAI-RA is a single-factor, five-item measure of how far generative AI augments research work. The study's reflexive twist is that the authors used GenAI within the scale development process itself and treat that as a demonstration that GenAI can be folded into psychometrically rigorous instrument construction.

## Relation to the argument

Neighbour, and the card that closes off the most obvious objection to the paper's novelty claim: *surely somebody has measured human-AI collaboration competence.* Somebody has. It does not contain a human counterpart either.

"Collaboration" here means one researcher working with a generative model. The dynamics being measured are dynamics between a person and a tool she prompts: how she divides labour with it, how she trusts and checks it, how far it augments her output. Nothing in either scale concerns a second person whom the AI is simultaneously interpreting, and nothing concerns a determination the system commits that binds two parties. The sample makes this structural rather than accidental — an academic writing a paper with ChatGPT has no counterparty in the loop. She has a manuscript. The word "collaboration" has been fully absorbed by the dyad, which is exactly what the paper's §9 sorting predicts for the human-machine communication tradition: the algorithm becomes a **partner**, and a partner is a second position, not a third.

The site difference matters too. HACD measures a chosen, voluntary, exit-able relation with a tool the researcher selected and can abandon. Algorithmacy's relation is unchosen, binding, and exit-less; the intermediary is not a collaborator but an adjudicator. Where HACD's respondent asks "is this output useful to me," the coordinated party asks "what did it just decide about me and the person on the other end." Those are different questions and no rotation of HACD's five factors turns one into the other.

One caution the manuscript should take seriously from this paper rather than use against it: four nested versions of a scale, from two factors to five, is what an unsettled construct looks like when it is measured before it is defined. If algorithmacy goes to instrument, the derivation-first commitment is what should keep its facet structure from doing this — and the paper should say so, because a reviewer will ask what stops algorithmacy becoming another flexible toolkit.

## Caution

Advance online publication, February 2026, no volume or issue yet; Crossref gives pages 1–47, which is a preprint pagination and will change on issue assignment. Read at abstract depth only, and this one needs more than most: the factor names for the four HACD versions are not in the abstract, and my claim that no factor contains a human counterpart rests on the sample and construct description rather than on item inspection. **Check the item list before citing this card's conclusion in the manuscript.** Sixteen EMI universities in one region, all respondents academic; nothing about work outside research. The authors' use of GenAI inside their own validation procedure is novel and unreplicated, and it is a reason for caution about the psychometrics rather than a credential. Do not treat HACD as an established instrument — it is weeks old at time of carding.
