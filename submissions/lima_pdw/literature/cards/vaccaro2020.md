## What it argues

A large-scale online experiment testing whether different forms of appeal improve users' experience of automated content-moderation decisions, measured on perceptions of Fairness, Accountability, Trustworthiness and feelings of Control (FACT). The result is a null and the authors call it surprising: **none of the appeal designs improved FACT perceptions relative to a no-appeal baseline.** The qualitative half shows why. When users write appeals they contest the decision, but they also contest more fundamental things — the goal of moderating content at all, the idea of automation, and the inconsistency of the system as a whole.

## Relation to the argument

The most important single source in this cluster for §5 and §6, and it is not currently in the manuscript. Section 5's hinge is that accountability is a relation between parties, that no private work manufactures a forum, and that a worker can get better at predicting how an appeal will be received but cannot cause it to be received. Vaccaro, Sandvig and Karahalios put the claim to an experiment from the other direction — they hold the worker constant and vary the *appeal mechanism* — and find that adding an appeal channel does not move perceived accountability. That is a designed appeal failing to supply the condition, which is not what §6 predicts. Section 6 says forums, statutory review and designed appeal operate on the condition a worker cannot supply and should move accountability; the closest experimental test says a designed appeal did not.

The finding cuts two ways and the manuscript should say which it takes. Read charitably for the paper, it supports the deeper claim: what users wanted was not a channel but standing, and a channel that cannot compel an answer is not a forum, so the null shows that appeal-shaped affordances do not equal answerability. Read uncharitably, it is the check §6 offers on itself — "if a well-designed appeal process turned out to improve workers' ability to read what a gate wants, or if a training programme turned out to produce answerability, the division is drawn in the wrong place" — arriving as a third possibility the section did not anticipate: an appeal process that produces neither. Either way, §6 cannot leave designed appeal on the institutional side of the division without engaging this result.

The qualitative finding is the second gift. Users appealing a determination contested the premise of the system, not only the decision. That is what §5 means when it says a worker who becomes expert at rendering account under a schema she did not set has become expert at absorbing its absence.

## Caution

An online experiment with a hypothetical or simulated moderation scenario rather than consequences borne by real accounts; the abstract does not report the sample or whether participants had genuine stakes, and stakes are the variable most likely to move the null. Consumer content moderation, not work. FACT is the authors' own measurement construct. Read at abstract depth — given the weight this source can bear on §6, it should be read in full before the manuscript relies on it, and the design details checked closely.
