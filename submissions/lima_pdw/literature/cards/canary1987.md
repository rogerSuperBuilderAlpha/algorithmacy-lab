# Canary, D. J., & Spitzberg, B. H. (1987). Appropriateness and effectiveness perceptions of conflict strategies. *Human Communication Research*, 14(1), 93–120.

**Identifier:** doi:10.1111/j.1468-2958.1987.tb00123.x  ·  **Read depth:** full_text  ·  **Source read:** Oxford Academic
**Source-tier:** primary_journal
**Evidence basis:** direct_read
**Cluster:** communication-competence

## What it argues

The empirical test that turned appropriateness and effectiveness from a definition into a measurement model. Canary and Spitzberg examine how conflict strategies — integrative, distributive, avoidant — are perceived on the two criteria, and find the criteria dissociate: a strategy can be effective and inappropriate, appropriate and ineffective. Integrative strategies score well on both; distributive strategies buy effectiveness at the cost of appropriateness; avoidance loses on both. Their 1989 follow-up (doi:10.1111/j.1468-2958.1989.tb00202.x) builds the perceived-competence model on this base, and the 1990 paper shows attribution biases systematically skew whose strategies get judged competent.

## Relation to the argument

Two uses. First, the dissociation of the criteria is a template for §7's separation of asymmetric interpretation from intent specification — the paper's argument that the two facets do not travel together is the same kind of claim, and here is a precedent showing the communication literature accepts such claims when the dissociation is demonstrated rather than asserted. §7 currently rests the separation on "the everyday observation," which is weaker than it needs to be given §11 could test it.

Second, the attribution-bias finding in the 1990 companion is a live threat to §10. If competence judgements are systematically skewed by who is judging and what they already believe about the actor, then a platform's rating apparatus — the paper's de facto measure of coordination success — carries the same bias, and stratified fluency could reflect a distribution of attributions rather than of capacity. That is a rival explanation the design in §11 has to address.

## Caution

Undergraduate samples, hypothetical and recalled conflict episodes, mid-1980s scenario methodology. The finding travels as a structural point about criterion dissociation, not as an effect size. Canary is first author.
