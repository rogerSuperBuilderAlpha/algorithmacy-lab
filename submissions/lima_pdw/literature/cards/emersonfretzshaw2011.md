## What it argues

Emerson, Fretz and Shaw treat fieldnote writing as the analytic act it is rather than as transcription that precedes analysis. They work through jottings made in the setting, the discipline of writing full notes soon after, the difference between describing what happened and recording what the fieldworker made of it, and the progression from notes through open coding and memos to a written account — with sustained attention to how a note's framing forecloses later readings.

## Relation to the argument

This is the citation for §6.2's two-column rule. Separating observation from inference on the page, mechanically, is a fieldnote convention with a literature behind it, and the protocol's own justification — that the inference column is where a sensitizing concept becomes a coding frame without anyone deciding to let it — is a compressed statement of these authors' central worry. The four-hour writing window is also their recommendation in substance.

## Caution

The book assumes participant observation across a long immersion; this study observes review sessions non-participantly and collects most material by interview, so the fieldnote apparatus applies to a smaller part of the corpus than the citation implies.
