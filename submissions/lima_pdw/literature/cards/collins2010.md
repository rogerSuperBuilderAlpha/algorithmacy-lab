## What it argues

Collins argues that "tacit knowledge" has been used as a single word for three unrelated things, and separates them. **Relational tacit knowledge** is tacit for contingent social reasons — nobody bothered to write it down, it is kept secret, it would take too long to explain — and it could in principle be made explicit. **Somatic tacit knowledge** is tacit because it is held in the body and its limits are limits of human physiology; a machine could in principle do it, and bicycle-riding belongs here. **Collective tacit knowledge** is the hard case: knowledge that resides in society rather than in any individual, acquired only through social immersion, and not reducible to rules or to bodies. Language use, knowing how much to tip, and driving in traffic are his examples, and his claim is that only this third kind is *strictly* tacit. He also inverts the usual framing by insisting that "explicit" is the concept needing analysis, and distinguishes several senses of making something explicit that are routinely run together.

## Relation to the argument

The most precise instrument in this cluster for §10, because it lets the paper say exactly which kind of tacitness it is claiming and thereby survive the obvious objection. A reviewer will say that whatever the gate rewards could be written down, and on Collins's scheme that is a claim that the knowledge is merely *relational* — withheld, not unstatable. §8's structural argument then has to be that the platform's non-disclosure makes the knowledge relationally tacit by design and permanently, which is a weaker and more defensible claim than unstatability, and one the paper can actually hold. Meanwhile the competency itself — reading what a gate wants, addressing an audience one cannot watch — is closer to collective tacit knowledge, acquired by immersion in a form of coordination. Separating the two answers a question §10 leaves open: what is tacit is the rule (relationally, by the platform's choice) and the competency for operating without it (collectively, by immersion). Collins gives the paper that sentence.

## Caution

A philosophical taxonomy from sociology of science, argued through everyday examples and the author's own fieldwork on gravitational-wave physics, not an organizational study; it establishes distinctions rather than findings. Collins's position is contested — his insistence that somatic tacit knowledge is in principle explicable puts him against Dreyfus and against most of the embodiment literature — so adopting the taxonomy takes a side. Metadata-level read from the publisher record; the three-way scheme is well attested in the reception, but verify the definitions against the book before quoting, and do not attribute the relational/somatic/collective labels to Polanyi.
