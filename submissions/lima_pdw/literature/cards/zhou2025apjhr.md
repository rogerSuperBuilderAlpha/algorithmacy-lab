## What it argues

Zhou and colleagues develop and validate what they call **the first scale of algorithmic competency (AC)**, across five samples of Chinese on-demand platform workers — ride-hailing drivers and food-delivery couriers. The construct has four dimensions. *Understanding AM* is a sophisticated grasp of how algorithmic management works and of its dynamic nature. *Embracing AM* is a willingness to trust the efficiency and accuracy of the system in matching workers to customers. *Leveraging AM* is the capacity to use algorithmic management strategically to the worker's own advantage. *Remediating AM* is the capacity to repair or supplement the system's deficiencies. The development sequence runs 99 semi-structured interviews (Sample 1) to 14 items, then expert content validation, then EFA at N = 275 (Sample 2, two items dropped), CFA at N = 213 (Sample 3, second-order four-factor model preferred), validity at N = 230 (Sample 4), and a three-wave antecedents-and-outcomes model at N = 225 (Sample 5). Twelve items survive, three per dimension; overall alpha = .85, dimension alphas .77 to .83.

The nomological net is the paper's second contribution. Peer social support and cognitive job crafting raise AC. AC in turn explains incremental variance in customer-oriented service behaviour and in identification with gig work, beyond related constructs. Their own discriminant work is unusually careful: AC correlates r = .37 with digital competence — and only r = .04, non-significant, with *Embracing AM*, which they attribute to digital-competence measures omitting attitude; r = .54 with competence need satisfaction, their highest and a convergent result; and r = .17 with perceived algorithmic management, a discriminant result they predicted. Dimension intercorrelations run .22 to .51, with Embracing the weakest-linked at .22 to .25.

The items themselves are the thing to read. Only the *Understanding* triad is knowledge-like ("I am familiar with the platform's AM"). *Leveraging* is strategic conduct toward the system ("I proactively explore AM rules to obtain more high-quality orders"). *Remediating* is repair ("I can supplement AM's shortcomings — imprecise navigation — through the help of WeChat groups or other tools"). *Embracing* is an attitude ("I think the platform AM is highly efficient, such as in customers-workers matching"). Nothing here is a technical-knowledge test.

## Relation to the argument

**Rival, and the most dangerous one in the cluster.** It occupies the name, the level of analysis, the population and the psychometric ground at once: an individual-level competency construct about algorithmic management, validated on platform workers, published in a peer-reviewed HRM journal a year before Lima. Any dismissal that calls it "technical knowledge" dies on contact with the twelve items — three of four dimensions are not knowledge at all. The manuscript's §9 line that AI literacy and algorithmic competency "measure what a worker understands about how such systems work" is accurate for Long and Magerko and wrong for Zhou et al., and the two should stop sharing a sentence.

The discrimination that survives runs on a single axis, and it has to be stated precisely because two of the twelve items do mention customers. **No item measures a capacity to coordinate with the human counterpart.** Item 8, under *Leveraging*, reads "I proactively explore AM rules to minimize negative customer feedback" — the customer there is a rating source feeding the algorithm, an output to be minimised, not a party being coordinated with. Item 11, under *Embracing*, reads "I think the platform AM is highly efficient, such as in customers-workers matching" — which names the matching function, the triad's actual mechanism, inside a validated algorithm-competence instrument. **Quote item 11 in §9 and pre-empt it**, because a reviewer who finds it first will present it as the counterpart appearing in the rival scale. It is not. Item 11 measures a *belief about the platform's efficiency*, an attitudinal item in the dimension that correlates weakest with the rest (.22 to .25); it does not measure any capacity of the worker toward the person on the other side of the match. The authors' own definition settles it: AC is a worker's "understanding of platform algorithms that assign and evaluate their work and their ability to adapt to and navigate those algorithms." No counterpart. The counterpart enters their study only as an *outcome variable* — customer-oriented service behaviour, measured with Peccei and Rosenthal's six-item scale — and that is the sharpest discrimination available: in Zhou et al.'s own model the counterpart matters empirically and still sits outside the construct. Zhou et al. measure a worker's competence *at* an apparatus; algorithmacy is defined on the worker-counterpart-through-system axis, and the triad is exactly what their scale leaves out. That is the manuscript's own structural claim, not one manufactured for the rebuttal. Three further discriminations back it up. Their site fixes their content — ride-hailing and food delivery under a dispatch-and-scoring regime, hence hot spots, appeals and WeChat groups — so the items do not transfer to a generative-AI knowledge-work cohort even if the construct does. Their *Embracing* dimension has no counterpart in algorithmacy, and algorithmacy's *implicit acquisition* and *stratified fluency* have none in theirs; their antecedents are social support and job crafting, which is a different developmental story. And their AC is inventoried from 99 interviews, where algorithmacy's facets are derived from what the form withholds.

Predict the correlations in advance. Their *Leveraging* should correlate moderately with algorithmacy's intent specification, their *Understanding* with asymmetric interpretation. Saying so before the roundtable is worth more than being shown it at the roundtable. The twelve items are already fielded as W2 Part 8 of the Hult cohort panel (test H4); the same block belongs in the GauntleTT panel the Lima paper promises.

## Caution

Five Chinese samples in one national on-demand context; the authors close by flagging generalizability limits from China's distinctive platform economy. Self-report throughout, with no platform-side behavioural criterion. The scale is fresh — published February 2025, still lightly cited — so its measurement properties have not been replicated independently, and no psychometric critique is yet available to lean on. The items were transcribed from the working-paper version; check them against the journal version before quoting them as final, since the working paper predates publication. Crossref returns volume 63, issue 2 and **no page range** for this article — do not invent one. Never cite this bare as "Zhou et al. 2025": an overlapping team published a different 2025 paper (Zhou, Lei, Cooke, Huang & Zhang, *Human Resource Management* 64(6), 1687–1707, doi:10.1002/hrm.70009) and the two are routinely confused.

**Independently re-confirms the "primary antecedent" FLAG.** Table 6, Step 2 of the hierarchical
regression: social support from peers b=.23 (p<.001); cognitive job crafting b=.41 (p<.001). Job
crafting is the numerically larger predictor, and it is explicitly defined by the authors as "active
cognitive changes individuals make to their jobs" — not a social mechanism. `PAPER.md` line 125's
"the primary organizational antecedents... are social mechanisms — informal peer support systems"
still overstates peer support relative to the paper's own reported coefficients. One nuance worth
noting: Zhou et al.'s own abstract and Discussion section consistently name social support from peers
*first*, ahead of cognitive job crafting, in their own prose (e.g., abstract: "AC is positively
influenced by social support from peers and cognitive job crafting") — so there is a genuine tension
between the authors' own narrative ordering (peer support first) and their own effect sizes (job
crafting larger). This is a defensible ambiguity in the source itself, not just a manuscript overclaim;
still, "the primary... antecedents... are social mechanisms" is the stronger of the two readings and
is not the one the coefficients themselves would lead with. Remains an open FLAG for the author's
judgment, not a clear-cut fix.
