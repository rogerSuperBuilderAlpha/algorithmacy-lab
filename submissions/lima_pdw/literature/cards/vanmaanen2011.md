## What it argues

Van Maanen sets out what ethnography, treated as work rather than as a stance, actually obliges: prolonged presence, the willingness to be surprised, the labour of turning fieldnotes into an argument, and a form of writing that keeps the fieldwork visible in the text. He addresses a management-school readership directly and is candid about what ethnography costs and what it will not deliver.

## Relation to the argument

More citable in an OS/OT paper than *Tales of the Field* because it is a journal article in a management venue, and it carries most of the same freight. It also justifies the design's most expensive commitment — two waves plus observation plus repository records over a sixteen-week course — as the prolonged engagement the method requires rather than as thoroughness for its own sake.
