## What it argues

Nieborg and Poell theorise **platformization** in cultural production and its product, the **contingent cultural commodity**: cultural goods that are modular, continually reworked, and dependent on platform infrastructures, markets and governance frameworks whose rules change. The commodity is contingent because what it is and whether it reaches anyone depends on conditions the producer does not set and cannot fix.

## Relation to the argument

The structural explanation for why temporal tracking is a standing requirement rather than an occasional one. If the good itself is contingent on infrastructures that keep changing, then no reconstruction of what the gate wants stays valid, and the worker's task is not to learn a rule but to keep re-learning it. Section 7 says the rule moves and nobody announces it; Nieborg and Poell say why the moving is intrinsic to the arrangement rather than a policy the platform could choose to stop.

The account also names the loss that intent specification incurs at the level of the product rather than the message. A modular good reworked for a changing platform is a different good, which is a stronger version of §7's compression claim.
