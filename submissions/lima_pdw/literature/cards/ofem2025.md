## What it argues

Ofem builds what the article calls the first **competence-paradox model** for AI-mediated work, on the observation that algorithmic dashboards promise sharper performance and routinely erode autonomy and trust — a contradiction goal-setting theory cannot accommodate. The method is a PRISMA-ScR scoping review across Scopus, Web of Science and PsycINFO (n = 82) followed by qualitative meta-synthesis, read through paradox theory.

Four mutually reinforcing tensions emerge: metric specificity versus discretion, cadence versus psychological safety, optimisation versus learning depth, and transparency-plus-voice versus trust. The article reports thresholds at which the tensions bite — prompts refreshing every five minutes, more than thirty per hour, or twenty optimisation nudges per shift — with autonomy dropping 0.40 SD, psychological safety 0.50 SD, and exploration 15% at those points. A five-lever HRD sequence follows: goal-calibration, hybrid coaching, explainable dashboards, rotational upskilling, and moderated voice forums.

## Relation to the argument

**Neighbour, and a warning about the word.** The title uses *competence* for a paradox in how algorithmic goal-setting interacts with worker capacity, not for a named individual construct — no scale, no dimensions, no measurement of a person. It nonetheless occupies the phrase "competence" beside "algorithmic management" in an HRD journal a year before Lima, and a keyword search will surface it.

Its substantive use to the manuscript is in §8 and §10. The fourth tension — *transparency and voice versus trust* — is a finding that disclosure does not straightforwardly repair what algorithmic direction withholds, which is friendly to §6's claim that one integrating condition admits no private supply and complicates any easy "just make it transparent" reply. And the paradox framing supports the manuscript's insistence that rising competence is not automatically a gain: on this account the very metrics that call forth competence are the ones that erode discretion.

The discrimination is straightforward and does not need arguing at length: the counterpart is absent, the unit of analysis is the work design rather than the person, and the levers are managerial interventions rather than a competency a coordination form demands.

## Caution

A scoping review and meta-synthesis of 82 studies, not primary research: **the effect sizes quoted (0.40 SD, 0.50 SD, 15%) and the thresholds (five minutes, thirty per hour, twenty nudges) are synthesised across a heterogeneous literature and must not be cited as measurements.** Abstract-depth read; the four tensions and five levers are named in the abstract and can be reported, but the review's inclusion criteria and the derivation of the thresholds were not checked. **Verify the author's given name before citing** — only the family name was confirmed.
