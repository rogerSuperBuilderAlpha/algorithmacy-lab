## What it argues

An annual corporate survey: **31,000 full-time employed or self-employed knowledge workers across 31 countries**, fielded 6 February to 24 March 2025, supplemented by Microsoft 365 telemetry and LinkedIn labour data. The report's thesis is the arrival of the "Frontier Firm," an organisation built around human–agent teams.

The variation it documents is between organisational strata, not between equivalently positioned workers: **"71% of Frontier Firm leaders say their company is thriving, compared to just 39% of workers globally"**; leaders report far higher familiarity with AI agents than employees (67% vs 40%); **"79% of leaders believe AI will accelerate their careers versus 67% of employees."**

## Relation to the argument

Its only defensible use in this manuscript is as a scale marker — evidence that the phenomenon under discussion is widespread and that practitioners are organising around it — and as a vivid instance of the perception gap between those who deploy algorithmic systems and those who work under them. That gap is thematically close to what §5 says co-optation withholds, and a single sentence citing it as industry self-report would be honest.

Everything else is off limits. The report measures no outcomes: no earnings, no attrition, no productivity independent of self-assessment. It cannot establish that outcomes vary among workers on the same system, and it cannot establish that the variance is unexplained, because it never estimates anything.

## Caution

**Grey literature produced by a vendor with a direct commercial stake in the conclusion that AI agents transform work.** No sampling frame, no response rate, no instrument, no analysis code, no peer review; the "Frontier Firm" is a marketing construct, not an analytic category. The leader/worker comparisons are between different populations answering differently framed questions. Percentages here are descriptive frequencies, not estimates. If the abstract leaned on this for an empirical claim, that claim must be re-sourced or dropped — a reviewer at an *Organization Studies* PDW will treat a Microsoft survey cited as evidence of a labour-market fact as a serious lapse.
