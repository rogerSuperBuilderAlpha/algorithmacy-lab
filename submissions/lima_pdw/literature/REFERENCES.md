## The four flags

**⚠ Stark & Vanden Broeck 2024 — the abstract over-reads them, but not by much.** Their abstract does
run the parallel the manuscript wants, in these words: *"Whereas actors in hierarchies command, in
markets they contract, and in networks collaborate, on platforms they are co-opted. The co-optation
of actors, assets, and activities is undertaken by algorithmic management."* So the fourfold series
is theirs, not an invention. But the fourth term in their series is **platforms** — an organizational
form — and what they set out to theorize is *"a style of management organized around algorithmic
practices,"* compared against scientific and collaborative management. They never call co-optation a
coordination mechanism, and they do not position it against Powell. The abstract's "stands as a
fourth coordination mechanism alongside hierarchies, markets, and networks" therefore asserts
something its own source does not, in a venue whose editors published that source. **This softens
`../DEPARTURES.md` row 1: the fix is precision, not retreat.** Say platforms are the fourth term and
co-optation is what happens to actors on them, cite Stark & Vanden Broeck for exactly that, and the
lab's H5 reframe and the abstract stop contradicting each other.

**⚠ Zhou et al. 2025 is a rival scale, not a passing mention.** It develops and validates what it
calls **the first scale of algorithmic competency**, across five samples of Chinese on-demand
platform workers, on four dimensions — understanding, embracing, leveraging, and remediating
algorithmic management. The abstract disposes of it in half of one sentence ("measure technical
knowledge rather than the competency to coordinate through the system"). That will not hold: the
four dimensions are not technical knowledge, and "leveraging" and "remediating" are close to what
algorithmacy's components claim. **The manuscript owes a real discriminant argument and, if the data
permits, an empirical discriminant test.** This is the single most exposed point in the abstract.
All twelve items, their loadings, and the discrimination the items actually support are in
[`ZHOU_2025_INSTRUMENT.md`](ZHOU_2025_INSTRUMENT.md) — the short version is that **not one of their
twelve items mentions the human counterpart**, which is the axis algorithmacy is defined on.

**⚠ Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond 2025 and Dell'Acqua et al. 2026 — check the direction of the
variance.** Both are cited to establish that *equivalently positioned* workers diverge. Brynjolfsson
et al. report a ~15% average productivity gain across 5,172 support agents **with heterogeneity that
tracks skill and tenure** — novice and low-skilled workers gain most. That is divergence *by
position*, which is the explanation the abstract's opening sentence needs to rule out, not evidence
for it. Dell'Acqua et al. report a jagged frontier where the same worker does better inside it and
worse outside it — divergence *by task*. Neither is fatal, and both remain the right literature to
engage; the variance puzzle simply has to be stated as what remains **after** skill and task are
accounted for. Read both directly before writing §1.

**⚠ Bedard et al. 2026 and Microsoft 2025 are not peer-reviewed.** An HBR piece by six BCG-affiliated
authors and a corporate research report. Both are legitimate as evidence that practitioners observe
the phenomenon; neither carries weight with an OS/OT roundtable as a finding. Use them for motivation,
never as the empirical warrant, and make sure at least one peer-reviewed source stands behind every
claim they support.

## Not in the abstract but owed

The abstract's argument leans on work it does not cite, and a roundtable will supply the names:

- **Powell 1990** on markets, hierarchies, and networks as *logics* — unavoidable once the
  fourth-term claim is made precise.
- **Kellogg, Valentine, & Christianson 2020** and **Vallas & Schor 2020** — the two answers the lab's
  own H5 reframe requires. Note that Kellogg is a co-author on Dell'Acqua et al. 2026 and is cited
  inside Stark & Vanden Broeck; she is unavoidable from three directions.
- **Rahman 2021** and **Cameron 2024** — divergent worker outcomes under opaque evaluation. Both were
  in the Dublin package and both are closer to the variance puzzle than the AI-productivity sources.
- **Dogruel algorithm literacy** and **Spreitzer psychological empowerment** — already discriminated
  in `../../../org_frontier/survey/cohort_algorithmacy/`; that work transfers.
