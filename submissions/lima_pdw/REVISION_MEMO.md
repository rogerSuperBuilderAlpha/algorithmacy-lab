# Revision memo — what the literature sweep found

*18 August 2026. Eight research agents, ~350 cards at [`literature/cards/`](literature/cards/), every
identifier Crossref-checked. Findings ranked by what they cost. `PAPER.md` stays hands-off: this
proposes.*

## Verdict

**The contribution survives, and it is better than the one currently claimed.** Nothing in the
literature occupies the position algorithmacy names. What does not survive is how four sections
state their case — and in four places a reviewer who opens a cited source will find the paper wrong
about it.

The good news first, because it changes what the paper should say rather than whether it can say it:

- **The gap is now a documented negative result, not an assertion.** Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum (2025)
  ran eight search terms over fifty algorithmic-literacy articles; the human counterpart appears
  nowhere. Gagrčin, Naab & Grub reach the same conclusion across 169 studies. Iyamu et al.'s scoping
  review of AI literacy *among workers* charts the field against Nutbeam's model — whose
  *communicative* level is literally "interacting with AI systems and explaining AI-mediated
  information" — and reports communicative literacies are **infrequently assessed**. The position is
  named as a slot and built into no instrument.
- **The cleanest statement of the whole result** is about what the field never combines. Abidin's
  visibility labour is the one construct with a real, specific human counterpart, and it *deliberately
  excludes the algorithm*. Every construct that centres the algorithm has no counterpart. Nobody has
  both.
- **Stratified fluency is genuinely unoccupied.** No instrument measures within-stratum variance;
  every one treats unevenness as an education gradient rather than as difference among equivalently
  placed workers.

## Tier 1 — a reviewer opens the source and finds the paper wrong

These cost the most because they are checkable in one click, and three of the four sources are in the
paper's own reference list.

### 1.1 §5 attributes to Okhuysen and Bechky a definition they do not give

**The paper's hinge rests on it.** §5 takes accountability to mean a forum with standing to demand
justification. Okhuysen and Bechky (2009: 483–490) define it as *responsibility allocation* — "who is
responsible for specific elements of the task." Worse, on the same pages they hold that accountability
"can be created through informal and emergent action as well as by formal means," and their worked
case is semiconductor assemblers **constructing accountability through emergent dialogue rather than a
formal structure**. A reviewer who opens the source finds coordinated parties doing the thing §5 says
cannot be done.

**Repair.** Import the forum definition from Bovens explicitly and say so. Then argue the difference:
emergent accountability in their cases runs among parties who can see and reach each other and hold
reciprocal standing — the preconditions co-optation removes. The full text is already in the repo at
`dissertation/current/paper1/exemplars/annals_coordination/text/okhuysen2009.txt`; read pp. 483–490
before rewriting.

**Second thread from the same pages:** they hold that the three conditions can **substitute** for one
another. §6's clean division has to answer that.

### 1.2 §8 defines competency as the thing its own anchor refutes

§8 defines competency as "the combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, behaviours and attributes
enabling effective performance" — the rationalistic attribute definition descending from Boyatzis and
Spencer — and then names **Sandberg (2000)** as its anchor. Sandberg's article exists to refute that
definition; his finding is that attributes do not distinguish competent from less competent workers,
and that ways of conceiving the work do.

**Repair, and it costs nothing.** Drop the KSAO clause. Define competency Sandberg's way — which §8
already half-says: "the worker's competence *is* her conception of what the system is doing." Then
rest the skill contrast on tool-indexing versus form-indexing, which survives every objection in 1.3.

**Read Sandberg (2000) first.** Crossref deposits no abstract for it, so its card is reconstructed
from reception rather than from the text.

### 1.3 §8's "skill is trainable by instruction" is contradicted by the theory of skill acquisition

Dreyfus and Dreyfus hold that instruction carries a learner through novice and advanced beginner and
no further, and that everything above comes from accumulated concrete experience that cannot be given
as rules. "Acquired through participation, not instruction" is the standard claim *about* skill.
Attewell (1990) adds that sociology runs four incompatible conceptions of skill, of which §8 rules out
only the positivist one — on the ethnomethodological conception algorithmacy is a textbook case. Le
Deist and Winterton note that in the UK functional tradition *competence* is the trainable-against-a-
standard term, inverting the contrast entirely.

**Repair.** As 1.2 — the tool-indexed/form-indexed contrast survives all three. Le Deist and
Winterton's **meta-competence** category is the best existing home if the paper wants to sit inside a
recognised typology.

### 1.4 §4's central precedent is disqualified by §8's own rule

§4 claims "this paper does for co-optation what the network literature did for networks," resting on
Ritter and Gemünden's **network competence**. That construct is a property of the **firm** — 308
German firms, firm-level items, organisational antecedents. §8 states that "a study that measured it
on firms would be measuring something else" and files firm-held constructs in the **capability** row,
which is exactly how §9 disposes of Stelmaszak et al. *The paper's own argument disposes of its own
precedent.* Schreiner, Kale and Corsten is worse: the construct is called a capability in its title.

**Repair.** Either drop the network precedent, or keep it and concede the level difference in the same
breath — the network literature named a competence for a form, at the firm level, and the seventh row
asks the same question at the level of the party. That concession is small and it removes an
inconsistency a careful reviewer will otherwise find.

**Also:** "three decades ago" is wrong. Ritter and Gemünden is 2003 — two decades from a 2026
submission.

### 1.5 §9's "none supplies the triad" is false about a source in the paper's own reference list

Hancock, Naaman and Levy define AI-MC as "interpersonal communication in which an intelligent agent
operates on behalf of a communicator by modifying, augmenting, or generating messages," introducing AI
into "human–human communication." That is three positions. §9 calls it "an agent serving the worker"
that inherits a dyad. Katsh and Rifkin's **fourth party** has held a non-human party position since
2001, and Wing, Martinez, Katsh and Rule (2021) theorise a fourth party that *decides*. Simmel already
separated the mediator who reconciles from the arbitrator who decides. Healy and Pekarek (2024) assert
the identical structural claim for gig work.

**Repair, and the resulting claim is stronger.** Stop counting positions. Run §9 on **standing**:
AI-MC's agent operates *on behalf of* the communicator — delegated, controllable, switchable off.
Algorithmacy's intermediary commits determinations binding on both parties and is controlled by
neither. **It adjudicates rather than serves.** Mediation research is defined by the third party
lacking binding authority; arbitration is binding but consensual and reasoned; the fourth-party
literature has the binding seat but discloses its criteria and asks what the *system* owes, never what
the party must be able to do.

## Tier 2 — claims the literature does not support

### 2.1 §1's variance premise is not in the literature

No peer-reviewed study shows outcome divergence among equivalently positioned workers that survives
after skill and task are accounted for. Every field study that looks for a skill moderator finds one:
Brynjolfsson/Li/Raymond (N=5,172), Noy & Zhang ("inequality between workers decreased"), Cui et al.,
Choi & Schwarcz, Otis et al. Dell'Acqua et al. supply the task moderator. Worse on §1's own terrain,
Cook et al. (2021, *REStud*, >1M drivers) document a 7% gender earnings gap among Uber drivers and
attribute it **entirely** to experience, where and when people drive, and speed. "Quit at different
rates" has no source at all — nothing in this literature measures attrition. And "the volume of
technology used predicts little of it" is contradicted by its only source: Bedard reports an
inverted-U, not a null.

**Repair.** State §1 as a puzzle, not a documented fact: the variance is real and repeatedly measured;
every published explanation lands on prior skill, task position or supply behaviour; the question is
what remains when those are subtracted, and nobody has posed it. §6 already concedes the rivals stand,
so this costs nothing and removes the paper's most attackable sentence.

**Two sources make the residual citable.** Krakowski, Luger and Raisch (2023) measure a human-machine
capability on individuals and find heterogeneity **orthogonal to prior skill**. Zhou, Lei, Cooke,
Huang and Zhang (2025) show one apparatus producing opposite outcomes by the worker's *construal* of
it — divergence by something she does rather than something she is. And **Barley (1986)** is the
organization-theory ancestor an OS/OT reviewer will expect: identical CT scanners, two matched
departments, divergent outcomes explained by neither technology nor prior skill.

### 2.2 "Nobody has named it" cannot survive as a claim about a name

Jarrahi and Sutherland coined "algorithmic competencies" in 2019. Zhou et al. validated a scale in
2025. Four further named constructs appeared between the abstract's acceptance and Lima. §4's table
row **Named as a construct? No** is indefensible as it stands.

**Repair.** Restate it as a claim about a **position**, and cite the three stocktakings rather than
asserting it. The §4 cell becomes something like *not on the axis that includes the counterpart*.

### 2.3 The Zhou dismissal will be destroyed on contact

The manuscript waves Zhou et al. off as measuring "technical knowledge." Only one of their four
dimensions is knowledge-like; Leveraging is strategic behaviour, Remediating is repair, Embracing is
an attitude. Two of their twelve items mention customers.

**Repair — pre-empt, do not hide.** Item 8 makes the customer a rating source to be minimised. Item 11
names the matching function, but measures a *belief about platform efficiency*, inside the dimension
correlating weakest with the rest (.22–.25). The decisive fact: **the counterpart appears in their
model only as an outcome variable**, never inside the construct.

## Tier 4 — free strengthening

- **Bovens (2010), "Two Concepts of Accountability"** — virtue as a quality predicated of one actor
  (a state) versus mechanism as a relation between actor and forum. §5 draws this line as though it
  were new. **The single highest-value addition in the sweep**: it converts the paper's hinge from
  assertion into restatement of a settled distinction.
- **Ziewitz (2019)** — build intent specification on it. SEO consultants must respond to the measure
  to score well, and *too much* adjustment gets flagged as gaming, so the competency is a judgement
  about how much adjustment the measure will tolerate. And the operator **declines to specify the
  boundary and delegates it** — common understanding withheld outbound, attributed as an act of the
  withholding party. Two consequences: the facet is hazardous rather than neutral, and anticipatory
  compliance is behaviourally identical but motivationally opposite, so an instrument that cannot
  separate them will mismeasure it.
- **Pinder & Harlos (2001)** — *acquiescent* silence (resignation) as against *quiescent* (fear).
  This names the first interview's pattern exactly, and yields a prediction the paper does not make:
  **a more competent worker reaches acquiescence faster, so higher algorithmacy predicts less
  contestation.** Counterintuitive, testable, and it complicates §6's distributive edge productively.
  With **Detert & Edmondson (2011)**, whose implicit voice theories predict withholding independently
  of channel availability — "the Discord existed and was not the place for this" — and **Felstiner,
  Abel & Sarat**, who supply the vocabulary: she named and blamed, and did not claim.
- **Rahman's fifth component, "who influences,"** names a failure §7 does not cover: not knowing which
  party to interpret.
- **Collins & Evans (2002), interactional expertise** — the first interview's participant pasting
  gate feedback into an AI and having it explained is fluency acquired through linguistic immersion
  without participating in the practice. Gives §11 a discriminant test with teeth: it predicts someone
  who talks accurately about the gate while her submissions do not correspondingly improve.
- **Clark & Brennan (1991)** on grounding is the precise machinery for §5's withheld common
  understanding.
- **Lind & Tyler** use **standing** as one of three relational judgements — a bridge to the
  dissertation's inherited standing problem that nobody has drawn.

## The method section, since the scale citations come out

Hinkin, Lambert & Newman and MacKenzie et al. go. **Minimum replacement set, five**, because the word
budget allows almost nothing:

1. **Gehman et al. (2018)** — theory–method fit. One sentence retires the three without conceding
   error, in the voices of the people who own the competing qualitative methods.
2. **Timmermans & Tavory (2012)** — abduction. Converts "you derived three facets then went looking
   for them" into a named stance.
3. **Blumer (1954)** — sensitizing versus definitive concepts. The protocol's move, verbatim in
   substance.
4. **Brannick & Coghlan (2007)** — insider research. Their four dynamics map one-to-one onto the §9
   risk table.
5. **Pratt, Kaplan & Whittington (2020)** — trustworthiness decoupled from replication.

**The strongest objection is not bias.** It is role duality plus non-free consent: the investigator
holds evaluative standing, so declining has a cost — and having designed the gate, he is the person
least able to see it as opaque. Answer with Brannick & Coghlan, plus **Anteby (2013)** (distance and
involvement are not opposite ends of one continuum) and **Ferguson, Yonge & Myrick (2004)** on faculty
recruiting their own students, which turns the §8 arrangements from improvisation into published
recommendation. **Mercer (2007)** lets the residual be conceded in someone else's words.

**The LLM instrument is defensible, and it is an asset.** Chopra & Haaland (2023), 395 AI-conducted
interviews, found a majority stated a strict preference for the AI interviewer over a human one —
which is the coercion problem the protocol exists to manage, solved by the instrument. Wuttke et al.
(2025) is the only randomised LLM-versus-human comparison and finds comparable quality. Xiao et al.
(2020) found a chatbot beat a survey box on informativeness, relevance, specificity and clarity, and
the mechanism was **probing** — the harness's "knock twice" rule, arrived at independently. Cite
against yourself too: **Nguyen & Welch (2026)** is sceptical, recent and in the journal your reviewer
reads, but their objection lands on *analysis*, which this design does not automate. Say so precisely.
**Schroeder et al. (2025)** name context collapse, which is why the transcribe-don't-compose rule, the
verbatim markers and the participant-reviewed file matter.

**One limitation to state rather than let a reviewer find:** participants run whatever editor and
model they have, which is what the standardisation literature warns against. Recording `model:` in the
response front matter mitigates; it does not eliminate.

1. **Sutherland et al. (2020), gig literacies.** Converging secondary summaries list **relationship
   building** among the five. If that is the authors' own heading, the counterpart is inside the
   nearest precedent and §9 needs rewriting. They also interviewed **19 clients alongside 20
   freelancers** — the paper you claim descent from had the third position in the room and left it out
   of the construct. That is a better sentence than a vague descent claim, and a live risk. Sage
   blocked retrieval.

3. **Dominguez Castillo (2026)**, "The ethics of algorithmic management: critical AI literacy and the

   the whole paper, completely unread.

   reliabilities, and none could be re-verified. Anyone citing the measure in §8 or §11 must read it.
