# Interview — people who helped run a program

For staff, operations, instructors, and partner organizations. Roughly twenty minutes, longer if
they want it.

Open with orientation, not with a question:

> I want to understand how the program actually ran, as opposed to how it was designed to run. Where
> those two came apart is the most useful thing you can tell me. Start wherever you like.

## A. Where you stood

> What was your part in it, and over what period?

> How much of what participants were doing could you actually see from where you sat?

> What did people come to you about?

*Follow the last one hard. The questions people brought are the spine of this interview — ask for
the ones that came up more than once, and for the one that came up most.*

## B. The gate, as it actually worked

> Walk me through what happened to a piece of work after someone submitted it.

> What did the person who submitted it get told, and when?

> What could they see of how the decision got made? What couldn't they?

> Did anyone ever ask you what the reviewers were actually looking for?

> When you answered that, what did you tell them — and did you know?

## C. When things changed

> Did the rules or the expectations shift at any point during a cohort?

> How did people find out?

> Was there anyone who didn't find out, or found out late? What happened to them?

> Did you ever change something and decide not to announce it? What was the thinking?

*That last question is a real one, not a trap. If they hesitate, say so.*

## D. When someone thought an outcome was wrong

This block matters more than the others. Do not rush it, and do not settle for a general answer.

> Did anyone ever come to you saying a decision about their work was wrong?

> What did they do — who did they go to, in what order?

> What actually happened? Did anything change?

> Was there a route for that, or did they have to invent one?

> Did anyone ever get an outcome reversed? Tell me about that one.

> Were there people you'd guess felt an outcome was wrong and never raised it? What stopped them?

## E. What people found hard

> Who did well in the program, and who struggled? Not names — what was the difference?

> Was that difference something you could have predicted on day one?

> Did anyone arrive good at working out what the program wanted, without being taught it?

> Did you see people getting better at that over the weeks, or was it fixed?

> What did people get wrong most often about how to present their work?

## F. Across programs

*Only if they saw more than one — Trinidad, Boston, or the Hult cohort. Skip otherwise.*

> What was different about how the gate worked between them?

> Did the same things go wrong in both, or different things?

> If you were setting one up again, what would you change about how decisions get communicated?

## Closing

> Is there anything about how this ran that I should have asked about and didn't?

> Anything you'd say to someone about to run one of these?

---

## Notes for the agent running this

**Ask about incidents, not impressions.** "It was confusing for people" is the beginning of an
answer. "Which time? What happened? What did they do?" is the answer.

**Block D is the one to protect.** If time runs short, cut E and F, never D.

**Do not supply vocabulary.** If they reach for a word and can't find it, wait. The word they land on
is worth more than the word you would have offered.

**Do not defend the program.** You are not its representative and neither is the person you're
talking to. If they criticize how something was run, write it down and ask what happened next.

**No leading on difficulty.** Never "was that frustrating?" Ask "what did you make of that?"
