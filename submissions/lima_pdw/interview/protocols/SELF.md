# Interview — the author

Roger interviewing himself, through an agent that will not let him summarize.

This is not human-subjects research and needs no approval. It is also the interview on the critical
path: no record exists anywhere of how the Trinidad gate ran, and the paper cannot describe it until
this file does. Everything else waits on this.

**The agent's job here is different from the other protocols.** He knows the theory. He will reach
for it. Your task is to keep him on events — what happened, in what order, who decided, what people
were told — and off interpretation. When he starts explaining what something *means*, stop him and
ask what happened.

Output goes to `response-<id>.md` as usual, anonymized per `ANONYMIZATION.md`. Roles get tokens even
here.

## A. Trinidad, as it ran

> Start with the shape of it. When did it run, how long, how many people, and what did a week look
> like?

> Who were the partners, and what did each of them actually do — not what the agreement said?

> How did work get submitted, and how did it get judged?

> Who made the call on whether a piece of work passed?

> What did the person who submitted it get told? In what form, and how long after?

> Could they see who decided, or how? Could they see the other submissions?

> Was there a written rule anywhere that participants could read?

*Push on that last one. A written rule participants could read is the single fact that decides how
this site gets described in the paper.*

## B. What people asked you

> What did participants come to you about, in the first week?

> What did they come to you about by the last week? Did the questions change?

> Was there a question you got over and over that you couldn't answer well?

> Did anyone ever ask you what the reviewers wanted? What did you say?

## C. When someone thought an outcome was wrong

> Did anyone ever tell you a decision about their work was wrong?

> What did they do about it, and who did they go to?

> Did anything change as a result? Ever?

> Was there a process, or did they have to invent one each time?

> Looking back — was there any route a participant could have taken, on their own, that would have
> got an outcome reconsidered?

*This block is the paper's only real empirical exposure. §5 claims accountability cannot be supplied
individually. If a participant reliably got outcomes reversed through their own effort with no forum
involved, that claim is wrong and the paper has to say so. Ask it honestly.*

## D. The design decisions, and why

> Why did you build the gate that way rather than some other way?

> What did you deliberately not tell participants, and why?

> What did you tell them that turned out not to be true, or not to stay true?

> What did you change mid-programme? What made you change it?

> Did you announce those changes? If not, why not?

## E. Across the three

> Boston and Hult run differently from Trinidad. Take them one at a time: what does a submission
> meet, and what does the submitter get back?

> The Boston cohort screens with a model and then the cohort votes. Whose decision is that, and does
> a participant experience it as one decision or two?

> At Hult the written reviews are public and the tally is not shown. Was that deliberate? What was
> the reasoning?

> Across the three, what did you make more visible over time, and what did you make less?

> Was there a moment you realized you had built the thing you were writing about?

## F. What you got wrong

> What do you think you got wrong about how these programs would go?

> Where were you the obstacle?

> What would someone who was in the Trinidad cohort say about your account of it, if they read it?

## Closing

> What is the one thing about Trinidad that has to be in the paper, that you have never written down?

> What are you least sure of, in what you've just said?

## Notes for the agent

**He will theorize. Do not let him.** If an answer contains the words *withheld*, *condition*,
*facet*, *construct*, or *form*, stop and ask: "what happened, though?" Ask for the incident. Ask for
the week it happened.

**Ask for numbers and dates and accept "I don't remember."** An honest gap in the record is usable;
a reconstructed number is not. Where he is unsure, write *(uncertain)* in the file next to it. That
annotation is what lets the paper mark recollection as recollection.

**Do not help him make the story coherent.** If two answers conflict, record both and note the
conflict. He is the only source on Trinidad, which means nothing here is checkable — so the file's
value depends entirely on it recording what he actually recalls rather than what fits.

**Block C is the one to protect**, then block D.
