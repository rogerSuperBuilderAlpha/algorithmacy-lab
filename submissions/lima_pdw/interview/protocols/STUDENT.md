# Interview — programme participants

For people who took part in a cohort: GauntleTT, Cursor Boston, or the Hult cohort program. Roughly
twenty minutes.

Open with orientation, not with a question:

> I want to understand what it was actually like to submit work into the review process — how you
> worked out what it wanted, and what happened when it went the way you didn't expect. There are no
> right answers here and nothing you say affects anything. Start wherever you like.

## A. Your work in the programme

> Which programme, and roughly when?

> What did a submission actually involve, from your side?

> How many did you make? Did they get easier?

## B. Working out what it wanted

> The first time you submitted something — what did you think would happen?

> What actually happened?

> How did you find out what the reviewers were looking for?

> Was there anywhere you could have read it? Did you look?

> Did your sense of what they wanted change over the programme? What changed it?

*Follow the specific. "I just figured it out" is the start of an answer — ask about the submission
where they figured it out.*

## C. Getting your work across

> When you put a submission together, did you do anything differently because of how it was going to
> be reviewed?

> Was there something about your work you wanted the reviewers to notice? How did you try to make
> them notice it?

> Was there anything you couldn't get across in the format? What was it?

> Did you ever have something good that landed badly, or something rushed that landed well? Tell me
> about that.

## D. When things changed

> Did what the reviewers wanted shift at any point?

> How did you notice?

> Did anyone tell you, or did you work it out?

> Was there a time you were still working to the old version? What happened?

> Did other people notice before you, or after? How did that get around?

## E. When an outcome seemed wrong

This block matters more than the others. Do not rush it and do not accept a general answer.

> Did you ever get a result on a submission that seemed wrong to you?

> Take me through that one. What did you submit, what came back, and what did you make of it?

> Did you do anything about it? Who did you go to?

> What happened?

> Was there a way to ask about it — a process, a person, anything?

> Did you ever get a result changed? What did it take?

> Were there times you thought something was wrong and didn't raise it? What made you leave it?

*The last question is often where the real answer is. Let the pause run.*

## F. How you learned it

> Where did what you know about the review process come from?

> Any of it from the course material, or from being told directly?

> Any of it from watching what happened to other people?

> Is there anything you know about how it worked that you couldn't say how you learned?

> If someone joined tomorrow, what would you tell them that nobody told you?

## Closing

> Is there anything about how this worked that I should have asked about and didn't?

> Anything you'd want the people who run it to know?

---

## Notes for the agent running this

**No leading, ever.** Not "was it frustrating?", not "did that feel unfair?" Ask "what did you make
of that?" and wait.

**Block E is the one to protect.** If time runs short, cut A and F, never E.

**Do not defend the programme, and do not agree with criticism of it either.** You are not its
representative. Record what they say and ask what happened next.

**Do not name concepts.** Introduce no vocabulary of your own — see `AGENT.md`. If they reach for a
word and can't find it, wait. The word they land on is the data.

**If they mention another person by name**, that is fine in conversation; the anonymizer handles it
when writing. Do not stop them to correct it.

**If an answer turns distressing** — a bad experience, a falling-out, something that clearly still
stings — do not probe it for detail. Note what they volunteered, ask if they want to continue, and
respect the answer.
