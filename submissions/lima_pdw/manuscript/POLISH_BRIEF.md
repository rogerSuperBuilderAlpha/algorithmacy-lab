## 4. House style — the actual rules, which are not the usual ones

This project's style guide **retired** several anti-AI rules that generic advice would reimpose. Do
not reinstate them:

- **Em-dashes are allowed and wanted**, at roughly 3–5 per thousand words, used to weld a gloss onto
  a term at first use. Do not strip them. Cut only the lazy parenthetical aside.
- **First person is required**, at 4–8 per thousand in the body — but it marks *authorial labour
  only* ("we take them in that order," "we predict"), never a claim. "We argue that the algorithm
  mediates" is wrong; the claim is the mediation.
- **Contrast pairs and analytic triplets are allowed** where each member carries distinct content.
- ***Moreover*, *However*, *Thus*, *For example*** and other relation markers are ordinary venue usage.

What is banned:

- **Emphasis openers**: *Crucially, Notably, Importantly, Note that, Stated plainly, To be clear, It
  is worth.* Currently zero. Keep it there.
- **Agentless passives opening a sentence** ("has been shown," "it has been argued"). Name the agent.
- **Performed rigor** — any sentence commenting on the paper's own care, warrant, or organizing
  choices. "We argue fit from X, not from Y" is a sentence *about arguing*; write the argument.
  This draft has had four such sentences removed. Do not reintroduce them.
- **A sentence ending in a citation parenthesis** where the next sentence needs a handle. Move the
  citation to subject position.

What carries the prose, and what to preserve when you edit:

1. **Tail-head linkage** — sentence N ends on the new item, sentence N+1 opens by naming it.
2. **End-focus** — the new item goes last, where the next sentence can reach it.
3. **Renaming the thread entity** across a stretch (*the construct → it → the competency*).
4. **Welding the gloss onto the term** by em-dash or appositive, then using the term as a subject.
5. **Ending paragraphs on a forward hook**, not an epigram.
6. **Grouping runs of parallel evidence** rather than one finding per sentence.
7. **Deliberate rhythm variation** — dense sentences punctured by short ones. Not uniform punchiness.

Measured on body prose (from `# Paper 1 of This Dissertation` to `# References`), excluding
tables and HTML comments:

| agentless passives | 0 | 0 |

**Three of these sit outside the house standard, and they point the same way: toward uniformity.**
Sentence-length variance is the one that matters. An earlier version of this paper ran a CV of 0.62
with nineteen short sentences; this one runs 0.48 with ten. The prose is more even, more nominalized,
and carries more abstract nouns per sentence than the venue's own register.

**The single most useful thing you can do is increase variance, not decrease it.** Break long
sentences where a genuine seam exists. Let some paragraphs land on a short, flat statement of fact.
Do not smooth. Do not make the rhythm more regular than you found it — that is this manuscript's
standing failure mode and the author will see it immediately.

The em-dash rate is high because several paragraphs use paired dashes as a default aside. Convert the
lazy ones to commas or full stops; keep the ones that weld a gloss onto a term at first use.

**The short sentences are the scarce resource.** These are the shortest in the body — do not
lengthen, merge, or subordinate them, and add more of their kind rather than fewer:

> The next section develops that construct.
> This structural boundary remains empirically falsifiable.
> The literacy analogy establishes operational symmetry.
> Neighboring constructs remain viable alternative explanations.
> Algorithmic coordination structurally breaks this relational model.

## 6. Repairs already made — do not undo them

Each was an error a reviewer or a source check caught, and **all eight survived the author's 26 August
rewrite intact** — verified. A polishing pass that "restores flow" can quietly reintroduce any of them.

- **Paper 1's condition-mapping is not attributed to the classics.** Okhuysen and Bechky's triad
  (predictability, common understanding, accountability) was assembled in 2009; Simon, Weber, Hayek,
  Powell and Granovetter never wrote in it. The text says "Paper 1, drawing on Hayek (1945), read
  that statistic as…" — keep Paper 1 as the agent. **Hayek says nothing about accountability,
  contract, or reputation.**
- **Spitzberg and Cupach (1984) are cited against the reading a model reaches for.** Their relational
  model holds competence is *not a property of a person* but a quality of a relationship judged by
  both parties. The paper uses that as the standard the arrangement cannot meet. Do not "correct" it
  back to competence-as-ability.
- **Spitzberg (2006) has five outcome criteria**, not four: appropriateness, effectiveness
  (subsuming task achievement and efficiency), coorientation, satisfaction, relationship development.
- **Long and Magerko's competency 10 is titled *Human Role in AI*.** The text says no competency names
  another person *as a party to an interaction the learner is conducting*. That narrowing is load-bearing;
  do not simplify it back to "none names another person," which is false.
- **The Sutherland quotation reads "were in fact valued,"** not "were apparent."
- **The Guzman and Lewis quotation is on p. 74**, not p. 73.
- **The gate is not a private vote.** At Hult the written reviews and upvotes are public and the
  *tally* is withheld; at Cursor Boston a model screens and the cohort votes with each participant
  seeing only her own score; for Trinidad no record survives. The variation is the argument.
- **The consent procedure is anonymity, not withdrawal.** Intake is anonymous, no record exists of
  who took part, and a submitted response cannot be withdrawn. Do not write a withdrawal mechanism.

## 7. The contribution claim, and the four scope conditions holding it up

The paper's negative claim is that no competence construct gives the human counterpart a position.
An adversarial sweep across nine literatures and three languages failed to falsify it, and found
that **scope condition 3 carries the whole argument.** All four must survive editing:

1. **Competence constructs only.** The three-party *position* has been named four times in print —
   Katsh and Rifkin's fourth party (2001), Curchod and colleagues' triad in *ASQ* (2020), Cameron's
   algorithmic labor triangle (2024), Healy and Pekarek (2024). Unscoped, the claim is false four
   times over. The paper concedes all four by name; **do not cut that concession.**
2. **The counterpart is a party to the interaction** — not the system's makers, not peers, not a
   downstream beneficiary.
3. **The intermediary reads both parties and binds them** — not a channel that carries, a delegate
   that serves, or a disclosed fourth party. *This is the condition doing the work.*
4. **It is a claim about a position, never a name.**

The limits section concedes three frontier near-misses (a JMIR medical-education preprint, Yang and
Liechty on social work, and the dating-app pincer of Dredge and Anderson with Hu and Zhan) and states
that none has been read in full. Keep that concession — it pre-empts the objection.

## 10. What actually needs polishing

Having ruled out the above, the useful work, in order of value:

1. **Sentence-length variance.** See §5. This is the first priority and the one the author will
   check. Break long sentences; let some paragraphs end short and flat.
2. **Nominalization.** The prose runs heavy on abstract nouns doing the work of verbs —
   "operationalization," "conceptualization," "implementation," "configuration." Where a noun phrase
   hides an action, name the agent and use the verb. This is the single largest source of the
   evenness described in §5.
3. **Paragraph-level cohesion.** Does each paragraph open on something the previous one ended with,
   and end on a hook rather than a verdict? That is where the style guide says the real gains are.
4. **Long paragraphs.** Several run past 300 words. Split where a genuine seam exists; do not chop
   for its own sake.
5. **The transitions between major sections**, especially into and out of the construct section.
6. **Anything that reads as machine-written even though it passes every rule above.** That is the
