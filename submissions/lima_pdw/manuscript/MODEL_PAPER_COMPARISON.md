# Model-Paper Comparison — Structural Templates for PAPER.md

Compiled 2026-08-29. Purpose: find a real, verifiable, well-regarded paper (or papers) whose
*architecture* — not topic — PAPER.md can be checked against or partially remapped onto. Three
rounds of search, each narrowing the brief. Nothing here has been applied to PAPER.md yet; this is
a reference document for a decision still open (see "Open decision" at the end).

## Round 1 — topically similar paper (superseded brief)

First pass allowed topical overlap (algorithmic management, platform work). Best match found:

**Bucher, E. L., Schou, P. K., & Waldkirch, M. (2021). "Pacifying the algorithm – Anticipatory
compliance in the face of algorithmic management in the gig economy." *Organization*, 28(1),
44–67.** DOI: 10.1177/1350508420961531. SAGE; 254 citations at time of search; verified via
Crossref metadata and abstract (full text blocked by SAGE/ResearchGate — 403 on both).

Names "anticipatory compliance practices," a typology of four worker behaviors under opaque
algorithmic management, grounded in a qualitative content analysis of ~12,000 scraped forum

already collected and reported), not a construct-and-design paper the way PAPER.md currently is,
and it doesn't run a systematic rival-construct elimination the way PAPER.md's Extant Constructs
section does. Useful mainly as a calibration point for what a *finished* qualitative study in this
topic area looks like — relevant to Paper 3, not to this paper's current architecture.

## Round 2 — the field's own rubric, plus a first structural template (any venue, any topic)

### The rubric

Two papers the field treats as its own standard for what a construct/theory paper must do — not
templates to fill in, but the scoring criteria a template should satisfy.

**Suddaby, R. (2010). "Editor's comments: Construct clarity in theories of management and
organization." *Academy of Management Review*, 35(3), 346–357.** DOI: 10.5465/amr.35.3.zok346.
Verified via AOM's DOI record; full text 403'd, criteria confirmed via a secondary source quoting
the article directly. Four criteria for construct clarity:
1. **Definition** — a real semantic/constitutive definition, not just a label.
2. **Scope conditions** — explicit boundaries on where the construct applies.
3. **Relationships to neighboring constructs** — how it differs from what's already out there.
4. **Coherence** — the first three organized into one consistent whole.

**Whetten, D. A. (1989). "What Constitutes a Theoretical Contribution?" *Academy of Management
Review*, 14(4), 490–495.** Verified by direct read of the full article (course-page PDF repost).
A complete theoretical contribution needs:
- **What** — the right constructs, comprehensive but parsimonious.
- **How** — the actual relationships between them ("arrows connecting boxes").
- **Why** — the underlying logic that justifies the model ("the theoretical glue"); propositions
  are what bridge Why back to testable claims.
- **Who, Where, When** — scope/boundary conditions on generalizability.

Whetten's line worth keeping in view: a list of constructs or a diagram alone is not a theoretical
contribution — "relationships, not lists, are the domain of theory."

### First template candidate

**Petriglieri, J. L. (2011). "Under threat: Responses to and the consequences of threats to
individuals' identities." *Academy of Management Review*, 36(4), 641–662.** DOI:
10.5465/amr.2009.0087. Confirmed conceptual — 8 numbered propositions as the primary contribution,
no primary data collection. Reviews a scattered prior literature on identity threat and integrates
it into one formal model. Full text 403'd (AOM); relying on verified abstract plus a verified
proposition list from a secondary source, not a section-by-section read.

Backups considered, not pursued further: Smith & Lewis (2011, AMR, paradox theory — strong exemplar
but synthesizes a field-level phenomenon rather than eliminating rival constructs one by one);
Zahra & George (2002, AMR, absorptive capacity — reconceptualizes an *existing* construct rather
than introducing a new one).

## Round 3 — same brief, restricted to *Organization Theory* or *Organization Studies* specifically, 2024+

The user asked for something more recent and in these two specific SAGE journals (not AMR/AMJ/ASQ/
JMS). *Organization Theory* is fully open access, which mattered — full text was actually reachable
this round, unlike every prior attempt.

### The chosen template — full text verified

**Räcker, T., Geiger, D., & Seidl, D. (2024). "From coordinating in space to coordinating through
space: A spatial perspective on coordinating." *Organization Theory*, 5, 1–18.** DOI:
10.1177/26317877241270124. Tagged "Theory Article" in its own running header. Full PDF obtained
from an open-access institutional mirror (University of Hamburg, `wiso.uni-hamburg.de`; the SAGE
`journals.sagepub.com` front door itself still 403'd automated access even though the article is
CC BY-NC open access — a bot-detection block, not an actual paywall). Read cover to cover.

**Confirmed: no primary data.** Every empirical reference in the piece is forward-looking ("this
opens up a novel empirical research programme," "empirical research may examine..."). Same posture
PAPER.md is in — construct/framework plus a research design, not construct plus completed findings.

**Actual section architecture, in order:**

1. **Abstract** — one paragraph: names the gap, states the theoretical move, states the payoff.
2. **Introduction** — defines the core phenomenon (coordination) via a standard citation (Okhuysen
   & Bechky, 2009), names the specific omission (coordination research treats physical space as
   passive backdrop/context), gestures at a second, separate literature — the "spatial turn" in the
   wider organization-studies field — that already treats space differently, and previews the
   paper's structure and contributions in the intro's final paragraph.
3. **"From Space as Context to Space as Product of Coordinating"** — the literature-positioning
   section. Two moves: (a) reviews how the coordination literature treats space (as context, citing
   several empirical exemplar studies), (b) reviews how the spatial-turn literature treats space
   (as actively produced, grounded in Lefebvre's 1991 theory of spatial production). This is the
   paper's version of a gap-finding review — but it crosses **two** literatures against each other,
   not a systematic elimination of many rival constructs one by one.
4. **"Configurations of Spatial Production in Coordinating"** — the actual theory-building. Crosses
   a borrowed typology (three dimensions of physical space: distance, boundary, movement, from
   Weinfurtner & Seidl 2019) against three Lefebvrian modes of *producing* space (designing,
   enacting, shaping the experience of). Assembles this into one synthesizing framework, presented
   as a single boxes-and-arrows figure: space-focused coordination practices → intermediate effects
   (e.g. differentiation, connection, visibility) → integrating conditions (accountability,
   predictability, common understanding) → coordination outcome.
5. **"Integrating Effects of Spatial Production"** — walks the framework cell by cell. Each spatial
   dimension (distance, boundary, movement) gets its own subsection, working systematically through
   the near/far or permeable/impermeable or fixed/flexible poles of each, each claim anchored to
   supporting literature rather than new data.
6. **A short "Configurational effects" subsection** — acknowledges the dimensions interact and can
   counterbalance each other, illustrated through one existing published case (an office redesign
   study by Zalesny & Farace, 1987) rather than new data of the authors' own.
7. **"Research Implications of the Spatial Perspective on Coordinating"** — two subsections: why
   the reframing matters, then an explicit, numbered four-item forward research agenda (space-
   focused coordinating in practice; inadvertent spatial production of non-space-focused practices;
   spatial configurations in practice; interrelations between dimensions/practices).
8. **Conclusion** — short, restates the core reframe (coordinating happens "through" space, not
   merely "in" it) and its practical stakes.

**Two structural facts that matter most for any remapping decision:**
- **No numbered propositions anywhere.** The theoretical claim is carried entirely by the crossed
  typology and the single synthesizing figure — not by a Whetten/Petriglieri-style proposition list.
- **No formal empirical design.** The "Research Implications" section is four suggestive paragraphs,
  not a specified study with sample targets, falsification criteria, or IRB status.

Flagged in passing, not pursued: Stark, D., & Vanden Broeck, P. (2024), "Principles of Algorithmic
Management," also in *Organization Theory* 5(2), DOI 10.1177/26317877241257213 — same authors
PAPER.md already cites extensively for coordinative co-optation, so not new information, just noted
so it isn't independently "rediscovered" later.

Two *Organization Studies* leads turned up by title only, **never verified beyond Crossref
metadata** (SAGE blocked full-text access there the way it did everywhere else this session except
the open-access *Organization Theory*): a 2025 piece on "mission drift to practice drift" theorizing
(DOI 10.1177/01708406251314591), and "Building a Trust Machine: Fidelity mechanisms in
organizations" (DOI 10.1177/01708406251326653). Neither should be treated as a confirmed match —
they're leads to chase if the Petriglieri/Räcker options don't hold up.

## What PAPER.md's current architecture looks like against Räcker et al.

| | Räcker, Geiger, & Seidl (2024) | PAPER.md (current) |
|---|---|---|
| Gap-finding review | Crosses **two** literatures (coordination research vs. the spatial turn) | Eliminates **seven** rival constructs one by one (Extant Constructs section) |
| Theoretical payload | One crossed typology + one synthesizing figure | Three constitutive operations + a comparison table (Table 1) + semantic-relations predictions |
| Propositions | **None** | Three numbered propositions plus an unnumbered stabilization-mechanism lead-in |
| Empirical design | Four suggestive paragraphs, no instrument, no sample target, no falsification criteria | A full insider-action-research design: harness interview protocol, three-wave survey panel, IRB status, falsification criteria, and a four-study future research agenda (Appendix C) |
| Length/apparatus | 18 pages, one figure, one table (none — actually zero tables), ~140 references | Main text + three formal appendices (A: full comparison table, B: full research design, C: research agenda) |

**Bottom line:** PAPER.md is more ambitious than this model on every axis — more rival constructs
eliminated, more formal propositions, and a far more developed empirical design. That's not
automatically a flaw (Suddaby's and Whetten's own criteria arguably favor the fuller treatment), but
it is a real choice point, not a mechanical resemblance to lean on.

## Open decision (not yet resolved, no edits made to PAPER.md)

**Option A** — treat Räcker et al. as evidence that OT/OS venues will accept, and reward, a leaner
theory paper, and correspondingly *compress* PAPER.md's seven-way construct elimination toward
something closer to a two-way literature-crossing, trading comprehensiveness for economy.

**Option B** — keep PAPER.md's current architecture (arguably better-justified by Suddaby's and
Whetten's own published criteria), and borrow only the *moves within* each section from Räcker et
al. — e.g., how tightly its introduction is scoped, how much of its theoretical payload rides on a
single figure rather than prose alone, how compact its forward-research section is relative to
PAPER.md's three full appendices.

Recommendation on record (not yet acted on): lean B. PAPER.md's actual documented weakness, per the
Fable panel review and PB's advisor feedback (see `reviews/fable_panel_2026-08-28/` and the earlier
revision work in this file's history), was overclaiming relative to the data collected so far — not
an excess of theoretical apparatus. Räcker et al. sidesteps the empirics-vs-claims problem almost
entirely rather than solving it, so it's a weak fix for that specific, already-identified issue.
