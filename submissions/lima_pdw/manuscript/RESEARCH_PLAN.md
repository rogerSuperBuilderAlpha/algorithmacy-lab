## What we are building toward

The paper after the introduction has three jobs, in this order:

1. Give every extant construct the strongest hearing the sources allow, then show with evidence
   exactly where each one stops.
2. Develop algorithmacy as a new construct, following a published model of construct development.
3. Report the qualitative study in the form those journals accept for methods and early findings.

The genre we want as a whole-paper model is a qualitative article that (a) starts from a field
observation, (b) tries the existing constructs, (c) shows they cannot cover it, (d) names its own,
and (e) takes that construct back into the field with a documented method. We already have three
such papers in the library at full text. We are short the one paper that develops a *competency*
construct from interviews, and we are short a full-text read of the one neighbour whose design
already includes the counterpart.

## Phase 1 — Deepen the extant constructs

The current draft names each construct and states the failure in one or two sentences. That is the
map. It is not the hearing. A reviewer who works in any of these literatures will treat a one-sentence
dismissal as a straw man.

### Steelman protocol (same for every construct)

Do this in a memo under `literature/steelmans/<slug>.md`. One construct per file. Do not write the
memo from the card. Write it from the article.

1. **Their claim, in their words.** Quote the definition. List dimensions or items. Name the
   population and the method.
2. **Best version.** State the strongest reading a sympathetic reviewer would give. If later papers
   extended the construct, include the extension.
3. **What they make the algorithm.** Channel / partner / agent / object of knowledge / object of
   navigation / object of interpretation / other. Keep their vocabulary.
4. **Hunt for the counterpart.** Count where a client, evaluator, rider, reviewer, or other
   participant appears: in the definition, in the items, in the outcomes, in the sample. Quote the
   closest sentences. This is where Zhou item 11 and Sutherland's client interviews live.
5. **The test.** Can a person who fully satisfies *their* construct still have failed to coordinate
   with a human counterpart through a system that reads both sides and commits a decision? If yes,
   the construct is a person–system line. Write that as one sentence with the evidence attached.
6. **What we inherit.** One thing we take from them (Rahman's shifting criteria; Spitzberg's
   mediated competence; Zhou's navigation). Do not pretend they did no work.
7. **What we still cannot get by adding items.** The missing piece is a position, not a variable.
   Show it on *this* construct, not as a slogan.

### Evidence standard for the constructs section of the paper

The revised section should be able to say, for each construct:

- what it is, with a quoted definition;
- what method produced it;
- where the counterpart appears, if it does, with a page or item number;
- the test result;
- the inherited piece.

No construct is introduced in order to be excluded from a review. Each is a boundary. The section
argues that those boundaries cannot include the other human as a party to the competence.

## Phase 2 — Develop algorithmacy on a published model

### Write-up model: Suddaby (2010)

The algorithmacy section of the paper should have four explicit jobs, in his order. The definition
is already welded in the locked introduction. Do not restate the literacy / numeracy / oracy block.
Point at it, then do the other three jobs.

| Suddaby element | Our content | Work still needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Locked. Interpret, specify, keep track, so that a person can participate in coordination that runs through a system which reads both parties and commits decisions neither controls. | Do not rewrite. Gloss only if a later section needs a term the intro already defined. |
| **Scope conditions** | The form in which a platform enrolls through an algorithm. Holds where the intermediary is opaque, adaptive, and binding on both parties. Fails (or becomes trivial) where the rule is published, static, or the parties meet without it. | Write the "does not apply" cases. Cameron and Rahman supply the conditions. A transparent, rule-published hierarchy is the clean negative case. |
| **Semantic relations** | The seven steelmans. Algorithmacy includes the counterpart; they specify a person–system line. Zhou's AC is the nearest name-collision. | Phase 1 memos are the evidence. Predict correlations with Zhou's *Understanding* and *Leveraging* before anyone asks. |
| **Coherence** | The three operations are required by the definition's closing clause, not collected from interviews. Literacy family: competence for a coordination, not a medium. Not a skill (rule undisclosed). Not a capability (she owns no routine). | Sandberg (2000) is the competence anchor — **after the PDF is read.** The current draft still risks the KSAO sentence he wrote the article to refute. |

### Development model: Sandberg (2000), then contrast Zhou

Sandberg shows how a qualitative study *produces* a competence construct: same role, different
conceptions of the work, competence is the conception. Zhou shows how a competency scale is
*invented from interviews* and then validated. We do a third thing: derive the operations from
what the form requires, then take them to the field as sensitizing concepts that can fail.

The algorithmacy section must say that difference in one paragraph, after both sources are read
in full. Until Sandberg is read, do not rebuild the definition around him.

### What "fully developed" means before the qualitative section starts

A reader who stopped at the end of the algorithmacy section should be able to:

- state the construct without the literacy parallel;
- say where it does and does not apply;
- name the nearest rival and the item-level reason it is not the same thing;
- say what would count as the construct being wrong (a person who satisfies Zhou's scale and
  still cannot reach a counterpart is evidence *for* us; a person who obtains answerability from
  the gate by skill alone, with no other party, is evidence *against* the missing-position claim).

## Phase 3 — Qualitative methods and findings, to standard

### What we already have

- A harness interview, three protocols (`interview/protocols/{STUDENT,OPERATIONS,SELF}.md`).
- Consent, anonymization, third-party intake. Bentley IRB #260511078 (exempt).
- A specialized course at Hult as the continuing site. GauntleTT as formation only.
- Several responses. The first, already described in the arm README: no contest because no
  channel was legible; feedback pasted into another AI. Transcripts ran thin; protocols were
  deepened.
- A design that is abductive, not Gioia and not grounded theory.

### What a methods section has to contain

Model the *jobs* on Rahman (pp. of his methods) and Cameron (researcher-as-participant honesty),
at our scale. Headings a reviewer expects:

1. **Site and why it fits.** Hult course: unexplained outcome, unseen evaluator, moving rule.
   Hard case for opacity (they build the systems). Argue fit; do not assert it.
2. **How the construct was formed, and why that is not the study.** GauntleTT in two sentences.
   Formation = content validity of language. Not evidence of structure.
3. **Access and position.** The investigator teaches the course. Advancement is the peer gate;
   he casts no vote. Consent and withdrawals sit outside the course. No interview about an open
   project. Cite Pratt et al. (2020) for why the audit trail is not a public transcript dump.
4. **The instrument.** Harness: task → unexplained outcome → interpretation and next act. One
   incident per block. Knock twice. No theoretical vocabulary introduced. Point at the protocol
   rather than reprinting it.
5. **Sample and status.** Who has responded, from which protocol, at what stage of the course.
   Theoretical, not numerical, justification (Pratt 2009). Do not write saturation without
   Saunders's rule. This draft reports early work.
6. **Analysis.** Abductive (Timmermans & Tavory 2012). Three operations as sensitizing concepts
   (Bowen 2006). First cycle in informant terms (Gioia discipline, not Gioia template). Bricolage
   (Pratt et al. 2022). Negative case: a participant who gets an answer from the gate by skill
   alone. A finding that lived practice carves differently from the derivation is a finding.
7. **Trustworthiness.** What is inspectable (protocol, coding rule, discard log, second reader
   if we have one). What is not deposited (raw responses — public repo). Tracy (2010) if asked
   for criteria.

### What a findings / early-results section has to contain

We do not have a completed analysis. The section must look like an *early qualitative report*,
not like a theory paper that mentions interviews.

Model: Rahman and Cameron organize findings by **named forms of conduct**, each illustrated by
incidents, then a condition that distinguishes them. At our stage:

- Organize by the interview's own blocks (working out what it wanted; getting work across;
  noticing change; what happens when it seems wrong), not by our three operations as if they
  had been found.
- Every claim is attached to an incident, not a characterization. The protocol already requires
  this; the write-up must too.
- Use participant words for the turn sentences (the harness already marks them).
- Keep the first documented case (forbearance with no forum; interpretation delegated to a
  tool). Add later responses the same way. Do not invent a second case.
- Close on what the instrument learned (thin transcripts → deeper follow-up), which is a
  methodological finding and belongs here.

Do not report themes that were not in the transcripts. Do not promote the three operations from
sensitizing concepts to "we found three themes."
