expects. Citations are to pages we have now read. Do not claim Gioia *and*

Pratt (2009: 856–857): there is no boilerplate, and “enough” is a function of

## 1. Site and why it fits

**License:** Pratt (2009) — sampling is justified by the theoretical question.

**Write:** The continuing site is a specialized course at Hult University.
Participants submit work to a gate that returns an outcome that is not fully
explained. Advancement depends on peers they cannot watch. The codebase moves
because other participants change it. Those three features are the conditions
the locked definition names as opaque, intermediary, and adaptive. The site is
a hard case for opacity, because the participants build the systems they also
submit to. We argue fit from the gate, the private vote, and the moving rule,
not from the employment status.

## 2. Formation is not the study

**Write:** The construct was formed while teaching GauntleTT. That bears on
whether the language of the instrument says what participants mean. It is not
evidence of the construct’s structure.

## 3. Access and position

**License:** Cameron (2024) for naming the researcher’s place in the field.
Pratt, Kaplan & Whittington (2020): trustworthiness is inspectable inference,
not a public transcript dump. Tracy (2010): sincerity and ethics.

**Write:** The investigator teaches the course. Advancement is decided by the
peer gate, in which he casts no vote. Consent and withdrawals sit outside the
course. No one is interviewed about a project whose outcome is still open.
Responses are anonymized before they leave the participant’s machine. The
audit trail is held for the committee; it is not deposited. This repository
is public.

## 4. The instrument

**License:** Pratt (2009) — show how you moved from question to talk.
Timmermans & Tavory (2012) — we enter with a theoretically derived construct
and let incidents surprise it.

**Write:** A harness interview. The participant completes a task, receives an
outcome that is not fully explained, and is asked how she interpreted it and
what she did next. One incident per block. A “no” gets one reframe, not a
second identical question. The interviewer introduces no theoretical
vocabulary. Protocols: student, operations, self.

## 5. Sample and status

**License:** Pratt (2009: 856) — no magic number. Saunders et al. (2018) — do
not write “saturation was reached” without a rule.

**Write:** We have obtained several responses. This paper reports early work.
We do not claim saturation. We do not claim a completed analysis. Who has
responded, from which protocol, and at what point in the course, will be
stated as the file stands at submission.

## 6. Analysis

**License:** Timmermans & Tavory (2012) — abductive analysis. Bowen (2006) —
sensitizing concepts start inquiry; they do not sort it. Gioia, Corley &
Hamilton (2013) — first cycle in informant terms. Pratt, Sonenshein & Feldman
(2022) — methodological bricolage: an arrangement of moves that communicates
trustworthiness. Mees-Buss, Welch & Piekkari (2022) — we decline the Gioia
data-structure template.

**Write:** Analysis is abductive. The three operations in the locked definition
are sensitizing concepts. First-cycle coding stays in the participant’s words.
We do not produce a Gioia data structure, because that ladder is built for
constructs that emerge from informant language, and we entered with a derived
construct we are trying to expose to refutation. A finding that lived practice
carves differently is a finding. The negative case is a participant who
obtains an answer from the gate by individual skill alone, with no other
party involved.

## 7. Trustworthiness

**License:** Tracy (2010: 840) — worthy topic, rich rigor, sincerity,
credibility, resonance, significant contribution, ethics, meaningful
coherence. We use the list as the standard if asked; we do not print all
eight as a checklist. Pratt et al. (2020) — transparency ≠ replication.

**Write:** What is inspectable: the protocol, the incident rule, the discard
log, the negative-case rule. What is not deposited: raw responses. Member
checking, if used, will be named. A second reader, if used, will be named.

## 8. Findings (early)

**License:** Pratt (2009) — show incidents in the body; interpret them.
Rahman (2021) / Cameron (2024) — organize by named forms of conduct, not by
our three operations as if they had been found.

**Write:** Organize by the interview blocks (working out what it wanted;
getting work across; noticing change; what happens when it seems wrong).
Every claim is attached to an occasion. Keep the first documented case:
forbearance because no channel was legible; interpretation delegated to
another system. Add later responses the same way. Close on what the
instrument learned (thin transcripts → deeper follow-up).

Do not write “we found three themes corresponding to interpret, specify, and
keep track.”
