## Why the current body failed

Okhuysen never writes a sentence that (a) introduces a new actor, (b) names a
method, (c) lists five properties, and (d) states the test, all at once. Their
body sentences do one job. Chandler argued one thing. The timetable did one
thing. Faraj and Xiao supplied one definition.

Ours stacked. The extant opener had four jobs. Spitzberg’s first paragraph
unloaded an entire model. Each construct closed on the same test in the same
words. That is the tell: a sentence that needs a rescue sentence was already
overloaded.

## Model sentences (quote, then match the job count)

Their opener (l. 142–144): “The advent of railroads in the eighteenth century
engendered considerable interest in coordination.” One topic, one claim, no
method, no second object.

Their named-scholar sentence (l. 143–145): “Chandler (1962, p. 23) argued that
railroads were the first of a new breed of organization due to their size and
complexity.” Named scholar. One claim. One because-clause.

Their mechanism sentence (l. 148–150): “The mechanism that enabled railroads
to coordinate on a large scale was the printed timetable (Stover, 1970).”
One mechanism. One job.

Their consequence (l. 151–153): “Timetables, for example, allowed a train to
‘own’ pieces of track at a specific time, which prevented collisions.”
Example, then what it did.

Their section turn (l. 307–309): “Recent research on coordination has extended
beyond organizational theory into a variety of disciplines such as computer
science, information systems, and sociology of work.” New stretch. One claim.
The list is of already-familiar discipline names, not untaught objects.

Their “for example” (l. 324–327): “For example, research in this domain has
focused on communicating (Ballard & Seibold, 2003), cooperation (Pinto, Pinto,
& Prescott, 1993), knowledge sharing (Bechky, 2003a; Carlile, 2002), and
interaction (Heath, Hindmarsh, & Luff, 1999).” Named labels, each cited.
Not a dump of untaught architecture.

## Checks (every slot, before the next slot opens)

1. **Model shape.** Quote their sentence. Ours must have the same number of
   jobs (usually one). If theirs has one comma-appositive, ours may. If theirs
   does not introduce two unnamed objects, ours may not.
2. **Context.** Read the sentence before and the planned sentence after. Ours
   must open on the item the previous sentence ended on.
3. **Novice.** Circle every noun. If the locked intro has not taught it, and no
   prior body sentence has taught it, rewrite or split.
4. **Plot.** The sentence must be a step in the author’s plot, not a leftover
   (seventh question, companion review, residual variance).
5. **Speed.** One slot per turn unless the author says continue.

Banned: “Crucially,” “Importantly,” “To be clear,” “we take that candor,”
“deserve the longest hearing,” “the strongest available foil,” agentless
“research has shown.” Zhou never as “Zhou et al. 2025” bare. Do not write
“nobody has named it.” Do not write “we found three themes.”
