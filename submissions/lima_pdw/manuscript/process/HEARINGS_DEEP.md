## Specimen 1 — Computer-Mediated Communication Competence

*Currently 249 words. Below, 520. Developed on **the test**: his outcome criteria have an
administrator, and our arrangement removes her.*

Spitzberg (2006) took mediation as his subject before anyone else in this literature, and the hearing
he deserves runs on his own terms rather than as an early stage the field has since passed. He defines
computer-mediated communication as "any human symbolic text-based interaction conducted or facilitated
through digitally-based technologies," then carries the interpersonal architecture into it intact:
motivation, knowledge, skills, context, outcomes. Motivation is "the ratio of approach to avoidance
attitudes, beliefs, and values in a given CMC context" (p. 640). Knowledge is "the cognitive
comprehension of content and procedural processes involved in conducting appropriate and effective
interaction in the computer-mediated context." Four skill clusters carry the behavioral load: attentiveness,
composure, coordination, expressiveness (p. 638). Five facets of context condition all of it (p. 644). Underneath sits a claim about what mediation does: it changes the parameters of
competence and leaves its structure alone, because the parties at either end remain the coordinators.

We have to refuse that claim, and refusing it well means first correcting a reading of Spitzberg that
would make the refusal cheap. His is not a model of a faculty lodged in one head. Competence in his
tradition is produced between people and judged by the partner. "Competent interactants can facilitate
the competence of cointeractants," he writes, and "an incompetent interactant can diminish a normally
competent cointeractant's performance" (p. 650). Messages reach that judgment already filtered through
receivers' expectations for messages in the medium. The counterpart is no late addition here. She is the model's instrument of
measurement. Five outcome criteria close it, and she administers two of them directly: appropriateness
is "perceived legitimacy or fit of a message to the context," and satisfaction is "positive affect
associated with fulfillment of positively valenced expectancies" (p. 648).

That is what makes the test sharp. Take a participant who is competent on every dimension Spitzberg
specifies — motivated, media-knowledgeable, attentive, composed, coordinated, expressive — and place
her in an arrangement where a system reads her work and her counterpart's and commits a determination
binding on both. Her competence has not fallen. The damage runs deeper into the model than that: her
counterpart cannot perceive the message, judge its legitimacy, or register an affective response to it,
so the two criteria that would score her have nobody to administer them. The arrangement has not
lowered her score. It has removed the instrument.

Spitzberg reported the model's empirical trouble himself, which is more than most construct papers
manage. Negatively worded items attenuated his subscales, the context and message factors proved less
multidimensional than he had drawn them, and a factor analysis of all items returned "four reliable
factors that roughly parallel motivation, knowledge, skills, and outcomes" instead of the structure he
proposed (p. 651). "Research on the CMC competence model is nascent," he wrote, in the article
proposing it.

What algorithmacy takes from him is the evaluative axis. Our three operations say what a participant
does; they do not say what doing it well amounts to, and appropriateness and effectiveness remain the
best available answer to that question. What we cannot take is the arrangement those criteria
presuppose — a receiver at the far end of a channel, reachable, whose judgment is the measure.

## Specimen 2 — Algorithmic Competency

*Currently 311 words. Below, 570. Developed on **the instrument**: the discrimination is available
only to a reader who has the twelve items open, and item 6 is worth more to us than item 11.*

Zhou, Lei, Liu, Huang, and Hou (2025) built the first validated scale in this family, and they built it
the way our own programme intends to. Ninety-nine semi-structured interviews with Chinese ride-hailing
drivers and food-delivery couriers generated fourteen candidate items; expert content validation cut
them; an exploratory factor analysis at N = 275 and a confirmatory analysis at N = 213 settled a
second-order four-factor model; a validity study at N = 230 and a three-wave design at N = 225 placed
the construct in a nomological net of antecedents and consequences. Twelve items survived, three per
dimension, at an overall reliability of .85. They define algorithmic competency as workers'
"understanding of platform algorithms that assign and evaluate their work and their ability to adapt to
and navigate those algorithms."

A hearing that files this as knowledge of how the system works has read the label and not the items.
Three of the four dimensions measure something other than knowledge. Understanding covers comprehension
of algorithmic management; Embracing measures willingness to trust its efficiency; Leveraging measures
strategic use of its rules for personal advantage; Remediating measures a worker's capacity to repair
what it gets wrong. Their discriminant work deserves saying plainly, because it is more careful than
most: digital competence correlates at .37 with the scale overall but at .04, not significant, with
Embracing — a null they predicted, on the grounds that digital-competence measures omit attitude.

So the question was never whether the counterpart is absent from their instrument. She is in it, in
four distinct roles. Not one of them makes her a party to the competency. Item 8 puts her behind a
score: "I proactively explore AM rules to minimize negative customer feedback." The object of that verb
is the rules. Item 11 names the triad's own mechanism inside a belief: "I think the platform AM
is highly efficient, such as in customers–workers matching." That item sits in the dimension
correlating weakest with the rest, and it asks nothing about any capacity toward the person matched.
Customer-oriented service behaviour enters their structural model as an outcome variable on Peccei and
Rosenthal's (1997) measure, so in their own design the counterpart matters empirically, gets measured,
and still sits outside the construct.

The fourth role is the one that matters most to us. Remediating item 6 reads "I can use platform APP
functions (i.e., reporting exceptions and appealing) to resolve vulnerabilities in AM," and the
interview behind it is explicit: a courier who receives an unfair customer rating uses recordings as
evidence to appeal to the algorithm. This is the only place in the rival scale where a worker contests
an outcome. The contest runs to the apparatus. The customer is the source of the grievance and
never its addressee.

A worker can therefore score at ceiling across all twelve items — she understands dispatch, trusts the
matching, exploits the rules for the orders she wants, and repairs the system's failures through WeChat
groups and the appeals function — and hold no capacity whatever for working out what the person on the
other side of the match wanted, or for making herself legible to that person. Nothing in the scale would
detect the failure. The counterpart enters their design only once the construct is finished, as a
dependent variable, and a thirteenth item would not move her, because the definition names two terms:
worker and algorithm.

What we take from Zhou and colleagues is their derivation discipline, and one empirical finding we have
been asserting without support. The antecedents of algorithmic competency in their data are social:
peer support, moderated by collectivism. That is the acquisition story our own implicit-learning
property needs and has never had.

## The remaining five — axis, and the unused material each will use

**Human–Machine Communication** → developed on **the objection from inside the field**. This is the
weakest hearing in the section and the easiest to fix, because the answer is already carded. Gibbs,
Kirkwood, Fang and Wilkenfeld (2021, `cards/gibbs2021.md`, full text) argue that HMC's individualism is
its defect and that institutional structure has to enter the account — the exact objection our hearing
raises, made in HMC's own journal. It does not defeat us, and the reason is precise enough to state:
structuration adds *rules and resources*, an institutional third term, and not a *party*. Their triad is
human, machine, structure; ours is worker, counterpart, system. Fortunati and Edwards (2020,
`cards/fortunati2020.md`, full text) supply the field's founding definition in the founders' own words,
and set the tone the hearing should take — a field asking a different question well, not a field that
missed something. Roughly 192 → 480 words.

**AI-Mediated Communication** → developed on **the parameters losing their referent**. The steelman
calls this the most dangerous hearing in §3 and it is: AI-MC is a three-party arrangement with two humans
in it by its own definition, so any argument from position-counting fails against a reviewer who works
here. The hearing should run entirely on standing, and the demonstration is theirs: take a person
competent at AI-MC and put her in front of a system delegated by neither party, and role orientation has
no role to name, optimization goal has no principal, autonomy is measured against a supervision that does
not exist. Switch the quoted definition to the p. 90 body version ("between people"), which pre-empts the
flattened-triad objection in our own quotation. Add Hancock and colleagues' own admission that disclosure
is the exception and the receiver's acceptance is assumed. Roughly 196 → 470 words.

**AI Literacy** → developed on **what the taxonomy inherited**. Competency 10 is already handled well.
What is missing is the transmission: Ng, Leung, Chu and Qiao (2021) compressed seventeen competencies into
four cells, and every subsequent instrument inherited that architecture, so the omission propagated
through the measures rather than being a feature of one 2020 paper. `steelmans/longmagerko2020.md` is the
longest in the set (1,594 words) and almost none of it is on the page. Cite by competency number
throughout; the ACM table remains unacquired. Roughly 260 → 500 words.

**Reactivity under Opaque Evaluation** → developed on **the near-miss that stops one step short**.
Rahman's five components are a proof of closure, not a list — "even if a freelancer makes headway in
uncovering one or a few components of the evaluation algorithm, other facets will remain opaque." His
fifth component, who influences the evaluation, is the closest any neighbour comes to our position, and
the distinction to draw is fine: not knowing which party moved the score is a problem about the score,
and ours is a problem about the party. Two unused facts earn their place. Online Appendix E records, per
client, whether that client knew how her actions affected freelancers' scores — he measured the
counterpart's ignorance of her own effect and left it out of the theory, because his theory is about
control. And reaching the counterpart meant leaving the arrangement, against a user agreement barring
off-platform payment for two years on pain of deactivation. His dependence-and-setback explanation is a
rival to any variance claim of ours and the hearing should concede it. Roughly 232 → 520 words.

**Gig Literacies** → developed on **the counterpart being in the sample**. Sutherland, Jarrahi, Dunn and
Nelson interviewed nineteen clients and walked the platform from both sides, one author as client and one
as freelancer (pp. 461–463). Their fifth literacy is *building relationships*, their heading and not our
gloss, and P10 wanted "a rapport and a little longer lasting relationship" for repeat business (p. 468).
Then the turn, which is the whole hearing: their most counterpart-heavy literacy is a strategy for
escaping mediation. P39, unable to recover a depressed score, took established clients off-platform.
They draw the Uber contrast themselves at p. 470. We take enrollment without standing, and situated
learning of an opaque rating. Roughly 243 → 500 words.
