   score, not just the submitter's progression. Mutual bindingness — the construct's differentiator

three most damaging findings — mutual bindingness, algorithmic assignment, a rule that itself moves

### The derivation, shown on the page

The paper claims at least eight times that the three operations "derive deductively" from the
triad's structural properties, and the derivation is never performed: opacity → *interpreting*,
adaptivity → *keeping track*, and *specifying intent* is grounded in channel constraint — a fourth,
unnamed property — while bindingness, the property doing all the differentiating work, generates no
operation. Replace the assertion at line 204 with the actual property → deficit → operation mapping,
in one paragraph. Two candidate fixes; **the rewriter chooses**:

- **(a) Name channel constraint as a fourth structural property** and show the full four-property
  mapping in a small exhibit (one paragraph plus a four-row table). Honest, but it changes the
  property count that recurs through the scope conditions.
- **(b) Derive *specifying intent* from bindingness directly** — because the verdict binds, every
  message is simultaneously evidence before a verdict, so the actor must encode for judge and
  counterpart at once. Org theory rewrite 2 gives the full paragraph ("opacity withholds the rule,
  so the actor must reconstruct it; bindingness turns every message into evidence…; adaptivity
  unsettles whatever the actor has learned…"), and hostile independently sketches the same reading.
  This keeps three properties and three operations, gives bindingness its operation, and dissolves
  the orphan problem in one stroke.

Both org theory and hostile lean (b); the synthesis leaves the choice open, and so does this
outline. Either way: stop writing "deductively" anywhere the mapping is not visible, and state the
honest relation to RQ4 (hostile R6): each operation answers one deficit the triad imposes; whether
the three exhaust the deficits — or attribution stands as a fourth — is what RQ4 tests. *Repairs org
theory Finding 2; hostile Reviewer 2, Finding 6.*

## The prose problem, stated once

The manuscript has almost none of the classic slop lexicon — no "delve," no "tapestry," no
"Crucially," zero agentless "has been shown" throat-clears. Its slop is uniformity of density, and
its deficits run *opposite* to the generic anti-AI checklist. The rewriter must not apply that
checklist in reverse.

- **Em-dashes: 0.7–0.8 per 1,000 words against the house target of ~3/1,000. This is underuse —
  add, don't cut.** The dozen-plus coined terms (*interpreting*, *specifying intent*, *keeping
  track*, *coordinative co-optation*, bindingness, the invisible cage…) mostly get flat
  separate-sentence definitions where a gloss-welding em-dash appositive would save words and hand
  the next sentence a subject. Weld the gloss at each term's first use. The thirteen existing
  dashes are all legitimate; keep them.
- **Gerund-subject + "requires/demands" openers: ~50 instances** ("Evaluating candidate constructs…
  requires analyzing," "Demarcating this theoretical boundary requires satisfying," "Establishing
  construct and discriminant validity requires embedding"…). One per section is a genre move;
  fifty is a drumbeat that evicts every named agent. Convert roughly half to named-agent or
  first-person subjects — I evaluate, I formalize, I predict, the construct derives.
- **Zero authorial "I" in a solo-authored paper** ("we" ×19–20). House calibration: solo work takes
  "I" at the analytic moves, 4–8 per 1,000 words. Convert the methods-section passives first —
  that is where hiding the agent is most self-undermining for an insider-action-research paper
  whose central methodological claim is that the researcher's position is an asset. (If a
  committee convention mandates "we," flag the choice consciously; do not keep it by default.)
- **Zero genuinely short declarative sentences in ~13,000 body words** — 483 body sentences, mean
  26.9 words, no punctures. Add short declaratives at the three or four highest-leverage verdict
  moments the reviews already named, and only there: after line 100's build ("An editor is not a
  judge."), after line 128 ("The scale cannot see the counterpart because the construct never let
  her in."), after line 172's four conditions ("No candidate meets all four."), optionally after
  line 27's boundary statement ("That position stands empty."). Do not sprinkle.
- **"Structural/structurally": 82–97 occurrences; "architecture": 43–50.** Cut roughly half of
  each — the intensifier uses ("structurally definitive," "structural impasse") where the sentence
  keeps its truth-value without the word; keep the analytic uses. Replace "architecture" with the
  plain word each instance means (design, gate, rule, model) in at least half its runs.
- **The definitional formula "opaque, adaptive… evaluates and binds both parties": ~18–23 runs.**
  The full formula earns one verbatim run per major section; after that, rename the thread entity
  (the triad → the gate → the arrangement → it). Same for "vacancy" ×6 and "rather than" ×77 —
  keep the contrasts a reader would actually reach for (~a third), and where one contrast recurs
  across sections (capacity-not-fairness appears five times), keep the strongest instance (lines
  257–259) and let the rest assert the positive claim.
- **The anchor drought**: the construct core's three operation definitions touch no case; the
  repair is specified in the construct section above (the pilot's LLM-routing incident as the
  rival-outcome anchor for *specifying intent*; the classroom pull request for the rest).
- **Self-narrated rigor**, four instances (lines 212, 261, 293, 553): state the thing, cut the
  sentence about stating it. All four are absorbed by repairs above.
