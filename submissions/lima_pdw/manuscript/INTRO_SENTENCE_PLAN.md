## Why the flagged sentence failed

Ours: “Paper 1 of this dissertation asked the same six questions of those
three ways and of two further ones.”

Okhuysen never writes a sentence that (a) introduces a new actor, (b) names a
method, (c) points at three already-named things, and (d) opens two unnamed
things, all at once. Their sentences do one job. A novice who has just learned
hierarchy / market / network is then hit with “six questions,” “those three
ways,” and “two further ones” before either further one exists. The next
sentence tries to rescue the six questions. That is the tell: a sentence that
needs a rescue sentence was already overloaded.

## Checks (every slot, before the next slot opens)

1. **Model shape.** Quote their sentence. Ours must have the same number of
   jobs (usually one). If theirs has one comma-appositive, ours may. If theirs
   does not introduce two unnamed objects, ours may not.
2. **Context.** Read the sentence before and the planned sentence after. Ours
   must open on the item the previous sentence ended on.
3. **Novice.** Circle every noun. If the reader has not been taught it, rewrite
   or split. “Six questions,” “two further ones,” “construct,” “form,”
   “integrative account,” and “coordinative co-optation” are untaught until a
   prior sentence has taught them.
4. **Plot.** The sentence must be a step in the author’s plot, not a leftover
   from an older draft (seventh row, residual variance, companion review).
5. **Speed.** If more than one slot is drafted in the same turn, the turn is
   invalid. One slot per turn unless the author asks to continue.
