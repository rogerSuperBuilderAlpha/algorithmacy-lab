# The Competency a Form Demands — section outline

Working outline for the Lima manuscript (`dissertation/current/paper2/PAPER.md`).
Written 19 August 2026 against that draft, the definition agreed the same day, and
Okhuysen and Bechky (2009) as the model of how a section states what it does.
This file proposes; it does not edit the manuscript.

---

## Abstract

Coordination research now has a clear account of what hierarchy, markets, and networks
deliver. It has not asked what each form requires of the person being coordinated. A
companion review treats co-optation as a fourth form and stops at the arrangement. This
paper takes up the next question and names the requirement under co-optation
*algorithmacy*: the ability to interpret, specify intent through, and keep track of an
opaque, adaptive intermediary, so that a person can take part in coordination that runs
through a system which reads both parties and commits decisions neither of them
controls. Two of the things the form withholds can be partly supplied by the person.
Accountability cannot. The study that would test this account is underway. This draft
reports the theory and the method, not completed findings.

**Keywords:** coordination forms, algorithmic management, communication competency,
platform work, co-optation, organizational form

**Note for reviewers of the accepted abstract.** Four changes: the coordination argument
comes first; there is no finished empirical evidence; the third facet is tracking change
over time, not tool use; the study site is Hult, not GauntleTT.

---

## 1. The problem

Equally placed people, using the same systems at the same intensity, get very different
outcomes. Hours and tools do not explain the gap, and neither do skill and task once
those are allowed for. The companion review described the arrangement. This paper asks
what the person must be able to do inside it.

We name that requirement **algorithmacy**.

Literacy is the ability to read and write, so that a person can take part in
communication that runs through text. Numeracy is the ability to use and interpret
quantitative information, so that a person can take part in situations that run through
number. Oracy is the ability to speak and listen, so that a person can take part in
communication that runs through speech. **Algorithmacy is the ability to interpret,
specify intent through, and keep track of an opaque, adaptive intermediary, so that a
person can take part in coordination that runs through a system which reads both parties
and commits decisions neither of them controls.**

Literacy, numeracy, and oracy are competences for a medium. Algorithmacy is a competence
for a coordination. The medium is the algorithm. The participation is with another human
being, through that algorithm.

Three things follow. A new question added to a published anatomy needs a rule. The
question has to be asked of all four forms, not only of the fourth. And the construct
has to be derived from what the form withholds, not collected from what workers happen
to report.

---

## 2. What the companion review established

That review compares coordination forms on six dimensions and finds that every familiar
form assumes the coordinated person has standing. The employee can be asked. The buyer
can walk away. The network partner chose the relationship. Platforms enroll people
without that standing.

Standing is what the arrangement gives or withholds. Competency is what the person must
supply. We do not reopen the review. We use it as the frame. The seventh question still
stands if some of its grades later move.

---

## 3. A seventh question

A new dimension can be added only if five conditions hold. The older literatures already
imply it. It is not a relabeling of an existing cell. It is asked of all four forms. It
can fail in each form. The published grades are left untouched.

Integrating conditions ask what the *form* must deliver. The seventh question asks what
the *person* must supply when the form does not. A claim about arrangements is tested
against arrangements. A claim about persons is tested against persons.

---

## 4. How much each form requires

The market requires almost nothing, because the public price does the work. Hierarchy
requires little, and is built that way: rules and decision premises exist so the
subordinate does not have to judge everything. Networks require a great deal. That
requirement already has a name — network competence — though the studies that named it
measure firms. The useful precedent is the question, not the unit of analysis.

Co-optation requires the most, and the coordinated person’s competency has not been
named. Related scales of “algorithmic competency” measure the person against the system.
They do not include the other human. The form presents as a market and costs the person
more than hierarchy does.

---

## 5. What co-optation withholds

Any coordination has to produce common understanding, predictability, and
accountability.

Under co-optation, understanding is blocked in both directions: she cannot see what the
other party meant, and she cannot make herself understood except through the system.
Predictability runs one way: the system forecasts her; she does not get a stable rule.
Accountability is missing as a relationship: she answers for everything; nothing answers
to her.

She can get better at reconstructing the first two. She cannot create a party that owes
her an explanation. That limit is the paper’s main result.

---

## 6. Two routes

Some withheld conditions can be shifted onto the person. Accountability cannot. Greater
fluency is additional unpaid coordination work. It does not repair the form. Whether
that rise is welcome depends on facts about the arrangement that this construct does not
measure.

---

## 7. Three facets

A facet is admitted only if it is something the person supplies because a condition was
withheld.

Withheld understanding yields two: reading the situation (what the system and the unseen
other party are doing), and getting intent through (making what she meant survive the
system). Withheld predictability yields a third: noticing when the rule moves without
being told. Accountability yields none. Managing the tools is a technical skill. It
answers an affordance of the software, not a withholding, and it is not a facet.

The three operations in the definition — interpret, specify, keep track — are these
three facets. The definition’s closing clause is why accountability never becomes a
fourth.

---

## 8. Competency, not skill and not capability

This is not a capability. Capabilities belong to the party that owns the routine, and
this party owns nothing. It is not a skill that can be taught as a rule, because the
rule is hidden. It is a competency: a way of understanding and acting in a role, learned
by taking part, tied to the form rather than to a particular tool.

The family it belongs to is literacy, numeracy, and oracy. Each is a competence for
taking part in a practice that runs through a medium. Algorithmacy is the member of that
family indexed to algorithmically mediated coordination.

Measure it on people. Interpret it against the arrangement.

---

## 9. Why neighbouring constructs are not enough

Each neighbour fails one side of the definition.

Computer-mediated communication treats the system as a channel. The two humans remain
the coordinators. Nothing in that picture requires the channel to interpret them or to
decide for them.

Human–machine communication treats the system as a partner. The relationship has two
positions: person and machine.

AI-mediated communication treats the system as an agent working for the speaker. The
counterpart is still reached by a message the speaker, or her agent, sent.

Algorithm sensemaking treats the system as something to be understood. A person can have
a perfect working theory of the algorithm and still not have coordinated with anyone.

AI literacy treats the system as something to be known. Knowing how a recommender works
does not get a counterpart to receive what you meant.

Algorithmic competency, as Zhou, Lei, Liu, Huang and Hou define it, is “understanding of
platform algorithms that assign and evaluate work, and the ability to adapt to and
navigate those algorithms.” The other human appears in their study as an outcome, not
inside the competence.

Gig literacies come closest. They are built from what workers report doing. We derive
the parts from what the form withholds, which is why there are three and why
accountability is not one of them.

Adding items to an AI-literacy or algorithmic-competency scale will not produce
algorithmacy. The missing piece is a position, not a variable.

---

## 10. How it is learned, and how it is distributed

People acquire it by doing the work, not by taking a class. There is no rule to teach.
What a course can convey is the tool and the interface. What it cannot convey is what
this gate rewards this month.

It is uneven among people in the same position. That unevenness is what connects the
construct to the opening puzzle. If the competency were uniform, it would explain none
of the variance that motivated the account.

---

## 11. The study

The program is a setting in which people learn by doing. They submit work, receive a
verdict without a full explanation, infer what was wanted, and try again. It is not
classroom instruction and not one-to-one tutoring. The idea was formed in one cohort
(GauntleTT). The study is run in another (Hult). Formation bears on whether the items
say what practitioners mean. It is not evidence of the construct’s structure.

We interview participants about four things. How they build a working theory of the
gate. Whether reading the situation and getting intent through appear as different
activities. Who notices silent rule changes, and how that noticing travels. What they
do when an outcome seems wrong, whom they approach, and what happens. If someone can
obtain a real answer from the gate by individual skill alone, with no forum involved,
the claim in section 5 is wrong.

We describe this design and the present state of fieldwork. We do not report findings.
The responses are incomplete.

---

## 12. Limits

The three facets are derived, not demonstrated. Alternative explanations have been
named, not ruled out. The seventh question is a second way of carving material the
companion review already called provisional. This paper does not measure a worker. The
study that would test the argument has not been analyzed.

A later study that measured the settled columns directly could move the ordinal
reading. Whether a platform could deliver accountability and keep the rest of what it
has is a design question this paper does not take up.
