# The Competency a Form Demands: Algorithmacy and the Co-optation Column

**Roger B. Hunt III** · Bentley University · rhunt@bentley.edu

*Dissertation Paper 2. Full paper for the Organization Studies / Organization Theory Paper
Development Workshop, Universidad de Piura UDEP, Lima, 5–7 October 2026. A revised version goes to
Paris, February 2027.*

---

## Abstract

Coordination forms are compared on what they deliver and never on what they demand. Hierarchy,
market and network have each been described by the operation that secures them, the conditions they
run on, the instruments that carry them and the ways they fail. None has been asked what it requires
of the party it coordinates. This paper adds that question to the anatomy of a coordination form and
answers it for all four columns, including the three that have never been asked. The answer is
ordinal, and the low end carries the argument. The market demands almost nothing, because the public
price supplies understanding and predictability without a private channel. Hierarchy economizes on
judgment by design. Networks demand a great deal, and network competence has been a named construct
for three decades. Co-optation demands the most and nobody has named it. This paper names it
algorithmacy and derives its structure from what the form withholds: where a coordination form fails
to deliver an integrating condition, that condition is displaced onto the party it was withheld
from, and where no institution supplies it, that party supplies it privately. Two of the three
integrating conditions can be supplied that way. One cannot. That division sets a structural limit
on what naming a competency can accomplish, and it is the paper's main result. The closing section
sets out the qualitative study that would test the account. **The instrument is built, approved and
unfielded at the time of writing, and nothing here rests on a response from a participant.**

**Keywords:** coordination forms · algorithmic management · communication competency · platform work
· co-optation · organizational form

# 1. The Column That Is a Generation Short

A driver in London learns what the app rewards by watching what it pays. She has no rulebook, no
supervisor to ask, and no colleague whose account of the system she can check against her own,
because the work is dispatched one trip at a time across a workforce that never assembles. Two
drivers on the same platform, in the same city, in the same week, earn differently and quit at
different rates, and the difference does not track the hours they work or the tools they use.

That variance is well documented and badly explained. Workers using the same systems at the same
intensity experience sharply different outcomes, and the volume of technology used predicts little
of it. Something else is doing the work.

A companion review grades co-optation as a fourth coordination form beside hierarchy, market and
network, on six dimensions drawn from what the three settled literatures answer. It fills one cell
outright and finds the other five occupied but incomplete — in its own vocabulary, a form a
generation short of settlement. That review takes the coordination form as its unit. It says what
the form does to the parties it binds, and it stops there, because the six questions it asks are all
questions about the arrangement.

This paper asks a seventh, and asks it of all four columns. **What competency does a coordination
form demand of the party it coordinates?** The question turns out to be latent in every one of the
three settled literatures and named as a construct in only one of them. Answering it for co-optation
means naming something the platform literature describes constantly and has never consolidated: the
competency a worker needs in order to coordinate, through a system that will not explain itself,
with a counterpart she may never meet.

The paper names it **algorithmacy**, derives its structure from what the form withholds, and is
precise about the part of the withholding no competency can repair.

Three things follow, in order. Adding a dimension to a published anatomy requires a rule, and §3 sets
one with five conditions the addition has to meet. Filling the row requires grading all four columns
rather than announcing that the fourth demands a lot, and §4 does that. And deriving the construct
requires saying where its parts come from, which §5 through §7 do by reading them off what the form
fails to deliver.

The result is a competency with three facets and a hole where a fourth would be. That hole is the
paper's main contribution and its main constraint. It marks the place where naming a worker's
capacity stops being useful, and it arrives from the structure of the argument rather than from
caution about how the argument might be read.

# 2. What Paper 1 Settled, and the Carving It Declared Provisional

The companion review builds an anatomy: six dimensions on which any coordination form can be
described, derived from works that place two or more forms on named dimensions — Powell (1990),
Williamson (1991), Bradach and Eccles (1989), Ouchi (1980), Demil and Lecocq (2006), Grandori and
Soda (1995), Adler (2001), Kolbjørnsrud (2018) and Jacobides, Cennamo and Gawer (2018) — together
with the accounts of what any coordination must deliver in Thompson (1967), Malone and Crowston
(1994), Okhuysen and Bechky (2009) and Puranam, Alexy and Reitzig (2014). All of it was fixed before
any co-optation source was coded.

**Mechanism** is the operation that secures the form and the standing of the party that operation
reaches. **Integrating conditions** are what the mechanism has to deliver in its own terms, following
Okhuysen and Bechky's (2009) three: accountability, predictability, common understanding.
**Antecedents** are the conditions favouring one mechanism over the others. **Constitutive
instruments** are the mechanism made concrete. **Failure modes** are what goes wrong once a mechanism
is pushed past its range. **Empirical signatures** are how a researcher spots the form in data
without anybody naming the mechanism.

Three rules keep the cells apart, and one firewall matters more than the rest: anything that sorts
transactions among forms belongs to antecedents, while the operation in force once the sorting is
done belongs to mechanism. One object may appear in more than one cell so long as each appearance
answers a different question.

The review grades on a three-point scale. *Filled* means the form states the cell's content in its
own terms and cited canonical work settles it. *Populated* means real research occupies the cell with
one identifiable step outstanding. *Open* means the dimension is located and nobody has supplied its
content.

Its verdict on co-optation — the form Stark and Pais (2020) named and Stark and Vanden Broeck (2024)
developed — is deliberately modest. Mechanism grades filled and provisional: metered
direction across the ownership boundary, secured by a forfeitable non-portable stake in place of a
wage, on a party enrolled without delegated standing. The other five grade populated.

Two features of that review matter here more than its verdict.

The first is a limitation it states about itself. **The six dimensions are one carving of this
material among several.** The review says so plainly and names the standing alternative. That
sentence is the warrant for what follows, because a carving declared provisional invites a second
one.

The second is a move the review makes and does not generalize. Reading the three settled literatures,
it finds a premise none of them states and each of them depends on: every mechanism runs on a
standing the coordinated party holds. Simon's (1997) employee granted the zone of acceptance. The
market's price-taker is free to walk away (Hayek, 1945). The network's partners chose each other
(Powell, 1990; Granovetter, 1985). Employment, contract and membership each stipulate that standing
in advance — Coase (1937) and Williamson (1975) on the first, Macneil (1978) on the second, Barnard
(1938) on the third — which is why the three settled columns never had to write it down. The review takes that premise and makes it a requirement, and holds all four
columns to it, **including the three that had never been asked**.

That is the move this paper repeats. Standing is what the coordinated party *holds*. Competency is
what the coordinated party must *supply*. A companion review added the first to the anatomy. The
second is added here.

The parallel is worth drawing out, because it also shows the limits of the borrowing. Standing is a
fact about the arrangement: employment confers it, a contract confers it, membership confers it, and
a platform withholds it. Whether a particular driver is skilled or unskilled changes nothing about
whether she can revise the terms on which she is assigned, priced and graded. Competency is a fact
about the party: it varies across people the arrangement treats identically, and that variance is
the only reason the seventh row explains anything the other six do not.

The two are related by direction of causation, and the direction matters. Withheld standing does not
produce competency; it produces a *demand* for competency, which some parties meet and others do not.
A form that stipulated standing in advance would generate no such demand, which is why the settled
columns' entries in the seventh row are small. The demand is the shadow the withholding casts, and
its size is what the row measures.

A reader who has not met the companion review may reasonably ask why its verdict should be taken as
given here. It is not, and nothing in this paper requires it. The seventh row needs the six
dimensions as a *frame* and needs the fourth column to be a candidate worth asking about. Both survive
if the review's grades are wrong in detail. Should co-optation turn out to fill more cells than the
review allows, the seventh row still asks what it demands of the party it coordinates, and the answer
in §4 does not move. Should it turn out to be no distinct form at all, the row collapses to three
columns and the question remains live for those.

One further feature of the companion review sets up §5. Its integrating-conditions cell does not read
like the other five. Where mechanism states an operation and instruments name artifacts, integrating
conditions for co-optation states a *partition*: the platform captures the coordination dividend and
the worker takes its inverse. A partition is a description of who ends up holding what, and a
description of that shape invites the question of what the holder does with it. That is the question
this paper takes up.

# 3. A Seventh Question, and the Rule for Admitting It

Adding a dimension to a template after its grades are published is a serious move, and the review
warns against it directly: a template reshaped after the coding would no longer be a test the
candidate can fail. That warning has to be answered rather than stepped around.

It is answered by the review's own limitation. The dimensions are declared one carving among several,
and the warrant for the template is stated as its utility. A second carving, declared as such, is
what that limitation invites. What it does not license is a carving whose only empty cell is the
candidate's, admitted by an author who then fills it. So the admission runs under a rule with five
conditions, all of which must hold.

**The question must be latent in all three settled literatures.** A dimension only the candidate's
literature asks is a dimension built to be won.

**It must be distinct from every existing cell under the firewall.** A relabelling of integrating
conditions adds nothing.

**It must be applied to all four columns.** A dimension graded only for the candidate measures the
candidate's advocate.

**It must have a declared way of failing in every column**, written before any column is graded.

**The frozen six grades must be untouched.** The seventh question is new for all four columns and
moves no existing cell. This condition answers the timing objection: nothing already published is
re-scored, and a reader who rejects the seventh row entirely still has the six-cell anatomy exactly
as the review left it.

Take the conditions one at a time, because each does work.

**Latency in all three settled literatures** is what stops the row being built for its winner. Every
one of the three has an account of what its coordinated party must be able to do, and none of the
three treats that account as a dimension. Weber's official holds a credential. Hayek's price-taker
reads a price. The network partner judges reliability and invests in a relation. Those capacities are
described in every case and compared in none, which is the definition of a latent dimension. Had only
the platform literature spoken about worker capacity, the row would be a device.

**Distinctness under the firewall** is the condition most easily failed, and §5 is where the paper
either meets it or does not. The firewall sorts selection claims from operation claims. The seventh
row belongs to neither: it asks about a *party*, where the six ask about an *arrangement*. Its nearest
neighbour is integrating conditions, and the two are close enough that the distinction has to be
stated in a form a reader can test — which is done at the end of this section.

**Symmetric application** is why §4 grades all four columns rather than announcing that co-optation
demands a lot. A row where three cells read "not applicable" and one carries the argument is not a
dimension of an anatomy. It is a claim wearing a table's clothes.

**A declared way of failing** is what makes the row falsifiable per column. The seventh row fails for
a form when no capacity of the coordinated party varies with outcomes under that form — when
everybody who is in the arrangement can do what the arrangement requires, so the question has no
purchase. For the market this comes close to being true, and §4 says so.

**Untouched grades** is the condition that answers the timing objection, and it is worth being exact
about what it protects. Nothing already published is re-scored. A reader who rejects the seventh row
entirely still has the six-cell anatomy exactly as the review left it, with its one filled cell and
its five populated ones. The seventh row is additive, and additions can be refused without cost to
what they were added to.

One distinction has to stay clean or the row collapses into the cell above it. Integrating conditions
ask **what the mechanism must deliver**. The seventh row asks **what the coordinated party must
supply when the mechanism does not deliver it**. The first is a property of the form. The second is a
property of the party the form reaches.

Here is the test. Take any claim and ask who could be wrong about it. "Hierarchy delivers
predictability by rule" is wrong if hierarchies turn out to be unpredictable — a claim about
arrangements, checkable against arrangements. "This worker detects rule changes better than that one"
is wrong if the two turn out to detect them equally — a claim about parties, checkable against
parties. They are related the way a debt is related to whoever ends up paying it, and confusing them
is the easiest way to read this paper wrongly.

# 4. How Much Competency Does Each Form Demand?

The seventh row is ordinal, and the interesting values sit at the low end.

**The market demands almost nothing, and it is settled at almost nothing.** The companion review is
explicit: the market pays its integrating debt cheaply, because predictability and common
understanding arrive with the public price and need no separate channel. Hayek's (1945) argument is that the price system is a marvel precisely because of how little any
participant must know. The price is a
sufficient statistic, and a trader who understands nothing about why it moved can still act on it
correctly. Accountability arrives through contract and reputation. A buyer needs to read a price and
enforce a contract, and both capacities are ordinary. This cell is filled with low-magnitude content,
and the low magnitude is the finding.

An objection is worth meeting here. Markets with asymmetric information demand a great deal of the
uninformed party — Akerlof's (1970) buyer facing a used car needs judgment the price does not supply. That
is right, and it is why the cell reads *almost* nothing rather than nothing. The demand rises exactly
as the market departs from the conditions that make the price sufficient, which is a point in favour
of reading the row as ordinal.

**Hierarchy demands little, and economizes on what it demands.** Weber's (1978) office holder needs
credentialled competence, and the credential is a real requirement with a real literature behind it.
But Simon's (1997) account of decision premises describes a form built so the subordinate need not
judge:
predictability comes from rule, common understanding from shared premises, accountability from the
chain of command. The organization supplies the judgment and the employee supplies acceptance within
a zone. A hierarchy requiring each subordinate to reconstruct the firm's objectives privately would
be a hierarchy that had failed at its own job.

**Networks demand a great deal, and somebody has named it.** Reciprocity, trust and reputation do not
arrive with a price or a rulebook. A partner reads a counterpart, judges reliability, and invests in a
relation whose returns are neither contracted nor commanded. The network literature named that capacity three decades ago and measured it: network competence
(Ritter & Gemünden, 2003), with alliance-management capability beside it (Schreiner, Kale, &
Corsten, 2009). This matters twice over. It shows the seventh question is answerable, and it
supplies the precedent — **this paper does for co-optation what the network literature did for
networks.**

**Co-optation demands the most, and nobody has named it.** The worker reconstructs a rule she cannot
read, from outcomes, against a counterpart she does not meet, through a system that changes without
announcement. The platform literature documents every part of this — folk theories of the algorithm and
anticipatory compliance under opaque evaluation (Rahman, 2021), the information asymmetries that make
reconstruction necessary (Rosenblat & Stark, 2016), the apparatus of algorithmic direction and
evaluation (Kellogg, Valentine, & Christin, 2020), the matching and control functions the platform
combines (Möhlmann, Zalmanson, Henfridsson, & Gregory, 2021), and the rating that cannot be carried
anywhere else (Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen, & Neysen, 2020). It consolidates none of it into a competency.

One contrast sharpens the gap. The *coordinator's* capacity in these arrangements is richly theorized.
Fligstein's (2001) socially skilled actor, framing participation and building the identities that
hold a field together, describes what a platform-as-incumbent does to keep its field settled. The
*coordinated* party's capacity has no such account. **The asymmetry in the literature mirrors the
asymmetry in the form.**

| Form | Competency demanded of the coordinated party | Named as a construct? |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Near zero. The public price supplies understanding and predictability | n/a — settled at low magnitude |
| Hierarchy | Low, and economized by design. Decision premises exist so the subordinate need not judge | Partly, as credentialled competence |
| Network | High. Reading a counterpart, judging reliability, investing without contract | **Yes**, for three decades |
| **Co-optation** | **Highest** | **No** |

Set the row out and a claim appears that the six dimensions cannot state. Co-optation presents as
market-like — no employment, no supervisor, be your own boss — while demanding more of the coordinated
party than hierarchy does. The form advertises the market's cheap integrating debt and charges the
worker privately for what the market supplies free.

That inversion is what makes the row worth adding. On the six dimensions co-optation reads as a
distinct arrangement with its own mechanism and its own instruments, and the reader learns what it
does. On the seventh it reads as an arrangement that costs its coordinated party more than either of
the two forms it superficially resembles, and the reader learns what it costs. Neither reading
replaces the other, and the second is available only once the question is asked.

# 5. What Co-optation Withholds, and the One Thing No Worker Can Supply

A coordination form owes its parties three things. Coordination research converges on accountability,
predictability and common understanding as what any coordinating mechanism has to produce, whatever
the mechanism is. Each settled form pays that debt in its own currency, and pays it to both parties.
Hierarchy delivers accountability by chain of command, predictability by rule, common understanding by
shared premises. The market delivers predictability by price, accountability by contract and
reputation, common understanding by the price itself. The network delivers accountability by
reputation and sanction, predictability by continuing relation, common understanding by shared norms.

The companion review grades co-optation's integrating-conditions cell populated, and its content is a
partition rather than a delivery: the platform captures the coordination dividend and the worker takes
its inverse. Read condition by condition, the partition is uneven in a way that decides the rest of
this paper.

**Common understanding is withheld in both directions.** The interface fixes what an instruction means,
and the counterpart is unmet. The worker cannot ask the rider what he wanted or see the reviewer's
reasoning, so she reads intent from outcomes alone. She also cannot make herself understood except
through whatever channel the system accepts.

**Predictability is withheld and inverted.** The apparatus forecasts her with precision and updates its
own rule without announcing it. The prediction runs one way.

**Accountability is withheld, and here the account changes.** Accountability, on the definition the
companion review adopts, is a forum with standing to demand justification. The worker answers for every
interaction through a record she does not author. Nothing answers to her.

The first two she can do something about. She can reconstruct what a counterpart wants from outcomes,
and she can learn to detect a rule change. Neither reconstruction restores the condition — a private
forecast is not a rule, and a folk theory is not shared understanding — but the work is possible, and
some workers do it far better than others.

**The third she cannot.** No degree of individual skill manufactures a forum. A worker who becomes
expert at rendering account under a schema she did not set has become expert at absorbing its absence,
and answerability has not moved. This is a structural limit rather than a hedge, and §6 and §12 both
follow from it.

The asymmetry deserves stating precisely, because it is the paper's hinge and it is easy to blur.
Predictability and common understanding are **states a party can be in**. A worker either has an
accurate expectation of what will pass review or she does not, and whether she has one depends partly
on her, so private work can move her from one state to the other. Accountability is **a relation
between parties**. It requires a second party who owes an answer, and no amount of work by the first
party creates the second one's obligation. A worker can become better at *predicting* how her appeal
will be received. She cannot, by becoming better at anything, cause the appeal to be received.

Two objections press here and both sharpen the claim rather than defeating it.

*A skilled worker can construct answerability through third parties* — she finds a journalist, a
regulator, a class action. That is right, and it is the institutional route of §6 arriving through
individual effort. What the effort produced is a forum, and the forum is what supplies the condition.
The competency located the route; it did not substitute for what the route reached.

*A worker who reliably gets outcomes reversed has effective answerability, whatever the mechanism.*
This is the serious version and it is exactly what the study in §11 is designed to look for. If a
participant reliably obtains answers through individual skill with no forum involved, the claim in
this section is wrong. The protocol says so before fieldwork rather than after.

# 6. Two Routes, and How They Divide the Withheld Conditions

Put the pieces together and a principle falls out.

> A coordination form's job is to make the integrating conditions available without a private
> competency. Where the mediator withholds one, the condition is not abolished. It is displaced onto
> the party it was withheld from. It may then be supplied institutionally, through accountability
> substitutes whose constitution is independent of the platform. Where those are absent or captured,
> it falls to the individual, and individually supplied it is a competency.

Two clarifications keep the principle honest.

**The private route is one of two, and the literature documents both.** Workers construct the algorithm
socially as they go, bypass it through out-of-app activity, and pool folk theory in forums. Statutory
review, designed appeal and worker forums each claim the answerability a withheld forum forecloses. A
principle recognizing only private supply would describe a subset and call it the set.

**The descent to the worker is motivated, and it is not entailed.** The structural claim is that the
standing a platform withholds does not vary from worker to worker, while the observed outcomes do
vary. A constant cannot explain a variable, so something at a lower level carries the variance — but
that argument licenses only *something*, never *this*. Prior expertise, task mix, hours worked and
general self-efficacy are live rival explanations, and one is directly supported: several of the
studies documenting the outcome variance attribute it to prior skill and report novice-levelling
effects. The design in §11 registers one discriminant test against general self-efficacy and names the
others as what a qualitative study must separate. **This paper does not claim to have defeated them.**

Now the division, which is the paper's main result. The two supply routes are not parallel alternatives
offering a choice. **They divide the withheld conditions between them.** Common understanding and
predictability can be supplied individually, and that is where the competency lives. Accountability can
be supplied only institutionally, and no competency reaches it.

That division does work a general principle would not. It says exactly where naming the competency
helps and exactly where it cannot, and the boundary is structural. The guard against reading this paper
as an argument for training workers to absorb a withheld standing is not a disclaimer at the end. It is
the finding.

One consequence belongs in the same breath. Where the competency is unevenly distributed across
equivalently placed workers, the form converts an unequal private capacity into unequal outcomes and
reports the result as performance. Naming the competency locates that transfer. It does not license it.

The division also predicts where interventions land, which is how it can be wrong. Training and
selection operate on the two conditions a worker can supply, so they should move outcomes on those
and leave accountability untouched. Forums, statutory review and designed appeal operate on the one
she cannot, so they should move accountability and do comparatively little for the other two. **If a
well-designed appeal process turned out to improve workers' ability to read what a gate wants, or if
a training programme turned out to produce answerability, the division is drawn in the wrong place.**
Those are checkable predictions, and they are the reason to state the division as a result rather
than as a caution.

# 7. Algorithmacy: Three Facets, Derived

**Algorithmacy is the competency to coordinate with a human counterpart through an opaque, adaptive
intermediary that commits determinations binding on both parties.**

Its structure is derived rather than asserted, under one rule: **a facet is what a worker does in the
absence of a withheld condition.** Nothing else admits.

| Withheld condition | What a worker can supply | Facet |
|---|---|---|
| Common understanding, inbound | Reconstruct a hidden counterpart's wants from outcomes alone | **Asymmetric interpretation** |
| Common understanding, outbound | Make intent legible in the few signals the system accepts | **Intent specification** |
| Predictability | Detect rule shifts as they happen | **Temporal tracking** |
| Accountability | *Nothing. It cannot be supplied individually* | *none* |

**Asymmetric interpretation** is the work of reconstructing what a counterpart wants when the
counterpart is unmet and the only evidence is what the system did. The driver infers from a rating
what a rider objected to. The cohort member infers from a rejection what a reviewer valued. The
inference runs backward from an outcome to a rule that was never stated, and it is checkable only by
being tried again.

**Intent specification** is the reverse direction: compressing what one means into the few signals a
system will carry. A submission is packaged so the gate reads it correctly; a profile is written so
the matching rule places it where the worker wants to be placed. Something is always lost in the
compression, and part of the competency is knowing what can afford to be lost.

**Temporal tracking** is noticing that the rule has moved when nobody announced it. What passed last
month fails this month, and the worker who detects the shift early acts on a different model of the
system than the worker still operating on last month's.

Common understanding carries two facets because it is bidirectional. Reading what a counterpart wants
and making oneself read correctly are different practices with different occasions and different
failures, and a worker can be expert at one while failing at the other. The driver who has worked out
exactly what the rating rewards, and cannot get a legitimate complaint recorded in a form the system
will process, has the first and lacks the second. Treating them as one facet would predict that the
two travel together, and the everyday observation is that they do not.

Why three and not more is a question the rule answers rather than the author. Each facet exists
because a specific condition was withheld and a worker can do something about it. A candidate facet
with no withheld condition behind it is excluded whatever its intuitive appeal, which is what happens
next.

**Machine orchestration is not a facet, and the cut is the rule working.** Earlier statements of this
construct named it as a fourth component. It answers no withheld condition. It answers a *tool
affordance* — that contemporary generative systems accept delegated and chained subtasks — and an
affordance of one mediator is not a property of a coordination form. It is also a skill in §8's precise
sense: tool-indexed, trainable by instruction, and dated. It was not a competency of a ride-hail driver
in 2019 and will not describe the same behaviour in 2029. A competency for a coordination *form* must
be recognizable across mediators, and the three facets are: the weaver reading a piece rate, the
sharecropper reading a merchant's ledger, and the cohort member reading a review gate are doing one
thing.

Orchestration keeps a job. It is a plausible **behavioural indicator** of the three facets composed —
inferring what a system will accept, compressing intent into a specification, noticing when behaviour
changes under you — and belongs in the validation design rather than in the construct.

A construct that shows its own rejected candidate is more credible than one that does not, and the cut
is what demonstrates the admission rule has teeth.

**On convergence, a disclosure.** The three facets appear in an earlier lab statement of this construct
and in the instrument built from it. Those are one lineage transcribed twice, and the derivation above
came third. The claim here is **coherence across levels**, and not independent corroboration by three
routes. The instrument's three scales — reading feedback back to the rule, packaging work for a
narrow format, noticing unannounced change — measure what the derivation predicts, which is worth
something and is worth less than an independent replication would be.

The honest version of what that coherence buys is modest and specific. It shows the derivation
produces a structure somebody could operationalize without straining, and that the operationalization
somebody already built lands on the same three parts. It does not show that three is the right
number, that the parts are separable in practice, or that the derivation would have produced this
structure in hands that had never seen the instrument. Only the first of those is settled by
argument; the other two need data.

What would count as independent corroboration is worth naming so that a later claim can be held to
it. A second researcher, given the withholding analysis in §5 and no sight of the instrument,
deriving the same facet structure. Or a factor analysis on a fielded scale recovering three
correlated factors matching the three facets, with the accountability items — if anyone wrote them —
failing to cohere. Neither has happened.

# 8. Competency, Not Skill and Not Capability

The choice among these three words is forced rather than stylistic, because each carries a different
commitment about where the thing sits and what varies.

**Not a capability.** The dynamic-capabilities literature (Teece, Pisano, & Shuen, 1997) works at the
firm level and asks what a firm can do that rivals cannot, as the resource-based view before it did
(Barney, 1991). Its microfoundations treat the individual as a component of a firm-level
capacity. The explanandum here is variance among workers facing an identical structural constant,
which is a different question at a different level. The decisive objection is possession rather than
level: **a capability is held by the party that owns the routine, and this party owns nothing.** The
platform holds the ratings, the graph and the rule. Calling algorithmacy a capability attributes to the
worker a possession the form is defined by denying her, so the word contradicts the theory it would be
applied to.

**Not a skill.** Skill is trainable by instruction, and the structural reason algorithmacy is not is
that the rule is undisclosed. There is nothing to instruct. What can be taught is a tool, and the tool
is not the competency. Skill is also tool-indexed and dates with the tool: prompt chaining in 2023 and
agent orchestration in 2026 are different skills, while the competency a coordination form demands has
to survive its mediator changing. That is the machine-orchestration cut, discharged here.

**A competency**, then: the combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, behaviours and attributes
enabling effective performance in a role or context. Two anchors carry it for this audience. Sandberg's (2000)
account of competence as ways of conceiving work is close to what asymmetric interpretation names — the
worker's competence *is* her conception of what the system is doing. And the communication-competence
lineage supplies the warrant for the name, through the competence/performance distinction descending
from Hymes (1972) and developed by Spitzberg and Cupach (1984): what a party is able to do in a
coordination, as against what she did on any occasion. Spitzberg (2006) carries the same distinction
into computer-mediated settings, which is the nearest prior case of a competence construct built for a
mediated channel.

| | Held by | Acquired through | Dates with | Explains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skill | the individual | instruction | the tool | task performance |
| Capability | the firm that owns the routine | organizational learning | the industry | firm-level advantage |
| Competency | the individual in a role | participation | the form | performance in a role |
| **Algorithmacy** | **the coordinated party** | **participation in mediated coordination** | **the coordination form** | **variance under a structural constant** |

Three consequences follow from that row, and each is a commitment the paper can be held to.

**The unit of measurement is the person, and the unit of comparison is the form.** Algorithmacy is
measured on workers and interpreted against arrangements. A study that measured it on firms would be
measuring something else, and a study that compared workers across arrangements without holding the
arrangement's withholdings constant would be comparing incomparables.

**The construct should travel across mediators and fail across forms.** Two workers on different
platforms, both facing opaque adaptive mediation, should be measurable on one scale. A worker in a
transparent, rule-published arrangement should score high trivially or not be scoreable at all,
because the conditions the facets answer are not withheld. That is a testable prediction and a
demanding one.

**A rise in average algorithmacy is not a good outcome on its own.** If a cohort's scores rise while
the form's withholdings stay fixed, what has risen is the amount of unpaid coordination work the
population absorbs. Whether that is welcome depends on facts about the arrangement that this
construct does not measure, and §6's division is what keeps the two questions apart.

# 9. What Five Constructs Make of the Algorithm

Five existing constructs cover parts of this ground. Sorting them by *what each makes the algorithm*
shows why none reaches the worker's coordination problem.

**A channel.** Computer-mediated communication treats the system as the medium through which two
parties reach each other (Spitzberg, 2006). The medium shapes the message and the parties remain the coordinators.

**A partner.** Human-machine communication treats the system as an interlocutor in its own right, and
studies the human-machine dyad (Guzman & Lewis, 2020).

**An agent serving the worker.** AI-mediated communication treats the system as acting on the worker's
behalf, augmenting or modifying what she sends (Hancock, Naaman, & Levy, 2020).

**An object of interpretation.** Algorithm sensemaking, and the folk-theory literature around it,
studies how workers construct accounts of an opaque system (Rahman, 2021; Curchod et al., 2020). This is the closest of the five and it is
the one this paper draws on most.

**An object of knowledge.** AI literacy and algorithmic competency measure what a worker understands
about how such systems work (Long & Magerko, 2020).

The diagnosis divides them. **The first three inherit a dyad** by placing the machine on one axis of a
two-party relation — worker and channel, worker and partner, worker and agent — and the triad's third
position never appears. **The last two grant the opacity and drop the counterpart.** Algorithm
sensemaking reaches interpretation and stops before coordination; a worker who has understood the
system perfectly has still not been told whether she coordinated with anyone. AI literacy measures
knowledge about a system, and knowing how a recommender works predicts nothing about getting a
counterpart to receive what you meant.

Two labels sit closer than the five and have to be beaten rather than cited.

**Gig literacies** (Sutherland, Jarrahi, Dunn, & Nelson, 2020) names the competencies freelancers
build to survive on platforms, and reads enrollment without standing as a competence burden. It is the nearest precedent and the paper claims
descent from it. The difference is derivation: gig literacies are collected from what workers report
doing, while algorithmacy's facets are derived from what the form withholds, which is what lets the
account say why there are three and why accountability yields none.

**AI as an organizing capability** (Stelmaszak, Joshi, & Constantiou, 2026) names a capacity arising
from human–algorithm relations at the organizational level. §8's possession argument applies directly: it is a capability of the organizing
party, and this paper's subject owns nothing.

What none of the seven supplies is the triad. Each of the five constructs is built for a relation
with two positions in it, whether the second position is a channel, a partner, an agent, an object of
interpretation or an object of knowledge. The arrangement this paper is about has three: a worker, a
counterpart she does not meet, and a system that interprets both and commits determinations neither
endorsed. A construct with two positions can describe any two of the three and will always be
describing a projection of the thing.

That is why the diagnosis is structural rather than a matter of coverage. Adding measures to AI
literacy will not produce algorithmacy, because the missing element is a *position*, not a variable.
The counterpart has to be in the account for the competency to be about coordination at all —
otherwise what is measured is a worker's relationship with a machine, which is a real subject and a
different one.

# 10. Two Properties: How It Is Acquired, How It Is Distributed

Two claims about algorithmacy are properties of the competency rather than facets of it, and they are
what make it worth measuring.

**Implicit acquisition.** Algorithmacy develops through participation in mediated coordination and not
through instruction. The structural reason is in §8: the rule is undisclosed, so there is nothing to
teach. What a training course can convey is the tool and the interface. What it cannot convey is what
this particular gate rewards this month, because nobody outside the system knows and the system does
not say. **The falsifier is direct**: if a cohort taught the system explicitly scores as well as a
cohort that only used it, implicit acquisition is wrong.

**Stratified fluency.** Algorithmacy is unevenly distributed across equivalently placed workers. This
is the property that connects the construct to the puzzle in §1, and it carries the distributive edge
from §6: a form that converts unequal private capacity into unequal outcomes, and reports the result as
performance, has made a competency do the work an institution used to do. **The falsifier**: if the
competency is roughly uniform across workers facing the same system, it explains none of the variance
that motivated the account.

Property A is what makes algorithmacy not a skill. Property B is what makes it not a capability.

Both properties are claims about a distribution rather than about any individual, and both are
consistent with a great deal of ordinary variation that has nothing to do with the construct. A
worker may score low because she joined last week, and implicit acquisition predicts exactly that.
A worker may score high because a friend on the platform told her what the gate rewards, and that is
implicit acquisition operating socially rather than a refutation of it. What would refute the
property is a population where explicit instruction produced the same distribution as participation.

Stratified fluency also carries an assumption worth surfacing, because it is where the account is
most exposed. It assumes the workers being compared face the *same* withholdings. Two drivers on one
platform in one city plausibly do. Two workers on different platforms, or in jurisdictions where one
has statutory review and the other does not, do not — and comparing them would attribute to
competency a difference produced by institutions. **The design in §11 holds the arrangement fixed by
studying one cohort under one gate**, which buys internal comparability at the price of the external
kind.

The two properties together are what make the construct worth measuring rather than merely worth
naming. A competency that everyone acquired equally, or that instruction could supply on demand,
would name a real capacity and explain nothing about why equivalently placed people fare
differently.

# 11. The Cohorts, and the Study They License

The construct was formed by teaching. **GauntleTT**, an intensive AI engineering cohort in Trinidad
and Tobago, run with the government's AI Ministry, the local MP in La Brea, and NGO partners, is where
the course and the construct came from. Participants built software and submitted work through a gate,
and the questions they asked about that gate — what does it want, why did it change, who can I ask —
are the questions the three facets name.

**That is a claim about content validity and about nothing else.** Formation in the field bears on
whether the items say what practitioners mean. It is not evidence about the construct's structure, and
treating it as evidence is this paper's easiest fatal error.

The course now runs as a full course at **Hult International Business School**, and Hult is the study
site. Sixteen weeks, participants building production software and submitting each project as a merged
pull request, with a gate that runs a written peer review and then a private vote.

**Site fit needs arguing rather than asserting**, because the participants build the very platforms they
coordinate through, and a system you built is a hard case for opaque mediation on unendorsed
objectives. The argument is threefold. The gate's votes are private, so an outcome arrives without its
reasoning. Advancement is decided by peers reading work the submitter cannot watch them read, so intent
must be inferred from outcomes. And the platforms change under any given participant because *others*
change them — a cohort of thirty means twenty-nine sources of unannounced revision. Where those three
hold, the withholding conditions hold, whatever the employment status.

The study is qualitative. Interviews at mid-cohort and exit, observation of review sessions, and the
coordination record the programme already leaves in its repositories, with a dated ledger of rule
changes so interviews can probe detection of specific ones. Its sharpest question is the one this paper
cannot settle from theory: **what does a participant do when a gate outcome strikes her as wrong, whom
does she approach, and what happens?** §5 claims accountability cannot be individually supplied. A
participant who reliably obtains answerability through individual skill alone, with no forum involved,
refutes that claim, and the protocol says so before fieldwork begins.

Four questions organize it, and each is chosen because the panel instrument cannot reach it.

**How does a working theory of the gate get built and revised?** From which incidents, on what
evidence, and with what abandoned versions along the way. A scale can show that scores rise. Only
process data shows the mechanism implicit acquisition claims.

**Do the inbound and outbound halves of common understanding appear as distinct practices?** §7
splits them on the argument that they fail differently. Participants either describe two activities
with different occasions or they describe one, and their descriptions are evidence either way.

**When rules change without announcement, who notices and how does noticing travel?** The dated
ledger makes this checkable: a participant who did not mention a change can be asked about that
specific change, and whether detection spread through the cohort speaks to whether the competency is
individually held or socially pooled.

**And the accountability question**, which is the study's sharpest and this paper's only empirical
exposure.

Analysis is abductive, with the three facets held as sensitizing concepts and not as a coding frame.
**A finding that lived practice carves differently from the derivation is a finding**, and the coding
scheme is committed before fieldwork so that it can be checked against what was actually done.

Two limits of the design belong here rather than in a later appendix. The cohort is small,
self-selected and technical, so a competency measured there is a competency in that population. And
the investigator teaches the programme he proposes to study, which is the protocol's central risk and
is handled structurally: advancement is decided by the peer gate, in which he casts no vote; a third
party outside the programme holds consent records and receives withdrawals; and no participant is
interviewed about a project whose outcome is still open.

**Status at the time of writing.** A companion three-wave instrument is built and approved — Bentley
IRB, exempt determination 11 May 2026 — and unfielded. The qualitative protocol is a separate
application, in preparation. No participant has been interviewed and no wave has been collected.
Nothing in this paper rests on a response from anyone.

# 12. What This Paper Cannot Say

The three facets are a derivation and not a finding. Whether they cohere as one competency, whether
they separate from one another, and whether they separate from general self-efficacy are open, and one
discriminant test is pre-registered while the others are not.

The rival explanations of §6 are named and not defeated. Prior expertise in particular has direct
support in the literature that motivates the puzzle, and this paper offers argument against it rather
than evidence.

The convergence between the derivation and the built instrument is coherence across levels within one
lineage, and not corroboration from independent routes.

The seventh row is a declared second carving of material the companion review already declared
provisional. A later paper could carve it differently, and the ordinal reading of the row is the part
most exposed to that.

The account is scoped to arrangements where all three withholding conditions hold together. Where the
mediator is transparent, or opaque but static, an older capacity suffices, and this paper marks that
boundary without theorizing what lies past it.

Nothing here measures a worker. The cohorts are where the construct was formed, and the study that
would test it has not run.

Two further limits are worth naming because a reader could otherwise take them from the argument
without the paper having earned them.

The ordinal reading of §4 rests on published accounts of what each settled form asks of its
coordinated party, and those accounts were written for other purposes. No one has measured how much
competency a market participant needs, because nobody had reason to ask. The row reports what the
literatures imply and does not report a measurement, and a study that measured the settled columns
directly could move them.

And the account says nothing about how the withholdings arose or whether they could be otherwise. It
takes the form as the companion review grades it and asks what that form demands. Whether a platform
could deliver accountability while keeping everything else it has is a design question this paper
does not touch, and the answer would not change the division in §6 — it would change which side of
the division a given arrangement sits on.
