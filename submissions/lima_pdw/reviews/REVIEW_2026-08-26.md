# Manuscript review — the live draft, 26 August 2026

Target: [`../manuscript/PAPER.md`](../manuscript/PAPER.md), "Algorithmacy: A Competency for
Coordinative Co-optation," 6,269 words with references, six sections. Venue: the OS/OT Paper
Development Workshop, Universidad de Piura, 5–7 October 2026; manuscript due 10 September.

This review is independent of [`REVIEW_2026-08-19.md`](REVIEW_2026-08-19.md). The draft has not
been revised since that review, so all ten of its findings stand; I re-verified four of them
against the PDFs and list the set once, compressed, as finding 11. Everything above finding 11
is new. Sources were checked against [`../literature/`](../literature/) — the steelmans, the
architecture memos, [`../literature/FINDINGS.md`](../literature/FINDINGS.md), and the publisher
PDFs directly where a page was in dispute.

---

## Verdict

**Major revisions, and this time two of them are not small.** The paper still makes one argument
and §3 still makes it well. But three claims in the draft are falsifiable as written — one by the
project's own files ("several responses" is one response), one by a paper the draft already cites
(Curchod and colleagues named the triad in *ASQ* in 2020), and one by a table a reviewer can open
in seconds (Long and Magerko's competency 10 is called *Human Role in AI*). And the paper's
budget is inverted: it spends 838 words recapping Paper 1 and 534 proposing the construct it
exists to propose. The register passes every lexical band, again — and the paragraph layer now
carries the tell the lexical layer used to: fourteen ", not" contrasts and seven "rather than"
constructions in 4,299 body words, with seventeen of the twenty-three paragraphs in §§4–6
landing on a negation. Each instance defends itself; the uniformity does not.

### 2. §3, L116 — the "missing position" is prior art in the paper's own reference list

The close of §3:

> In every case a person stands on one side and a system on the other. Adding items will not
> move that line. The missing piece is a position, not a variable.

The prior review showed this claim falsifiable by the ODR fourth-party literature (its finding
3). The exposure is worse than that, and it sits in the venue this paper courts. Curchod,
Patriotta, Cohen and Neysen (2020) — **cited in this draft at L60, for ratings portability** —
name the position outright in *ASQ*: customer reviews "enact triadic relationships among the
platform operator, buyers, and sellers," held open by a **visibility gap** (the buyer's track
record hidden, the seller's visible to all) and by a coalition of interests between anonymous
buyers and a distant platform owner. Cameron (2024, *ASQ*) draws the same figure as the
"algorithmic labor triangle: app–worker–customer—(merchant)" and is not cited anywhere in the
draft. Both are documented in [`../literature/models/curchod2020.md`](../literature/models/curchod2020.md)
and `FINDINGS.md`. A reviewer who knows either paper reads "the missing piece is a position" as
a claim that the position went unnoticed — and the draft itself cites one of the papers that
noticed it.

Scoped to competence constructs, the claim survives, because Curchod and Cameron theorise power,
not competence. The paper never states that scope. The concession costs a paragraph and buys the
venue's own geometry as a foundation. Replace L116's final three sentences (and see finding 9 on
cutting the recital that precedes them):

> The position itself is not unnoticed. Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen and Neysen (2020) set customer
> reviews "in place of traditional dyadic exchanges" and named the triad they enact — platform
> operator, buyers, sellers — with a visibility gap that lets the buyer judge unseen; Cameron
> (2024) drew the same figure for on-demand work as the algorithmic labor triangle. Both are
> theories of power, and neither writes a competence for the party the triangle traps. That is
> the line the seven hearings drew: every construct that specifies a competence puts a person on
> one side and a system on the other. The missing piece is a competence written for a position
> the field has already mapped — and writing it is the next section's work.

Add Cameron (2024) to the references. Curchod's visibility gap also belongs in §4 as the
published antecedent of the interpreting operation; one clause does it.

### 3. §4 — 534 words for the contribution, 838 for the recap

Section budgets, measured: §2 (Paper 1 recap) 838 words; §3 (seven hearings) 1,975; **§4
(algorithmacy) 534**; §5 (empirical) 635; §6 (limits) 355. The construct the paper exists to
propose gets less space than the summary of a different paper, and the shortage shows exactly
where the prior review already pressed: semantic relations answered in three circular sentences
(L124; its finding 8), the three-operations derivation stopping short of a cardinality argument
(L126; its finding 9). Two further debts are new, and both are cheques the locked opening
writes:

- **The locked introduction promises, at L44: "That is why we argue that it fulfills the
  requirements Paper 1 defined." §4 tests Suddaby's requirements and never returns to
  Paper 1's.** A cold reader finishes §4 with the promised demonstration unperformed. One
  sentence closes it — after the scope paragraph: "These are also the terms Paper 1's open cell
  demands: an account of what a person must be able to do, stated so a researcher could
  recognize its absence."
- **The locked abstract calls algorithmacy "a new, satisficing construct" (L30). The word never
  recurs.** "Satisficing" is a term of art — Simon's stopping rule against an aspiration level —
  and the body neither names the aspiration level nor says in what sense the construct
  satisfices rather than optimizes. Left uncashed, it reads as decoration in the paper's most
  visible sentence. The body owes one sentence, most naturally at §4's close: "The construct
  satisfices in Simon's sense: it is not the complete account of the person in this form, but it
  is sufficient for the open cell, because it is the first that includes the counterpart."

And §4 is where the paper's missing half-page belongs: **what organization theory gets if the
construct is real.** The draft builds algorithmacy and never says what changes — whether the
form's viability depends on how the competency is distributed, what a form full of
low-algorithmacy participants selects for, what the construct lets a coordination researcher ask
that Kellogg, Valentine and Christin's control frame does not. A PDW exists to develop exactly
this, and the draft gives the panel nothing to develop. The reallocation: cut ~300 words from §2
(finding 6) and the ~120-word recital from §3 (finding 9), and spend them here.

### 4. §3, L90 — "none names another person" is falsifiable, and the fix is already written

The AI literacy hearing:

> Every competency is a relation between a person and a technology, and none names another
> person.

Long and Magerko's competency 10 is titled **Human Role in AI**: "Recognize that humans play an
important role in programming, choosing models, and fine-tuning AI systems." A reviewer with the
table — and the table is the article's centrepiece — finds the title in seconds, and the
sentence is absolute enough that finding it is enough. The arm's own steelman
([`../literature/steelmans/longmagerko2020.md`](../literature/steelmans/longmagerko2020.md)) and
`FINDINGS.md` both flag the sentence and both supply the narrowed form, which is stronger than
the original because it can be checked in both directions. Replace:

> Every competency is a relation between a person and a technology. The one that names humans at
> all — competency 10, *Human Role in AI* — names them as the system's makers, and no competency
> names another person as a party to an interaction the learner is conducting.

The same steelman holds a second, unused result that bears on the whole paper: **Long and
Magerko build the literacy-family argument themselves** — literacy as the ability to communicate
through written language, then digital, computational, scientific and data literacies by
analogy. The locked introduction runs the same move as if it were the paper's own, and §4 never
touches it. That matters beyond etiquette: AI literacy sits on the same shelf the introduction
reaches for *and is a person–system construct*, so the family analogy discriminates nothing —
membership in the literacy family is compatible with exactly the boundary the paper rejects. The
analogy is doing naming work, not analytic work, and §4 should say where the analogy breaks: the
family's members carry two operations each (read and write, speak and listen, use and
interpret), and algorithmacy needs a third precisely because its medium moves. One insertion in
§4, after L126's derivation:

> The literacy family supplies the name and then marks the limit of the analogy. Long and
> Magerko (2020) place AI literacy on the same shelf — literacy, then digital, computational and
> data literacies — and their construct still runs person-to-system, so the shelf itself settles
> nothing. And each older member carries two operations, one receptive and one expressive; the
> third operation here has no analogue, because text and number hold still while a person reads
> them. Keeping track is what a competence acquires when the medium is also a moving party.

### 5. §3's selection rule leaves the counterpart-first flank open

The seven hearings are selected by one question — "what the construct makes of the algorithm"
(L66–68) — so every construct heard is algorithm-centred, and the finding that none includes the
counterpart is partly an artefact of the sieve. The prior review's Abidin line covers the
social-media case. The organizational case is nearer home and undefended: the coordination
literature §2 itself draws on holds counterpart-facing competence constructs with no algorithm
in them — relational coordination above all, a property of communicating and relating among
parties doing interdependent work. A hostile reviewer's cheapest relabelling attack is not
"algorithmacy is Zhou plus items"; it is **"algorithmacy is relational coordination performed
under opacity."** No card in the 387-card library covers Gittell or relational coordination, so
the flank is not even provisioned. One sentence at §3's opening closes it:

> Constructs that already include the counterpart exist on the other shelf — relational
> coordination names a quality of communicating and relating among parties to interdependent
> work — and they presuppose exactly what this arrangement removes: parties who can see, reach,
> and answer one another. The review therefore asks what each construct makes of the algorithm,
> because the algorithm is what stands where that mutual visibility used to be.

Acquire one relational-coordination source to anchor the clause before Lima.

### 6. §2 — Paper 1's condition-mapping is attributed to the classics

Three sentences lay Okhuysen and Bechky's triad onto the classic authors as if the authors had
written it:

- L50: "Simon (1997) and Weber (1978) argued that this authority delivers predictability by
  rule, common understanding by shared premises, and accountability by a chain of command."
- L52: "Hayek (1945) showed that predictability and common understanding travel cheaply in that
  form … **and that accountability arrives through contract and reputation.**"
- L54: "Powell (1990) and Granovetter (1985) argued that accountability then arrives by
  reputation and sanction, predictability by continuing relation…"

Hayek (1945) says nothing about accountability, contract, or reputation; Simon and Weber never
wrote in the triad's vocabulary, which Okhuysen and Bechky assembled in 2009. The mapping is
Paper 1's — a legitimate and interesting move — and §2's phrasing hands it to authors a reviewer
can check in an afternoon. The Hayek sentence is the worst offender because *The Use of
Knowledge in Society* is the one paper in the set every OS/OT reviewer has actually read. Name
the true agent:

> Hayek (1945) showed why the price can carry the load: it is a public statistic no participant
> has to reconstruct in private. Paper 1 read that statistic as delivering predictability and
> common understanding, and located accountability in contract and reputation.

Apply the same repair to the Simon/Weber and Powell/Granovetter sentences ("Paper 1, drawing on
Simon (1997) and Weber (1978), located predictability in rule…"). This also discharges most of
what §2 owes: with the mapping owned, the four form-paragraphs can shrink to their
taking-a-place clauses — the only content §3 and §4 ever use — and §2 lands near 500 words. The
comparison table the architecture memos prescribed (`FINDINGS.md`, "Two concrete jobs") would
make the open cell visible instead of narrated and absorb the cut detail at zero prose cost.

### 7. §5, L138 — the site's three features are mapped to the wrong three terms

> Participants submit work to a gate that returns an outcome that is not fully explained. Their
> advancement depends on peers they cannot watch. The codebase moves because other participants
> change it. Those three features are the conditions the definition names as opaque,
> intermediary, and adaptive.

Read against the locked definition's own glosses (L44: opaque = the rule cannot be observed;
adaptive = the rule may change; intermediary = the algorithm that mediates between the person
and her counterpart), the mapping fails in the middle: **peers are the counterpart, not the
intermediary** — the gate is the intermediary — and the moving codebase is not the rule unless
the paper says the gate's effective criteria move with it, which it never does. A reviewer who
lines the two triples up finds the construct's own site misdescribed in the construct's own
vocabulary. The deeper problem the mislabeling hides: opacity is defined person-relatively ("the
person cannot observe the rule"), so at a site where participants build the systems they submit
to, the scope condition threatens to exclude the site — and L138 answers by assertion ("We argue
fit from the gate, the private vote, and the moving rule"), which is a sentence about arguing,
not an argument. Replace the paragraph's middle:

> Participants submit work to a gate that returns an outcome that is not fully explained: the
> gate is the intermediary, and its unexplained verdict is the opacity the definition names. Its
> effective rule moves, because the codebase the gate tests against moves with other
> participants' work. And the parties it reads on the far side are peers who vote in private — a
> counterpart who judges without being seen judging. The site is a hard case for opacity,
> because participants build the systems they also submit to; what stays unobservable to them is
> not the code but the vote and the criteria behind it.

That last clause is the argument L138 currently only gestures at, and it is the honest form of
the scope condition: opacity is a property of the person–arrangement pair, and the arrangement
withholds the rule even from its builders.

### 8. §5, L144 — the methods paragraph claims an analysis that does not exist

> Analysis is abductive (Timmermans & Tavory, 2012): we entered with a derived construct, treat
> the three operations as sensitizing concepts, keep first-cycle coding in the participant's
> words (Gioia, Corley, & Hamilton, 2013), decline the data-structure template…

Present tense, practice claims — and the arm's own audit
([`../interview/METHODS_AUDIT.md`](../interview/METHODS_AUDIT.md), summarised in `FINDINGS.md`)
records that no codebook, coding rule, memo file, or discard log exists. Abduction is the claim
that bites, because Timmermans and Tavory give it machinery — inference from *surprise*, through
revisiting and defamiliarization — and a design with no record of what surprised the analyst
cannot show it abduced anything. With one response in the bucket, "keep first-cycle coding in
the participant's words" describes work that has not happened. A methods reviewer asks for the
trail; the paper has none to point at. Shift the paragraph to design tense and add the missing
warrant for sensitizing concepts (Bowen 2006 is carded and was always the intended cite):

> The design is abductive (Timmermans & Tavory, 2012): we enter with a derived construct and
> hold the three operations as sensitizing concepts, which start inquiry rather than sort it
> (Bowen, 2006). First-cycle coding, when the file supports it, will stay in the participant's
> words (Gioia, Corley, & Hamilton, 2013); we decline the data-structure template (Mees-Buss,
> Welch, & Piekkari, 2022) and treat the design as bricolage (Pratt, Sonenshein, & Feldman,
> 2022). No coding cycle is complete. The negative case is a participant who obtains an answer
> from the gate by individual skill alone.

Add Bowen (2006) to the references. The same tense discipline governs L142's "Three protocols
are in use" — in use with what returns? The sample statement the research plan marked "due by
submission" (who responded, from which protocol, at what stage) is still absent, and finding 1's
repair supplies it in the same breath.

## AI-slop audit

The standing verdict is that uniformity of optimization is the tell. This draft's uniformity is
no longer lexical; it is rhetorical, and it has one signature.

**1. The antithesis cadence is the draft's universal close.** Seventeen of the twenty-three
paragraphs in §§4–6 end on a negation or an "X, not Y" contrast. Three consecutive paragraph
endings in §5, verbatim: "…not of a magic number of interviews" (L134); "…that formation is not
the study site" (L136); "…not from employment status" (L138). Then: "…not by the three
operations as if they had been found" (L146); "…not algorithmacy, and it leaves the counterpart
where she was" (L150). §6 runs five for six: "they cannot close those questions" (L158), "a
frame rather than a finding" (L162), "not a claim that findings…travel" (L164), "…that this
construct does not measure" (L166), and the closer at L168. Every one of these passes the
retired antithesis quota's individual test — the wrong alternative is usually one a reader
would reach for. Together they are a metronome, and the global style file names this exact
engine as the first Claude default to kill. The cure is not deletion but variance: keep the
contrast where the wrong alternative is live (L136, L150), and let the rest end on the fact —
"Bentley IRB issued an exempt determination on 11 May 2026" is a better paragraph ending than
any verdict about it.

**2. The hearing-opener template, seven for seven.** Every §3 subsection opens with the same
move — temporal-comparative marker, literature as agent, verb of arrival: "The first answers
came from…" (L72), "That channel picture could not hold once…" (L78), "A further move kept…"
(L84), "A different literature asked…" (L90), "Platform work then named…" (L96), "A still
nearer account asked…" (L104), "The nearest account put…" (L110). These are backward hooks —
mechanism 7, and the prior review rightly praised them — but seven instances of one shape is a
template, and the last two turn proximity itself into a performed countdown ("still nearer…
nearest"). Break two: open the Zhou hearing on the scale ("Twelve items, four dimensions, five
samples: Zhou, Lei, Liu, Huang, and Hou (2025) built the first validated scale in this
family…") and the Rahman hearing on the score ("The 2015 Success Score replaced a transparent
five-star system with an opaque hundred-point number; Rahman (2021) built the invisible cage
from what workers did next.").

**3. The triple recital** (finding 9): announce, hear, recite. The third pass is cadence, not
content.

**4. Formulas already on record, still in place:** "Adding items" ×4 across §§3–4; "still of
the apparatus" ×4 in one paragraph (L98); the subsection verdict-epigram drumbeat with "leave(s)
unasked" ×2 (L92, L106). The prior review's prescriptions stand; none has been applied.

**5. What is not slop, for the record.** "A person takes her place" ×5 across the five forms
and "treats it as an object of" across the hearings are declared analytic parallelism doing
real work. The L126 triple ("To interpret is… To specify intent is… To keep track is…") carries
distinct content per member. The short-sentence punctures ("She is enrolled." "We deepened the
protocols.") are the house rhythm working. The draft's problem is not that it optimizes badly
but that it optimizes every paragraph the same way.
