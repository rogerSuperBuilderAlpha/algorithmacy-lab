**Manuscript:** "Algorithmacy: A Competency for Coordinative Co-optation" (PAPER.md, revision of 2026-08-28)
**Reviewer lens:** CMC / HMC / AI-MC / communication competence theory, as assigned by JCMC, New Media & Society, or Human-Machine Communication

## Verdict

**Major revisions.** The single most important fix: reconcile the paper's strongest structural claim — that under algorithmic mediation the human counterpart "never occupies an evaluative position" and the relational instrument of competence is "systematically dismantled" — with the paper's own field site, where peer reviews and upvotes are *public* and only their aggregation is concealed. As written, the Spitzberg/relational-breakdown argument is falsified by the Hult gate the paper itself describes. The claim survives in a weaker, more precise form — the intermediary severs the *verdict-bearing link* between counterpart appraisal and consequence, rather than abolishing appraisal — and that weaker form is both true and sufficient for the construct. Until it is restated, the paper's communication-theoretic spine and its empirical site contradict each other.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and argument structure

### 1.1 Overall architecture

The argument's skeleton is sound and, in places, genuinely strong. The delimited novelty claim — no construct combines an active human counterpart with an opaque, independently consequential, evaluating-and-binding intermediary, rather than the cruder "every construct is a dyad" — is exactly the right shape, and the manuscript polices it well: the Introduction concedes outright that Hancock et al. and Zhou et al. already have two humans in frame, which is what several extant constructs would have used to embarrass a cruder claim. The seven hearings each open on the construct's strongest ground before bounding it, which is the correct rhetorical ethics for this genre. The four explicit boundary conditions in "What These Boundaries Share" are the most useful paragraph in the section, because they convert the review from a tour into a test.

That said, three load-bearing claims do not survive as written, one rebuttal dodges the hardest version of its objection, and the novelty claim needs to absorb a rival literature the paper has not named. I take these in the order a hostile referee from my field would raise them.

### 1.2 The claim a hostile referee attacks first: "the counterpart never occupies an evaluative position"

The Spitzberg (2006) hearing is, at the level of exposition, the best-informed treatment of that model I have seen outside the CMC literature itself. The paper correctly identifies the model's components (motivation, knowledge, skills, context, outcomes), the four skill clusters, the five outcome criteria including co-orientation, and — importantly — it preempts the reductive reading by locating competence in the relational tradition, where competence is a judgment rendered by a co-interactant, not a trait residing in a head. The paragraph on Spitzberg's own reported psychometric limitations (attenuated reliabilities from reverse-coded items, lower-than-theorized multidimensionality, the four-factor omnibus EFA) is accurate to the self-critical character of that article and does the paper credit: most citations of Spitzberg 2006 do not know the measure was preliminary and said so.

The breakdown argument built on this foundation, however, proves too much. The paper claims the intermediary "prevents the counterpart from directly observing the communicative exchange, assessing its contextual legitimacy, or registering an affective response," so that "the evaluative criteria lack an interlocutor to administer them" (Extant Constructs, CMC section, third paragraph), and later that "the human counterpart never occupies an evaluative position where mutual communicative competence can be openly negotiated" (Categorical Delineation, final paragraph). Now turn to the Empirical Strategy: at Hult, "written peer reviews and upvotes remained public while the underlying aggregate tally remained concealed." The peers *do* observe the submission, *do* assess its legitimacy, *do* register responses, and do all of it in public. What the gate conceals is how those appraisals aggregate into the binding verdict — the weighting, the tally, the threshold. The paper's own site therefore instantiates a condition its own theory section declares structurally impossible.

The fix is available and strengthens the paper. The precise claim is that the intermediary severs the link between counterpart appraisal and consequence: the counterpart may appraise, even visibly, but her appraisal reaches the focal actor only as an input to an opaque aggregation she does not control and cannot trace, so the *relational negotiation of competence* — the mutual, iterative calibration Spitzberg and Cupach (1984) built the model on — cannot occur, even where raw appraisal is visible. Rahman's TalentFinder (appraisal invisible) and the Hult gate (appraisal visible, consequence-mapping concealed) then become two points on one dimension rather than a contradiction. This restatement also rescues the scope conditions, which currently cannot admit the Hult site without embarrassment.

### 1.3 The second attack: inheriting Spitzberg's criteria while deleting their administrator

The paper writes: "Algorithmacy directly inherits Spitzberg's evaluative axis… appropriateness and effectiveness remain the most robust criteria available in communication theory. What algorithmacy cannot adopt, however, is the structural topology those criteria presuppose." A competence theorist will not let this pass, because it is the exact tension the relational turn of the 1980s existed to resolve. Spitzberg and Cupach's central move was that competence is an *impression* — an evaluation rendered by an interactant — not a property of behavior. Appropriateness is *defined* in the manuscript's own quotation as "perceived legitimacy or fit," which requires a perceiver. If the topology that supplies the perceiver is removed, appropriateness does not survive as a criterion with a different administrator; it reverts to an unadministered abstraction, and algorithmacy quietly becomes an *ability* construct of precisely the pre-relational kind Spitzberg and Cupach spent a decade dismantling.

The paper does not need to abandon the move — it needs to own it. Sandberg (2000), already in the manuscript, supplies the license: competence as the worker's interpretive conception of work is an ability-side account, and the paper can say plainly that where the structural conditions for relational co-construction are absent, competence necessarily reverts from an interactionally negotiated judgment to an individually held capacity, with the counterpart's appraisal surviving only as an opaque input to the verdict. At present the manuscript asserts the breakdown of the relational model in one sentence ("Algorithmic coordination structurally breaks this relational model") and moves on. That sentence is the paper's single deepest engagement with communication competence theory and it is asserted, not argued. Three or four sentences would argue it; I supply a candidate in Part 3.

### 1.4 The Guzman & Lewis / Gibbs et al. hearing: accurate summary, dodged objection

The HMC self-definition is represented fairly. Guzman and Lewis (2020) do stipulate the machine-as-communicator as the field's constitutive unit; Fortunati and Edwards (2020) do formalize it in the inaugural editorial; and the paper is right that this is the field's own boundary, not an external caricature. One caution: Guzman and Lewis also observe that communicative AI blurs the mediator/communicator distinction — the same system can occupy both roles — so the clean CMC-channel/HMC-interlocutor bifurcation the paper leans on is a heuristic their article itself complicates. A sentence acknowledging this would inoculate against an HMC referee who knows the text well; it does not threaten the argument, since the blurring is grist for the paper's own claim that the intermediary is neither pure channel nor pure interlocutor.

The engagement with Gibbs, Kirkwood, Fang, and Wilkenfeld (2021) is the section's most ambitious move and its least finished. The paper's rebuttal is compositional: structuration's third element is an institutional context, not an interdependent human counterpart, so the structurational triad and the coordination triad are different triads. True, and worth saying. But it is not the hardest version of the objection. Gibbs and colleagues' deeper charge is *methodological individualism* — that analyzing person–machine encounters as bounded interactions obscures how agency is distributed across users, machines, and institutional structures. Aimed at this manuscript, the charge reads: a construct that locates the capacity for navigating an opaque institutional arrangement *inside an individual's head* repeats the individualism being criticized; the "competency" is itself structurationally produced and distributed. And the manuscript's own evidence arms the objection: Zhou et al.'s finding that the competency's antecedents are "irreducibly social — informal peer support systems," and Sutherland et al.'s "informal peer sensemaking networks," are cited as support for experiential acquisition, apparently without noticing that they describe a socially distributed capacity, not an individually held one.

The answer exists and is standard micro-foundations doctrine: socially acquired, individually exercised. A capacity can be produced through peer sensemaking and still be unevenly held and individually deployed at the moment of coordination — indeed the paper's Uneven Distribution property *requires* individual-level variance, which is what distinguishes a micro-foundation from individualism. But the manuscript must make this argument where Gibbs et al. are engaged, not leave the reviewer to construct it. As written, the rebuttal answers the objection's letter and dodges its point.

### 1.5 The Hancock, Naaman & Levy hearing: right to call it the hardest case, wrong demarcation axis

The paper is correct, and creditably candid, that AI-MC is "the most critical analytical challenge in the review": Hancock, Naaman, and Levy (2020) theorize exactly two humans plus a computational agent, and any node-counting critique dies on their definition. The five dimensions (magnitude, media type, optimization goal, autonomy, role orientation) are reported accurately, and the receiver-vulnerability material — rare disclosure, presumed human authorship, the assumed acceptance of agency — is a fair and productive reading.

The demarcation, however, rests its full weight on the wrong property. The paper's decisive claim is that the AI-MC parameters become "structurally unfillable" because the definition requires the agent to operate "on behalf of a communicator," whereas "the algorithmic third party operates on behalf of no one present." An informed AI-MC referee will contest the premise: Hancock and colleagues themselves problematize the optimization-goal dimension, entertaining systems whose optimization serves interests not aligned with the sender's — the smart-reply system tuned for the platform's ends is a live example in their discussion. If AI-MC's own parameter space already accommodates a platform-serving optimizer, then "on behalf of no one present" is a contested reading of their framework, not a structural impossibility within it, and the "unfillable parameters" claim overreaches. (The author should check this against the version of record before Lima; if Hancock et al.'s text confines optimization strictly to sender goals, the claim stands as written, but my recollection of the article is that it does not.)

The demarcation that survives every version of this challenge is already in the manuscript, one sentence later, in a subordinate position: "within their model, the counterpart's exposure is restricted to a symbolic message; within ours, the counterpart and the focal worker are simultaneously exposed to an authoritative, binding determination." *That* is the load-bearing distinction — AI-MC's agent modifies, augments, or generates *messages*; it never ranks, matches, scores, or terminates. Message-operation versus verdict-rendering is a categorical difference no reading of Hancock et al. can erode, and it should carry the demarcation, with the principal-less property demoted to supporting evidence. So: the paper's assessment of AI-MC's threat level is right; its chosen escape route is the one an informed reviewer can block; and its safe route is already on the page, mislabeled as secondary.

### 1.6 Falsifying the novelty claim: the nearest rival the paper has not named

Instructed to attack the novelty claim from my field's literature, here is the strongest attack I can construct. The "conceptual encirclement" paragraph claims symmetry: constructs centering the algorithm bracket the counterpart, and "the framework that centers the human counterpart" — Abidin (2016), the sole representative — "systematically brackets the algorithmic architecture." That singular is a cherry-pick, and a NM&S referee will name the counterexamples immediately: Cotter's account of influencers "playing the visibility game" theorizes actors pursuing a *human audience* through an opaque, adaptive, evaluating Instagram algorithm, with both poles explicitly in frame (Cotter, 2019, *New Media & Society*); Bishop's "algorithmic gossip" theorizes communally produced knowledge of YouTube's algorithm mobilized to reach audiences through it (Bishop, 2019, *New Media & Society*); and DeVito's work on folk theories and algorithmic self-presentation puts counterpart-directed communication through opaque curation at the center of a running research program. The broader folk-theories-of-algorithms literature, which is large and lives in exactly the journals this section draws from, receives no hearing at all — the two systematic reviews cited (Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum; Gagrčin et al.) gesture at it but do not substitute for engagement with its strongest triadic instances.

Does the novelty claim die? No — it narrows, and the narrowed version holds. Three differences survive scrutiny. First, none of these is a formalized *competence construct*: they are accounts of practice, knowledge, and gossip, without constitutive operations, scope conditions, or a measurement program — the Suddaby apparatus this paper supplies. Second, the audience in creator-visibility work is an aggregate reached for attention, not an interdependent counterpart in the collective execution of work; there is no jointly executed task. Third — and this maps onto the paper's own fourth boundary condition — the algorithm in that literature evaluates and binds the *creator* while merely curating for the audience; it does not render one verdict binding both parties, which the paper's definition requires. So the claim survives as: no construct *formalizes an individual competence* for coordinating interdependent work with a counterpart through an evaluating-and-binding intermediary. But the encirclement paragraph must be rewritten to face this literature rather than to have its symmetry broken by the first referee who has read Cotter, and Table 1's implicit completeness claim should be softened to match. The current text, which the author's notes say has not yet had a communication-studies fidelity check, reads as if the visibility-labor pole of my field contained one paper.

### 1.7 The literacy / numeracy / oracy analogy

The analogy does rhetorical work the construct's structure cannot ratify, and the paper half-knows it. Two problems. First, the internal disanalogy: literacy's counterpart (the author behind the text) is reached through a medium that does not evaluate or bind anyone. The moment the intermediary becomes a *party* — the paper's central innovation — the analogy to media-competencies breaks, and the manuscript concedes this in its own terms ("Whereas literacy, numeracy, and oracy constitute competencies for a medium, algorithmacy operates as a competency for a coordination form"). That concession means the analogy is genealogical — it names the construct and places it in a lineage — not structural. Genealogical is a legitimate job (Long and Magerko are correctly credited for the lineage), but the manuscript also extracts structural predictions from it: the claim that established literacies have exactly two operations, one receptive and one expressive, and that keeping track is the third operation the moving medium adds. That two-operation characterization is contestable on its own terms — literacy scholarship since Freire has insisted on critical-evaluative operations irreducible to reading and writing, and numeracy models routinely include interpretation, application, *and* critical evaluation — and oracy is the weakest member throughout: it is a British education-policy coinage (Wilkinson), not a communication-theory construct, and its "medium," live talk, is itself adaptive in ways interactants track constantly (repair, recipient design), which quietly undermines the claim that keeping track is what distinguishes algorithmacy's medium. Second, the register profile specifies the analogy is welded once; it currently also anchors the operational-symmetry argument in Constitutive Operations. Recommendation: keep the analogy as nomenclature and lineage, delete the two-operation structural derivation, and derive keeping track solely from what the triad withholds — which the "Why Each Operation Requires the Full Triad" subsection already does better, and which is the paper's genuinely strong argumentative move.

### 1.8 What is accurate and should be defended as-is

For balance, and because a PDW author needs to know what *not* to concede in Lima: the Rahman (2021) hearing is the best in the section — the five properties, the two reactivities, the client-interview methodology and its triangulation-only purpose, and the off-platform disintermediation point are all faithful to that article, and the observation that Rahman *empirically documented* the counterpart's blindness to her own evaluative impact while his construct remained dyadic is a genuinely sharp reading a referee will respect. The Zhou et al. hearing's item-level analysis (Items 6, 8, 11) is the right way to argue that a counterpart present in the nomological net is absent from the construct. The Sutherland et al. hearing's treatment of the fifth literacy — rapport as an *exit* strategy from mediation, not a competence within it — is exactly right and is the paper's cleanest boundary argument. And the recursive-cycle answer to the reflective/formative measurement question (Constitutive Operations, final paragraph) is more sophisticated than most construct papers manage; keep it.

### 1.9 Verification flags (fidelity of the recently rewritten section)

The author's own revision notes say this section has not had a specialist fidelity check. Before Lima, verify against versions of record:

1. **Long & Magerko (2020) definition quote.** The manuscript renders it "…evaluate AI technologies critically; communicate and collaborate effectively with AI…". My recollection of the original is "…critically evaluate AI technologies; communicate and collaborate effectively with AI…". Word order inside quotation marks must match exactly.
2. **"Confirming their pre-registered hypothesis" (Zhou et al. section).** Scale-development papers in *Asia Pacific Journal of Human Resources* are rarely pre-registered; if Zhou and colleagues did not pre-register, this word fabricates a methodological virtue and a referee who checks will distrust every number in the paragraph. Verify or cut "pre-registered."
3. **All page-anchored quotes in the section** (Spitzberg pp. 630, 638, 640–641, 644, 648, 650–651; Guzman & Lewis pp. 71–74; Hancock et al. p. 90; Rahman pp. 956, 963, 976; Sutherland pp. 457, 461–470) — the section was rewritten recently and merged from a separate draft; every quote and page number needs one pass against the PDFs. I flag rather than adjudicate; my spot-checks found the Spitzberg and Rahman apparatus plausible and the Long & Magerko quote suspect.
4. **Table 1's "Passive transmission channel" for Spitzberg.** Spitzberg 2006 gives media factors and medium-specific knowledge more theoretical texture than "passive channel" concedes; the table's simplification is defensible shorthand, but the body text already carries the accurate version, so consider "Transmission medium; recalibrates parameters of an interpersonal model" for the cell.
5. **"Her cognitive inferences constitute the primary dependent variable of the empirical agenda" (AI-MC section).** Hancock et al.'s agenda spans sender effects, receiver perception, trust, and interpersonal dynamics; "primary" overstates. "A central dependent variable" is safe.

## Part 2 — AI-slop / prose-uniformity audit (disciplinary credibility only)

A separate reviewer holds the full copyedit brief; I flag only what would hurt this paper with a communication-studies reader, who is — occupational hazard — unusually alert to machine-textured prose.

1. **The seven hearings share one template, visibly.** Each runs: sympathetic exposition → preemption of a "reductive reading" → ceiling-mastery counterfactual → breakdown claim → "what algorithmacy inherits from X." The closing move alone appears seven times in near-identical form ("Algorithmacy directly inherits…", "What this literature contributes…", "From this literature, we adopt…", "What we adopt from Long and Magerko…", "What algorithmacy inherits from Zhou and colleagues…", "Three foundational insights transition from Rahman's framework…", "What algorithmacy inherits from Sutherland and colleagues comprises…"). The inheritance move is good *once per paper as a labeled convention* or varied in construction; uniform sevenfold repetition is the "uniformity of optimization" the house style names, and to a referee it reads as generated. Vary the syntactic frame, or announce the convention once ("each hearing closes on what the construct inherits") and then compress several closings to a clause.
2. **The ceiling-mastery counterfactual runs five times** ("An actor exhibiting mastery across every dimension…", "An individual may exhibit comprehensive mastery…", "An actor exhibiting comprehensive competence…", "an individual could achieve ceiling performance across all seventeen competencies…", "A worker could therefore achieve ceiling scores across all twelve items…"). It is the section's best analytic device and its most conspicuous formula. Keep it in full twice — Spitzberg and Zhou, where the item-level grounding earns it — and reduce the others to a clause.
3. **Superlative drumbeat.** Nearly every hearing crowns something: "the most critical analytical challenge," "the most formidable critique," "the most definitive theoretical significance," "the most direct conceptual challenge," "approaches closest," "the operation most exposed." Individually fine; as a system it is a tell. Two or three survive; the rest should state the point without ranking it.
4. **Lexical inflation concentrated in the rewritten section.** "Foundational" appears roughly a dozen times (foundational parameters, foundational article, foundational insights, foundational properties, foundational conceptual inquiries, foundational 2020 framework, foundational definition, foundational support…); "structural/structurally/structurational" saturates whole paragraphs; "systematically" recurs past the point of meaning. The rest of the manuscript (Empirical Strategy, the appendices) runs noticeably plainer, and the seam between the merged Google-Doc prose and the repo prose is legible — a referee who suspects drafting assistance will point at exactly this section.
5. **One banned opener.** "Crucially, this contestation routes exclusively to the technical infrastructure" (Zhou et al. hearing) — "Crucially" is on the register profile's banned list. Fix supplied below.
6. **"Candidly acknowledge" / "candid assessment"** appears twice in the Hancock hearing — the praise-hedge formula, doubled. Once, or zero.

None of this is fatal at a PDW; all of it is the kind of texture that makes a communication-studies discussant open the fidelity question in public, which is the one question this section cannot yet afford.

## Part 3 — Line-level revisions (ready to paste, in the paper's register)

**3.1 The counterpart's evaluative position (CMC hearing, third paragraph — the most important fix).** Replace "Rather, because the intermediary prevents the counterpart from directly observing the communicative exchange, assessing its contextual legitimacy, or registering an affective response, the evaluative criteria lack an interlocutor to administer them. The algorithmic arrangement does not merely depress the communicator's competence score — it systematically dismantles the relational instrument required to measure it." with:

> Rather, the intermediary severs the link between the counterpart's appraisal and its consequence. The counterpart may still observe the exchange and register a judgment — in some arrangements invisibly (Rahman, 2021), in others in public view — but that judgment reaches the focal actor only as one input to an opaque aggregation neither party controls, so the mutual, iterative calibration through which Spitzberg's criteria are administered cannot occur. The algorithmic arrangement does not merely depress the communicator's competence score; it disconnects the relational instrument from the verdict that binds her.

**3.2 The matching fix in Categorical Delineation (final paragraph).** Replace "The human counterpart never occupies an evaluative position where mutual communicative competence can be openly negotiated." with:

> The human counterpart may render appraisals — visibly or invisibly — but she never occupies a position from which mutual communicative competence can be negotiated, because the aggregation that converts her appraisal into consequence is withheld from both parties. Where the structural conditions for relational co-construction are absent, competence necessarily reverts from a jointly enacted judgment to an individually held capacity — the interpretive conception of work Sandberg (2000) describes — with the counterpart's appraisal surviving only as an opaque input to the binding verdict.

**3.3 Re-anchoring the AI-MC demarcation (AI-MC hearing, third paragraph).** Replace "These parameters do not merely fail to describe the phenomenon — they become structurally unfillable because the foundational definition of AI-mediated communication requires the computational agent to operate 'on behalf of a communicator' (p. 90). In our coordination setting, the algorithmic third party operates on behalf of no one present at the interaction." with:

> The decisive difference, however, is categorical rather than parametric. Hancock and colleagues' agent operates on messages — modifying, augmenting, or generating them — and every dimension of their framework characterizes that operation. The intermediary in our setting renders verdicts: it matches, ranks, and can terminate, and no setting of magnitude, autonomy, or optimization goal converts an agent that rewrites a message into a party that binds both interactants to a determination neither commissioned. Their definitional requirement that the agent operate "on behalf of a communicator" (p. 90) marks the same boundary from the other side: in our coordination setting, the algorithmic third party operates on behalf of no one present at the interaction.

**3.4 The Gibbs et al. rebuttal (HMC hearing, after "The capacity this field theorizes remains, by structural definition, a competence oriented toward the machine.").** Insert:

> A second version of their critique cuts closer: a construct locating the capacity inside an individual might itself repeat the methodological individualism Gibbs and colleagues indict. The construct's two foundational properties answer it. Algorithmacy is socially acquired — Zhou and colleagues' (2025) antecedents and Sutherland and colleagues' (2020) peer sensemaking both show the capacity produced collectively — yet individually held and individually exercised at the moment of coordination, which is what its uneven distribution across formally equivalent participants requires. A micro-foundation with social antecedents is not an individualism; it is the level of analysis at which a structurational account must eventually cash out.

**3.5 The encirclement paragraph (What These Boundaries Share).** Replace "Abidin (2016) theorizes the strategic self-presentation… distinct from algorithmic visibility. Extant literature thus exhibits a symmetrical theoretical omission…" with:

> Abidin (2016) theorizes the strategic self-presentation that followers direct at a named human counterpart while deliberately bracketing the algorithm; Cotter (2019) and Bishop (2019) put both poles in frame, theorizing creators who pursue human audiences through an opaque, adaptive algorithm that evaluates their visibility. Yet even this creator-visibility scholarship stops short of the position our construct occupies: it theorizes practice and communal knowledge rather than a formalized competence, its audience is an aggregate pursued for attention rather than an interdependent counterpart in jointly executed work, and its algorithm evaluates and binds the creator alone rather than rendering one determination that binds both parties. Organizational theory currently possesses no construct that formalizes an individual competence integrating both poles of this triadic coordination challenge.

(Add Cotter 2019, *New Media & Society* 21(4), and Bishop 2019, *Convergence* 25(1) — verify the venue and pagination against the versions of record — to the references.)

**3.6 The banned opener (Zhou hearing).** Replace "Crucially, this contestation routes exclusively to the technical infrastructure." with:

> This contestation routes exclusively to the technical infrastructure.

**3.7 The literacy analogy's structural overreach (Constitutive Operations, final paragraph of the pre-triadicity material).** Replace "The literacy analogy establishes operational symmetry. Established literacies encompass two core operations — one receptive and one expressive (reading and writing, listening and speaking, interpreting quantitative data and computing with it) — because texts and numbers remain static artifacts during user engagement. Keeping track becomes a distinct operational requirement when the mediating infrastructure operates as an autonomous, dynamically moving party." with:

> The analogy supplies lineage and a name, not the operational architecture. Texts and numbers do not evaluate their users, so no inventory of reading's or computing's operations can be transposed onto a mediating party that does; the three operations are derived instead from what the triad withholds, in the subsection that follows. What the lineage does mark is the point of departure: keeping track has no counterpart among the established literacies because their media hold still.

**3.8 The Long & Magerko quotation (AI Literacy hearing, first paragraph).** Pending verification against the version of record, amend to:

> Long and Magerko (2020) define AI literacy as "a set of competencies that enables individuals to critically evaluate AI technologies; communicate and collaborate effectively with AI; and use AI as a tool online, at home, and in the workplace" (p. 2).

**3.9 De-duplicating one ceiling counterfactual (HMC hearing).** Replace "An individual may exhibit comprehensive mastery within human–machine communication — treating the computational system as an interlocutor, decoding its symbolic cues, and sustaining relational rapport — without ever coordinating interdependent labor with another human through an automated system that simultaneously evaluates and binds both parties." with:

> Full mastery of this repertoire — the system treated as interlocutor, its cues decoded, rapport sustained — still coordinates nothing with the human on the far side of the arrangement.

## Findings, ranked most-damaging first

1. **Internal contradiction between the breakdown claim and the field site.** The claim that the counterpart cannot observe, assess, or affectively register the exchange (CMC hearing ¶3) and "never occupies an evaluative position" (Categorical Delineation, final ¶) is contradicted by the Hult gate's public peer reviews and upvotes (Empirical Strategy, "Why Hult Instantiates"). Fix: 3.1 and 3.2 — restate as severing the verdict-bearing link between appraisal and consequence.
2. **The encirclement's counterpart pole rests on one cherry-picked paper.** "The framework that centers the human counterpart" (Abidin, singular) is falsified by Cotter (2019), Bishop (2019), and the folk-theories/algorithmic-self-presentation literature (What These Boundaries Share ¶3). The novelty claim narrows — to the absence of a *formalized competence construct* for interdependent work under a doubly binding verdict — and survives in that form. Fix: 3.5.
3. **The AI-MC demarcation stakes everything on a contestable property.** "Structurally unfillable" parameters and "on behalf of no one present" (AI-MC hearing ¶3) can be blocked by Hancock et al.'s own treatment of platform-serving optimization; the message-operation/verdict-rendering distinction, already present but subordinated, is the demarcation that survives. Fix: 3.3, plus verification of Hancock et al.'s optimization-goal discussion.
4. **The Gibbs et al. rebuttal answers the compositional objection and dodges the individualism objection** — which the paper's own citations of Zhou's social antecedents and Sutherland's peer sensemaking arm (HMC hearing ¶¶2–3). Fix: 3.4.
5. **Inheriting Spitzberg's criteria while deleting their administrator is asserted, not argued** (CMC hearing ¶5; Categorical Delineation, final ¶). Competence reverts to an ability construct; Sandberg licenses the reversion; the paper must connect them. Fix: 3.2.
6. **The literacy/numeracy/oracy analogy is genealogical but is used structurally** (Introduction — locked — and Constitutive Operations); the two-operation characterization of established literacies is contestable and oracy is the weakest member. Fix: 3.7 in the unlocked section; leave the locked introduction, where the analogy's use is definitional rather than derivational.
7. **Quote and fact fidelity in the freshly merged section is unverified**: the Long & Magerko word order (3.8), "pre-registered" attributed to Zhou et al., and every page-anchored quote needs one pass against the PDFs before Lima (Part 1, §1.9).
8. **Prose uniformity concentrated in the rewritten section**: the sevenfold inheritance close, the fivefold ceiling counterfactual, the superlative drumbeat, "foundational"/"structural"/"systematically" inflation, one banned opener ("Crucially"), doubled "candid." Fixes: 3.6, 3.9, plus the Part 2 program.
9. **Minor accuracy softeners**: Table 1's "passive transmission channel" undersells Spitzberg's media factors; "primary dependent variable" overstates the receiver's place in Hancock et al.'s agenda; Guzman & Lewis's own mediator/communicator blurring deserves one acknowledging sentence.

## Closing note

The paper's biggest genuine strength, from this lens: it reads my field's foundational texts the way the field itself reads them — the relational location of Spitzberg's model, the stipulated (not accidental) dyad in HMC, Rahman's clients documented but untheorized, Zhou's counterpart present in the nomological net and absent from the construct. Most organization-theory papers that borrow communication constructs summarize abstracts; this one argues from item wordings, outcome criteria, and methodological appendices, and its four boundary conditions turn the review into a falsifiable test rather than a tour. That is rare, and it is why the section is worth the repair work rather than a rewrite.

The one thing only the author can supply, which no reviewer can fabricate: the ground truth of the Hult gate. Whether peer appraisal there is genuinely public, whether the algorithm assigns reviewer pairs, and what exit or appeal rights exist are not details — finding 1, the paper's most damaging, turns entirely on what the gate actually shows and withholds, and the manuscript's own comment block concedes these facts are unconfirmed. Until the author establishes them from the running system, the communication-theoretic spine of this paper rests on an empirical description no one else can check.
