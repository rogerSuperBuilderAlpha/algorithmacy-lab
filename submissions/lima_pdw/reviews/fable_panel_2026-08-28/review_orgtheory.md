# Review — Organization Theory / Management Theory Specialist

**Manuscript:** "Algorithmacy: A Competency for Coordinative Co-optation" (`manuscript/PAPER.md`, 18,204 words incl. appendices and references)
**Panel:** Fable six-seat panel, 2026-08-28. **Lens:** OT/OS construct rigor, coordination-forms fit, novelty, PDW readiness.

---

## Verdict

**Major revisions.** The single most important fix: the paper asserts, five times, that algorithmacy is separable from the standing question — "the micro-foundation coordinative co-optation needs regardless of how that structural question resolves" — and never once argues it, while its own Proposition 4 supplies the counterexample logic: if transparency changes the competency required, so, plausibly, does standing. Replace the repeated assertion with one argument for why the three operations are invariant across candidate resolutions of standing (or scope the construct honestly to the current standing-less state), and the paper's central framing move becomes defensible instead of a target.

That is the most important fix because it is the paper's spine. The most *damaging* finding at a roundtable, though, is #2 below: judged by the paper's own Table A1 recognition criteria, the Hult site is not coordinative co-optation.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and argument structure

### 1.1 Suddaby's four criteria, scored as the paper demands

The paper invokes Suddaby (2010) on itself (§ "Algorithmacy: A Construct for Coordinative Co-optation"), so it gets scored by them.

**Definition: pass, with one wince.** The definitional sentence in the introduction — interpret, specify intent through, track — is precise, welded once in the literacy/numeracy/oracy pattern as the register profile requires, and the three glosses (opacity, adaptivity, intermediary) are exact. The wince is "we formalize algorithmacy as a satisficing construct" (Introduction, ¶2). Constructs do not satisfice; theorists do. The later passage makes the same error more visibly: "The construct also satisfies Simon's (1997) formal behavioral sense: it establishes an aspiration threshold and terminates once that theoretical requirement is satisfied" (Theoretical Implications). A construct does not establish aspiration thresholds or terminate; the *theorizing* stops at an aspiration level. This is a two-sentence fix (Part 3, R2), but as written it is precisely the kind of borrowed-term looseness Suddaby's essay warns against, deployed in the section that cites Suddaby.

**Scope conditions: pass.** Opaque, adaptive, mutually binding; the hybrid boundary (freelance marketplaces) and the core cases (dispatch queues, blind peer-evaluation) are cleanly separated. This is the best-executed of the four criteria.

**Semantic relations: half-pass.** The section "Semantic Relations and Hypothesized Covariation" maps algorithmacy against exactly two of the seven reviewed constructs (Zhou et al., Rahman). The other five — including the two that got the longest hearings, Spitzberg and Sutherland — get relational placement only implicitly, inside their hearings, and never as hypothesized covariation. Suddaby's criterion is relationships to *neighboring constructs*, plural and systematic. Either extend the table (Spitzberg's appropriateness/effectiveness criteria are explicitly inherited — that is a semantic relation begging to be formalized; Sutherland's platform literacy is named a "strong alternative" in the conclusion but given no predicted covariation) or state why only the two measured rivals warrant predictions.

**Coherence within the broader framework: pass on paper, undercut by the micro-foundations problem in 1.2.**

### 1.2 The load-bearing claims a hostile referee attacks first

**Attack 1: the standing/competency separability claim.** The move appears in the abstract, the introduction, the "One clarification" paragraph, the Simon passage, and Dissertation Coherence — five statements, zero arguments. The claim is: algorithmacy is "the individual capacity a participant must exercise to act within whatever standing the form eventually assigns her." For that to hold, the three operations must be invariant across candidate resolutions of standing. But the paper's own machinery says competency requirements covary with structure: Proposition 4 states that changing one structural property (transparency) reduces or qualitatively changes the algorithmacy required. Standing is a structural property of exactly the same kind. If regulation granted the enrolled actor an employee-like hearing or a buyer-like exit right, the intermediary would bind differently — and "keeping track," at minimum, changes character when a rule shift can be contested rather than only detected. A hostile referee will run Proposition 4's own logic against the separability claim and the paper currently has no answer on the page.

The answer exists, and it is not hard: the operations are defined against opacity, adaptivity, and bindingness — features of the *arrangement* — and every candidate resolution of standing that leaves those three features intact leaves the competency intact; resolutions that remove them (mandated transparency, appeal rights that de-bind the verdict) shrink the form itself out of the construct's scope conditions, which is Proposition 4 generalized. One paragraph making that argument converts the paper's most-repeated assertion into its most defensible claim, and lets four of the five repetitions be cut (see Part 2 on the repetition itself).

A related wince for this audience: the paper uses "micro-foundation" as its genre label — abstract, introduction, clarification paragraph, Dissertation Coherence — and never touches the micro-foundations literature (Felin & Foss 2005; Felin, Foss, & Ployhart 2015; Barney & Felin 2013). In OT that term is not a synonym for "individual-level construct"; it carries an aggregation obligation — show how the macro pattern is produced or sustained by individual action and interaction. The paper actually *has* the aggregation mechanism: Proposition 3 (competent navigation absorbs friction that would otherwise surface as breakdown, stabilizing the arrangement) is a genuine micro-to-macro link, and it is buried in a subsection titled as a paradox. Cite the micro-foundations literature once, and promote Proposition 3 explicitly as the clause that earns the term (Part 3, R7). Without it, "micro-foundation" reads as branding.

**Attack 2: the Hult site fails the paper's own recognition criteria.** Table A1's empirical-recognition row for coordinative co-optation reads: "Enrolled participants who operate neither as employees, buyers, nor chosen partners." Hult students are formally enrolled members of a university, in a credit-bearing course, facing a gate their accountable instructor designed, inside an institution that certainly has grade-appeal procedures. By the paper's own row, that is hierarchy with an algorithmic instrument — Kellogg, Valentine, and Christin's (2020) contested terrain *inside* an employment-like membership relation — not enrollment without standing. The paper anticipates the objection ("hierarchy wearing an algorithmic costume") but answers it entirely at the level of gate mechanics: concealed weighting, no instructor vote, moving codebase. Those establish opacity, adaptivity, and within-gate bindingness; they do not touch the standing dimension, which is the very dimension Paper 1 says defines a form's mechanism. The defense addresses the wrong column of its own table.

Two honest repairs are available, and the paper should pick one explicitly: (a) argue that the gate, not the course, is the arrangement — standing *within the gate* is what Table A1's row describes, and within the gate neither party is employee, buyer, or chosen partner; or (b) concede the site is a controlled analogue that reproduces the form's operating conditions rather than a naturalistic instance, which is what Appendix C's fourth study already implies (Part 3, R9). As written, a discussant who has read Appendix A closes the trap in one move.

Connected to this: the two flagged unknowns (reviewer assignment; exit/appeal rights) are flagged with admirable honesty, but the paper does not say what turns on them. The appeal-rights fact is load-bearing, not housekeeping — a formal appeal routed to a human authority softens the bindingness on which the site's claim to instantiate the form depends, and the Table B1 module-4 disconfirmation row ("appeals route exclusively to technical administrators") presupposes an answer to it. Say so (Part 3, R8). Also: the phrase "Two of PB's six diagnostic questions" appears in the submission's main text. No workshop reader knows who PB is; this is an advisor's initials leaked from the revision process into the manuscript. It must come out.

### 1.3 The novelty claim, actively falsified

The delimited claim — no construct combines an active human counterpart with an opaque, independently consequential, evaluating-and-binding intermediary — is well engineered. It survives the obvious rivals because the paper pre-empts them: node-counting attacks die against Hancock et al. (handled), the triadic structural literature (Katsh & Rifkin, Curchod et al., Cameron, Healy & Pekarek) is distinguished on the theory-of-power vs. participant-capacity axis, and the systematic reviews (Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum; Gagrčin et al.; Iyamu et al.) close off the "someone measured it" escape. My attempts to kill it:

- **Jarrahi & Sutherland (2019), "Algorithmic management and algorithmic competencies."** This is the namesake construct — the earliest paper to put "algorithmic competencies" in a title — and it sits in the manuscript's own reference list *unengaged in the text* (only the 2020 Sutherland et al. paper gets a hearing). I believe the claim survives contact — their competencies are worker-vs-algorithm appropriation moves, dyadic in exactly the way the paper's four conditions exclude — but a reviewer who finds the direct-namesake rival in the bibliography and nowhere in the argument will not extend the benefit of the doubt. Give it two sentences inside the Sutherland hearing or cut the entry.
- **Rosenblat & Stark (2016).** Uber drivers managing *passengers* — soliciting ratings, pre-empting complaints — because passenger ratings feed an opaque, binding system. That is counterpart-directed action under a binding intermediary, empirically documented in a paper the manuscript cites only for "information asymmetries" in Appendix A. It does not kill the claim — no construct, no dimensional structure — but it means the *phenomenon* is older and better documented than the Manky (2026) paragraph implies, and the paper's narrow claim ("no construct") should never be allowed to read as the broad one ("unnoticed capacity"). Which brings me to:
- **The introduction violates the paper's own narrowing.** The first paragraph states "Organization theory has not named the competency an individual must exercise to coordinate within this algorithmic architecture." That is the un-narrowed form. The register profile's own rule is right: Zhou et al. (2025) *did* name a competency — against the system; the claim that survives is that no one has named the one required when the counterpart is still in the picture. The body (¶3 of the introduction, "What These Boundaries Share") gets this exactly right; the opening sentence undoes it (Part 3, R1).
- **Cameron (2024) is mischaracterized, and she is the nearest OT-native rival.** "What These Boundaries Share" files her under "macro-structural theories of power, surveillance, and institutional domination" that articulate "no actionable communicative capacity." Cameron's ASQ paper is a worker-level account of choice-making practices — the "consent games" are individual navigation behaviors described in fine grain. The defensible distinction is not macro vs. micro; it is that Cameron theorizes what those practices *produce* (consent, stability) rather than the capacity that differentiates who navigates well — she has the practices without the competence construct. Drawn that way, the distinction is sharper *and* sets up Proposition 3, which is Cameron's mechanism with a competency-distribution twist. Drawn as written, it is a mischaracterization that any ASQ-literate discussant — and this is an OS/OT workshop; they will be there — can puncture from the floor (Part 3, R6).

**Verdict on novelty: survives, in its narrowed form only — and only if the introduction's opening is brought into line, Jarrahi & Sutherland is engaged, and the Cameron placement is corrected.** The claim as engineered in "What These Boundaries Share" (four analytical conditions) is genuinely strong work; the exposure is entirely at the edges the paper itself left loose.

### 1.4 The four propositions: contribution or restatement?

- **Proposition 1** (competency improves own outcomes, not procedural justice) is, as stated, close to unfalsifiable — an individual capacity can hardly rewrite institutional rules by definition, so the null is near-analytic — and its substance restates a critique the paper itself reports from its sources: Rahman's reactivity deepening subordination, Sutherland et al.'s literacies "shifting systemic coordination burdens entirely onto participant competence." The fix is to state the falsifiable version: high-algorithmacy participants *could* in principle win appeals, force disclosure, or teach peers — arrangement-level effects; P1 predicts gains stay private. That version is testable and honestly cites its lineage (Part 3, R10).
- **Proposition 2** (uneven distribution as a new axis of inequality among formal equivalents) is the second-level digital divide (Hargittai 2002; van Dijk) transposed to a new object. Real, worth stating, not novel — cite the lineage or a reviewer will supply it less charitably.
- **Proposition 3** is the paper's genuine theoretical contribution. Skilled navigation absorbing friction that would otherwise surface as visible breakdown is a competency-distribution mechanism for stability — kin to Cameron's manufactured consent and Burawoy behind her, but neither states it as a property of a *distributed capacity*. It is also, as argued above, the clause that earns "micro-foundation." It is currently the third item in a subsection framed as a caveat. Promote it.
- **Proposition 4** is a sound boundary-condition proposition (institutional design substituting for individual competence — an Okhuysen & Bechky substitution logic, worth one citation) and, to the paper's credit, it is turned against the construct's own durability. Its unacknowledged cost is that it arms Attack 1; resolving 1.2 resolves this.

Net: one contribution (P3), one good boundary proposition (P4), two formalized restatements presented without lineage (P1, P2). The section's framing — "none of them favorable to reading algorithmacy as an unalloyed good" — is honest and will play well at a PDW; the lineage gaps will not.

### 1.5 Coordination-forms fit and other specialist winces

- **The five-form typology arrives as settled when it is Paper 1's contested move.** Treating hierarchy/market/network — a *governance*-forms literature (Powell 1990; Williamson 1991) — and Okhuysen & Bechky's *coordination-mechanisms* literature as one "coordination forms" stack, and adding Selznickian co-optation (a strategy, in most readings, not a form) as a fifth column, is a synthesis a reader of this paper cannot evaluate and will want to contest. Paper 2 needs one honest sentence: Paper 1 argues for this construction; here it is a premise. Without it, the opening paragraph reads as though "classic scholarship maps three canonical coordination forms" were textbook consensus in those terms. It is not; Powell explicitly fought the placement of network on that continuum.
- **"Stark and Vanden Broeck (2024) defined coordinative co-optation in *Organization Theory*" (Theoretical Implications) — verify against the version of record before Lima.** Their paper is "Principles of algorithmic management"; Stark & Pais (2020) discuss platform co-optation of users. Whether either source uses the compound term "coordinative co-optation," or whether the compound is the dissertation's synthesis, determines whether this sentence is attribution or misattribution. If the coinage is the author's, write "building on," and own it — a named synthesis is a contribution, a false attribution at a workshop where people know Stark's work is a credibility hole. I could not verify the term from here; this is a check only the author's PDFs can settle.
- **Orphan references.** Jarrahi & Sutherland (2019) (discussed above), Bamberger & Pratt (2010), and Bothello et al. (2019) appear in the reference list and nowhere in the text. The latter two look like intended supports for the unconventional-context and Caribbean-site arguments in Appendix B — either cite them there (both would genuinely help: Bamberger & Pratt is *the* editorial defense of unconventional research contexts) or cut them. House rule: every entry cited, every cite resolving.
- **Verify the transcribed numbers.** Zhou et al.'s r=.37 / r=.04, N=275/213/230/225, α=.85; Spitzberg's page-anchored quotes; Rahman's eighteen clients / eighty freelancers. I have no reason to doubt them and no ability to check them from here; the density of page-cited quotation in the seven hearings is a strength *only if* every one survives a source check, and at a PDW with the cited authors' students in the room, one wrong page number taxes all the others.
- **Claim-first violations.** The Extant Constructs section opens on a definitional wind-up ("A construct, within this theoretical framework, designates a formalized conceptualization of participant capacity…") — two sentences of throat-clearing before the Gittell material starts earning anything. The section's actual claim (the seven differ in where they put the counterpart, and none combines both poles) should open it. Several hearings likewise open on scene-setting rather than the boundary verdict; the Spitzberg and Hancock hearings each bury their verdict a full paragraph deep. In a 7-hearing, ~7,000-word section, claim-first openings are what keep a reader oriented.
- **How were the seven selected?** The four analytical conditions that bound the review arrive *after* the hearings, in "What These Boundaries Share." Retrofitted criteria are a standard reviewer target: one sentence at the head of Extant Constructs stating the selection rule (constructs of individual capacity vis-à-vis an algorithmic system with any claim to a human counterpart, drawn from communication, HCI, and organization literatures) closes it. Related exposure: five of the seven hearings are communication/HCI constructs; the OT-native candidates (Cameron's practices, Curchod's coping strategies, Kellogg et al.'s resistance repertoire) are handled in two compressed paragraphs. For *this* venue that ratio is inverted; the Cameron fix (1.3) partially repairs it.

### 1.6 The appendix restructuring: discipline, not evasion

It helps, and I would defend it at the roundtable. Table A1 is Paper 1's material and belongs in an appendix of Paper 2; the research-design detail in Appendix B is more complete than most published papers' method sections and its honesty about the two uncoded transcripts is exactly right ("neither counts as evidence for or against the three constitutive operations" is the sentence that buys the paper its credibility); Appendix C keeps the falsification criteria specific. Two caveats. First, the main-text/appendix seam leaks: whole sentences ("A zone, a term, a relation, a seat…", the Stark enrollment sentence) appear verbatim in both the Paper 1 summary section and Appendix A — an appendix may restate, but verbatim duplication reads as assembly rather than writing. Vary one instance. Second, the one thing the appendix restructuring *cannot* carry for the main text is the site-instantiation argument (Attack 2): the standing concession has to happen in the main text, because that is where the claim is made.

One unclaimed strength the design section should claim: the investigator wrote the gate's aggregation rule. That means ground truth about the concealed rule exists — participants' interpretings can be validated against the actual weighting, a design asset almost no platform study has (Rahman could never check freelancers' theories against Upwork's actual algorithm). The paper treats insider status only as a positionality problem to manage; it is also the single rarest measurement advantage the design owns.

## Part 2 — AI-slop / prose-uniformity audit (disciplinary-credibility items only)

A separate reviewer holds the copyedit brief; I flag only what damages the argument's credibility for an OT/OS reader.

1. **The Extant Constructs section is written in a different, inflated register from the rest of the paper, and the uniformity is itself the tell.** Counts, whole file: *structural* 77, *empirical* 74, *theoretical* 59, *systematic(ally)* 22, *foundational* 20, *constitutive* 19 — the bulk concentrated in the seven hearings. The introduction and the construct section read like the genre anchors; the hearings read like a machine holding a rigor-pose. Two mechanical signatures: (a) the participial-opener drumbeat — "Formulating one of the earliest systematic accounts…", "Synthesizing scholarship across…", "Investigating the structural transformation…", "Rejecting this assumption requires…" — the same sentence shape opening hearing after hearing; (b) noun-stack glosses where a verb would do ("complete this conceptual encirclement from the opposing pole," "the structural omission… thus became institutionalized across the broader psychometric tradition seeded by the foundational 2020 framework"). This is precisely the "performed rigor" the house file names, and for an OS reader it undercuts the section doing the paper's heaviest lifting.
2. **Self-narrated rigor, the house style's flat ban.** "We evaluate seven candidate perspectives in sequence, engaging each on its strongest theoretical ground before establishing the analytical limits of its scope" — the paper grading its own fairness. "This framework presents the most critical analytical challenge in the review, where any superficial critique based merely on node enumeration inevitably fails" — the paper praising its own sophistication before performing it. "Rejecting this assumption requires first addressing a reductive reading of Spitzberg that would render the critique trivial" — narrating care instead of enacting it. "…not yet established with the precision a workshop reviewer is entitled to expect" — ranking its own candor. Each has a one-sentence enactment available (Part 3, R3–R5, R8).
3. **The paper grades its sources.** "Rigorous demarcation of theoretical boundaries," "with foundational precision," "candid assessment," "candidly acknowledge," "formidable critique," "the most definitive theoretical significance," "vital empirical finding." Reviewed literature does not need adjectival applause, and an OT reader registers the pattern as a paper reassuring itself. Strip the evaluative adjectives; keep the verdicts.
4. **Verbatim repetition of the gap sentence.** "Active human counterpart [+ opaque/binding intermediary]" recurs five times in near-identical wording (abstract, introduction ×2, "What These Boundaries Share" ×2). House rule: one strong instance plus one compressed reformulation. Keep the introduction ¶3 statement (it is the best one) and the four-conditions version in "What These Boundaries Share"; compress the rest ("the counterpart and the binding intermediary never appear in the same construct" is a portable reformulation the later text can carry).
5. **The separability assertion repeats five times** (abstract, introduction, clarification ¶, Simon ¶, Dissertation Coherence). Repetition without argument reads as anxiety. One argued instance (1.2) plus one abstract mention suffices.
6. **The "hearing" metaphor** (4 uses) is good once — juridical framing suits a section that steelmans before bounding — and mannered by the fourth. Keep two.
7. **Em-dashes: 170 in 18.2k words (~9.3/1000)** against the venue-measured ~3/1000. The gloss-welding uses are correct and house-sanctioned; the paired dash-parenthetical bolting an aside mid-sentence ("— an inductive aggregation that lacks an architectural principle…") is the crutch form and accounts for much of the excess. Thin by roughly half, keeping the appositive-weld uses.
8. **Not slop, for the record:** the recurring end-of-hearing "what algorithmacy inherits from X" move is a sustained analytic template, not decoration — it is the section's best structural feature and should stay (vary the phrasing, keep the move). Likewise the short-sentence punctures ("Coordinative co-optation's column thins at exactly this point," "The next section formalizes that construct") are the house rhythm working.

## Part 3 — Line-level revisions (ranked by value)

**R1 — Introduction ¶1, the novelty sentence (aligns the opening with the paper's own narrowing).**
Replace: "Organization theory has not named the competency an individual must exercise to coordinate within this algorithmic architecture — whatever her formal standing within it — and we develop that account here."
With: "Zhou, Lei, Liu, Huang, and Hou (2025) have named the competency a platform worker exercises against the system itself; organization theory has not named the competency she must exercise when a human counterpart remains in the picture, reachable only through that system — whatever her formal standing within the arrangement — and we develop that account here."

**R2 — Introduction ¶2 and Theoretical Implications, the Simon misuse.**
Replace: "we formalize algorithmacy as a satisficing construct that incorporates the counterpart into an algorithmically bound triad"
With: "we formalize algorithmacy to a satisficing standard in Simon's (1997) sense — the construct names the individual capacity Paper 1's open cell requires and stops there — incorporating the counterpart into an algorithmically bound triad."
And replace: "The construct also satisfies Simon's (1997) formal behavioral sense: it establishes an aspiration threshold and terminates once that theoretical requirement is satisfied."
With: "Our theorizing stops at an aspiration level in Simon's (1997) sense: the threshold is the individual capacity Paper 1's fifth column left unnamed, not the standing question the column also raises, and we do not theorize past it."

**R3 — Extant Constructs, section opening (claim-first; kills the self-narration).**
Replace: "We evaluate seven candidate perspectives in sequence, engaging each on its strongest theoretical ground before establishing the analytical limits of its scope. **R4 — Spitzberg hearing, ¶2 opening.**
Replace: "Rejecting this assumption requires first addressing a reductive reading of Spitzberg that would render the critique trivial. His framework does not locate communicative competence as an isolated cognitive faculty residing within a single individual's mind."
With: "Spitzberg's model is not a solitary cognitive faculty lodged in one individual's mind, and reading it as one would make our critique too easy."

**R5 — Hancock hearing, ¶2 opening.**
Replace: "This framework presents the most critical analytical challenge in the review, where any superficial critique based merely on node enumeration inevitably fails. By its own formal definition, AI-mediated communication specifies a triadic configuration incorporating two human interactants and a computational agent."
With: "Counting nodes will not separate this framework from ours: by its own definition, AI-mediated communication already specifies a triad — two human interactants and a computational agent."

**R6 — "What These Boundaries Share," the Cameron/triadic-structures paragraph.**
Replace: "Yet all four formulations operate fundamentally as macro-structural theories of power, surveillance, and institutional domination. None articulates an actionable communicative capacity or behavioral sensibility for the focal actor navigating inside the triadic apparatus."
With: "Yet these formulations theorize what the triangle is and what it produces — power asymmetries, visibility gaps, manufactured consent — not a participant capacity with dimensional structure. Cameron (2024) comes closest: her drivers navigate the arrangement through constant, confined choices, described in fine behavioral grain. Her account explains what those practices produce — consent, and a stable arrangement — but not what capacity separates the driver who navigates well from the one who does not. That capacity is the missing term, and Proposition 3 below returns to her mechanism from the other side."

**R7 — "One clarification" paragraph, the micro-foundations obligation.**
Append: "The term micro-foundation carries an aggregation obligation (Felin, Foss, & Ployhart, 2015): an individual-level construct grounds a form only if its distribution across participants tells us something about how the form holds. Proposition 3 below is that clause — the distribution of algorithmacy shapes whether the arrangement absorbs friction or surfaces it as breakdown — and it, not any single participant's outcomes, is where the micro-competency meets the macro-form."

**R8 — Empirical Strategy, the flagged-unknowns paragraph (removes "PB," removes the self-ranked candor, adds what turns on the answer).**
Replace: "Two of PB's six diagnostic questions are not yet established with the precision a workshop reviewer is entitled to expect, and we flag rather than guess at them: …"
With: "Two facts about the gate are not yet established, and we flag rather than guess at them: whether the algorithm assigns which peers review which submissions, or whether pairing is left to self-selection; and what exit or appeal rights a participant holds if she disputes a verdict. Both are being confirmed for the Paper 3 course design. The second is load-bearing rather than procedural: a formal appeal routed to a human authority would soften the bindingness on which this section's instantiation claim depends, and Table B1's fourth disconfirmation criterion presupposes its answer."

**R9 — "Why Hult Instantiates Coordinative Co-optation," closing (the standing concession; pick repair (b) unless the author can argue (a)).**
Append: "One criterion in Table A1 the site does not meet, and we concede it rather than argue around it: enrolled students hold formal membership in a university hierarchy, with an accountable instructor standing behind the gate they face. Within the gate, neither submitter nor evaluator is employee, buyer, or chosen partner — but the gate sits inside a membership relation the form's naturalistic instances lack. We therefore claim the course as a controlled analogue that reproduces the form's operating conditions — opacity, adaptivity, mutual bindingness — not as a naturalistic instance of enrollment without standing; carrying the instrument to naturalistic sites is the fourth study's job (Appendix C)."

**R10 — Proposition 1 (falsifiable, with lineage).**
Replace: "**Proposition 1.** Higher algorithmacy improves a participant's own coordination outcomes without improving the procedural justice of the arrangement she coordinates within; the intermediary remains exactly as opaque and as unaccountable to her as before."
With: "**Proposition 1.** Higher algorithmacy improves a participant's own coordination outcomes while leaving the arrangement's procedural justice unchanged: gains concentrate in private navigation rather than in disclosure won, appeals granted, or rules made legible to others. Rahman (2021) documents the individual half of this pattern — reactivity that deepens subordination — and Sutherland, Jarrahi, Dunn, and Nelson (2020) its structural half, literacies that shift coordination burdens onto the participant; the proposition extends both to a competency developed enough to be worth teaching."

**R11 — Repetition triage (not a rewrite; a deletion list).** Keep the gap statement at introduction ¶3 and the four-conditions version in "What These Boundaries Share"; compress the abstract's version to one clause; delete the near-verbatim recurrences in introduction ¶2 and the opening of "What These Boundaries Share" ¶1, carrying the compressed reformulation "the counterpart and the binding intermediary never appear in the same construct" instead. Keep the separability claim in the abstract and the (newly argued, per 1.2) clarification paragraph; cut it from the introduction, the Simon paragraph, and Dissertation Coherence.
