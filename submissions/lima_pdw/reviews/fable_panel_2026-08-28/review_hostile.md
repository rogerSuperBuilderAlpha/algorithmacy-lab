# Reviewer 2 — Hostile Review

**Manuscript:** "Algorithmacy: A Competency for Coordinative Co-optation" (LIMA2026-002, OS/OT PDW, Universidad de Piura, October 2026)
**Reviewer role:** adversarial senior reviewer; every objection below quotes the text it attacks.

---

## VERDICT

**Major revisions — and I want to be explicit that this is the PDW-calibrated verdict. Submitted to *Organization Studies* or *Organization Theory* as it stands, this paper would be rejected**, and not on a technicality: its novelty claim survives only by a definitional carve-out engineered against its closest rival literature, its flagship site contradicts the construct's own scope conditions on two facts the paper admits it has not confirmed, and its abstract asserts an empirical status ("piloted across two developmental transcripts") that the paper's own Appendix B contradicts.

**The single most important fix:** resolve the contradiction between the standing-independence claim ("the micro-foundation coordinative co-optation needs regardless of how that structural question resolves," §Algorithmacy) and the construct's own scope conditions, which make algorithmacy trivial wherever exit and direct negotiation exist — because the two "flagged" Hult facts (reviewer-pair assignment; exit/appeal rights) are precisely the facts that decide which side of that scope boundary the site falls on. Until those two facts are confirmed, the paper cannot claim the site instantiates the form, cannot claim the construct is non-trivial there, and cannot claim the construct is standing-independent. Everything else is downstream of this.

## PART 1 — Theoretical rigor and argument structure

### 1.1 The novelty claim survives by a carve-out written to exclude its strongest rival

The paper's load-bearing claim is stated three times in nearly identical words: "no construct combines an active human counterpart with an opaque, independently consequential intermediary that itself evaluates and binds both parties" (Abstract; Introduction; §What These Boundaries Share). The seven hearings are conducted with care. But the claim is not tested against the seven constructs alone — it is tested against the literature, and the paper itself cites the literature that sinks it, then defines it away in a single clause.

In §What These Boundaries Share, the paper concedes: "In online dispute resolution, Katsh and Rifkin (2001) conceptualized the digital infrastructure hosting an interaction as an active 'fourth party' that exerts autonomous systemic effects over human disputants." That is: two active human counterparts (disputants), coordinating through a computational intermediary that is autonomous, consequential, and — in binding arbitration and automated blind-bidding designs, which are the canonical ODR cases — *evaluates and binds both parties*. This is the paper's claimed unpopulated position, occupied in 2001, in a book the paper cites.

The paper's escape is the third of its four boundary conditions: the intermediary must be distinguished from "a procedural fourth party operating within transparent dispute protocols" (§What These Boundaries Share). Transparency is doing all the work in that clause, and it is not an honest criterion, for two reasons. First, transparency is not constitutive of Katsh and Rifkin's fourth party; nothing in the fourth-party concept requires the disputants to see the procedure's internals, and plenty of deployed ODR (eBay's resolution engine, algorithmic settlement tools) is exactly as opaque to its users as the paper's "gate." Second — and this is the damning part — the paper's own use of Wing, Martinez, Katsh, and Rule (2021) in §Theoretical Implications refutes the carve-out: "Wing... argue that an institutional intermediary owes disputants procedural transparency... Algorithmic platform coordination operates by systematically withholding these protections." So the ODR literature has already theorized the *opaque, protections-withholding* fourth party — that is what Wing et al.'s normative argument is *about*. The paper cannot simultaneously (a) exclude ODR from the novelty claim on the ground that its fourth party is transparent and (b) cite ODR scholars theorizing the fourth party's opacity as the normative frame for algorithmacy's three operations. One of these two uses of the same literature must go, and either way the novelty claim has to be re-derived, not re-asserted.

The honest version of the claim is narrower: *ODR theorized the opaque binding intermediary from the system-design and normative side; no one has built the individual-level competency construct for the participant inside it.* That claim might survive. But the paper does not make it — it makes the stronger claim and hides the retreat in a relative clause. The same maneuver appears with Curchod et al. (2020), Cameron (2024), and Healy and Pekarek (2024): "all four formulations operate fundamentally as macro-structural theories... None articulates an actionable communicative capacity." Fine — but then the contribution is a levels-of-analysis translation of an already-mapped structure, and the paper should say so in the abstract instead of "no construct combines," which invites exactly this review.

**And one more, which is worse because it is silent:** Jarrahi and Sutherland (2019), "Algorithmic management and algorithmic competencies: Understanding and appropriating algorithms in gig work," sits in the reference list (Refs, l. 400) and is cited *nowhere in the body of the paper*. A paper whose contribution is a competency construct for algorithmic coordination lists a prior publication literally titled "algorithmic competencies," from the same author team as one of its seven reviewed constructs, and never gives it a hearing. Either it belongs in the seven-construct review — in which case its absence is a hole in the paper's central table — or it does not belong in the references. As shipped, a reviewer is entitled to suspect the paper knows this rival exists and chose not to look at it. That suspicion is fatal at a top venue.

### 1.2 The standing/scope contradiction: the construct is not standing-independent, and the paper's own scope conditions prove it

The paper's central framing device — repeated in the abstract, the introduction, §Paper 1 in Brief, and §Algorithmacy — is that algorithmacy is "the micro-foundation coordinative co-optation needs regardless of how that structural question [standing] resolves" (§Algorithmacy, "One clarification bears on everything that follows"). This is presented as modesty. It is actually incoherent, and the paper's own §Scope Conditions demonstrates the incoherence.

Scope Conditions: "The construct does not operate — or operates only trivially — in contexts governed by codified, static administrative rules, or in open market environments where transacting parties negotiate contractual terms directly." So whether algorithmacy is a real competency *at any given site* depends on whether participants there hold exit rights, appeal channels, and direct-negotiation capacity — that is, on their *standing*. If the standing question resolves toward "enrolled participants hold meaningful exit and appeal rights," algorithmacy at that site "operates only trivially" by the paper's own definition. The construct is therefore not standing-independent; its non-triviality is a direct function of how standing resolves. "A construct describing what a person can do is not a claim about what she is owed" is a nice sentence, but what a person *needs to be able to do* depends entirely on what she is owed and can invoke — the paper's Proposition 4 says exactly this ("transparency mandates are a policy path that does not require developing the competency at all"). The paper's own paradox section refutes its own framing device.

This matters because the "micro-foundation, not standing" move is functioning as an escape hatch, not a theoretical position. It lets the paper claim relevance to coordinative co-optation while declining to say a single falsifiable thing about what coordinative co-optation structurally requires. Ask the direct question: *what structural features must an arrangement have for algorithmacy to be the competency it demands?* The paper's answer is its scope conditions — opacity, adaptivity, mutual bindingness, no exit. But those are structural claims about the form, i.e., partial answers to the standing question the paper five times insists it is not answering. The paper is doing standing theory while disclaiming standing theory, and the disclaimer buys it immunity from being wrong. Cut the immunity. State the structural requirements as claims and defend them.

### 1.3 The two "flagged gaps" are not honest housekeeping — they are the site classification

§"Why Hult Instantiates Coordinative Co-optation, Not a Hybrid Form" concedes, in its second paragraph: "Two of PB's six diagnostic questions are not yet established... **whether the algorithm assigns which peers review which submissions**, or whether pairing is left to self-selection; and **what exit or appeal rights** a participant holds if she disputes a verdict."

Look at what the paper's own Table A1 says the form's operation *is*: "Enrollment: an algorithm matches parties **who did not choose each other**, ranks them, and can close accounts." Algorithmic matching of unchosen parties is the *defining operation* of coordinative co-optation in the paper's own comparative table, and whether the Hult gate performs it is one of the two unconfirmed facts. Exit rights are the paper's own diagnostic separating market standing from enrollment (Table A1, "Empirical recognition": "an unconstrained right of refusal"), and that is the other unconfirmed fact. These are not two loose ends among six; they are the two questions on which the section's title claim turns. A section headed "Why Hult Instantiates Coordinative Co-optation, Not a Hybrid Form" whose body admits it cannot yet establish whether the site has the form's constitutive operation is not flagging a gap — it is asserting a conclusion while conceding the premises are unverified. The title makes a claim the section then withdraws. You cannot argue the site is X while admitting you have not confirmed the facts that would make it X. Self-disclosure earns credit only when the disclosed uncertainty is peripheral to the claim; here it *is* the claim.

Retitle the section as a conditional ("If the gate assigns pairs and forecloses appeal, Hult instantiates the form") or confirm the facts before the manuscript deadline. There is no third option.

### 1.4 The site contradicts the construct even if both facts resolve favorably

Set the two open facts aside and grant them. The construct is built for an "unseen human counterpart" who "remains structurally inaccessible" (§Human–Machine Communication) and reachable only "through" the intermediary — the paper repeatedly disqualifies rival constructs (Sutherland's freelancers, Rahman's disintermediating workers) because their counterparts can be reached by *exiting* the algorithm. Now read the paper's own description of Hult: "written peer reviews and upvotes remained public" (App. B); the site "allow[s] participants to observe the counterpart judging while the decisive scoring mechanism stays out of view" (App. B, Research Site). The counterpart at Hult is *visible* — her name, her words, her vote. And she is a classmate: a person the participant shares a physical room, a Slack, and a semester with. The disintermediation that the paper's scope conditions treat as construct-breaking — "transacting parties frequently retain the capacity to migrate their collaborative relationships off-platform" is what demotes Upwork to a "hybrid boundary" (§Scope Conditions) — is not merely available at Hult; it is unavoidable. Students can just *talk to each other*. On the paper's own hybrid criterion, a co-present classroom cohort is a more extreme hybrid than Upwork, and the paper's flagship site sits outside the core of its own construct's domain. What remains opaque at Hult is the aggregation weighting — the algorithm — not the counterpart. Which means the competency the site can actually exercise and test is opacity-navigation with respect to the *system*: Zhou et al.'s (2025) construct, the very one the paper must show algorithmacy is distinct from. The site selects for the rival.

The paper needs an answer to this in the text, not in a future appendix. The only visible candidate — that the *aggregate verdict* is what binds, and no amount of hallway conversation reveals the weighting — rescues opacity but not the triad: it concedes that the counterpart is fully reachable outside the intermediary, which is the exact ground on which the paper excluded gig literacies.

### 1.5 The four propositions: two truisms, one disjunctive hedge, one real claim with no operationalization

The paradox section presents "four propositions... none of them tested here" as a contribution. Examine each for what observation could falsify it:

- **P1** — "Higher algorithmacy improves a participant's own coordination outcomes without improving the procedural justice of the arrangement... the intermediary remains exactly as opaque and as unaccountable to her as before." The second clause is true *by construction*: an individual-level capacity cannot alter an institutional property by definition — the paper defined algorithmacy as "a capacity to operate within an arrangement, not a judgment on the arrangement" one paragraph earlier. The only falsifiable fragment is "improves a participant's own outcomes," which is not a paradox but the construct's basic validity claim. P1 is the definition restated with an ominous tone.
- **P2** — unevenness "becomes a new axis of inequality among people the arrangement itself declares interchangeable." This is the Foundational Properties assumption (unevenness) plus the analytic observation that an unevenly distributed advantage produces unequal outcomes. That is what "advantage" means. Nothing could disconfirm P2 that would not first disconfirm the assumption it restates.
- **P3** — competent participants "stabilize a coordination arrangement that would otherwise register as fragile or unfair" by absorbing friction. This is the one genuine claim in the set — it predicts a negative association between cohort-level algorithmacy and visible breakdown/complaint, which is observable. But "would otherwise register as fragile or unfair" is a counterfactual with no stated comparison condition, no measure of "registering," and no account of how one distinguishes friction absorbed from friction absent. As written it cannot be scored.
- **P4** — transparency "should reduce, **or qualitatively change**, the algorithmacy required." The disjunction is an escape hatch: any observed result — reduction, increase, no change in level but change in kind — satisfies "reduce or qualitatively change." Delete the second disjunct or P4 predicts everything.

And note where testing is deferred: "the third and fourth studies of the research agenda (Appendix C)" — i.e., beyond even Paper 3, into studies with no site, no instrument, and no timeline. Propositions whose tests are two unwritten papers away are not propositions; they are mood.

### 1.6 The measurement model is incoherent on the paper's own account

§Constitutive Operations concludes the three operations "are neither reflective indicators of one underlying latent trait... nor strictly formative components... They are closer to sequential stages in a recursive cycle." Then Appendix B and Appendix C commit Paper 3 to "latent-factor dimensionality, internal coherence, and discriminant separation" and "pre-registered latent correlation thresholds." A construct that is by its author's own statement neither reflective nor formative has no defined latent structure for a discriminant latent-correlation test to be a test *of*. The paper waves this away — "The two strategies are not in tension" — by assertion, in one sentence, with no citation to any psychometric literature on modeling stage-structured constructs. Suddaby (2010), whom the paper invokes as its clarity standard, would call this an unresolved semantic relationship at the construct's core. Additionally: the "pre-registered latent correlation thresholds" are invoked three times (§Semantic Relations; App. B; App. C) and never stated. What is "moderate"? .3? .5? Registered where? A pre-registration claim with no registration, no values, and no repository reference is a rigor costume. Finally, the discriminant test is to run on a single credit-bearing course cohort; Zhou et al. needed samples of 275, 213, 230, and 225 for the same class of analysis. The paper models its psychometric ambitions on a protocol its site cannot come within an order of magnitude of powering, and nowhere says so.

### 1.7 The empirical section: what the abstract claims and what the paper contains are different things

The abstract: the design is "piloted across two developmental transcripts." Appendix B, Instrument Piloting: "The first is a student-protocol response read informally for instrument function; **the second, recorded 22 August 2026, has not yet been read at all.**" One transcript, read informally, is not "piloted across two transcripts." This is not pedantry — the abstract is the only part of a PDW paper every panelist reads, and it inflates the paper's empirical status by exactly the margin the body then painstakingly disclaims. The body's candor ("neither counts as evidence for or against the three constitutive operations") does not cure the abstract; it convicts it.

More broadly: the "Paper 3 will do the real work" architecture lets the paper claim an "empirical strategy" as its third contribution (Introduction: "Third, we present a concise empirical strategy") while the strategy's total realized content is one uncoded transcript, one unread transcript, a site whose classification is unconfirmed (§1.3), and a sample that cannot power the planned test (§1.6). A research design is a legitimate PDW contribution. But then the introduction's third step should be labeled a *design*, and the abstract should not use "piloted" as if piloting had occurred at protocol strength. And two items buried in Appendix B deserve daylight: (a) the Trinidad cohorts' "operational logs were not preserved," so gate mechanics there will be reconstructed via a "self protocol" — the investigator interviewed by an automated agent about his own two-year-old recollections — which is memory, not records, and cannot support the appendix's claim that "these diverse implementations **demonstrate** that opacity constitutes a structural property of the coordination arrangement rather than an artifact of a single platform interface." Three sites *designed by the same person* demonstrate nothing about opacity being structural rather than artifactual; they demonstrate that one designer builds opaque gates. "Demonstrate" must go. (b) "The instructor exercises no vote within the gate (Appendix B)" — a factual claim about the site, sourced to the paper's own appendix. A paper cannot cite itself as evidence for its site's properties.

### 1.8 The appendix relocation is triage, not architecture

The revision note frames moving Table A1, the site narrative, and the research agenda to appendices as giving "the construct more room." What it actually does is relocate the paper's three weakest load-bearing elements — the Paper 1 dependency (a comparison table from an unpublished companion paper no reviewer can check), the site justification with its unpreserved logs and self-interviews, and the research agenda whose studies 3 and 4 carry the only tests of the propositions — to the place a time-pressed panelist will skim. The main-text "Empirical Strategy" is four paragraphs, two of which are disclaimers. The result is a main text that reads clean because everything contestable has been moved offstage, and every §1.3–1.7 problem above lives in an appendix. At a PDW, whose entire point is to workshop the contestable parts, this is backwards: the site argument and the pilot status belong in the body, where the panel can work on them.

### 1.9 Smaller but real

- **"Satisficing construct"** (Introduction) is used 250 lines before it is glossed (§Theoretical Implications, via Simon). A construct-clarity paper that leaves its own self-description undefined for six sections fails the Suddaby standard it cites.
- **The literacy analogy cuts against itself.** The paper leans on literacy/numeracy/oracy for legitimacy, then declares "Whereas literacy, numeracy, and oracy constitute competencies for a medium, algorithmacy operates as a competency for a coordination form" (Introduction). If the category is different, the analogy is not a warrant — it is a brand. Why is the algorithm not simply a medium? The paper never argues it; it stipulates it.
- **Keeping track's triadicity hangs on an evidence-free "rarely."** "A shift in them is rarely visible as a direct system signal, and becomes visible instead as a change in what the counterpart's behavior... rewards" (§Why Each Operation Requires the Full Triad). "Rarely" is an empirical frequency claim with zero support — and the paper's own site contradicts it: at Hult "the underlying source code remains inspectable" (App. B). Where the codebase is inspectable, rule shifts *are* system-visible, and keeping track collapses into the system-facing monitoring the paper says it is not.
- **Uneven distribution is circular.** "If the competency were uniformly distributed, it could not explain the persistent variance in outcomes observed among participants" (§Foundational Properties). The property is postulated because the construct needs it to explain variance the paper has not measured — and Rahman's rival explanation of that same variance (platform dependence, evaluation shocks), which the paper itself calls "a formidable rival hypothesis" (§Reactivity), is acknowledged and then never neutralized.
- **Manky (2026) is conscripted.** "an explicitly counterpart-directed interpretive capacity... that currently lacks formal theoretical conceptualization" — Manky theorizes those practices as work-security strategies; "lacks formal conceptualization" is the paper's assertion about someone else's framework, made in the sentence that uses his data as the construct's only naturalistic sighting.
- **The manuscript ships with its own editorial memo.** Lines 9–32 are an HTML comment containing advisor initials, revision rationale, and the sentence "Confirm before this goes to reviewers." I am a reviewer, and it went to me unconfirmed. Relatedly, "Two of **PB's** six diagnostic questions" (§Why Hult Instantiates) — "PB" is never defined anywhere in the manuscript body. A reader who is not the author's advisor has no idea who PB is. Both must be stripped before any external eye sees this file again.

## PART 2 — AI-slop / prose-uniformity audit (adversarial lens)

The house style and register profile ban self-narrated rigor, emphasis markers, and uniformity of optimization. The Extant Constructs section — the paper's longest and most polished — violates all three, and the violations are not cosmetic: each one is prose doing rhetorical work to pre-empt an objection the argument has not actually answered.

**1. The seven-fold template is uniformity of optimization at section scale.** Every construct hearing runs the identical four-beat arc: (i) a steelman announcement ("Rejecting this assumption requires first addressing a reductive reading of Spitzberg that would render the critique trivial"; "Dismissing this scale as mere cognitive knowledge... misreads the underlying psychometric operationalization"; "This framework presents the most critical analytical challenge in the review, where any superficial critique based merely on node enumeration inevitably fails"); (ii) a hypothetical master who fails structurally ("An actor exhibiting mastery across every dimension of Spitzberg's model... experiences a structural impasse"; "An individual may exhibit comprehensive mastery within human–machine communication..."; "A worker could therefore achieve ceiling scores across all twelve items..."; "an individual could achieve ceiling performance across all seventeen competencies..."); (iii) a verdict sentence; (iv) an inheritance paragraph ("What algorithmacy inherits from X..."), seven times. This is the machine tell the style file names: every section the same shape, every third sentence auditioning. Worse, the steelman announcements are *self-narrated rigor* — sentences advertising the review's own fairness — which CLAUDE.md rule 4 bans outright ("No self-narrating rigor... zero instances of a sentence commenting on the paper's own care"). "We evaluate seven candidate perspectives in sequence, engaging each on its strongest theoretical ground before establishing the analytical limits of its scope" (§Extant Constructs) is a sentence about the paper's virtue, not about constructs.

**2. Banned and inflated vocabulary at diagnostic density.** "Crucially, this contestation routes exclusively to the technical infrastructure" (§Algorithmic Competency) — "Crucially" is on the register profile's banned list by name. "Foundational" appears 18 times ("foundational assumption," "five foundational parameters," "five foundational conceptual inquiries," "foundational precision," "Three foundational insights," "two foundational theoretical insights," "Foundational Properties"...); "systematically" 15 times; "structural/structurally" 53 times. When every boundary is "structural," every insight "foundational," and every omission "systematic," the words stop discriminating — and the drumbeat is doing rhetorical work: it makes seven ordinary literature-review judgments sound like theorems. "Theoretical formulations of visibility labor complete this conceptual encirclement from the opposing pole" is performed rigor in a single sentence — "encirclement" asserts a completeness the paragraph's two citations (one construct, one review) cannot deliver.

**3. Disclaimers as escape hatches.** "This paper does not resolve that structural question" appears, in variants, five times (Abstract; Introduction ×2; §Paper 1 in Brief; §Algorithmacy "One clarification"). The style file bans verbatim repetition of the gap sentence; this is verbatim repetition of the *anti*-gap sentence, and its function is insurance, not information — each repetition buys the paper another exemption from the standing question its scope conditions quietly answer (Part 1, §1.2). Similarly, "we flag rather than guess at them" and the revision note's "flagged, not invented" narrate candor about the two Hult gaps instead of resolving them — a concession dressed as a virtue, when the concession guts the section it sits in (§1.3). And the pilot section's triple disclaimer ("neither is coded, and neither is evidence for the construct"; "none of them a finding about algorithmacy"; "an argument for the instrument's readiness, not for what participants' accounts will show") is honest in the body while the abstract banks the word "piloted" — the disclaimers launder the abstract's overclaim.

**4. The "singular conclusion" sentence.** "the seven construct hearings alongside the literature's overarching meta-analyses yield a singular conclusion — no extant competence construct accords the interdependent human counterpart a constitutive analytical position" (§What These Boundaries Share). This lands one paragraph *after* the ODR fourth-party concession and one boundary-condition clause away from the carve-out that makes it true (§1.1). The rhetoric of inevitability ("yield a singular conclusion") is covering a definitional maneuver. Claim-first prose earns its claims; this one is purchased.

**5. Cohesion.** Credit where due: tail-head linkage and end-focus are mostly present, and the Paper 1 section ("A zone, a term, a relation, a seat") is genuinely good. But the Extant Constructs prose runs at a uniformly high Latinate density — "Formulating one of the earliest systematic accounts," "Underpinning this formulation is a foundational assumption," "This structural limitation subsequently propagated directly into psychometric operationalization" — with almost no short-sentence puncture for pages at a stretch. The style file's rhythm rule (dense sentences punctured by short ones) is obeyed in the Paper 1 and Constitutive Operations sections and abandoned in the seven hearings, which is itself evidence the hearings were optimized separately. Read the Spitzberg section aloud; nothing under twenty words occurs until its final paragraph.

9. **Site–counterpart problem (new paragraph, §Why Hult Instantiates or §Scope Conditions).** The paper must state and answer the objection of §1.4: peers at Hult are visible and co-present, so by the Upwork criterion the site is hybrid. If the answer is "the binding aggregate is unreachable through any off-platform channel," write that, and then explain why the same answer does not rehabilitate Upwork's Job Success Score.

## FINDINGS, RANKED MOST-DAMAGING FIRST

1. **Standing-independence is contradicted by the paper's own scope conditions** — the construct's non-triviality depends on exit/negotiation facts, i.e., on standing; the "micro-foundation, not standing" framing is an escape hatch that immunizes the paper from falsifiable structural claims. (§Algorithmacy "One clarification" vs. §Scope Conditions vs. Proposition 4.)
2. **The Hult site's classification rests on the two facts the paper admits are unconfirmed**, and those facts are the form's constitutive operation (algorithmic matching of unchosen parties) and its market-boundary diagnostic (exit) per the paper's own Table A1. The section title asserts what the section withdraws. (§Why Hult Instantiates, l. 297–301; Table A1, l. 486.)
3. **The site contradicts the construct regardless:** the Hult counterpart is visible, named, and co-present, and off-channel contact is unavoidable — the exact hybrid condition that demoted Upwork — so the site's residual opacity is system-facing and selects for Zhou et al.'s rival construct. (App. B Research Site, l. 526; §Scope Conditions, l. 183.)
4. **The novelty claim survives only via a carve-out ("transparent dispute protocols") that misdescribes ODR's fourth party**, while the paper's own use of Wing et al. (2021) proves ODR already theorized the opaque, binding, protections-withholding intermediary; and Jarrahi & Sutherland (2019), titled "algorithmic competencies," sits uncited in the reference list with no hearing. (§What These Boundaries Share, l. 167–169; §Theoretical Implications, l. 273; Refs l. 400.)
5. **The abstract misstates the empirical status:** "piloted across two developmental transcripts" vs. Appendix B's "the second... has not yet been read at all." (l. 36 vs. l. 552.)
6. **Propositions 1, 2, and 4 are unfalsifiable as written** (definitional, definitional, disjunctively hedged); P3 alone has content and lacks any comparison condition; all four are deferred beyond Paper 3 to unbuilt studies. (l. 279–287; App. C.)
7. **The measurement model is self-declared neither reflective nor formative, yet Paper 3's headline test is a latent-correlation discriminant test with unstated "pre-registered" thresholds on a single-course sample that cannot approach the N of the rival scale it tests against.** (l. 243; l. 189–191; App. B–C; cf.
