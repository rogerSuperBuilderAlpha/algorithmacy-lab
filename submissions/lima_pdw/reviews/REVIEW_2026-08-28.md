# Findings, most damaging first

## 1. The Hult site may fail the paper's own scope conditions

The construct is built on a counterpart who cannot be reached. "Unseen" appears eight times, and every
statement of the contribution turns on it: the counterpart is "structurally inaccessible," "structurally
barred from occupying an evaluative position," reached only "through a binding intermediary."

The scope conditions then rank arrangements by how completely they sever that reach. Online freelance
platforms are demoted to "a hybrid boundary" because "transacting parties frequently retain the capacity
to migrate their collaborative relationships off-platform." The core of the domain is "on-demand dispatch
networks, algorithmic piecework queues, and peer-evaluation systems that withhold score tallies."

Now the site. A sixteen-week credit-bearing course at Hult, where the counterparts are "student peers
whose qualitative reviews and upvotes remain publicly visible." These people share a classroom for four
months. They can find each other in a hallway. Their reviews are signed and public. What the arrangement
withholds is the *aggregate weighting*, not the counterpart.

The paper anticipates one objection here and answers it well — participants build the systems they submit
to, and opacity survives because the scoring weights are withheld even from builders. That is a good
paragraph. But it answers the wrong objection. **The words "co-present," "face-to-face," and "same room"
appear zero times in the manuscript.** A referee who has read the scope conditions will ask why a
freelance platform is a hybrid boundary case for permitting off-platform contact while a classroom, where
contact is continuous and free, sits at the core.

The answer is available and the paper should make it. Co-presence outside the gate does not restore
standing inside it: a student can ask a classmate anything except what she voted and why, because the
gate withholds exactly that, and no amount of hallway access recovers it. Reaching a counterpart socially
is not the same as answering her judgment. Make that argument explicitly in **Scope Conditions and
Boundary Limits**, and again in **The Research Site**, or narrow the claim to what the site can carry.

## 2. The construct is defined three different ways in three places

- **Abstract:** "the ability to interpret a traidic, opaque, adaptive intermediary, specify intent through
  it, and track its unannounced shifts."
- **Introduction:** "the capacity to interpret an opaque, adaptive intermediary, specify intent through
  it, and track its shifting parameters."
- **Constitutive Operations:** "Interpreting: Reconstructing, from ambiguous and fragmented system
  feedback, the underlying actions, constraints, and intentions of **both the algorithmic intermediary and
  the unseen human counterpart**."

The first two make interpreting an operation on the machine. The third makes it an operation on the
machine *and the counterpart* — which is the paper's entire novelty. A referee comparing the abstract
against §4 finds the contribution present in one and absent in the other. The paper cites Suddaby (2010)
for construct clarity and the first criterion is a precise definition; this fails it in the paper that
invokes it.

Two further problems in the abstract's version. **"Traidic" is a typo for "triadic"** — in the definition
sentence, in the abstract, in a paper going to a workshop panel. And *triadic* is the wrong modifier for
*intermediary* even spelled correctly: triadic describes the arrangement, and the intermediary is one
term inside it. An intermediary cannot itself be triadic.

Pick the §4 wording, and propagate it verbatim to both other locations.

## 3. The paper concedes one of its three operations to Rahman, in a sentence standing alone

> A dyadic competence model restricted to the person–system relationship requires only the interpretive
> dimension, which Rahman's (2021) framework already captures.

This is a one-sentence paragraph in **Constitutive Operations**, and it hands a hostile referee the claim
that a third of the construct is not new.

It is also inconsistent with the paper's own Rahman hearing, which argues the opposite at length: "Within
Rahman's model, uncertainty regarding which entity altered the metric remains an epistemic problem
concerning the behavior of the score itself. Within algorithmacy, by contrast, the coordination problem
concerns deciphering the latent intentions and communicative expectations of the human counterpart."
Under §4's own definition of interpreting, which reaches the counterpart's intentions, Rahman captures
half of it at most.

Either cut the sentence or restate what is true — that Rahman captures the system-facing half of
interpreting, which is why the operation is stated over both terms.

## 4. The uneven-distribution property is argued in a circle

> If the competency were uniformly distributed, it could not explain the persistent variance in outcomes
> observed among participants navigating identical algorithmic environments.

This assumes the competency explains the variance in order to establish that the competency varies. And
the paper has already granted, in the Rahman hearing, that "behavioral variance is driven by structural
platform dependence and historical evaluation shocks" and that this "presents a formidable rival
hypothesis to individual-level capacity models."

Restate the property as a prediction the third study tests, which is what it actually is, rather than as
a property the construct possesses.

Acquire it or attribute it through Zhou. Do not construct the entry from a secondary source.

Two traps in those entries, both recorded on the cards: Edwards here is **Autumn** Edwards, not Chad; and
the Gibbs author list is Gibbs, Kirkwood, Fang and Wilkenfeld, **not** Gibbs, Kim and Boyraz, which is a
different collaboration and a routine miscitation.

**Entry, never cited:** Bamberger and Pratt (2010), Bothello, Nason and Schnyder (2019), Merton (1987),

The first three are not housekeeping. They were the **warrant for the Trinidad strategic-research-site
argument**, and that argument now stands on the author's say-so with only ILO (2025) and Chigbu (2026)

colleagues in *Organization Studies* were acquired to support. The workshop's distinctive aim is
theorizing from emerging contexts "without reducing such contexts to mere empirical sites." The section
that answers that aim is the one whose citations were dropped. Restore them.

## 9. Smaller theory problems

**"Satisficing" is attached to the wrong object.** The abstract calls algorithmacy "a satisficing
construct," and **Theoretical Utility** says "the construct also satisfies Simon's (1997) formal
behavioral sense: it establishes an aspiration threshold and terminates once that theoretical requirement
is satisfied." Satisficing in Simon is a decision procedure an *actor* follows against an aspiration
level. Making it a property of a *construct*, where the theorist is the one who stops searching, is a
metaphorical extension. It reads as intended — the construct is sufficient for the open cell and not a
complete account of the enrolled actor — but a reviewer who works on bounded rationality will wince.
Either say it without Simon's term, or say explicitly that the analogy is to the theorist's stopping
rule.

**Table 1 collides with Hancock's Table 1.** The AI-MC hearing cites "(Table 1, p. 91)" while the paper's
own Table 1 is the five-forms comparison. Write "their Table 1" or drop to "p. 91."

**The research questions are sentence fragments.** "Formally, we ask:" is followed by three lowercase
clauses ending in commas and a period. In the paper's most formal move, formalize them.

**Prior-art check — the novelty claim holds, narrowed, and the paper narrows it correctly.** I tried to
break it. Katsh and Rifkin, Curchod and colleagues, Cameron, and Healy and Pekarek all name the triad and
the paper concedes all four. The three frontier near-misses are conceded in the conclusion. What carries
the claim is scope condition 3 — the intermediary reads both parties and binds them — and the paper says
so. This is the strongest part of the argument and it should not be touched.

# Part 3 — line-level rewrites, ranked by value

Each is shorter and more verbal than what it replaces. Register held: "we" for our moves, third person
for sources, closed em-dashes.

**1.** Abstract definition. Replace:

> the ability to interpret a traidic, opaque, adaptive intermediary, specify intent through it, and track
> its unannounced shifts

with the §4 wording, so all three statements agree:

> the ability to interpret an opaque, adaptive intermediary and the counterpart it stands between, to
> specify intent through it, and to track its unannounced shifts

**2.** The Spitzberg close. Replace:

> The algorithmic arrangement does not merely depress the communicator's competence score—it
> systematically dismantles the relational instrument required to measure it.

with:

> The arrangement has not lowered her score. It has removed the instrument.

**3.** The Zhou opener of the second paragraph. Replace:

> Dismissing this scale as mere cognitive knowledge of system mechanics misreads the underlying
> psychometric operationalization.

with:

> A hearing that files this as knowledge of how the system works has read the label and not the items.

**4.** The Zhou counterpart turn. Replace:

> The analytical limitation of their instrument is therefore not an absolute omission of the human
> counterpart, but rather the structural status accorded to her across the scale.

with:

> The question was never whether the counterpart is absent from their instrument. She is in it, in four
> distinct roles. Not one of them makes her a party to the competency.

**5.** The Spitzberg test opener. Replace:

> This relational architecture clarifies the theoretical boundary.

with:

> That architecture sharpens the test.

**6.** The uneven-distribution property. Replace:

> If the competency were uniformly distributed, it could not explain the persistent variance in outcomes
> observed among participants navigating identical algorithmic environments.

with:

> We predict that algorithmacy varies across participants the platform treats as equivalent, and that the
> variance survives controls for technical skill, task mix, and tenure. The third study tests it. Rahman
> (2021) supplies the rival: dependence and prior score shocks, not capacity.

**7.** The Rahman concession. Replace the standing sentence:

> A dyadic competence model restricted to the person–system relationship requires only the interpretive
> dimension, which Rahman's (2021) framework already captures.

with:

> A dyadic model needs only the system-facing half of interpreting, and Rahman (2021) supplies it. The
> operation is stated over both terms because the counterpart's intentions have to be reconstructed too,
> and no dyadic model reaches them.

**8.** The scope-condition repair, new text for **Scope Conditions and Boundary Limits**:

> Physical co-presence does not lift an arrangement out of this domain. Participants who share a classroom
> can ask one another anything except the one thing the gate withholds — what a peer scored and on what
> basis. Social access to a counterpart is not the same as an answer to her judgment, and it is the
> judgment that binds.

# Closing

**The genuine strength.** `# Extant Constructs` is now the best part of the paper by a distance, and it
is doing something most competing-constructs sections never do: it argues each rival's strongest case
before it rules. The Gibbs paragraph — an objection to our thesis raised from inside the field we are
criticizing, answered on a precise distinction between an institutional third term and a party — is the
kind of move that makes a workshop panel take the construct seriously. The falsification criteria in
Table 2 and the four studies each with a stated disconfirming result are real, not decorative. And the
paper is honest about having two interviews.

**What only the author can supply.** Two things. First, whether the letter to the conveners went, and if
not, what to say to them about a paper that no longer carries the Caribbean evidence its accepted title
promised. Second, and worth more than every repair in this file: the second interview transcript, uploaded
22 August, is still uncoded, and neither transcript has yet answered the question the construct actually
turns on — **what the participant thought the gate was doing.** Sandberg is the paper's anchor for
competence as a conception of work, and the empirical section reports only what the participant *did*.
One sentence of what she believed the gate was deciding, in her own words, would be the only place in
this manuscript where the construct and the data touch.
