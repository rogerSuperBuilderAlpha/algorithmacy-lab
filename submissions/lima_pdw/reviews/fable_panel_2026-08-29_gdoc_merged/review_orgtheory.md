# Peer review — Organization Theory lens
**Manuscript:** "Algorithmacy: A Competency for Coordinative Co-optation" (PAPER_gdoc_merged_2026-08-29.md, 18,337 words incl. appendices)
**Reviewer role:** senior OT reviewer, OS/OT PDW Lima 2026

---

## Verdict

**Major revisions.** The single most important fix: demonstrate — do not assert — that the intermediary *binds both parties* at the Hult field site. Mutual bindingness is the one property that separates algorithmacy from all seven rival constructs in Table 1, and the manuscript never shows what binding determination the gate executes on the *peer evaluator*. If bindingness runs one way at the flagship site, the paper's differentiator fails at its own empirical home, and everything downstream (scope conditions, Table 1's last row, the four analytical conditions) inherits the wound.

---

## Step 0 — Register

This is a solo-authored construct-development paper in the AMR/Suddaby genre, aimed at an *Organization Theory* / *Organization Studies* readership, deliberately formal, deliberately restrained on normative claims, with a "she/her" counterpart convention and a sustained per-construct template (status of algorithm → status of counterpart → boundary → what algorithmacy inherits). I hold it to the bar of Rahman (2021), Cameron (2024), and Suddaby (2010): named agents, claim-first, first person for the analytic moves, dense sentences punctured by short ones, every abstraction touching a case. The per-construct template and the definitional property-triple are deliberate analytic structures, not tics, and I do not flag them as such — I flag only where the template's *entry lines* go identical or the triple recurs verbatim past the point of doing work.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and argument structure

### What is genuinely strong

The comparative core (lines 52–174) is the best construct-boundary work I have seen at a PDW. Seven candidate constructs, each read closely enough to quote page numbers, each interrogated with the *same* structural question (where does the model put the human counterpart?), each closed with an explicit inheritance statement saying what algorithmacy takes and what it rejects. The Zhou et al. section (118–130) is exemplary: it engages the rival's psychometrics at item level (Items 6, 8, 11), uses their own discriminant-validity numbers, and turns their antecedent findings into support for the socialization claim. The Rahman section does the same, and honestly names Rahman's dependence/shock findings as "a formidable rival hypothesis" that any test of algorithmacy must control for. The Suddaby checklist is tracked explicitly (definition, scope, semantic relations, coherence), the nomological network makes directional, falsifiable predictions including a *predicted null* (Embracing), and Appendix B states disconfirming criteria per module. Whetten's What and Where/When are satisfied. The Why is where trouble starts.

### Finding 1 (most damaging): mutual bindingness is asserted, not demonstrated

The construct's definitional differentiator, stated six times verbatim, is an intermediary that "evaluates and binds both parties." Now read the site description (line 291): the submitter's progression depends on a score she cannot inspect — bindingness, yes. The peer evaluators "contribute assessments to an opaque algorithm whose ultimate weighting logic remains hidden from them." That is *opacity* toward the evaluator, not *bindingness* upon her. What determination does the gate execute on the reviewer? Does her own standing, grade, or progression turn on the gate's verdict about someone else's pull request? The manuscript never says. The same asymmetry haunts the canonical cases: on Uber, ratings bind the driver; the passenger faces matching, not verdicts. The Cotter comparison (line 166) concedes the point for Instagram — "the platform algorithm evaluates and binds the creator alone" — which means the paper already knows one-sided bindingness disqualifies a case. A hostile referee will ask which real settings survive the two-sided test, and whether the answer is "almost none except the course the author built." Fix: either (a) specify the reviewer-side binding mechanism at Hult concretely (e.g., reviewing accuracy feeds the reviewer's own gate score — if true, say so, with the mechanism), or (b) weaken the definitional property to "evaluates both parties and binds at least the focal actor," and re-run Table 1's last column under the weakened property to show the vacancy still holds. Option (a) is far better if it is true.

### Finding 2: the deduction that isn't shown

The paper claims, at least eight times, that the three operations "derive deductively" from the structural properties of the triad — this is its stated superiority over Long and Magerko's inductive catalog (line 116). But the derivation is never performed. The three structural properties are opacity, adaptivity, bindingness (line 182). The three operations map as: opacity → *interpreting*; adaptivity → *keeping track*; and *specifying intent* → ...what? Line 220 grounds it in "the narrow, standardized signaling channels permitted by the intermediary" — channel constraint, which is not one of the three named properties. Meanwhile bindingness, the property doing all the differentiating work in Finding 1, generates no operation at all. So the advertised architecture is three properties and three operations with a two-thirds mapping, a fourth unnamed property (channel constraint) carrying one operation, and the headline property carrying none. A construct-clarity referee in the Suddaby tradition will catch this in one read. Fix: either add channel constraint as a fourth named structural property and show the full property→deficit→operation table (one paragraph and a small exhibit would do it), or derive *specifying intent* from bindingness (plausible: because the verdict binds, every message is simultaneously evidence — encoding under evaluation). Then stop saying "deductively" until the deduction is on the page.

### Finding 3: does the Hult site instantiate coordinative co-optation at all?

The "Why Hult Instantiates Coordinative Co-optation, Not a Hybrid Form" section (289–293) anticipates the hybrid-form objection but answers only opacity and adaptivity. Stark and Vanden Broeck's form is defined by *automated enrollment of unchosen parties* and *unilateral termination*. At Hult: enrollment is chosen (students register for a course), termination does not exist (the gate cannot expel anyone), participant standing is heavily formalized (tuition contract, grade-appeal rights, instructor duty of care — the very "unformalized standing" the form is defined by is fully formalized here by the university), and — the paper's own concession at line 293 — whether peers are even algorithmically matched or self-selected is "an ongoing design specification" still being documented. Unchosen matching is a defining feature of the form, and the manuscript admits it does not yet know whether the site has it. Compounding this: the investigator *designed the gate*. The opacity is researcher-manufactured. The "conservative hard case" framing (line 551) addresses where opacity comes from, not the objection that the field site instantiates the construct by construction. Fix: reframe the site honestly as a *purpose-built analog* of coordinative co-optation — a laboratory instantiation of the three structural properties, valid for construct calibration, with Study 4's naturalistic sites carrying the generalizability burden. That framing is defensible and already half-present in Appendix C; the current framing ("instantiates the form, not a hybrid") overclaims and invites the kill-shot.

### Finding 4: internal inconsistency on where evaluative attribution lives

Line 212 subsumes the candidate fourth operation *within interpreting*: "We integrate this attribution capacity directly within *interpreting*." Line 304 then says RQ4 "explicitly decouples error attribution from *keeping track*," and Appendix B (line 517) tests it as "a candidate fourth dimension rather than a component of longitudinal tracking." So the theory section houses attribution in interpreting while the methods sections test whether it separates from tracking. These are different claims: the falsification design must test attribution against the operation it was folded into. Fix: one consistent statement — attribution is provisionally a facet of *interpreting*; RQ4 and Study 1 test whether it separates from *interpreting* (and, secondarily, from tracking).

### Finding 5: the opening analogy instantiates the rival construct

The pilot/flight-automation analogy (line 15) is a *dyad* — a human supervising a machine, the exact supervisory-control structure the paper spends eight thousand words showing cannot capture algorithmacy. There is no second human on the far side of the autopilot; there is no binding verdict on the pilot. A specialist will wince, because the paper's own Table 1 would classify the pilot analogy under AI literacy or HMC. The Orientation section as a whole is also a second opening — it does the Introduction's job at lower resolution, then the Introduction starts again. Fix: cut the Orientation section or fold its literacy-lineage sentence into the Introduction, and replace the pilot with the analogy the paper already owns: the student and her unseen peer reviewers on either side of the gate (line 29), which is triadic, concrete, and yours.

### Finding 6 (prior-art trap): the novelty claim narrows but survives

I tried to kill the claim "no construct combines an active human counterpart with an opaque, consequential intermediary that evaluates and binds both parties."

- **Nearest rivals the paper already handles well:** Cotter's visibility game, Katsh & Rifkin's fourth party, Curchod et al.'s asymmetric triad, Cameron's labor triangle, Healy & Pekarek. The system-design vs. participant-capacity distinction on ODR is clean and holds.
- **Rival the paper does not address:** the *imagined audience* literature (Litt, 2012, "Knock knock, who's there?"; Litt & Hargittai, 2016; Marwick & boyd's context collapse). That tradition theorizes precisely an individual capacity to encode communication for unseen human others reached through an algorithmically curated channel — which is *specifying intent* by another name, minus bindingness. It will not kill the construct (no binding verdict, no evaluation of both parties), but a communication-adjacent referee will name it within minutes, and its absence from a seven-construct sweep that includes Abidin and Cotter is conspicuous. Cite it, place it in the Table 1 logic (counterpart present, intermediary curates but does not bind), and the flank is covered.
- **The operation-level claim is already conceded:** Manky (2025) is cited as documenting "an explicitly counterpart-directed interpretive sensibility" in the wild (line 174). So counterpart-directed *interpreting* exists in prior empirical work; the novelty is the *bundle* (three operations under three named structural properties) and the *mutual-bindingness triad*. The manuscript mostly says this, but the Introduction's flat "Organization theory has not yet named the sensibility..." reads wider than what survives. Narrow it to the bundle-plus-bindingness claim there.
- **The term itself:** I am not certain "algorithmacy" is unclaimed — cognates of the numeracy/literacy family get coined independently and I cannot verify priority from here. Run a search of the term (and "algoritmacy") before Lima and be ready to cite or distinguish any prior use. I flag this as *uncertain*, not as an error.

### Finding 7: the Simon "satisficing" appropriation

"We formalize algorithmacy to a satisficing threshold in Simon's (1997) behavioral sense" (line 23), echoed at line 253 ("adopting an aspiration level in Simon's sense"). Satisficing describes a *decision-maker's* aspiration level over alternatives; a construct does not satisfice, and using Simon as warrant for "we decline to theorize institutional justice" stretches the concept past what a Carnegie-school reader will grant. The underlying move — bounded scope, stated plainly — is fine and needs no borrowed authority. Say what the construct does and does not cover; drop the satisficing dress.

### Polemic and lead-burying

Genuinely little to flag on polemic — the "Paradox the Construct Raises Rather Than Resolves" section is a model of normative restraint, and Propositions 1–3 convert what could have been moralizing (competency as complicity) into falsifiable mechanism. One borderline line: "Algorithmic coordination regimes operate by systematically inverting these standards" (line 255) — "systematically inverting" implies design intent the evidence does not establish; "withholding" is what Curchod et al. actually document, and the sentence's own enumeration already carries the force. On lead-burying: the Orientation section (Finding 5) is the one true wind-up; elsewhere the paper is claim-first to a fault. The "Dissertation Coherence" section is fine for a PDW but must go before journal submission.

### Citation apparatus (verified against the manuscript's own reference list; external verification flagged where I cannot do it)

- **Riordan (1995)** is cited (line 553) and **absent from the references**. Confirmed by search of the full file.
- **Yurek, Vasey, & Havens (2008)** is cited (line 576) and **absent from the references**. Confirmed.
- Table 1 cites "**Mohlmann, 2021**" (line 68) but the reference is Möhlmann, Zalmanson, Henfridsson, & Gregory (2021) — should be "Möhlmann et al., 2021," with the umlaut. Also, that MISQ paper is about matching-meets-control, not reactivity; folding it into the "Reactivity under Opaque Evaluation" row needs a word of justification or a swap.
- The **authorless JMIR preprint entry** (line 334) has no in-text citation — the Clinical Medicine bullet (line 322) makes its claim uncited. An entry with "authors not retrievable" cannot anchor a boundary claim at a top-tier venue; find a citable source or cut the bullet.
- **Gagrčin, Naab, & Grub (2024)** is listed as *New Media & Society, 28*(1) — NM&S volume 28 corresponds to 2026, not 2024. Likely an online-first/print mismatch. I cannot verify from here; check against the version of record rather than taking my word.

## Part 2 — AI-slop audit (register-aware)

The paper contains none of the crude lexicon (no *delve*, *tapestry*, *testament*, *landscape*-as-filler; no "Crucially/Notably" openers; *foster* appears only inside a Long & Magerko quotation, exempt). The slop here is subtler and structural: uniformity of optimization at the sentence and paragraph level. Counts below are from the full file.

**1. The gerund-subject + "requires/demands" drumbeat.** "Evaluating candidate constructs... requires analyzing" (54); "Isolating this micro-foundational capacity requires evaluating" (50); "Demarcating this theoretical boundary requires satisfying" (172); "Construct clarity... requires establishing... Satisfying these criteria demands" (178); "Developing algorithmacy... requires decoupling" (180); "Establishing construct and discriminant validity requires embedding" (192); "Tracking the developmental trajectory... requires linking" (310). Seven-plus section- and paragraph-openers on one template is a machine tell: same shape, agentless, and it hides the author. Failure: nominalization + identical openers + agent-hiding. Rewrite (line 192, in register): *"I embed algorithmacy in a nomological network of four neighboring constructs and state each relation as a testable prediction."* Same fix pattern for the others: name the agent (I/the paper/the construct), active verb, vary the entry.

**2. Self-graded rigor.** The paper repeatedly awards itself adjectives: "yields a definitive conclusion" (172), "establish rigorous discriminant validity" (122 — Zhou's rigor, but the adjective is the author's grade), "The underlying boundary across this literature is precise" (27), "a formidable rival hypothesis" (142 — this one earns itself; keep it), "To maintain methodological transparency" (293 — narrating the virtue of the disclosure about to be made). House rule: state the limitation or the verdict; never narrate the care. Rewrite (172): *"Against these four conditions, none of the seven frameworks accords the counterpart a constitutive position within an opaque, binding architecture."* The verdict is stronger without "definitive." Rewrite (293): cut the clause; open on "Two operational parameters of the gate remain undocumented: ..."

**3. Zero rhythm variation.** In ~13,000 words of body prose I count **three** genuine short sentences ("This capacity represents a foundational communicative sensibility"; "This distinction establishes an explicit theoretical boundary" — and both are abstract tag-closers, not punches). The corpus exemplars land "It does not." / "The grant has a boundary." after dense builds. This manuscript's sentences run uniformly 25–45 words, subordinate-clause-dense, for eighteen thousand words. Uniform density is the same tell as uniform punchiness, inverted. Fix: at each section's load-bearing verdict, land it short. E.g., after the Spitzberg breakdown paragraph (78): *"...an isolated input to an opaque aggregation that neither party controls. The instrument still reads; the verdict no longer answers to it."* Or simply: *"The counterpart still judges. The judgment no longer lands."*

**4. Em-dashes: 13 in 18,337 words (~0.7 per 1,000).** Not overuse — *underuse*, for this house. The style file sets gloss-welding at roughly 3/1,000, and this is exactly the genre that needs it: the paper introduces *interpreting*, *specifying intent*, *keeping track*, *coordinative co-optation*, *bindingness* and mostly glosses them in separate sentences. The existing dashes are all legitimate (e.g., 236, 238 — appositive glosses; keep). Add, don't cut. Example (line 31): *"We define algorithmacy as three operations — interpreting the intermediary's shifting rule, specifying intent through its constrained channels, and keeping track of its drift — and the sensibility is the capacity to run all three at once."*

**5. Verbatim formula repetition.** "opaque, adaptive" ×11; "evaluates and binds" ×6; "architecture(s)" ×50; "triadic" ×24; "constitutive" ×23; "formaliz-" ×26; "vacancy" ×6. The definitional triple should recur — it is a term of art — but eleven verbatim runs is a drumbeat; after the Scope section, compress to "the three structural properties" or "the triad's properties." "Architecture" is the worst offender: it serves as structure, model, system, design, and framework in different sentences; at 50 occurrences it has stopped meaning anything. Replace at least half with the plain word each instance actually means.

**6. Agent-hiding passives (few, but at load-bearing moments).** Line 160: "the computational system **is reduced to** a passive transmission conduit... the counterpart **is demoted to** an exogenous evaluative score" — this is the synthesis paragraph, the paper's key move, and the agents (the rival frameworks) vanish exactly where naming them is the argument. Rewrite: *"Where a framework admits the counterpart, it reduces the system to a conduit, a delegate, or an obstacle; where it makes the intermediary opaque and binding, it demotes the counterpart to a rating source."* Also line 255 ("contestation channels are structurally foreclosed") and 553 ("Role duality is managed through") — name who forecloses, who manages.

**7. "We" for a solo author.** The house calibration (per Rahman 2021, Cameron 2024, Suddaby 2010, and the AoM guide) is "I" for solo work, marking authorial labor at the analytic moves. The manuscript uses "we" sparsely and never "I." For OS/OT this is a deliberate-register call the author may defend, but the current usage is the worst of both: too sparse to own the moves, plural without a coauthor. Pick "I" and use it at the joints (I evaluate, I define, I predict).

**8. Concrete-anchor drought in the construct section.** Lines 178–282 (~4,500 words, the construct's home) contain almost no case: the student-and-gate vignette (29), the courier's appeal (126), and Sandberg's Volvo optimizers are the paper's only concrete moments, and none appears in the operations subsections. "Specifying intent" cries out for its own two-sentence case — the pilot participant who routed her explanation through an LLM (312) is sitting right there in the methods section and belongs at line 220 as the anchor for dual-audience encoding. Every abstraction touches a case; currently the three operations touch none at their point of definition.

**What I did not flag:** the per-construct inheritance paragraphs (a sustained analytic template — legitimate; just vary the four near-identical openers "Algorithmacy inherits/directly inherits/directly incorporates/inherits two foundational insights"); the "she/her" counterpart convention (deliberate and effective); the definitional triple's existence (only its verbatim frequency); the restrained hedging ("remain open empirical questions that subsequent psychometric testing must resolve" — flat and specific, exactly right).

## Part 3 — Line-level revisions, ranked by impact

1. **Line 291 (bindingness at the site)** — after "while peer evaluators contribute assessments to an opaque algorithm whose ultimate weighting logic remains hidden from them," add the missing mechanism or the honest concession. If true: *"The gate binds the evaluator as well: her review record feeds her own composite score, so a verdict she cannot see prices work she performed on both sides of the exchange."* If not yet true: *"Whether the gate binds the evaluator symmetrically — whether her review record enters her own score — is a design parameter the formal protocol fixes before deployment; without it, bindingness at this site runs one way."*
2. **Line 204 (the derivation)** — replace "derived deductively from the structural constraints of the mediated triad" with the actual derivation: *"Each structural property imposes one informational deficit, and each deficit demands one operation: opacity withholds the rule, so the actor must reconstruct it (interpreting); bindingness turns every message into evidence before a verdict, so the actor must encode for judge and counterpart at once (specifying intent); adaptivity unsettles whatever the actor has learned, so the reconstruction must be maintained (keeping track)."* This also repairs Finding 2 in one stroke.
3. **Line 15 (the analogy)** — cut the pilot. Replace with: *"A student submits a pull request to an automated gate. Her peers' reviews are public; the rule that converts them into a verdict is hidden from everyone, the instructor included. She must coordinate with people she can see through a rule she cannot."* Then the Introduction's fuller version (29) becomes a return, not a repeat.
4. **Line 21 (novelty claim, narrowed)** — replace "Organization theory has not yet named the sensibility an individual must exercise to coordinate with another person reached exclusively through an opaque, binding algorithm" with: *"Fragments of this sensibility surface across adjacent literatures — counterpart-directed inference in Lima's ride-hailing fleets (Manky, 2025), audience-encoding under algorithmic curation (Litt, 2012) — but organization theory has neither named the bundle nor tied it to the structural form that demands it."*
5. **Line 23 (Simon)** — replace "we formalize algorithmacy to a satisficing threshold in Simon's (1997) behavioral sense, defining the individual sensibility required to navigate this triadic structure without making unearned claims about broader institutional justice" with: *"I formalize algorithmacy as a bounded behavioral construct: it defines the sensibility the triad demands of a participant and stops there, leaving institutional justice to the governance literatures equipped to judge it."*
6. **Line 160 (synthesis passives)** — as given in Part 2, item 6.
7. **Line 172 (self-graded verdict)** — as given in Part 2, item 2.
8. **Line 212 / 304 (attribution's home)** — at 304, replace "explicitly decouples error attribution from *keeping track*" with *"tests whether error attribution separates from *interpreting*, the operation it is provisionally folded into (see above), and secondarily from *keeping track*."*
9. **Line 220 (anchor the operation)** — append: *"The pilot run supplied the case: one participant satisfied the gate's parser by routing her rationale through an external language model — machine-legible, peer-illegible — which is precisely the failure mode dual-audience encoding names."*
10. **Line 255** — "operate by systematically inverting these standards" → *"withhold each of these standards in turn: proprietary rules stay unobservable, determinations carry no accountable office, contestation has no channel, and enrollment substitutes for consent."*

## Closing note

The paper's biggest genuine strength is discipline: seven rival constructs each engaged at page-and-item level, each closed with an explicit statement of what algorithmacy inherits from it — a boundary-drawing exercise that most construct papers gesture at and this one actually performs, down to a predicted null correlation and named falsification criteria per interview module. That machinery is real and rare, and it is why the paper deserves major revisions rather than skepticism.

The one thing only the author can supply, which no reviewer can fabricate: the fact of the matter about the gate he built. Does the reviewer's own standing turn on the gate's verdicts? Are peers matched or self-selected? What happens, concretely, when a Trinidad or Hult participant contests an outcome? The construct's fate now hangs on design parameters and field particulars that exist in the author's course infrastructure and pilot records and nowhere else — no literature search reaches them, and the revision cannot be finished until he writes them down.
