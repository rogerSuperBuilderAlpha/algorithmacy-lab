## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor & argument structure

### 1.1 How accurately are the communication-studies constructs characterized?

This is the manuscript's strongest suit, and I want to say so plainly before the criticisms: the Spitzberg (2006), Guzman & Lewis (2020), Hancock et al. (2020), and Sutherland et al. (2020) sections are close, page-cited readings that engage each framework's actual architecture rather than a caricature of it. The Zhou et al. (2025) section reads individual scale items. Almost no organization-theory manuscript does this. That said, a communication-studies specialist winces in four places:

**(a) Spitzberg as "passive conduit" — overdrawn in the prose, correct in the table.** Table 1 gets it right: "Transmission medium; recalibrates parameters of an interpersonal model." But the section opening (line 74) says Spitzberg "imports the classical architecture of unmediated interpersonal interaction directly into digital settings by treating technology as a passive conduit," and later that mediation "recalibrates the speed and bandwidth of message exchange while leaving the fundamental structure of interpersonal competence undisturbed." Spitzberg's model contains a distinct media-factors component — medium characteristics enter as consequential variables conditioning competence, not as a null channel. A Spitzberg-literate referee will read "passive conduit" as evidence you skimmed the model the rest of the section proves you did not skim. The defensible claim — and the one your argument actually needs — is narrower: Spitzberg treats the medium as a *parameter set within a dyadic architecture*, never as an autonomous third party with evaluative authority. Say that; drop "passive conduit" for this framework and reserve it for the transmission-model shorthand in Table 1's vocabulary. (You may keep "conduit" language for the generic CMC transmission tradition; just don't pin it on Spitzberg's model specifically, which is the most media-sensitive artifact that tradition produced.)

**(b) Internal inconsistency on Guzman & Lewis.** The Introduction (line 25) says HMC "position[s] the machine itself as the communicative partner, entirely displacing the second human actor." The HMC section itself (line 84) is more careful: "Guzman and Lewis acknowledge that communicative technologies can blur mediator and communicator functions simultaneously." Both cannot stand. The section's version is the accurate one — Guzman & Lewis explicitly discuss AI in mediator roles, and the field's definition ("at least one of the interaction partners is a machine") does not expel the second human; it just doesn't theorize her. Fix the Introduction's "entirely displacing" to match the section's precision: HMC *does not theorize* the far-side human; it does not *banish* her. A hostile HMC referee (and the HMC journal community is small and will be asked to review) will seize on "entirely displacing" as a strawman and stop reading charitably.

**(c) The Hancock et al. definitional quote needs verification against the version of record.** Line 96 quotes AI-MC as "mediated communication between people in which a computational agent operates on behalf of a communicator by modifying, augmenting, or generating messages to accomplish communication or interpersonal goals" (p. 90). My memory of the published JCMC definition runs "interpersonal communication in which an intelligent agent operates on behalf of a communicator…" — different in two load-bearing words ("interpersonal" vs. "mediated…between people"; "intelligent agent" vs. "computational agent"). I cannot verify from memory and will not assert a correction; per the standing caution, **check the PDF before submission**. The same goes for the embedded quote "the message receiver is assumed to understand and accept that agency" (line 98) — if it is a paraphrase, unquote it. Everything else in the Hancock section (five parameters, receiver vulnerability, disclosure rarity) matches the paper as I know it.

**(d) The Spitzberg inheritance is asserted, not earned — this is the deepest theoretical problem in the paper.** Line 80: "Algorithmacy directly inherits Spitzberg's normative evaluative criteria while rejecting the relational topology his model presupposes." Line 238 then concedes the mechanism: under algorithmic coordination "competence necessarily reverts from a jointly enacted judgment to an individually held capacity." But Spitzberg and Cupach define appropriateness *relationally* — "perceived legitimacy or fit of a message to the context," where the perceiver is the co-interactant whose appraisal has consequence. If the intermediary severs appraisal from consequence (your own Section on Spitzberg, line 78, argues exactly this), then what is the evaluative referent of "appropriateness" inside algorithmacy? Three candidates are live: (i) the counterpart's appraisal, now inconsequential but still real; (ii) the gate's verdict, consequential but not a perceiver; (iii) some hybrid ("legibility to the counterpart conditional on surviving the gate"). The manuscript never chooses. A communication competence specialist will attack here first, because the paper simultaneously claims (α) Spitzberg's criteria are "the most robust theoretical standards available" and (β) the structural conditions those criteria presuppose are absent in your setting. As written, you inherit the *names* of the criteria while the construct that gave them content has been dismantled. The fix is one paragraph: define appropriateness and effectiveness *for the triad* — e.g., effectiveness = the communicative act survives the gate AND the counterpart decodes the intended meaning (your "specifying intent" already implies this dual criterion); appropriateness = fit to the counterpart's expectations *as reconstructed under opacity*, which is precisely what "interpreting" produces. That paragraph turns your biggest vulnerability into a contribution: a triadic re-derivation of the two most canonical criteria in communication competence theory.

**(e) Minor winces.** (i) The abstract calls the seven-construct comparison "a systematic review." Seven hand-selected frameworks compared against a structural question is a *structured comparative evaluation*, not a systematic review in the PRISMA-adjacent sense the phrase now triggers; a methods-literate referee will pounce on the word, and you don't need it — your actual warrant is the three cited systematic reviews (Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum 2025; Gagrčin et al. 2024; Iyamu et al. 2026), which is a stronger move than claiming the label yourself. (ii) The literacy/oracy/numeracy lineage is used well, but "oracy" has a named coiner (Wilkinson, 1965) and citing him costs one line and buys credibility with literacy scholars. (iii) "Reactivity under Opaque Evaluation" sits in a table of "competence frameworks" (abstract's phrasing) though it is a behavioral typology, not a competence model — the abstract's "seven established competence frameworks" overclaims what the table actually holds; "seven candidate constructs" (the Introduction's own phrase) is accurate everywhere it appears.

### 1.2 Polemic and moralizing

Nearly absent, and this restraint is a genuine strength. The paper repeatedly and explicitly decouples capacity from fairness (lines 180, 253, 257–277), refuses to make algorithmacy a virtue, and grounds its one normatively hot passage — "Algorithmic coordination regimes operate by systematically inverting these standards: proprietary decision rules remain unobservable, automated determinations lack administrative accountability, contestation channels are structurally foreclosed, and default enrollment displaces voluntary consent" (line 255) — in Wing et al.'s (2021) enumerated ODR criteria, which is exactly how the harness says polemic should be earned: a vivid line naming mechanisms already established. No rewrite needed. The only borderline case: "quietly absorbed by participants" (line 259) — kept, because the aggregation mechanism it summarizes is specified and falsifiable two sentences later (line 261).

### 1.3 Buried leads and the double opening

The manuscript has **two openings**, and the first one is the weaker. "Orientation and Theoretical Motivation" (lines 13–17) opens on the most nominalized sentence in the paper ("This article formalizes an emergent, widely shared organizational phenomenon within the conceptual apparatus of organization theory: the interpretive sensibility individuals exercise when navigating work mediated by automated systems") and spends a paragraph on the pilot/flight-deck analogy before the Introduction then re-opens, far better, on "Organization theory lacks an account of how individuals coordinate with one another when an automated platform mediates their relationship" (line 21). That Introduction sentence is the best opening line in the manuscript and it is currently buried under 300 words of wind-up. The pilot analogy is also the wrong analogy for this paper's own thesis: a pilot monitoring autopilot is a *dyad* (human–machine, no far-side human counterpart) — it is an HMC scenario, i.e., precisely the configuration the paper spends its second section arguing is *not* the phenomenon. A referee who notices will use your own Table 1 against your opening image. Recommendation: cut the Orientation section entirely, or reduce it to two sentences folded into the Introduction, and if you keep an opening image, use the one you already have and which no rival paper can use — the student's pull request at the gate (line 29), which is triadic, concrete, and yours.

Elsewhere the paragraphs are claim-first to a fault (see Part 2 on uniformity). I found no genuinely buried leads in the construct sections; the setups (e.g., line 54's warm-up before the seven hearings) earn their claims.

### 1.4 The two claims a hostile communication-studies referee attacks first

**Attack 1 — "Your criteria are Spitzberg's, but Spitzberg's criteria cannot survive your setting."** Detailed in 1.1(d). As written it does **not** survive: the inheritance claim and the reversion claim contradict each other until the evaluative referent is specified. One paragraph fixes it, and the fix strengthens the paper.

**Attack 2 — "The triad-with-opacity is not vacant; my literature has lived there for a decade."** This is the prior-art attack, and the communication-studies version of it is sharper than the paper's current defenses anticipate. See 1.5. Verdict: the novelty claim **narrows and survives**, but only if the paper states the narrowed claim itself instead of leaving the referee to extract it.

A third attack worth pre-empting from the org-comm side: **manufactured opacity.** The Hult gate's opacity is designed by the researcher-instructor. The "Why Hult Instantiates Coordinative Co-optation" section (line 291) ably rebuts the *hybrid-form* critique but not the *ecological-validity* critique — that opacity engineered by a course designer who is also the investigator differs in kind from opacity that platforms maintain adversarially against reverse-engineering at commercial stakes. Appendix B's "hard case" framing (line 551) gestures at this but frames it as a test of opacity's *source*, not of its *ecology*. One honest sentence conceding the difference and pointing to Study 4's naturalistic sites (line 633) as the answer would close the hole. (Per the author's standing note: IRB/registration coverage is settled and I do not re-raise it.)

Also flag, small but real: the paper wavers on where evaluative attribution would live if it is *not* a fourth operation. Line 212 subsumes it under *interpreting*; RQ4 (line 302) tests it against *interpreting*; but line 304 says RQ4 "explicitly decouples error attribution from *keeping track*." Pick one null-hypothesis home (interpreting, per lines 212 and 302, and Appendix C's Study 1) and make line 304 agree.

### 1.5 Prior-art trap: falsifying the novelty claim against communication studies

The claim under test (line 27): "no construct combines an active human counterpart with an opaque, consequential intermediary that evaluates and binds both parties." I tried to kill it with the nearest communication-studies rivals:

1. **Folk theories of algorithmic feeds / algorithmic self-presentation — DeVito and colleagues (CHI/CSCW, 2017–2018), Bucher's "algorithmic imaginary" (2017).** This is the nearest rival *not currently cited*, and its absence is the biggest hole in the review. DeVito, Birnholtz, Hancock and colleagues model exactly a person presenting herself *to human audiences through an opaque, adaptive algorithmic curator* — a triad with opacity, adaptivity, and folk-theoretic "interpreting." The paper's existing rebuttal machinery (the three dimensions deployed against Cotter at line 166) does apply — the audience is an aggregate, the work is not jointly executed, and the algorithm binds the presenter alone, not both parties — but the machinery must be aimed at this literature explicitly, because a JoCMC/CHI-adjacent referee will name DeVito or Bucher in the first paragraph of their report. **Verdict: novelty survives on "mutually binding" and "interdependent joint work," not on "triad + opacity" per se. The paper should state its narrowed claim in exactly those terms.**
2. **Cotter (2019), visibility labor.** Already engaged, and the three-dimension rebuttal (line 166) is sound. Survives.
3. **Katsh & Rifkin's "fourth party" (2001).** Already engaged twice (lines 27, 168) with the correct move: same structure, system-design perspective, no participant-capacity model. Survives.
4. **Algorithm awareness/skills scales (Gran, Booth & Bucher 2021; Dogruel et al.; Zarouali et al.; Klawitter & Hargittai 2018 on Etsy sellers' algorithmic skills).** Mostly covered by proxy through the Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum and Gagrčin et al. reviews. Klawitter & Hargittai is the one worth a look — sellers reaching *buyers* through Etsy's opaque search ranking is close to the triad — but the ranking binds the seller, not the buyer, so the rebuttal holds. Survives.
5. **"Algocracy" (Aneesh 2006/2009; Danaher 2016) — a naming collision, not a construct collision.** "Algorithmacy" will be misread and mis-typed as "algocracy," the established STS/governance term for rule-by-algorithm. One sentence at the term's christening — algocracy names the regime, algorithmacy names the individual capacity exercised inside it — costs nothing and inoculates against a whole class of confused reviews. As it stands the collision is unaddressed.

Net: the vacancy claim is defensible but the paper defends a broad version ("no construct combines counterpart + opaque intermediary") while its true, defensible version is narrower ("no construct models individual capacity where the intermediary evaluates and binds *both* parties engaged in *interdependent* work"). Line 172's four analytical conditions already encode the narrow version — the Introduction and abstract should claim only that.

## Part 2 — The AI-slop audit (register-aware)

Constraint restated before writing: the job is to make this paper better at being a Suddaby-genre construct paper in the author's house style — not to convert it to generic prose, and any proposed fix more nominalized than the original is the disease. Also noted: this manuscript's failure mode is the *opposite* of the classic tells — no "delve," no "tapestry," no "crucially," no landing-line epigrams, almost no filler transitions. Its slop is **uniformity of density**: one sentence machine run for 12,771 words.

### 2.1 The measured profile (main text, 12,771 words)

- **Em-dashes: 10** (0.78/1k words). **Not overused — underused.** The house floor for gloss-welding is ~3/1k; the venue exemplar corpus runs 2.93/1k. The existing uses are good (line 162's "communicative literacy—operationalized as…—represents" is a proper gloss-weld; line 238's "capacity—reflecting the interpretive conception of work described by Sandberg (2000)—with" is a legitimate appositive). The paper defines a dozen terms ("opacity means… Adaptivity means… Intermediary designates…", line 33) in separate flat sentences where the em-dash appositive would weld gloss to term and hand the next sentence a subject. No flags for excess; the recommendation runs the other way.
- **"Rather than": 77 occurrences** in 18,337 words — the antithesis machine in this manuscript's register. The house style retired the antithesis quota *for contrasts a reader would actually reach for*; perhaps a third of these qualify (e.g., "derives… deductively… rather than aggregated inductively," line 204 — real contrast, keep). The rest are reflexive ("an empirical capacity to be measured rather than a normative virtue to be maximized," line 277, restates line 259's identical contrast; "historical grounding rather than operational architecture," line 214; "a strategic research site… rather than…" pattern across Appendix B). When the same X-rather-than-Y contrast recurs across sections — capacity-not-fairness appears at lines 180, 253, 257, 259, 277 — that is the verbatim-repetition slop: keep the strongest instance (line 257–259, where it does propositional work) and let the others assert the positive claim alone.
- **Gerund-subject openers: ~51** ("Evaluating candidate constructs… requires," "Developing algorithmacy… requires decoupling," "Establishing construct and discriminant validity requires embedding," "Demarcating this theoretical boundary requires satisfying," "Formalizing algorithmacy reorients," "Specifying these institutional mechanics ensures," "Satisfying these criteria demands a systematic specification…"). This is the uniformity tell. One or two per section is a legitimate genre move; fifty-one is a drumbeat, and because the template puts an abstract process in the subject slot, it systematically evicts the named agents the house style demands. The cure is not zero — it is variance: let a scholar, the construct, or "I" hold the subject slot in half of them.
- **Short sentences: effectively zero.** I searched the main text for declaratives under eight words; every hit was a citation fragment. The corpus rhythm — dense build punctured by "It does not." — never occurs once in 12,771 words. Mean sentence length is high *and unvaried*. This, combined with the gerund template, is why careful readers will report the paper as "exhausting" without being able to name a single bad sentence.
- **Rigor-performing adverbs:** "systematic/systematically" ×20, "precise(ly)" ×10, "directly" ×36, "structural(ly)" ×97, "explicitly" ×7. "Structural" is the paper's load-bearing term and most uses are earned; but as an *intensifier* ("structurally barred," "structurally presumed," "structurally foreclosed," "structurally concealed") it performs rigor the sentence already has. Cut the intensifier uses, keep the analytic ones.
- **First person: zero authorial "I."** All seven "I" hits are quoted Zhou et al. scale items. The paper is solo-authored and uses "we" (20 hits). House style rule 1 is explicit: solo work uses "I" for authorial labor (Rahman 2021, Cameron 2024, Suddaby 2010 all do), coauthored work uses "we." Convert the labor verbs — "I formalize," "I evaluate each framework," "I adopt Long and Magerko's literacy genealogy" — and leave claims impersonal. (If the author's committee mandates "we" for dissertation papers, that convention wins; flag it consciously rather than by default.)
- **Concrete anchors: unevenly distributed.** The seven-construct hearings are well-anchored — the courier compiling audio recordings and timestamped logs (line 126), participant P39 migrating clients off-platform (line 152), P10's rapport-building (line 150), Lima drivers reading metrics for bodily safety (line 174), the pull-request-at-the-gate vignette (line 29). But the 100-line construct core (lines 176–282: scope conditions, semantic relations, tripartite model, categorical delineation, implications, propositions) contains **not one concrete instance**. The three constitutive operations — the heart of the paper — are defined (lines 206–208) without a single worked example of a person interpreting, specifying, or tracking. The manuscript already owns the perfect anchors (the pilot participant who routed her explanation through an external LLM, line 312; a code comment written for both the test suite and an unseen peer, line 220 gestures at it abstractly). Thread one running example — one student, one pull request, one gate verdict — through the three operation definitions and the recursive-cycle paragraph. This is the single highest-yield prose fix in the paper.

### 2.2 Specific flags with rewrites

**Flag 1 — nominalized double opening (line 15).**
> "This article formalizes an emergent, widely shared organizational phenomenon within the conceptual apparatus of organization theory: the interpretive sensibility individuals exercise when navigating work mediated by automated systems."

Failure: abstract-noun stack ("an emergent, widely shared organizational phenomenon within the conceptual apparatus") delaying the referent; duplicates the Introduction's superior opening.
Rewrite (if the section survives at all): "Individuals now coordinate everyday work through automated systems they cannot inspect. This article gives that interpretive sensibility a name and a place in organization theory." — Better: cut the section and let line 21 open the paper.

**Flag 2 — agentless-passive pair (line 160).**
> "Where a human counterpart is present, the computational system is reduced to a passive transmission conduit… Conversely, where the algorithmic intermediary is modeled as opaque and binding, the counterpart is demoted to an exogenous evaluative score…"

Failure: "is reduced," "is demoted" — the reducing and demoting agents are the frameworks under review, and hiding them blunts the paper's own indictment.
Rewrite: "Where a framework admits a human counterpart, it reduces the computational system to a passive conduit, a partisan delegate, or an obstacle to circumvent. Where a framework models the intermediary as opaque and binding, it demotes the counterpart to an exogenous rating source."

**Flag 3 — the gerund-requires template at its worst (line 178).**
> "Satisfying these criteria demands a systematic specification of algorithmacy's boundary conditions, dimensional structure, ontological status, and analytical utility as an individual-level micro-foundation for organizational coordination."

Failure: nominalization stack ("a systematic specification of… boundary conditions, dimensional structure, ontological status, and analytical utility") performing the work it announces; the sentence adds nothing to the Suddaby sentence before it.
Rewrite: "The next four subsections do that work: they fix the construct's scope conditions, its three operations, its ontological category, and its relations to neighboring measures." (First-person itinerary, one only — the house style permits exactly one compact roadmap, and this is where it belongs.)

**Flag 4 — self-narrated rigor (line 293).**
> "To maintain methodological transparency, two specific operational parameters of the gate mechanism warrant explicit identification as ongoing design specifications in the empirical protocol."

Failure: the sentence narrates the virtue of stating something instead of stating it (house style rule 4: zero exemplar instances of a paper commenting on its own care).

**Flag 5 — the repeated formula phrase.** "Opaque, adaptive [and mutually binding] intermediary that evaluates and binds both parties" and close variants appear 8+ times ("binds both" family), with "opaque" ×50. The construct's definition should recur once, verbatim, at definition (line 31) — after that, the house style's thread-renaming mechanism applies: *the triad → the gate → the arrangement → it*. The Rahman and Sutherland sections already do this well; the construct core and Conclusion do not (line 328 re-runs the full formula twice in two sentences).
Rewrite of line 328's opener: "Algorithmacy isolates the capacity an individual needs inside the triad: to interpret it, to speak through it, and to track it as it moves." (Then the aggregation sentence, without re-listing the three properties a fourth time.)

**Flag 6 — passive hiding the author (Appendix B, line 553).**
> "Role duality is managed through deliberate structural safeguards rather than methodological disavowal (Riordan, 1995; Anteby, 2013)."

Failure: agentless passive on the sentence where the agent *is the point* — insider action research turns on who manages the duality.
Rewrite: "I manage the role duality through structural safeguards rather than disavowal (Anteby, 2013): my instructional role stops at macro-level course design, and the gate votes without me." (Also: Riordan 1995 has no reference entry — see 2.3.)

**Flag 7 — anchor-free operation definitions (lines 206–208).** Not a quote-level failure but the section's governing one: three abstract definitions with no case. Rewrite pattern (for *specifying intent*): "**Specifying intent:** structuring a communicative act so its meaning survives the intermediary's narrow channels — a pull-request description written so the test suite parses it *and* the unseen peer reviewer understands why the change matters." One clause per operation buys the whole tripartite model its first concrete ground.

The four-condition parallel structure at line 172 and the fivefold typology summaries are analytic parallelism carrying distinct content — house-style-legitimate. The dense comparative table prose in Appendix A is genre-appropriate table apparatus. And the paper's liveliest passages — the courier's audio recordings, P39's exit, the Lima drivers — are exactly what more of the paper should sound like; nothing there is flagged.
