## Four facts the author confirmed, load-bearing — state as fact, do not hedge or re-derive

1. **The gate binds the peer reviewer too.** Her review accuracy/quality feeds her own gate score —
   not just the submitter's progression. Mutual bindingness, the construct's whole differentiator
   against all seven rival constructs in Table 1, is real at the flagship site. It was simply never
   stated. This resolves org theory's Finding 1, hostile's Finding 2, and methodology's site-fit
   attack in one stroke — the fix is disclosure, not redefinition.
2. **Reviewers are algorithmically assigned**, not self-selected. The "matches unchosen parties"
   scope condition for coordinative co-optation (Stark & Vanden Broeck, 2024) is satisfied. This
   resolves methodology's Finding 4 and hostile's Finding 4 (the "is documenting" hedge in "Why Hult
   Instantiates" can become a flat statement).
3. **The evaluation rule itself shifts** (weights/thresholds get retuned), not merely the codebase
   substrate. Adaptivity and *keeping track* are honestly satisfied. This resolves hostile's Finding
   5 (the site is not structurally rigged toward the disconfirming pattern for *keeping track* —
   merge-driven codebase churn under a fixed rule was the worry; the rule itself moving is a
   different, stronger, and true claim).
4. **Pilot count is two harness transcripts** — one read informally for instrument function (the
   participant who routed her explanation through an external LLM rather than addressing the peer),
   the second not yet read at all. This is already stated correctly in the current file's
   "Instrument Piloting" section (confirmed by direct grep) — the problem flagged by hostile
   (Finding 1) and methodology (§1.6) is that (a) elsewhere in the file this same incident gets
   narrated as *confirming* dual-audience encoding when it is closer to the construct's own stated
   *disconfirming* pattern for that operation, and (b) "Instruments and Status" in the main text
   doesn't surface the plain numbers at all, leaving them buried in Appendix B.

**The throughline: the paper was underclaiming its own site, not overclaiming it.** The rewrite's
job on the empirical-strategy front is mostly disclosure of real, already-true mechanisms — not
inventing new hedges, and not softening claims that are actually already earned.

## Cross-cutting findings, ranked by convergence (independently reached by 2+ reviewers)

### 1. Mutual bindingness asserted, not demonstrated (org theory F1, methodology Attack 2, hostile F2)
**Now resolved by confirmed fact #1.** State the reviewer-side binding mechanism explicitly wherever
the definition or Table 1's differentiator is invoked — org theory's suggested location (empirical
strategy section) and phrasing are a good starting point; state it, don't gesture at it.

### 2. The pilot narrative is internally contradictory (hostile F1, methodology §1.6, non-specialist Part 1 item 18)
The current text describes the LLM-routing incident as both "entirely bypassing" the peer counterpart
and "satisfying... peer comprehension" in adjacent sentences. Table B1's own disconfirming criterion
for *specifying intent* is close to what actually happened. Fix: narrate it honestly as a rival
outcome the protocol now has to distinguish, not as confirming evidence — this is a *stronger*
methods story, not a weaker one, and three reviewers (including the non-specialist, who called it
"the paper's most underused asset") independently want this incident surfaced more, not hidden more.
State the exact N ("one pilot administration," "the second transcript unread") — never "pre-qualitative
runs" or "diagnostic runs" as a vagueness euphemism.

### 3. The advertised "deductive derivation" isn't shown on the page (org theory F2, hostile F6)
Three structural properties (opacity, adaptivity, bindingness) are claimed to generate three
operations, but the mapping is two-thirds complete at best — bindingness generates no operation,
and *specifying intent* is actually derived from an unnamed fourth property (channel constraint).
Fix: either name channel constraint as a fourth structural property and show the full mapping, or
derive *specifying intent* from bindingness directly (every message becomes evidence before a
verdict, so it must be encoded for judge and counterpart at once — this reading is available and
both org theory and hostile independently sketch it). Do the derivation in one paragraph; stop
claiming "deductive" until it's shown, not just asserted.

### 4. Evaluative attribution's parent operation wavers (org theory F4, comm studies 1.4, methodology 1.7, hostile F6)
Four independent reviewers found the same inconsistency: one passage subsumes the candidate fourth
operation ("attribution") within *interpreting*; another frames RQ4 as testing it against *keeping
track*. Pick **interpreting** as the stated null-hypothesis home (this is where the majority of
passages already point) and make every other reference agree, including Appendix B and Appendix C's
Study 1 description.

### 5. Prose uniformity, independently measured by three reviewers with consistent numbers
- Em-dashes: 0.7–0.8 per 1,000 words across the whole file, against this project's own house-style
  target of ~3/1,000 for gloss-welding. **This is underuse, not overuse** — do not apply a generic
  anti-AI em-dash ban in the rewrite; add gloss-welding appositives at first use of coined terms
  (a dozen-plus terms currently get flat, separate-sentence definitions where a welded gloss would
  both save words and hand the next sentence a subject).
- "Structural"/"structurally": 82–97 occurrences. "Architecture": up to 50. Cut roughly half of each
  as pure intensifier use; keep the analytic uses.
- Zero authorial "I" in a solo-authored paper (all "we"); house style calls for "I" marking authorial
  labor at the analytic moves in solo work. Convert methods-section passives specifically — that's
  where hiding the agent is most self-undermining for an insider-action-research paper whose whole
  methodological claim is that the researcher's position is an asset.
- Gerund-subject + "requires/demands" openers: ~50+ instances, a genuine uniformity tell distinct
  from the classic slop lexicon (this manuscript has almost none of the classic tells — no "delve,"
  "tapestry," "crucially"). Vary roughly half to named-agent or first-person subjects.
- Zero genuinely short declarative sentences in ~13,000 words of body prose. Add punctures at the
  three or four highest-leverage verdict moments (each reviewer supplies specific candidate
  locations and exact short sentences — see their Part 3 sections).
- The 100-line construct core (scope conditions through propositions) has no concrete anchor
  anywhere in it, while the seven-construct hearings are well-anchored. Thread the pilot's LLM-routing
  incident and/or the classroom pull-request vignette through the three operations' own definitions,
  not just through the literature review.

### 6. Real prior-art gap: algorithmic folk-theories / imagined-audience literature (comm studies 1.5, org theory F6)
DeVito and colleagues' work on folk theories of algorithmic feeds, Bucher's (2017) "algorithmic
imaginary," and Litt's (2012) "imagined audience" concept are the nearest uncited rivals — people
managing self-presentation to human audiences through an opaque, adaptive, curating algorithm. Two
independent reviewers expect a comm-studies-literate discussant to name this within minutes. The
novelty claim **survives, narrowed**: none of that literature requires *mutual* bindingness or
*interdependent joint work* — the algorithm curates/ranks but doesn't bind the audience, and no work
is jointly executed. State the narrowed claim explicitly (the four analytical conditions already
in the paper encode it; the abstract and Introduction currently claim the broader, more attackable
version). See Phase 2 research for citation verification before these go in.

### 7. Naming collision: "algorithmacy" vs. "algocracy" (comm studies 1.5 item 5)
Unaddressed in the current draft. One disambiguating sentence at the term's first use: algocracy
(Aneesh) names the governance regime; algorithmacy names the individual capacity exercised inside
it. Verify the Aneesh citation before adopting (Phase 2).

## What is genuinely strong — do not "fix" these in the rewrite

All six reviewers, independently, praised the seven-construct hearing sections as the paper's real
achievement: item-level engagement with Zhou et al.'s scale (Items 6, 8, 11), page-cited close
readings of Rahman and Sutherland, and — rare in this genre — an explicit statement of what
algorithmacy inherits from each rejected framework rather than just why it's rejected. Preserve this
architecture and this level of engagement; the rewrite's job is disclosure, derivation, and prose
variance, not re-arguing settled, well-executed comparative work. Also preserve: the honest
power-limitation admission already in Appendix B (methodology 1.1), the pre-specified disconfirming
case for the tripartite model (a participant who succeeds through pure technical mastery alone;
methodology 1.1), and the restrained, non-moralizing treatment of the algorithmacy/fairness paradox
(comm studies 1.2, org theory's polemic-check finding almost empty).
