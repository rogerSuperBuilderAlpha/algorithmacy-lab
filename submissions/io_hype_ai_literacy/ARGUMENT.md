# Argument: AI literacy as a hyped remedy

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. Angle sharpened against the OT paper (see FLAGS 2).]

**P1.** "AI literacy" is promoted by vendors, universities, policy bodies and training providers as the remedy for the risks of AI at work. It uses literacy as a metaphor and repeats the print-literacy campaigns' promise that individual competence brings security.
- *Sources:* `berthon_ethics_course/literature/cards/kamperman2026ai.md` (pp. 35–36); `berthon_ethics_course/literature/cards/graff1991literacy.md` (pp. 2, 321).

**P2.** Many of the problems the remedy is sold for are triadic. A system evaluates two people and binds both, and the operator's rule is opaque and changes. A competence defined as knowledge of how AI works (the literacy frameworks) does not reach that relation.
- *Sources:* `lima_pdw/literature/cards/long2020.md`; `dxd_foundation/literature/library/oeldorfhirsch2023what.md` (p. 693: literacy "should not be solely the responsibility of the social media user"); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/starkvandenbroeck2024.md` (p. 14).

**C.** The hype around AI literacy is organizationally useful because it places the deficit in the individual and leaves the arbiter unexamined. Hype studies should treat the AI-literacy remedy market as an object: who produces the remedy, how it is certified and mandated, and what it moves out of view.
