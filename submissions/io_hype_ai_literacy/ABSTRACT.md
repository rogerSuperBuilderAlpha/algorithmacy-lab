**Title:** Selling Literacy for a Triadic Problem: AI Literacy as an Organized Remedy

## Abstract
Hype attaches to remedies as well as to technologies. Alongside the promises made for generative AI, a second set of promises has grown up around "AI literacy": the claim that people who understand how AI works will be safe, employable and in control. Universities, vendors, consultancies, certification bodies and regulators now produce and mandate AI-literacy programmes. This paper treats that remedy as an object of hype studies. Historians of literacy have shown that print-literacy campaigns promised individual advancement that the social order did not deliver. Recent critics argue that AI-literacy initiatives use literacy uncritically as a metaphor and repeat that promise. We add an organizational argument about the problems the remedy is sold for. Many of them are triadic. A booking platform ranks a hotel on its guests' reviews, a marketplace scores a seller on buyers' ratings, and an AI tool can draft a manager's appraisal of an employee. In each case a system evaluates two people and binds both to its verdict. Knowing how the model works does not tell either party whose input moved the verdict, how a message will be scored, or when the rule changed. A reading remedy for a triadic problem is attractive to its sponsors because it places the deficit in the individual and leaves the arbiter unexamined. We propose a research agenda on the AI-literacy remedy market with four questions. Who produces and certifies the remedy? How do mandates turn it into a compliance obligation? What expectations does it carry? What does it move out of view? The questions cover the producers of the remedy, the organizations that adopt and mandate it, and the actors who contest it.

**Keywords:** hype; AI literacy; literacy myth; algorithmic management; expectations
