## Aim (compressed)
- **The core idea.** Phygital marketing should be human-first, and "human" covers the whole stakeholder network: consumers, workers, supply partners and communities. The call contrasts phygital with omnichannel, which only manages connectivity across touchpoints. It anchors the field in Batat's Phygital Research Paradigm (2024b) and in Addis et al. (2022).
- **Three concerns:**
  - ecosystem innovation set against stakeholder well-being: cognitive overload, system complexity and digital burnout;
  - accessibility, equity and ethics: surveillance, algorithmic bias and tokenistic accessibility;
  - methods, including phyginography, affective computing, biometric tracking and hybrid designs.

## Topics listed (abridged)
- **Well-being and fatigue:**
  - aligning commercial performance with consumer and employee well-being;
  - preventing technological fatigue, cognitive strain and disengagement.
- **Inclusion and ethics:**
  - accessibility, equity and inclusion through phygital enablers;
  - auditing "performative inclusivity" and ethics-washing.
- **Data, regulation and adoption:**
  - data governance, biometric ethics, surveillance and privacy;
  - regulation and the responsible deployment of AI, IoT and spatial computing;
  - cultural, demographic and generational variation in adoption and resistance.
- **Agents and authenticity:**
  - virtual agents, digital concierges, smart interfaces and AR/VR in stakeholder relationships;
  - authentic connection, brand trust and emotional resonance alongside automation.
- **Methods, impact and governance:**
  - empirical methods such as phyginography;
  - societal, environmental and well-being impacts;
  - integrating policy, ethics and practice.
