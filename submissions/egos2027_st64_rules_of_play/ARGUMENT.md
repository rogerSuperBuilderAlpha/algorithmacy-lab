# Argument: Who sets the rules of play, and what keeps people connected through the machine

[DRAFTED by Claude, 26 Oct 2026. For the author's ruling.]

**P1.** When an opaque algorithm mediates work between two people, it sets rules that neither of them wrote, and it changes them without notice. Its verdicts bind both people.
- *Sources:* `lima_pdw/literature/cards/starkvandenbroeck2024.md` (p. 14); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/kellogg2020.md` (p. 387: there is often "no procedure ... to contest"); `lima_pdw/literature/cards/rahman2021.md`.

**P2.** Human connection at work depends on each person being able to tell what the other meant and did. Through such an algorithm, every message to the other person is also a message to the evaluator, and every result mixes the other person's judgement with the rule's.
- *Sources:* `lima_pdw/literature/cards/bucherschouwaldkirch2021.md` (anticipatory compliance; chat read as monitored); `dxd_foundation/literature/library/jhaver2018algorithmic.md` (hosts' "double negotiation" with guests and the algorithm).

**C.** Connection survives algorithmic mediation through two practices that let people play by rules they did not set without losing each other. The first is specifying intent so the other person can read it through the system. The second is tracking the rules as they change. People lose connection in two ways. One is pacifying the algorithm and abandoning the counterpart. The other is treating the system as a neutral channel and missing the rule that is judging them both.
