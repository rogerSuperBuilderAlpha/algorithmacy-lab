### **Section A: Questions of Clarity (Beginner & Introductory Level)**

#### **Q1 (Beginner - Clarity):** *"In simple terms, what is 'algorithmacy', and how is it different from basic digital literacy or coding skills?"*
* **Preformed Response:**
  > "Digital literacy usually means knowing *how to use* a software tool—clicking buttons or writing code. **Algorithmacy** is the higher-level cognitive capacity to comprehend, evaluate, and negotiate the *hidden intentions and structural rules* of an algorithmic system.
  > 
  > Think of it this way: Literacy isn't just knowing how to spell words; it's understanding the author's argument and intent. In an algorithmic platform—like a rideshare app or a medical diagnostic tool—the user is not just interacting with a screen. They are coordinating with another human through an algorithm that acts as a 'discourse deputy' for the designer. Algorithmacy is recognizing that this interaction is an irreducible **three-way relationship** (User \\(\leftrightarrow\\) Platform \\(\leftrightarrow\\) Designer) rather than a neutral two-way tool."

#### **Q2 (Beginner - Clarity):** *"Why can't we just break down a 3-way interaction into three separate 2-way pairs? What do we actually lose?"*
* **Preformed Response:**
  > "What we lose is **synergy**—information or causal power that exists *only* when all three elements act together. 
  > 
  > Consider a 2-input XOR logic gate. If you look at Input A alone, it gives you 0 bits of information about the Output. If you look at Input B alone, it gives you 0 bits. If you measure the correlation between A and B, it is zero. But if you look at A and B *together*, they determine the Output completely. If you force an XOR gate into pairwise channels, the system's predictive power collapses to pure noise. That complete loss of joint relationship is what we call **Causal Collapse**."
