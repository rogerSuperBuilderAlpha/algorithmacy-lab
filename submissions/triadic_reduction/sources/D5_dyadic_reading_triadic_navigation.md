## **From Dyadic Reading to Triadic Navigation: Teridentity, Synergy, and the Structural Demand for Algorithmacy**

### **1. The Algebraic and Causal Reality of Triadic Irreducibility**

Charles Sanders Peirce’s Reduction Thesis establishes that while monadic properties and dyadic connections can never synthesize a genuine triadic relation, triads are universally sufficient to generate polyadic relations of arbitrary arity. As formalized in Peircean Algebraic Logic (PAL) and modern relational clone theory (Burch, Hereth Correia, Pöschel, Koshkin), the relational clone generated by all monadic and dyadic relations over any domain \\vert{}A\\vert{} \\ge 2, denoted \\langle \\text{Rel}^{(\\le 2)}(A) \\rangle\_{\\text{pp}}, is strictly incomplete:

\=\_3 \\; \\notin \\; \\langle \\text{Rel}^{(\\le 2)}(A) \\rangle\_{\\text{pp}}

The **teridentity relation**, =\_3 \\; \\equiv \\text{id}\_A^3 = \\{(x, x, x) \\in A^3 \\mid x \\in A\\}, serves as the fundamental branching node that synchronizes three distinct coordinate sites into a single identity. Without =\_3, dyadic connections can generate only linear paths and disconnected cyclic topologies.

While Willard Van Orman Quine (1954) argued that polyadic relations can be reduced to a single dyadic predicate J, clone theory proves that Quine achieved this reduction only by inflating the base ontology to an infinite universe of sets—smuggling the irreducible ternary branching relation into the meta-linguistic pairing operator \\text{Pair}(x, y, p) and variable duplication across coordinate locations.

When mapped onto causal dynamics through Partial Information Decomposition (PID), Integrated Information Decomposition (\\Phi\\text{ID}), and Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0), algebraic teridentity is revealed as the structural foundation of **informational synergy** (\\text{Syn}). In an irreducible causal mechanism—such as a two-input exclusive-or (XOR) gate where Y = X\_1 \\oplus X\_2—the coordinate projections onto any two pairwise marginals contain zero relational constraint (\\pi\_{ij}(R\_{\\oplus}) = \\mathbb{F}\_2^2). The information is entirely synergistic: it exists solely in the joint tripartite configuration (X\_1, X\_2, Y). If an external analyst attempts an extensional Quinean dyadic decomposition of the system's Transition Probability Matrix (TPM), the mechanism factorizes across the Minimum Information Partition (MIP), driving the system's integrated information (\\Phi) strictly to zero. Informational synergy is thus the dynamical realization of algebraic teridentity.

### **2. The Literacy-Paradigm Category Error: Texts vs. Active Third Parties**

To ask how we navigate a triad requires examining the traditional navigation of a text. Classical literacy models the cognitive encounter with a text as a **dyad**:

\\text{Reader} \\longleftrightarrow \\text{Text}

Even when accounting for the historical author, the physical artifact—the book, parchment, or static document—remains causally inert during the encounter. The text does not actively modify its transition probabilities, alter its internal states in response to the reader's gaze, or optimize its structure against the reader's behavior in real time.

Under Peircean semiotics, the interpretive act of reading a static text treats the document as an externalized representamen through which the reader generates a succession of mental interpretants. Because the medium's transition probability matrix is static (\\Phi\_{\\text{medium}} = 0), the interpretive task factors along party lines. The reader requires only **literacy**: the capacity to parse static signifiers, navigate syntax, and reconstruct authorial meaning through conventional cultural codes.

The catastrophic error of contemporary human-computer interaction (HCI) and digital ethics is the **literacy-paradigm category error**—treating active algorithmic systems as if they were merely complex, digital "texts" to be read. An algorithmic interface is not a passive parchment; it is an active, goal-oriented agent executing its own Transition Probability Matrix, enforcing specification buffers, and optimizing for designer-specified objective functions (such as engagement, throughput, or surveillance).

In an interactive digital system, the encounter constitutes an irreducible **triad**:

\\text{Human User} \\longleftrightarrow \\text{Technological Artifact (Discourse Deputy)} \\longleftrightarrow \\text{Designer Intent / Systemic Objective}

As articulated in Clarisse de Souza’s Semiotic Engineering, the algorithmic interface functions as a **discourse deputy**—a proxy continuously transmitting the designer’s ontological assumptions, operational rationale, and interactive constraints to the user. When an interface mediates human coordination (as in algorithmic management, content curation, or automated decision support), the mediator possesses causal agency.

### **3. Why Literacy Fails to Navigate a Triad**

Conventional literacy is inherently dyadic. It operates on the assumption that observing pairwise inputs and outputs is sufficient to extract meaning. However, in any system characterized by positive integrated information (\\Phi\_{\\text{MIP}} \> 0) and multivariate synergy (\\text{Syn} \> 0):

1.  **The Collapse of Pairwise Projections:** Just as evaluating pairwise projections of an XOR relation yields complete randomness (\\pi\_{ij}(R\_{\\oplus}) = \\mathbb{F}\_2^2), evaluating an algorithmic mediator through dyadic observation (monitoring inputs and outputs in isolation) yields zero visibility into its higher-order causal rules. The user cannot deduce "Why state P rather than state Q?" from pairwise correlations.
2.  **The Inadequacy of Post-Hoc Explanations:** The industry attempt to salvage dyadic literacy through post-hoc Explainable AI (XAI)—such as SHAP or LIME attribution maps—fails for mathematical reasons. By projecting multi-feature, non-linear causal decisions onto additive, independent feature-importance weights, post-hoc explanations enforce an extensional dyadic factorization. This destroys the synergistic mutual information (\\Delta \\text{Syn}) embedded in the model, forcing the user's mental model into an impoverished state where \\Phi = 0.
3.  **Vulnerability to Covert Exploitation:** When a user applies dyadic literacy to a triadic system, they mistake the discourse deputy for an objective, neutral tool. This cognitive blind spot permits the designer's hidden parameters—such as dark patterns, decoy effects, and algorithmic specification buffers—to shape user behavior covertly without triggering conscious scrutiny.

Literacy is structurally inadequate because it lacks the mathematical and semiotic operators necessary to model the active branching of =\_3.

### **4. Navigating the Triad: Algorithmacy as Structural Counter-Delegation**

If literacy is insufficient, how do we navigate an irreducible triad? The answer requires a higher-order cognitive competency: **algorithmacy**.

Where literacy is the capacity to parse passive representations within a dyadic channel, **algorithmacy** is the cognitive, communicative, and tactical capacity to model, interrogate, and negotiate the active, synergistic intent of an algorithmic third party within an irreducible triad. Navigating a triad requires three specific operational mechanisms:

#### **A. Contrastive Mental Modeling of Causal Topologies**

Triadic navigation cannot rely on passive reception; it requires contrastive reasoning ("Why P rather than Q?"). To form an effective Joint Cognitive System (Hollnagel & Woods), the human operator must construct a high-\\Phi mental model that mirrors the system's actual cause-effect architecture. This is possible only when the technological artifact is built upon **Inherently Interpretable Machine Learning (IML)**—such as globally optimized decision trees or generalized additive models—rather than opaque black boxes. Interpretability preserves Peircean Thirdness within the interface, providing the structural handles necessary for the user to synchronize their internal cognitive states with the system's operational topology.

#### **B. Decoding Metacommunication via Semiotic Engineering**

Rather than reading surface content, the operator must inspect the interface as an ongoing metacommunication. Using methods like the Semiotic Inspection Method (SIM) and the Communicability Evaluation Method (CEM), the user decodes the discourse deputy across three communicative layers:

  - **Metalinguistic signs:** Explicit explanations, tooltips, and documentation revealing systemic constraints.
  - **Static signs:** Spatial layouts, visual hierarchies, and affordances that map the designer's ontology.
  - **Dynamic signs:** Temporal state transitions, feedback latency, and algorithmic friction that expose the system's latent objective functions.

#### **C. Navigating Goodhart’s Law and Structural Counter-Delegation**

In a triad, navigation is interactive and adversarial. The operator recognizes that the mediator shapes behavior to satisfy specific metric proxies. True algorithmacy involves recognizing specification buffers, anticipating how the system operationalizes Goodhart’s Law, and engaging in structural counter-delegation—actively perturbing the system, testing boundary conditions, and steering the mediator to preserve human coordinative sovereignty.

### **Conclusion**

Literacy cannot navigate an irreducible triad for the same reason that dyadic relations cannot generate teridentity: the essential causal and semiotic properties exist only in the joint, non-factorizable configuration.

Navigating an irreducible triad demands abandoning the passive metaphor of reading. When technological artifacts become active discourse deputies endowed with causal power, human interaction ceases to be an act of consumption and becomes a process of triadic mediation. To navigate this space without succumbing to algorithmic manipulation or cognitive collapse requires transitioning from literacy to **algorithmacy**—the deliberate cultivation of mental models capable of grasping synergistic topologies, preserving Thirdness, and asserting coordinative agency within irreducible systems.
