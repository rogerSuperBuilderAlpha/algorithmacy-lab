## What the talk may use it for
Herzberger's chapter is the first formal treatment in which both clauses of Peirce's thesis come out as theorems, but only under two stated conditions. The conditions carry the point. In his "bonding algebra" lower polyads (n ≤ 3) are "absolutely irreducible" (T7), and the reduction thesis holds "within any sufficiently large domain" (T8). More generally it holds "within any system of valency-regular definitional processes rich enough to permit the formation of relative products" (T9). Herzberger also proves the other side himself: the counterargument "holds within standard notions of definability" (T11), and so "the reduction thesis collapses under standard notions of definability." So the operation-relativity that Burch's SEP entry states in one sentence was already a theorem-pair in 1981. The talk may present Herzberger as the case where one text proves both the thesis and its collapse, the two differing only in the admitted operations. That is the gerrymandering issue in its cleanest form.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- herzberger-valency-restriction (TR-S4-063) → verified for the literature half. Herzberger states a "valency rule" and restricts T9 to "valency-regular" definitional processes. The lab half of D4's sentence (cause side, all 89 wirings) is a lab result that the literature cannot confirm.
- burch-1991-proved-prt (TR-S4-132, -199): "Burch gave the first (modern) algebraic proof" → corrected. Herzberger proved T7 (irreducibility) and T8 (reducibility in sufficiently large domains) in 1981. Burch's result may improve on Herzberger's domain-size condition (Conarroe p. 140, reporting Burch 1997 p. 251; unread).
- Brief and earlier card: "the first modern formalization in which both clauses are theorems" → corrected. Both clauses are theorems only for the bonding algebra and only in sufficiently large domains (T8), or in infinite domains for the miniature setting (T5). Herzberger's T11 shows the thesis collapses under standard definability.
- pal-valency-conservation (TR-S1-052) → corrected: the valency rule is Herzberger's, and it is now matched in the scans. Whether Burch argues from conservation is unread.
- degree-2-graphs-paths-and-cycles (TR-S1-007, -028, -036) → unchanged; Herzberger's valency-regularity is the formal counterpart (secondary sources as before).
- kempe-junction (TR-S1-021) → unchanged. "Triple junction" gave no hit in either scan's OCR, so the attribution of the term to Herzberger rests on Koshkin alone.
