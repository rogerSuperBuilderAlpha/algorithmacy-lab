## What the talk may use it for
Skaggs, a design semiotician, sets Peirce's semiotic triad (sign, referent, interpretant) beside IIT 3.0. He finds four points of agreement: consciousness as relational structure, nesting, a subject set against an environment, and no consciousness without an effect on the system. Exclusion and continuity divide the two theories, and he proposes a "dwell" period to reconcile them. The paper is informal and qualitative. It uses no mathematics from IIT: no φ, no partitions, no MIP. It never mentions Peirce's logic of relatives, the reduction thesis, teridentity, clone theory or information decomposition. The talk can cite it as the prior Peirce–IIT paper whose conclusions differ in kind from the lab's formal move. Skaggs himself observed that "Peirce is not referenced in the IIT literature".

## Notes
- The paper summarises IIT 3.0 loosely. For example, it lists the postulates in its own paraphrase and treats "recurrence" as a postulate (p. 315). Do not cite it for IIT's content.
