## What the talk may use it for
Burch's own short exposition of PAL and of his proof is this chapter, and it is the most economical primary source to read before quoting him. From snippets alone it is clear that he names the thesis as having "both a negative and" a positive part. He ties the positive part to "hypostatic" abstraction and builds PAL from named operators, COMMA, QUANT, HOOKID and PRODUCT. The talk may cite the chapter as Burch's exposition. It should not quote a sentence from it until someone reads the pages, because every snippet below is a single OCR line from one scan.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- burch-branching-teridentity (TR-S4-064) → Conarroe (conarroe2020relations, p. 140) reports that the COMMA operation is PAL's "algebraic analog of" the branching of a line of identity, citing this chapter's p. 244. A snippet here names COMMA first among the PAL operators and mentions an operator's "interdefinability with the teridentity relation". What that "its" refers to was not visible. Burch's own wording is still unread.
- pal-translation-needs-hypostatic-abstraction (TR-S4-146) → the snippet ties hypostatic abstraction to "the positive part" of the thesis, which fits the register's correction (constructive clause, not translation of quantification theory). It is a single OCR line and does not verify the claim.
- burch-1991-proved-prt (TR-S4-132, -199) → Conarroe (p. 140) reads this chapter's p. 251 as saying that Burch's result "generalizes beyond Herzberger, by eliminating a stipulation that Herzberger had to make about the size of the relevant domain". That is Conarroe's paraphrase, seen once. If it holds, it is the one respect in which Burch's proof advances on Herzberger's T8, which holds only "within any sufficiently large domain" (herzberger1981theorem).
