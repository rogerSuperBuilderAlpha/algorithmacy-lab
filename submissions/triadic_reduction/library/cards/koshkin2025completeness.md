## What the talk may use it for
This paper closes the finite-domain question that koshkin2024reduction left open. Under primitive positive (pp) composition, which allows conjunction, shared variables and existential quantification, every n-ary relation with n ≥ 4 on *any* domain reduces to ternary relations (Theorem 2; for infinite domains the proof needs D to be well-orderable, i.e. the axiom of choice). The proof reads relations as multivalued "functions" and uses Post's functional completeness of 2-input functions. On finite domains with |D| ≥ 3, every relation reduces further to binary relations (Theorem 3 with Theorem 2). On Boolean domains that further step fails: ¬I3 is pp-irreducible to binaries (Theorem 4). Translated into Peirce's bond algebra, where n ≥ 4 pp-reducibility equals bond reducibility (Theorem 5) and teridentity is bond-irreducible on any domain with two or more elements (Theorem 6), the result "gives a uniform proof of Peirce's reduction thesis on finite domains". Koshkin notes that Bodnarčuk-Kalužnin-Kotov-Romov 1969 already implied the finite-domain binary result non-constructively.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- arxiv-2606-19492 (TR-S4-057): D1 works-cited "31. Functional completeness and primitive positive decomposition of…" → verified. The paper exists under the full title above and is published in *Logic Journal of the IGPL* 33(2), 2025, article jzae077. The arXiv posting (June 2026) comes after the journal version. D1's body gives no visible inline anchor for [31], so its intended use cannot be read off D1.
- ternary-completeness (TR-S4-015, -035, -094, -191) → corrected. (1) TR-S4-015, "the clone generated by relations of arity ≤ 3 is all of Rel(A)", holds in the co-clone (pp) sense on every domain (Theorem 2). On finite domains a single ternary Sheffer relation suffices (Corollary 1), and for |D| ≥ 3 binaries suffice. (2) TR-S4-035 and -191, "every n-ary relation is built from teridentities and dyads": in bond form this holds on infinite domains (koshkin2024reduction, Theorem 14) and on finite domains with |D| ≥ 3 (Theorems 2, 3, 5 here). It fails on Boolean domains, where ¬I3 is not a bond of binaries and teridentities (Theorems 4-5). Relations of arity ≥ 4 still reduce to ternaries there. The proof is Koshkin's pp/functional-completeness argument, not "PAL". (3) The register notes on these rows say "Koshkin reports that reduction to triads fails on finite domains". That is refuted: Theorem 2 proves reduction to ternaries on every domain.
- koshkin-finite-domain-obstruction (TR-S4-125, -126, -212) → corrected. Finite domains obstruct only one-parameter hypostatic abstraction for large relations (p. 5) and, on Boolean domains, pp-reduction of ternaries to binaries (Theorem 4). TR-S4-212's "finite domains inherently obstruct dyadic reduction" is true only for |D| = 2 and pp-composition. Bond reduction to dyads fails on every domain, finite or infinite (Theorem 6).
- irreducibility-only-without-domain-expansion (TR-S4-089): "the impossibility holds only absent domain expansion" → corrected. Domain extension is the escape route only for pp/projoin reduction to binaries. Hypostatic abstraction needs it on finite domains for large relations (p. 5), and every relation is small on an infinite domain. Bond irreducibility of teridentity does not depend on the domain (Theorem 6, |D| ≥ 2), and on |D| ≥ 3 pp-reduction to binaries succeeds without any extension (Theorem 3).
- teridentity-not-generated-from-dyads (TR-S4-091, -092): cite Koshkin 2024 for non-reducibility → corrected. Koshkin proves bond-irreducibility of non-degenerate ternaries (koshkin2024reduction, Theorem 16) and gives an elementary proof for I3 (Theorem 6 here). The register note ("mainly the reduction clause") undersells this. The irreducibility with negation allowed on finite domains belongs to Hereth Correia & Pöschel 2006 / Hereth & Pöschel 2011, as koshkin2022reduction (p. 18) says. In the pp/co-clone sense I3 is *not* irreducible, since I3(x,y,z) = (x=y) ∧ (y=z).
- burch-1991-proved-prt (TR-S4-041) → corrected. Koshkin's proofs are (i) a derivation of the thesis on infinite domains as a corollary of projoin results (koshkin2024reduction, Theorem 14) and (ii) this paper's uniform finite-domain proof. Both use pp-composition and bond explication, not PAL.
- logicians-formalized-pal-clone-theory → corrected. This paper is the one Koshkin paper that works inside clone theory, through functional completeness and co-clone bases. Even here he places the Pol-Inv connection in 1960s universal algebra, credits the first pp-complete binary system to Bodnarčuk et al. 1969 (p. 3), and dates the link between PAL and pp-decomposition to Hereth & Pöschel 2004 (p. 15).
