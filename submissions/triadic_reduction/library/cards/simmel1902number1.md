## What the talk may use it for
The dyad passage. Simmel's claim at p. 45 is that a pair has no whole over and above its two members: if one fails to perform, "only the other remains." A triad adds, for each pair, a second route through the third, the "broken line" beside the straight one. At p. 46 he adds that the same third who binds can also divide, and that the pair lacks both effects. This is the English serialization of the chapter he revised into *Soziologie* (1908, pp. 93-94 for the same passage; see simmel1908soziologie).

**Slide-safe (page images, re-checked 2026-09-24):** the "only the other remains" sentence (p. 45), the broken-line sentence (p. 45), the intruder sentence (p. 45) and the "both phenomena are lacking" sentence (p. 46). The A-B-C sentence is slide-safe only with "[sic]" after the second "immediate", or in Wolff's wording (1950: 135, "their indirect one"), because the 1902 print misprints it.

## Loci
All matched character-for-character against the scan images; line-break hyphens joined.
- p. 44 — "we might characterize the dyad-group. Since in this case each element has only another individual by its side, but not a multiplicity which ultimately constitutes a higher unity" — Small's own "dyad". Page image.
- p. 45 — "the decisive factor in this case is that each must actually perform something, and that, when he refuses to do this, only the other remains, without any superindividual energy such as, even in the case of a combination of only three, is in some measure present." — the dyad has no superindividual residue.
- p. 45 — "Where three elements, A, B, C, constitute a community, there is added to the immediate relationship which exists, for example, between A and B, the immediate relationship which they gain by their common relation to C." — the scan prints "immediate" twice; the German (1908: 93) has "die mittelbare" for the second, and Wolff (1950: 135) renders it "their indirect one". Quote with [sic] or use Wolff.
- p. 45 — "This is unquestionably a sociological enrichment, apart from the bond by the straight and shortest line; each pair of elements are now joined by a broken line." — the broken-line passage.
- p. 45 — "There is no relationship so complete between three that each individual may not, under certain circumstances, be regarded by the other two as an intruder" — the third also divides.
- p. 46 — "it is usual in precisely such finely tuned combinations for three to constitute at the same time three parties" — affect, not structure.
- p. 46 — "The sociological structure of the combination in twos is consequently distinguished by the fact that both phenomena are lacking: both the strengthened attachment through a third, ... and also the interruption and distraction of pure and immediate reciprocation." — the dyad lacks both the binding and the dividing third.

## Notes
- The lab's C1 quotation of the A-B-C sentence ends in an ellipsis before "apart from the bond", which hides the doubled "immediate". The talk should not reproduce the 1902 misprint silently.
- "Superindividual energy" is Small's rendering of "überindividuelle Kraft" (1908: 93).
- Part I ends at p. 46 with "[To be continued.]"; the triad typology (mediator, *tertius gaudens*, *divide et impera*) is in Part II (simmel1902number2).
