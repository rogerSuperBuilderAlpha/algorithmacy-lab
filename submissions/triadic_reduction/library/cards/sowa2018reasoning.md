## What the talk may use it for
Sowa gives a compact modern gloss of Peirce's Beta existential graphs next to their predicate-calculus translations. His Table 3 shows the teridentity as a ligature joining three lines, translated as ∃x∃y∃z(male(x) ∧ human(y) ∧ african(z) ∧ x = y ∧ y = z). It also shows the equivalent single-variable form, in which the teridentity disappears into one variable, x, used three times. This is a citable illustration of how first-order syntax absorbs the teridentity through a variable that fills three places. Sowa also quotes Peirce calling the branch point a "point of teridentity".

- p. 996 — "The syntactic junction or point of teridentity asserts the identity of something denoted by all three." — part of a block quotation that Sowa presents as Peirce's ("Peirce continued", p. 997). **No Peirce locus is given for it.** The block quote carries no citation, and Sowa inserts his own bracket ("[Figure 6 shows]"). The only attribution is the blanket rule on p. 993: "Unless otherwise cited, all quotations by Peirce are from the New Elements of Mathematics, pages 3:162 to 3:169" (NEM, ed. Eisele, 1976, his ref. [48]). That is a range, not a page, and NEM was not checked. Treat the sentence as Sowa's transcription; the peirce cluster owns the primary.
- p. 996 — "which shows that that three lines of identity refer to the same individual. Peirce called such a junction a ligature." — Sowa on the coreference node [x y z].

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- journal-applied-logics-entry (TR-S4-056) → corrected. D1's entry "26. Journal of Applied Logics (College Publications)" links a whole issue: *Journal of Applied Logics – IfCoLog* 5(5), August 2018. The only article in it on Peirce's graphs is Sowa's, cited above. D1 gives no inline anchor for [26], so which article D1 meant is inferred, not certain.
- teridentity-branching-node (TR-S1-006, -013, -027, -035) → corrected. The EG reading is verified for -006 and -013. A three-way ligature junction is a teridentity, "point of teridentity" in the quoted Peirce passage, and it translates as x = y ∧ y = z (Table 3). This does not rescue TR-S1-005, which attaches the branch to the relative product (see herethcorreia2004power). TR-S1-027's "fundamental" does not occur in Sowa's pp. 995–997, and TR-S1-035's "join-irreducible" has no source (see herethcorreia2006teridentity).
- fol-variable-sharing-hides-teridentity (TR-S1-010) → verified with a correction. Table 3's "One label" row shows three occurrences of one variable doing the work of the teridentity. Two occurrences would be a plain line of identity.
