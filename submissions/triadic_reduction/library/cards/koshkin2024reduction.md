## What the talk may use it for
The paper studies reduction of relations under three operations of increasing strength: joins (conjunction with shared variables), projoins (joins followed by projection, i.e. all primitive positive formulas) and bonds (Peirce's relative-product algebra, where no variable is shared by more than two factors). Joins leave "almost all" relations irreducible. Projoins reduce every relation on an infinite domain to binaries by hypostatic abstraction, which needs a 1-key and so works exactly when |R| ≤ |D|. For n ≥ 4, projoin and bond reducibility coincide. Theorem 14 states Peirce's thesis in bond form for infinite domains. The paper then defines *ternarity* (the minimal number of ternaries in a bond reduction to arity ≤ 3) and proves ter(R) ≥ n − 2 for every non-degenerate n-ary R on any domain, with equality when |R| ≤ |D|, and so for every non-degenerate relation on an infinite domain. On finite domains the equality fails already for n = 4 (Herzberger's quaternary on a 3-element domain has ternarity 4). Whether any n ≥ 4 relation on a finite domain is irreducible is left open; the author suspects not, and koshkin2025completeness later proves that none is. For the S4→S5 bridge, the paper states the hinge in one line: under the uniform distribution on a relation's tuples, degeneracy across a bipartition of its places is statistical independence of the two blocks (p. 7). It then reads ternaries as "relays in information exchange" and asks, as an open problem, whether ternarity bounds measures of information exchange like those Tegmark studies (pp. 23, 30).

- p. 1 (abstract) — "Our algebraic framework unifies natural joins and data dependencies of database theory and relational algebra of clone theory with the bond algebra of C.S. Peirce."
- p. 1 — "decomposition of relations in algebras of relational clones or co-clones is closely related to Peirce’s, but was developed independently of his work" — Koshkin does not claim clone theory; he credits Post, Lau, and Pöschel & Kalužnin.
- p. 3 — "However, we do not resort to a central device of clone theory, the Pol-Inv Galois connection"
- p. 3 — "The bond algebra is, more or less, Peirce’s project-join algebra." — Theorem 13 (p. 20) sharpens this: projoin and bond reducibility coincide for n ≥ 4 and diverge on ternaries.
- p. 4 — "prove that ternarity of non-degenerate n-ary relations is always n − 2 on infinite domains (Theorem 16)"
- p. 4 — "We establish a close connection between complete bond reductions and subcubic graphs"
- p. 7 — "Note that if we assign equal probability to all tuples of R degenerate over a partition Λi then aΛi will be statistically independent random vectors on this sample space [29]." and "When testing for degeneracy it is sufficient to consider bipartitions only." — the formal hinge of the information-integration pointer: under the uniform distribution on tuples, degeneracy over a bipartition is statistical independence of the two blocks of places, i.e. zero mutual information across that cut. (The converse, that zero mutual information across a cut forces the relation to be a product there, is my inference from the support of a product distribution; Koshkin states only the forward direction.)
- p. 11 — "join reductions leave asymptotically ‘almost all’ relations irreducible" — Theorem 8: the share of join-reducible relations goes to 0 as |D| → ∞, but not as n → ∞ on a fixed domain.
- p. 12 — "This is a reduction of sorts, but it is no longer a join, rather a projection of a join." — hypostatic abstraction is a projoin (Theorem 9, p. 14).
- p. 14 — "on infinite domains, any relation admits a 1-key." — this follows from |D|^n = |D|, which the paper notes (p. 15) is equivalent to the axiom of choice.
- p. 21 (Theorem 14, "Peirce’s reduction thesis") — "Any n-ary with n ≥ 3 on an infinite domain reduces to a bond of unaries, binaries and teridentities. The only bond irreducible relations on such domains are all unaries, non-degenerate binaries, and non-degenerate ternaries."
- p. 23 — "This suggests that ternaries play the role of relays in information exchange among the attributes, and their number quantifies the ‘complexity of relating attributes’." — the motivation for ternarity (§8), drawn from branch points in bonding diagrams.
- p. 23 — "Mutual information between attributes has been studied in the context of database theory [29], and, more recently, as a measure of information integration in biological systems [36]." — [36] is Tegmark 2016 (tegmark2016improved); [29] is Malvestuto 1986, "Statistical treatment of the information content of a database".
- p. 24 (Definition 10) — ternarity "is the minimal number of ternaries in its subternaric bond reductions, and ∞ if no such reductions exist."
- p. 24 — "Whether this holds on finite domains, i.e. whether ter (R) < ∞ for all R, is an open problem"
- p. 27 (Theorem 16) — "Ternarity of any non-degenerate n-ary R with n ≥ 2 satisfies ter (R) ≥ n − 2. In particular, any non-degenerate ternary is bond irreducible. If, moreover, |R| ≤ |D| then ter (R) = n − 2. In particular, ter (R) = n − 2 for all relations on infinite domains." (the spacing "ter (R)" is pdftotext's)
- p. 29 — "Problem 1: Are there irreducible n-ary relations with n ≥ 4?" and "While we suspect a negative answer to the first problem"
- p. 29 — "Counting arguments we used would not settle the question alone"
- p. 29 — "We already saw that the strongest form of the reduction thesis, that gives ternarity of non-degenerate n-ary relations as n − 2, fails on finite domains."
- p. 30 — "One can think of minimal reductions as revealing the structure of information processing within a relation, which suggests an affirmative answer." — the answer is to Problem 4 (uniqueness of bond graphs of minimal ternaric reductions).
- p. 30 — "In [29] a measure of information exchange among relation’s attributes is introduced and studied, similar measures are studied in computational biology [36]."
- p. 30 — "Problem 5: Is there an information-theoretic interpretation of ternarity, e.g. bounds on measures of information exchange in terms of it?" — the bridge is posed as an open problem, not a result.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- koshkin-2024-bib (TR-S4-022, -047, -100, -106, -208): "Koshkin (2024), Log. J. IGPL / 'Logical Journal of the IGPL', arXiv:2406.14094" → corrected: *Logic Journal of the IGPL* 32(5): 779-809, doi 10.1093/jigpal/jzad010, online 2023, issue 2024. D1's truncated entry "2. from relational databases to Peirce's reduction thesis" is this paper.
- logicians-formalized-pal-clone-theory (TR-S4-001, -007, -065, -069, -072, -078): Koshkin "developed"/"formalized" universal algebra, PAL or relational clone theory → corrected. He unifies database joins, the co-clone (projoin) algebra and Peirce's bond algebra and extends the results (ternarity, finite-domain counterexamples). He explicitly credits co-clone theory to Post and to the Pol-Inv tradition and says his approach avoids its Galois connection (pp. 1, 3). He did not translate PAL into clone theory; Hereth Correia & Pöschel 2004 did.
- ternary-completeness (TR-S4-015, -035, -094, -191) → corrected, see the verdict under koshkin2025completeness. For this paper: the bond thesis ("unaries, binaries and teridentities") is Theorem 14 *on infinite domains*, and the finite case is open here (p. 24, Problem 1). "Subcubic hypergraph networks" should read *subcubic graphs* (bond graphs of reductions to arity ≤ 3; p. 4, §9).
- koshkin-unifies-databases-bonds-csp (TR-S4-101, -118, -207) → verified (abstract, p. 1). CSP enters through co-clones and projoin completeness (pp. 2-3). The talk should date it 2023 (online) or 2024 (issue), not "In 2024" as the first appearance.
- koshkin-ternarity-measure (TR-S4-119, -209, -211): "Koshkin introduces 'ternarity' as a measure of relational complexity … quantifying relational non-factorizability" → corrected. The term and definition are Koshkin's (Def. 10, p. 24), first introduced as "positive ternarity" in koshkin2022reduction. It counts the minimal number of ternaries needed to *build* a relation from pieces of arity ≤ 3 (his gloss is "complexity of relating"). It is not a measure of non-factorizability, because it is finite exactly when the relation *does* factor down to arity ≤ 3.
- koshkin-ternarity-n-minus-2 (TR-S4-210): "ter(R) = n − 2 on infinite domains" → corrected, and not invented: Theorem 16 (p. 27) holds for *non-degenerate* n-ary R. A degenerate product of two non-degenerate binaries has arity 4 and ternarity 0. The equality also holds on finite domains for small relations (|R| ≤ |D|).
- koshkin-ternarity-irreducible-proof (TR-S4-107): "proves, via bond algebra, that ternarity is an irreducible measure" → refuted as worded. He proves the lower bound ter ≥ n − 2 from Listing's formula on subcubic bond graphs (Lemma 4, Theorem 16). No claim about an "irreducible measure" appears.
- koshkin-infinite-domain-reduction-to-ternary (TR-S4-108, -124) → corrected. Theorem 14 confirms *both* clauses in bond form on infinite domains; the register note ("not the irreducibility of triads") is wrong, since Theorem 16 proves non-degenerate ternaries bond-irreducible on any domain. Hypostatic abstraction reduces higher relations to *binaries* under projoin (Corollary 1, p. 14). Only after bond explication do binaries plus teridentities appear.
- koshkin-projoins-bonding-hypostatic (TR-S4-120, -123) → corrected. TR-S4-120 is verified: hypostatic abstraction is a projoin (p. 12, Theorem 9). TR-S4-123 overstates: bonds are a restricted subclass of projoins (no attribute shared by three factors). The two notions of reducibility coincide for n ≥ 4 and differ on ternaries (Theorem 13).
- koshkin-infinite-domain-1-key (TR-S4-121) → verified (p. 14), assuming the axiom of choice.
- koshkin-join-reductions-leave-irreducible (TR-S4-122) → verified (Theorems 7-8, p. 11), asymptotically in domain size.
- koshkin-finite-domain-obstruction (TR-S4-125, -126, -212) → corrected. On finite domains, what fails is (a) one-parameter hypostatic abstraction for *large* relations (|R| > |D|; e.g. ¬I3 on |D| = 2, Example 6) and (b) the equality ter = n − 2 (Herzberger's quaternary; Theorem 17). Reduction to ternaries itself is left *open* (Problem 1), with the author expecting it to succeed. The talk cannot say that "finite domains obstruct" reduction, and TR-S4-212's "dyadic reduction" is wrong except on Boolean domains in the projoin sense (koshkin2025completeness, Theorem 4).
- koshkin-finite-counting-arguments (TR-S4-127, -128) → corrected. The counting theorems (3, 8, 10, 17) show that almost all relations are irreducible under *restricted* means (Cartesian product, join, projoin with a bounded number of parameters, bonds of bounded ternarity). By the author's own statement, they do not show that irreducible relations exist under unrestricted projoin or bond (p. 29).
- burch-cited-by-koshkin-hp (TR-S4-129): Burch 1991 in Koshkin's bibliography → verified: ref. [8] here, in koshkin2022reduction, and in koshkin2025completeness. The Hereth Correia & Pöschel half belongs to another cluster (HP 2004 cites "[Bur91]").
- dau-koshkin-evaluate-burch (TR-S4-130) → corrected. This paper follows Burch ("following Peirce and Burch", §6) rather than evaluating him; the evaluation is in koshkin2022reduction. The Gemini sources' "Dau & Hereth Correia (2006)" has the published author order (Crossref doi 10.1007/11671404_7; dau2006instances). Koshkin's ref. [13] here reverses it, as "J. Hereth Correia, F. Dau" (p. 30), and gives the pages as 106-118 where the volume has 105–118. That paper does discuss Burch's juxtaposition restriction (dau2006instances, p. 106), which is the one sense in which it "evaluates" Burch.
