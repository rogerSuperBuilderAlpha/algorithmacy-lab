## What the talk may use it for
Peirce answers Kempe here. He calls Kempe's 1886 memoir "a formidable objection to my views." Kempe showed (§68) that spots joined by lines of one kind can represent any relationship. Kempe also showed (§330) that an auxiliary spot lets a triad be drawn with two-ended links. Peirce concedes more than the lab's papers report: Kempe "virtually shows that my algebra is perfectly adequate to expressing that A gives B to C," once "this action" is added to the universe as a new object. His rejoinder makes two points. First, Kempe's diagrams have three kinds of element, and "the attachment of lines to spots" carries mediation. Second, the diagram cannot show how the abstraction "this action" is derived from the concrete ideas. That derivation is hypostatic abstraction, the step the positive clause needs for its minted term E. Peirce modifies his position here, "but not to surrender it."

## Loci
- CP 3.423 (Open Court p. 3417) — "But Mr. A. B. Kempe, in his important memoir on the 'Theory of Mathematical Forms,' presents an analysis which amounts to a formidable objection to my views." — the challenge, in Peirce's words.
- CP 3.423 — "He thus represents every possible relationship by a diagram consisting of only two different kinds of elements, namely, spots and lines between pairs of spots." … "It causes me somewhat to modify my position, but not to surrender it." — what Kempe did, and the verdict.
- CP 3.423 — "In the third place, the spots, or units, as he calls them, involve the idea of firstness; the two-ended lines, that of secondness; the attachment of lines to spots, that of mediation." — the reply: mediation sits in the attachment.
- CP 3.424 (Open Court pp. 3417-3418) — "But Mr. Kempe (§ 330) virtually shows that my algebra is perfectly adequate to expressing that A gives B to C" — the concession.
- CP 3.424 — "This is accomplished by adding to the universe of concrete things the abstraction 'this action.' But I remark that the diagram fails to afford any formal representation of the manner in which this abstract idea is derived from the concrete ideas." — the rejoinder: hypostatic abstraction is not represented.
- CP 3.422 — "the ideas which belong to the three forms of rhemata are firstness, secondness, thirdness; firstness, or spontaneity; secondness, or dependence; thirdness, or mediation." — Thirdness glossed as mediation (supports thirdness-gloss-mediation).

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- thirdness-gloss-mediation (rows TR-S1-045, -061, -064): "genuine mediation, representation, and lawful integration" → corrected. "Mediation" is Peirce's gloss (CP 3.422, and CP 1.337 and 1.356 on peirce1931papers and peirce1890guess). "Lawful integration" is not his phrase, and it must not be read as IIT integration.

## Notes
- Pagination: the issue's table of contents puts the article at p. 3415, and the running heads place the Kempe passage on pp. 3417-3418. The CP source note to §2 gives "pp. 3416-8". Cite 3415-3418 and CP 3.415-424.
- CP 3.423 note 1 gives Kempe's memoir as "Philosophical Transactions for 1886 [pp. 1-70]". Peirce names it "Theory of Mathematical Forms". The published title is "A Memoir on the Theory of Mathematical Form" (kempe1886memoir).
- **Lab papers.** `thinkers/peirce/paper.md` l. 272-274 says Peirce's answer to Kempe, "in 'The Critic of Arguments' and the letter to Lady Welby, was that the junction is the triad — 'no branching of a line can result from putting one line on the end of another'." Two corrections. (1) The aphorism is from the 1904 Welby letter (CP 8.331), which does not mention Kempe (peirce1904letter). (2) The 1892 reply puts mediation in "the attachment of lines to spots" and concedes that Kempe's §330 construction expresses giving. The junction-as-relative argument is in the 1897 *Monist* (pp. 170-171, peirce1897logic) and the 1903 Lowell lecture (CP 1.346). A version the sources support: Peirce granted that Kempe's auxiliary spot expresses the triad in dyadic links, and he answered that the spot and its three attachments are themselves the mediation, minted by an abstraction the diagram does not represent.
