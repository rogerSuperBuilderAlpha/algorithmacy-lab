## What the talk may use it for
Conarroe is where the "gerrymandered" label enters the literature. He uses it once, conditionally, and not quite as Koshkin reports. The thesis explains the reduction thesis as a consequence of two restrictions in Existential Graphs: lines join only one to another, and a line branches only in threes, at a point of teridentity. It then defends both restrictions as non-arbitrary. The gerrymandering charge appears only as what one could say if the thesis were taken narrowly, as a property of one peculiar system. Conarroe grants that such an objection "would not be far off base" and then offers two ways out. One is his defence of the restrictions; the other is a sketched meta-argument that any reduction of triads uses triadicity at the meta-level. The talk may cite him as the sympathetic source of the objection that Koshkin 2022 answers. It should call the work an M.A. thesis from Colorado, not a Virginia dissertation.

## Loci
Printed page numbers (the PDF runs four pages ahead: printed p. 142 = PDF p. 146):
- abstract (p. ii) — "In this paper, I explain how this metalogical property results from two semantic restrictions on how graphs or diagrams may be constructed. I then defend these two restrictions."
- p. 4 — "(i) when these lines are joined, they can only be joined one to another (rather than three or more joining at a node), and (ii) when a line “branches”, it must branch in threes." — the two restrictions; also "To an extent, he crafted it so that the Reduction Thesis would hold."
- p. 101 — "one could claim here that we are arguing in a circle—constructing a logical system with certain restrictions and then triumphantly pointing out a property of that system, which is a direct result of this restriction." — he then argues that the restriction is "non-arbitrary".
- p. 142 — "merely understood in that sense, one can simply argue that the logical system itself—i.e. EG—is gerrymandered in a way to ensure something like the Reduction Thesis holds. And in matter of fact, this objection would not be far off base." — the gerrymandering passage; the only use of "gerrymander" in the thesis.
- p. 142 — Conarroe quotes Burch's SEP entry (Winter 2018 edition) that Peirce's "constructive resources are to be understood to include only negation, a generalization of de Morgan's relative product operation, and … ‘the teridentity relation’", and concludes: "So if we restrict the logical toolkit from the beginning, then we end up with a different logical system."
- p. 143 — "One way is by defending the legitimacy of the restriction on conceptual combination that it makes, which I have already attempted in the previous chapters. But I believe there is also another way" — the second way is the meta-level argument.
- p. 146 — "any metalogical proof, like Quine's, that reduces triads to dyads must itself employ triadicity. To give a proper proof of this would go beyond my current scope" — the meta-argument is a sketch, which he develops with Hintikka (pp. 146ff.).
- p. 141 — of Hereth Correia & Pöschel 2006 he writes that the paper is "extremely technical, and, regrettably, not well understood by me" and that he is "in no place to comment on it".

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- koshkin2022reduction's reference "R. Conarroe. Relations and Peirce's Graphical Logic. PhD thesis, University of Virginia, 2020" → corrected. It is Roger Conarroe's M.A. thesis, University of Colorado Boulder, 2020 (title page; CU Scholar record). Virginia is where he took his B.A. and first M.A. The talk brief's "UVA dissertation" inherits Koshkin's error.
- Koshkin (p. 4): the concern "under the catchy name of “gerrymander”, recently resurfaced in (Conarroe, 2020)" → verified, with a nuance. The word and the argument are on p. 142, and Koshkin's quotation of it matches. Conarroe voices the charge conditionally, against the thesis read narrowly, and his own programme is to defend the EG restrictions, which Koshkin's summary leaves out.
- Koshkin (p. 4): Conarroe "argues that a response equal to Peirce's ambition … requires a meta-argument … and suggests Hintikka's formalization" → verified in substance (pp. 142–146). Conarroe offers the meta-argument as one of two responses (p. 143) and only gestures at it (p. 146).
- burch-restriction-undermines-universality (TR-S4-147–149; card herethcorreia2006teridentity) → Conarroe is an evaluative source for a gerrymandering worry, but he aims it at EG's restrictions (join two at a time; branch in threes), not at Burch's juxtaposition restriction, which he never discusses.
- burch-branching-teridentity (TR-S4-064; card burch1991reduction) → p. 140 reports that Burch 1997 introduces "a PAL operation for this called COMMA", the algebraic analog of a branching line of identity (citing Burch, PRT, 244). One secondary transcription; see burch1997reduction.
- herzberger-valency-restriction (TR-S4-063; card herzberger1981theorem) → p. 138 quotes Herzberger p. 52: "The union of any µ-ad with any ν-ad gives [µ+ν-2λ]-ad, where λ is the number of bonds of union". Independent of Koshkin, and it matches the scans (see herzberger1981theorem).

## Notes
- Conarroe transcribes Burch 1991 (APRT) at pp. ix, xv, 1–2, 5, 22, 27, 39–40, 118, 120, 121 and Burch 1997 (PRT) at pp. 235–237, 244–245, 251. These are the only transcriptions of those books available in this pass; burch1991reduction and burch1997reduction record them as reported, not read.
- Conarroe cites Quine's "Reduction to a Dyadic Predicate" as "Quine (1953)" at pp. 224–225, which looks like a reprint pagination, not JSL 19 (1954) 180–182. That is a question for the quine1954reduction card's owner.
- The Kerr-Lawson he cites (p. 8 and elsewhere) is "Peirce's Pre-Logistic Account of Mathematics" (Studies in the Logic of CSP, 1997), not "Stripped Down Burch".
