## What the talk may use it for
The article has not been read, so everything here is as reported by koshkin2022reduction and herzberger1981theorem. As reported by Koshkin (pp. 2, 4), this is the first scholarly article devoted to the reduction thesis. It coins the name "Peirce's thesis" and, after reviewing Kempe-style reductions to dyads, warns that the thesis can be saved only by restricting the allowed operations, which would make it an artifact of one's favoured operations. That is the worry Koshkin calls "gerrymander". As reported by Herzberger, whose wording was matched in two scans, Skidmore "finds in these facts a refutation of Peirce's thesis" — the facts being that the thesis collapses under standard notions of definability. The talk may cite Skidmore, as reported by Koshkin and Herzberger, as the origin of the objection that every later proof (Herzberger, Burch, Hereth & Pöschel) answers by fixing an operation set. It should not quote him directly until the article is read.
