## What the talk may use it for
Koshkin answers his title question with a no. He grants that Herzberger's valency rule and Burch's restriction on juxtaposition tie the thesis to privileged operations, then replaces them with an "invariant" formulation. Any first-order operation may be used, but its branch points and quantified places are explicated as bonding with pluridentities (I_n, hence teridentity). Under that explication, connected triads are irreducible by any logical operation, and pairing-style reductions to dyads use a hidden triple junction, which is to say a triad. The paper also introduces "positive ternarity" (the minimal number of triads in an explicated positive representation; it equals n − 2 for small non-degenerate n-ads). Its informational passage (pp. 14–15) is the literature's own bridge from S4 to S5, and it is narrower than the lab's gloss. Koshkin reads the places of a relation as "information channels", assigns equal probability to every tuple (on finite domains), and suggests that the minimum mutual information over all bipartitions of the places, the "cruelest cut", would measure *how close a relation comes to being degenerate* more finely than positive ternarity, which cannot tell teridentity from any other non-degenerate triad. He adds that "such measures of 'information integration' are studied in biology (Tegmark, 2016)". The pointer names Tegmark's family of measures, not IIT's Φ, and a footnote calls the extensional notion of information behind it "too narrow on Peirce's view" and only a "tentative means of quantifying it" (note 18, p. 22).

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- triadic-binding-shifted-to-metasyntax (TR-S3-060): reductions "covertly shift irreducible triadic relations into meta-syntax or pairing functions" → corrected. This paper is the source the claim needs: pairing and variable identification absorb triads syntactically (pp. 2, 7). "Nominalist/descriptive" is Gemini's framing, and the mechanism Koshkin names is identification of variables and branch points, not "meta-syntax". The register note that "Koshkin argues the opposite worry" is wrong: he concedes the worry for Herzberger and Burch and then rebuts it.
- burch-1991-proved-prt (TR-S4-041): Koshkin as a "generalized proof" → corrected. This paper gives invariant *formulations* with sketched topological proofs, and it concludes that the thesis is *not* setting-dependent. The register's gloss ("argues that the thesis is setting-dependent") inverts its conclusion.
- koshkin-gerrymandered (TR-S4-050): D1 works-cited "Is Peirce's reduction thesis gerrymandered? (arXiv 2406.14058)" → verified; the arXiv number is correct, and the paper is published in TCSPS 58(4) 2022.
- pairing-relation-irreducible-triad (TR-S4-079): "relational clone theory shows the pairing operation is itself an irreducible triadic relation" → corrected. What Koshkin shows, in an EG/FOL explication and not in clone theory, is that the pairing *reduction* is a triple junction that explicates to a teridentity (p. 7).
- dau-koshkin-evaluate-burch (TR-S4-130): "Koshkin (2024) evaluates Burch" → corrected. The evaluation of Burch is in this 2022 paper (§3, p. 8). The 2024 IGPL paper cites Burch and follows his derivation.
- pal-vindicated-peirce (TR-S4-196): "Peirce was mathematically vindicated" → corrected. Koshkin agrees ("settled in Peirce's favor", p. 3), and the register note ("Koshkin argues the vindication depends on privileged operations") gets him backwards. The vindication came in PAL and its upgrades (Burch; Hereth Correia & Pöschel), not in "universal algebra and clone theory", and in 2022 Koshkin still listed finite-domain questions as open (p. 20).
- hp-removed-burch-restriction (TR-S4-195): Hereth Correia & Pöschel removed Burch's restrictions → Koshkin independently reports this (p. 15; the "upgraded version of PAL" in HP 2004, p. 10). The HP texts themselves belong to another cluster.
- no-prior-unification-peirce-quine-iit (TR-S5-109, and the shared correction on TR-S5-009, -055): "the Peirce-Quine debate has remained completely isolated from information theory" → corrected, i.e. false as stated. This paper links degeneracy of relations to mutual information across the "cruelest cut" and to Tegmark's "information integration" measures (pp. 14–15), and koshkin2024reduction repeats the pointer (pp. 23, 30) and states the underlying equivalence of degeneracy with statistical independence under the uniform distribution (p. 7). The register's current correction attributes the passage to "Koshkin (2024) (arXiv 2406.14058) p. 13 approx." That conflates two papers: 2406.14058 is this 2022 TCSPS paper, the passage sits on pp. 14–15, and the 2023/24 IGPL paper is 2406.14094. The link is a tentative pointer, not a formal unification, so the absence claims about a formal Quine–Burch–Φ unification (TR-S5-009, -055) are not refuted by it.

## Notes
- The lab's Peirce bib entry is stale. `koshkin_gerrymandered` in `org_frontier/thinkers/peirce/literature/references.bib` is `@unpublished`, "Preprint, PhilSci-Archive 23543". The paper is a refereed article: TCSPS 58(4): 271-300 (2022), doi 10.2979/csp.2022.a886447. The PhilSci and arXiv copies are author versions.
- `thinkers/peirce/paper.md` l. 44-48 and `literature/deep_research_report.md` l. 31-35 misstate the paper in three places. (1) "all of these tie the thesis to privileged operations": Koshkin says this of Herzberger and Burch and exempts Hereth & Pöschel 2011, whose proof he says already carries the invariant version (p. 2). (2) "sketches an informational reading instead": the main move is the invariant explication, and the informational reading is a tentative gloss (note 18, p. 22: "tentative means of quantifying it"). (3) "genuine when fixing one place constrains the others": this is Koshkin's description of non-degeneracy, which he says only approximates genuineness from above, because genuine means irreducible (p. 19).
- The Peirce paper's sentence (`thinkers/peirce/paper.md` l. 47–48; `literature/deep_research_report.md` l. 35 has the same phrase): "he points to 'information integration' measures as the analogous quantity [koshkin_gerrymandered]. That is the quantity this lab computes." The first half is verified and the second needs correcting. Koshkin does point to "information integration" measures (pp. 14–15). What he points to them *for* is narrower than "the analogous quantity": the minimum mutual information over bipartitions of a relation's places, under the uniform distribution on its tuples, as a finer measure of how close a relation comes to *degeneracy*. Degeneracy only approximates genuineness from above (p. 19), and Koshkin calls the informational measure "tentative" (note 18, p. 22). His source is Tegmark 2016, whose measures are defined on Markov transitions, and Tegmark rejects the plain one-time mutual information across a cut (ϕofs) as an integration measure (tegmark2016improved, p. 19). The lab computes IIT 4.0 Φ on transition probability matrices, a causal quantity in the same family that Koshkin gestures at but not the measure he sketches. A corrected sentence could read: "Koshkin suggests, tentatively, that the minimum mutual information across bipartitions of a relation's places, the 'cruelest cut', would measure how close a relation comes to degeneracy, and he notes that such measures of 'information integration' are studied in biology, citing Tegmark (2016); his 2023 paper leaves an information-theoretic reading of ternarity as an open problem. The lab's Φ is a causal, dynamical member of that family." The same sentence also calls the paper a "preprint"; it is the TCSPS 2022 article.
- Metadata settled: TCSPS 58(4): 271–300, September 2022, in Crossref, in the arXiv journal-ref, and in Koshkin's own citation of the paper (koshkin2024reduction, ref. [21]: "58 (2022) no. 4, 271-300").
- Koshkin's reference list gives the Dau paper as "J. Hereth Correia and F. Dau" (p. 24) and cites it in the text as "Hereth Correia and Dau, 2006" (pp. 2, 9, 17). The published order, in Crossref and on the paper itself, is Dau first (dau2006instances). The talk should write Dau & Hereth Correia 2006.
