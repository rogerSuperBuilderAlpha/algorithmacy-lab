## What the talk may use it for
Oizumi, Albantakis and Tononi give the IIT 3.0 formalism that IIT 4.0 replaced. They measure distances between repertoires with the earth mover's distance (EMD), and they partition systems unidirectionally to find the minimum information partition (MIP). Integrated information over the MIP is Φ^Max, and they call the resulting structure a MICS. The paper is the source of IIT's "zombie" claim. They argue that a purely feed-forward system, however large, forms no complex, because its input layer has no causes within the system and its output layer has no effects on it. They conclude that behaviour alone cannot settle whether a system is conscious.

Pages are the article's (1–25); quotations matched against the pdftotext layer of the publisher PDF.
- p. 1 (abstract) — "there can be true ‘‘zombies’’ – unconscious feed-forward systems that are functionally equivalent to conscious complexes." — the zombie claim.
- p. 1 (abstract) — "integrated information as information specified by a whole that cannot be reduced to that specified by its parts." — IIT 3.0's integration.
- p. 4 (glossary) — "In IIT 3.0, the Wasserstein distance, also known as earth mover’s distance (EMD)." — the IIT 3.0 distance D.
- p. 4 (glossary) — "The TPM is determined by the mechanisms of a system and obtained by perturbing the system into all its possible states." — perturbational TPM.
- p. 16 — "called ‘‘ports-in’’ and ‘‘ports-out’’" — interface elements of the main complex, not a definition of the complex.
- p. 19 — "Therefore, systems with a purely feed-forward architecture cannot generate consciousness." — the feed-forward argument, extended "no matter how numerous its elements and complicated its connectivity" (p. 19).
- p. 21 — "whether a system is conscious or not cannot be decided based on its input-output behavior only." — structure over behaviour.
