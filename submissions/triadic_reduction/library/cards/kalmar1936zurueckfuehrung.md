## What the talk may use it for
Kalmár's is the first of the two results Quine 1954 names in n. 1 as "reminiscent of, but distinct from" his
own. Kalmár works on the decision problem. He proves that every formula has an equivalent formula, one that
is satisfiable exactly when the original is, containing a single binary function variable (that is, one
dyadic predicate variable) and a prefix of a fixed form. Quine separates his result from this one: his
concerns the translatability of an interpreted theory, whereas Kalmár's concerns "the likeness of
quantificational schemata in point of consistency or inconsistency" (Quine, n. 1). For the talk, Kalmár links
Löwenheim to Quine. His first sentence and n. 1 trace the line of results back to Löwenheim 1915, § 4, by way
of Herbrand. The reduction to one dyadic predicate therefore sits in a line of results in logic that runs
from Löwenheim through Herbrand and Kalmár to Quine. None of these papers, as read, answers Peirce.

## Loci
- p. 137, opening (scan) — "In Verschärfung eines Löwenheimschen Satzes 1) hat Herbrand 2) bewiesen, daß man sich beim Entscheidungsproblem 3) ohne Beschränkung der Allgemeinheit auf solche Formeln 4) beschränken kann, die drei binäre (und keine sonstigen) Funktionsvariable enthalten; oder aber auf solche Formeln, die eine einzige, und zwar ternäre, Funktionsvariable enthalten." — the lineage: a theorem of Löwenheim's, sharpened by Herbrand.
- p. 137, n. 1 (scan) — "L. Löwenheim, Über Möglichkeiten im Relativkalkül [Math. Annalen 76 (1915), 447—470], insb. § 4." — the Löwenheim theorem meant is § 4 of the 1915 paper, which is the reduction to binary relatives (lowenheim1915moglichkeiten). The citation independently confirms vol. 76.
- p. 138 (scan) — "III. Zu jeder Formel 𝔄 kann man eine gleichwertige 7) Formel 𝔅 mit einer einzigen, binären, Funktionsvariablen und mit einem Präfix von der Form (1) konstruieren 8)." — the main theorem. The letters are Fraktur capitals in the scan.
- p. 138, n. 7 (scan) — "Zwei Formeln heißen gleichwertig, falls entweder beide erfüllbar, oder beide unerfüllbar sind." — equivalence here means sameness of satisfiability. It is not a translation of an interpreted theory, which is the contrast Quine draws in n. 1.

## Notes
- Quine's n. 1 (Cambridge transcription) cites "Compositio mathematica, vol. 4 (1936), pp. 137–144". Numdam and the PDF cover sheet give "tome 4 (1937)". Kalmár's n. 8 cites Ackermann in Math. Ann. 112 (1936), so the paper went in during 1936. Use 1936 to match Quine, and note that the volume is dated 1937.
- Kalmár's n. 1 also cites his own simpler proof of Löwenheim's theorem: "Über einen Löwenheimschen Satz", Acta Szeged 7 (1934/35), 112–121. It was not read.
