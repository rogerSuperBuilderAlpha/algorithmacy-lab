## What the talk may use it for
Burch's own one-paragraph verdict on the Peirce–Quine standoff is here. He says both men were right, because the answer turns on which constructive resources one allows. That is the gerrymandering worry stated by PAL's own author as a plain fact, not as an objection. The talk can quote it at the S3→S4 hinge. It should say in the same breath that Koshkin (koshkin2022reduction, p. 4) quotes this very sentence as the "humbled version" of the thesis he sets out to replace, and denies that Peirce and Quine can both be right. The entry also gives Burch's list of Peirce's resources: negation, a generalized relative product and teridentity. The list leaves out juxtaposition (Cartesian product), which the Hereth/Pöschel papers treat as a PAL operation restricted in Burch's proof. In an encyclopedia summary that omission is harmless. It does mean the entry cannot stand in for the book on what PAL allows.

## Loci
Section 14, "Peirce's Reduction Thesis" (the SEP has no page numbers; cite the archive edition, e.g. Fall 2026):
- §14, sentence 1 — "Peirce's so-called “Reduction Thesis” is the thesis that all relations, relations of arbitrary adicity, may be constructed from triadic relations alone, whereas monadic and dyadic relations alone are not sufficient to allow the construction of even a single “non-degenerate” (that is: non-Cartesian-factorable) triadic relation." — Burch's statement of both clauses; "non-degenerate" is glossed as non-Cartesian-factorable.
- §14 — "As it turns out, both Peirce and Quine were correct: the issue entirely depends on exactly what constructive resources are to be allowed to be used in building relations out of other relations." — the sentence the talk wants. The sentence before it says the thesis "was for over a century doubted by many, especially after the publication of a proof by Willard Van Orman Quine that all relations could be constructed exclusively from dyadic ones"; the entry gives no year or title for Quine's proof.
- §14 — "An exact exposition and proof of Peirce's Reduction Thesis was finally accomplished in 1988 (Burch 1991), and it makes clear that Peirce's constructive resources are to be understood to include only negation, a generalization of de Morgan's relative product operation, and the use of a particular triadic relation that Peirce called “the teridentity relation”" — the proof dated 1988, published 1991; the three resources.
- §14 — Peirce derived dyadic identity "from two instances of the triadic identity relation by two applications of the relative product operation of de Morgan", and held the relative product more fundamental "than, say, the Boolean product or the Boolean sum."

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- The talk brief's paraphrase ("both Peirce and Quine were correct: the issue entirely depends on exactly what constructive resources are to be allowed") → verified verbatim, §14. It is Burch's wording in the SEP, current revision 11 Feb 2021; the same wording already stood in the 2014 revision.
- koshkin2022reduction p. 4 cites this sentence as "Burch (2021)" → verified. The date matches the 2021 substantive revision.
- burch-1991-proved-prt (TR-S4-005, -097, -132, -141, -199) → this entry is Burch's own claim that the proof was "accomplished in 1988 (Burch 1991)". It says nothing about the juxtaposition restriction, so it cannot settle the register's restriction rows; see burch1991reduction.
- prt-citation (TR-S1-018, -025, -032) → for a one-line citable statement of the named thesis by its PAL author, this entry serves. Pair it with the peirce cluster's cards for Peirce's own texts.
- quine-did-not-refute-peirce (TR-S3-098, card quine1954reduction) → this entry supports a "both right, relative to resources" reading, not "Quine did not refute Peirce". The talk should quote Burch here and Koshkin's rejoinder together rather than pick one.
