## What the talk may use it for
The chapter has not been read, so its content here is as reported by koshkin2022reduction and conarroe2020relations, plus two sentences matched in the scan. It opens by conceding that "the reduction of polyads to dyads is commonplace in present logical theory" and then argues that this "has no real force". Its reason is that Beta graphs match first-order logic only in theorems — "theorem isomorphism is a weak condition". As reported by Koshkin (p. 8), the chapter explains why Peirce treated juxtaposition (Cartesian product) as "more of a listing device" and privileged the relative product. As reported by Conarroe (pp. 101–102, note 90), it says the relative product "was the ‘privileged mode’ of combination" (Brunning p. 255). If the chapter bears this out, it gives the talk a historical reason why Burch could restrict juxtaposition, and why teridentity, as identification of variables, is where the triad hides.
