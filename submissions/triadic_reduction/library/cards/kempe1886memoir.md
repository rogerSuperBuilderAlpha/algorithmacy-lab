## What the talk may use it for
Kempe's memoir is the first serious challenge to Peirce's negative clause. §68 shows that any diagram with lines of several sorts can be redrawn with plain links alone by adding auxiliary units: each coloured line becomes a coloured unit joined by two links. §330 draws an "isolated triad" (three units, each unique with respect to the other two) as three units linked to one auxiliary unit λ. That is a triad drawn in two-ended links, and it is also, in plain sight, a three-way junction. Peirce read §330 as virtually showing that his dyadic algebra could express "A gives B to C" (CP 3.424). His reply put the mediation in the attachment of lines to spots, and in the abstraction that mints the auxiliary unit (peirce1892critic).

## Loci
- §68, pp. 12-13 — "It can readily be shown that every form which admits of representation by means of graphical units and lines of different sorts, can, by the introduction of additional or auxiliary graphical units, be represented by means of graphical units and links only." — the general reduction to links. Matched against the page images.
- §68, p. 13 — "In the place of any red line substitute a red unit (i.e., one coloured red, instead of having a letter inside), this unit being connected by two links to the two units which were joined by the red line (fig. 20)." — the construction.
- §328, p. 57 — "A collection of units which is such that each unit of the collection is unique with respect to the residue of the collection may be said to be isolated." — definition behind "isolated triad".
- §330, p. 57 — "We may graphically represent an isolated triad as in fig. 61, where λ is an auxiliary graphical unit; a non-isolated triad being represented as in fig. 62, without links or auxiliary units. The same mode of representation may also be applied in the case of isolated n-ads where n ≠ 3." — the triad drawn by one auxiliary unit with three links (fig. 61). ## Notes
- **The exchange, settled.** (1) Kempe 1886 (§68, §330): lines of any sort reduce to links plus auxiliary units, and an isolated triad is drawn as a λ-unit with three links. (2) Peirce 1892, "The Critic of Arguments" II (Open Court 6: 3417-3418; CP 3.423-424): Kempe's analysis is "a formidable objection". Kempe's diagrams have three kinds of element, and the attachment of lines to spots is mediation. §330 "virtually shows" that the dyadic algebra expresses giving by adding "this action" as an object. The diagram does not represent how that abstraction is derived, so Peirce modifies his position "but not to surrender it." (3) Peirce 1897, *Monist* (p. 168; p. 178 n. 1): Kempe treats a relationship as "a complex of bare connexions of pairs of objects". In Kempe's notation bonds between pairs of spots are "insufficient", because there the spots stand for objects and the bonds for relations. (4) The 1904 Welby letter (CP 8.331) does not address Kempe.
- Peirce's recurring title "Theory of Mathematical Forms" (CP 3.423) is a slip for the published "Theory of Mathematical Form".
- Kempe added "Note to a Memoir on the Theory of Mathematical Form" (Proc. R. Soc. Lond. 42 (1887): 193-196, doi 10.1098/rspl.1887.0040). The Crossref record was confirmed and the text was not read.
- The memoir's title-page footnote says the title was changed and some final sections were cut at the referees' request.
