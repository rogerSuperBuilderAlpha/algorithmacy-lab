## What the talk may use it for
This is the set card. The CP paragraphs the talk quotes are listed here with their source manuscripts and dates, as the CP editors give them. Works the talk cites as works have their own cards: peirce1870description (CP 3.144), peirce1890guess (CP 1.354-416, including 1.363), peirce1892critic (CP 3.415-424), peirce1897logic (CP 3.456-552) and peirce1904letter (CP 8.327-341). The logical argument runs through four loci: CP 1.345-347 (1903), CP 1.363 (1887-88 in W6, c. 1890 in CP), CP 1.473 (c. 1896) and CP 2.274 (c. 1902). CP 1.346-347, from the 1903 Lowell lectures, puts both clauses in graph form. No joining of one- and two-tailed spots yields a three-tailed graph, a three-way node of identity lines is itself a triadic idea, and three-tailed graphs build every higher number of tails.

## Loci
- CP 1.345 (Lowell Lectures 1903, III; vol. 1 p. 176, page image) — "you will convince yourself thoroughly that every genuine triadic relation involves thought or meaning. Take, for example, the relation of giving. A gives B to C. This does not consist in A's throwing B away and its accidentally hitting C" — giving; thought. The print italicizes "meaning", "giving" and "A gives", and the sentence continues "like the date-stone, which hit the Jinnee in the eye."
- CP 1.345 (vol. 1 p. 176, page image) — "If that were all, it would not be a genuine triadic relation, but merely one dyadic relation followed by another. There need be no motion of the thing given. Giving is a transfer of the right of property." — the degenerate imitation.
- CP 1.346 (vol. 1 p. 176, page image) — "In existential graphs, a spot with one tail —X represents a quality, a spot with two tails—R—a dyadic relation. Joining the ends of two tails is also a dyadic relation. But you can never by such joining make a graph with three tails." — the negative clause in graph form (the print has a footnote mark after "relation", pointing to vol. 4, bk. II).
- CP 1.346 (vol. 1 p. 176, page image) — "You may think that a node connecting three lines of identity Y is not a triadic idea. But analysis will show that it is so." — the branching node is a triad (teridentity).
- CP 1.347 — "while a graph with three tails cannot be made out of graphs each with two or one tail, yet combinations of graphs of three tails each will suffice to build graphs with every higher number of tails." — both clauses.
- CP 1.347 — "And analysis will show that every relation which is tetradic, pentadic, or of any greater number of correlates is nothing but a compound of triadic relations." — the positive clause.
- CP 1.473 ("The Logic of Mathematics; An Attempt to Develop My Categories from Within", c. 1896) — "Every triad is either monadically degenerate, dyadically degenerate, or genuine. A monadically degenerate triad is one which results from the essence of three monads, its subjects. A dyadically degenerate triad is one which results from dyads. A genuine triad is one which cannot be resolved in any such way." — the grades.
- CP 1.473 — "That A is father of B and B father of C is a genuinely dyadic degenerate triad." … "A is mother of B and B is wife of C. Here the two component dyads are more independent of one another. This is a purer case of the dyadic degenerate triad." — the exemplars.
- CP 1.474 — "A triad may be explicated into a triadic tetrad. Thus, A gives B to C becomes A makes the covenant D with C and the covenant D gives B to C." — a second minted-term example, alongside CP 1.363's sale.
- CP 2.274 ("Syllabus", c. 1902; vol. 2 p. 156, page image) — "A Sign, or Representamen, is a First which stands in such a genuine triadic relation to a Second, called its Object, as to be capable of determining a Third, called its Interpretant, to assume the same triadic relation to its Object in which it stands itself to the same Object." — the sign.
- CP 2.274 (vol. 2 p. 156, page image) — "The triadic relation is genuine, that is its three members are bound together by it in a way that does not consist in any complexus of dyadic relations." — genuineness defined.
- CP 2.228 (vol. 2 p. 135, page image) — "A sign, or representamen, is something which stands to somebody for something in some respect or capacity. It addresses somebody, that is, creates in the mind of that person an equivalent sign, or perhaps a more developed sign. That sign which it creates I call the interpretant of the first sign." — the interpretant as a further sign.
- CP 5.484 ("A Survey of Pragmaticism", c. 1907) — "by 'semiosis' I mean, on the contrary, an action, or influence, which is, or involves, a coöperation of three subjects, such as a sign, its object, and its interpretant, this tri-relative influence not being in any way resolvable into actions between pairs." — semiosis is irreducibly triadic. Vol. 5 p. 332, page image; the print italicizes "semiosis" and "three" ("a coöperation of *three* subjects").
- CP 5.484 (vol. 5 p. 332, page image) — "All dynamical action, or action of brute force, physical or psychical, either takes place between two subjects … or at any rate is a resultant of such actions between pairs." — the dyadic contrast case.
- CP 1.25 — "Firstness is the mode of being which consists in its subject's being positively such as it is regardless of aught else. That can only be a possibility." — Firstness.
- CP 1.303 — "we must think of a metaphysical monad as a pure nature, or quality, in itself without parts or features, and without embodiment. Such is a pure monad." — monad and quality.
- CP 1.324 — "You have a sense of resistance and at the same time a sense of effort. There can be no resistance without effort; there can be no effort without resistance." — Secondness as reaction.
- CP 1.325 — "The idea of second is predominant in the ideas of causation and of statical force. For cause and effect are two; and statical forces always occur between pairs." — Secondness and pairs.
- CP 1.337 — "By the third, I mean the medium or connecting bond between the absolute first and last." — Thirdness as mediation.
- CP 1.339 — "The easiest of those which are of philosophical interest is the idea of a sign, or representation." — Thirdness and representation.
- CP 1.26 — "To say that a prediction has a decided tendency to be fulfilled, is to say that the future events are in a measure really governed by a law." — Thirdness and law.
- CP 3.144, 3.423-424, 8.330-332 — see peirce1870description, peirce1892critic, peirce1904letter.

**Slide-safe (matched on the printed page):** CP 1.345 giving vs throwing (vol. 1 p. 176), CP 1.346 the three-tail sentence (p. 176), CP 1.363 (pp. 187-188; see peirce1890guess for the sale example), CP 2.274 (vol. 2 p. 156) and CP 5.484 (vol. 5 p. 332). CP 1.473-474, 1.25, 1.303, 1.324-325, 1.337-339 and 1.26 are matched in scan OCR and transcription but were not checked on a page image. Recheck them before they go on a slide.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- firstness-monad-entropy (TR-S1-011, -037, -042, -072, -079): Firstness as quality, immediacy and possibility is Peirce's (CP 1.25, 1.303, 1.357). "Shannon entropy", "marginal entropy H(X)", "baseline noise" and "uncoupled uncertainty" are the sources' analogy with no Peircean locus → corrected. "Ground" (from the 1867 "New List") was not checked.
- secondness-dyad (TR-S1-012, -038, -073, -080): Secondness as reaction, resistance and pairs is Peirce's (CP 1.324-325, 1.358; CP 8.330 ties it to "any dyadic relation not involving any third"). "Formalize pairwise coupling", the alignment with equality and composition operations, and "Shannon mutual information" are the sources' framing → corrected.
- thirdness-gloss-mediation (TR-S1-045, -061, -064): mediation and representation are Peirce's glosses (CP 1.337, 1.339, 1.356, 3.422), and law is his (CP 1.26). "Lawful integration" is not his phrase and must not be equated with IIT integration or Φ → corrected.
- semiosis-irreducibly-triadic (TR-S1-062): verified. CP 5.484 says semiosis is a "coöperation of three subjects … not being in any way resolvable into actions between pairs". CP 2.274 says the sign's three members are bound "in a way that does not consist in any complexus of dyadic relations." "Meaning generation" is the source's paraphrase. Peirce's nearest wording is CP 1.345, where every genuine triadic relation "involves thought or meaning."
- peirce-reading-representamen-interpretants (TR-S6-016): "reading treats the document as an externalized representamen through which the reader generates a succession of interpretants" → corrected. The Peircean part holds. The interpretant is itself a sign, "an equivalent sign, or perhaps a more developed sign" (CP 2.228, vol. 2 p. 135), and the requirement runs to "the Third's Thirds and so on endlessly" (CP 2.274, vol. 2 p. 156). Reading, documents and "externalized" are the source's application, and no Peirce locus names them. EP2:478 (1908) was not checked.
- sign-object-interpretant-categories (TR-S1-063): verified in outline. CP 2.274 calls the Sign "a First", the Object "a Second" and the Interpretant "a Third". The ordinals are places in the triadic relation. Saying the representamen "maps to Firstness" as a category goes beyond the text.
- thirdness-is-teridentity (TR-S1-081): corrected. Genuine Thirdness is the sign–object–interpretant relation (CP 8.332, "In its genuine form"), and Thirdness has degenerate grades (CP 8.331). See peirce1904letter.
- prt-origin-date (TR-S1-067, -077): corrected. The first statement is CP 3.144 (1870). See peirce1870description.

## Notes
**Audit of the lab's Peirce quotations** (`org_frontier/thinkers/peirce/exegesis.md` and `paper.md`):
- Matched verbatim: CP 2.274 (both sentences), CP 1.363 ("…when we attempt to imitate…two blank places in the combination"), CP 8.331 (the branching aphorism, the giving passage with marked ellipses, "a degenerate form of Thirdness in which the thirdness is externally appended"), CP 1.473 (grades and exemplars; the ellipsis is marked), CP 1.345 (giving; the ellipsis drops the date-stone simile), CP 5.484 (semiosis), and CP 1.345 "every genuine triadic relation involves thought or meaning".
- **Paraphrase inside quotation marks:** (1) `paper.md` l. 105-106 gives "A makes with C a certain transaction E" and "E is a sale of B for the price D" as CP 1.363. These strings are Koshkin's paraphrase (arXiv 2406.14058, p. 3). Peirce's words are on peirce1890guess, which also records that the critical edition (W6) reads the two letters the other way round ("A makes with B … a sale of C"). (2) `paper.md` l. 281 reads: "The claim Peirce most often states, 'A is determined by B and C together,'". Those words are not Peirce's, and the quotation marks are wrong. No string of the form "determined by … together" occurs in either CP transcription (a whole-text search), in the CP 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 scan OCR, or in the 1870, 1892 and 1897 scans. An Open Library full-text search of every scanned book on archive.org returns 0 hits for "determined by B and C together". "A is determined by B and C" returns 2 hits, Weyl on functions and a chemistry text, and none is Peirce. The same wording sits in the lab's code docstring (`thinkers/peirce/forms.py` l. 107: Peirce's "A determined by B and C"). The nearest Peirce comes is "welded into one fact" (CP 1.363), "bound together by it" (CP 2.274) and "a coöperation of three subjects" (CP 5.484). The talk should drop the quotation marks and the "most often states", or replace the line with one of those three. (3) `paper.md` l. 103 and 114, `exegesis.md` l. 69, `methods.md` l. 54 and `literature/deep_research_report.md` l. 41 say the dynamical object "determines and is not determined", and l. 114 and the report put the phrase in quotation marks. That string does not occur in CP. In the archive.org full-text index it returns 10 hits, none of them Peirce (the 1884 *Objectivity of Truth*, Price 1769, Otis on Virgil and others). Treat it as the lab's gloss and remove the quotation marks. The EP2 and Welby texts were searched only through that index.
- **Koshkin's statement, not Peirce's:** `exegesis.md` C5 quotes the two clauses "some triadic relations cannot be reduced to combinations of monadic and dyadic ones" / "all tetradic and higher adicity relations can be reduced…". These match Koshkin (arXiv 2406.14058, p. 1) verbatim, and `exegesis.md` labels them as his. That is correct as labelled.
- **Dates.** CP 2.274: CP editors "c. 1902" (from the "Syllabus"). `exegesis.md` gives 1903 with EP2:272-273, and that EP2 locus was not checked (peirce1998essential). CP 1.363: CP "c. 1890", lab "c. 1887-88"; W6 dates it 1887-88 (peirce1890guess), so the lab is right. CP 1.473: CP "c. 1896", which matches the lab. CP 5.484: the CP 8 bibliography dates the group c. 1907. It notes an earlier c. 1906 dating, and the lab gives 1907.
- The scans of vols. 1-6 are the Belknap offset reprint of the first edition, so paragraph numbers, pages and text are those of 1931-35. CP 5.484 is now matched on the printed page (vol. 5 p. 332). CP 8.331 is matched in scan OCR of the printed CP 8 and in two independent editions of the letter, but not on a page image (peirce1904letter).
- **EP2 cross-references (not page-checked).** The archive.org full-text index shows that the CP 2.274 sentence is in EP2 (item essentialpeirces0000peir). A secondary work cites it as "Peirce 1998, pp. 272-273", which agrees with `exegesis.md`, but the EP2 page was not seen.
