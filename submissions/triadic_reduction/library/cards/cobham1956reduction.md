## What the talk may use it for
Cobham strengthens Quine 1954. Under somewhat stronger assumptions on the fragment of set theory, a symmetric
dyadic predicate G is definable from Quine's F, and F is definable back from G. Any interpreted first-order
theory therefore translates into one whose only predicate is symmetric and dyadic. Cobham's opening paragraph
restates Quine's construction independently: a dyadic F, and a universe that contains Θ's universe and is
closed under x, y ↦ {x, y}.

## Loci
- p. 56, opening (publisher transcription only) — "In establishing this result Quine takes for the universe of Θ′ a set which comprehends the universe of Θ and has the property that if x and y are members then so is {x, y}. For this fragmentary set theory the distinctness of x from {x, y} and {{x}} is assumed." — an independent statement of Quine's domain enlargement and assumptions.
- p. 56 (publisher transcription only) — "It follows from this extended result that the theory Θ is translatable into a theory in which the only primitive predicate is symmetric and dyadic." — the result.
