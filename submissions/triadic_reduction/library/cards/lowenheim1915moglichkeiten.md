## What the talk may use it for
Löwenheim proved the reduction to binary relatives 39 years before Quine, as §4, Satz 6. For every relative
equation f = 0 there is a binary equation F = 0 that is identically satisfied, not identically satisfied, or
never satisfied exactly when f = 0 is. The result is an equivalence of equations, not a translation of
interpreted theories. The construction enlarges the domain. Löwenheim takes a new domain whose elements are the
ordered pairs of old elements, with (i, i) identified with i. He maps each quaternary relative a onto a binary
relative A over that pair-domain, and treats ternaries as a special case of quaternaries. He then adds two
binary relatives, V and H, that pick out a pair's first and second members. On a finite domain of n individuals
the new domain has n² elements, so the reduction needs no infinite universe. Löwenheim does not mention Peirce
here. He works in Schröder's notation and cites Schröder.

## Loci
- p. 463, §4 heading — "Zurückführung des höheren Relativkalküls auf binären." — the section's title and subject.
- p. 463, Satz 6 — "Jede Relativgleichung bzw. Zählgleichung ist einer binären äquivalent, d. h. ist eine beliebige Relativgleichung bzw. Zählgleichung f = 0 gegeben, so kann man eine binäre Relativgleichung bzw. Zählgleichung F = 0 angeben, welche dann und nur dann identisch bzw. nicht identisch bzw. niemals (für kein Wertsystem der Parameter) erfüllt ist, wenn das entsprechende von f = 0 gilt" — the theorem is an equivalence of satisfaction status.
- p. 463 — "Ebenso sind auch alle Probleme, die sich mit Hilfe des ternären und höheren Relativkalküls lösen lassen, auch schon im binären zu erledigen." — Löwenheim's own reading: whatever the ternary and higher calculus can settle, the binary calculus can settle.
- p. 463 — "Es sei nun zunächst eine quaternäre Relativgleichung vorgelegt; die ternäre läßt sich als ein Sonderfall hiervon auffassen." — ternaries are handled as a special case of quaternaries.
- p. 464 — "Wir betrachten einen neuen Denkbereich, dessen Elemente die Elementenpaare des alten 1¹ sind" — the domain enlargement: pairs of old elements.
- p. 464 — "Der neue Denkbereich 𝔈, den wir im folgenden zugrunde legen wollen, entsteht nun aus 1², indem man (i, i) durch i, (j, j) durch j, usw. ersetzt." — diagonal pairs are identified with the old individuals, so 𝔈 has the size of 1². The letter is a Fraktur capital in the scan.
- p. 464 — "Jedem quaternären Relativ a, dessen Indizes 1¹ zu durchlaufen haben, ordnen wir nun ein binäres Relativ A zu, dessen Indizes 𝔈 durchlaufen sollen" — the coding of a quaternary as a binary over the pair-domain.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- dyads-need-infinite-embedding (TR-S3-040): the only escape is an infinite set-theoretic embedding → refuted. Löwenheim's pair-domain over a domain of n individuals has n² elements, with no set theory involved.
- quine-1954-aimed-at-peirce (TR-S3-001 note): Löwenheim gave the reduction earlier → verified. §4, Satz 6, pp. 463ff.
- prt-negative-clause (TR-S1-048, TR-S1-071): the reduction needs "domain expansion" → verified for Löwenheim, whose construction does change the domain. "Artificial" is Gemini's word.

- The same paper holds the Löwenheim–Skolem result (Satz 2, p. 450: "Jede Fluchtzählgleichung ist bereits in einem abzählbaren Denkbereich …", from OCR only).
- p. 465 (OCR only, not quoted): the defining properties of V and H must also be written into the equation. The binary equation therefore carries extra structural conditions that hold the pair-domain in place.
- Koshkin 2024 (arXiv 2406.14094 v1, p. 14) suggests that Löwenheim was the first to prove reduction to binaries under projections of joins: "Löwenheim might have been the first to state and prove this result in a more formal manner in 1915 [27]. His proof used somewhat more cumbersome iterated pairing construction instead of hypostatic abstraction." On p. 21 he adds that the result "once made it controversial" (Peirce's thesis, that is) because two notions of reducibility were confused.
- Quine 1954 does not cite Löwenheim, but the paper it does cite in n. 1 descends from him. Kalmár's paper opens: "In Verschärfung eines Löwenheimschen Satzes 1) hat Herbrand 2) bewiesen" and points n. 1 to this paper's § 4 (kalmar1936zurueckfuehrung).
