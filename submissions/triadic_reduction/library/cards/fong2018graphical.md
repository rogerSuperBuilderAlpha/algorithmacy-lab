## What the talk may use it for
Fong and Spivak give a graphical calculus for regular logic. Regular logic is the fragment of first-order logic built from =, true, ∧ and ∃, which is the primitive-positive fragment (the paper's keyword) that koshkin2025completeness works in. Formulas are drawn as wiring diagrams: shells with ports, and wires joined at black dots. The paper's aim is categorical. It proves that regular categories are essentially reflective in "regular calculi", which are ajax 2-functors from a free bicategory of relations to posets. Its point for the talk is the hypergraph framing: a regular category's relations form a hypergraph category, and the Frobenius maps of that structure are the diagonal x ⊆ x × x × x.

**Role of the branching node.** A black dot where n wires meet identifies the variables on those wires. Dots of any arity are allowed (Remark 5.6), and composition amalgamates connected dots into one (Example 5.7). An n-way dot therefore equals any connected tree of three-way dots, though the paper does not spell this out. The monoid and comonoid maps of the hypergraph structure are the ternary diagonal subobject (Prop. 3.15), which is the teridentity. The paper treats it as structure every object carries, the "adjoint monoid" the authors introduce. It does not treat it as a special relation.

**Reduction thesis.** Peirce is not mentioned, in either version. The paper does not name teridentity, existential graphs or any reduction question. For the talk it counts as independent evidence that category theorists reached Peirce's branching identity without his vocabulary. It is not a participant in the debate.

- p. 27 (Remark 5.6) — "When multiple wires meet at a point, our convention will be to draw a dot iff the number of wires is different from two."
- p. 27 (Example 5.7) — composition proceeds by "amalgamating any connected black dots into a single black dot".
- p. 34 (Prop. 6.13) — "The monoidal category underlying IntRelP is a hypergraph category."
