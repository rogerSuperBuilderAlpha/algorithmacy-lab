## What the talk may use it for
This is the primary locus for the valency arithmetic. In §5 Peirce states that joining a μ-ad to a ν-ad by λ bonds gives a (μ+ν−2λ)-ad. With one bond (λ = 1), two relations joined at one blank leave m+n−2 blanks. From the formula he draws both halves of the thesis. Even-adic relatives can yield only even-adic ones, so dyads never yield a triad. Any odd-adic relative except a monad can, by repetition, produce a relative of any adicity, and "out of triads all polyads can be constructed." The formula holds only on one condition, that each bond joins exactly two blanks. Peirce earns that condition earlier in the article (pp. 170-171) by arguing that a node where three or more loose ends meet is itself a relative, and a four-way node a tetradic one. Section 4 also records his view of Kempe (p. 168), and a footnote on p. 178 says bonds between pairs of spots are "insufficient" in Kempe's method.

**Slide-safe (page images):** the §5 heading and Fig. 17 sentence, the μ+ν−2λ passage (p. 183) and the four-way-node passage (pp. 170-171). Quote the formula as printed, "(μ + ν − 2λ)-ad", with "adinity" and "perissid" glossed on the slide.

## Loci
- p. 183 (§5, headed "Triads the primitive relatives.") — "That out of triads all polyads can be constructed is made plain by Fig. 17." — positive clause, graphical form. Page image.
- p. 183 — "Fig. 18 shows that from two triads a dyad can be made. Fig. 19 shows that from one triad a monad can be made. Fig. 20 shows that from any even number of triads a medad can be made." — triads also yield the lower adicities. Page image.
- p. 183 (§5) — "In general, the union of a μ-ad and a ν-ad gives a (μ+ν−2λ)-ad, where λ is the number of bonds of union. This formula shows that artiads, or even-ads, can produce only artiads. But any perissid, or odd-ad (except a monad), can by repetition produce a relative of any adinity." — the valency arithmetic; with λ = 1 it is m+n−2. Matched against the page image.
- pp. 170-171 (§4) — "a little exercise of generalising power will show that such a four-way node is really a tetradic relative" … "Thus, it becomes plain that every node of bonds is equivalent to a relative; and the doctrine of valency is established for us in logic." — why a branching junction counts as a relation and not as free wiring. Page image of p. 171 (the Fig. 3 node joins "kills", "to gratify" and "John it is that").
- p. 168 (§4) — "Mr. Kempe seems to consider a relationship to be nothing but a complex of bare connexions of pairs of objects, the opinion refuted in the last section." — Peirce's charge against Kempe (see kempe1886memoir).
- p. 178 n. 1 — "in Mr. Kempe's method, the spots represent the objects, whether individuals or abstract ideas, while their bonds represent the relations. Hence, my own exclusive employment of bonds between pairs of spots does not, in the least, conflict with my argument that in Mr. Kempe's method such bonds are insufficient." — the two graph notations are not the same notation.
- p. 165 (§3) — "Is relation anything more than a connexion between two things? For example, can we not state that A gives B to C without using any other relational phrase than that one thing is connected with another?" … "I shall answer it in the negative." — the argument against reducing relation to bare pairwise connexion.
- p. 184 — "We naturally choose three as the smallest number which will answer the purpose." — why the primitive is the triad and not some larger odd-ad.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- prt-origin-date (rows TR-S1-067, TR-S1-077): "originates in Peirce's 1897 logic of relatives" / "stated … in 1897" → corrected. The 1897 article restates the thesis and gives it the μ+ν−2λ formula. Both clauses appeared first in 1870 (peirce1870description, p. 374).

## Notes
- The formula appears in print in this article. The same arithmetic in words is c. 1890 ("A Guess at the Riddle", CP 1.363: two triads joined by one X have four blanks, two dyads joined by one X have two). The 1903 Lowell version (CP 1.346-347) states it in graph terms. Those loci are on peirce1890guess and peirce1931papers.
- "Perissid" and "artiad" are Peirce's words for odd-adic and even-adic relatives, and "adinity" is his word for adicity.
- The *Monist* issue is headed "Vol. VII. January, 1897. No. 2."
- The talk does not need the CP 3.456-552 paragraph numbers, which were not matched paragraph by paragraph. Cite the article by its *Monist* page.
- Peirce's example "A sells C to B for the price D" is not in this article. It is in CP 1.363.
