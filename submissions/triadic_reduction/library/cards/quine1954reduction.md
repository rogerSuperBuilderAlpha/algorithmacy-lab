## What the talk may use it for
Quine proves a translatability theorem. Take any interpreted theory Θ written in first-order quantification
theory. It translates into a theory Θ′ whose only predicate letter is one dyadic letter, 'F'. Two things make the
proof work. Quine assumes "a fragment of set theory" that guarantees the unordered pair {x, y} exists and that
x differs from {x, y} and {{x}}. He also enlarges the universe: Θ′ ranges over Θ's objects together with {x, y}
for every x and y in Θ′'s own universe, and so is closed under pairing. Tuples are right-nested Kuratowski pairs.
An atomic sentence F_n w1…w_dn is coded by one object, nw1;w1;w1;w2;…;w_dn, which carries the predicate's index
n as nw1 (Quine's notation for w1;w1;…;w1 with n terms), and F holds reflexively of that code. Peirce's name
does not appear on p. 180 or in either footnote, which are the only parts read; pp. 181–182 remain unread.
Footnote 1 sets the result beside Kalmár's (kalmar1936zurueckfuehrung) and the Church–Craig–Quine result
(craig1952reduction), as "reminiscent of, but distinct from" both. It does not set the result against the
reduction thesis. Koshkin 2022 (n. 1) states that Quine never related the 1954 reduction to Peirce, then or
later. Other writers did that, Christopherson and Johnstone (1981) among them (christopherson1981triadicity).

## Loci
- p. 180, opening (publisher transcription) — "Consider any interpreted theory Θ, formulated in the notation of quantification theory (or lower predicate calculus) with interpreted predicate letters. It will be proved that Θ is translatable into a theory, likewise formulated in the notation of quantification theory, in which there is only one predicate letter, and it a dyadic one." — the theorem and its scope.
- p. 180 (publisher transcription) — "Let us assume a fragment of set theory, adequate to assure the existence, for all x and y without regard to logical type, of the set {x, y) whose members are x and y, and to assure the distinctness of x from {x, y} and {{x}}." — the set theory assumed is a fragment, not an axiomatic system. The unbalanced "{x, y)" is in the transcription.
- p. 180 (publisher transcription) — "Let us construe the ordered pair x; y in Kuratowski's fashion, viz. as {{x}, {x, y}}, and then construe x;y;z as x;(y;z), and x;y;z;w as x;(y;z;w), and so on." — Kuratowski pairs, not Wiener pairs. Tuples nest to the right.
- p. 180 (publisher transcription) — "Now let Θ′ be a theory whose notation consists of that of quantification theory with just the single dyadic predicate ‘F’, interpreted thus:" — the predicate is 'F'. The paper has no 'J'.
- p. 180, formula (1) (publisher's scanned image, transcribed here) — "Fxy =: (∃z)(x = {y, z}) ∨ . x = y . (∃n)(∃w1)…(∃w_dn)(F_n w1…w_dn . x = nw1;w1;w1;w2;w3;…;w_dn)." — F is not set membership. Its first disjunct says x is a pair-set with y as a member. Its second makes F reflexive on the code of each true atomic sentence of Θ.
- p. 180 (publisher transcription) — "The universe of Θ′ is to comprise all objects of the universe of Θ and, in addition, {x, y) for every x and y in the universe of Θ′. (Of course the universe of Θ may happen already to comprise all this.)" — the domain enlargement, and Quine's note that no enlargement happens when Θ's universe already contains the pairs.
- p. 180 (publisher transcription) — "Now I shall show how the familiar notations ‘x = y’, ‘x = {y, z}’, etc., and ultimately the desired ‘F1w1…wd1’, ‘F2w1…wd2’, etc. themselves can all be defined within Θ′." — pairing and identity are defined inside Θ′ from F. The paper posits no triadic 'Pair' primitive and moves nothing into meta-syntax. The two quoted formulas are inline images in the extract; this card transcribes them.
- n. 1 (publisher transcription) — "The other is that of Church, Craig, and Quine, which assures reducibility not merely to a dyadic predicate but to a symmetric one; it differs from the present result in supposing the universe to consist of natural numbers." — the paper's own frame of reference, Kalmár (1936) and Church, Craig and Quine (1952), with no Peirce.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- quine-1954-citation (TR-S3-006, -025, -035, -036, -043, -048, -055, -069, -094, -095): JSL 19(3): 180–182 → verified. Crossref and Cambridge both give issue 3, September 1954, DOI 10.2307/2268616, byline "W. V. Quine". TR-S3-036 is a bare volume link and not a citation. "Universal syntactic translation" is Gemini's phrase.
- quine-1954-aimed-at-peirce (TR-S3-001, -028, -047, -054, -091, -096): a counterproof, counter-thesis, "debate" with Peirce, or a paper that "claimed to disprove Peirce" → refuted. Peirce is not named on p. 180 or in either footnote. Koshkin 2022 (TCSPS 58(4); arXiv 2406.14058 v1, n. 1, p. 21) says Quine "made no mention of Peirce, PRT or what his 1954 reduction to dyads meant for it then, or later", and that "His silence is usually interpreted as a courteous submission that PRT is false, but he never did say so himself." The anti-Peirce reading belongs to the reception. Koshkin's p. 1 counts Löwenheim 1915 and Quine 1954 among the "counterclaims" to the thesis "to the effect that all polyads are reducible to dyads", and his n. 1 then says Quine never connected the result to Peirce. Burch's SEP entry (2021, §14) says the thesis "was for over a century doubted by many, especially after the publication of a proof by Willard Van Orman Quine". Koshkin's one named example of someone who "pressed" the construction "as an objection to PRT" is Christopherson and Johnstone 1981 (christopherson1981triadicity, metadata only).
- quine-did-not-refute-peirce (TR-S3-098) → corrected. Two grounds hold. Quine never claimed a refutation (Koshkin 2022, n. 1). And Burch (SEP 2021, §14, read directly): "As it turns out, both Peirce and Quine were correct: the issue entirely depends on exactly what constructive resources are to be allowed to be used in building relations out of other relations." Koshkin 2022 (p. 4) adds that for Peirce to be right "Quine has to be wrong in a deeper conceptual sense, despite being correct in a technical algebraic sense." Do not rest the claim on a "Quinean Fallacy".
- quine-1954-single-dyadic-predicate (TR-S3-002, -007, -015, -042, -049, -070, -074, -079, -084, -097) → corrected. The scope is "any interpreted theory Θ, formulated in the notation of quantification theory". The one predicate is 'F', not 'J'. It is not membership. The proof assumes a fragment of set theory and a universe closed under pairing. TR-S3-074 is closest to Quine's wording.
- quine-tuples-encode-polyadic-predicates (TR-S3-008, -076, -077) → verified. Nesting is to the right, x;(y;z). The code also prefixes nw1;w1 to carry the predicate's index.
- quine-kuratowski-pair (TR-S3-009, -022, -031, -051, -099, -100, -108) → corrected. Quine names Kuratowski's pair {{x}, {x, y}}. "Wiener-Kuratowski" is wrong. The read text contains no claim "to avoid ternary primitives".
- quine-wiener-pair (TR-S3-010) → refuted.
- quine-predicate-reification (TR-S3-012) → corrected. The paper assigns no entity to a predicate. The index n of F_n enters each tuple's code as nw1, Quine's name for the tuple w1;…;w1 of n terms ("Let us refer to w, w;w, w;w;w, etc. as 1w, 2w, 3w, etc.").
- quine-translation-schema (TR-S3-013, -026, -107) → refuted. Neither J(<x1..xn>, c_R) nor J(<x1,x2>, y) appears. By (1), F_n w1…w_dn holds when F relates the code nw1;w1;w1;w2;…;w_dn to itself. The first disjunct cannot hold when x = y, because Quine assumes x ≠ {x, z}.
- quine-J-is-membership (TR-S3-014, -078) → corrected. Only F's first disjunct is membership-like: x = {y, z}. The second is a reflexive marker on codes.
- quine-pairing-relation-pair (TR-S3-020, -056, -065) → corrected. No Pair(x1, x2, p) predicate exists. 'x = {y, z}' is defined within Θ′ from F.
- quine-reduction-by-domain-inflation (TR-S3-003, -011, -017, -018, -019, -027, -029, -034, -050, -059, -071, -101, -102) → corrected. The universe of Θ′ is Θ's universe closed under unordered pairing. The text says nothing of an "inductive set universe", ∅, or the word "infinite". On a well-founded reading, closure under pairing is infinite even when Θ's universe is finite. Quine adds that Θ's universe may already contain all this. "Solely/only" overstates, and the "meta-theoretic ontology" of TR-S3-029 is wrong because the pairs sit in Θ′'s object-level universe. TR-S3-018 and TR-S3-102 are correct: the translation does not stay within Θ's universe.
- quine-reduction-presupposes-set-theory (TR-S3-075) → verified, with a qualification: Quine assumes a fragment of set theory, and says so.
- teridentity-smuggled-in-quine-pairing (TR-S3-004, -005, -033, -057, -072, -081, -104, -105) and quine-smuggles-teridentity (TR-S5-385) → corrected, attributed to Koshkin 2022, not to Quine's text or to Burch. Koshkin argues that "the pairing construction, as formalized in set theory, relies on the very FOL devices that are explicated as using triads" (p. 2). His formula (5), p. 7, writes the pairing step as a triple junction ∃t[R(1)(t, x1) ∧ R(2)(t, x2) ∧ R̂(t, x3)] that "conceals triadicity". PAL simulates a triple junction with the teridentity I3 (p. 5, Fig. 2a), so in his explication the pairing reduction uses a teridentity. Four parts of the rows are wrong. The device is an existentially quantified pair-element t joined by three dyads, not a triadic Pair(x, y, p) primitive, which Quine does not have. The mechanism Koshkin names is "syntactic absorption" of variable identification in the object language, not "meta-syntax" or "meta-linguistic coordinate binding". The argument is an explication in EG/FOL, not a "universal-algebraic" or clone-theoretic proof, and "join-irreducible" is not Koshkin's term (TR-S3-081, -104). And Koshkin speaks of "the set-theoretic pairing construction" and "pairing reductions a la Löwenheim and Quine" (p. 4), not of Quine's definiens (1) line by line.
- triadic-binding-shifted-to-metasyntax (TR-S3-023, -024, -045, -052, -058, -086, -093, -103) → corrected. The charge that the pairing reduction hides triadic binding has a published source: Koshkin 2022, pp. 2 and 7 (above). Three parts need correction. Nothing moves into meta-syntax: Quine defines pairs and identity inside Θ′, and Koshkin locates the hidden triad in object-level FOL devices (variable identification, branch points). Quine assumes "a fragment of set theory", not "the full apparatus of axiomatic set theory" (TR-S3-023). And the theorem does not "fail" (TR-S3-093): Koshkin calls Quine "correct in a technical algebraic sense" and wrong only "in a deeper conceptual sense" (p. 4). "Expanding ontology into sets" (TR-S3-024) is right, p. 180.
- quine-use-mention-fallacy (TR-S3-106, TR-S5-114) → corrected. A published source makes a version of the charge. Koshkin 2022 (p. 2) writes that "proposed reductions to dyads, when taken in anything other than a narrow technical sense, emerge as instances of the use/mention error pointed out by Peirce". He means the pairing reductions of Löwenheim and Quine (p. 4). His word is "error", not "fallacy", and he limits it to readings beyond the narrow technical sense. TR-S5-114's clause "that destroys intrinsic cause-effect power" has no source; see the dyadic-decomposition-phi-zero group.
- quine-falls-into-kempe-trap (TR-S1-008, -015) → refuted as an attribution to Peirce, who died in 1914. Peirce's objection targets Kempe (CP 3.424, as quoted by Koshkin 2022, p. 3). Koshkin 2022 (p. 4) says that objection "is often pressed against pairing reductions a la Löwenheim and Quine as well", and he himself applies Peirce's use/mention diagnosis (CP 8.331) to reductions to dyads (p. 2). The diagnosis of Quine is Koshkin's, not Peirce's.
- peirce-quine-conflict-branching-node (TR-S3-085) → corrected. Quine 1954 says nothing of branching. Koshkin 2022 does set Peirce against Quine and ties the question to the triple junction (pp. 4, 7), so the framing has a published source; "historical conflict" still overstates, since Quine never engaged the thesis (n. 1).
- quine-higher-arity-no-logical-status (TR-S3-016) → unverifiable. It is not in p. 180 or the notes. Pages 181–182 unread.
- quine-nominalist-program-vs-peirce (TR-S3-030) → refuted for this paper. The construction quantifies over sets {x, y}.

## Notes
- Reprinted in Quine, *Selected Logic Papers* (Random House 1966; enlarged ed. Harvard 1995), per the wvquine.org bibliography (fetched 2026-09-24; its entry reads "Journal of Symbolic Logic (September 1954), 19(3): 180-182 [reprinted in W. V. Quine's Selected Logic Papers]"). That reprint was not seen, and page numbers are not given. The archive.org items that offer the book as open downloads are user uploads of an in-copyright book and were not used.
- Pages 181–182 remain unread. They carry the definitions of identity, of pairs and of the F_n inside Θ′, and footnote 2 ("The preceding definition of ‘x = y’ applies only to simple variables…"). A full read of p. 181 would let the talk quote the defining clause for F_n, and would settle whether Peirce's name appears anywhere in the paper. Koshkin 2022 (n. 1) says it does not.
- Löwenheim 1915 (lowenheim1915moglichkeiten) had already reduced ternary and higher relatives to binary ones through a domain of element pairs. Quine does not cite him in the notes read. The paper Quine's n. 1 does cite, Kalmár's, builds on him: Kalmár's paper opens by sharpening "eines Löwenheimschen Satzes" and footnotes Löwenheim 1915, "insb. § 4" (kalmar1936zurueckfuehrung, p. 137, scan).
- Koshkin 2022 (n. 1): "Not in 1981, when he simplified the reduction procedure in the 4th edition of Mathematical Logic". Neither the book nor the edition label was checked. Koshkin's bibliography does not list it.
- Koshkin 2022 (n. 1) also names two Quine texts on Peirce: the 1935 review, the only place Quine "discussed the reducibility to triads clause" (quine1935review), and the 1995 essay "Peirce's Logic" (quine1995logic), where Quine again said nothing of the 1954 reduction. Both are metadata only.
- Earlier versions of this card and of the register attributed n. 1, the Burch SEP quotation and the 1981 *Mathematical Logic* simplification to "Koshkin (2024)". All three are in Koshkin 2022, TCSPS 58(4), arXiv 2406.14058 (n. 1 on p. 21 of arXiv v1; Burch on p. 4). The 2024 IGPL paper (arXiv 2406.14094) does not contain them.
- The lab's gloss (org_frontier/thinkers/peirce/paper.md:39), "set-theoretic pairing reduces any relation to dyads", is right about the device. It omits that the universe changes. It also misses that Quine's own result is a translation of theories, not a decomposition of relations inside a fixed domain.
