## What the talk may use it for
The standard English edition of the triad chapter, translated from the 1908 German. Chapter 4, "The Triad" (145-169), has four sections: §1 the sociological significance of the third element (145), §2 the non-partisan and the mediator (145-153), §3 the *tertius gaudens* (154-162), §4 *divide et impera* (162-169). The broken-line passage sits in the previous chapter, "The Isolated Individual and the Dyad", at p. 135, not in "The Triad". Cite Wolff when quoting modern English; cite the 1902 AJS text (simmel1902number1, simmel1902number2) when quoting Simmel's first English.

**Slide-safe (all loci matched on the page image):** p. 135 broken line, p. 145 "configurations of two", p. 157 leverage and "paralyze one another", p. 162 the definition of *divide et impera*, and p. 163 the legal prohibitions.

## Loci
- Contents, p. xiii — "IV. The Triad 145 / 1. The Sociological Significance of the Third Element 145 / 2. The Non-Partisan and the Mediator 145 / 3. The Tertius Gaudens 154 / 4. Divide et Impera 162" (OCR; section starts confirmed by running heads and the p. 162 image).
- p. 135 (image) — "The fact that two elements are each connected not only by a straight line—the shortest—but also by a broken line, as it were, is an enrichment from a formal-sociological standpoint." — broken line, in "The Triad vs. the Dyad".
- p. 145 (image) — "In the cases under examination now, the third element is at such a distance from the other two that there exist no properly sociological interactions which concern all three elements alike. Rather, there are configurations of two." — Wolff's rendering of "Zweierkonfigurationen" (1908: 103).
- p. 145 (image) — "The group-forming, mediating function of a third element will be discussed in a later context." — the postponement.
- p. 157 (image) — "the quantity of his power is determined exclusively by the strength which each of them has relative to the other." — *tertius gaudens* leverage.
- p. 157 (image) — "What alone is important is that the forces of two antagonistic elements paralyze one another and thus actually give unlimited power to the intrinsically extremely weak position of a third element not yet engaged in the issue."
- p. 162 (image) — "The distinguishing nuance consists in the fact that the third element intentionally produces the conflict in order to gain a dominating position." — opening of §4, *Divide et Impera*.
- p. 163 (image) — "Here, above all, belong the legal prohibitions against political organizations" — the first step of the *divide et impera* scale.
- Index — "Divide et impera, 162-169"; "Triad, 135-136, 145-169" (OCR).

## Notes
- The slacker_thirds card gives the non-partisan and mediator as "145-47"; that is where the roles are defined (the non-partisan who "produces the concord of two colliding parties" is at 146-47). The §2 section runs 145-153.
- Wolff also uses "coalition" (pp. 156, 165), as does Small in 1902; see simmel1902number2.
- "Dyad" and "triad" are Wolff's words, not Simmel's. His translator's notes (OCR, p. 123 and p. 135) say so: "Never Simmel's term, but shorter and more convenient than his, which here, for instance, is 'Zweierverbindung' ('union of two')", and for triad, "Again not Simmel's term, but again more convenient than 'Verbindung zu dreien' (association of three)". Small's 1902 text says "combination of two" and "combination of three". Small's 1902 translation, however, already says "dyad" and "triad": "the dyad-group" (I: 44) and "the triad scheme" (II: 182), both on the page images, and "dyad and triad configurations" (II: 189) in the text layer (simmel1902number1, simmel1902number2). The accurate statement is that Simmel's German has no such nouns ("Zweierverbindung", "Verbindung zu dreien"), and that both English translators supplied them. A slide that uses "dyad" should quote a translation and name the translator.
