## What the talk may use it for
The talk can cite this article, with Koshkin 2022 as the warrant, as an early published case of someone else
turning Quine's pairing construction against Peirce's reduction thesis. Quine did not do this himself. The
card cannot say what the article argues, because nobody on the project has read it. Koshkin gives it as an
example ("e.g."), not as the first instance. The title hints at a distinction between formal triadicity and
the category of Thirdness, but only a reading of pp. 241–246 can show whether the authors concede the logic
to Quine and defend Thirdness on other ground.

## Notes
- Who first pressed the pairing construction against the thesis, and when, is not settled by any source read. What the sources support:
  (1) Koshkin 2022 (p. 1) counts Löwenheim 1915 and Quine 1954 among "counterclaims" to the thesis, meaning their results contradict it. Neither author directed his result at Peirce: Löwenheim cites Schröder (lowenheim1915moglichkeiten), and Quine never mentions Peirce (Koshkin n. 1).
  (2) Skidmore 1971 (skidmore1971triads), per Koshkin (p. 2), reviewed "Kempe style reduction to dyads" and raised the gerrymandering worry. Koshkin does not say Skidmore discussed Quine's pairing construction.
  (3) Christopherson and Johnstone 1981 are Koshkin's named example of pressing "the pairing construction" as "an objection to PRT".
  (4) Koshkin (p. 4) reports that Peirce's anti-Kempe objection "is often pressed against pairing reductions a la Löwenheim and Quine as well", without names.
  (5) Burch (SEP 2021, §14) says the thesis was "doubted by many, especially after the publication of a proof by Willard Van Orman Quine", without names or dates.
  On this evidence the talk can say "by 1981 at the latest", citing Christopherson and Johnstone via Koshkin. It cannot say "first".
- The first author's given name appears as "Rosemarie" only in AI-generated web-search summaries. Those are not evidence, so the card keeps Koshkin's initial "R.".
