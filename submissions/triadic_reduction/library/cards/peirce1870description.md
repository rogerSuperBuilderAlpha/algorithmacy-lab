## What the talk may use it for
This memoir holds the first statement of both clauses of the reduction thesis, in one sentence and without proof. In Peirce's 1870 vocabulary an "absolute term" is a monadic predicate, a "simple relative" a dyadic one, and a "conjugative" a relative with two or more correlates, so a "conjugative of two correlates" is a triadic relative. The sentence says monads do not build dyads, dyads do not build triads, and every relative of more than three places reduces to triads. Peirce gives a worked example only for the positive clause ("winner over of —, from —, to —"), and he says he has "studied this part of my notation but little." The origin date for the talk is therefore 1870, not 1897.

**Slide-safe:** the two-clause sentence and the "studied this part of my notation but little" sentence, cited as "Peirce 1870, p. 374 (CP 3.144)". Both were read on the page image of the 1870 printing.

## Loci
- p. 374 (CP 3.144) — "A relative term cannot possibly be reduced to any combination of absolute terms, nor can a conjugative term be reduced to any combination of simple relatives; but a conjugative having more than two correlates can always be reduced to a combination of conjugatives of two correlates." — both clauses, 1870. Matched against the page image.
- p. 374 (CP 3.144) — "The treatment of conjugative terms presents considerable difficulty, and would no doubt be greatly facilitated by algebraic devices. I have, however, studied this part of my notation but little." — the thesis is asserted here, not argued.
- p. 375 — "It is evident that in this way all conjugatives may be expressed as production of conjugatives of two correlates." — the positive clause generalized. It opens p. 375, not p. 374; CP 3.144 runs across both pages. Page image.
- p. 317 — "Communicated January 26, 1870." — the date that makes this the origin.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- prt-origin-date (rows TR-S1-067, TR-S1-077): "the irreducibility thesis originates in Peirce's 1897 logic of relatives" / "Peirce stated his Reduction Thesis in 1897" → corrected. Both clauses first appear in this memoir, p. 374, communicated 26 January 1870 (CP 3.144). Peirce gives the valency argument c. 1890 ("A Guess at the Riddle", CP 1.363, card peirce1890guess), answers Kempe in 1892 (CP 3.423-424, card peirce1892critic), and restates the thesis with the bonding formula in the 1897 *Monist* article (card peirce1897logic). 1897 is a later statement, not the first.

## Notes
- Date: the memoir was communicated on 26 January 1870 (p. 317), and Crossref dates the printed volume 1873. Cite it as 1870 with the volume imprint noted. It is sometimes also said to have circulated as an 1870 offprint, which I did not check.
