## What the talk may use it for
CP 8.331 holds three things the talk wants. The first is the one-line argument that Thirdness cannot be built from Secondness: "no branching of a line can result from putting one line on the end of another." The second is the giving example. Genuine giving makes C "the possessor according to Law", while laying B down for C to pick up is "a degenerate form of Thirdness in which the thirdness is externally appended." The third is Peirce's criticism of his own algebra of dyadic relatives, the one Schröder took up. Peirce says the algebra employs the very triadic relations it does not recognize, because "every combination of relatives to make a new relative is a triadic relation irreducible to dyadic relations." That self-criticism answers every later dyadic reduction, Quine's included, but Peirce aimed it at his own dyadic algebra, not at Kempe and not at Quine. CP 8.330 and 8.332 give his own definitions of Secondness and of genuine Thirdness.

**Slide-safe:** the branching aphorism, the two giving sentences and the self-criticism. The CP 8 scan agrees with Hardwick and Wiener, both edited from the letter itself, with two exceptions (Notes). Cite "CP 8.331 (letter to Lady Welby, 12 Oct. 1904)". Do not add a CP 8 page, which is unchecked. The Hardwick page is also unchecked, except that the letter begins on p. 22.

## Loci
- CP 8.331 — "Thus only can the indispensableness and irreducibility of thirdness be made out, although for him who has the mind to grasp it, it is sufficient to say that no branching of a line can result from putting one line on the end of another." — the valency aphorism. CP 8 scan ("indispensable-ness" hyphenated across a line), Hardwick, Wiener.
- CP 8.331 — "It does not consist [in] A's putting B away from him and C's subsequently taking B up. It is not necessary that any material transfer should take place. It consists in A's making C the possessor according to Law." — genuine giving. CP 8 scan and Hardwick. "[in]" is a CP bracket, and Hardwick prints plain "in".
- CP 8.331 — "But now suppose that giving did consist merely in A's laying down the B which C subsequently picks up. That would be a degenerate form of Thirdness in which the thirdness is externally appended." — the degenerate imitation. CP 8 scan, Hardwick, Wiener.
- CP 8.331 — "The criticism which I make on [my] algebra of dyadic relations, with which I am by no means in love, though I think it is a pretty thing, is that the very triadic relations which it does not recognize, it does itself employ." — D1's quotation, in its true wording.
- CP 8.331 — "For every combination of relatives to make a new relative is a triadic relation irreducible to dyadic relations." — the reason, in the next sentence. CP 8 scan, Hardwick, Wiener. Hardwick and Wiener print "that algebra", and "[my]" is the CP editors' substitution (Notes).
- CP 8.331 — "Even in the most degenerate form of Thirdness, and thirdness has two grades of degeneracy, something may be detected which is not mere secondness." — degenerate Thirdness. CP 8 scan, Hardwick, Wiener.
- CP 8.330 — "I consider the idea of any dyadic relation not involving any third as an idea of Secondness" — Peirce's own dyad-Secondness link. Hardwick and Wiener; the CP 8 scan matched only in part (OCR).
- CP 8.332 — "In its genuine form, Thirdness is the triadic relation existing between a sign, its object, and the interpreting thought, itself a sign, considered as constituting the mode of being of a sign." — genuine Thirdness is the sign relation. CP 8 scan, Hardwick, Wiener.

## What the preliminary sources claim about it
- quote-cp8331-triadic-relations-it-employs (row TR-S1-009): D1 quotes "the very triadic relations which it does not recognize it itself employs" (CP 8.331) → corrected. The locus is right. CP and Wiener read "the very triadic relations which it does not recognize, it does itself employ", and Hardwick prints the same words without the comma. No printing has "it itself employs". The subject is Peirce's own algebra of dyadic relations, the one Schröder took up. The reason follows at once: "every combination of relatives to make a new relative is a triadic relation irreducible to dyadic relations." The register's note that the context is Kempe is also wrong, since Kempe is not mentioned anywhere in CP 8.327-341.
- secondness-dyad (rows TR-S1-012, -038, -073, -080): the dyad-Secondness link is Peirce's (CP 8.330). The pairing with mutual information is not → corrected.
- thirdness-is-teridentity (row TR-S1-081): "Thirdness is representation, mediation and law, and it is the Sign-Object-Interpretant relation" → corrected. Peirce says this of Thirdness "in its genuine form" (CP 8.332), and he adds that thirdness "has two grades of degeneracy" (CP 8.331), so not every Thirdness is the sign relation. Representation and mediation are on peirce1931papers (CP 1.337-339), and law is on CP 1.26 and 8.331. The group label's "teridentity" is not in the row, and it is not Peirce's gloss of Thirdness here.

## Notes
- **"[my]" is an editorial substitution.** Hardwick 1977 and Wiener 1958 both read "The criticism which I make on that algebra of dyadic relations". CP 8.331 cuts the preceding sentences, which Hardwick prints: they begin "As to my algebra of dyadic relations, Russell in his book …". CP marks that cut with ". . ." and replaces "that" with "[my]". A slide should either keep CP's brackets or quote "that algebra" from Hardwick. It should never print "my algebra" without brackets.
- **Date and source.** The CP 8 scan's editorial note reads: From a letter dated "1904 Oct 12" to "My dear Lady Welby." Hardwick's letter list gives "October 12, 1904" at p. 22, and the next letter (2 November 1904) at p. 36. Wiener heads it "P. O. Milford, Pa. 1904, Oct. 12". A secondary source (*Semiotic Perspectives*, 1993) cites the aphorism as "Hardwick, 28-29", and another (*Semiotic Themes*, 1981) cites Lieb 1953, pp. 12-13, for this letter. Neither page was checked.
- **What "verified" rests on.** Full-text-search snippets are scan OCR of the printed pages, and they are independent of the Past Masters transcription. They are short windows, though, so each quotation was matched in overlapping 5-7-word phrases, not read whole on a page image. Treat the wording as verified and the CP 8 page as unknown. The archive.org lending copies block page images and search-inside for anonymous use, and I did not borrow them.
- The CP text marks an editorial ellipsis inside 8.331 ("you introduce a mental element. . . ."). The quotations above stop before it or start after it.
- **Lab papers.** `exegesis.md` C1 and C3, and `paper.md` l. 77 and 91-93, quote CP 8.331 correctly, with the ellipses marked. `paper.md` l. 273 ties the 8.331 aphorism to the Kempe exchange, but the letter does not mention Kempe (see peirce1892critic).
