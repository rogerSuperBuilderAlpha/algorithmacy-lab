# Kellogg, K. C., Valentine, M. A., & Christin, A. (2020). Algorithms at work: The new contested terrain of control. *Academy of Management Annals*, 14(1), 366–410.

**Identifier:** 10.5465/annals.2018.0174 · **Read depth:** full_text (local PDF and text in `dissertation/research/library/pdfs/kellogg2020.*`, author-deposited copy of the published article; the claims below were re-read against that text on this date) · **Source-tier:** primary_journal · **Evidence basis:** direct_read · **Relation last checked:** 2026-09-15

## What it argues

Employers now control work through algorithms, and that control differs in kind from the technical and bureaucratic regimes Edwards (1979) described. The review synthesizes organization studies, sociology, communication, and platform-labor research through Edwards's "contested terrain" lens and proposes six mechanisms, the "6 Rs": employers use algorithms "to direct workers by restricting and recommending, evaluate workers by recording and rating, and discipline workers by replacing and rewarding" (p. 372). Four affordances make algorithmic control distinct — it is more *comprehensive*, *instantaneous*, *interactive*, and *opaque* than earlier technologies — and the paper decomposes opacity, following Burrell (2016), into "three main reasons: intentional secrecy, required technical literacy, and machine-learning opacity" (p. 372). Scheduling appears under restricting: platforms schedule work "in finely grained, opaque, and unpredictable ways," and food-delivery platforms "restricted information about available shifts." The second half catalogs worker responses under a term the authors coin, *algoactivism* (pp. 390–391): individual practical action (noncooperation, leveraging algorithms, personal negotiation with clients), platform organizing, discursive framing around fairness, accountability and transparency, and legal mobilization. The authors treat algorithmic systems as "contested instruments of control" whose outcomes vary across organizations and individuals.

## Relation to the argument

The management-theory frame for the gig-work case and the source that lets the paper state, in a vocabulary the venue recognizes, what the design affordances would intervene in. The 6 Rs locate each affordance: **exposing optimization targets** acts on *rating* and *rewarding*, where the criteria are withheld; **branching/counterfactual exploration** acts on *restricting* and *recommending*, where the worker sees one option and not the set it was chosen from. The three-part decomposition of opacity is the paper's most useful analytic tool. It separates what an affordance can fix — required technical literacy, which is what scaffolding addresses — from what it cannot: intentional secrecy is a policy choice, and machine-learning opacity may defeat any interface. **Intentional frictional transparency** is therefore scoped by this source to the middle term. Algoactivism supplies the demand side: workers already build algorithmacy collectively, through forums and decoding solidarity, so the affordances formalize an existing practice rather than inventing one. The "interactive" affordance — control that adjusts to recorded behavior in real time — is also the reason a static explanation (`radercottercho2018`) will not do for workers; the target moves as they act.

## Caution

An integrative review, so its evidence is secondhand and its 6 Rs are the authors' synthesis of a young literature, not a validated taxonomy. The "contested terrain" frame is normatively loaded toward control and resistance; use the mechanism content without importing the stance. The review covers control of workers an employer directs, including employees under algorithmic supervision, so not every finding transfers to independent-contractor gig work. Page references follow the published pagination as marked in the local text (opacity and the 6 Rs on p. 372; algoactivism from p. 390). The earlier dissertation card on this source reads it for a different question (co-optation as a coordination form); the relation section above is written fresh for the scaffolding paper.
