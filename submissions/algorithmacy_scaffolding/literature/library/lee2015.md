# Lee, M. K., Kusbit, D., Metsky, E., & Dabbish, L. (2015). Working with machines: The impact of algorithmic and data-driven management on human workers. *Proceedings of the 33rd Annual ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (CHI '15), 1603–1612.

## What it argues

The paper names *algorithmic management* — "software algorithms that assume managerial functions and surrounding institutional devices that support algorithms in practice" — and studies it on Uber and Lyft through 21 driver interviews, 12 passenger interviews, and archival analysis of 128 forum posts and 132 company communications. Three algorithmic features map onto Mintzberg's managerial roles: *assignment* (proximity-based dispatch), *informational support* (the in-app surge display), and *evaluation* (passenger ratings plus acceptance rate). Each breaks in a characteristic way. Assignment is opaque — "they say 'oh we route it to the closest driver' or whatever but who really knows what's going on behind the scene" (P4) — and the paper's heading for the consequence is "More knowledge more advantage": drivers who understood the rule built workarounds (toggling the app at traffic lights to shrink the pickup radius), while those who did not rejected rides and lowered their own ratings. The authors read the opacity as strategic: companies "may also desire a degree of user ignorance to prevent the system from being gamed." Surge information was too volatile to act on; more than half of drivers ignored it. Evaluation was context-blind, penalizing all rejections equally. A driver who also drove a taxi supplied the counterfactual: taxi dispatch let him see every incoming request and choose, and "Uber and Lyft's automated assignment got rid of this fine level of control and predictability." With no manager to ask, drivers turned to forums for "fragmented social sensemaking," which worked for rating tips but "fell short on subject matters where only an authority figure had the right information." The design discussion proposes "structured online social sensemaking of algorithmic features where individuals can build on each other's knowledge."

## Relation to the argument

The gig-work case's best single source for the novice–expert gradient, because it documents the gradient inside one workforce and prices it. "More knowledge more advantage" is the paper's thesis in the field: algorithmacy is unequally distributed among drivers and the difference shows up in earnings and ratings. Two affordances get their clearest grounding here. **Branching/counterfactual exploration** has a native instance in the taxi-dispatch comparison — a worker who could see all requests and choose strategically, set against one who sees a single ping with a countdown; the affordance would restore the request set as a visible branch. **Visual chunking** has its failure case in the surge display: a real-time heat map that flickered "by the second" carried information drivers could not chunk into decisions, so they ignored it — a design that exposes the signal without chunking it at a usable grain. The strategic-ignorance finding sets the boundary for **exposing optimization targets**: the paper predicts platform resistance, and any proposal must say what can be exposed without enabling gaming. The "structured social sensemaking" recommendation is the closest thing in the literature to a scaffolding design brief for workers, and it is stated by HCI authors in a design register the scaffolding section can adopt directly.

## Caution

Twenty-one drivers across thirteen US cities with average tenure under five months, in 2014; a small early-adopter sample the authors flag, with no access to company representatives or the algorithms themselves. The Mintzberg mapping is the authors' interpretive frame. Interview quotations are illustrative, not frequencies. The authors note that drivers "desired little (or did not feel entitled to) direct control over the assignment algorithm," which complicates a design argument that assumes demand for branching affordances; the paper should engage this rather than cite past it. The earlier dissertation card on this source reads it for coordination theory; the relation above is fresh.
