# Ehsan, U., Liao, Q.V., Passi, S., Riedl, M.O., & Daumé III, H. (2024). Seamful XAI: Operationalizing Seamful Design in Explainable AI. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 8(CSCW1).

## What it argues

Seamless AI hides the mismatches between design assumptions and deployment reality, and hiding them disempowers users when the system fails; seamful design, imported from ubiquitous computing (Chalmers; Inman and Ribes), proposes revealing those seams strategically so that users can act on, contest, or appropriate the system. The authors define a seam in AI as a mismatch between the assumptions built into a system and the reality of its deployment context (their running example: a lending model updated under a three-loan rule that now lags a five-loan regulation), and they offer a three-step design process — anticipate breakdowns, craft the seams that explain them and locate them in the AI lifecycle, then filter and design with the seams on a "Reflection Card" justified by actionability, contestability, or appropriation. Forty-three participants (AI practitioners, XAI researchers, and end-users including loan officers) ran the process in a scenario-based co-design session of about 65 minutes; they generated 85 seams, every participant produced at least one, and 35 of the 47 seams they chose to reveal sat in deployment stages. Participants reported that seams gave "peripheral vision" outside the black box, answered the "why not" question that feature-level explanations cannot, and made trust "no longer an all-or-nothing play" — the AI "neither idiotic nor God-like."

## Relation to the argument

This is the peer-reviewed home of the seams vocabulary the essay uses for affordance 4 ("frictional transparency — seams that make users interrogate assumptions"), and it gives that affordance a definition, a design procedure, and a rationale that is about *what* to reveal (infrastructural mismatches) rather than merely *that* something is revealed. It also extends affordance 1: the paper argues that the most useful transparency is often not the model's internals or its confidence but the boundary conditions under which its assumptions hold — which reframes "expose the optimization target" as "expose where the target was set and where it no longer applies." Zamfirescu-Pereira et al. (2023) reach for the same seamful/seamless contrast in their design implications, so the two papers together give the essay a line from prompt tooling to seams that does not depend on the essay's own coinage.

## Caution

The authors call their findings "formative rather than evaluative": there is no control condition, no behavioural outcome, no measure of whether seams change reliance or accuracy, and the setting is a lending decision-support scenario, not a generative-AI interface. The 43 participants are mostly practitioners already sympathetic to responsible-AI work; only six loan officers, consulted separately, represent the end-user side. Seams here are authored by designers in advance, not surfaced by the system at interaction time, so the paper does not show that interface-level friction produces interrogation. Use it for the concept and the procedure; do not use it as evidence that seamful interfaces work.
