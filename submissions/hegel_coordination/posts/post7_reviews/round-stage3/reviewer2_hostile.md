## FINDINGS, RANKED MOST-DAMAGING FIRST

### 1. Self-contradiction: "Hegel means integrating work" vs. "Hegel's necessity is ethical, not causal" (MOST DAMAGING)

This is the essay contradicting its own hedge, and it sits on the exact guard the series polices (homology, never identity of referents).

Section "Hegel Already Drew the Line the Fork Needs," topic sentence:
> "The strongest evidence that Hegel means integrating work, and not a mere constraint, is that he draws the distinction himself, inside the same passage, and comes down on the side this post has been reading for."

And the section's closing line:
> "Read onto the catalog's fork, the closure is the contingent constraint … and the standing is the necessary integration the reformers stripped out with it"

Both say Hegel's good *is* causal integration ("means integrating work"; "the standing is the necessary integration"). But "The Bridge, and Where It Thins" spends its whole length denying exactly this:
> "It cannot say the good is recognition rather than a coordination benefit, an information channel, or a risk pool. Hegel can, and does: the good is honor, ethical membership… It cannot say the standing is a coordination effect. Hegel's necessity is ethical; the catalog's is causal."

You cannot have both. If the standing *is* the necessary integration, the bridge does not thin and the downgrade section is theater. If Hegel's good is recognitive and causally invisible (your actual, correct position), then Hegel does not "mean integrating work" — he means a bond that cannot be removed, which is the *boundary*, not the causal *content*. The digest is explicit: "homology of boundary, not identity of criterion." The topic sentence trades homology for identity.

**Exact rewrite (topic sentence), author voice:**
> "The strongest evidence that Hegel means a bond that cannot be removed, and not a mere privilege that could, is that he draws the distinction himself, inside the same passage, and comes down on the side this post has been reading for."

**Exact rewrite (closing line):**
> "Read onto the catalog's fork, the closure is the contingent constraint — the part that could go, the car dealer's franchise-law position — and the standing is what lands in the necessary cell the reformers stripped out with it, though what the standing *is* stays Hegel's to say and the instrument's to miss."

That keeps Hegel on the necessary side of the boundary without claiming he means the causal quantity the essay later, rightly, says the instrument alone can name.

### 2. Overclaim on Herzog: "the fork … sits already latent in the literature"

Section "What Hegel Says the Corporation Does," last paragraph:
> "A recent reading converging on coordination without borrowing a word of the irreducibility vocabulary is evidence the fork this post runs sits already latent in the literature, ahead of the instrument that computes it."

Herzog earns one prong, not the fork. The digest: Herzog reads the corporation "as the social spaces where economic ethos is formed and the labour market is regulated" — a *coordination-function* reading that "blunts the objection that the coordination reading is the lab's import." Coordination-as-function is one leg. The *fork* is the two-way sort — necessary integration vs. contingent constraint, decided by the bypass operation — and nothing in Herzog runs that operation or draws that contrast. Saying the fork "sits already latent in the literature, ahead of the instrument that computes it" both overreads Herzog and quietly implies the instrument computes the fork on the corporation, which the honest-scope section spends a page denying.

**Exact rewrite:**
> "A recent reading converging on coordination without borrowing a word of the irreducibility vocabulary is why the coordination half of this reading is not the lab's import: a Hegel scholar working the primary text reached it first, and the instrument only sorts what the text already puts in play."
