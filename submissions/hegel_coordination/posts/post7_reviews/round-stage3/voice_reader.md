## Section-by-section critique

### Opening (unheaded)

### "What Hegel Says the Corporation Does"
This section runs the antithesis machine at its highest local density in the piece — every one of its four main paragraphs closes on a "not X, it is Y" move:

- "The cabinetmaker's problem is not that he might be poor. It is that his work reaches the universal only as an accident..."
- "But they are not why Hegel calls the corporation a second ethical root."
- "It is recognition... not a thing you find by cutting a causal graph."
- "Take the corporation out and the result is not a cleaner relation between the individual and the state. It is a heap of individuals and a state with nothing to grip..."

Four instances of the same shape in four consecutive paragraphs is the "run past once a paragraph" problem stated almost literally. Two are load-bearing (the recognition/causal-graph line is the section's actual thesis and should stay as is). The other two are candidates for flattening:

> Flag: "The cabinetmaker's problem is not that he might be poor. It is that his work reaches the universal only as an accident, and the whole apparatus of mutual service leaves him, as a person, alone."
> Rewrite: "The cabinetmaker's real problem is not poverty. His work reaches the universal only as an accident, and the whole apparatus of mutual service leaves him, as a person, alone."

> Flag: "Take the corporation out and the result is not a cleaner relation between the individual and the state. It is a heap of individuals and a state with nothing to grip — the atomism the *Philosophy of Right* is built to overcome."
> Rewrite: "Take the corporation out and what is left is not a cleaner relation between individual and state but a heap of individuals facing a state with nothing to grip — the atomism the *Philosophy of Right* is built to overcome."

Both rewrites keep the contrast (which is genuinely the content) and drop the hard two-sentence reset, so the device isn't spent twice before the section is even done.

Minor: "Klikauer... rather than a mere business form" and two sentences later "Ross... instead of leaving it a historical curiosity" repeat the identical rather-than shape back to back across two different scholars. Not damaging on its own, but worth varying one — e.g., "Ross (2008) had already carried the doctrine into business ethics, applying §§250–256 to present-day institutions" (cut "instead of leaving it a historical curiosity" — the sentence doesn't need the tail to make its point).

### "Necessary for Whom"
The clearest single violation in the piece. One paragraph makes the identical "scope this claim narrower" move five separate times:

> "'Necessary middle' cannot mean necessary for every producer civil society puts to work; it means necessary for the tradesman a corporation actually admits... The cabinetmaker who opened this post is not the rabble — he has a trade, a shop, and customers... Scope the claim to him and it survives; state it as a solution to the standing problem of civil society in general and it overreaches... The correction costs the argument nothing it needed: the fork this post runs... was always a fork about the corporation's own members, not about everyone civil society touches. But it does mean the opening paragraph's cabinetmaker stands for a narrower class than 'everyone in civil society'..."

Every sentence in this paragraph restates the same correction through a fresh antithesis. The content — Ruda's rabble forcing a scope correction — is genuinely the essay's best structural move (it also sets up the platform-driver parallel three sections later), which makes the over-explaining a real shame. Rewrite:

> "That forces a correction. Hegel's own page has a population the corporation never reaches — the rabble is bound into civil society and admitted by no trade — so 'necessary middle' has to mean necessary for the tradesman a corporation actually takes in, not for everyone civil society puts to work. The cabinetmaker who opened this post has a trade, a shop, and customers; he is not the rabble, and the corporation was built for exactly his case. The fork this post runs — necessary integrator or contingent gatekeeper — was always a fork about the corporation's own members. That costs the argument nothing it needed, but it does mean the opening paragraph's cabinetmaker stands for a narrower class than 'everyone in civil society,' and the platform section below inherits the remainder."

Five antithesis moves down to two, same claim, no lost content.

## Findings, ranked most damaging first

1. **The hard two-sentence "not X. It is Y." stop recurs 5–6 times across the piece**, most damagingly diluting its own best instance at the close of the platform section. Fix by flattening two of the earlier uses (opening section, flagged above); leave the ending alone.
2. **"Necessary for Whom" makes the same scope-correction move five times in one paragraph.** The content is the essay's best architectural move (Ruda's rabble → scope correction → platform precedent); the prose currently buries it in repetition. Exact rewrite given above.
3. **Self-narrating rigor cluster**, concentrated at two points: "here is where the honesty has to start rather than end" (Fork section) and "I want to stop at that sentence rather than walk past it" (platform-reflection paragraph). Both announce a move the essay is about to make instead of just making it. Exact rewrites given above.
4. **"The paper's most quotable line lives on the other side of the stop"** — a step outside the essay to admire its own craft mid-argument. Cut.
5. **Section-closing epigram drumbeat**: 6 of 10 sections end on a short, often-antithetical epigram ("...ahead of the instrument that computes it," "...run through a machine instead of a market," "...not in what the nineteenth century did to it," "...on the platform the estrangement is the point," "Hegel's necessity is ethical; the catalog's is causal"). Individually most of these are earned; in aggregate the rhythm becomes predictable. No blanket fix — "Three Operations, Kept Apart" and "Hegel Already Drew the Line" already vary it well and can serve as the model for one or two more sections to flatten.
6. **Minor: matched rather-than pairs land close together** (Klikauer/Ross in the opening section; the "agree/disagree" construction twice in "The Experiment His Century Ran"). Low stakes, easy one-line fixes given above.

## Biggest genuine strength

The Ruda/platform architecture. The prose execution around it needs the trims above, but the structural idea is the strongest thing in the draft and should not be touched.
