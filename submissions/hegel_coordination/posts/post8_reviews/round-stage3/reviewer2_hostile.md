**Flag (the anti-Taylor verdict inverts on the receipts).** Here the misclassification does its worst damage:
> "his remedy is unavailable by the causal structure of the case, not because anyone chose to withhold it… a bypass that does not exist." (lines 258–260)
> "his mediator is necessary, so the exit all three are arguing about is, for him, not available." (line 275–276)

If the gig platform is a *contingent* capture (monopoly, margin 2.0) — which is what the catalog says — then the bypass *does* exist in principle, and it was foreclosed by market power, i.e., "someone chose to withhold it" (antitrust, competition, a rival platform). That is *exactly* the case where Taylor's exit-republicanism is right: restore the bypass, break the capture. The essay's confident "Taylor's wall is a bypass that does not exist" holds only for the **intrinsic** cell (the integrator no restored edge removes), and the essay has not shown the gig platform is that cell — its own catalog says the opposite. So the strongest anti-Taylor sentence in the post is the one the receipts least support. See Finding 1 for the fix.

**Flag (internal inconsistency: does the test read the objective half, or something adjacent?).** Within a few paragraphs the essay says three non-equivalent things:
- "He has Hegel's objective condition and lacks the subjective one" (line 293) — the test reads the objective condition.
- "The bypass test reads something *adjacent to* the first condition and is silent on the second" (line 324) — it reads something *near* it, not it.
- "the causal criterion operationalizes at most the objective half of Hegel's test" (line 328) — it reads the objective half.

"Adjacent to" and "operationalizes the objective half" are not the same claim, and the honest one is "adjacent." Neuhouser's first condition is that the institution *furnishes the objective preconditions of members' freedom* — a positive enabling fact. The bypass test reads that a member is *trapped* (exit closed), which is not a precondition furnished; it is nearer the opposite. The essay's own better line — "the closing of the exit, which is freedom's precondition and not freedom" (line 320) — uses "precondition" in the sense of *when the question becomes live*, not Neuhouser's sense of *what freedom objectively requires*. Pick one. **Fix (author voice):** "The bypass test does not read either of Neuhouser's conditions. It reads a fact prior to both — that the exit is shut — and shutting the exit is what makes his two-part question the one that now has to be asked. That is less than operationalizing half the test. It is finding the doorway to the test."

"No other market" is a monopoly premise the bypass test does not compute; monopoly is a contingent constraint. Consequence: on the receipts, the driver's cell is *contingent-foreclosed* (margin 2.0), which is precisely where Taylor's remedy — restore the bypass, break the capture — applies, so the post's flat "Taylor's remedy is unavailable by the causal structure" (line 258) does not follow. **Fix (author voice):** rewrite the driver passage to "the driver routes through a gate my own catalog files as contingent — a capture carrying the whole margin, the way franchise law rides the car dealer.
