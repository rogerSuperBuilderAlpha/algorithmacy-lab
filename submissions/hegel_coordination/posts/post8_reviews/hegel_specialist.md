# Peer review — Paper 8, "At Home in the Other"
## Reviewer lens: Hegel / German idealism (accuracy and scholarly fairness)

**VERDICT: Minor revisions.** The exegesis is careful, well-sourced, and — on the load-bearing move (a
whole can be irreducible and estranging) — genuinely strong. One fix is more important than all the others:
**the spine equates three things Hegel keeps on two different levels.** Non-severability (coupling), voice /
coordinative sovereignty, and being-at-home / concrete freedom are run together in the thesis sentence and in
"Exit and Voice, Made Causal" ("concrete freedom … *is* the freedom of voice"), even though "The Line I Have
to Hold" argues, correctly, that they are not the same thing. The manuscript mounts a superb guard against
letting *irreducibility* stand in for being-at-home. It does not mount the symmetric guard against letting
*voice* stand in for being-at-home — and its own phrasing walks into that error. Fix that identity slide and
the piece is close to publishable; leave it and a Neuhouserian referee will say the essay demotes Hegel's
freedom to an institutional-design fact after promising not to.

---

## Step 0 (what this is / does the central move survive)

This is an essay that reads Hegel's *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* as a 170-years-early statement of the lab's
"coordinative sovereignty," maps Hegel's two freedoms onto Hirschman's exit/voice made causal by a partition
test, and then — the spine — refuses the flattering inference that the irreducible wholes the test finds are
the free ones (warehouse and co-op are partition-twins; the instrument reads coupling, not recognition).

The central move survives, and it survives *because* of the refutation line, which is the best thing in the
piece: the concession that the causal criterion tracks only the objective half of Hegel's freedom, with
Neuhouser naming the subjective half it misses, is exactly the right concession and it is made out loud. What
does not yet survive clean is the *upstream* identification — the essay lets "voice / coordinative
sovereignty" and "being-at-home / concrete freedom" collapse into each other in three places, which is the
same category error it correctly forbids on the irreducibility side.

## Part 1 — Exegesis (ranked most-damaging first)

### 1. The identity slide: voice / coordinative sovereignty is *not* being-at-home, and the prose says it is (most important)

Hegel's ladder has three rungs, and the essay's own argument needs all three kept apart:

- **Coupling / non-severability** — you are genuinely *in* an other, exit would cost you something you are.
  This is at most the **objective precondition**.
- **Voice / standing / coordinative sovereignty** — the institutional form the demand for subjective freedom
  takes once exit is foreclosed. Still on the **objective-institutional** side: it is a structural feature of
  how terms get set, not an achieved state of the member.
- **Being-at-home / concrete freedom (*Beisichselbstsein*)** — rational self-recognition in an institution
  whose content is rational. The **subjective achievement**, which for Hegel *is* freedom (§260; EL §24 Z2).

The manuscript separates rung 1 from rung 3 beautifully in "The Line I Have to Hold": the warehouse worker
has rung 1 and lacks rung 3, the instrument reads only rung 1, "the difference between them … is invisible to
it." That is correct and it is the paper's spine.

But it collapses rung 2 into rung 3 twice, in identity language:

- "Concrete freedom, being-at-home-in-the-other, **is** the freedom of voice: the freedom of a member so
  bound into a whole that exit is empty" (§ "Exit and Voice, Made Causal").
- The thesis sentence: coordinative sovereignty "**is** *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* rebuilt for a
  platform-mediated world."

A Hegelian referee will press exactly here. Voice — standing to make terms hold — is not being-at-home. You
can have voice and be estranged (Hegel's *Pöbel*/rabble have formal standing and rights and are precisely
*not* at home in ethical life, §244–245). You can be relatively at home with little formal voice (the family,
§158; trust in the state as *Gesinnung*, §268 — being-at-home is recognition and trust, not co-determination
of terms). So voice is the *form the objective demand takes*, one more thing on the furnishing side, and
having it is no more identical to being-at-home than being-trapped is. The guard the essay mounts against
"irreducibility = home" has to be mounted with equal force against "voice = home," and the current phrasing
does the opposite.

The essay's *considered* position is already right: coordinative sovereignty is defined a paragraph later as
"a **demand** made on them … not a property my instrument confers," and "Hegel grounds the demand in the
rational content of the institution … I cannot measure that." That is the correct two-level view. So this is
a consistency-and-phrasing fix, not a conceptual rebuild: bring the thesis sentence and the "Made Causal"
section into line with the Line section by making voice/coordinative sovereignty the *objective-institutional
condition under which being-at-home becomes possible when exit is foreclosed* — never its equivalent.
(Rewrites in Part 3.) If the author genuinely means the identity — that having voice just *is* concrete
freedom — this drops to a major revision, because that is the platform-apologetic reading the piece exists to
refuse.

### 2. The subjective half is mislocated as *affirmation / attitude*; for Hegel it is *rational recognition*

Repeatedly the missing half is glossed as a preference or attitude: Neuhouser's second condition is that
members "**subjectively affirm** it"; the instrument is silent on "a fact about whether **members affirm** the
whole"; the second question is "**attitudinal**." This undersells Hegel and, worse, invites the wrong repair.

For Hegel and for Neuhouser the subjective condition is not raw affirmation. It is **rational recognition** —
members can, *understanding* the institution, endorse it *because its content is rational*. The direction of
warrant matters: a whole is not rational because members happen to affirm it; their affirmation is warranted
because the whole is rational. False consciousness is on the table (members may affirm an irrational
arrangement, or fail to recognize a rational one). If the missing half is merely "attitude," a reader will
reasonably answer, "then survey the workers" — and a satisfaction survey would close the gap. It would not,
and the reason it would not is exactly that Hegel's subjective condition is a *joint* fact about rational
content *and* warranted recognition, not a poll of preferences. Reframing the second condition as rational
recognition (not affirmation) both is more faithful and protects the essay's own limit-claim: the thing the
instrument misses is normative, not a datum you could collect with a questionnaire.

### 3. The "two components come apart" reading understates that their coming-apart is, for Hegel, itself the mark of unfreedom

"Being-at-home-in-the-other has two components, and they come apart." True as an analytic reconstruction
(and it is a Neuhouser-flavored reconstruction, not something Hegel states as two separable conditions —
worth signaling as the interpreter's move rather than as plainly "in" §24/§7). But there is a sharper and
more faithful version available, and it *helps* the argument. For Hegel, in a *rational* institution the
objective structure and the subjective disposition are a **unity** — that unity is what *Sittlichkeit* means
and what makes the institution rational. The two halves come apart precisely in **defective, estranging**
institutions: that coming-apart *is* alienation (*Entfremdung*), the unhappy consciousness, the rabble. So the
warehouse is not just "objective half present, subjective half absent" as a neutral split; it is Hegel's own
diagnostic picture of an *un*free institution — one where a member is in the other without being at home in
it. Say that, and the instrument's blindness lands harder: the test cannot see the very split that, for
Hegel, *is* the presence of unfreedom. As written, the "they come apart" framing sounds like a neutral
analytic convenience; Hegel's point is that their coming-apart is the alarm.

### 4. Two orphan references — Honneth and Roy are in the list but nowhere cited in the body

House rule and APA both: every entry is cited, every cite resolves. **Honneth (1995)** appears in References
but nowhere in the body — and recognition theory is doing real work in the argument ("Recognition theorists
link Hegelian freedom to membership without going through Hirschman," which is currently *uncited*). Attach
Honneth to that sentence; it is the natural and correct home. **Roy (2006)** — the "*In seinem Anderen bei
sich selbst zu sein*" article, the one paper in the list actually *about* the phrase the essay is built on —
appears only in the References and the (deleted-before-posting) sourcing note. Cite it at the §24 discussion
or cut it. As it stands, after the byline and sourcing note are deleted, Roy is orphaned. Easy, concrete fix;
it is a defect only because the piece is otherwise scrupulous about sourcing.

### 5. "Concrete freedom = non-severability" sits in tension with Hegel's prizing of the modern right to exit

A quieter worry under #1. The causal mapping makes concrete freedom the freedom of the *non-severable*
member. But for Hegel, being-unable-to-leave is not itself a mark of concrete freedom — it is closer to a
feature of *pre-modern* ethical life that he criticizes. The modern state's rationality includes the **right
of subjective particularity**: the freedom to choose one's estate and profession (§206), the refusal of caste,
the insistence that the modern individual is *not* simply bound by station. Hegel's "at home in the other" is
compatible with a formal right of exit; what makes it freedom is *recognition of rational content*, not the
closing of the exit. The essay knows this (it is the whole burden of the Line section), but the exit/voice
mapping, taken alone, reads as "non-severable = concrete-free," which is the pre-modern picture Hegel rejects.
The fix is the same as #1: the partition test locates *which freedom is even on the table* (when exit is
foreclosed, the only freedom left to *ask about* is the concrete/voice kind), not *whether the member is
concretely free*. Keep the verb "ask about"; drop the verbs of identity.

### 6. The co-op is a *candidate* home, not automatically a home — and "governed by its members" is not quite Hegel's criterion

The CoopCycle/warehouse contrast is the best illustration in the piece and it is faithful in its main claim
(the instrument cannot tell them apart). Two calibrations:

- The binary "one is a home and the other a cage" slightly overstates. A worker-governed co-op pursuing
  irrational ends, or one whose members despise it, has the *governance precondition* and is still not a
  Hegelian home. The co-op is a **candidate** home — it has the objective-institutional condition that makes
  being-at-home *possible* — not a guaranteed one. Hedging this protects the guard on the voice side (#1): it
  says out loud that voice-in-the-structure is not yet being-at-home.
- Hegel is not a workplace-democracy theorist, and "governed by its members" is not his differentiator.
  Direct universal participation is, for Hegel, the road *to* the Terror; his "voice" is mediated (estates,
  the Estates Assembly, §301ff). His criterion for the rational institution is **rational content plus
  recognition** (§260), and member governance is *evidence/mechanism* for that, not the criterion itself. The
  essay's own best line — "Hegel grounds the demand in the rational content of the institution" — gets this
  exactly right; the co-op example, by resting the whole difference on "who governs," tilts more Hirschman/
  democratic than Hegel. A one-clause acknowledgment fixes it.

**Strengthening opportunity (optional, but it is Hegel handing you your own argument):**

- **§75 / §161–163: marriage is *not* a contract.** Hegel attacks (Kant's) contractual account of marriage
  precisely because a contract presupposes *separable* persons who could exit — the very severability the
  essay makes its hinge. That is Hegel running the exit-critique himself, on the essay's own terms. It would
  strengthen the §158 paragraph enormously and costs one sentence.
- **§250–256, the *Korporation*.** The CoopCycle courier-cooperative maps onto Hegel's *corporation* — the
  ethical institution of civil society where the member "has his honor in his estate" (§253), a "second
  family," a site of *recognition*. That is the textual home for "co-op as a candidate Hegelian whole," and
  it is far more precise than a generic appeal to member governance.

### Passages I checked and judged faithful (so the author knows what *not* to touch)

- **PR §5, the freedom of the void.** "Freedom of the void," "raised to the status of an actual shape and
  passion," "the fanaticism of destruction of the whole existing social order" — faithful to Nisbet and to
  Hegel's meaning. One nuance worth a half-sentence: in §5 the void is a *moment* of the will (its
  universality/negativity), real and necessary, that turns destructive only when *absolutized* as the whole.
  The essay treats it almost entirely as pathology (seasteaders, crypto-libertarians). That is rhetorically
  fine, but Hegel affirms the capacity to abstract as an essential moment of the free will; the disease is
  making it the *only* moment. "When the flight from every determination becomes a program" already gestures
  at this — one clause could make it explicit and pre-empt a specialist's objection that you have made a
  moment into a mere error.
- **PhG ¶590, the Terror.** The reading (a freedom that recognizes no institution as its own can only
  negate; its sole deed is meaningless death) is faithful to "Absolute Freedom and Terror." **Verification
  note:** the freely accessible full texts online are Miller ("the coldest and meanest of all deaths," "no
  more significance," "cutting off a head of cabbage"); I could not independently re-verify Pinkard's
  "coldest, emptiest death of all," "no inner amplitude and no inner fulfillment," and "chopping off" against
  a readable Pinkard text this pass. The wording is *consistent* with the German (*kälteste, platteste Tod*;
  *keinen inneren Umfang*) and with Pinkard's diction ("amplitude" for *Umfang* is characteristically his),
  so I allege no error — but confirm against the physical Cambridge 2018 copy before posting, as your
  sourcing note already flags.
- **EL §24 Z2.** The sense is right: freedom as *bei sich selbst in seinem Anderen*, self-dependence,
  self-determination; and "Freedom exists only where there is no other for me that I am not myself." **Two
  cautions.** (a) The exact "freedom is *precisely* this" (vs. "just this") G/S/H wording I could not confirm
  independently; your note already gates it at Hackett p. 60 — keep it gated. (b) §24 Z2 is a passage in the
  *Logic* about the *Concept* being at home with itself in its other; calling it "defining the free will" is
  Hegel's own extension but conflates the logical and practical registers slightly. "Hegel's most compressed
  statement of the structure of freedom" would be exact; "defining the free will in the *Encyclopaedia
  Logic*" overstates by a hair.
- **PR §7 (friendship/love) and §158 (love).** Faithful. Note for the reader (not a fix): friendship/love in
  §7 are the *Zusatz*'s illustrations of a formal point about the concrete will (individuality as the unity
  of universality and particularity), not Hegel's primary argument — the essay foregrounds them, which is
  fine for the register.
- **PR §260, concrete freedom.** "The state is the actuality of concrete freedom" and the paraphrase of the
  continuation (particular interests reaching full development and passing over into the universal, known and
  willed as one's own) are faithful. Reading §260 as "a demand on the institution" is exactly right.
- **Patten (1999).** Fairly used, accurately paraphrased (freedom realized and sustained through rational
  institutions). No promoted quotation. Good.
- **Neuhouser (2000).** The two-condition reconstruction is fair (see #2 for the one calibration). If
  anything you *under*-load his subjective side — Neuhouser's subjective conditions include understanding,
  affirmation, *and* a settled disposition (*Gesinnung*) to act for the institution's sake. Loading more onto
  the subjective side only strengthens your concession, so consider it.

## Part 2 — Register and slop (essay register: first person correct here)

First person is right for this venue — the global writing-style file and the Annals calibration both license
it for the essay/review genres, and the task specifies it. The repo's blanket no-first-person rule is the
dissertation spine, not this series. So "my claim," "my lab," "the line I have to hold" are all in register.
Judged against the invariants that *do* hold in every register:

**Named agents / active voice — strong.** "Hegel had a name for that freedom," "Hegel traces the same
logic," "Neuhouser drew the standard out of Hegel and made it a test," "Patten put the point cleanly,"
"Hirschman gave the twentieth century its cleanest vocabulary." This is exactly the corpus's practice. No
agentless-passive throat-clears. Clean.

**Concrete anchors — excellent.** Seasteader, crypto-libertarian, the executive framing obligation as a
constraint on optionality; five competing grocery stores; the ride-hail driver; the Amazon warehouse;
CoopCycle, App Store, WeChat, Som Mobilitat. Every abstraction touches a case. This is the house style working
as intended.

**The antithesis machine — elevated but mostly load-bearing.** The essay is *built* on genuine binaries (void
/ concrete, exit / voice, severable / non-severable), so most "not X but Y" carries content and should stay.
A few paragraphs stack two negations where one is decoration:
- "not felt as an inner state" and "not a condition of the solitary inner self but something realized" in the
  same short stretch (§ "Freedom Lives in Institutions") — keep one.
- "Why This Is Not Just Berlin Again" is antithesis-dense by its nature (it is a *not-Berlin* argument); it
  reads fine, but watch that the section does not become a metronome of "it does not say X; it says Y."
Net: no blanket cut — the density is justified by the subject — but trim the stacked doublings.

**Self-narrating rigor — a few to cut (this is the repo's #2 priority tic).**
- "The concession has a scope, and **stating it honestly means stating it narrowly**." This narrates the
  virtue of honesty — very close to the banned "stating it honestly matters more than stating it strongly."
  Cut to the scope itself.
- "File that. It is **the honest limit** of the whole reading." "Honest" is self-congratulation; "File that"
  is a good essayistic beat — keep the beat, drop the adjective.
- "Hold the structure of the claim apart from its warm examples, because it does cold work later." Mildly
  self-narrating (announcing the maneuver). Essay register tolerates it; trim if you want it leaner.

**Uniformity of optimization — several sections land on an epigram.** "The line is the difference between a
diagnosis and an apology, and it runs exactly where Hegel drew it." / "Both halves are the finding." / "It
tells you when you are in it. That is not nothing…" Individually these are good. As the shape of *every*
section close it becomes the polish-tell the style file warns about. Let one or two sections just stop on the
argument rather than the aphorism.

**Verbatim repetition — one real instance.** The "tell the gig driver his freedom is his right to quit = the
freedom of leaving the only market there is" formulation appears in "Coordinative Sovereignty Is This Claim"
and again in the close of "The Line I Have to Hold," in near-identical shape. Your sourcing note credits
de-duplication of the Terror and co-op material; this one slipped. Keep the stronger instance (the Line-section
one lands harder), refer back in the other.

**Rhythm — good.** Dense builds punctured by real short declaratives ("It fails." "The void has to keep
leaving. That is the whole of what it can do." "File that."). Varied, not a punchy metronome. This is the
corpus cadence done well.

## Part 3 — Exact line rewrites (in the author's voice)

**(A) The identity slide, "Exit and Voice, Made Causal" (the most important single fix).**

> *Current:* "Concrete freedom, being-at-home-in-the-other, is the freedom of voice: the freedom of a member
> so bound into a whole that exit is empty, whose freedom has to be found in the coordination or nowhere."

> *Rewrite:* "For the member so bound into a whole that exit is empty, the only freedom left to ask about is
> the concrete kind — being-at-home-in-the-other — and its only available form is voice. Voice is not yet
> that freedom; it is the shape the demand for it has to take once leaving is off the table. Whether the
> member is actually at home in the coordination, or only trapped in it, is the further question, and the
> partition test does not reach it."

**(B) The thesis sentence, opening section.**

> *Current:* "Hegel's alternative — freedom as being at home with oneself in the other, *Beisichselbstsein im
> Anderen* — is the thesis my lab has been calling coordinative sovereignty, stated a hundred and seventy
> years early in a grammar the lab does not have."

> *Rewrite (keeps the hook, loses the overshoot):* "Hegel's alternative — freedom as being at home with
> oneself in the other, *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* — is the thesis my lab has been circling under the
> name coordinative sovereignty, a hundred and seventy years before we had the grammar to restate its
> institutional half. Coordinative sovereignty operationalizes the objective condition of Hegel's freedom —
> voice, standing, a share in the terms; the recognition that turns a coordination into a home it leaves to
> Hegel, and to the members."

**(C) The subjective half as recognition, not affirmation (Neuhouser paragraph and the Line section).**

> *Current:* "its members must subjectively affirm it — grasp it as their own, be at home in it."

> *Rewrite:* "its members must be able to *recognize themselves* in it — to grasp its ends as rational and,
> understanding them, endorse them as their own. Not that they happen to approve of it; that they could,
> seeing what it is, be at home in it."

> *Current (Line section):* "His second is attitudinal, a fact about whether members affirm the whole, and no
> measurement of causal structure will ever return it."

> *Rewrite:* "His second is recognitive — whether members can find their own reason in the whole's ends — and
> no measurement of causal structure will ever return it. It is not a preference you could poll; it is a
> question about the whole's rational content and the members' warranted recognition of it, and both lie past
> what a partition can see."

**(D) The co-op as candidate home, not guaranteed home (Line section).**

> *Current:* "But one is governed by its members and the other governs them, and that is the whole difference
> between a home and a cage."

> *Rewrite:* "But one is governed by its members and the other governs them — and that is the difference
> between an institution that *could* be a home and one that structurally cannot. Even the co-op is only a
> candidate: member governance is the condition under which being-at-home becomes possible, not proof that the
> members are at home. What it is not is a cage by design. That much the warehouse is, and the co-op is not —
> and it is exactly that much the partition test cannot see."

**(E) Cite Honneth (and optionally Roy) where the argument already leans on them.**

> *Current:* "Recognition theorists link Hegelian freedom to membership without going through Hirschman;"

> *Rewrite:* "Recognition theorists link Hegelian freedom to membership without going through Hirschman
> (Honneth, 1995);"

> And at the §24 discussion, if Roy is to stay in the list, a trailing "(see Roy, 2006, on the phrase)"
> earns its keep; otherwise cut Roy from References.

**(F) Kill the self-narration, keep the scope (Line section).**

> *Current:* "The concession has a scope, and stating it honestly means stating it narrowly. No firm has been
> run through the classifier for this claim."

> *Rewrite:* "The concession has a narrow scope. No firm has been run through the classifier for this claim."

**(G) Optional strengthening — one sentence at §158 that hands you Hegel running your own argument.**

> *Add after the §158 marriage sentences:* "Hegel makes the point in the register of exit himself: marriage,
> he insists, is not a contract (§75, §163), precisely because a contract binds separable persons who could
> walk away, and spouses are no longer separable in that sense. The severability the partition test measures
> is the thing Hegel already knew marriage had left behind."

## Closing note

**Biggest strength.** The refutation line is the real contribution and it is executed with unusual
discipline. "A whole can be irreducible and estranging" — the warehouse and the co-op reading as partition-
twins, the instrument returning "irreducible" for a marriage and a sweatshop alike — is not only faithful to
Hegel; it is the correct diagnosis of what an IIT-style coupling measure can and cannot underwrite, and it
refuses the platform-apologetic inference at exactly the point where a weaker essay would have banked it. The
Neuhouser concession, made out loud rather than buried, is what earns the piece its scholarly seriousness.
Hold that spine; it is why the paper works.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** Decide, and state, whether coordinative sovereignty *is*
being-at-home or is the *objective-institutional condition* of it — and then make every sentence obey that
decision. The body already commits to the second (voice is "a demand," recognition "I cannot measure"); the
thesis sentence and the "Made Causal" section still assert the first. Only the author knows which claim the
series is actually making across Papers 5–8, and the fix is not something a reviewer can impose without
choosing the thesis for you. Choose the two-level reading — coordinative sovereignty operationalizes the
objective half; being-at-home needs the subjective half the instrument is silent on — and the guard you mount
so well against "irreducibility = home" finally covers the flank it currently leaves open: "voice = home." Do
that, and a Hegelian referee accepts "the instrument reads the objective half and is silent on the ethical
half" as an honest scope statement rather than a rigged concession or a demotion of Hegel's freedom to a
coupling fact.
