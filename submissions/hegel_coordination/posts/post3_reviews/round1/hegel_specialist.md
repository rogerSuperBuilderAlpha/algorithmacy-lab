# Hegel-specialist review — "The Real Middle Term" (Post 3, draft v3)

**Reviewer lens:** Hegel and German idealism; accuracy of the exegesis and fairness to the secondary
literature. Everything I assert below as a textual correction I verified against a source before asserting
it: the German of the *Wissenschaft der Logik* syllogism and mechanism chapters (Projekt Gutenberg
edition), the "die reelle Mitte" and free-mechanism passages verbatim, the §182 German, and Stein (2016)
in full — I obtained the Cambridge PDF and checked the paper's three Stein quotations character by
character against pp. 149–151.

---

**VERDICT: Minor revisions, with one mandatory fix.** The single most important fix: re-ground the
mechanism-rung claim in the internal structure of Hegel's mechanism chapter. The solar system is not
mechanism simpliciter; it is *absolute* (free) mechanism, mechanism already exhibiting the Concept and
already passing beyond itself. As written, the paper plants its criterion at "the level of forces and
centers and orbits" as if that level were conceptually neutral ground. It is not — and the accurate
version is *stronger* for the paper, because Hegel's own chapter divides into an external-mechanism
moment (the formal mechanical process, whose operation is literally *communication*, Mitteilung — a
conduit) and a free-mechanism moment (the center, the reelle Mitte — an integrator). Hegel drew the
paper's boundary *inside the mechanism chapter itself*, and the paper currently misses its best text.

---

## Step 0 — Register and bar

Register: first-person academic essay, doctoral bar, APA apparatus — a Substack-series paper that argues
like a journal article and speaks like an essayist ("Start with the sentence, because it sounds mad").
Bar: the house style's invariants (named agents, claim-first, concrete anchors, verified citations,

## Part 1 — Exegesis and scholarly fairness (ranked, most damaging first)

### 1.1 The mechanism-rung placement flattens absolute mechanism — and misses the paper's best text
(§"The Center Is a Real Middle," §"The Move The Resemblance Does Not License," lines 117–142, 240–248)

The paper names "absolute mechanism" once (line 118) and then spends the rest of its length talking
about "the mechanism rung," "the level of forces and centers and orbits," a middle that is "a center of
mass, not a term in a proof." A Hegel reader winces three times here.

First, absolute mechanism is not mere mechanism. Hegel's chapter runs mechanical object → mechanical
process → absolute mechanism, and the third moment is the *truth* of the first two: the totality of
center and law "constitutes free mechanism" ("Diese Totalität … macht den freien Mechanismus aus" —
verified verbatim in the German). The central body is not one object among others that happens to sit in
the middle; Hegel calls it "the genus, but individual universality of the single objects and their
mechanical process" ("der die Gattung, aber individuelle Allgemeinheit der einzelnen Objekte und ihres
mechanischen Processes ist" — verified). The center is the objects' own immanent universality, their
"self," toward which they *strive* — which is exactly why the syllogistic structure shows up there and
why the section then *transitions to chemism*. The reelle Mitte appears at the point where mechanism
stops being merely mechanical. So "the middle of this syllogism is a center of mass" (line 123) is a
modernizing flattening: for Hegel the middle is "the essential determinateness" — objective universality
— of which the central body is the bearer, not a hunk of matter with a barycenter. The phrase is
rhetorically effective and philosophically loose in a way the rest of the paper is not.

Second, the "coincidence" framing (lines 137–142, 240–248) undersells the paper's own position and
slightly misreads Hegel's. The paper says it is "odd for Hegel" (following Sans's flagged oddity, fairly
cited) that his most extended inferential structure sits in the mechanism chapter, and then banks the
oddity as a lucky coincidence for the criterion. But on the free-mechanism reading it is not odd for
Hegel either, and it is not a coincidence for the author: absolute mechanism is where objectivity *first*
exhibits integrated, syllogistic structure — where a system of objects first stops being an aggregate. A
criterion whose entire job is to tell aggregates from integrated wholes naturally has its best purchase
at exactly the boundary where integration first appears. The oddity dissolves, and its dissolution is
friendlier to the bridge than the oddity was. Calling it a coincidence twice ("that coincidence returns
below"; "the coincidence … now earns its keep") leaves the paper holding a weaker card than the one in
its hand.

Third — and this is the constructive point — the paper grounds its conduit in the wrong Hegel text and
misses the right one sitting two subsections earlier *in the same chapter as the reelle Mitte*. Hegel's
formal mechanical process operates by *communication* (Mittheilung): motion, heat, magnetism in the
bodily register, and in the spiritual register "laws, customs, rational representations in general" are
"communicable" contents that "penetrate the individuals in an unconscious way," spreading "as a scent
freely spreads in the unresisting atmosphere" (all verified in the German: "Die Gesetze, Sitten,
vernünftige Vorstellungen überhaupt, sind im Geistigen solche Mittheilbare, welche die Individuen auf
eine bewußtlose Weise durchdringen"; "wie ein Duft in der widerstandslosen Atmosphäre sich frei
verbreitet"). The communicated determinateness remains "an external one" on the product. That is a
conduit, described by Hegel, inside the very chapter whose culmination is the reelle Mitte. The chapter's
own arc — external communication of determinateness → center as immanent universality — *is* the
conduit-to-integrator ascent, in the objective register, on one continuous stretch of text. The paper's
bridge would be shorter, sturdier, and more accurate if it walked across this arc instead of importing
the rote poem and the detached prayer from the chapter's introduction (see 1.2).

The comfort claim needs one honest qualification in exchange: because absolute mechanism is already the
Concept showing through objectivity — already proto-teleological in the objects' striving and the
immanent law — planting the criterion there is not planting it in Concept-free territory. The paper's
Paper-4 deferral handles the *upward* direction (above mechanism) but not the *inward* one (how much
Concept Hegel has already invested in the center). One sentence fixes this; Part 3 supplies it.

### 1.2 "Spiritual mechanism" is not Hegel's name for a defective syllogistic middle
(§"The External Middle," lines 148–159)

The paper writes: "A great deal of the syllogism material is a complaint against bad middles … His name
for the defective case is 'spiritual mechanism.'" That misattributes the term twice over. "Spiritual
mechanism" (geistiger Mechanismus) belongs to the *mechanism* chapter's introduction, in the Objectivity
section — not to the syllogism chapter, and not to the theory of middles at all. Verified German: "Wie
der materielle Mechanismus, so besteht auch der geistige darin, daß die im Geiste bezogenen sich einander
und ihm selbst äußerlich bleiben," followed immediately by the examples — "ein mechanisches Gedächtniß,
die Gewohnheit, eine mechanische Handlungsweise" — mechanical *memory*, habit, mechanical action, where
"the peculiar penetration and presence of spirit is lacking." The rote poem and the detached prayer are
Hegel's illustrations of the mechanical as a category of spirit (mechanical memory), reached through Sans
pp. 205–206; they are not his analysis of a middle term, defective or otherwise. There is no middle term
in a rote recitation on Hegel's account — there is external connectedness of contents, which is a
different (though related) failure.

Hegel's actual name for the defective middle, within the syllogism material, is the one the paper quotes
two paragraphs later: the immediate or formal syllogism, the Verstandesschluss of EL §182, whose middle
is "only the abstract particularity." The paper has the right text; it has it in the wrong order and
under the wrong heading. The fix costs two sentences: keep the spiritual-mechanism examples (they are
vivid and Hegel's own), but introduce them as the mechanism chapter's account of external connection in
spirit, converging with the syllogism chapter's internal complaint against the abstract middle — two
registers, one diagnosis. That is also more useful to the paper, since it then has Hegel drawing the
external-connection line in *both* the subjective and the objective logic, which is what "the complaint
is mine" needs.

A related nuance worth one clause: Hegel does not simply condemn mechanical memory. In the *Philosophy of
Spirit* (§§461–464) mechanical memory is a necessary moment on the way to thought. The paper does not
contradict this, but "his examples are worth sitting with because they are so concrete" invites a reader
to take rote memory as pure defect; Sans's own anti-reductionist caveat, which the paper quotes later
(p. 206), is the guard rail. No change strictly required, but a specialist reader will notice.

### 1.3 Stein: quotations verified correct; "grant and locate" is adequate but leaves the paper's best
Stein card on the table — and sits in tension with the receipts section
(§"Stein's Knife," lines 252–290)

First, verification. I checked all three quotations against the Cambridge PDF of Stein (2016):

- p. 149: "the determination of the elements with which the syllogism works and which it must therefore
  presuppose stems from the concept, and so the standard by which to define the determination of the
  elements is thus not the syllogism but the concept" — verbatim correct, page correct. (Stein
  italicizes "determination"; the paper drops the emphasis without a note — see Part 2 on APA.)
- p. 151: "neither express objectivity's identity and simultaneous difference of the conceptual
  elements' external realities nor determine what the elements specifically are" — verbatim correct,
  and the full sentence does sit on p. 151, so the v2 correction landed.
- p. 151: "Applying the syllogistic structure to elements determined by the concept might show how and
  that they relate but it will not tell us what they are, i.e., what they are determined as" — verbatim
  correct. (Stein italicizes "are"; again the emphasis is silently dropped.)

The characterization is also fair. Stein's article is a review essay on Vieweg's disjunctive-syllogism
reading of the monarch, and his general claims about the syllogism's derivativeness are made in that
context — but they are stated as general claims ("The syllogism does not determine the elements or their
externality, it presupposes their determination by the concept," p. 150), so the paper's extraction is
legitimate. Bonus: Stein's own footnote 10 quotes di Giovanni p. 588 at length, which independently
confirms the paper's page pin for the syllogism-chapter opening.

Now the deeper point, which the paper walks past. The receipts section reports the lab's finding that a
form with "the identical strict-mediation wiring" still factors to zero "because the outer parties' own
update rules break the loop: 'The read functions, not the wiring, decide'" (lines 194–197). Read that
against Stein and the finding is not merely compatible with him — it *reproduces his priority ordering
inside the causal register*. Stein: the relational form presupposes the determination of the elements;
what unifies is decided by what the elements are, not by the arrangement. The lab: the relational shape
(wiring, topology) underdetermines integration; what decides is each element's intrinsic determination —
its update rule, which is the causal register's thin analogue of "what the element is." The instrument's
own results enact Stein's thesis at their own level. This converts Stein from a boundary the paper
politely accepts into a partial *confirmation* the paper's own data supplies, and it is a much stronger
answer than "grant and locate": the instrument was never a form-first instrument in Stein's sense,
and the receipts prove it.

Two cautions if the author takes this up. (a) It must not be oversold: an update rule is a causal
essence, not a conceptual determination. The instrument still knows nothing of person, market, monarch;
Stein's "what they are determined *as*" is semantic, and the confirmation is structural. State it as an
echo of the ordering, not an answer to the Concept. (b) Taking it up requires fixing an internal tension
the paper already has: the Stein section says the criterion "knows only the shape of the dependence among
three nodes" (line 267), but the receipts section says shape (wiring) is precisely what does *not*
decide. The paper contradicts itself between line 197 and line 267. The truth is that the instrument
reads the elements' mechanisms (a thin "what") and is blind to their meanings (the thick "what") — say
that, and both sections align and the Stein response sharpens.

### 1.4 "At the close of the doctrine of the Concept" — a placement error
(§"Everything Rational Is a Syllogism," lines 67–69)

"That is why the syllogism sits where it does in the *Logic*, at the close of the doctrine of the
Concept, the last and richest form of subjectivity before Hegel turns to objectivity." The apposition
self-corrects, but the head phrase is wrong: the Doctrine of the Concept comprises Subjectivity,
Objectivity, and the Idea, and it closes with the absolute Idea, not the syllogism. The syllogism closes
*Subjectivity* (the subjective concept). Any Hegel reader will catch it. One-word-class fix in Part 3.

### 1.5 A guard-brush: "the boundary the test is built to find"
(§"The Receipts," lines 213–214)

"He drew the boundary the test is built to find, and he drew it in the same place." The series guard is
never to claim Hegel anticipated the causal criterion, and the paper's official formulation (lines
333–335: "not that Hegel anticipated the criterion, but that the boundary he drew and the boundary it
computes are the same boundary") is clean. This sentence is the closest brush: "the boundary the test is
built to find" phrases Hegel's distinction teleologically, as if the test were completing something Hegel
started. It will read as anticipation to exactly the hostile reader the guard exists for. A small rewrite
keeps the cadence and loses the risk (Part 3). Elsewhere the guard holds: "Hegel got to it first" (line
20) and "drawn a century and a half early" (line 28) attach to the *distinction*, which is the licensed
claim, and the "Move The Resemblance Does Not License" section is exemplary — genuinely the best guard
discipline in the series so far.

### 1.6 The mortal Greek is Roman
(§"Everything Rational Is a Syllogism," lines 56–57)

"the school exercise with the mortal Greek … Gaius is mortal." Gaius (Hegel's Cajus) is a Roman name; the
mortal *Greek* of the school exercise is Socrates. Either name Socrates in the first clause or drop the
nationality. Trivial, but it is the kind of slip that costs credibility with the audience this paper most
wants.

### 1.7 §182: "rational but non-conceptual" — check the conjunction at the gated spot-check
(§"The External Middle," lines 163–165)

The German of §182 runs "Dieser Schluß ist das Vernünftige als begrifflos" — the rational *as*
concept-less — not "rational *but* non-conceptual." The paper attributes the "rational but
non-conceptual" wording to Brinkmann/Dahlstrom, whose §181–182 text the sourcing note already flags as
one-source and gated for a physical spot-check. Add to that spot-check: confirm whether B&D render "als"
with "as" or "but." The difference is small but real — Hegel is not conceding rationality with a
qualification; he is naming the mode in which the formal syllogism is rational, namely without the
concept. If B&D have "as," the paper's paraphrase frame ("Hegel's verdict is that this syllogism is …")
needs the quotation adjusted. The companion quote — "the subject joins itself together with itself by
means of this mediation" — matches the German construction ("daß das Subjekt durch die Vermittlung sich
mit sich selbst zusammenschließt"; verified via the German §182), with the same one-source caveat on the
exact English.

### 1.8 What is deployed fairly (say so on the record)

- **The headline quote and its reading.** "It is not just that the syllogism is rational but that
  everything rational is a syllogism" — I verified the German ("Der Schluß ist daher nicht nur
  vernünftig, sondern alles Vernünftige ist ein Schluß") and the immediately preceding sentence ("Der
  Schluß ist somit der vollständig gesetzte Begriff; er ist daher das Vernünftige"), which the paper
  renders in order and in sense. Stein's fn. 10 independently confirms di Giovanni p. 588. The
  structural (not proof-theoretic) reading is sound, and the middle-replaces-copula gloss is accurate to
  the chapter's opening move (the syllogism as "the restoration of the concept in the judgment," the
  copula's abstract link filled by the mediating unity).
- **The reelle/reale Mitte philology.** Verified verbatim in the German: "Die erstere, die wesentliche
  Bestimmtheit macht dagegen die reelle Mitte zwischen den vielen mechanisch auf einander wirkenden
  Objekten aus." Hegel's word is *reelle*; treating Sans's "reale Mitte" as his modernized gloss is
  exactly right, and the paper's care in marking the line "between citing Hegel and citing his best
  reader" is the sort of thing referees remember favorably.
- **The government/citizens/needs triad.** Verified: "Die Regierung ist das absolute Centrum …" occurs
  in the SL absolute-mechanism section, as the paper says, and the "system of three syllogisms" tag is
  correctly deferred to EL §198R.
- **Sans's non-reductionist caveat.** The paper quotes "spirit as such transcends mechanism" and builds
  its own boundary-drawing section on it. That is fair dealing with a source whose ontology the paper is
  about to narrow past; Sans would recognize himself here.
- **Redding.** The paper scopes him honestly ("his book does not treat the objective syllogism of the
  mechanism chapter"), which respects the sourcing note's own caveat. One residual: the p. 43 quotation
  sits inside Redding's discussion of Robin Smith's thesis; "his reconstruction … runs through" is
  acceptable, but keep the sourcing note's warning attached through proof stage.

### 1.9 Load-bearing weak point a hostile referee attacks first

Not the exegesis — the *scale claim* hiding in "at the rung where Hegel himself locates his most concrete
real middle, his distinction … is the distinction my criterion computes" (lines 246–248). The receipts
are three-to-six-node Boolean forms; Hegel's absolute mechanism is a totality with immanent law. The
paper's own caveat paragraph (lines 206–211) is well built and nearly sufficient; the referee's remaining
move is "same boundary, incommensurable scales." The free-mechanism revision of 1.1 is also the best
defense here, because it relocates the claim from "my criterion works where Hegel's example lives" to
"Hegel's own chapter draws the boundary my criterion computes, between its formal-process moment and its
center" — a claim about the *shape of the distinction*, which does not depend on scale.

## Part 2 — Register and slop audit

The v3 tone pass did its job; this is a clean draft by the house bar. Residue:

- **Em-dashes: 20 in roughly 5,600 words**, several of them paired mid-sentence bolts. Most earn their
  place (the qualification-then-reveal at line 93 is good practice). Three crutch pairs to convert: lines
  93–97 stack two dash pairs in one paragraph (the Anselm/Descartes aside can take parentheses); lines
  240–241 ("sits at the mechanism rung — the real middle of his clearest worked case is a center of mass
  — and…") bolts a full clause mid-sentence and reads better split; line 189 ("— it 'forwards iff
  resume-signal AND manager-profile' —") can take parentheses since it is a quoted technicality, not a
  reveal.
- **Landing-line drumbeat, residual.** Eight of nine sections still exit on an epigram ("…the term
  through which the whole was a whole"; "…he drew it in the same place"; "…whether its middle is
  necessary or contingent"; "…at the rung where his own showcase middle sits"; "Where both can reach,
  they agree…"). Each is good alone; in series they are the uniformity tell the house style names. Let
  two sections just stop — the receipts section can end on its scope caveat (which also resolves the
  1.5 guard-brush), and "Necessary Middles" can end on the platform example without the epigram.
- **"Located" as a verbal tic.** Four uses: "no located answer" (76), "grant it and locate it" (274),

  reads as a mannerism to an outside reader. Keep two ("grant it and locate it" is the section's spine;
  "no located work" carries the novelty bound), recast the others ("the objection had, until recently,
  no answer with a text behind it"; "In every case found").
- **Performed candor: essentially clean.** "worth sitting with because they are so concrete" (line 150)
  is the one borderline instance — it narrates the value of the examples instead of letting them land.
  Cut "because they are so concrete."
- **Antithesis budget: within bounds.** The conduit/convey vs. integrator/commit contrast recurs by
  design and carries the argument; the incidental "not X but Y" instances (e.g., "a computable
  distinction, not a metaphor," line 209) are load-bearing. No action.
- **Named agents, active voice: pass.** Sans argues, Stein concludes, Hegel writes, the lab computed,

- **APA mechanics (copyeditor overlap, but they gate accuracy):**
  1. Two Hegel 2010 entries require **2010a/2010b**; in-text "1816/2010" must become "1816/2010a"
     throughout.
  2. **Vieweg is named with no citation or reference entry** (line 324) — ironic, since Stein's article

  4. **Harvey's "productive middle term"** (line 326) is a direct quotation without a page number; the

  5. **Stein's italics** ("determination," p. 149; "are," p. 151) are silently dropped; APA wants the

## Part 3 — Exact rewrites (ranked by value)

**R1 (fix 1.1, the mandatory one). Line 123**, "The mediation is concrete: the middle of this syllogism
is a center of mass, not a term in a proof." →

> "The mediation is concrete — and it is more than a barycenter. Hegel calls the central body the
> 'individualized universality of the single objects and their mechanical process' (Sans, 2018, p. 203):
> the center is the objects' own immanent universality, the term through which they are a system at all.
> This is what he calls absolute, or free, mechanism — mechanism at the point where it stops being an
> aggregate, the last moment before the chapter hands off to chemism. The middle of this syllogism is a
> real center, not a term in a proof."

**R2 (fix 1.1, the coincidence paragraphs). Lines 139–142**, "It is odd for Hegel. It is not odd for me.
The rung of the ladder where Hegel plants his clearest real middle is exactly the rung where a
causal-irreducibility criterion is at home. That coincidence returns below." →

> "It is odd only until the chapter's own structure is on the table. Hegel's mechanism chapter runs from
> the formal mechanical process — whose operation he names *communication*, determinateness passed from
> object to object and remaining external to each, laws and customs spreading through individuals 'as a
> scent freely spreads in the unresisting atmosphere' — up to absolute mechanism, where the center holds
> the objects as one system. The chapter's arc is the ascent from conduit to integrator, run in the
> objective register. That is why the real middle lives here, and why a criterion built to tell
> aggregates from integrated wholes is at home on this rung: Hegel put the boundary itself on it. The
> point returns below."

(And at **lines 240–248**, replace "the coincidence from two sections back now earns its keep" framing
with the same structural claim: the criterion touches Hegel's text not by luck of placement but because
the mechanism chapter internally draws the conduit–integrator line, from Mitteilung to Mitte. Add the
one-sentence honesty tax: "One qualification is owed in return: absolute mechanism is already the Concept
showing through objectivity — the center is immanent universality, not neutral machinery — so the rung my
instrument reads is the top of Hegel's mechanism, not its floor. The reckoning above that rung is still
Paper 4's.")

**R3 (fix 1.2). Lines 149–151**, "A great deal of the syllogism material is a complaint against bad
middles, and the complaint is precisely the distinction my criterion is built to draw. His name for the
defective case is 'spiritual mechanism,' and his examples are worth sitting with because they are so
concrete." →

> "Hegel draws the complaint in two registers. In the mechanism chapter's opening he names a 'spiritual
> mechanism': just as in material mechanism, the things connected in spirit 'remain external to one
> another and to spirit itself' — mechanical memory, habit, a mechanical way of acting. His examples are
> concrete. Someone recites a poem in a flat, monotonous voice…"

Then, at the §182 paragraph (line 160), open: "The syllogism chapter draws the same line from inside:
the immediate, formal syllogism — the syllogism of the understanding — connects its extremes through a
middle that is 'only the abstract particularity'…" — so the two registers converge instead of being
conflated.

**R4 (fix 1.3). After line 197** ("The read functions, not the wiring, decide."), add:

> "Hold that sentence; it comes back when Stein's objection arrives, because it is Stein's objection —

And in the Stein section, **replace lines 266–268** ("It does not know that A is a person and B is a

> "It does not know that A is a person and B is a market and M is a coordinator. What it reads is thinner

## Closing note

**Biggest strength.** The paper's guard discipline at its central joint. "The Move The Resemblance Does
Not License" is the best section in the series so far: it states exactly what Sans establishes, exactly
what he does not claim, and takes the unlicensed step by name, in first person, with the narrowing
defended as a design choice ("thin and decidable is what makes it an instrument"). Referees almost never
see a bridge paper that marks its own load-bearing weld this honestly — and the verification work behind
it is real: every Hegel and Stein quotation I checked, checked out, down to the vowel in *reelle*.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** The paper's one abstraction without a lived anchor is its
most important case: the intermediary "that looks like overhead, a cost to be cut, and turns out to be
the term through which the whole was ever a whole" (lines 316–317). Every other mechanism in the paper
touches a concrete case — Gaius, the sun, the rote prayer, the Boolean receipts. This one, the case the
whole program exists for, is still hypothetical. One real instance from the author's own consulting or
teaching — a named-in-kind, suitably anonymized middle that someone cut and the process dissolved — would
do more for the paper than any exegetical repair I have listed, and no reviewer can fabricate it.
