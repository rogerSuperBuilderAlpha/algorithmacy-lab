### F2 — The sharpest Hegelian objection is conceded by adjective, not by structure (teleology / Ng–Kreines). Substantive.

Draft text (from "The Move the Resemblance Does Not License"):
> "Sans's ontology is a Hegelian ontology, which means it is teleological and self-realizing … The genuine middle, at its fullest, is not gravity holding rocks in orbit. It is a whole producing and sustaining its own unity, a middle that is the whole's own act. That is a real structure. It is not a cause-and-effect structure whose parts either survive a partition or do not."

This is the right concession, and it meets the refutation condition. But it disarms the teleology objection with mood-words — "self-realizing, transcending mechanism, alive" — where the *structural* form of the objection is sharper and the draft can afford to name it. On the Ng/Kreines immanent-teleology reading, Hegel's genuine middle is not merely irreducible; it is the **whole as prior to and the ground of its parts** — the parts are the parts they are *because of* the whole they compose. The partition test is mereologically **symmetric**: it takes the parts and their update rules as given and asks only whether some cut is lossless. It has no way to read "the whole grounds its parts," because it starts from the parts. So the homology conduit-vs-integrator captures *reducible vs. irreducible* but not the asymmetry — prior whole vs. assembled parts — that is the specifically Hegelian content of *rational*. Pressing this sharpens rather than weakens the paper, because it locates precisely what the instrument reads and what it structurally cannot, and it connects directly to Stein (whose Concept "determines the elements") two sections later.

Suggested insertion (author voice), after "a middle that is the whole's own act":
> "And the gap is not just one of richness. Hegel's real middle is the whole standing *prior* to its parts — the parts are what they are because of the unity they make. My test runs the other way: it takes the parts and their rules as given and asks only whether any cut of them is lossless. That asymmetry — whole-before-parts against parts-first — is the one thing the partition can never read, because it begins where Hegel ends. It is also, exactly, Stein's complaint, arriving early."

This keeps the graded-fit architecture and hands the Stein section its setup.
