**VERDICT: Not ready to post. Footnote plumbing is clean and most quotations are verbatim-confirmed, but three citation problems must be fixed first — one outright misattribution, one review-sourced quote carried on a bare (and probably wrong) page cite, and a draft-vs-print translation gap.**

## Ranked issues (most-damaging first)

### 1. [^9] Ng — review-sourced quote on a bare, probably-wrong page cite; body paraphrase tracks a reviewer's line (¶6, lines 60–64)
This is the top integrity risk in the piece.

- **The quote.** "life opens up the space of reasons itself" is real — Ng does write it (confirmed the phrase exists via the book's promotion of "the Idea puts forward the thesis that life opens up the space of reasons itself"). **But the page is unconfirmed and likely wrong.** The located NDPR review (Gentry) quotes "life opens up the space of reasons" and keys it to **pp. 234, 281** — not p. 10, and without "itself." The scaffold marks the p. 10 attribution as review-sourced via Chaput/Suther and *not yet confirmed against the book*. As printed, [^9] gives a bare "p. 10" that reads as first-hand primary sourcing for a page the one review actually in hand contradicts.
  - **Correction:** open the book. Confirm both the exact wording ("...itself") and the page. If "...itself" sits at p. 10 and the plain form at pp. 234/281, cite p. 10 and keep "itself." If you cannot open it before posting, attribute honestly: `Ng, *Hegel's Concept of Life* (2020), quoted in [reviewer], [venue]` — do not print a bare book page.
- **The body paraphrase.** "inner purposiveness, which Kant had allowed only as a way of regarding organisms, becomes for Hegel constitutive of what they are" (lines 62–63) is the **regulative→constitutive gloss** that the scaffold explicitly says *not* to attribute to Ng: the tidy sentence "Where Kant saw this objective principle of purposiveness as merely regulative, Hegel takes it as constitutive" is **reviewer Gentry's** paraphrase, keyed to Ng p. 43 (confirmed on the NDPR page). The essay paraphrases rather than quotes it, and the underlying regulative-to-constitutive thesis *is* genuinely Ng's, so this is not a fabricated attribution — but it is Ng's argument stated in the reviewer's frame, hung off a quote the reader will take as primary. **Confirm the paraphrase against Ng's own text**, not the review, before the sourcing note (which currently carries the warning) is deleted.

### 2. [^8] *Encyclopaedia Logic* §135 — one quoted fragment is from a different work (¶7, lines 50–54)
- "if the whole is taken and made what its notion implies, i.e. if it is divided, it at once ceases to be a whole" — **verbatim §135 (Wallace). Confirmed.**
- "is untrue to this extent, that the notion and the reality of the relation are not in harmony" — **verbatim §135 (Wallace). Confirmed** (full sentence: "The relation of the whole and the parts is untrue to this extent, that…").
- the whole "consists of parts, so that it is not anything without them" — **NOT in *EL* §135.** This is *Science of Logic*, "The Essential Relation" (the scaffold's own quote bank assigns it there: "The whole accordingly consists of parts, so that it is not anything without them"). Citing it to "*Encyclopaedia Logic* §135, trans. Wallace" is wrong on both the work and, since Wallace did not translate the *Science of Logic*, the translator.
  - **Correction:** either delete the "consists of parts" clause (the §135 material alone carries ¶7), or give it its own footnote: *Science of Logic*, Doctrine of Essence, "The Essential Relation," with the actual SL translator named.

### 3. [^10] Malabou — verbatim but review-sourced; edition/page and a capitalization point (¶9, lines 80–81)
- "it is not stasis but metamorphosis that characterizes Absolute Knowledge" is **verbatim-confirmed on the NDPR review of *The Future of Hegel*, keyed to p. 134** — but it is sourced from the review, not the book. The footnote's bare "p. 134" reads as first-hand.
- **Edition year:** NDPR/SUNY list the edition the review used as **2010**; the essay cites **Routledge 2005**. Confirm p. 134 corresponds to the **2005 Routledge** printing specifically before printing the page.
- **Chicago capitalization:** the original opens "**It** is not stasis…". Introduced here by a full clause and a colon ("Catherine Malabou put the edge on it:"), a complete-sentence quotation should keep the capital — "…on it: 'It is not stasis…'" — or bracket the lowercase "[i]t". As printed it silently alters the source.
- The sourcing note does flag this quote for confirmation; the fix is to execute the check, not merely delete the note.

### 4. Translation attribution — quoted wording is the marxists.org draft; footnotes cite the print (¶3–4)
The Pinkard fragments are all verbatim against the **freely circulated marxists.org Pinkard text** (I checked each against it):
- §20 "The true is the whole. However, the whole is only the essence completing itself through its own development… essentially a result, and only at the end is it what it is in truth." — **exact.**
- §3 "the whole is the result together with the way the result comes to be." — **exact.**
- §17 "everything hangs on apprehending and expressing the true not as substance but rather even more as subject." — **exact (in the draft).**
- §18 "this self-restoring sameness, the reflective turn into itself in its otherness." — **exact.**

But [^1]–[^5] cite "Pinkard (Cambridge, 2018)," and the scaffold warns the **published Cambridge 2018** text differs slightly from the circulating draft, most likely at §17 ("not just as substance but just as much as subject") and possibly in paragraph numbering. **Confirm §17 and §18 wording and the §-numbers against the physical Cambridge 2018 edition.** If the print reads "not just… just as much as subject," the ¶4 quotation and the surrounding gloss ("A substance is what it is. A subject is what it is by taking itself up") must be reconciled. (Sourcing-note bullet 1 already names this; it must be closed, not deleted.)

### 5. [^6] Taylor — a cited page that is never quoted (¶5, lines 32–36)

Internally coherent; the only open question is whether the print edition renumbers §17/§18 (see item 4).

## Minor mechanical / consistency

- **Em-dashes: 14** in ~1,200 words; several paragraphs carry two (¶3, ¶5, ¶6, ¶9). Defensible in this essay register and by the house-style file, but two or three are decorative — trim on the read-aloud pass.
- **En-dash "cause–effect structure" (line 44).** True en-dash, appears once, so internally consistent. Defensible as a relational en-dash (cf. "mind–body"). No change required; just be aware it is the only en-dash and keep it consistent if the compound recurs in later posts.
- **Citation format is not uniform (Chicago).** Standardize to "City: Publisher, year":
  - Subtitles: [^7] Kreines and [^10] Malabou give full subtitles; [^9] Ng drops its subtitle (*Self-Consciousness, Freedom, Logic*). Pick one policy.
  - Publisher rendering: [^1]/[^6] "Cambridge," [^7]/[^9] "Oxford" (cities standing in for CUP/OUP) vs [^10] "Routledge" (publisher name). Mixed. Use either full press names or full "City: Publisher" throughout.
  - [^8] gives no place/year/edition for the Wallace *Encyclopaedia Logic*; add publication data (and, per item 2, a separate SL cite).
- **"Concept" / "notion".** The body quotes Wallace's "notion" (lines 53–54) and then uses "concept" in its own voice (line 55) for the same *Begriff*. Not wrong, but a reader may trip; consider a parenthetical or one consistent term outside the quotation.
- **"cosmic-spirit reading" (line 33).** The author's own gloss on Taylor, not attributed — fine. Just don't let it drift into a sourced label; the scaffold notes Pippin's "cosmic unitary mind" tag for Taylor is unconfirmed.
