## Summary
Hegel's mature political philosophy and the series' single most-cited primary text: civil society as a system of all-round interdependence whose unity is external (§§182–189), the rabble as a structural side-effect (§§243–245), the Korporation as the second ethical root of the state (§§250–256), freedom as being-at-home-in-the-other realized through institutions (§§5, 7, 158, 260), and the state-as-organism membership passages (§§269–278). The series reads these as the same aggregate/configuration boundary the partition test draws, at homology — never as Hegel anticipating Φ.

## Critical notes & threats
- Nisbet exact wording is the recurring gate: cite by § only until the physical 1991 Cambridge copy is checked (archive.org item elementsofphilos0000hege is a borrowable path).
- Keep Knox out of any quotation (banned): his "caste" for Zunft, "without rank or dignity," "frightful," and capital-C "Corporations" have leaked into drafts and must be reverted to Nisbet.
- Homology guard: §183/§189 civil-society "external" (unwilled, Verstand-self-subsistent) is a DIFFERENT sense of external from partition-survivable; the drafts must keep the two registers distinct and never let the structural reading annex Hegel's ethical/economic complaint.
