## Claim (correct altitude)
Hegel's syllogism — two extremes holding together through a middle — is the mediated triad, and his complaint against merely external middles is the conduit/integrator distinction: the boundary he drew from communication to the real middle inside the Mechanism chapter and the boundary the partition criterion computes are boundaries of the same shape (a homology, not an identity of referents, and not an anticipation of Φ).

## Guard check
Clean. No anticipation-of-Φ claim: the bound is explicit ("not that Hegel anticipated the criterion, but that the boundary he drew and the boundary it computes are boundaries of the same shape"), and "the same boundary" was downgraded to same-shape everywhere it carries weight. No demotion of Φ: the instrument is affirmed ("thin and decidable is what makes it an instrument"; the receipts are "computed," the exact computation "needed" per STRUCTURAL_FINDINGS §7). Two watch notes only: the close's panel-approved "the same boundary the criterion reads, not yet the same computation" is the single spot above same-shape altitude — keep both halves together in any later edit; "a cut and a count" in the final paragraph is method description, not demotion.

## Refutation condition
Sans defeats the "merely a logical form" objection — Hegel's objective syllogism is a real structure of the whole — but his ontology is teleological and self-realizing, explicitly transcending mechanism, so it licenses "the syllogism is a real structure," not "a causal cause-effect structure whose irreducibility is partition-sensitive." That last move is the series' own to make; if the post cannot make it, it rests on Sans's ontology and collapses into a strong footnote of Post 4. The v5 draft meets the condition: it names the step as its own, grades the fit (tightest at the conduit pole, loosest at the integrator pole, where the test reads only the causal shadow of the real middle), and enters the topology gap (rotating vs fixed-star middle) as a real limit.
