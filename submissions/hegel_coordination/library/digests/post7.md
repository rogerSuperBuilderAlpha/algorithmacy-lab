## Claim (correct altitude)
Hegel argues that civil society requires the Korporation as a mediating institution without which the individual has no standing — a claim that some intermediaries are necessary integrators, drawn a century before any instrument existed; his closed-guild/open-corporation line and the catalog's contingent/necessary line fall on the same boundary (homology of boundary, not identity of criterion, and not anticipation of Φ).

## Refutation condition
From SERIES_PLAN §7: Hegel's corporations confer honor and ethical standing, not coordination; if the integration reading requires ignoring what he actually says corporations do, say so and downgrade the bridge. The v3 draft executes the downgrade explicitly — it concedes the welfare functions (§§252–253), concedes that the standing is recognitive and invisible to the instrument, and holds the bridge at boundary level only (\"Hegel's necessity is ethical; the catalog's is causal\"). Ruda adds a second, unhandled refutation edge: if the corporation cannot reach the rabble, \"necessary middle\" must be scoped to those the corporation admits, or the necessity claim overreads Hegel.

## Primary passages (pinned editions)

- **PR §252+R — corporation rights; "like a second family"** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[corrected]_  
  The corporation looks after its members' interests, co-opts by skill, protects against contingency, trains newcomers — in short comes on the scene like a second family. German locus for "zweite Familie" confirmed at §252 (Anmerkung). NOTE: the repo passage card pins this to §253R — the card is wrong, the draft is right.
- **PR §253+R — "without the honour of belonging to an estate"** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[gated]_  
  Outside an authorized corporation the individual is without Standesehre (German "ohne Standesehre" confirmed in the §253 Anmerkung); Knox renders "without rank or dignity" (confirmed via IEP). Nisbet's exact English remains unconfirmed in the primary.
- **PR §255 — "the second ethical root of the state"** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[gated]_  
  "As the family was the first, so the Corporation is the second ethical root of the state" — locus verified; Knox has "the one planted in civil society," the draft's Nisbet-attributed "based in civil society itself" is unconfirmed, and the trailing "itself" needs a page check.
- **PR §255 Addition — abolition foil; "a corporation is not a closed guild"; "miserable guild system"** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[gated]_  
  German "Korporation ist keine geschlossene Zunft" confirmed in the §255 Zusatz; the ossify/"miserable guild system" and "abolition of corporations in recent times" lines corroborated as Addition text (IEP-family sources). Correct locus (Addition, not §255R) per series plan. Nisbet wording gated.
- **PR §207 — "recognition both in his own eyes and in the eyes of others"** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991); Knox-stable) — _[verified]_  
  Rectitude and estate-honour: the individual makes himself a member of a moment of civil society by his own act and thereby gains "recognition both in his own eyes and in the eyes of others" — confirmed at §207 via Knox (IEP, marxists.org; Knox prints "one's own eyes").
- **PR §303R — the "formless mass" polemic** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[gated]_  
  "a crowd, i.e. a formless mass whose movement and activity can consequently only be elemental, irrational, barbarous, and terrifying" — locus verified as the §303 Remark (Marx's Critique quotes it); the exact string circulates attributed to this passage, but Nisbet's page is unchecked (Knox: "elementary… frightful").
- **PR §308 (with §§305–308) — representation through corporations** (Wood/Nisbet (Cambridge 1991)) — _[gated]_  
  Society "is not dispersed into atomic units" but makes the appointment "articulated into associations, communities, and Corporations" — locus standard and verified; exact wording gated, and the capital-C "Corporations" looks Knox-derived (Nisbet lowercases) — check before posting. The v3 landed-estate/business-class split (§§305–308) is the standard reading.
