My job is to make it sound more like the "I" who wrote paragraph one all the way through, not to sand it toward journal prose.

**Verdict: minor revision.** The philosophy is careful, the guards are correctly placed (homology stated before the metaphor lands, recognition never conflated with Φ, no demotion anywhere I can find), and the numbers match the receipts. What needs work is entirely surface: the voice keeps slipping out of "I" into an institutional third person, and the sections have started closing on the same rhetorical hinge so often that a reader who's made it to section six can predict the last clause of section seven.

**Single most important fix:** the essay is written in first person almost everywhere — "my criterion," "I follow him," "I want to meet it" — except for five places where it switches to talking about itself as "this paper," and one place, at the exact moment the piece is supposed to be most personally honest, where it refers to its own author in the third person: *"The measure brackets the pauperization. The author does not."* That line is in the paragraph the v3 review specifically flagged as "one thing only the author can supply" — and it's the one paragraph that flinches away from "I" to supply it. Fix that lapse and the five "this paper does X" academic-subject constructions, and most of the register problem in the piece disappears with it.

## Section-by-section

### Opening (untitled)

**Flag — academic-paper subject creeping into first-person voice.** Two instances close together:
> "This paper is about that coincidence and its exact limit."
> "The work of this paper is to hold the agreement and the difference together at once, and to keep either from annexing the other."

The essay's own persona is "I" — it just isn't using it here. **Rewrite:**
> "I want to spend the rest of this essay on that coincidence, and on its exact limit."
> "My job here is to hold the agreement and the difference together at once, and keep either from annexing the other."

**Flag — section-closing antithesis epigram (first instance of a pattern that recurs at nearly every section break).**
> "...this paper reads what he found against what the instrument finds — two roads to one verdict on one case, not one road walked twice."

The "not one road walked twice" tail is the mechanism announcing itself; the image already lands without it. **Rewrite:**
> "...I want to read what he found against what the instrument finds: two roads that arrive at the same case."

**Minor — "Say the limit before the coincidence gets any further" is the first of six imperative-command paragraph openers scattered through the piece (see cross-cutting finding below). Flagging once here, not repeating at each occurrence.**

### What Hegel Had Actually Read

**Flag — imperative opener.** "Start with the fact that grounds everything else." This is the instructor's voice, not the confiding "I" used two paragraphs later ("I follow him only that far"). **Rewrite:** "One fact grounds everything else."

**Flag — imperative opener, same paragraph type two paragraphs later.** "Be concrete about the mechanism, because Hegel was, and because his source was." **Rewrite:** "Hegel was concrete about the mechanism. So was his source." (Plain declarative does the same work without addressing the reader as "you.")

No antithesis-density problem in this section — it's actually the cleanest section in the piece, and "Waszek's contribution is reception, not citation" is a single, load-bearing use of the device. Leave it.

### The System of Needs, Interwoven

"Hold on the word 'interwoven.'" — I'd keep this one. It echoes the section's own title and sets up a real payoff two sentences later. Not every imperative opener is a tic; this is the device used well. No flag.

### Division of Labor: The Tie Tightens, the Task Hollows

Clean section. The closer — "The natural inference... is the one he does not draw. Neither does the test built to weigh it." — is short and earns its epigram shape by actually doing comparative work (Hegel and the test both decline the same inference), not by running the negation machine. No flag; this is the pattern's good version, worth using as the template for fixing the others.

### The Verdict: External Unity

**Flag — the Verstand paragraph makes the anti-anticipation guard twice, back to back, with two different negation formulas.**
> "But it is an analogue to my result, not an anticipation of it. Hegel's Vernunft is a claim about self-relating spirit, and nothing in the Encyclopaedia treats it as a measure over causal structure. Hegel filed the market under the faculty that fixes and relates without constituting; that filing is the verbal form of my result, not its ancestor."

"Not an anticipation of it" and "not its ancestor" say the same thing twice, bookending a middle sentence that already makes the point. This is the paragraph doing the antithesis machine twice in one breath — worth fixing carefully since the content here is guard-critical (never claim Hegel anticipated Φ). **Rewrite, preserving the guard while cutting the duplicate:**
> "But it is an analogue to my result, not an anticipation of it. Hegel's Vernunft is a claim about self-relating spirit, not a measure over causal structure, and nothing in the Encyclopaedia treats it as one. He filed the market under the faculty that fixes and relates without constituting — the verbal shape of my result, arrived at for reasons that have nothing to do with mine."

**Flag — "Notice what he did not say"** is another imperative opener (see cross-cutting finding), but I'd argue for keeping it: it introduces a genuine pivot (arguing from absence — Hegel never called the market a heap) that the essay needs stated crisply. If you're cutting imperative openers to break the pattern, cut elsewhere and keep this one.

The section closer — "Organization is not wholeness, and Hegel kept that distinction in 1821, where the market's apologists and its harshest critics both tend to lose it" — is a signature line. Don't touch it; the tail clause does real work (contemporary stakes), it isn't decoration.

### The Lab's Version: A Quorum That Reads Everyone

**Flag — self-narrating-rigor relapse.** The v3 sourcing note claims the "say plainly" family of tics was cut. One survived, or crept back:
> "But say plainly how far the receipt reaches. No form in this corpus represents a price or a market scalar directly..."

This is exactly the announced-honesty pattern the house style (and the prior review pass) targets. **Rewrite:**
> "But the receipt only reaches so far. No form in this corpus represents a price or a market scalar directly..."

Otherwise this section is the strongest writing in the piece — the k=1/k=2/k=3 walk-through is plain, numerate, and doesn't reach for antithesis once. The em-dash count is high here (six in two paragraphs) but they're doing real parenthetical-gloss work ("At k = 1 — any single party enough to fire the mediator —") rather than emotional punctuation; I wouldn't cut them.

### Specialization Tightens the Tie and Never Closes the Seam

This section carries the piece's best original move — the liver/heart vs. shoemaker/farmer contrast — and it's the section with the highest concentration of "not X, it is Y" constructions in the draft. I'm not flagging most of them, because the antithesis here is load-bearing: the whole argument *is* a contrast (specific coupling vs. public scalar), and the parallel phrasing across the two paragraphs ("the heart's output is not a public number... it is blood" / "the farmer does not need this shoemaker, he needs shoes") is doing comparative work, not decorating. This is the "liveliest sentences" the brief says not to flag.

**Flag — the section closer restates the point with the same "not X, but Y" shape a third time, immediately after two paragraphs that already used it twice.**
> "Both the criterion and Hegel tell the two apart by the same feature: not how much the parts depend on each other, but whether a common variable can carry the dependence or a specific irreducible constraint must."

By the third iteration the device has stopped surprising. **Rewrite, keeping the content, dropping the negation frame:**
> "Both the criterion and Hegel tell the two apart by the same feature: whether a common variable can carry the dependence, or a specific irreducible constraint has to."

### The Other Register: What Civil Society Does to People

**Flag — imperative opener.** "Read the pages and the ethical charge is unmistakable." Minor; could stay if you're keeping a couple of these, but it's the fifth of six in the piece and this section already has enough momentum from its own content. **Rewrite:** "The ethical charge, once you're in these pages, is unmistakable."

**Flag (highest priority in the whole draft) — the temper paragraph.** This is the passage the prior review round flagged as "one thing only the author can supply," and the sourcing note admits it: *"It is drafted, not his; the author should own it or rewrite it."* As written, it still reads engineered rather than owned — it opens with self-narration, stacks two negations in its first sentence, and at its emotional center drops "I" for "the author":

> "I should say what that costs, because it is not nothing and it is not comfortable. My instrument returns the same verdict on a civil society that feeds its lowest members and on one that grinds them into Hegel's rabble — and the pauperization is the thing Hegel spends the most pages on, the thing that would have mattered most to anyone actually living in the system of needs. There is a real sense in which the test is deaf to the loudest thing in the room. I do not think that makes the structural verdict false; externality and injustice are different facts, and a criterion earns its keep partly by refusing to collapse them. But I would not want the cleanness of 'external unity, aggregate' to read as indifference to which civil society a person has to live in. The measure brackets the pauperization. The author does not, and the bracketing is a cost I am paying for a narrow, decidable answer, not a discovery that the suffering does not matter."

**Rewrite** (keeps every claim, including the guard-critical defense sentence — "a criterion earns its keep partly by refusing to collapse them" — intact and unweakened; drops the self-narrating opener, the third-person lapse, and the trailing double negation):

> "That costs something. The instrument returns the same verdict on a civil society that feeds its lowest members and on one that grinds them into Hegel's rabble. The pauperization is what Hegel spends the most pages on — it's the thing that would have mattered most to anyone actually living in the system of needs — and my test is deaf to it. I don't think that makes the structural verdict false. Externality and injustice are different facts, and a criterion earns its keep partly by refusing to collapse them. But I don't want the cleanness of 'external unity, aggregate' to read as indifference to which civil society a person has to live in. The measure brackets the pauperization. I don't."

Ending on "I don't" is a shorter, quieter landing than the original's trailing "not a discovery that the suffering does not matter" — it says the same thing without the machine.

### Kain and the Remedy in the Other Register

Mostly clean. The Lazarus paragraph closer is the single densest antithesis moment in the draft:
> "Naming Lazarus here is not a concession that the paper's move has been made before; it is the paper marking exactly where its move is not that one."

Three problems stacked in one sentence: two negations bookending an "it is X" pivot (the exact "It is not X. It is Y" form flagged in the brief), and "the paper" as the acting subject instead of "I." **Rewrite:**
> "Naming Lazarus here isn't a concession that this move has been made before. It's me marking exactly where mine isn't that one."

**Minor — "exactly" doublets.** "exactly why Hegel puts the corporation and the state next" (Other Register section) and "exactly why redress has to come from institutions above the market" (this section) are the same intensifier construction twenty lines apart; "exactly as external as before" (temper paragraph) and "exactly as it was" (this section, twice: "leaves the market's formal status... exactly as it was" and again a paragraph later) cluster the same way. Not damaging individually, but the word is doing the same rhythmic job four times in two adjacent sections. Vary one pair — e.g., "leaves the market's formal status untouched" instead of "exactly as it was" on the second occurrence.

### Same Side, Two Boundaries

**Flag (second-highest priority) — the paragraph carrying the piece's most important philosophical guard stacks three negations and repeats language already used in the Verstand section.**
> "His condition is recognition, a claim about self-relating spirit, not a measure over causal structure. It is not higher integrated information, and a causal criterion neither entails it nor stands in for it."

"Not a measure over causal structure" restates, almost verbatim, "nothing in the Encyclopaedia treats it as a measure over causal structure" from the Verstand paragraph four sections earlier — the same guard, stated twice in near-identical words, which the house style calls out as a specific failure mode (say a point once, refer back, don't re-argue it). Combined with two more negations in the same two sentences, this is the most mechanical stretch in the closing section — ironic, since it's carrying the load-bearing guard against claiming Hegel anticipated Φ. **Rewrite, preserving the guard, cutting the repeat and two of the three negations:**
> "His condition is recognition, a claim about self-relating spirit. A causal criterion doesn't entail it and can't stand in for it."

**Flag — the "[adj] enough that A, [adj] enough that B" construction repeats verbatim in shape from the Necessity paragraph five sections earlier** ("strong enough that no one leaves, loose enough that the system re-founds itself") to this section's closer ("small enough to see the agreement through and real enough that mistaking it for identity would falsify both men at once"). Two uses in 4,000 words isn't an emergency, but they land in the two most argumentatively weighty spots in the piece, which makes the echo more noticeable, not less. **Rewrite of the second instance:**
> "the gap between them is small enough to see the agreement through, and it would falsify both men to mistake it for identity"

The very last sentence of the piece — "They do not mean the same thing by the line, and the corporation is the first place that difference starts to cost something..." — should stay untouched. It's forward motion into the next post, not a closing epigram, and it earns its shape.

## Cross-cutting finding: the imperative-opener pattern

Six paragraphs across the piece open with a command aimed at the reader rather than a first-person or declarative sentence: "Say the limit..." / "Start with the fact..." / "Be concrete about the mechanism..." / "Hold on the word..." / "Notice what he did not say." / "Read the pages...". Two of these ("Hold on the word," "Notice what he did not say") are doing real structural work and should stay. The other four are the device running past its welcome — recommend flattening at least two of the remaining four ("Say the limit," "Start with the fact") to plain declaratives, per the rewrites above.

## Findings, ranked most damaging first

1. **Third-person self-lapse in the temper paragraph** ("The author does not") plus the five "this paper is/does" academic-subject constructions elsewhere. This is the single fix that would do the most for the piece — it's a genre bleed, the dissertation's no-first-person convention leaking into a piece explicitly written in "I."
2. **Guard-critical passage stacks three negations and repeats itself** in "Same Side, Two Boundaries" — the anti-anticipation guard gets stated twice across the piece in near-identical words and three times negated in two sentences at its second occurrence.
3. **Section-closing antithesis epigram, run past once per paragraph in three places** — the Verstand paragraph (two guard-restatements), the Lazarus closer (triple-stacked negation, also carries finding 1's "the paper" issue), and the Specialization section's third repetition of a device its own first two uses already spent.
4. **Self-narrating-rigor relapse** — "But say plainly how far the receipt reaches" reintroduces the exact tic the v3 pass reports cutting.
5. **Imperative-opener overuse** — four of six instances are interchangeable and should be thinned to keep the two that earn their place.
6. **Minor "exactly" doublet clustering** in two adjacent sections — cosmetic, lowest priority.

## Biggest genuine strength

The organism/market contrast in "Specialization Tightens the Tie and Never Closes the Seam" — the heart-and-liver vs. shoemaker-and-farmer pairing is the sharpest, most original move in the piece, and it's the one place the essay's argument and its voice are the same thing: concrete nouns doing philosophical work without leaning on a citation. It's also the place with the heaviest antithesis density in the draft, and it should be left almost entirely alone — the device there is structural, not decorative, because the argument itself is a contrast. That's the difference the rest of the review is trying to draw: cut the antithesis machine where it's running on empty, not where it's the argument.
