### "The Verdict: External Unity" — where the sharpest objection lands
Flag (most damaging). The draft writes:

> "Hegel calls it this *in the first instance* — *zunächst* — before the corporation and the state raise it into something its members inhabit; 'external' is his verdict on civil society considered under its first moment, not his last word on it."

"Not his last word" is treated as a hedge; it is actually the whole teleological structure of the *Philosophy of Right*, and leaving it as a hedge is the one place a Hegel referee scores a hit. For Hegel civil society taken by itself is an *abstraction* whose truth lies past it; the same contradiction that makes it external — the rabble above all — is what drives it beyond itself into the corporation and the state. That means the two verdicts do not carry the same modal weight, and the draft never says so. Insert after the `zunächst` sentence, in voice:

> *"And 'not his last word' is carrying more than a hedge. For Hegel the externality is not a resting classification but a moment staged to be overcome: civil society taken by itself is an abstraction whose truth lies past it, and the contradictions that make it external — the rabble first among them — are what push it into the corporation and the state. So the two verdicts do not even weigh the same. My test's 'aggregate' is terminal; it is the whole answer for the case as wired. Hegel's 'external' is provisional by design, a verdict he issues in order to negate. The market is the one case where a terminal verdict and a verdict-staged-for-supersession read the same word off the same object — and that they read it toward opposite ends, mine to close the question and his to open the next moment, is part of what keeps the coincidence from collapsing into identity."*

This is the concrete form of the teleology (Kreines/Ng) objection: Hegel's whole-making category is immanent purposiveness, and his "external" names spirit not-yet-self-related, not a static causal heap. The draft's Verstand guard ("nothing in the Encyclopaedia treats [Vernunft] as a measure over causal structure") already blocks the strong version; the modal-asymmetry paragraph blocks the version that survives it.

The Verstand exposition itself (EL §§79–82, cited to 1991b Hackett) is accurate and paraphrase-only, matching the digest's verification against §80 (fixity of determinations) and §82 (the speculative moment). Note for the record: the gloss collapses Reason into the speculative moment and elides the dialectical moment (b) of §79 — standard and defensible for this register, no fix needed. Dropping the old "inner teleology" phrase from this gloss was correct; keep it out.

## BIGGEST GENUINE STRENGTH

The double-duty analysis of "external" is the real contribution and it is pinned correctly. The draft refuses even the series' own "same boundary, different instrument" formulation and holds the homology one notch safer — **same verdict, two different boundaries** ("The two verdicts land together. The two boundaries do not"), with Hegel's `äußerer`/`Verstand`-self-subsistent sense and the criterion's partition-survivable sense shown to coincide on the market and to *part on the organism* ("An organism's unity is unwilled too, and no cut preserves it"). That organism disanalogy is the load-bearing test: it proves the two senses of "external" are genuinely distinct and their market-agreement is a finding, not a definition. Combined with the temper paragraph — which owns the cost that the test returns the same verdict on a just and a monstrous civil society while explicitly defending the bracketing as "the price I am paying for a narrow, decidable answer, not a discovery that the suffering does not matter" — this is a homology that does real work without ever claiming Hegel prefigured Φ and without making the instrument look hollow. That is the hardest balance in the whole series, and this post holds it.
