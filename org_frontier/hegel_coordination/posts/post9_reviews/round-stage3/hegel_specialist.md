### "What Hegel refuted, and where it stood up" — the placement/incommensurability tension (FINDING 1)

The Logic/Spirit two-ladder distinction is done correctly and is rare to get right: Objectivity (mechanism → chemism → teleology) tops out in the Idea (Life, then Cognition) *inside the Logic*, and recognition is a Spirit category, not a rung of Objectivity. That is exactly right.

The problem is the placement claim. Flagged text:

> "So the first column reads: Hegel wins the classification. The causal criterion sits low on his ladder, his whole means more than irreducibility, and a purely causal test is deaf to purpose, self-maintenance, and self-knowledge."

and, earlier:

> "The answer Hegel forces is: near the bottom, upper mechanism at best."

These assert a single graded ordering with a definite low position. But the chemism paragraph has already denied that such an ordering exists:

> "That is the rung where the two scales cross."

If the scales *cross*, there is no "low on his ladder" simpliciter — a chemically coupled system ranks *higher* on Φ than an organism while ranking *lower* on Hegel. A Hegelian reifies nothing worse than turning the categories of the Concept into a scale of coupling-tightness, which is the very move the draft condemns in the sublation section ("no partition scalar … reaches that immanence"). The draft has cover ("Neither ordering grades by partition-sensitivity"), but the summary sentence drops it. Carry it into the summary.

**Exact rewrite (author voice):**
> "So the first column reads: Hegel wins the classification. His ladder is no scale of integration, so the criterion does not take a rung on it so much as fall below the first rung that counts — it answers to his mechanism and to nothing higher — and his whole means purpose, self-maintenance, and self-knowledge, none of which a cut can hear."

And for the earlier line, change "near the bottom, upper mechanism at best" to "no higher than mechanism, and not even ranked by his ladder — mechanism is just the coarsest thing on it that a cut can answer to."

The Ng handling is strong and correct — inner purposiveness as *the logical form of the living whole, not an observer's lens* is Ng's actual thesis, and HC1's direction-of-explanation point ("the direction of definition running from the whole's purpose down to the part — is what a causal test brackets") already anticipates the Kreines sharpening (the reality of the kind grounding its instances). No need to add Kreines; the objection is met. This is the post's intellectual spine and it holds.
