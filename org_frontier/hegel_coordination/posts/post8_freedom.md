# At Home in the Other

*Roger Hunt · draft v3 (delete this byline before posting). Rebuilt from the five-reviewer panel
(posts/post8_reviews/). The load-bearing fix: the exit/voice mapping is re-routed through the mediator
(Paper 7's necessary/contingent bypass test) instead of through the member — the interchangeable driver
is severable from the platform-whole, so what forecloses his exit is that the mediator is necessary, not
that the whole needs him. The identity is downgraded (coordinative sovereignty is the objective-
institutional condition of being-at-home, not its equivalent — voice is not yet home); the subjective
half is reframed as rational recognition, not affirmation; a temper paragraph is added (flagged below).
Sourcing note at the end.*

---

There is a picture of freedom that says: leave. Whatever holds you, walk away from it, and the walking away
is the freedom. A job that makes demands, a town that expects things, a marriage that asks you to change
your plans — each is a limit, and a free person is one who can always shed the limit and stand clear. Push
the picture to its edge and you get the person who owns nothing he cannot abandon by Friday, joins nothing
he cannot quit, loves no one he cannot leave, and calls the whole arrangement liberty. He is touched by
nothing. He also does nothing, builds nothing, and is no one in particular, because being someone in
particular means being held.

Hegel had a name for that freedom, and it was not a compliment: the freedom of the void. His alternative —
freedom as being at home with oneself in the other, *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* — is the problem my lab
has been circling under the name coordinative sovereignty, the better part of two centuries before we had
the grammar to restate even its institutional half. Freedom, for Hegel, is not what you keep by standing
outside every whole. It is a quality you can only have inside one. What the coordination program adds is
narrow and, I will argue, real: it can read, off a coordination's causal structure, which members have been
stripped of the exit that the freedom-of-the-void story assumes — and it stops there, at the threshold of
the question Hegel actually cared about, which it cannot enter.

## The Freedom of the Void

Start with the freedom Hegel rejects, because the rejection is precise and the target is alive today. In the
*Philosophy of Right*, Hegel describes one side of the will: its power to abstract from every content, to
treat any determination it finds itself in as a mere limit and flee it. The will can always step back from
what it wants, what it has, what it is, and hold itself apart, pure and empty and unbound. Hegel does not
deny this is a real capacity — it is an essential moment of the free will, its universality and negativity.
He denies that it is freedom when it is made the *only* moment, and he names that: "the freedom of the
void," which he says "is raised to the status of an actual shape and passion" (Hegel, 1821/1991a, §5,
Remark).

The void is not an abstraction Hegel reaches for from nowhere; he means a real political actor. When the
flight from every determination becomes a program, he writes, it can only destroy: it tears down whatever
structure exists, suspects everyone attached to any order, and mistakes its own emptiness for purity. He
calls it "the fanaticism of destruction of the whole existing social order" (Hegel, 1821/1991a, §5, Remark).
The fanatic of absolute freedom cannot build an institution, because every institution is a determination,
and determination is the enemy. The will that will not be held ends by leveling.

Hegel traces the same logic to its end in the *Phenomenology*, where "absolute freedom" runs straight into
terror. A freedom that recognizes no institution as its own, that treats every determinate arrangement as an
alien imposition on the general will, has nothing left to do but destroy. "The sole work and deed of
universal freedom is thus death, namely, a death which has no inner amplitude and no inner fulfillment"
(Hegel, 1807/2018, ¶590). Hegel does not let the sentence stay abstract: it is "the coldest, emptiest death
of all, having no more meaning than chopping off a head of cabbage or swallowing a mouthful of water"
(Hegel, 1807/2018, ¶590). The void does not build a better order; it cannot, because building is
determining, and it is at war with determination as such. It clears the ground, again and again, and calls
the clearing freedom.

Set that beside the exit ideal in its modern dress and the fit is uncomfortable. The purest contemporary
version of the freedom of the void is the fantasy of exit as such: the seasteader who will float beyond
every jurisdiction, the crypto-libertarian whose whole political theory is the right to leave, the executive
who frames every obligation as a constraint on optionality. Each treats attachment as a tax on freedom and
detachment as its fulfillment. Hegel's verdict lands on all of them. A freedom whose content is only the
power to sever is a freedom you cannot inhabit, because the moment you commit to anything — a craft, a city,
another person — you have accepted a determination, and by the void's own standard you are less free for it.
The void has to keep leaving. That is the whole of what it can do.

Hegel's move is to deny that determination and freedom are opposites. The will is most free not when it is
emptiest but when it gives itself a content it can recognize as its own and stays with it — wills itself, so
that in the determination it is still with itself rather than lost in it (Hegel, 1821/1991a, §7). Freedom is
not the absence of a limit. It is a relation to one, the relation where the limit is yours, and holding it
is how you are you.

## Being at Home in the Other

The relation has a name, and it is Hegel's most compressed statement of the structure of freedom (see Roy,
2006, on the phrase). In the *Encyclopaedia Logic*: "freedom is precisely this: to be at home with oneself
in one's other, to be dependent upon oneself, to be the determining factor for oneself" (Hegel, 1830/1991b,
§24 Addition 2). And a
line on: "Freedom exists only where there is no other for me that I am not myself" (Hegel, 1830/1991b, §24
Addition 2). Read those slowly. Freedom is a particular way of standing toward an other — being with
yourself in it, so that what looks from outside like a limit is, from inside, an extension of you. It is the
right relation to an other, not the absence of one.

Ordinarily we think dependence and freedom trade off: the more I am bound up with something outside me, the
less free I am, because now my state turns on what I do not control. Hegel says that at a low level this is
right and at the highest level it inverts. There is a kind of other in which I am not diminished but
completed, where depending on it is not a subtraction from my agency but its exercise. In that other I am at
home. The German idiom carries it: *bei sich* is being-at-home, the way you are in your own house,
unguarded, yourself. Freedom is being at home while not being alone.

Hegel's cases are not metaphysical; they are friendship and love. In the Addition to §7:

> we already possess this freedom in the form of feeling, for example in friendship and love. Here, we are
> not one-sidedly within ourselves, but willingly limit ourselves with reference to an other, even while
> knowing ourselves in this limitation as ourselves. (Hegel, 1821/1991a, §7 Addition)

The friend who reorganizes his week around yours has not lost a slice of his freedom to you. He is doing
something he could not do alone — being a friend — and the limit is the medium of it, not the cost. In the
account of the family Hegel is blunter: love is "the consciousness of my unity with another," in which "I am
not isolated for myself … but win my self-consciousness only through the giving-up of my independence"
(Hegel, 1821/1991a, §158 Addition). Hegel makes the point in the register of exit himself: marriage, he
insists, is not a contract (§75, §163), precisely because a contract binds separable persons who could walk
away, and spouses are no longer separable in that sense. The severability a partition test measures is the
thing Hegel already knew marriage had left behind.

Hold the structure apart from the warm examples, because it does cold work later. Being-at-home-in-the-other
has two components — one objective, that you are genuinely in an other, bound to it, not standing clear; one
subjective, that you recognize the bond as your own and are at home in it rather than trapped by it. (The
two-part split is a reconstruction, Neuhouser's more than Hegel's own; Hegel states it as a unity.) The
friend and the spouse have both, which is why they are examples of freedom and not of captivity. And that is
the sharp point, not a neutral one: for Hegel the two halves come apart only in *defective* institutions,
and their coming-apart is what alienation *is* — being in the other without being at home in it. Almost
everything hard about applying this to the wholes my lab studies turns on the fact that the objective
component can hold while the subjective one fails, and that this failure, for Hegel, is not a gap in a
measurement but the presence of unfreedom.

## Freedom Lives in Institutions, or It Does Not Live

Hegel does not leave freedom in the private warmth of love. His largest claim is that freedom is real only
when it is built — carried by institutions that outlast any individual's mood, not felt as an inner state.
The *Philosophy of Right* argues that a will is actually free only when it lives inside a family, a civil
society, and a state whose arrangements give its freedom a standing shape. Patten (1999) put it cleanly: for
Hegel freedom is not a condition of the solitary inner self but something realized and sustained through
participation in rational institutions. You do not become free first, privately, and then optionally social.
You become free by being a member of the right kind of whole.

Which whole is the right kind is Hegel's whole question, and his formula at the level of the state is worth
setting beside my own work. "The state is the actuality of concrete freedom" (Hegel, 1821/1991a, §260), and
concrete freedom consists, on Hegel's account, in personal individuality and its particular interests
reaching full development and winning recognition of their right, while at the same time passing over of
their own accord into the interest of the universal, known and willed as the individual's own. The condition
is a demand on the institution: a whole realizes freedom only if its members can both pursue their own ends
and find their own reason in the whole's ends — not sacrificed to it, not merely useful to it, but at home
in it.

Neuhouser (2000) drew the standard out of Hegel and made it a test, and his version is the rival I have to
reckon with most carefully. He reconstructs Hegel's "social freedom" as the criterion by which the family,
civil society, and the state count as rational at all. An institution is rational, on his reading, when it
meets two conditions together: it must furnish the objective preconditions of its members' freedom, and its
members must be able to *recognize themselves* in it — to grasp its ends as rational and, understanding
them, endorse them as their own. Not that they happen to approve of it; that they could, seeing what it is,
be at home in it. Both, or the institution fails the test. Neuhouser's Hegel does not certify a whole as
free because it is powerful, or irreducible, or because everyone in fact depends on it.

That two-part standard is where Hegel's freedom and my instrument meet, and it is where they fail to be the
same thing. The first condition is objective and structural — the kind of thing you might hope to measure.
The second is recognitive: whether members can find their own reason in the whole's ends. It is not a
preference you could poll; it is a question about the whole's rational content and the members' warranted
recognition of it, and both lie past what a partition of a causal structure can see. That gap is where the
reading finally stops, and the co-op and the warehouse below are where it stops hardest.

## Coordinative Sovereignty and the Objective Half

Here is why I care, beyond the pleasure of reading Hegel. For a couple of years my lab has been working out
a concept we call coordinative sovereignty, and it answers the same problem *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen*
answers, for a platform-mediated world, though it reaches only one half of it. Coordinative sovereignty is
"the autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated — standing and effective voice within a mediation one
cannot leave, rather than independence from it or control over it" (coordinative_sovereignty/README.md). It
is deliberately not a claim about who owns the pipes: "Digital sovereignty asks who owns the infrastructure;
coordinative sovereignty asks what autonomy the actor coordinating through it can have"
(coordinative_sovereignty/README.md). Its currency is "voice, made effective — not the chance to speak, but
terms that hold because the coordinated actors have the standing to make them hold"
(coordinative_sovereignty.md). Hold onto that word, *voice*: it is the objective-institutional condition of
being-at-home — a share in how the terms get set — and it is not yet being-at-home itself. A member can have
voice and be estranged, as a rabble with formal standing is estranged; a member can be at home with little
formal voice, as in the family. Voice is the form the demand for the subjective half has to take once exit
is foreclosed, not the subjective half achieved.

The setup is this. More and more of what people do runs through mediators they cannot leave — the platform
that is the labor market for a driver, the app store that is the only road to a billion phones, the payment
rail every merchant clears through. These are not conveniences you take or leave; they are necessary
mediators, and removing one does not route the coordination around it. It collapses. Here the old picture of
freedom — independence, standing clear, exit — does what Hegel said the freedom of the void does. It fails.
Telling a gig driver that his freedom lies in his right to quit the platform is telling him his freedom lies
in leaving the only market there is. Independence, demanded of a necessary mediation, leaves you not
sovereign but stranded.

So the coordinative-sovereignty claim is: for a member of an irreducible coordination, the freedom worth
arguing about cannot be exit. It has to be something available while in — standing, effective voice, a share
in how the terms get set. That claim is the *objective-institutional* correlate of Hegel's turn against the
void: it says where being-at-home would have to be built, for someone who cannot leave, without pretending
the building is done. The program states the hinge in its own vocabulary — "exit where a gate is contingent,
voice where a mediator is necessary" (coordinative_sovereignty/README.md) — which is Paper 7's
necessary/contingent distinction, and it is the distinction this post now makes causal.

## Exit and Voice, Made Causal

The mapping is narrower than I first thought, and one piece of it is new. Hirschman (1970) gave the
twentieth century its cleanest vocabulary for what a member can do about a whole that is failing him: exit —
leave, take his business elsewhere — or voice — stay and agitate to change it from inside. Loyalty is what
holds him in long enough to use voice instead of bolting. His dichotomy has been mapped onto almost
everything, and one mapping is already established: exit and voice line up with Berlin's (1958/1969) negative
and positive liberty, and readers have made that pairing before. What appears unmade is narrower — the
mapping of Hegel's freedom of the void and concrete freedom onto exit and voice specifically, done causally.
Recognition theorists link Hegelian freedom to membership without going through Hirschman (Honneth, 1995);
Hirschman's dichotomy migrates into democratic theory without crossing into Hegel's two freedoms.

The causal apparatus that tells exit from voice is not the partition test on its own — which grades whether a
coordination is a whole — but the bypass counterfactual laid on top of it, the operation Paper 7 was built
around: it grades whether the mediator a member routes through can be gone around. Exit is real when the
thing between a member and what he needs is a *contingent* gate: open the bypass and he reaches the market
directly, so leaving costs him nothing he cannot replace. Exit is a fiction when the mediator is *necessary*
— when no bypass restores a direct tie, because the mediator is the market and routing around it routes to
nowhere. Then leaving is either impossible or self-destroying, and the only leverage left is from inside.
That is voice. Loyalty, in this reading, is not sentiment; it is whatever holds the member in long enough
that voice is his live option and exit is not — the switching costs, the shared history, the sunk
investment, the norm.

Two cases make the difference concrete, and they turn on the mediator, not the member. A shopper at one of
five competing grocery stores routes through a *contingent* gate: strike this store and she reaches the same
milk down the road, so her exit is real and the threat to leave gives her whatever leverage she has. The
driver whose whole livelihood clears through one ride-hail app routes through a *necessary* one: the app is
the market, and no bypass reaches around it to the riders. Notice what this does and does not say about the
driver. The platform runs on without him — one interchangeable worker out of a pool built to lose them — so
on the causal structure of the platform he is severable, a part the whole does not need. What makes his exit
empty is not the platform's dependence on him; it is that he has no other market, and the mediator between
him and the one market cannot be gone around. Same word, "leave," and it names a freedom for the shopper and
a fiction for the driver — because her mediator is contingent and his is necessary.

Lay Hegel over the pattern. The freedom of the void is the freedom of exit absolutized: the standing option
to sever, treated as freedom itself. It works only where the mediator is contingent — where a bypass exists
and leaving costs nothing you cannot replace. Where the mediator is necessary, that freedom is gone, and the
only freedom left to *ask about* is the concrete kind, being-at-home-in-the-other. Its only available form is
voice — but voice is not yet that freedom; it is the shape the demand for it has to take once leaving is off
the table. Whether the member is actually at home in the coordination, or only trapped in it, is the further
question, and the bypass test does not reach it. What the test reads is a fact about the mediation — that it
is Paper 7's necessary/contingent verdict, run on stylized models so far and, as the next section concedes,
on no firm yet.

## Why This Is Not Just Berlin Again

The mapping earns its keep only if it says more than Berlin's (1958/1969) "Two Concepts of Liberty" already
says. Berlin split freedom into a negative kind — the absence of interference — and a positive kind —
self-mastery. Hegel's void and concrete freedom slot into that pair so neatly that a reader could call this a
synonym. If the exit/voice mapping is only Berlin restated, it hands the whole thing back to the normative
camp, because Berlin sorts two things worth wanting, weighed against each other, and the program is supposed
to do something the normative readings cannot.

What keeps it from Berlin is that the axis is not a value axis and, more than that, it is *computed*. Berlin
sorts freedoms by what they are worth. The exit/voice split sorts members by whether the mediator they route
through is contingent or necessary — the bypass margin, zero or full, that Paper 7 reads off the
cause-and-effect structure. It does not say voice is nobler than exit, or the reverse. It says exit is on the
table where the mediator is contingent and off it where the mediator is necessary, before anyone decides
what to prize. A libertarian who values exit above everything and a communitarian who values voice above
everything face the same verdict when they look at a gig driver: his mediator is necessary, so the exit both
are arguing about is, for him, not available. The reading sits upstream of the value quarrel. It tells you
which freedoms a member can have and leaves the ranking to the reader — and it does so with an operation, not
a relabeling, which is the whole of what "made causal" buys.

## The Co-op and the Cage

Now the danger, and it is the reason this post could go badly wrong. Everything so far tempts a conclusion
that is false and flattering: that if the program can find the wholes no partition severs, it has found the
wholes where members are at home, the free ones. That would be wonderful for the platforms and it is not
true. A whole can be irreducible and estranging, and the closing of an exit is not being-at-home; it is
often the opposite.

Take the case that hurts, and read it carefully, because the naive reading gets even the structure wrong.
An Amazon warehouse worker is bound the way the driver is bound, and for the same reason — not because the
operation could not run without him, since it replaces him by the next shift, but because the operation is
the only market for his labor and no bypass reaches around it. His exit ends everything for him and nothing
for it. He is a member in the strong sense the earlier papers name, inside a coordination he cannot leave,
and he is not at home. He has Hegel's objective condition and lacks the subjective one, and that gap — the
being-in without the being-at-home — is, in Hegel's own terms, not a missing datum but alienation itself.
The instrument reads the mediation and returns "no bypass." It cannot read the estrangement. It would return
the same verdict for the warehouse and for a marriage, and the difference between them — the whole
difference Hegel cared about — is nothing a partition sees.

Put a courier-member of a platform cooperative like CoopCycle beside the warehouse worker and the instrument
fails to tell them apart. In a real cooperative the members are bound as tightly: the enterprise is theirs,
their livelihoods run through it, walking out costs them what it costs the warehouse worker. On the criterion
the two would read as twins — the same necessary mediator, the same closed exit. But one is governed by its
members and the other governs them, and that is the difference between an institution that *could* be a home
and one that structurally cannot. Even the co-op is only a candidate: member governance is the condition
under which being-at-home becomes possible, not proof that the members are at home, and a co-op pursuing
irrational ends or one its members despise has the condition and not the achievement. What the co-op has that
the warehouse lacks is not yet freedom; it is the standing where freedom could grow — the ethical shape
Hegel gave the *Korporation*, the trade organized into a body whose members have their recognition in it
(§§250–256). The measure reads the mediator in both and returns the same verdict, because the thing that
separates them — rational content, and whether the members can recognize their own reason in the whole's
ends — is what a partition does not see. A test that cannot tell a co-op from a warehouse is not measuring
freedom. It reads whether the mediator can be gone around, and returns "it cannot" for both — the closing of
the exit, which is freedom's precondition and not freedom.

This is where Neuhouser's two-condition standard comes back to collect. His Hegel certifies an institution as
free only when it furnishes the objective preconditions of freedom *and* its members can recognize
themselves in it. The bypass test reads something adjacent to the first condition and is silent on the
second. It can tell you a member is bound into a whole he cannot leave. It cannot tell you whether the whole
gives him his freedom or takes it — whether he is a spouse or a serf. That second question, for Hegel, is not
a footnote to freedom. It is freedom. A reviewer holding Neuhouser can say, correctly, that the causal
criterion operationalizes at most the objective half of Hegel's test and drops the half that does the
ethical work.

And here I want to stop being the instrument's advocate. I think that concession is right, and it is the part
of this reading I am least easy about. I built a test to tell a genuine whole from a heap, and it hands the
same verdict to the marriage and the sweatshop — it is most confident about a coordination exactly where
Hegel would have been most alarmed by it. The tool reads the closing of the exit, which is the precondition
of the thing, and goes silent on the thing; and the case where that silence is worst — the warehouse that is
a cage and reads like the co-op that could be a home — is not a corner case but the one that matters most for
what the platforms are doing to people now. I do not think the silence makes the verdict false; the exit
really is closed, and a test that reads the closing should say so. But it costs me something to have built an
instrument whose competence runs out exactly at the border of freedom, and I would rather name the cost than
let a clean verdict read as a clean conscience.

The scope is narrow, and I should say it in my own voice: I have run no firm through the classifier for this.
The program's own accounting is direct — the operationalization "introduces no new computation," and "the
bridge to organizations is unbuilt: the parties are nodes in a Boolean model, and standing is explored on
those models, not measured on any firm" (instruments/formal_standing.md). The App Store developer, the
Amazon Marketplace seller, the CoopCycle courier-member, the Som Mobilitat member are cases classified
against the criterion, populated cells in a taxonomy, not systems run through Φ. The warehouse-versus-co-op
contrast is an interpretive application of the criterion to two real arrangements, not a computed result
about either.

What survives is narrower than the temptation in either direction. The program does not certify freedom and
does not measure being-at-home. It locates, in the wiring, the members for whom the question of being-at-home
becomes urgent and exit stops being an answer. Where the mediator is contingent, Hegel's void is a live
option and the liberal story of freedom-as-exit still runs. Where it is necessary, that story is finished,
the driver is in the whole for good, and the only freedom left to argue about is the Hegelian one — voice,
standing, recognition within a coordination he cannot leave. The instrument does not answer that argument. It
tells you when you are in it. That is not nothing, because the loudest current answer to the gig driver is
still "you're free, you can quit," and the bypass test is what shows that answer to be the freedom of the
void — a real severance, available to no one who matters, worth exactly what leaving the only market there is
is worth.

Coordinative sovereignty, then, is not a property my instrument confers on the wholes it finds irreducible.
It is a demand made on them — the demand that a coordination no one can leave owe its members voice, since it
has taken their exit. Hegel grounds the demand in the rational content of the institution, in whether its
members can recognize themselves in it. I cannot measure that. What I can measure is when the demand comes
due: when the mediator has closed the exit and thrown the whole weight of freedom onto voice. Hold that line,
and the reading is a tool for finding where the political question lives. Drop it, let irreducibility stand
in for being-at-home, and the same reading becomes a certificate of contentment for every whole strong enough
to trap the people inside it. The line is the difference between a diagnosis and an apology, and it runs
where Hegel drew it: between being in an other and being at home in it.

## Freedom in the Whole

Hegel's freedom is a quality of membership, not a distance from it. You are free not when you have escaped
every whole but when you are inside the right one and can recognize it as your own — at home in the other,
depending on it and thereby on yourself. The coordination program can do one honest thing with that claim and
must refuse a second. It can read, off the causal structure, which members route through a contingent
mediator and can still leave, and which route through a necessary one and are in for good — and so tell where
exit remains a freedom from where only voice does, before anyone argues about which to prize. It cannot read
whether a member who is in for good is at home or trapped, and so it cannot, on its own, tell a Hegelian
whole from a well-built cage.

The instrument draws the line between the members who can leave and the members who cannot. Which of those
unleaveable wholes is a home and which is a cage, it cannot say — and that, not the drawing of the line, is
where freedom actually lives. Hegel would have said the sorting is only the doorway to the question of
freedom, not the room where it is decided. He would have been right. But it is the doorway, and until now no
instrument had found it.
