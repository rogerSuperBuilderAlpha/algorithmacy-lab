## Verdict

**Major revisions — narrowly.** The prose is publishable and the philosophical spine is sound; the formal
bridge is not yet, and the repairs are localized (about a page of prose, no restructuring). **The single
most important fix:** restore, in the body this time, the honesty flag that v1 carried in its sourcing note
— "the bridge is an analogy at the level of the constraint operator, not a computed classification of this
passage" — and stop asserting cell membership in the indicative. v3 dropped that sentence from the sourcing
note, never had it in the text, and meanwhile asserts three times that the classification has effectively
been made ("the master–slave binding sits in the contingent cell," line 226; "In the program's terms, that
is a contingent broker," line 277; "exactly what the test would classify as a constraint-held conduit,"
line 338). No model of the passage has been built or scored. The program's own essay says "naming its edge
is part of placing it" — the taxonomy is for triads of mediated flow, and this paper crosses that edge
without naming it, in violation of the program's own stated practice.

## Part 1 — Formal rigor and argument structure

### Section-by-section

**Opening + framing (lines 8–34).** The narrative opening is a setup, not a buried lead — it earns the
constitution/dissolution split in paragraph three and should stay. One formal worry seeded here: "a binding
that is held, that registers on a crude test as real" (line 29). At this point "crude test" reads as an
intuitive check ("are they bound? plainly"). But at line 245 the paper concedes that the program's own
partition test would also register the held binding as real. A reader who connects the two will conclude
the partition test is the crude test that force fools. That is an accidental instrument demotion, and it is
avoidable: the partition test is not fooled when it reads a coerced binding as integrated — coercion is
causation, and present integration is exactly what the test measures. What the partition test does not
read, because it is not asked, is what the integration rides on. Keep "crude test" for the folk check if
you like, but the later text must make the is-not-fooled point explicitly (see Part 3, R5).

**Recognition Makes a Two-Person Whole (36–72).** The criterion gloss is the load-bearing formal sentence
of the section: "A whole, in the program's sense, is a configuration whose parts are members of one
another: no way of cutting the system leaves what it does intact on either side of the cut" (62–64). Two
drifts, one of which matters. (1) "What it does" is behavioral language, and IIT's integration is not about
preserved behavior. The partition comparison in Oizumi et al. (2014) is over the system's cause–effect
structure — the differences the parts make to one another — not over its input–output function. The
distinction bites in exactly the direction this paper cares about: a feed-forward mimic can do the same
thing as an integrated system and carry Φ = 0. "Leaves what it does intact" therefore glosses integration
as functional robustness, which is the fragility/robustness drift this series has been warned off before.
The fix is one clause: cut the system and it no longer makes the same differences to itself. (2) "On
either side of the cut" mildly misstates the procedure — Φ is evaluated on the whole system under the
partition, not by inspecting each side separately. Minor; fold into the same rewrite. The citations are
apt as deployed: Oizumi et al. (2014) is the right anchor for the integration measure (IIT 3.0 is where
the MIP-over-cause-effect-structure formulation lives), and Christensen (2024) is the right anchor for
reading that formalism at the scale of social structure, which is precisely what the parenthetical claims
and no more. The paragraph's insistence that the causal test and the recognition criterion are "not the
same test" is exactly right and well placed; it is the discipline the rest of the paper strains against.

**Why You Keep the Other Alive (74–98).** Formally clean. The death case is correctly staged as the limit
the dissolution theorem approaches — this pays off later and the paper uses it well. No specialist wince.

**The Dissolution Theorem (100–137).** The Hegel exposition is accurate and the quotations are the
verified Pinkard wording per the sourcing note (I did not re-litigate a documented adversarial
verification run; the run's residue is honestly listed). The de-dramatized restatement (131–136) is the
best paragraph in the paper. One formal observation for later use: "The binding is real in the sense that
it holds. It is empty in the sense that it fails to do what it was for" is already the two-register
distinction (causal reality vs. normative worth) that the bridge section needs; when the bridge wobbles,
it wobbles by forgetting this paragraph got it right.

**A Century of Readers / The Structural Reading Is Mainstream (139–198).** The anti-novelty section is the
paper's structural insurance and it works: seven readers, incompatible programs, one verdict, and the
explicit concession that the program "cannot claim to have discovered that domination-held bindings
self-destruct." The narrowed contribution — "a general class and a decision procedure, not a new result" —
is the right size. One prior-art gap, though (see F7 below): the *general-class* move itself has a
precedent the paper does not cite. Grammar: "Before saying what a causal program can add, the ground here
is crowded" (140) is a dangling modifier — the ground is not saying anything.

**A Binding That Is Held but Not Irreducible (200–251).** This is where the formal findings concentrate;
they are ranked below (F1–F4). Briefly, section by claim: the construct exposition (broker, restore-the-tie
counterfactual, car dealer, contingent irreducibility) is faithful to the lab essay. The transfer to
master/slave is where the strain lives — the intervention is mis-specified (F2), the cell assignment is
asserted rather than analogized (F1), and the self-liquidating-broker mapping overclaims precision (F4).
The truthmaker paragraph (241–251) is the paper's conscience and mostly does its job: "This bridge does not
show that Hegel was measuring causal irreducibility" keeps the never-claim-Hegel-anticipated-Φ guard
intact. The sentence "A domination-held binding could score perfectly stable on a causal partition test for
as long as the coercion held" (245–246) makes formal sense in substance and errs in wording: substance
first — yes, a coerced dyad is genuinely integrated on Φ-style measures while the coercion holds. Model it
and see: the master's command states constrain the slave's action states; the slave's labor and
acknowledgment states constrain the master's consumption and status states; fear is a mechanism like any
other. Every partition of that system destroys cause–effect information in both directions, so Φ > 0. The
test is valence-blind, and that blindness is not a defect; it is the division of labor the paper's own
truthmaker paragraph defends. The wording error: "perfectly stable" is not a thing the partition test
scores. The test reads present irreducibility, a synchronic property; stability is a dynamical/durability
property that lives in a different layer of the program (the constraint-durability study). Say "score as
integrated," not "score perfectly stable" (Part 3, R3).

**The Objections the Causal Reading Inherits (253–315).** Philosophically the strongest section — Wood,
Fanon, Cunniff, and Saunders are each given their best lines, and the paper takes the hits rather than
dodging them. Two formal slips inside it. First, the Fanon paragraph's program-vocabulary gloss (277–279):
"a contingent broker that is perfectly stable while its constraint holds, doing the contingent job of
extracting labor." Three problems in one clause: (a) "perfectly stable while its constraint holds" again
conflates class with durability — in the program's terms this case is *contingent with a durable
constraint*, and near-tautological as written (everything contingent holds while its constraint holds; that
is what contingent means); (b) the broker in this casting is the slave, and the slave does not extract
labor — the master extracts it, the slave performs it; the broker's "job" here is relaying the world's
goods, worked, to the master; (c) "in the program's terms" again presents an unbuilt model as a reading.
Second, "severed at no cost to either party's genuine coordination, because there was no genuine
coordination" (228–229, and its echo here) misstates the program's own accounting. In the lab essay, the
contingent cell carries the *full Φ as its margin*: when the bypass opens, the system sheds all its
integration. The cost of severing is the whole of the measured integration — the program's point is that
what is lost was constraint-held rent, not earned work, not that nothing is lost. And "genuine" is a
normative word doing causal work: causally there *is* coordination in the coerced dyad (that is why it
scores as integrated two sections later); what it lacks is integration that survives the constraint. The
current wording has the paper contradicting its own line 245.

**What a Causal Test Can Still Ask (317–340).** The one earned move is real, but the paper attributes it to
the wrong operation and overstates what the rival cannot do — see F2 and F5. Also the closing "That is the
one thing a low instrument can ask a high theory" (338–339): "low" is rank language, and rank language is
the road to "a calculator." The instrument is not lower than recognition theory; it works in a different
register (causal, not normative). Recast as register, not altitude (Part 3, R7).

**Filing, Not Finding (342–364).** The verdict is the sanctioned one and lands well. "Hegel drew it first
and hardest at the place where the constraint is another person's freedom" (357–358) stays on the right
side of the anticipation guard: drawing the structural signature first is not anticipating the formalism,
and the paper has already disclaimed the stronger reading twice. One missed opportunity here (F9): the
paper notes glancingly that Hegel's case "worse than collapses — it inverts" (225), and the inversion is
precisely what the four-cell state machine cannot represent. Opening the bypass on a contingent broker
yields a residual dyad (the manufacturer and buyer still trade); freeing the slave yields no residual dyad
and an asymmetric endowment — the slave walking toward independence, the master toward atrophy. The
instrument has no cell for inversion. Owning that explicitly would be the strongest possible warrant for
"filing, not finding": the filing captures the collapse; the inversion is Hegel's surplus that the filing
cannot file.

### Ranked findings

**F1 (most damaging). The v1 honesty flag has quietly disappeared, and the paper now asserts what it once
flagged as analogy.** Verified in git: v1 (commit 4c8184b) closed its lab-bridge note with "the instrument
proper is built for mediated-flow triads, so the bridge is an analogy at the level of the constraint
operator, not a computed classification of this passage. Flagged as such in the text." v3's sourcing note
(lines 475–479) retains the construct list but drops that sentence entirely, and no equivalent appears in
the body — the term "constraint operator" itself now survives only in the sourcing note (line 475), never
in the text. Meanwhile the body asserts cell membership in the indicative three times (lines 226, 277,
338). The mapping is defensible *as a construal* — the Φ formalism is agnostic about what a node is, so
casting the world as the third node is legitimate in Christensen's terms even though it strains the
Burt/Obstfeld vocabulary, where all three positions are actors — but a construal must be named as one, the
cell placements must be conditional ("would sit," "would classify") until a model is built and scored, and
the paper must say that none has been. This is not a hedge for humility's sake; it is the difference
between the paper's two guards holding and failing. Fix: one body paragraph (Part 3, R1) plus mood changes
at the three assertion sites.

**F2. The paper conflates the partition test with the restore-the-tie counterfactual, and the conflation is
load-bearing in the "one earned move" section.** These are distinct operations and the paper defines only
one of them. Line 243 defines "the program's test" as the partition test: "reads a cause-and-effect
structure and asks which partition it cannot survive." That operation, run on the arrangement as it stands,
reads a coerced binding as integrated — the paper says so itself at 245. It therefore *cannot* "sort a held
binding by what would survive the lifting of the force" (332), because both of the dominations the section
distinguishes (real-work-holding and gate-only) score as integrated while the force holds. The sorting is
done by the classifier's counterfactual: intervene on the constraint, re-run the partition test on the
modified system, and compare the two readings. The contingency margin is the Φ lost between them. One
operation is synchronic and reads the system as it is; the other is an intervention plus two synchronic
readings. The paper's "one earned move" is real, but it belongs to the second operation, and the section
credits it to the first. The fix costs three sentences (Part 3, R4) and the paper is better for them,
because the two-step structure is exactly what a recognition account lacks.

**F3. "Lifting the coercion" is not the dealer's counterfactual, and the paper says it is.** "Run the
counterfactual the program runs on the dealer. Lift the coercion. Free the slave" (222–223). But the
dealer's counterfactual restores a forbidden edge between the *outer* parties — strike the franchise law,
let manufacturer and buyer meet. Nothing in the master/slave configuration forbids the master from dealing
with the world directly; he always could, and his project is why he does not. The coercion instead compels
the *middle* party — it holds the slave on the mediating edge. So the intervention the paper actually runs
(free the slave) is mechanism-change on the middle node, not edge-restoration between the endpoints. These
are different operations in the program's own state machine: opening the bypass is one named transition;
removing the compulsion that holds a party in place is the reversal of "erecting a constraint," a different
edge of the machine. The two are adjacent — each deletes a constraint and reads what remains — and the
analogy survives at that level, which is exactly the level v1's flag named ("at the level of the constraint
operator"). But "the counterfactual the program runs on the dealer" is false as written. Note also that the
true bypass counterfactual (give the master a direct world-tie and ask whether the slave stays in the core)
yields a different and less Hegelian result: the slave drops out because the master could farm, which is a
story about substitutability of labor, not about the worthlessness of coerced recognition. The paper should
run the honest operation and say which one it is (Part 3, R2).

**F4. The self-liquidating-broker mapping overclaims precision, and the two bridges cast the roles
incompatibly.** Bridge one makes the *slave* the third party (broker between master and world). Bridge two
makes the *master* the self-liquidator — but the master occupies no brokerage position at all; he is an
endpoint. Obstfeld's (2005) *tertius iungens* liquidates itself by a specific structural operation:
completing the join creates the direct tie, the bypass, which writes the broker out of the core
(edge-creation; in the lab essay's terms, iungens-carried-to-completion reads *reducible*). The master's
project liquidates by a different operation entirely: degrading a party toward thinghood until there is no
recognizer left (node-deletion, the death case). Different failure modes, different state-machine edges,
different objects failing (the broker exits the core; the master's binding loses a member). What the two
share is the schema — a project whose completion removes the precondition of its own payoff — and that
schema is genuinely illuminating, but it is weaker than "self-liquidating in precisely that way, with the
signs reversed" (236–237). "Precisely" plus "signs reversed" performs a formal identity the constructs do
not support. Two casting choices in one paper are fine for analogies and fatal for classification talk;
one more reason F1's flag must return. Soften to the schema and name what differs (Part 3, R6).

**F5. "The question a recognition account... cannot pose" (353–354) overclaims; the account cannot *decide*
it.** Abolitionists, historians, and every recognition theorist worth the name have posed the question of
what would survive emancipation; posing it requires no partition. What the recognition account lacks is a
decision procedure — a formal object on which the counterfactual can be run and a margin read off. The
program's contribution is the computability, and the paper's own framing elsewhere ("a general class and a
decision procedure," line 33) already says so. Same fix at the parallel site in "What a Causal Test Can
Still Ask" ("What it cannot do is sort..." is fine — "sort" is a decision word — but the section must then
attribute the sorting to the right operation per F2).

**F6. The criterion gloss drifts functional.** Detailed above under the section pass; the fix is R8. Also
covered there: Oizumi et al. (2014) and Christensen (2024) are apt for exactly what is claimed of them; no
citation inflation detected on the formal side.

**F7. Prior-art check on the "general class" claim: Elster.** The paper claims the program contributes "a
general class the result turns out to be one instance of" (197–198). Jon Elster's *Sour Grapes* (1983),
chapter II, built a general class with Hegel's case in its extension four decades ago: states that are
essentially by-products — states (esteem, love, recognition among them) that cannot be brought about by
command or force because the manner of production destroys the product. Coerced recognition being worthless
is a textbook instance of Elster's class. The novelty claim survives, but only narrowed: Elster's class is
*intentional* (what cannot be produced at will), the program's is *structural* (what does not survive the
constraint's removal), and the program adds a decision procedure and a margin where Elster has a category.
The paper should cite Elster and draw that line, or a referee who knows *Sour Grapes* will draw it less
charitably. (Flagged as check-against-source: verify the chapter and wording against a copy before citing;
I am confident of the construct and its Hegelian instance, and per this harness's own rule the exact
locators must be verified, not trusted to a reviewer's memory — including mine.)

**F8. Instrument-demotion watch: two sites, both borderline, both fixable in a clause.** (a) The "crude
test" at line 29 merging, via line 245, into the partition test (detailed in the section pass; fix R5
makes the is-not-fooled point). (b) "A low instrument can ask a high theory" (338–339): rank language;
recast as register difference (R7). Elsewhere the paper's modesty is the sanctioned kind — "small and, I
think, still worth the trouble" and "filing, not finding" state what the work does without trading the
tool away.

**F9. The inversion is the bridge's honest limit, and the paper walks past it.** Detailed under "Filing,
Not Finding" above. The contingent cell predicts collapse to a residual dyad; Hegel predicts inversion —
asymmetric endowments, the slave's formation through work and fear, the master's atrophy. The four-cell
machine has no state for that. Two sentences owning it would strengthen the verdict the paper already
reaches (optional but high-value; R9).

**F10 (minor).** Dangling modifier at line 140 (R10). And "separated by two centuries and one operation"
(251) is now correct — v1's "twenty centuries" was wrong and v3 fixed it; noted so nobody "fixes" it back.

**Load-bearing weak points a hostile referee attacks first:** (1) the unflagged two-party-to-triad transfer
(F1+F3) — "you built an instrument for triads, ran it on a dyad by fiat, and reported the output as a
classification"; (2) the operation conflation (F2) — "your one earned move against the normative readers is
credited to a test that cannot make it." Both survive *if fixed as above*; neither survives as written.

## Part 2 — Slop audit (register-aware)

The v3 tone pass did real work; this is a clean draft by the corpus's standards. Counts, body only
(5,234 words, lines 1–364):

- **Em-dashes: 46 (~8.8 per 1,000 words).** High even under the permissive house rule. The good uses —
  naming a coined construct ("what Obstfeld (2005) calls the *tertius iungens* orientation... — succeeds by
  making itself unnecessary," 233–235) and staged reveals ("and worse than collapses — it inverts," 225) —
  should all stay. The crutches to convert: the paired dash-parenthetical bolting a clause mid-sentence,
  e.g. "Take a third that sits in the middle of a coordination — a broker between two others, in the sense
  the network literature has studied since Simmel's *tertius* and Burt's (1992) structural holes — and ask
  why it is there" (206–208; the aside is citational, parentheses do it better), and "The relation lacks
  the reciprocity that genuine recognition requires — the master does not do with regard to himself what he
  demands the slave do — and so:" (111–112; split it). Converting four or five pairs brings the count under
  control without touching a load-bearing dash.
- **Antithesis density: ~11 explicit ", not X"/"rather than" forms plus a handful of "It is not X. It is Y"
  — roughly 3 per 1,000 words overall, inside budget, but clustered.** The closing two sections stack them
  ("not the result but a different truthmaker"; "not a defect... It is a feature"; "not because... which
  would be an obscene thing to say, but because"; "filing, not finding"). "Filing, not finding" is the
  signature and stays; cut or restate one of its neighbors so the signature is not the fourth antithesis in
  two pages.
- **Landing-line drumbeat: the real uniformity tell here.** Eight of nine sections exit on a polished
  epigram ("...they draw the same boundary"; "...the relation is gone" / "...slower and harder to see";
  "...fails to do what it was for"; "...more room around this text than the textbooks imply"; "...the only
  one the bridge can carry"; "...every reading has to carry"; "...the instrument is blind to"; "...the
  third Hegel means"). Each is good alone; together they are a metronome. Let one or two sections simply
  stop — the Kojève/Williams section and the objections section are the natural candidates, since their
  content is inventory rather than turn.
- **Performed candor: nearly clean.** "I will keep insisting on the difference, because the resemblance is
  close enough here to swallow it if I let it" (70–71) narrates vigilance but also states a real mechanism
  (the resemblance *is* the hazard); keep. "Pretending otherwise would be the worst thing this bridge could
  do" (309) is the one residue of the v1 virtue-narration register; it can go without loss, since the
  sentence before it already does the work.
- **Agentless passives: none found.** The `has been / have been` hits are all present-perfect actives with
  named agents ("The lab has been sorting," "the commentators have been saying"). The highest-leverage
  house-style check passes.
- **Repeated openers:** two sections open "Here is the trap..." / "Here is the construct..." — vary one.
  The imperative openers ("Start with," "Set out," "State the theorem") are a deliberate voice choice and
  should stay; they are the liveliest thing in the register and flagging them would be the false positive
  Step 0 warns about.
- **Quotes and signature lines:** all quoted material exempt and, per the sourcing note, verified in a

## Closing note

The paper's biggest genuine strength is its discipline about size: the anti-novelty section arms the
mainstream reading with seven verified voices before the program says a word, the objections section gives
Wood, Fanon, Cunniff, and Saunders their best lines rather than their weakest, and "filing, not finding" is
a verdict the whole structure actually earns — almost. The gap between "almost" and "earns" is the one
thing only the author can supply, and it is not prose: build the n=3 model. Master, slave, world; a
mechanism for the fear-held edge; the classifier in `classifier/contingency.py` run twice, coercion on and
coercion off. The modeling choices — what the world node computes, which dependency the fear is — are
