# Hegel-specialist review, round 2 — "The Real Middle Term" (Post 3, draft v4)

**Reviewer lens:** Hegel and German idealism. Charge: find what v4 still gets wrong or leaves weak after the
first panel and the grounding run, not to re-report fixes already made. Everything I assert as a textual
correction below I verified against a source this session: di Giovanni's *Science of Logic* (the archived
Cambridge translation, full text) for the Mechanism-chapter passages, Sans (2018) for the secondary
quotations, and the Wallace *Encyclopaedia* plus a section-number check for §198. The reelle/free-mechanism
German was verified in the prior round and I did not re-litigate it.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Minor-to-major revisions — closer to major on one axis.** The exegesis is now largely sound and the
sourcing is honest; the problem that remains is not a bad quote but a **live internal contradiction at the
paper's own hinge**, and it recurs three times.

**The single most important fix:** the paper cannot say both (a) "the mechanism rung is the one rung on
Hegel's ladder where the middle is a causal middle and nothing more — where the criterion's question applies
without translation loss" (line 264–266) **and** (b) "absolute mechanism is already the Concept showing
through objectivity — the center is immanent universality, not neutral machinery" (line 164–166). Hegel's
reelle Mitte — the very node the paper maps to Φ > 0 — is the *most* Concept-loaded moment of the whole
chapter, not a causal middle "and nothing more." The honesty tax is stated in one paragraph and then
contradicted two paragraphs later. Discharge it: the fit is tightest at the conduit pole (communication *is*
causal-and-nothing-more) and loosest at the integrator pole (the center is already the objects' immanent
essence). Say that, and the bridge survives as a same-shape claim. Leave both sentences standing and the
first hostile Hegelian pulls the thread.

## 2. Step 0 (two lines)

**Register:** deliberately first-person, conversational-scholarly Substack philosophy — essayistic openers
("Start with the sentence, because it sounds mad"), earned em-dashes, signature landing lines, all
on-purpose and off-limits as "prose failures."

**Bar:** make it better at being itself — verified Hegel, the two series guards held (no anticipation claim;
no demotion of Φ), the causal/conceptual seams marked in the open — not converted into impersonal journal
prose.

Both guards hold in v4. The paper never says Hegel anticipated Φ (it stays at "same boundary, drawn by him,
in his own chapter") and it never demotes the instrument (Φ is "a verdict," the criterion does "real work,"
the receipts are the affirmative payload). Where a sentence brushes the anticipation guard it is flagged
below (Finding 3), but it is a brush, not a breach.

## 3. Section-by-section

### Opening (lines 11–41)

The two-teams vignette and the one-paragraph statement of the criterion are the strongest opening in the
series. No exegetical issue. One seed of the central problem is planted here and worth watching: line 39,
"An integrator commits — its own state turns on both parties at once." That is the paper's *causal* definition
of the integrator (joint dependence of the middle's state). Hold it against what Hegel's real middle actually
is (below), because the paper will later claim these are the same object "without translation loss," and they
are not the same object — they are the same *shape*. The opening is fine; the equivocation it seeds detonates
at line 264.

### "Everything Rational Is a Syllogism" (43–71)

Sound, and the Gaius/copula reading is accurate to the chapter's opening move. The §181R quote checks out in
sense. The placement fix from round 1 ("at the close of subjectivity") landed correctly (line 68). No new
issue. One quiet dependency to name for later: the whole non-formal reading of "everything rational is a
syllogism" is about to be carried by Sans alone (Finding 5).

### "The Objection That Would Sink the Paper" (73–107)

This section defeats *one* skeptic — the "it's just formal logic / a template laid over the world" skeptic —
and defeats it well with Sans. The Sans quotations verify verbatim: "conceptual relations as such are real"
(sans.txt 192), "really existing, objective universal" (144), the Anselm/Descartes comparison and "suggests"
(625–632, and v4 correctly changed "implies" → "suggests"). The Redding scope note (101–103) is honest and I
endorse it: Redding does not treat the objective syllogism, the paper says so, good.

**What is missing is the harder skeptic** (Finding 5). Granting that Hegel's syllogism is "a structure out in
the world and not only a form of argument" (84) is precisely what a post-Kantian reader (Pippin, Pinkard)
would *not* grant in the flat sense the paper needs. On that reading the "objectivity of the Concept" is the
objectivity of thought's self-determination, not causal structure lying around in things. Sans's own
anti-reductionism — which the paper quotes approvingly — leans that way. The paper answers the formalist and
never faces the deflationist, and the deflationist is the one whose objection actually bites the bridge.

### "From Communication to the Center: Hegel's Own Ascent" (109–173)

This is v4's spine and it is, in the main, a real and defensible reading. I verified every load-bearing
quote against di Giovanni:

- "composition, mixture, aggregate" (p. 631) — the aggregate, confirmed.
- "and this is communication, which occurs without transition into the opposite" (p. 635) — **verbatim
  confirmed** (di Giovanni, 12.138).
- scent "spreading in the unresisting atmosphere"; "Laws, morals, rational conceptions... pervade
  individuals" (pp. 635–636) — confirmed.
- "the real middle term between the many interacting objects; it unites them in and for themselves and is
  their objective universality" (p. 641) — **verbatim confirmed** (12.143).
- "the central body which is the genus or rather the individualized universality" (p. 641) — confirmed.
- free mechanism, the three-syllogism totality, "pervasive gravity that persists self-identical" (pp.
  642–643) — confirmed.
- the reelle/reale philology (line 149–153): Sans writes "reale Mitte" (sans.txt 733), Hegel's word is
  reelle. The paper's care here is exactly right and referees will remember it.

So the arc is real: aggregate → communication → center, inside one chapter. **But two things strain, and one
is a gift the paper is leaving on the table.**

**(a) The strain — "Communication is the conduit... content relayed intact between parties" (132–133).**
Read the passage. Hegel's communication has *no middle term.* The determinateness "continues undisturbed from
one person to another, generalizing itself unaltered — like a scent freely spreading" (di Giovanni p. 635–636).
It generalizes *itself*; nothing relays it. The middle term (Mitte) enters the chapter only at the center.
So Hegel's ascent is not bad-middle → good-middle. It is **no real middle → a real middle** — diffusion
without a center, then a system with one. That is a *stronger* mapping for the paper than the one it prints,
because the paper's own criterion says a conduit's middle is "a bystander" that "rides along" (line 195), i.e.
not a genuine middle at all. Hegel and the criterion agree that the conduit stage *has no real middle*. The
paper should claim that agreement, not flatten it into "communication is the conduit" as though Hegel's
communication were itself a relaying third.

**(b) The gift not taken — the one sentence at di Giovanni p. 641 that draws the paper's whole distinction in
a single Hegelian breath.** Right where the reelle Mitte appears, Hegel writes: "Universality exhibited
itself first in the relation of communication, as present only through positing; as objective universality,
however, it is the pervading immanent essence of the objects." That is the conduit/integrator contrast —
posited-external universality *versus* immanent-essence universality — in *one sentence*, with the ascent
marked by Hegel himself ("first in communication... however, as objective universality"). The paper quotes
the two poles from two pages and *asserts* the arc between them; this sentence *is* the arc, in Hegel's hand.
Quote it. It is the best single piece of text the paper has and it is not on the page.

**(c) "on one continuous stretch of text" (155) glides over the real mechanical process.** Between
communication (formal process, p. 635) and the center (absolute mechanism, p. 641) sits the *real* mechanical
process — reaction, resistance, the stronger overpowering the weaker, "transition into the opposite" actually
happening. The paper's arc is a two-station selection from a four-station chapter (object → formal process →
real process → absolute mechanism). "One continuous stretch" is true of the *chapter* but oversells the
*smoothness*: a Hegelian will note that the dialectical engine between the two poles — violence and
subjugation — is exactly the material the coordination program should most want, and it is skipped. Not fatal;
one clause of acknowledgment closes it.

**(d) The honesty tax (164–166) is stated but not propagated.** "absolute mechanism is already the Concept
showing through... the top of Hegel's mechanism, not its floor" is correct and well put. The problem is that
the paper then forgets it (line 264). See Finding 1.

Hand-forward to Post 6 (169–173): government/citizens/needs each the middle of the other two (di Giovanni p.
642) and "the state is a system of three syllogisms" — I confirmed §198 carries exactly this ("Like the solar
system, so for example in the practical sphere the state is a system of three syllogisms," with the I–P–U /
U–I–P / P–U–I triad following). Accurate. Good hand-off. One proof-stage nit: confirm B&D place the tag in
the Remark (§198R) rather than the main §; Wallace runs it in the body.

### "The External Middle, in Both Logics" (175–197)

Clean. The two-register convergence (formal syllogism's abstract middle in §182; communication in the
mechanical process) is the round-1 repair and it holds. The §182 "rational but non-conceptual [*begrifflos*]"
rendering is the one still gated for a physical spot-check per the sourcing note; nothing to add.

### "The Receipts" (199–237)

Not my lane (the formal reviewer verified the corpus), and the numbers I can check are right: 24/256 = 9.375%
→ "9.4 percent," and "nine in ten are conduits" ≈ 90.6%. The census-as-Hegelian-punchline ("most middles are
defective middles") is fair and earns its keep. The scope caveat (229–237) is honest. No exegetical issue.

### "The Move the Resemblance Does Not License" (239–274)

The best section in the series, again — and now the site of the paper's worst internal contradiction, because
this section is *right* and line 264 contradicts it. Lines 244–262 say plainly that Sans's ontology is
teleological and self-realizing, that the genuine middle "is a whole producing and sustaining its own unity,"
that this is "not a cause-and-effect structure," and that the criterion asks a deliberately thinner question.
All correct. Then line 264–266 turns around and says the mechanism rung is "a causal middle and nothing more
— where the criterion's question applies without translation loss." Those cannot both be true of the reelle
Mitte, and the reelle Mitte is the rung. See Finding 1 for the fix.

One re-attribution error in this section, and it is exactly the class of error the paper prides itself on
catching: line 248 quotes "in things spiritual the center, and the union with it, assume higher forms"
attributed to **(Sans, 2018, p. 206)**. That sentence is **Hegel's** (di Giovanni p. 641 — I read it in the
reelle-Mitte paragraph), which Sans is quoting. By the paper's own standard at line 153 — the line "between
citing Hegel and citing his best reader" — this is Hegel, not Sans. Re-attribute it (Finding 4).

### "Stein's Knife" (276–315)

The quotations verify (round 1 checked them character by character; I did not re-run). The *argument* is where
v4 still overclaims. "That is Stein's priority thesis, reproduced inside the causal register by the instrument
itself" (303–304) and "it is Stein's objection, confirmed by the instrument on its own cases" (222) and "my
data enforce it" (308) are pitched a full step above what the caveat then concedes. Stein's thesis is that the
syllogism is derivative *of the Concept* — a claim in a metaphysics of conceptual determination. The lab's
finding (read-functions beat wiring) is a claim about which layer of a *causal* specification carries
integration. They share an *ordering*; the instrument cannot "confirm," "reproduce," or "enforce" a thesis
about conceptual determination, because it never reads conceptual determination. The caveat (304–307) says
the right thing — causal not conceptual — but the verbs around it re-inflate past it. See Finding 2, including
a subtlety about *which* boundary is Stein's.

### "Necessary Middles and Contingent Ones" / "Prior Art" / "Two Middles" (317–388)

The three-operations discipline (partition / bypass / removal) is clean and the prior-art bound is honestly
drawn ("no located work reads Hegel's middle term against network brokerage... that step is this paper's
own"). The close is de-templated as promised. One residual overclaim lives in the last section: "the ascent
from communication to the center reads as an ascent from Φ = 0 to Φ > 0 in everything but notation" (384).
This is the same pathology as Findings 1 and 2 — the epigram overshoots the careful sentence two lines down
("a model no one has built — and under the corpus's own definitions it is not even well-posed yet"). If the
model is not built and the case is not well-posed, the difference is *more* than notation. See Finding 3.

Minor throughout: "Post" and "Paper" are used interchangeably for the same series items ("Paper 4's," line
167; "Post 6's text," 173; "Paper 2," 328). Pick one noun.

## 4. Findings, most damaging first

**F1 — The hinge contradicts itself: "causal middle and nothing more" vs. "already the Concept showing
through." (lines 164–166 vs. 264–266) — the fix that decides the verdict.**
Hegel's reelle Mitte is emphatically not a causal middle and nothing more. The text: the center is "the
pervading immanent essence of the objects," "their concept existing in and for itself," the thing they
"strive" toward, "a self-determining principle to which the objects inhere," and — Hegel says it outright —
"the central body has therefore ceased to be a mere object" (di Giovanni p. 641). That is the paper's chosen
integrator. So the arc's two poles sit at opposite ends of conceptual investment: the conduit (communication)
is pure mechanism, the integrator (center) is mechanism passing into the Concept. "Without translation loss"
is true at the bottom of the arc and false at the top. The honesty tax already knows this; line 264 forgets
it. **Fix (Roger's voice), replacing 264–266:**

> What the arc buys the step is this. The conduit pole is a causal middle and nothing more — communication is
> mechanical straight through, and the criterion reads it with no loss. The loss creeps back at the other
> pole. Hegel's real middle is already the objects' own immanent essence, the thing they strive toward, a
> center that has "ceased to be a mere object"; my partition test reads only its causal shadow, a middle that
> commits on both parties at once. The fit is tightest at the bottom of the arc and loosest at the top. The
> criterion tracks the shape of Hegel's climb — relay to real middle — and reads less and less of what he
> means by *real* the higher the climb goes. That is the honest width of the bridge, and it is enough.

This keeps the affirmative case (the criterion tracks the shape; Φ does real work) while paying the tax in
full.

**F2 — Stein: the verbs overclaim past the caveat, and the analogue is mis-drawn. (222, 303–304, 308)**
Two problems. First, "confirmed / reproduced / enforce" claim more than a structural rhyme. Second, and
deeper: in the lab, *both* wiring and read-functions are causal structure — neither is Stein's Concept. The
genuine analogue of Stein's Concept is the modeler (which the paper gets right at 310–311). So the read-
function plays "the determination of the element," but that determination is *handed down by the modeler* —
which means the two mappings (read-function = Stein's "what"; modeler = Stein's Concept) only cohere if the
paper says the modeler is what fixes the read-functions. It half-says this and never joins them. **Fix
(Roger's voice):** at 222 —

> Hold that finding; it comes back when Stein's objection arrives, because it rhymes with Stein's objection —
> the same ordering, read off the instrument's own cases.

and the tail of the Stein section (303–308) —

> That is the ordering Stein insists on, surfacing one register down: the relation does not fix its relata,
> and the relata's own determinations fix whether the middle is genuine. The rhyme must not be oversold. An
> update rule is a causal determination, not a conceptual one; Stein's "what they are determined *as*" is
> semantic, and the instrument reads only its thin causal shadow. And Stein's Concept is not the update rule
> — it is whoever wrote the update rule, the modeler who hands the elements their determinations. So the
> instrument does not confirm Stein's thesis. It echoes its order, in a register Stein was not talking about.
> That is enough for my purpose and no more than that.

**F3 — "in everything but notation" is the guard-brush v4 didn't retire. (384)**
The anticipation guard holds everywhere else, but this epigram says Hegel's ascent *is* the Φ = 0 → Φ > 0
ascent up to notation — which the very next clause denies (no model, not well-posed). A hostile reader quotes
this line as "you said Hegel computed Φ in words." **Fix (Roger's voice):**

> the ascent from communication to the center has the shape my criterion reads as Φ = 0 giving way to Φ > 0 —
> the same boundary, not yet the same computation, since the model is not built and, on the corpus's own
> terms, not yet well-posed. Testing the reading is owed, not done.

**F4 — Hegel-within-Sans misattribution slipped through v4's cleanup. (248)**
"in things spiritual the center, and the union with it, assume higher forms" is Hegel (di Giovanni p. 641),
not Sans. The v4 note claims four such quotes were re-attributed; this is a fifth that wasn't. By the paper's
own line-153 standard it must move. **Fix (Roger's voice):**

> ...and "spirit as such transcends mechanism" — the last of which is Hegel's own line, pressed by Sans to
> the same end: "in things spiritual the center, and the union with it, assume higher forms" (Hegel,
> 1816/2010b, p. 641; quoted in Sans, 2018, p. 206).

**F5 — The bridge rests on one reader for the move that matters, and answers the wrong skeptic. (89–107)**
Two linked weaknesses. (i) The non-formal, "real structure of wholes" reading is carried by Sans (2018)
alone; Redding is honestly scoped out of the objective syllogism. A hostile referee says the whole ontological
turn hangs on one twenty-page chapter. Name a second ontological reader of the Subjective Logic's syllogism
(Winfield's *Hegel's Science of Logic* commentary, or Burbidge) to de-risk. (ii) More seriously, the skeptic
the paper defeats is the formalist ("it's just valid-inference theory"); the skeptic it needs to defeat is
the post-Kantian deflationist who grants the syllogism is not formal logic but denies it is "a structure out
in the world" in the causal-realist sense the criterion wants. Sans's own anti-reductionism (which the paper
quotes) is closer to that deflationist than to the paper's realism. One paragraph naming this second reader,
and conceding that the paper's "real structure" is being read *thinly* — as causal shape, not as the Concept's
self-determination — would close the gap. The "Move the Resemblance" section already does most of this work;
it just needs to name the opponent.

**F6 — "Communication is the conduit" flattens Hegel's better point: communication has no middle. (132–133)**
Constructive, not merely corrective. Hegel's communication is lossless diffusion with no relaying third; the
Mitte appears only at the center. The paper's own criterion already says a conduit's middle is a bystander.
So Hegel's arc — no real middle → a real middle — *matches the criterion more exactly* than the printed
"conduit-middle → integrator-middle" does. **Fix (Roger's voice), replacing 132–133:**

> That is the conduit — content passed intact between parties who stay external to one another and to it. And
> notice what Hegel's communication does without: a middle. The determinateness generalizes itself; no third
> term relays it. The middle proper arrives only at the center. So Hegel's ascent is not bad-middle to
> good-middle — it is no real middle to a real one, which is exactly what my test calls a conduit: a form
> whose middle rides along as a bystander while the pair does the work.

**F7 — The p. 641 "objective universality however" sentence should be quoted. (constructive, §"From
Communication to the Center")**
The single sentence at di Giovanni p. 641 draws the paper's distinction in Hegel's own words
("Universality exhibited itself first in the relation of communication... as objective universality, however,
it is the pervading immanent essence of the objects"). Quoting it converts the arc from an asserted stitch of
two pages into one Hegelian breath. Highest-value *addition* available. (Note it also carries the honesty tax
of F1 on its back: "immanent essence," not causal joint-function — so quote it and pay F1 in the same move.)

**F8 — "one continuous stretch" skips the real mechanical process. (155)**
Minor. One clause acknowledging that the arc runs *through* the real process (reaction, resistance,
subjugation) before it reaches the center forecloses the specialist objection and flags the material for a
later paper.

## 5. Close

**Biggest genuine strength.** The paper found, and now walks across, the arc that was sitting in Hegel's own
Mechanism chapter — aggregate to communication to the reelle Mitte — and it did the primary-source work to
back every quote. That is a real discovery, honestly sourced, and the "Move the Resemblance Does Not License"
section marks its own load-bearing weld more candidly than almost any bridge paper does. The verification held:
every di Giovanni and Sans passage I checked this round checked out, down to "reale" vs. "reelle." The bones
are excellent. What remains is that v4 fixed the *arguments* and left three *landing-lines* pitched half a
step above what the arguments support (F1, F2, F3) — and each epigram is contradicted by a careful sentence
within a few lines of it. Bring the epigrams down to the level of their own hedges and the paper is sound.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** Round 1 asked for a lived instance of the case the whole
program exists for — the intermediary "that looks like overhead... and turns out to be the term through which
the whole was ever a whole" (line 346). v4 still states it in the abstract. Every other middle in the paper
touches a concrete case: Gaius, the sun, the scent in the atmosphere, the q213 car dealer, the Boolean
receipts. This one — the affirmative payload, the reason the instrument is worth building — is still
hypothetical. One real, anonymized middle from the author's own consulting or teaching that someone cut and
the process dissolved would do more for the bridge than any exegetical repair on this list, and no reviewer
can fabricate it.
