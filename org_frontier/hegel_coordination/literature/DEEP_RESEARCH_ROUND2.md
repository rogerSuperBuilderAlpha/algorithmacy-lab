## The load-bearing output: corrections, not confirmations

1. **Post 8's *Beisichselbstsein* locus is pinned.** The passage card was OPEN. The phrase has a primary
   home at *Encyclopaedia Logic* §24 Zusatz 2 ("die Freiheit ist eben dies, in seinem Anderen bei sich
   selbst zu sein") and a second practical home at *PR* §7 (Remark and Addition, concrete freedom "in
   friendship and love"). It is a family of formulations, so Post 8 cites the pair: EL §24 Z2 for the
   definition, PR §7 for the will/social version. The candidate "Encyclopaedia §158A" is a mis-pin — the
   love passage is *PR* §158, not *Enc.* §158. Both German sentences and both English phrases were fetched
   and confirmed at their exact sections. `[✓]`

2. **Post 8's Hirschman connection is genuinely unmade.** A hard search (Wikipedia biography, Adelman's
   biography, a Scholar-Gateway full-text corpus sweep 1988–2026, PhilPapers) found no work mapping
   Hegel's freedom of the void (*PR* §5–7) and concrete freedom (§260) onto Hirschman's exit/voice/loyalty.
   The corpus returns the two literatures as disjoint sets. Hirschman's own Hegelian formation is
   documented (his first essay was on the Hegelian philosophy of the family), and one non-scholarly
   Substack essay opposes "Landian exit" to "Hegelian love," but neither is the mapping. Post 8 can claim
   it as a small original contribution. The safe construal is causal: exit = low-cost severability (low Φ),
   voice = costly-to-sever coupling (high Φ), loyalty = what holds the coupling intact. A loose reading
   collapses into Berlin's negative/positive liberty and back into the normative-attitudinal camp, so the
   partition-sensitivity construal has to be explicit. `[✓]` (as a confirmed absence)

3. **Post 8's Neuhouser anchor upgrades from preview to verified — and doubles as a threat.** *Foundations
   of Hegel's Social Theory: Actualizing Freedom* (Harvard, 2000, 332 pp.) is confirmed, including the
   two-condition standard for a rational institution: it must furnish the preconditions of its members'
   freedom, and members must subjectively affirm it as their own. That rationality test is
   normative-attitudinal, so a reviewer can say the Φ criterion displaces Hegel's actual freedom test. The
   open-connection to run down: Neuhouser's objective component is what an irreducibility test
   operationalizes, and his "being-at-home-in-the-other" subjective component is coordinative sovereignty
   (voice within irreducible coordination). Cite the 2000 first edition, not the 2009 reprint. `[~]` (no
   page-numbered in-text quote pulled this run)

4. **Post 3's "just shape" objection now has an answer.** Georg Sans, "'Everything Rational is a
   Syllogism': Hegel's Logic of Inference," ch. 10 in *The Aftermath of Syllogism* (Bloomsbury, 2018),
   argues in print that Hegel's Syllogism chapter is an ontological argument: "conceptual relations as such
   are real," the chapter "aims at establishing the Concept as something objective, to wit really
   existing." Three load-bearing quotes were verified verbatim from the author PDF. Paired with the primary
   locus (*SL* §1437, "everything rational is a syllogism") and Redding's *Conceptual Harmonies* (Chicago,
   2023) as convergent support, this stops Post 3 collapsing into a footnote of Post 4. **Caveat that must
   ride with the anchor:** Sans's "ontological" is teleological/self-realizing, not causal-partition —
   his objective syllogism explicitly transcends mechanism (the solar system's "real middle term" is
   gravity; in spiritual things the connection is not mechanical). So the anchor licenses "the syllogism is
   a real structure of the whole," and the move from there to a causal-irreducibility criterion is a move
   the series makes, not one Sans supplies. `[✓]`

5. **Post 3's Stein citation was wrong in the plan.** There is no Stein/Wretzel volume titled *Hegel's
   Doctrine of the Concept*. The load-bearing source is Sebastian Stein, "Hegel's Monarch, the Concept and
   the Limits of Syllogistic Reasoning," *Hegel Bulletin* 37/1 (2016): 145–155 — full text read, quotes
   verbatim-confirmed. In the real Stein/Wretzel volume (*Hegel's Encyclopedia... A Critical Guide*, CUP
   2021) the syllogism chapter is Redding's ("Political Ontology and Rational Syllogistic") and the Concept
   chapter is Kervégan's; Stein's own chapter is on Spinoza. Stein's point is a threat as much as an
   anchor: he makes the syllogism derivative of the Concept, which "cannot determine what the elements
   specifically are." `[✓]`

6. **Post 3's Vieweg citation was mislocated.** The chapter is Klaus Vieweg, "The State as a System of

## What each concept established

### Post 1 — Two holisms
- **#1 Kreines, *Reason in the World* (OUP 2015, ISBN 978-0-19-020430-3).** Reads the *Logic* as a
  "metaphysics of reason" — grounds and conditions of explanation, reasons "always found in immanent
  concepts, universals or kinds," neither Kantian nor Spinozist. This legitimizes reading "the true is the
  whole" as ontology, which the causal criterion needs to have a Hegelian target. Two cautions. Kreines's
  holism is explanatory, not efficient-causal, which hands a reviewer the ground/cause distinction against
  a partition-of-cause-effect reading. And the NDPR reviewer (Yeomans) calls it graded orders of
  explanation "rather than holism in the traditional sense," so specify *explanatory* holism or the anchor
  is imprecise. One quoted clause ("rather than holism in the traditional sense") did not surface verbatim
  in the fetched review — drop the quotation marks until the printed text is checked. `[~]`
- **#2 Stern & Taylor.** Located and verified: Charles Taylor, *Hegel* (CUP, 1975); Robert Stern,
  *Hegelian Metaphysics* (OUP, 2009), which reprints his BJHP article "Hegel, British Idealism, and the
  curious case of the concrete universal" 15(1) (2007): 115–153. Three Taylor quotes were grep-confirmed
  against the full-text scan, including "the individuals only are what they are by their inherence in the
  community" and "a whole whose parts are intrinsically related to each other... where each can only be
  understood by its relations to the others." Their holism is constitutive/internal-relations/conceptual —
  a different species from synchronic causal-partition holism, which is exactly the "two holisms" framing:
  genuine Hegel, different kind. The Stern concrete-universal quote (p. 156) is review-sourced (via Ng),
  not read in the book. `[✓]`
- **#3 Ng, *Hegel's Concept of Life* (OUP 2020), deepened.** The sharpest threat, now pinned: inner
  purposiveness moves regulative→constitutive (p. 43), and Hegel "grounds his entire system of reason on
  the basis of internally purposive form" (p. 234); life "opens up the space of reasons itself" (p. 10);
  life as "original judgment" / *Ur-teil* (pp. 171, 259). Why this is anti-causal-partition: living
  self-relation requires diachronic self-constitution, telos, being-for-itself, and the whole's own
  self-partitioning — none registered by a synchronic partition measure. This is the concession Post 4 must
  make its content: Φ captures irreducibility-as-fact, not irreducibility-as-living-self-relation. Page
  quotes are review-reported (NDPR/Gentry, C-SCP/Chaput, boundary2/Suther); the p. 43 sentence is Gentry's
  paraphrase, not a verbatim Ng block quote. `[~]`

### Posts 4 & 6 — Mechanism / teleology / organism
- **#10 Kreines, "The Logic of Life"** (Cambridge Companion to Hegel and 19th-C Philosophy, 2008, ch. 13,
  pp. 344–377). Re-confirmed. Hegel is a realist, not a regulativist, about the living whole: organisms
  "manifest true internal purposiveness," "explicable in teleological terms," of which "we can have
  objective knowledge." That grounds the Post 4 "Hegel wins the classification" move — he objectively
  places the organism on the configuration side of the boundary. It is also the threat: the ground of that
  irreducibility is inner teleology / self-production, which a Φ measure neither entails nor requires (Φ
  could score high for a system that is in no sense self-productive). The series must argue the causal
  criterion *tracks* Hegel's teleological ground, not that it replaces it. Interior page quotes rest on
  kreines.net, not the bot-protected chapter PDF. `[✓]`
- **#11 Chemism** (*SL/EL* Objectivity §§200–203; Ebeturk, "Between Chemism and Life," and Koch,
  "Mechanism, External Purposiveness, and Object Individuation," both *Hegel Bulletin* 44/1, 2023). Not
  untranslatable. Chemism reads as transient, externally-triggered, self-extinguishing integration —
  objects that are "the absolute impulse towards integration by and in one another," saturating into an
  inert neutral. The mismatch is exact: Hegel orders the ladder by self-determination of the Concept, the
  series orders it by irreducible integration, and chemism is the rung where the two orderings dissociate —
  a chemically coupled system can be more irreducible yet rank lower. Concede it: Φ maps the
  aggregate/configuration cut cleanly and under-determines the chemism/teleology cut, which is Hegel's own
  upgrade condition. Chemism is positioning terrain, not an embarrassment. Primary quotes verbatim; article
  bodies confirmed only at the DOI landing. `[✓]`
- **#12 *Glied* teleological vs partition-visible** — the HC1 finding. Teile/Glieder is **not** the
  partition test. A *Glied* is defined teleologically, by reciprocal means-end contribution to the whole's
  self-maintenance (*EL* §216, §218). The partition test captures only the extensional symptom (severed =
  "a hand in name only"). The residue: a defender of the causal reading can argue self-maintenance is
  itself a dynamical/attractor property and thus partly partition-adjacent — Post 6 should concede the
  extensional overlap and hold that the intensional criterion (purpose, direction-of-definition) is what Φ
  brackets, or the "proto-Φ smuggling" that Post 4 forbids creeps back in. Corti (2022, HPLS) and Ng (2017,
  Oxford Handbook) ground the residue. Primary quotes verbatim; loci corrected (see correction 8). `[✓]`
