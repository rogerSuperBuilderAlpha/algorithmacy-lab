# Series plan — nine posts

Each post states one claim, anchors it in at most two primary passages, bridges to one lab construct,
and names its refutation condition — what a fair Hegel reader could say that would sink it. Passage loci
are working pointers; the `passages/` card gets built and verified before drafting. Series name and
cadence are the author's call; the working assumption is one post every two or three weeks.

## 1. Two holisms

**Claim.** "The true is the whole" and "a whole is what no partition preserves" are different holisms —
one developmental and self-knowing, one synchronic and causal — and the series exists to read them
against each other, not to merge them.
**Passages.** *PhG* Preface: the true as the whole (¶20, Pinkard); the result together with its becoming
(Preface, early ¶¶ — verify locus).
**Lab bridge.** The constitution criterion; what "flagged as such" means in the OT manuscript.
**Refutation condition.** If the difference reduces to synchronic-vs-diachronic bookkeeping, the series
premise thins; post 4 is where that gets tested rather than assumed away.

## 2. The first constitution proof, and the first dissolution theorem

**Claim.** Master and slave shows binding-by-domination is self-undermining: the master's recognition
comes from someone unfree and is therefore worthless — a structural argument that constraint-held wholes
dissolve from inside.
**Passages.** *PhG* ¶¶178–196 (independence and dependence of self-consciousness).
**Lab bridge.** Contingent irreducibility; the constraint operator; the self-liquidating broker.
**Refutation condition.** Honneth and Brandom read the dialectic as normative, not structural; the post
must show the structural reading adds something theirs lacks, or concede the bridge is analogy.

## 3. Everything rational is a triad

**Claim.** Hegel's syllogism — a middle term through which two extremes hold together — is the mediated
triad, and his complaint against merely external middles is the conduit/integrator distinction.
**Passages.** *EL* §§181–193 ("everything is a syllogism"); *SL*, The Syllogism (di Giovanni).
**Lab bridge.** The strict-mediation triad; necessary vs. contingent middles.
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's syllogism is a logical form, not a causal wiring; if the post cannot
say what survives that objection beyond shape, it shrinks to a footnote of post 4.

## 4. Where Hegel files the microscope

**Claim.** On the *Logic*'s ladder — mechanism, chemism, teleology — exact causal irreducibility is at
best upper mechanism. This is the strongest objection the coordination program has received, and the
post makes it in Hegel's voice before answering in the lab's.
**Passages.** *EL* §§194–212; *SL*, Objectivity (mechanism/chemism/teleology).
**Lab bridge.** What Φ deliberately brackets (purpose, self-maintenance, experience) and what the
bracketing buys (a decidable test).
**Refutation condition.** Built for Hegel to win on the classification and the lab to win on the method;
if either side sweeps, the post failed as written.

## 5. Interdependence without unity, 1821

**Claim.** Civil society is Hegel computing the lab's flagship result verbally: universal
interdependence — the system of needs, Smith and Ricardo read and digested — whose unity is external.
Connection was already not constitution.
**Passages.** *PR* §§182–189 (system of needs; the economists named at §189R); §183 or §289R on the
external state (verify locus of *Not- und Verstandesstaat*).
**Lab bridge.** The interior-threshold quorum; "a whole that reads everyone can still be an aggregate."
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's deficiency claim is normative (civil society fails at *freedom*), not
causal; the post must keep the two registers distinct while showing they draw the same boundary.

## 6. Parts and members

**Claim.** *Teile* vs. *Glieder* — the severed hand — is the partition test spoken a century early, and
the state-as-organism passages are Hegel's membership account. One deliberately self-limiting toy
encoding of the *EL* §198R state-triad shows what the microscope sees there and what it constitutively
cannot; the failure is the exhibit.
**Passages.** *PR* §§267–269, §276R (organism); *EL* §198R (the state's mutually mediating syllogisms —
verify).
**Lab bridge.** The complex as boundary-as-output; the membership law. The toy encoding, if built, goes
through the standard protocol first (HC5).
**Refutation condition.** If the encoding captures everything Hegel says about members, post 1's premise
was wrong; if it captures nothing, the bridge is decoration. The interesting outcome is the documented
middle.

## 7. The Korporation and the catalog

**Claim.** Hegel argues civil society requires mediating corporations to give individuals standing —
an argument that some intermediaries are necessary integrators, made before the vocabulary existed.
**Passages.** *PR* §§250–256.
**Lab bridge.** The irreducibility catalog's necessary/contingent sort; what "integrating work" means.
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's corporations confer honor and ethical standing, not coordination; if
the integration reading requires ignoring what he actually says corporations do, say so and downgrade
the bridge.

## 8. Freedom in the whole

**Claim.** *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* — at home with oneself in the other — is coordinative
sovereignty's thesis in Hegel's grammar: freedom as a quality of membership in an irreducible
coordination, against the freedom of the void, with exit/voice mapped onto negative/concrete freedom.
**Passages.** *PR* §5+R (the void), §7, §260 (concrete freedom); verify the *Beisichselbstsein* locus
(*EL* §24A2 or *PR* Introduction additions).
**Lab bridge.** Coordinative sovereignty, whole cloth. Anything durable feeds back to that program.
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's institutions earn the "at home" claim through their rational content,
not their irreducibility; a platform can be irreducible and estranging. The post has to hold that line
or it becomes apologetics for the wholes the lab studies.

## 9. The ledger

**Claim.** A sober close: what Hegel refutes in the program (the mechanism objection, wherever it stood
up), what the program refutes in the reading of Hegel it started with (coordination was not his glue),
what translated into researchable questions (HC1–HC6 status), and what resisted translation — with
Aufhebung vs. the operators, and the core-merger exhibits as the one computed structure that resembles
sublation, as the closing case.
**Passages.** *PR* Preface (the owl of Minerva) — and nothing else new.
**Lab bridge.** The dissolution operators; q210/q211 merged cores.
**Refutation condition.** None; the post is the scoreboard, and it inherits every unresolved objection
above.
