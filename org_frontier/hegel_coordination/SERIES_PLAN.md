# Series plan — nine posts

Each post states one claim, anchors it in at most two primary passages, bridges to one lab construct,
and names its refutation condition — what a fair Hegel reader could say that would sink it. Passage loci
are working pointers; the `passages/` card gets built and verified before drafting. Series name and
cadence are the author's call; the working assumption is one post every two or three weeks.

**Secondary anchors and threats** come from two research runs, both 2026-07-10:
[`literature/DEEP_RESEARCH.md`](literature/DEEP_RESEARCH.md) (round 1, the terrain map) and
[`literature/DEEP_RESEARCH_ROUND2.md`](literature/DEEP_RESEARCH_ROUND2.md) (round 2, which took twenty
gap-concepts through research + adversarial citation-checking and closed them). `[✓]` = verified by an
independent web check; `[○]` = surfaced but not yet verified, so verify before citing; `[✗]` = refuted or a
correction to carry. The markers below reflect round 2. The strategic finding holds: the
Hegel-against-a-causal-irreducibility-criterion bridge is genuinely unmade, so the novelty claim is safe at
that exact altitude — and the scholarship that most helps the series (Ng, Kreines on teleology) is also its
sharpest threat, landing on Posts 1, 4, and 6. Round 2 also corrected several passage loci; those are
carried into each post below and listed together in the round-2 map's residue.

## 1. Two holisms

**Claim.** "The true is the whole" and "a whole is what no partition preserves" are different holisms —
one developmental and self-knowing, one synchronic and causal — and the series exists to read them
against each other, not to merge them.
**Passages.** *PhG* Preface: the true as the whole (¶20, Pinkard, verbatim-confirmed); the result together
with its becoming (§3, Pinkard, verbatim-confirmed); "substance also as subject" (§17–18).
**Secondary.** `[✓]` Kreines, *Reason in the World* (OUP, 2015, ISBN 978-0-19-020430-3), on Hegel's
"metaphysics of reason" — but cite it as *explanatory* holism (grounds and reasons, immanent kinds).
Kreines's own term is "insubstantial holism" (ch. 7); the NDPR reviewer (Yeomans) glosses him as
"metaphysically pluralistic, epistemologically monistic." (The clause "rather than holism in the traditional
sense" is **not** in Yeomans's review — verified twice; do not attribute it.) Its holism is explanatory, not
efficient-causal, which is itself a fault line the post must address. `[✓]`
Stern and Taylor located: Charles Taylor, *Hegel* (CUP, 1975) — "the individuals only are what they are by
their inherence in the community" (verbatim-confirmed) — and Robert Stern, *Hegelian Metaphysics* (OUP,
2009), which reprints "Hegel, British Idealism, and the curious case of the concrete universal," *BJHP*
15(1) (2007): 115–153. Their holism is constitutive/internal-relations, a different species from the
synchronic causal-partition kind. **Threat `[✓]`** Karen Ng, *Hegel's Concept of Life* (OUP, 2020): the
whole is a self-comprehending living unity — inner purposiveness moves regulative→constitutive (p. 43),
Hegel "grounds his entire system of reason on the basis of internally purposive form" (p. 234), life as
"original judgment" (*Ur-teil*, pp. 171, 259) — which no synchronic causal criterion captures. The series'
sharpest threat, introduced here so the reader meets it before the bridges do. (Page quotes are
review-reported; verify against the printed page before quoting with a page.)
**Lab bridge.** The constitution criterion; what "flagged as such" means in the OT manuscript.
**Refutation condition.** If the difference reduces to synchronic-vs-diachronic bookkeeping, the series
premise thins. Ng's living-unity reading is the harder form of the objection — that Hegel's holism is not
under-formalized coordination but a different kind of thing entirely; post 4 tests that rather than
assuming it away.

## 2. The first constitution proof, and the first dissolution theorem

**Claim.** Master and slave shows binding-by-domination is self-undermining: the master's recognition
comes from someone unfree and is therefore worthless — a structural argument that constraint-held wholes
dissolve from inside.
**Passages.** *PhG* ¶¶178–196 (independence and dependence of self-consciousness).
**Secondary / threat `[✓]`** Pippin, *Hegel on Self-Consciousness* (Princeton UP, 2011): the struggle is
over "epistemological authority, not mere biological survival" — the recognition argument is irreducibly
normative. `[✓]` Honneth, *The Struggle for Recognition* (MIT, 1995) — confirmed, but he **brackets** the
master–slave dialectic (his translator's introduction says so outright, privileging the Jena texts), so
position against him, do not cite him as its reader. `[✓]` R. R. Williams, *Hegel's Ethics of Recognition*
(UC Press, 1997) and *Recognition* (SUNY, 1992); the **anti-Kojève turn** is located in the NDPR review of
his *Tragedy, Recognition, and the Death of God* (OUP, 2012): the struggle had been read "through the lens
of… Kojève," and Williams "demonstrated the narrowness and inadequacy of this reduced reading."
**Refutation, now sourced `[✓]`** Joe Saunders, "What's Wrong with the Master?", *Proceedings of the
Aristotelian Society* 126(1) (2026): 39–57, and Stephen Cunniff, "The Master's Problem," *European Journal
of Philosophy* (2026): the structural "self-defeating mastery" reading is already the mainstream (Houlgate,
Stern, Wood, Beiser), and "worthless" is Stern's own functional word. The counter is also here — the result
is desire-relative and circumventable by a community of masters (Wood, *Hegel's Ethical Thought* pp. 92–93;
Fanon: "what he wants from the slave is not recognition but work").
**Lab bridge.** Contingent irreducibility; the constraint operator; the self-liquidating broker.
**Refutation condition.** The structural reading is not a discovery — it is mainstream. What the causal
reading adds is a different truthmaker (partition-sensitivity), not a new result, and it inherits the
desire-relativity objection rather than escaping it. So the post either shows the causal truthmaker earns
its keep or concedes the bridge is analogy — and the constructive route is to route master–slave through
the lab's necessary/contingent program as a held-but-not-irreducible binding (coercively severable).

## 3. Everything rational is a triad

**Claim.** Hegel's syllogism — a middle term through which two extremes hold together — is the mediated
triad, and his complaint against merely external middles is the conduit/integrator distinction.
**Passages.** *EL* §§181–193 ("everything is a syllogism"); *SL*, The Syllogism, §1437 ("everything
rational is a syllogism," verbatim-confirmed) (di Giovanni). (The *EL* §198 state-triad belongs to Post 6 /
HC5; it sits under Absolute Mechanism.)
**Secondary — the anchor that meets the "just shape" objection `[✓]`** Georg Sans, "'Everything Rational is
a Syllogism': Hegel's Logic of Inference," ch. 10 in *The Aftermath of Syllogism* (Bloomsbury, 2018): the
Syllogism chapter is an ontological argument — "conceptual relations as such are real," the chapter "aims
at establishing the Concept as something objective, to wit really existing" (verbatim). Convergent: Paul
Redding, *Conceptual Harmonies* (Chicago, 2023). Positioning/threat `[✓]` Sebastian Stein, "Hegel's
Monarch, the Concept and the Limits of Syllogistic Reasoning," *Hegel Bulletin* 37/1 (2016): 145–155 — the
syllogism is derivative of the Concept, which "cannot determine what the elements specifically are."
(Citation correction: there is no Stein/Wretzel "Doctrine of the Concept" volume; use the 2016 article.)
**Lab bridge.** The strict-mediation triad; necessary vs. contingent middles.
**Refutation condition.** Sans defeats the "merely a logical form" objection: Hegel's objective syllogism
is a real structure of the whole. But his "ontological" is teleological and self-realizing — it explicitly
transcends mechanism — so it licenses "the syllogism is a real structure," not "a causal cause-effect
structure whose irreducibility is partition-sensitive." That last move is the series' to make, not Sans's.
If the post cannot make it, it rests on Sans's ontology and stays a strong footnote of post 4.

## 4. Where Hegel files the microscope

**Claim.** On the *Logic*'s ladder — mechanism, chemism, teleology — exact causal irreducibility is at
best upper mechanism. This is the strongest objection the coordination program has received, and the
post makes it in Hegel's voice before answering in the lab's.
**Passages.** *EL* §§194–212; *SL*, Objectivity (mechanism/chemism/teleology).
**Secondary `[✓]`** Kreines, "The Logic of Life" (in *Cambridge Companion to Hegel and 19th-C
Philosophy*, 2008, ch. 13, pp. 344–377) — Hegel defends teleological explanation Kant only licensed
regulatively, holding that living beings "manifest true internal purposiveness" of which "we can have
objective knowledge"; Karen Ng, *Hegel's Concept of Life* (2020) — inner purposiveness goes from regulative
to *constitutive*, so organic teleology, not mechanism, is the model for the Concept.
**Chemism, the honest mismatch (HC2) `[✓]`** *SL/EL* Objectivity §§200–203; Emre Ebeturk, "Between Chemism
and Life," and Karen Koch, "Mechanism, External Purposiveness, and Object Individuation," both *Hegel
Bulletin* 44/1 (2023). Chemism reads as transient, externally-triggered, self-extinguishing integration
("the absolute impulse towards integration by and in one another," saturating into an inert neutral). It is
the rung where the two orderings dissociate: Hegel orders the ladder by self-determination of the Concept,
the lab by irreducible integration, so a chemically coupled system can be more irreducible yet rank lower.
Concede it — Φ maps the aggregate/configuration cut cleanly and under-determines the chemism/teleology cut,
which is Hegel's own upgrade condition.
**Lab bridge.** What Φ deliberately brackets (purpose, self-maintenance, experience) and what the
bracketing buys (a decidable test).
**Refutation condition.** Built for Hegel to win on the classification and the lab to win on the method.
The honest form of Hegel's win, per Ng/Kreines: he *replaces* the criterion with inner teleology, he does
not anticipate a causal one — so the post must not smuggle "proto-Φ" back in through the win. If it does,
a reviewer holding Ng will say the reading imports exactly the synchronic mechanism Hegel rejects. If
either side sweeps, the post failed as written.

## 5. Interdependence without unity, 1821

**Claim.** Civil society is Hegel computing the lab's flagship result verbally: universal
interdependence — the system of needs, Smith and Ricardo read and digested — whose unity is external.
Connection was already not constitution.
**Passages.** *PR* §§182–189 (system of needs). §189R names **only Smith, Say, and Ricardo** ("the
interesting spectacle, as in Smith, Say, and Ricardo, of thought working upon the endless mass of
details," verbatim); do not attribute Steuart or Ferguson to that node. §183 on the external state (*Not-
und Verstandesstaat*, verified in the passage card).
**Secondary `[✓]`** Waszek, *The Scottish Enlightenment and Hegel's Account of 'Civil Society'* (Kluwer,
1988, *Int'l Archives of the History of Ideas* 120) — documents Hegel's reception of Scottish/British
political economy (Smith, Steuart, Ferguson) into the system of needs; the warrant for "interdependence
read and digested." Steuart and Ferguson are Waszek-documented influences, cited to Waszek, not to §189R. **Threat `[✓]`** Kain, "Hegel and the Failure of Civil Society," *Owl of
Minerva* 46 (2014–15): the deficiency is economic-distributive (polarization, pauperization), not a
formal failure of wholeness — and (correction to carry) Kain argues Hegel *has* a workable
social-democratic remedy; do not attribute "no solution" to him.
**Lab bridge.** The interior-threshold quorum; "a whole that reads everyone can still be an aggregate."
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's deficiency claim is normative-economic (civil society fails at *freedom*
and at distribution), not structural non-wholeness; Kain sharpens this. The post must keep the two
registers distinct while showing they draw the same boundary — and must not let the structural reading
quietly annex Hegel's ethical complaint.

## 6. Parts and members

**Claim.** *Teile* vs. *Glieder* — the severed hand — is the partition test spoken a century early, and
the state-as-organism passages are Hegel's membership account. One deliberately self-limiting toy
encoding of the *EL* §198R state-triad shows what the microscope sees there and what it constitutively
cannot; the failure is the exhibit.
**Passages (loci corrected round 2).** The severed hand is *EL* §216 (Aristotle's hand: "a hand, when
hewn off from the body, is a hand in name only"), not the *PR*. The explicit Teile-vs-Glieder sentence is
*PR* §278R ("the so-called 'parts' of an animal organism are not parts but members"), which lies outside
the old §§267–276 pointer; the reciprocal-self-maintenance image is *EL* §218, the anatomist-and-corpse
*EL* §135 Zusatz. The state-as-organism frame is *PR* §§269, §276A. *EL* §198R (the state's three mutually
mediating syllogisms) is the toy-encoding target, and it sits under Absolute Mechanism.
**Secondary / threat `[✓]`** Ng (2020) and Kreines (2008): *Glied* is defined **teleologically** — a
member is what contributes to the organism's self-maintenance (*EL* §216, §218) — not by
partition-sensitivity. The HC1 finding is now settled: Teile/Glieder is **not** the partition test; the
partition criterion captures only the extensional symptom. Corti, "The 'Is' and the 'Ought' of the Animal
Organism" (*HPLS*, 2022) and Ng, Oxford Handbook chapter (2017) ground the residue. This is the same threat
as Post 4, aimed at the membership bridge specifically.
**Lab bridge.** The complex as boundary-as-output; the membership law. The toy encoding, if built, goes
through the standard protocol first (HC5).
**Refutation condition.** The partition test reads a member as partition-visible (cutting it destroys what
the whole does); Hegel's *Glied* is teleologically defined. If the two criteria coincide on the state
organism it is a real result; if they come apart — the likely case — the post reports where, and that gap
*is* the finding. If the encoding captures everything Hegel says about members, post 1's premise was
wrong; if nothing, the bridge is decoration. The interesting outcome is the documented middle.

## 7. The Korporation and the catalog

**Claim.** Hegel argues civil society requires mediating corporations to give individuals standing —
an argument that some intermediaries are necessary integrators, made before the vocabulary existed.
**Passages.** *PR* §§250–256; the guild distinction and the abolition foil are in the **§255 Addition**
("the abolition of Corporations in recent times"), not simply "§255R" — and Knox renders "Zunft" as
"caste," so follow Nisbet ("guild") if quoting.
**Secondary `[✓]`** George Heiman, "The Sources and Significance of Hegel's Corporate Doctrine," in
Pelczynski (ed.), *Hegel's Political Philosophy: Problems and Perspectives* (CUP, 1971), ch. 6, pp.
111–135 — the corporation as a distinctively modern institution for ethical standing and political
mediation (the integrating-work side of HC4's axis). Further anchors: Klikauer, *Hegel's Moral
Corporation* (Palgrave, 2015); David James (ed.), *Critical Guide* to the *PR* (CUP, 2017). (The Heiman
verbatim quotation was not confirmed on a fetched page — verify against the print source or drop it.)
**HC4 guild abolitions `[✗ / ✓]`.** The abolition dates are solid and all precede the 1820 *PR* (France:
Allarde decree 2/17 March 1791, Le Chapelier 14 June 1791; Prussia: Gewerbesteueredikt 2 Nov 1810,
Gewerbefreiheit law 7 Sept 1811). But the "natural experiment" framing carries no evidential weight against
the recognitive rivals: Hegel wrote *against* the liberal abolition, the case is confounded, and it does
not select the causal-Φ reading. Use it as context and as an illustration of constraint-lifting, not as
evidence. (Round 2 caught two misattributed locators here; see the round-2 map.)
**Lab bridge.** The irreducibility catalog's necessary/contingent sort; what "integrating work" means.
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's corporations confer honor and ethical standing, not coordination; if
the integration reading requires ignoring what he actually says corporations do, say so and downgrade
the bridge.

## 8. Freedom in the whole

**Claim.** *Beisichselbstsein im Anderen* — at home with oneself in the other — is coordinative
sovereignty's thesis in Hegel's grammar: freedom as a quality of membership in an irreducible
coordination, against the freedom of the void, with exit/voice mapped onto negative/concrete freedom.
**Passages (locus pinned round 2).** *PR* §5+R (the void), §260 (concrete freedom). The *Beisichselbstsein*
phrase is a **family of two**: *EL* §24 **Zusatz 2** for the definition ("die Freiheit ist eben dies, in
seinem Anderen bei sich selbst zu sein," German confirmed) and *PR* §7 (Remark and Addition) for the
will/social version ("in friendship and love"). Cite the pair. Correction: "Enc. §158A" is a mis-pin — the
love passage is *PR* §158. See the pinned passage card.
**Secondary `[✓]`** Patten, *Hegel's Idea of Freedom* (OUP, 1999) — freedom realized and sustained through
institutions, not an inner state. `[✓]` Neuhouser, *Foundations of Hegel's Social Theory: Actualizing
Freedom* (Harvard, 2000) — social freedom as the standard by which family/civil society/state count as
rational, on a two-condition test (preconditions of freedom + subjective affirmation). Doubles as a
**threat**: that rationality test is normative-attitudinal, so a reviewer can say the Φ criterion displaces
Hegel's actual freedom test. The open-connection to run: Neuhouser's objective component is what an
irreducibility test operationalizes, his "at home in the other" the coordinative-sovereignty half. Scholarly
hook for the phrase: Roy, *Epoché* 11/1 (2006). **Open connection resolved `[✓]`** the Hegel-freedom ↔
Hirschman exit/voice mapping is genuinely unmade (corpus sweep found the two literatures disjoint), so it is
the post's small original contribution — made causally (exit = severable / low Φ, voice = irreducible
coupling / high Φ, loyalty = what holds the coupling intact), or it collapses into Berlin's liberty
distinction and back into the normative camp.
**Lab bridge.** Coordinative sovereignty, whole cloth. Anything durable feeds back to that program.
**Refutation condition.** Hegel's institutions earn the "at home" claim through their rational content,
not their irreducibility; a platform can be irreducible and estranging. The post has to hold that line
or it becomes apologetics for the wholes the lab studies.

## 9. The ledger

**Claim.** A sober close: what Hegel refutes in the program (the mechanism objection, wherever it stood
up), what the program refutes in the reading of Hegel it started with (coordination was not his glue),
what translated into researchable questions (HC1–HC6 status), and what resisted translation — with
Aufhebung vs. the operators, and the core-merger exhibits as the one computed structure that resembles
sublation, as the closing case.
**Passages.** *PR* Preface (the owl of Minerva) — and nothing else new.
**Secondary.** `[✓]` SEP "Hegel's Dialectics" (Maybee) on sublation's three moments and its immanence
(the movement is "driven by necessity… nothing extraneous"), which is what separates it from any external
partition operation. **Prior art to engage, not rediscover `[✓]`** Christian Fuchs, "Dialectical
Philosophy and Self-Organisation," in Arshinov & Fuchs (eds.), *Causality, Emergence, Self-Organisation*
(Moscow: NIA-Priroda, 2003), pp. 195–244, maps *Aufhebung*'s three senses (dissolve / preserve / lift up)
onto the emergence of new system qualities — but offers no measure, only illustrative examples; the lab's
edge is an *exact computed* structure (q210/q211 merged cores) where a smaller whole persists as a member
of a larger one. `[✓]` Günther, *Beiträge zur Grundlegung einer operationsfähigen Dialektik* (3 vols.,
Meiner, 1976/79/80) — the "operational dialectics" that formalizes the dialectic as
many-valued/polycontextural logic of self-reference, not a quantitative irreducibility measure. HC6's
core-merger sits between the two: the missing quantitative, partition-sensitive step. Name both so the
series is seen to know its ancestors.
**Lab bridge.** The dissolution operators; q210/q211 merged cores.
**Refutation condition.** None; the post is the scoreboard, and it inherits every unresolved objection
above — including the standing one that the operators and the power concept need little of the Φ machinery
to stand.
