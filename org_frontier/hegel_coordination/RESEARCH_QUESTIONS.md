# Research questions — the spine (HC1–HC6)

Project-local numbering (HC = Hegel/coordination). These are mostly textual-analytical questions and do
not enter the computational pipeline's global q-numbering; the two flagged candidates (HC5, HC6) would
graduate through the standard six-stage protocol if pursued as probes. Each question names its sources,
its lab bridge, and what would count as an answer.

## HC1 — Is Teile/Glieder the complex-membership distinction?

**Question.** When Hegel distinguishes parts from members — an aggregate has parts, an organism has
members, and a severed member is no longer what it was — is he stating the property the complex
computes, or a teleological property (contribution to self-maintenance) the complex does not touch?
**Sources.** *PR* §§267–276; *EL* organism passages; secondary: Kreines on Hegel's naturalism.
**Lab bridge.** Complex membership; the exclusion postulate the OT reviewers asked about.
**Answer looks like.** A passage card sorting Hegel's member-criteria into partition-visible and
partition-invisible, with the residue named. Feeds post 6.

## HC2 — The ladder as a graded aggregate/configuration scale

**Question.** Mechanism, chemism, teleology: does Hegel's ladder order arrangements by anything the
lab's vocabulary can restate (external coupling → affinity/binding → self-maintaining whole), and where
exactly does he place the boundary the criterion places at Φ > 0?
**Sources.** *SL* Objectivity; *EL* §§194–212.
**Lab bridge.** The aggregate/configuration boundary; what the criterion brackets.
**Answer looks like.** A three-row mapping with the mismatches carried honestly — chemism especially,
which has no obvious lab analogue. Feeds post 4.

## HC3 — Is recognition Hegel's commit?

**Question.** Reciprocal recognition constitutes the parties as what they are. Does the recognition
relation satisfy the membership law's two halves by construction — each party constraining and
constrained by the joint determination (bidirectionality), the relation sensitive to each party as that
party (identity sensitivity, the thing the interior quorum lacks)?
**Sources.** *PhG* ¶¶178–196; Honneth; Brandom's normative-statuses reading as the rival.
**Lab bridge.** The membership law; identity sensitivity vs. outcome pivotality.
**Answer looks like.** Either a clean structural translation, or a documented finding that recognition's
normativity does work no causal predicate can carry — both publishable in a post. Feeds posts 2 and 8.

## HC4 — The Korporation as necessary integrator

**Question.** Hegel's argument that civil society requires corporations (*PR* §§250–256): is it an
integrating-work argument (the corporation computes a standing no direct tie reproduces) or a
constraint argument (guild privilege holding a conduit in place)? Hegel's own century dissolved the
guilds, which reads like a constraint-lifting natural experiment on his claim.
**Sources.** *PR* §§250–256, §255R; the corporation-to-estate wiring in §§288–308.
**Lab bridge.** The irreducibility catalog's coding rule; the restore-the-tie counterfactual.
**Answer looks like.** A catalog-style card for the Korporation, coded both ways with the load-bearing
rule named. Feeds post 7.

## HC5 — The state-triad toy encoding *(probe candidate)*

**Question.** *EL* §198R sketches the state as three mutually mediating syllogisms. Encode the sketch as
a small transition system, compute Φ and the complex, and document what the encoding captures and what
it constitutively cannot — the announced expectation is partial failure, and the failure's exact shape
is the finding.
**Path.** Through the standard protocol (hypotheses fixed before computation) if pursued; otherwise the
post proceeds on the textual analysis alone.
**Lab bridge.** Encoding-as-theoretical-commitment; the level-of-analysis caution from the OT reviews.
**Feeds.** Post 6.

## HC6 — Sublation as core-merger *(probe candidate)*

**Question.** Aufhebung preserves what it cancels; the dissolution operators evict. The lab's computed
core-merger exhibits (q210/q211: a channel merges two triadic cores into one spanning core) are the one
formal structure where a smaller whole persists inside a larger one. Is that a non-metaphorical foothold
for sublation, and what would distinguish merger-that-preserves from merger-that-replaces in the
cause-effect structure?
**Path.** Starts as analysis of the existing q210/q211 results; any new form goes through the protocol.
**Lab bridge.** The dissolution operators' known incompleteness (the final-gate panel's "not exhaustive"
finding); a possible fourth operator.
**Feeds.** Post 9, and possibly the dissolution program proper — this is the question most likely to
outgrow the series.

## Not questions

Two things the spine deliberately excludes. No question of the form "did Hegel anticipate Φ" — post 1
settles why. And no Hegel-wide survey; the series touches the *Phenomenology*'s self-consciousness
chapters, the *Logic*'s syllogism and objectivity sections, and *Philosophy of Right* §§182–320, and
otherwise stays home.
