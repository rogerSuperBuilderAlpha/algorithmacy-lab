## Claim (correct altitude)
On Hegel's Objectivity ladder (mechanism → chemism → teleology), the partition/Φ criterion reaches only the top of mechanism and reports the same aggregate/configuration cut Hegel draws at the base — but the two orderings run on different axes (Hegel grades self-determination, the instrument grades resistance to partition), so they cross inside the chemical process and part decisively at teleology, where Hegel replaces the criterion with inner purposiveness rather than anticipating a causal one; the homology is same-boundary/different-instrument, never "Hegel anticipated Φ."

## Guard check
Clean on both prohibited moves. (1) Anticipation: the draft explicitly refuses it — "He does not anticipate a causal-irreducibility criterion and wait for someone to compute it. He replaces it" — and the v2 tripwire phrase "cleaner anticipation" is now "cleaner description" (line 160). The word "anticipation" survives only in the guard/negative sense. (2) Demotion of Φ: no sentence calls Φ decorative, hollow, unnecessary, or a calculator; the draft affirms "The instrument's contribution is not diminished by having a ceiling" and "A tool that could not say where its ceiling is would be the one to distrust." ONE WATCH-ITEM, not a breach: the author-owned temper paragraph in "What the Bracketing Buys" ("There are days I think 'richer' is what we call a criterion that cannot be wrong because it cannot be run... I mind conceding the classification more than the papers before this one minded theirs") aims its barb correctly at Hegel's criterion, but the surrounding register drifts toward the self-defeating tone CLAUDE.md flags (modest ≠ self-defeating). It does not demote Φ; the author must own or soften it before posting — the sourcing note already marks this as the one paragraph the author must ratify.

## Refutation condition
Built for Hegel to win the classification and the lab to win the method. The honest form, per Ng and Kreines: Hegel replaces the unity criterion with inner teleology; he does not anticipate a causal one. The post fails if it smuggles proto-Φ back in through the win — if it lets partition-irreducibility become what Hegel's teleology "was really measuring," a reviewer holding Ng will say the reading imports the exact synchronic, cut-from-outside mechanism Hegel's ladder is built to escape. It also fails if either side sweeps: if the lab demotes Hegel's classification to a mere calculator-verdict, or if the concession tips into demoting Φ. The crossing inside chemism (pp. 647–649) is the load-bearing exhibit that makes the anticipation reading false rather than merely disavowed; if that crossing were removed or shown to depend on an uncomputed cross-system comparison, the paper's one original result collapses to "our formalism is decidable and Hegel's criterion is not," which is table stakes, not a finding.

## Secondary sources (anchors / threats / prior-art)

- **kreines2008** — Kreines, "The Logic of Life: Hegel's Philosophical Defense of Teleological Explanation of Living Beings," in Beiser (ed.), Cambridge Companion to Hegel and 19th-Century Philosophy (2008), ch. 13, pp. 344–377 *[anchor, gated]*  
  Living beings "do manifest true 'internal purposiveness,'" "explicable in teleological terms," and "we can have objective knowledge of this natural teleology" (p. 346); "Hegel accords great philosophical importance to Kant's discussions of teleology... and yet also disagrees with Kant's central conclusions" (p. 344). - **ng2020** — Ng, Hegel's Concept of Life: Self-Consciousness, Freedom, Logic (OUP 2020) *[threat, verified]*

- **ebeturk2023** — Ebeturk, "Between Chemism and Life: Is Hegel's Teleology Misplaced?" Hegel Bulletin 44/1 (2023), pp. 46–73 *[threat, verified]*  
  Argues teleology is misplaced and "the logic of 'Life' and internal teleology should precede" external teleology, allowing "a direct passage from 'Chemism' to 'Life'" (p. 46). - **koch2023** — Koch, "Mechanism, External Purposiveness, and Object Individuation: from Mechanism to Teleology in Hegel's Science of Logic," Hegel Bulletin 44/1 (2023), pp. 148–170 *[prior-art, verified]*
