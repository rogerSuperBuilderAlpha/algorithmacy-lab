## Step 0 — register

The first-person film-essay register is part of the chapter's design. This review treats it as settled and
audits the apparatus: note keys, note-to-sentence fit, bibliography coverage, arithmetic, Chicago
mechanics, and quotation hygiene. The current text has fixed the large defects from the earlier
copyedit pass, but it is not yet press-clean.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor and structure

   `1` through `71`, plus `18a`, `20a`, `47a`, and `71a`. The calls are in strict ascending order. No
   body call lacks a definition. No definition is orphaned. No key is called twice in the body.

5. **The Hansen arithmetic error survived.**

   Verified from the quoted sentence in the chapter. The body says:

   > calling cinema "the single most inclusive cultural horizon in which the traumatic effects of modernity were reflected, rejected or disavowed, transmuted or negotiated" — though she bounds herself to 1920s-to-1950s mainstream Hollywood, and *negotiated* is the last of four verbs in a list that opens with rejected.

   The printed list has five participles: reflected, rejected, disavowed, transmuted, negotiated. It opens
   with reflected.

   Paste-ready fix:

   > calling cinema "the single most inclusive cultural horizon in which the traumatic effects of modernity were reflected, rejected or disavowed, transmuted or negotiated" — though she bounds herself to 1920s-to-1950s mainstream Hollywood, and *negotiated* is the last of five participles in a list that starts with reflected and reaches rejection before it reaches negotiation.

6. **One percentage sentence is arithmetically possible but not grammatically safe.**

   The Kuleshov paragraph says:

   > on the gravestone sequence all twenty made the spatio-temporal link and eleven read sadness into the face, on the soup sequence the figure was 30 percent, and on the child sequence nobody showed the effect at all.

   "The figure" has two possible antecedents: spatio-temporal linking or sadness. The sentence means the
   emotion effect, but it makes the reader count backward.

   Paste-ready fix:

   > on the gravestone sequence all twenty made the spatio-temporal link and eleven read sadness into the face; on the soup sequence, six of twenty read hunger; on the child sequence, nobody showed the effect at all.

   Keep "six of twenty" only if the source gives 30 percent on a base of twenty.

7. **Chicago serial comma use is inconsistent in the body.**

   Notes and bibliography mostly use the serial comma. The body often does not. Examples:

   - "Rivera, Soderstrom and Uzzi" -> "Rivera, Soderstrom, and Uzzi"
   - "Katherine Kellogg, Melissa Valentine, and Angèle Christin" already follows the rule; keep this as
     the model.
   - "Münsterberg, Benjamin and Hansen" -> "Münsterberg, Benjamin, and Hansen"
   - "film, cinema, montage or shot" -> "film, cinema, montage, or shot"

   Apply the serial comma across the body unless preserving a quotation.

8. **Short forms and italics still need a Chicago sweep.**

   Verified from the current notes and bibliography.

   - Note 43 uses "Berg's own definition." The surname is Ramírez Berg. Use "Ramírez Berg's own
     definition."
   - Note 62 and the bibliography should italicize the film title inside the article title: "What Would
     *Jaws* Do?"
   - Note 52 has "beschikking" in roman. If treated as a foreign legal term, italicize it on first use:
     "*beschikking* of April 4, 2023." If treated as part of the Dutch title of the decision, capitalize
     consistently with the bibliography.
   - Note 61 and the bibliography read as if "6, 1–17" were page data. Use "article 6, 1–17."
   - Note 48 gives "*Proceedings of FAccT '25*" on first citation. The bibliography has the full
     proceedings title. The note should too, or should introduce the abbreviation.

9. **The bibliography sort keys mostly pass.**

   Verified from the current bibliography. Ramírez Berg files under R, May under M, Van Kleek under V,
   De Massis under D inside Kwon et al., and Gagrčin under G with the diacritic. The cases are separated
   from works cited. The primary/documentary section is a useful split.

   Two small cleanup points remain:

   - The *New York Times* item beginning "A $23,000 Film..." should be alphabetized by the first

   - The primary/documentary section mixes database records, web pages, periodicals, a film, and
     government publications. That is acceptable, but the missing access data makes it look less finished

10. **Quotation hygiene is better, but two quotations still need author-only checks.**

   - Note 37 has been demoted to paraphrase. Good. It still says "Pierson, quoted in Marc Savlov" and
     then says the chapter is paraphrasing rather than quoting. That is awkward but safe.
   - Note 71a quotes the oblique-strategies card and says Ron Rosenbaum gives the line, while several

## Part 2 — The four bans, and AI-slop audit

**Ban 1, metaphor doing argumentative work:** no copyediting objection. The remaining figures are titles
or are backed by literal claims nearby.

**Ban 2, throat-clearing:** one small hit. "One more thing the questions do not do is demote" announces a
move the next sentence makes directly. It is not a copyediting priority.

**Ban 3, meta-commentary / forward-backward reference:** mostly clear. The old "described above" problem
is gone. "What it adds instead is a fourth thing the three questions do not reach" is a cross-reference,
but it is also the sentence that names the new power, so it can stand.

**Ban 4, unexplained jargon:** mostly outside this lens. From a copyediting standpoint, "nonterminal,"
"assortative matching," and "apperceptions" are the terms most likely to need a short appositive for a
general reader. The chapter already glosses several harder terms well.

**AI-slop audit:** no major copyediting finding. The current prose still uses distinction-heavy syntax,
but this chapter is an argument about distinctions. The copyediting risk is not cadence. It is apparatus
finish.
