# Peer Review 01 — Film-Narrative-Theory Lens

**Chapter:** "Austin Hosts, the Camera Engineers: *Slacker* and the Structural Third" (Roger Hunt)
**Reviewer frame:** a film narrative theorist reading in the lineages of Bordwell (constructivist narration), Branigan (narrative comprehension / focalization), Chatman (story/discourse, the cinematic narrator), the network-narrative literature, Ramírez Berg's alternative-plot taxonomy, and Poulaki on complex causality/self-organization.

---

## Step 0 — Register

The register is a deliberately first-person film-studies chapter for an edited collection on Linklater's *Slacker* — an essayistic, argument-driven house style, not impersonal journal prose. I judge it against that genre's invariants: named agents and active voice, claim-first sentences, concrete anchors, verified citations, rhythm variation, no slop. The first person, the em-dashes, and the analytic triplets/parallelism are correct for the genre and are not flagged. Slop here means the specific residue that survives even in good essay prose: agentless passives, verbatim repetition, uniform paragraph shape, filler transitions, empty tag-closers, and citations I cannot confirm.

## Part 1 — Theoretical rigor & structure, §1–§7

### §1 — The coherence puzzle

**Argument.** The section poses the puzzle cleanly: a plotless, protagonist-less, hundred-character relay that "should be unwatchable" yet was watched, enjoyed, and — the load-bearing premise — followed without anyone getting lost. The Linklater-taxi opening is used well as the film's own account of its method ("hold the film to its word"), and the reconciliation of Macor's "least auteur film ever made" with Rosenbaum's "very subtle, extremely well-crafted structure" is the chapter's pivot: the coordinating work belongs to *a position, not a person* — a position an author or an algorithm can occupy. That is a genuine and economical hinge.

**Construct fairness.** The lineage survey (Rosenbaum, Pierson, the 1991 critics, Stone's Deleuzian reading, Macor) is fair and non-strawmanned. The Deleuze handling is especially disciplined: "The Deleuzian account names what has come loose; it doesn't name what still binds." That is the right way to clear rival ground without dismissing it.

**What a referee would demand.** One premise carries the entire chapter and is asserted, not shown: "Audiences watched it and enjoyed it, and never seemed lost in it." The whole puzzle — and all of §6 — rests on this reception claim, yet §1 offers no evidence that viewers *followed* (as opposed to drifted, dozed, or enjoyed *because* they stopped tracking). Canby's "14-course meal of desserts" and Ebert's "we don't get a story, but we do get a feeling" arguably cut the other way — they describe pleasure *without* the following the chapter needs. Demand: either cite reception evidence for the specific claim that audiences tracked the relay, or soften "never seemed lost" to a defended, narrower claim (e.g., "reviewers reported orientation rather than confusion"). This recurs in §6 and is the chapter's most exposed empirical premise.

*Minor:* "Scholarship has read Linklater through this speech before (Speed 2007)" uses an abstract collective agent where a named one is available — see Part 2.

### §2 — The structural third

**Argument.** The theoretical engine. Simmel's two-to-three step, the brokerage lineage (Burt's structural holes, Obstfeld's *tertius iungens*, Gould–Fernandez), then the counter-move: Feld's *focus* as a third that "brokers no pair and reads no one," furnishing an environment so ties fall out of it. The coinage — *host* (focus-as-third) vs *engineer* (pair-brokering third) — and the three-question test (what does it act on; is it dispensable or constitutive; does it read kinds or the particular parties) are the analytic apparatus the rest of the chapter runs on. The "neighbors" paragraph (Oldenburg, Klinenberg, Anderson, Lofland) is well-read and correctly positions Lofland as closest ("her locational order... already *is* the host's code").

**Construct fairness / load-bearing weak point #1 (the three-question test vs the camera).** The test is airtight as stated, but it does not cleanly license the chapter's own title. Apply the three questions to the camera, as §4–§5 eventually force us to: (1) *acts on the field or these parties?* — it picks *this* scene next, engineer-like; (2) *dispensable or constitutive?* — it is the only way the viewer reaches the next scene, engineer-like; (3) *reads kinds or the particular parties, adjusting?* — it does **not** read anyone back ("The camera selects and sequences; it doesn't score. Nothing a character does feeds into the next pick," §5). On axis 3 the camera behaves like a *host*, not an engineer. So the camera is a hybrid: engineer on 1–2, host-like on 3. The chapter knows this — §5's "symmetry breaks" *is* axis 3 failing — but §2 defines "a pure engineer... reads the parties themselves" and then names the camera "the engineer" without warning the reader that the film's engineer fails exactly the reading test. Demand: when the three-question test is introduced, state that the camera passes 1 and 2 but not 3, and that axis 3 is precisely where platforms differ (they read you back). This costs one sentence and converts an apparent equivocation into the chapter's sharpest distinction. As it stands the point survives, but only because the reader does the reconciling the author should have done.

**Overclaim to soften.** "Focus theory and brokerage theory have somehow never been introduced. Consider this the introduction." This is a strong originality claim and it reads as overstated — Feld's foci and network brokerage are routinely discussed in the same network-sociology conversation. VERIFY, or soften to "have rarely been seated in the same triad" / "have not, to my knowledge, been contrasted as two ways of holding Simmel's third position." The narrower claim is defensible and still does the work.

**Cut/keep.** Keep the whole apparatus; it is the chapter's contribution. "That's what lets Austin and the camera share a sentence" is a tag-closer that mostly restates — see Part 2, low priority.

### §3 — Austin hosts

**Argument.** The story-world / setting analysis, and it is strong. The claim that plausibility "is the city's doing — Austin stages none of them, it makes all of them possible, in bulk" is the host thesis made concrete. The sociology is well-marshalled and, importantly, *inverted* where inversion is earned: the monologues are licensed **not** by Goffman's civil inattention but by its opposite (the rule that makes an opened encounter cost more to close than to endure), and Milgram's familiar stranger is named as the resting state that *crisis* breaks — tying directly back to the body in the street that opens the film. That is precise, load-bearing use of the sources, not decoration.

**Construct fairness.** Lofland (locational order → parochial realm), Goffman (open region), Milgram (familiar stranger + crisis), and the agglomeration economics (Duranton & Puga: sharing/matching/learning) are all used accurately. The political honesty is a real strength: "The blindness that matters here is blindness to persons, not to kinds... the same code that lets a white twenty-something pitch Madonna's pap smear on the Drag polices other bodies off it, and a mile east the film hosts no one at all." The host is named as exclusionary, not romanticized. And the Gaughen geography complication — the film's map is fictional, the city hosts the *milieu* not the *map* — is exactly the honest move that sets up §4 ("Something else does the joining").

**What a referee would demand.** Very little. §3 is almost entirely urban sociology with no film-formal analysis, but that is by design (the camera is §4's job), so it is not a fault. One optional demand: the "improving the odds without proposing the match is the host in a sentence" formula is good once; watch that the "X in a sentence / in a word" construction (recurs in §5 as "*tertius gaudens* with a meter") does not become a tic.

### §4 — The camera engineers

**Argument.** The film-theory heart, and where a narrative-theory referee spends most time. The close-ish reading of the first handoff (Linklater → the matricide driver → the bystander: "Two abandonments in the opening minutes... teach you the rule you'll watch by") is the chapter's best passage of actual film analysis. The Pierson craft note is handled with real care: "He doesn't mean there are no cuts; the arrest itself is cut together. He means no cut breaks the flow." The constraint paragraph — "the camera can only leave with a body the city has already put in the frame... The engineering rides on the hosting" — is the hinge that makes host and engineer interdependent rather than parallel, and it is correctly flagged as the difference the platforms abolish.

**Construct fairness — the Bordwell/Chatman handling and load-bearing weak point #2 (anthropomorphism).** The chapter stages the classic dispute correctly: Bordwell (narration is a *process*, not a person; the camera-as-storyteller is "an anthropomorphic fiction") vs Chatman (every film implies a narrating agency immanent to it). It then "rest[s] on the weaker thing both grant" — the *narration* as process — and makes "the camera" shorthand for it, needing "only that its selections are systematic and that you take them as what happens next." This is the right defensive move, and it is executed honestly. But it does not fully survive the chapter's own diction. Having disavowed agency via Bordwell, the chapter then relies on relentlessly *agentive* verbs — the camera "picks," "reads," "engineers," "composes," "governs"; §5's "one third composes; the other governs" is the thesis line. A Bordwellian referee will say: systematicity is not agency, and "engineer" is the most agentive metaphor in the book. The inoculating sentence ("the argument needs only that its selections are systematic") is doing a great deal of work and is stated once, quietly, then abandoned as the metaphor escalates. Demand: own the personification explicitly as a *controlled* figure — one sentence conceding that "engineer," "picks," and "governs" are deliberate shorthand for a process with no one home, and that nothing in the argument requires a homunculus. This is the difference between a referee waving it through and a referee writing "the chapter reintroduces precisely the anthropomorphism it claimed Bordwell licensed it to avoid." As written, it *survives*, but by a margin the author should widen.

**The two-graph / star formalism.** Elegant and largely fair. Story world = a bare chain (Berg's "daisy chain," *Slacker* as his example); narration = a star, every scene tied to the one off-screen hub that selected it; the camera's path is "what welds the first graph into the second." Two cautions. (a) This is, in narratological terms, the fabula/syuzhet distinction (Bordwell) or story/discourse (Chatman) re-drawn as two graphs — the chapter never says so. A referee will want the author to *acknowledge* the re-description and defend the value-add (the value-add is real: two *species of Simmelian third*, plus the platform bridge). Naming it costs nothing and pre-empts "this is story/discourse with new labels." (b) The chapter borrows Bordwell's balk as "the datum" ("This isn't a network so much as a wiggly, knotted string," Bordwell 2008, 215) and then reinterprets the *string* as the camera's path and adds the *star* as its own contribution. Fine, but be plain: the star is the author's, not Bordwell's — Bordwell's string is a chain metaphor, not a hub metaphor. The chapter is *mostly* honest here ("The string is the camera's path... The film runs two graphs at once"), but a reader could mistake the star for Bordwell's. One clause fixes it.

**The viewer-vs-character clarification and load-bearing weak point #3 (who is coordinated).** This is the single most important structural issue, and the chapter both raises and under-resolves it. §2–§3 say the third "coordinates people who never directly interact" — people meaning *characters* (the moon-landing man and his captive listener). But §4 corrects course with unusual candor: "it isn't the characters... the camera coordinates *me*, the viewer, with the next of them. The dyad the camera brokers is viewer-and-character, run serially down the chain." That correction is admirable — and it fractures the tidy symmetry of §2's closing line, "Austin hosts while the camera engineers... working the same crowd." They are **not** working the same crowd on the same relation. The host coordinates character↔character (furnishes encounters among the diegetic strangers); the engineer coordinates viewer↔character (delivers the next thing to look at). Host and engineer therefore operate on *different planes* — story-world versus discourse — and are not two species acting on one dyad. The "one third composes; the other governs" comparison, and the whole platform bridge, quietly depend on treating them as commensurable thirds. Demand: add an explicit paragraph reconciling this — the host is a third *among the characters*, the engineer a third *between viewer and character*; what unifies them as "thirds" is the Simmelian *position*, not a shared relatum, and the star/chain graph should label which nodes are characters, which are scenes, and where the viewer sits. This does not sink the chapter; the §4 correction shows the author sees it. But right now the reader is handed the tension and left to resolve it. **This is the referee's number-one required fix.**

**The Poulaki engagement.** Correctly named "the most serious rival." Poulaki (2014) explains coherence in centerless multi-character films through parts interacting until the film self-organizes — reflexive loops, recurring motifs — *without any broker*. The chapter grants the resonance (the talk circles media/paranoia/conspiracy; the film ends with the cast turning cameras on themselves) and then draws the line: "What recurrence can't do is make the join from this scene to the next one; theme rhymes across the cuts but doesn't cause them. The host supplies the rhyming, the relay supplies the joins, and her mechanism has no seat for the relayer." This is a genuinely good rebuttal and the division of labor (host = rhyming, relay = joins) is clarifying. A Poulaki defender has one live counter the chapter does not close: in a self-organizing account the cuts are *motivated by the system*, an emergent regularity, not by a "relayer" that needs a "seat" — i.e., Poulaki would deny that "who joins scene to scene" requires an occupant at all, exactly as Bordwell denies narration requires a narrator. Note the structural rhyme: the chapter's answer to Poulaki ("there must be a relayer") is in tension with its concession to Bordwell ("no one need be home"). Demand: one sentence distinguishing a *systematic selection process* (which the chapter is entitled to, post-Bordwell) from a *seated relayer* (which Poulaki will refuse) — otherwise the anti-Poulaki move and the anti-anthropomorphism move pull against each other.

**Absent interlocutor — Branigan / focalization.** Branigan is named in the reviewer brief and is the most conspicuous no-show. The relay ("narration following a body until another body crosses it") is, in Branigan/Genette terms, a migrating *external focalization* — the narration attaches to a body, then re-attaches. Naming it as shifting focalization would sharpen §4 and, more importantly, would arm §6: Branigan's schema-based *narrative comprehension* is precisely the cognitive account of the "competence" §6 needs and currently sources only from Benjamin/Kuleshov. Demand (strong suggestion, not blocking): engage focalization in §4 and Bordwell/Branigan constructivism in §6.

**Shot-level thinness.** For a narrative-theory chapter the actual formal analysis is light — the mechanism ("in the shot or across a cut that rides the movement") is asserted more than demonstrated. One full handoff read at the level of framing, offscreen space, eyeline, and character movement would convert an assertion into a demonstration and is the kind of thing only someone who has watched the film frame-by-frame can supply. See closing note.

### §5 — The new engineers

**Argument.** The payoff of the "engineer" concept, and it earns its keep. Uber as pair-brokering third (the ~15-second blind accept; only the algorithm sees the queue; nothing lets rider and driver settle anything themselves), Stark & Pais's co-optation and "triangular geometry" ("rankings but not ranks... accounts but no accountability"), Ajunwa's *tertius bifrons*, Stark & Vanden Broeck building platform theory "straight from the triad." The chapter's stated value-add is properly narrow: "the platform is an engineering third, and by the time it arrived we'd spent a century watching one work."

**Where the symmetry breaks — the strongest passage.** "The camera selects and sequences; it doesn't score... The platform's third reads everything back... It prices each encounter it composes: surge is composition as incentive. And it can deactivate, which is to say delete a party from the field... The camera can pass you over. It cannot remove you from Austin. One third composes; the other governs." This is the best analytic writing in the chapter — the score/telemetry break, the pricing break, and the deactivation break are three real, non-overlapping differences, and "composes vs governs" is *earned* here rather than asserted. The Adorno anticipation ("cinema's aesthetic ends were economic ends too... the difference that survives him is telemetry") is exactly the objection a referee would raise, pre-empted honestly.

**Load-bearing tension carried over from §4 (feed vs dispatch).** The chapter concedes in §4 that "the camera's nearest kin is not Uber's dispatcher... but the feed" — the recommender that brings one viewer to the next thing, brokering no pair. Yet §5 leads with Uber/dispatch (a two-party broker), justified as "the harder comparison... since the way it breaks is instructive." This is defensible and openly flagged, but it leaves the titular "engineer" straddling two disanalogous machines: the dispatch broker (picks *pairs*, reads *parties*) and the feed (serves *one* viewer, brokers *no* pair). By the chapter's own three-question test the camera is the *feed's* kin, not dispatch's — which loops back to weak point #1. Demand: state once, cleanly, that the camera is a *feed-type* engineer (single-user, no scoring) and that dispatch is invoked as the *limit case* the feed is drifting toward, so the reader is not left thinking the camera "picks pairs." The pieces are all here; they need one ordering sentence.

**Minor.** "the shots of it that exist are stolen from outside" — agentless passive, see Part 2.

### §6 — Cinema as training

**Argument.** The most ambitious and most speculative section: where did a 1991 audience get the competence to follow a feature-length engineering third and enjoy it? Benjamin (film trains the "apperceptions and reactions needed to deal with a vast apparatus"), Hansen's vernacular modernism (with the correct emphasis that her audience *negotiated*, was not merely drilled), Kuleshov (viewers read relations between shots never together), continuity editing as trained-in reflex, and Linklater's own channel-surfing/remote-control account of the design. Then Beller (looking as value-productive labor), Manovich (database vs narrative), Ciafone (network film as neoliberalism's flattering self-portrait — "the daisy chain... is that form with the flattery stripped").

**Construct fairness.** The sources are represented accurately and the chapter is careful to *bracket* rather than annex the strongest ones: "My claim is narrower than Beller's, and I'm bracketing his value theory rather than refuting it." The two closing limits — training-to-follow is not training-to-contest; the contesting skill is being built now "the hard way," by platform workers pooling theories of a dispatcher they can't see — are the section's best political turn and connect back to §5's "governs."

**Load-bearing weak point (shared with §1).** The section both asserts and disclaims its central mechanism, and the disclaimer is the honest thing: "That last step is a conjecture about reception, and I'll flag it as one: nobody has shown that the skill a moviegoer brings to a cut is the skill a driver brings to the dispatch queue, and only a transfer study could." The falsifiable version offered (continuity-editing fluency predicting speed of building a working theory of an algorithm; Möhlmann et al.) is genuinely well-formed and to the chapter's credit. But the inferential distance from Kuleshov ("viewers infer a relation between two shots") to the chapter's need ("viewers can follow an unseen brokering third across a plotless hundred-minute film and enjoy it") is *large*, and honesty about the last step does not shrink the earlier steps. Demand: (a) recruit Bordwell/Branigan constructivism to bridge Kuleshov-to-competence — the trained *schema* is narratology's own answer and it is missing; (b) tie §6's claim back to §1's under-supported "never seemed lost," since they stand or fall together. If §1's reception premise is softened, §6 must be too.

**Fairness note.** The Benjamin reading is nuanced (the wager was *hopeful* — an examiner, if a distracted one — and the chapter's thesis is "that training captured, the examiner's posture kept, the exam taken away"). That is a fair and non-appropriative use of a text often flattened in this literature.

### §7 — Enclosure

**Argument.** The distribution coda: the chain that carried *Slacker* was "a chain of thirds, every one of them human and reachable" (Pierson as professional *tertius iungens*, the Dobie, Orion's advance, video stores), then two enclosures — Miramax/Perren (studios capturing the indie chain, but the third "was still a person you could name and fight"), and the streaming turn (specialty-division shutdowns c. 2008–10, CODA/Fair Play sales, Smits on brokers adapting to Netflix/Amazon, the proprietary recommender). The sharp move: the enclosure runs through the *infrastructure* (what is acquired, licensed, surfaced), not the *interface* — so Frey's finding that viewers ignore recommendations does not rescue anyone. "By the time you ignore it, the platform has already chosen what was acquired." The Oblique Strategies close ("Withdrawing in disgust is not the same thing as apathy") lands the film's politics well and "The platform keeps the day" is a strong final signature.

**Construct fairness.** Careful and non-overreaching; the Frey complication is exactly the kind of self-imposed counter-evidence a good referee rewards.

**What a referee would demand / cut.** §7 is the section a *narrative-theory* referee cares least about — it is media-industries economics, largely detached from the host/engineer narratology that is the chapter's distinctive contribution. It is defensible as the "enclosure" payoff, and the through-line (thirds you can name and fight vs proprietary thirds you can't read) is real. But if the collection imposes length limits, this is the first place to compress: the CODA/Fair Play dollar figures and the Smits/Tzioumakis detail could lose a third of their words without weakening the argument. Demand: keep the "infrastructure not interface" point (it is the section's reason to exist) and tighten the industry recitation around it.

**Minor.** "Most of the specialty divisions were shuttered in a wave around 2008–2010" and "the nineties boom absorbed into a studio niche-market strategy" — agentless passives, see Part 2.

### Load-bearing weak points — summary of what survives

1. **Who is coordinated (the plane-mismatch), §2 vs §4.** The most important. Host coordinates character↔character; engineer coordinates viewer↔character. "Working the same crowd" overstates commensurability. *Survives* because §4 self-corrects, but the correction must be promoted into an explicit reconciling paragraph. **Required.**
2. **The three-question test vs the camera (axis 3), §2/§5.** The camera fails the "reads the parties" test, so "the engineer" is a hybrid label. *Survives* — §5's "symmetry breaks" is this very point — but §2 should flag it on introduction rather than leaving the reader to reconcile.
3. **Anthropomorphism, §4.** The agentive metaphor ("engineer," "picks," "governs") strains the Bordwellian disavowal it rests on, and pulls against the anti-Poulaki insistence on a "seated relayer." *Survives* by a narrow margin; widen it with one sentence owning the figure as controlled shorthand.

None is fatal. All three are addressable with sentences, not surgery — which is why the verdict below is minor, not major.

## Part 2 — AI-slop audit (register-aware)

Register defends the first person, the em-dashes, the triplets, the abstract lines, and the signature lines; none of those are flagged. The following are genuine residue.

**1. Agentless passive — HIGH — §5, line 224.**
> "Simmel has been seen here before — Ajunwa reads the hiring platform as a two-faced *tertius*..."

"has been seen" is a true agentless passive, made conspicuous by the named agents arriving one clause later. Rewrite in the piece's own active first person:
> "Others reached Simmel here before I did — Ajunwa reads the hiring platform as a two-faced *tertius*, and Stark and Vanden Broeck build the platform's theory straight from the triad."

**2. Agentless passive — HIGH — §7, line 330.**
> "Most of the specialty divisions were shuttered in a wave around 2008–2010 (Tzioumakis 2023)..."

Who shuttered them is the whole point (the studios). Rewrite:
> "The studios shuttered most of their specialty divisions in a wave around 2008–2010 (Tzioumakis 2023), and theatrical indie distribution thinned."

**3. Abstract-collective agent for a named one — MEDIUM-HIGH — §1, line 15.**
> "Scholarship has read Linklater through this speech before (Speed 2007)..."

A named scholar is right there. Rewrite:
> "Lesley Speed read Linklater through this speech before I did (Speed 2007), so I'm not claiming a discovery — only that we should hold the film to its word."

**4. Uniform paragraph-closer shape — MEDIUM — passim.**
The short blunt declarative closer is effective once but is the chapter's dominant paragraph-ending shape: "Something else does the joining." / "The camera takes it." / "The movies had." / "The platform keeps the day." / "One third composes; the other governs." The signature lines are off-limits and stay. But the *habit* of ending on a two-to-five-word punch has become a metronome (this is the closer-position cousin of the mechanized-opener tic). Fix: let three or four paragraphs instead close on the long sentence that carries the idea, so the punch lands where it is chosen, not where it is expected. No single sentence is the failure; the uniform placement is.

**5. Agentless passive — LOW-MEDIUM — §5, line 253.**
> "the shots of it that exist are stolen from outside. Nobody is allowed to shoot the matching engine."

Rewrite the first clause active:
> "reporters and researchers steal the few shots of it that exist. Nobody is allowed to shoot the matching engine."

**6. Agentless nominalization — LOW — §7, line 324.**
> "Perren's history of Miramax shows the nineties boom absorbed into a studio niche-market strategy..."

Rewrite:
> "Perren's history of Miramax shows studios absorbing the nineties boom into a niche-market strategy, indie inside Disney, booming harder for the capture (Perren 2012)."

**7. Empty tag-closer — LOW — §2, line 92.**
> "That's what lets Austin and the camera share a sentence."

Mostly restates the prior clause. It is doing light justificatory work for the coinage, so it is borderline rather than clear slop; if trimming for space, this is expendable.

*Checked and cleared (not slop, register-correct):* "Two items in the record are almost too good, but they're true, so I'll use them" (voice, not self-narration of virtue); "Consider this the introduction" (voice); "Something else does the joining" / "The camera takes it" (individually fine — the issue is only their cumulative placement, item 4); all em-dashes; all triplets; the abstract and signature lines.

## Verdict

**Minor revisions (accept with minor revisions).** The argument is coherent, the sources are used accurately and often inventively, the chapter anticipates its strongest objections (Adorno, Poulaki, Frey, the reception conjecture) and flags its own limits with unusual honesty. The three load-bearing weak points are real but each is closable with a paragraph or a sentence, not restructuring.

**Single most important fix:** promote §4's buried self-correction into an explicit reconciliation of *who each third coordinates* — Austin among the characters, the camera between viewer and character. Until that is on the page, "two thirds working the same crowd" and "one composes, the other governs" rest on a commensurability the chapter has not earned. (See the §4 paste-in above.)

## Biggest genuine strength (from this lens)

The host/engineer distinction is a genuinely generative reframing rather than an analogy dressed up. It re-describes the oldest distinction in film narratology — story/discourse, fabula/syuzhet — as *two species of Simmelian third*, and then makes that re-description do analytic work the original distinction cannot: it turns *Slacker* into a working model of the difference between a coordinating environment and a coordinating broker, and it lets that difference travel to the platform without collapsing into loose "film-as-metaphor-for-algorithm" gesture. "One third composes; the other governs" is earned by §5's three concrete breaks (scoring, pricing, deactivation), not asserted. And the chapter's discipline about its own reach — bracketing Beller instead of annexing him, flagging the transfer claim as a conjecture and offering a falsifiable form of it — is exactly the intellectual honesty a referee hopes for and rarely gets.

## The one thing only the author can supply

A single handoff read shot by shot. The mechanism at the center of the chapter — "the narration following a body until another body crosses it, in the shot or across a cut that rides the movement" — is currently asserted, and the whole "engineer" claim would convert from argument to demonstration if one relay were analyzed at the level of framing, offscreen space, eyeline, and character movement (how, precisely, does the camera *pick up* the stranger — a look, a crossing, a pan, a match on movement?). No reviewer can fabricate that reading; it requires the author's eye on the film. The same is true of the reception premise: only the author can go find (or concede the absence of) evidence that 1991 audiences *followed* rather than merely enjoyed drifting. Those two — the frame-level demonstration and the reception grounding — are the author's to supply, and they are what would move this from a very good chapter to an unanswerable one.
