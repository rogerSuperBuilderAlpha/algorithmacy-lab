## Part 1 — Formal soundness under the new insertions

### 1.1 The three-question test still holds, and it is good sociology

The test (§2) — what does the third act on, could the encounter have happened without it,
what does it read — maps cleanly onto the formal literature without saying so: question one
is the dyadic-versus-n-adic scope that separates every Gould & Fernandez role from Feld's
focus; question two is Burt's nonredundancy (a broker earns only where the parties cannot
route around, which is also Stovel & Shaw's instability point); question three is the
information condition. Footnote 13's gloss that G&F and Stovel & Shaw "stay inside this
frame" is accurate — all five G&F roles are defined on a triad conditioned on group
membership, and Stovel & Shaw define brokerage on a bridged dyad. The corners-not-binary
paragraph (matchmaker as skippable pair-picker; feed as reader that brokers no pair) is
exactly right and preempts the obvious referee attack. No wince in §2.

### 1.2 THE FINDING: the club micro-thirds flunk the chapter's own test (§5)

> "So are the film's small engineering thirds: a doorman holding a guest list that's
> missing Steve's name, a paid-entry stamp pressed onto a cousin's licked wrist, a barman
> emptying the club at two."

Run the trio through the three questions:

- **Doorman + guest list.** Pair-picking: no — he admits or refuses individuals into a
  setting; he never selects which two people meet inside. Only-route: yes. Reads
  particulars: yes (a name-level list). Two of three. In Gould & Fernandez's typology this
  is precisely the **gatekeeper** — the one brokerage role the example actually fits — and
  gatekeeping is admission control on a group boundary, not mediation of a pair.
- **Entry stamp.** It reads a token, not a party; it is a credential. Only-route at best.
  One of three, generously.
- **Barman emptying the club at two.** Acts on the whole crowd, uniformly, by clock, reading
  no one. On the chapter's own test that is *host-side maintenance* — zero of three. As
  written it is the weakest link a hostile referee will pull.

The paragraph's real point — these thirds are visible, arguable, gameable, where the
platform's are proprietary — survives entirely; the classification is what strains. Fix
with a clause that names them as boundary work (Part 3, R1). Two bonuses come free: "gate
work" quietly foreshadows §7, where Smits's literal subject is distribution *gatekeeping*;
and the barman becomes usable against §5's own deactivation point if the author ever wants
it — the club's eviction is universal, visible, and by clock; the platform's deactivation is
targeted, invisible, and by score. (Optional; costs words.)

### 1.3 "Handing the selection out" — half true as written (§4, PixelVision)

> "A film that ran on a hidden selector ends by handing the selection out."

What gets handed out is the *camera* — the shooting position. The selection — which
footage reaches the viewer, where it sits, when it cuts — never leaves the hub: the passed
camera famously holds no film (participation without capture, which is actually the sharper
point), and even the ending the sentence celebrates is there because the hidden selector
chose to end on it. In the chapter's own §4 terms (the coordinated party is the viewer),
the guests never gain the seat that coordinates viewers; the hub gave the spokes cameras,
not the hub. The line is too good to lose and one clause saves it (Part 3, R3).

### 1.4 The star/chain/welding paragraph: sound, one ambiguity, one enrichment (§4)

As revised the paragraph is formally solid and its best sentence is real graph reasoning in
plain English: "Strip out every durable character-to-character tie … and what still holds
the film together is the welding" is a deletion test — remove all lateral edges and
connectivity survives only through the hub, which is what maximal hub-dependence *means*.
That sentence is the chapter's strongest formal move and should not be touched.

Two notes, neither fatal:

- **Direction of the weld.** "The camera's path is what welds the first shape into the
  second" — chain into star. The cleaner statement is that the chain is the star's
  traversal record: the hub picks the leaves (star), and visiting them in order strings
  them into a chain. The sentence survives because the deletion test that follows does the
  clarifying, but the two shapes also live on different node sets (characters with shared
  sidewalks; scenes tied to the hub), so a formalist can ask what exactly is being welded
  to what. Optional tightening in Part 3 (R11); as written it passes.
- **The *Ulysses* quote is carrying more than the chapter spends.** In Ithaca the "series"
  is Molly's suitors — men who never meet one another and form a series *only through her*.
  That is the star-told-as-a-chain in miniature: serial in the telling, hub-dependent in
  the structure, which is the exact double shape the paragraph just built. One sentence
  would cash it (Part 3, R12, optional, word-funded). The quoted phrases themselves are
  accurate to Joyce ("the last term of a preceding series," "originating in and repeated to
  infinity"). Whether the bridge scene reads *these lines* is a disc check — author list.

### 1.5 The chaos monologue as Poulaki's rival (§4): the right shape of argument

Letting the film voice the self-organization account ("there is no structure," "the
underlying order is chaos") and answering "But somebody chose where his scene goes" is
formally correct: shared motifs are node attributes; attribute resonance rhymes across
edges but generates no edge. "A theme rhymes across the cuts but doesn't cause them" is the
right claim at the right grain, and the division of labor (host carries resonance, relay
makes joins) was accepted July 3 and still holds. The monologue quotes are new film content
— author disc/script verification, per the July 3 rule.

### 1.6 The choice-set paragraph is Feld operationalized — but the Uber contrast overreaches (§4)

"The camera can only leave with a body the city has already put in the frame" is the focus
constraining the tie pool — Feld's actual mechanism, staged. Excellent addition. But:

> "A dispatch algorithm is not limited to whoever happens to be standing nearby."

Uber's matcher is famously proximity-based — nearest-driver / minimum-ETA within a radius.
A platform-literate referee will pounce. The true disanalogy is one step over: the platform
*assembles and steers its own crowd* (onboarding, incentives, surge repositioning), where
the camera takes the street as the city made it. Fix in Part 3 (R4) — and the fix deletes
an antithesis instance as a side effect.

### 1.7 Smaller formal notes

- **"The parallel is exact" (§5)** — then two paragraphs later the symmetry "breaks in two
  places." Scope the exactness to the operation before the breaks arrive: "The formal
  parallel is exact." (R7.)
- **"…and all three are the same animal" (§4)** — the sentence grades kinship (nearest kin,
  cousin) and then flattens the grades it just drew, one paragraph after distinguishing the
  two faces. The chapter has a better word than "animal" and has been using it since §2:
  the seat. (R9.)
- **"a mile east the film hosts no one at all" (§3)** — the host seat belongs to the city,
  not the film; the film inherits its coverage. "the film's Austin hosts no one at all"
  keeps the seat assignments straight (+1 word). Minor.
- **§7's "the three questions I've been circling" collides with §2's three-question test.**
  The chapter contains two indexed sets with the same index: §2 announces "To tell them
  apart I ask three questions" (act-on / only-route / reads), and §7's closing set is the
  abstract's triad (when does hosting become engineering / what does navigation take / what
  makes thirds contestable), which the chapter never numbered before this sentence. Worse,
  §7's *first answer reuses §2's three dimensions*, so a reader who resolves the reference
  to §2 — the only "three questions" they were ever given — half-confirms the misreading.
  Delete the numeral (R2). July 3's item 14 seeded questions at §1's end; whatever revision
  followed, the current §1 seeds none, so the numeral now points only at the wrong target.
- **Simmel, Burt, Obstfeld, Feld glosses** — all checked, all accurate: the
  mediator / tertius gaudens / divide-et-impera triad is Simmel's; "an empty space between
  two disconnected people is worth money to whoever stands in it" is Burt at the right
  grain; Obstfeld's iungens gloss is right; Feld's focus ("ties form among them as a side
  effect") is a fair plain rendering of foci organizing joint activity. §5's "Simmel's
  profiteering third with a meter running" correctly calls back tertius gaudens.
- **§7's pirate-TV chain** works formally: a distribution chain of nameable persons is the
  reachability contrast the section needs, and the Backpacker's tape gives the film-internal
  double. (Content detail — Kansas City, dub-and-acquaintance — is disc-verify.)
- **Prior-art check on the two faces (§4).** Attention-side selection vs labor-side
  matching is adjacent to two literatures the chapter already handles: attention-economy
  (bracketed via Beller, correctly) and matching-market intermediaries (owned via the
  matchmaker mixed case). Ajunwa's *tertius bifrons* — a literally two-faced third — sits
  safely in note 39; if it ever moves into the text, disambiguate: her two faces are the
  platform's postures toward workers and consumers, not the chapter's attention/labor
  split. As is, no collision. The novelty claim as scoped July 3 (setting-beside-broker,
  same test) survives this pass; nearest rival remains Small & Adler's space-and-ties
  review, which surveys settings as tie contexts but never seats the setting in the third
  position under a common test with brokers.

### 1.8 Load-bearing weak points, ranked as a hostile referee would

All three survive with one-clause repairs. Nothing in the new insertions requires
