# Research synthesis — what the four sweeps yield, keyed to the outline

Four verified research files (`film_slacker.md`, `film_theory.md`, `urban_thirds.md`,
`platforms_streaming.md`), consolidated references in `../literature/references.md`. This brief maps the
strongest finds onto `../outline.md`, lists corrections to the accepted abstract, states the novelty position,
and collects the do-not-use and library-check items.

## Strongest anchors by outline section

**§1 The coherence puzzle** — Use "roughly one hundred characters" (Criterion: "over a hundred"; Pierson:
"about a hundred"). The critical consensus already describes the relay ("passing the baton," Ebert's "cue
ball with a camera," the La Ronde lineage) — cite it, then reinterpret drift as brokerage. Existing answers
to the coherence question to position against: Rosenbaum (hidden craft), Pierson (seamless links), Macor
("least auteur film ever made"), Stone (the form and content of slack). None splits the work into host +
broker.

**§2 The structural third** — Simmel "The Triad" (Wolff, 1950, 145–69): mediator / tertius gaudens / divide
et impera. The brokerage line after Simmel (Burt 1992; Obstfeld 2005) is all pair-brokering thirds — **the
hosting third is a genuinely unoccupied cell: a third that coordinates the whole field while brokering no
pair.** Oldenburg's "third place" is an ordinal third with a literal in-venue host — state the distinction
once, then use it: third places are the hosting third's furniture, and Oldenburg's greeter contrasts with
the city that hosts without one. Mandatory prior-art citation: Ajunwa's "tertius bifrons" (2020) already
puts Simmel's third onto platforms.

**§3 Austin hosts** — Lofland 1973 is the load-bearing warrant: cities order strangers by *appearances and
locations* — a legible code, not introductions. Goffman civil inattention; Milgram familiar strangers
(Slacker as a feature-length familiar-stranger field); Jacobs sidewalk ballet. Material base: 1986 oil crash
→ Texas real-estate collapse → falling Austin rents (verified testimony: ~$515→$350/mo) → slack time; 1990
census 465,622. Two gifts: (1) "Live Music Capital of the World" resolution passed **August 29, 1991 — seven
weeks after the national release**: the city naming its hosting function after the film documented it;
(2) Gaughen 2014: the film's spatial continuity is partly fictional — the city hosts, but only the camera
connects. Linklater's "100 percent Austin product" is the primary-source hosting anchor.

**§4 The camera engineers** — Bordwell, *Poetics of Cinema* ch. 7, section titled **"Narration Makes
Networks"** (p. 207): connectivity is a narrational product. Slacker's taxonomic home is Ramírez Berg's
**"daisy chain plot"** (2006) — never revisit a protagonist; *Slacker* is his named example — the limit case
where the only edges are the camera's relay: **a star graph whose hub is off-screen.** Linklater's own
words: "a comprehensive architecture that you can pass through and not feel completely disoriented" (Savlov
2011). Discipline the metaphor with narrator theory (Bordwell 1985: personified camera is "an
anthropomorphic fiction") — cast the engineering third as *impersonal narrational agency*, which strengthens
the platform analogy (Uber's matcher is exactly that). "Hyperlink cinema" is a foil: hyperlinks are
user-actuated, Slacker's transitions are broker-actuated — the same error that makes feed ranking feel like
browsing.

**§5 The new engineers** — Stark & Pais 2020: the platform neither hires nor contracts but co-opts;
"rankings but not ranks... accounts but no accountability"; triangular geometry. Mechanics: Rosenblat &
Stark 2016 (destination-blind acceptance, ~15-second window, only the algorithm sees the queue); Lee et al.
2015 (algorithmic assignment). The contrast sentence the section builds to: the city is a commons its
parties inhabit and can renegotiate; the platform is a third present in every match and party to none.
Simmel's tertius gaudens is the critique foil — the platform monetizes the gap the city would simply have
let close.

**§6 Cinema as training** — Benjamin, Second Version, *Selected Writings* 3:108: film trains "the
apperceptions and reactions needed to deal with a vast apparatus" (cite SW3, not *Illuminations*, for the
training sentence). Kuleshov gives the mechanism: viewers infer relations between shots never co-present —
a century of practice trusting third-party composition of adjacency is the perceptual repertoire
feed-reading requires. Guard: training to *follow* is not training to *contest*.

**§7 Enclosure and the questions** — The film's own path was a chain of human, negotiable, addressable
thirds: $23k → Dobie Theatre (July 1990, year-long run) → Pierson as producer's rep → Orion Classics
($100k advance, July 5, 1991) → ~$1.2M gross → video window. Then the demolition: Tzioumakis 2023 (five
studio specialty divisions shuttered 2008–2010), Lobato 2019 (platform interfaces deciding national
catalogs), Sundance now clearing through streamers (CODA $25M to Apple; Fair Play $20M to Netflix). The
recommender is the engineering third installed over cinema itself. Politics anchor from the film:
the Oblique Strategies card — "Withdrawing in disgust is not the same thing as apathy" (verified in
Rosenbaum) — nonparticipation in plot mirrors nonparticipation in the market; both still coordinated.

## Novelty position (state it at this grain)

Already claimed, must cite: Simmel's third onto platforms (Ajunwa 2020; Stark & Vanden Broeck 2024);
apparatus theory onto algorithmic subject-formation (Sirůček 2025); montage → trained perception → digital
economy (Beller 2006); editing ↔ algorithm as composition (Manovich 2001, 2005); movie culture →
algorithmic culture (Hallinan & Striphas 2016; Striphas 2015). **Unclaimed, ours:** the hosting/engineering
split within the third (the non-brokering coordinating third has no name in the Simmel-to-Burt line);
*editing as brokered matching* (not attention extraction, not composition); and Slacker as the named limit
case — the daisy chain whose only network edges are narrational. The database-narrative literature
(Manovich, Kinder) never mentions Slacker: open ground.
