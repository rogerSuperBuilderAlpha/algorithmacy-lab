## Finding 0 (high, 3-0 unanimous across four survived claims)
Schwan & Ildirar (2010) show that for first-time adult viewers, comprehension of edited discontinuities is driven not by an image's similarity to natural perception but by the presence of a familiar narrative 'line of action'; where that line is absent, extended prior film experience is required. This is your core naive-viewer evidence beyond Benjamin and Hansen and it BOTH supports (learned competence for conventions lacking a natural action line) AND complicates (everyday-action knowledge, not film literacy, bootstraps much comprehension) the trained-competence claim.

**Sources:** Schwan, S. & Ildirar, S. (2010). 'Watching Film for the First Time: How Adult Viewers Interpret Perceptual Discontinuities in Film.' Psychological Science 21(7): 970-976. DOI 10.1177/0956797610372632 ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797610372632

**Evidence:** Verbatim abstract: 'it is not the similarity to conditions of natural perception but the presence of a familiar line of action that determines the comprehensibility of films for inexperienced viewers. In the absence of such a line of action, extended prior experience is required for appropriate interpretation.' Design: 20 inexperienced adult viewers from southern Turkey vs. medium- and high-experienced viewers, 14 clips with typical discontinuities. Confirmed via PubMed PMID 20530390. Merges survived claims 0, 1, 11, 12.

## Finding 1 (high, 3-0 unanimous across eight survived claims)
Ildirar & Schwan (2015) find first-time viewers CAN spontaneously notice basic pictorial (object identity), causal (activity chains), and conceptual (gaze-to-object, i.e. eyeline-match) commonalities across cuts because these draw on everyday perception — but this grasp is 'to a large degree fragile,' revealing they lack 'a basic notion of what constitutes a film.' Experienced viewers, by contrast, presume a film is a coherent whole and actively search for meaning across shots even when rules are violated. The acquired conception-of-the-medium is the trained competence your chapter posits; the everyday bootstrapping complicates a pure-training account.

**Sources:** Ildirar, S. & Schwan, S. (2015). 'First-time viewers' comprehension of films: Bridging shot transitions.' British Journal of Psychology 106(1): 133-151. DOI 10.1111/bjop.12069 ; https://doi.org/10.1111/bjop.12069

**Evidence:** Verbatim abstract confirms bootstrapping of pictorial/causal/conceptual commonalities 'due to their close relationship with everyday perception and cognition' yet 'to a large degree fragile, indicating the lack of a basic notion of what constitutes a film.' Conclusion (verbatim): experienced viewers 'presume that films form a coherent whole... actively search for meaningful relationships between shots, partly ignoring salient inconsistencies. In contrast... first-time viewers do not seem to have the notion of films as coherent narratives.' Sample: 20 first-time, 20 low-, 20 high-experience Turkish viewers. Confirmed PubMed PMID 24654735; DOI resolves at Wiley. Merges survived claims 13, 15, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22. Author order is Ildirar & Schwan (not Schwan & Ildirar).

## Finding 2 (high, 3-0 unanimous)
The same first-time-viewer work shows the native perceptual capacity does NOT extend to most editing conventions: naive viewers failed shot/reverse-shot, establishing shots, POV, and distance/inside-outside transitions — misattributing camera-position changes to bodily turns and reading an establishing-shot-to-closeup as a character leaving the scene, with eyeline matches the surviving exception. This SUPPORTS the claim that fluency with edited sequences is a trained competence for all but the most perceptually salient cuts.

**Sources:** Ildirar, S. & Schwan, S. (2015). British Journal of Psychology 106(1): 133-151. DOI 10.1111/bjop.12069 ; Schwan, S. & Ildirar, S. (2010). Psychological Science 21(7): 970-976. DOI 10.1177/0956797610372632

**Evidence:** The vivid granular findings (camera-position changes attributed to bodily turns; establishing-to-closeup read as the other person leaving; eyeline matches as the exception; failure on shot/reverse-shot, establishing, POV) trace primarily to the 2010 Psychological Science paper and are reaffirmed in 2015. Predictive-inference and shot/reverse-shot success rates diverge sharply between experienced (up to 100%) and first-time (0-60%) viewers. Cite both papers for these specifics. Survived claim 14.

## Finding 3 (high, 3-0 unanimous across five survived claims)
Tim J. Smith's Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity (2006 PhD thesis; 2012 Projections) grounds continuity editing in natural attentional/perceptual mechanisms rather than a film-specific learned code: cuts are hidden during natural periods of attention withdrawal (e.g. saccades, occlusion), and perceptual expectations are matched across cuts without a coherent internal representation of space. This COMPLICATES the trained-competence thesis — but Smith also holds that continuity conventions 'formalize insights about viewer cognition latent in the filmmaking community for nearly a century,' which SUPPORTS your century-long editorial-refinement framing (the fit comes from filmmakers tuning to viewer cognition, not audiences being trained).

**Sources:** Smith, T.J. (2012). 'The Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity.' Projections 6(1): 1-27. DOI 10.3167/proj.2012.060102 ; Smith, T.J. (2006). 'An Attentional Theory of Continuity Editing.' PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, School of Informatics. hdl:1842/1076 ; https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/1076

**Evidence:** Projections abstract (verbatim): AToCC 'identifies the critical role visual attention plays in the perception of continuity across cuts and demonstrates how perceptual expectations can be matched across cuts without the need for a coherent representation of the depicted space' and 'formalizes insights about viewer cognition that have been latent in the filmmaking community for nearly a century.' Thesis abstract (verbatim): 'The intention of most film editing is to create the impression of continuous action... by presenting discontinuous visual information' and 'Continuity editing rules identify natural periods of attention withdrawal that can be used to hide cuts.' Note for the author: Smith argues editing works via AUTOMATIC perception, which cuts against a pure trained-competence reading. Merges survived claims 2, 3, 8, 9, 10.

## Finding 4 (high, 3-0 unanimous across three survived claims)
Smith & Henderson (2008) provide the first empirical demonstration of 'edit blindness': ~25% of continuity-edited cuts joining two viewpoints of the same scene went undetected, rising to ~33% when the cut coincided with a sudden onset of motion; continuity-adhering cuts produced MORE edit blindness than rule-violating cuts. Most missed edits were due to inattentional blindness as viewers attended to narrative, not merely low-level saccadic/blink suppression. This grounds the claim that editing works by exploiting and managing how viewers deploy attention.

**Sources:** Smith, T.J. & Henderson, J.M. (2008). 'Edit Blindness: The relationship between attention and global change blindness in dynamic scenes.' Journal of Eye Movement Research 2(2):6, 1-17. DOI 10.16910/jemr.2.2.6 ; https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2264

**Evidence:** Abstract verbatim: 'edits constructed according to the Continuity Editing Rules result in greater edit blindness than edits not adhering to the rules. A quarter of edits joining two viewpoints of the same scene were undetected and this increased to a third when the edit coincided with a sudden onset of motion.' On mechanism: 'the majority seem to be due to inattentional blindness as viewers attend to the depicted narrative.' Conclusion: 'first empirical evidence of edit blindness and its relationship to natural attentional behaviour.' Merges survived claims 4, 5, 6.

## Finding 5 (high, 3-0 unanimous)
Smith & Mital (2013) show that free viewing of dynamic scenes produces greater attentional synchrony (multiple viewers' gaze converging on the same on-screen locations at the same moments) and longer fixations than static scenes, with gaze largely under exogenous control (center bias, motion, faces). This is the empirical basis for a filmmaker's ability to predict and guide where the audience looks — directly supporting the 'hidden editorial third that selects what you see next' framing.

**Sources:** Smith, T.J. & Mital, P.K. (2013). 'Attentional synchrony and the influence of viewing task on gaze behavior in static and dynamic scenes.' Journal of Vision 13(8):16, 1-24. DOI 10.1167/13.8.16 ; https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2193975

**Evidence:** Abstract verbatim: 'Free viewing of dynamic scenes showed greater attentional synchrony, longer fixations, and more gaze to people and areas of high flicker compared with static scenes.' PMID 23863509. Attentional synchrony is the paper's operational term for gaze clustering. Caveat: viewing task CAN modulate gaze, so guidance is strong but not absolute. Survived claim 7.

## Finding 6 (high, 3-0 unanimous across two survived claims)
Kuleshov effect empirical status: genuinely unsettled but not nonexistent. The original footage is lost and prior replications produced conflicting/unreliable results (per Barratt et al. 2016), yet that controlled replication (36 participants, 24 neutral-face sequences, six emotional contexts, KDEF stimuli) found 'some sort of Kuleshov effect does in fact exist' — participants chose the context-appropriate emotion category more than alternatives, with valence and arousal in expected directions (though eye-tracking did not show the expected between-condition differences).

**Sources:** Barratt, D., Cabak Rédei, A., Innes-Ker, Å. & van de Weijer, J. (2016). 'Does the Kuleshov Effect Really Exist? Revisiting a Classic Film Experiment on Facial Expressions and Emotional Contexts.' Perception 45(8): 847-874. DOI 10.1177/0301006616638595 ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0301006616638595

**Evidence:** Abstract verbatim: 'The original film footage is lost and recent attempts at replication have produced either conflicting or unreliable results' and 'some sort of Kuleshov effect does in fact exist. For each emotional condition, the participants tended to choose the appropriate category more frequently than the alternative options, while the answers to the valence and arousal questions also went in the expected directions.' Merges survived claims 23, 24.

## Finding 7 (high, 3-0 unanimous across two survived claims)
The strongest single Kuleshov result for your thesis: Ildirar & Ewing (2018) recreated the classic montages (neutral face + soup, gravestone, playing child) and found the effect PRESENT in experienced film viewers (explicitly only in the sadness condition) but ABSENT in first-time viewers, who despite fully understanding each shot's content perceived the shots as 'wholly separate,' 'independent photographs,' did not relate them spatiotemporally, and lacked 'the notion of what constitutes a film.' Direct evidence that reading edited juxtapositions is a trained, experience-dependent competence — though the effect was weak/partial even in experienced viewers.

**Sources:** Ildirar, S. & Ewing, L. (2018). 'Revisiting the Kuleshov Effect with First-Time Viewers.' Projections: The Journal for Movies and Mind 12(1). Berghahn Journals ; https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/id/eprint/21897/3/21897.pdf

**Evidence:** Abstract verbatim: 'Results confirm the presence of the Kuleshov effect for experienced viewers (explicitly only in the sadness condition) but not the first-time viewers, who failed to perceive continuity between the shots.' Full text: first-time viewers 'despite an intact ability to perceive and understand the content of each shot... perceived them to be wholly separate from each other and did not relate them spatiotemporally.' Attribute to Ildirar & Ewing 2018. Merges survived claims 16, 17.

## How this lands in §6 (the argument upgrade)

The chapter's "only a real study could show it" hedge is now partly answerable. The studies exist and
they show the disposition is **experience-dependent**: Ildirar & Ewing 2018 recreated the classic
montages and found the Kuleshov reading present in experienced viewers but ABSENT in first-timers, who
saw the shots as "wholly separate." Ildirar & Schwan 2015 / Schwan & Ildirar 2010: naive viewers bridge
cuts that ride everyday perception (object identity, causal chains, eyeline) but fail the medium-specific
conventions (shot/reverse-shot, POV, establishing→closeup) and lack "the notion of what constitutes a
film" — experienced viewers presume the whole coheres and actively hunt meaning across cuts. Smith
(2012, 2008, 2013): continuity rides natural attention, filmmakers tuned the craft over ~100 years to
predict and guide gaze, and continuity cuts go literally undetected ("edit blindness"). Net: the
disposition to presume a hidden arranger's sequence coheres, and to search for its sense, is trained; the
scaffold it's built on is ordinary perception. What stays unproven is only the transfer to the dispatch
queue.
