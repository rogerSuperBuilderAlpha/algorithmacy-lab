# Research-gap analysis for the fresh rewrite (2026-07-22)

Whole-paper read after the panel-2 slop purge. The chapter's argument and citation base are mature
(two ten-panel rounds), so these are genuine gaps, not padding. Ranked by how much a quality
reference would strengthen a load-bearing claim.

## Gap 1 (highest) — §6 audience-training claim has no empirical/cognitive grounding

The whole "cinema trained audiences to read an engineering third" argument rests on Benjamin
(1936), Hansen (1999), Kuleshov (1974), Beller (2006) — theory and assertion. The chapter itself
concedes "nobody has shown that the skill a moviegoer brings to a cut is the skill a driver brings
to the dispatch queue… only a real study could." Both review rounds named this the weakest
load-bearing point. There is a real empirical literature the chapter ignores:

- **Cognitive film theory / empirical editing comprehension**: James Cutting, Jordan DeLong &
  Christine Nothelfer, "Attention and the Evolution of Hollywood Film" (*Psychological Science*
  2010) — Hollywood's shot structure measurably evolved toward the natural statistics of human
  attention. Tim J. Smith, "The Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity" (*Projections* 2012)
  and the "Where's Wally" / attentional-synchrony eye-tracking work — continuity editing exploits
  reflexive gaze control. Stephan Schwan & Sermin Ildirar on first-time film viewers (adults with
  no prior film exposure) — editing comprehension is partly LEARNED, partly bootstrapped from
  real-world perception. Sermin Ildirar & Stephan Schwan, "First-Time Viewers' Comprehension of
  Films" (2015). These directly test the chapter's core claim about learned fluency with cuts.

- **Kuleshov effect empirical status** (fixes the overclaim the panel flagged): the original
  footage is lost; Stephen Prince & Wayne Hensley, "The Kuleshov Effect: Recreating the
  Classic Experiment" (*Cinema Journal* 1992) failed to replicate cleanly; Dean Mobbs et al.
  (2006) and Daniel Barratt, Anna Cabak Rédei, Åse Innes-Ker & Joost van de Weijer, "Does the
  Kuleshov Effect Really Exist?" (*Perception* 2016) partially replicate under constraints. Citing
  these lets the chapter state the effect's status honestly rather than as folklore.

Verification needed: exact venues/years/pages for each; whether Schwan/Ildirar's finding cuts for
or against "learned." Deep-research target.

## Gap 2 (medium) — platform-as-tertius and recommender-gatekeeping could be freshened (2021–2026)

§5/§7 lean on Rosenblat & Stark (2016), Lee et al. (2015), Stark & Pais (2020), Kellogg et al.
(2020), Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024), Hallinan & Striphas (2016), Frey (2021). The
platform-governance and recommender-as-cultural-gatekeeper literatures have moved. Candidates to
verify: recent work theorizing recommender systems as gatekeepers/curators (post-2020); newer
algorithmic-management syntheses beyond ride-hail. Only add if a source genuinely advances a claim
the chapter makes; the field-freshness is a nice-to-have, not a hole.

## Gap 3 (low) — the "hosting third" has thin film-and-the-city grounding

single high-value source (e.g., on the cinematic figuration of urban co-presence) fits.
