# CANDIDATE_A — design

## 1. What the chapter is for

It gives a reader of a Linklater book something to do with the feature every review of *Slacker* names — the handoff — by showing that the film passes the job of joining strangers between its own cut and its characters, and that the difference is the one we now argue about with platforms.

## 2. The frame, from the evidence

Two questions about anything standing between strangers.

**Reach (the abstract's "hosting").** Does it hold people where they can summon each other, without naming which two? A sidewalk does. Nobody assigns the moon-landing man to the man looking for Gary; he overhears "You seen Gary around?" and opens with "Did I overhear you say you got a friend that's missing?" (segs. 4–5, subtitle-derived). What follows runs a hundred and thirty transcript lines. The mechanism is Schegloff's rule that a summons, once answered, commits the answerer to stay.

**Handoff (the abstract's "engineering").** Does it decide which specific party comes next, for a specific participant, in sequence? *Slacker*'s cut does this to viewers; Uber's queue does it to drivers.

The two are independent: four cells, not two kinds. Crossing all four, a separate **capacity register** — price the exchange, evaluate a party, remove a party, withhold the reason — facts about market position, contract and law, entailed by neither question.

The chapter's spine is the film's own finding: **the joining migrates.** Of the thirty-four transitions the transcript covers, fifteen carry a spoken bridge — a repeated line, an overheard question, an invitation — seventeen carry none, two are equivocal. Through segment 16 the cut joins almost alone; from 17 to 31 the characters take over, offering rides, addresses, guest lists, a licked wrist stamp; at dawn the cut takes it back, over a loudspeaker car and a tape-recorder rant. The count is subtitle-derived; the disc may revise it.

## 3. Answers to the constraints

1. **Sorting.** The two questions point opposite ways: Austin 1990, reach-yes/handoff-no; a door policy, no/no — it subtracts encounters, arranges none — yet holds three capacities; *Slacker*'s cut, no/yes over viewers; Uber, no/yes over people who must live with the pairing; Prime Video Slate, no/yes over works. Representation does not answer uniformly, because handoff comes in a degree the transcript measures — how much joining the sequence does that no diegetic relation does — and in *Slacker* that degree swings.
2. **Both ends.** "Which cell" and "which capacities" are separate claims, made separately; non-selecting environments hold capacities too.
3. **Attributed powers.** The running text concedes that choosing which two parties meet entails no power to price or expel, with the counterexample from the film: the door that says "Three dollars," "Sorry, Steve. Don't see you," and "It's past 2:00. Everybody out" chooses no pairs (segs. 28, 31).
4. **Which city.** The coordination claim is about the constructed film. Gaughen documents one three-mile discontinuity, and Linklater says the continuity is syntactic — "If you didn't know Austin, the film makes sense… They're still walking right to left." Census figures appear as conditions the fiction drew on, never as evidence that anything was coordinated.
5. **Training.** I take the critical option: what cut-reading demonstrably builds is the competence to follow an arrangement someone else made. Measured cut-reading is partial (55% of experienced viewers on the gravestone sequence, 0% on the child sequence), and the one transfer test on record found no general advantage. *Slacker*'s own reception supplies the narrow positive case — 1990 Austin viewers audited the handoffs and told Linklater the geography was wrong. They could contest a handoff's content; nothing measured shows they could contest its order.
6. **Falsifier.** Dense, legible, co-present environments do not produce the claimed coordination: exposure segregation runs 67% higher in the ten largest metros than in small ones, and visitor concentration explains 26% of street-level mixing against 44% for amenities and residential mix. Segments 33 and 34 falsify it inside the film — public address with no addressee. Beyond agglomeration, the frame buys the summons rule, a mechanism at the scale of one exchange.
7. **Prior art.** I engage the German *Theorien des Dritten* distinction between concrete and abstract thirds, and the platform work treating matching and attention-direction as one function, coexisting inside single platforms (O'Reilly et al.; Meyer et al.) — all marked second-hand. The second is the strongest complication, and the grid concedes it by construction: the axes are independent so one platform can hold both. Simmel defers the group-forming third and says he will return.
8. **Withholding.** Yes, a distinct capability; the list becomes four. Amazon: "we cannot provide additional details about why any specific title was or was not selected." Uber: 30% of deactivated drivers were given no reason. The boundary case sits outside — a cut never explains itself either, but the viewer has no decision to appeal. The doorman does explain, "You're not on the list," which is what makes him arguable.
9. **Closure.** The dated fact: Smits's 2017 fieldwork found Amazon's submission "unfiltered, with no gatekeeping restrictions"; the same company's page, retrieved 1 August 2026, reads "Access to self-service submissions is by invitation only." One channel tightened. It then confronts the counter-evidence: the middle tier refilled at 6.46% against the vanished divisions' 6.91%, and works reaching audiences grew in number.

## 4. The abstract's commitments

Simmel's third → the reach/handoff split, his deferral quoted. Austin/camera → reach versus handoff, conceding the Austin in evidence is the edited one. Uber → same handoff question, different capacities: the airport queue hands a driver his next party, resets his position if he refuses, hides the fare. Training → narrowed and inverted per constraint 5. Closing questions → contestability located in whether the instrument is held and transferable rather than in whether it explains itself: the licked stamp, the three-dollar cover, the PixelVision handed over with "Pass it around… But I want my camera back."

## 6. What the frame gives up

It gives up Austin. The real city becomes background and the argument is about how a fiction was assembled, so a reader wanting 1990 urban history gets none. It gives up two tidy named kinds: a grid plus a capacity register is harder to carry than "hosts and engineers." It gives up the strong training claim and the flattering ending. And its spine rests on a count taken from subtitles, which the disc could move.
