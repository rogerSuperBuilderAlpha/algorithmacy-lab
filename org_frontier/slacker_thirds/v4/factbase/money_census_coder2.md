# Money census — coder 2

Independent coding of all 35 segments of *Slacker* (1991) on two dimensions, WORK and EXCHANGE,
against `research/slacker_transcript.md` (subtitle-derived, dialogue only, no speaker names) with
segment boundaries taken from `v4/factbase/scene_ledger.md`. Segments are not renumbered.

Nothing here rests on the image. The transcript is the only evidence used, and no claim is made
about what anyone is visibly doing. A prior census of this film exists; it was not read.

Quotations are verbatim. The transcript wraps sentences across lines, so a quotation spanning a
wrap is joined with a single space; `…` marks an elision inside a quotation. Odd spacings
("thatjust", "toJudgment") are the transcript's own and are reproduced as found.

### Does the prediction hold?

**It holds only partly, and the part that fails is the load-bearing one.**

The prediction has two halves. The first — that the film's coordinating positions are shown working — holds. The taxi driver is driving a fare in segment 1, the café counter takes orders in segments 11 and 25, the door names a price and refuses a name in segments 28 and 30, and venue staff clear the room at closing in segment 31. No one staffing a coordinating position is left unshown, with one qualification given below.

The second half — that these are *the only* people working — fails. Four other kinds of paid work are audible, and none of them staffs the film's coordinating infrastructure:

- **The T-shirt and magazine seller (segment 9).** "Can I sell you a shirt? You know?… look at this magazine. Buy this magazine." A vendor working the street to the public.
- **The children selling Cokes (segment 14).** "- Hey, you want a Coke? - Half price." A priced offer to passing strangers, with stock heavy enough to complain about: "This shit's getting heavy."
- **The newspaper seller (segment 11).** "- Paper? - No, thank you. Two-for-one special." Working the same sidewalk as the coffee shop, and refused.
- **The store security worker (segment 21).** "Uh, I'm with the security in the store. One of my employees saw you put something in the bag that you haven't paid for." He supervises staff, and the segment contains the film's only audible shift handoff: "- Hey, would you take over? - Okay, I got it."
- **The police (segment 2).** "Officer Bozzio and Love with the police department," on duty at a hit-and-run.

The security guard is a gatekeeper by type, so a loose reading of "coordinating position" absorbs him; the vendors and the police are not absorbable on any reading. Street vending in particular is a straightforward counterexample: three separate segments show goods offered to the public for money by people who coordinate nothing.

The prediction's second clause — that "everyone else pitches, trades, or refuses work" — is also too strong, though it fails by overreach rather than by contradiction. Only two segments contain a refusal of work (4 and 20), twelve contain no relation to paid work at all, and eight only talk about it. Pitching and trading are real but confined: six segments sell, six solicit, three price a door, and twenty do neither.

**One person in a coordinating position is not shown working: the door in segment 29.** The cover charge there is quoted by a companion — "- What's the cover? - Uh, I think it's five bucks" — and no staff voice is audible in the segment at all. The price is in force with nobody heard enforcing it.

**One number deserves flagging on its own: SEEKING is zero.** In 35 segments nobody applies for, asks after, or arranges paid work. The single sentence in the film that describes a job search is read off a postcard, in the third person, about a man who has left town: "So now he's looking for a job that doesn't involve much work." If a coder assigns that to SEEKING, the count becomes 1 and DISCUSSING drops to 7.

## Method note

**What counted as "paid work."** PERFORMING was assigned where labor is done for money inside the segment, whether employed or self-employed, and where the transcript carries a line that shows it happening: an order taken and repeated back, a price named at a door, a list checked, a badge claimed on duty, goods offered to the public at a price. Two consequences follow. Vending counts — the T-shirt seller, the newspaper seller and the Coke-selling children are working, since they offer stock to passing strangers for money in the moment. A one-off attempt to resell a personal object to named acquaintances does not count as a job; the pap-smear pitch in segment 8 is coded SELLING on the Exchange dimension and DISCUSSING on WORK, because the seller has one item, one route of prospects, and calls it "one of a kind." That line between public vending and private resale is the census's main discretionary rule, and a coder who drew it elsewhere would move segments 8, 9 and 14.

Unpaid activity was kept out of PERFORMING throughout, however much it resembles a job: the daughter serving coffee to her father in segment 22, the dictation role-play in segment 4, the burglary in segments 22–23, the PixelVision "project" in segment 30, and the interviewer's "project we're working on" in segment 20, which names a project and never a wage.

**People talking about jobs they no longer hold, or never held.** A statement about a job not being done in the segment was coded DISCUSSING, or REFUSING where the statement carries a refusal, and never PERFORMING. This covers "Still unemployed" (8), "I graduated a couple of years back. Just pretty much hanging out" (17), the future-tense "I'll get a job… when I hear the true call" (20), and the retrospective "I had lunch right out front there every day that summer" (22). Unverifiable claims to a profession were treated the same way and given no evidentiary weight of their own: "I'm a medical doctor. I own a mansion and a yacht," delivered inside a compulsive loop (11); "I was just talking with my publisher at this small press" (17); "I have this friend, all right, she's a gynecologist in Hollywood" (8); and the Old Anarchist's Catalonia service, which his own daughter contradicts inside the segment (22). The film also uses "work" repeatedly in non-monetary senses — "I have some very serious work for them" about guns (9), "working on yourself" (16), "us working for it" (24) — and none of those was counted toward any WORK code.

**Where the ledger's boundaries made the coding arbitrary.** Four places.
