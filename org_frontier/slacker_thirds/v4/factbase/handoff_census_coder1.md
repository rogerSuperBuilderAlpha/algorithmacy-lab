# Handoff census — coder 1

Every transition between the 35 dialogue segments of *Slacker* (1991), coded on two independent
dimensions against `research/slacker_transcript.md` (subtitle-derived, dialogue only, no speaker
names) and the segment boundaries in `v4/factbase/scene_ledger.md`. Segment numbering is the
ledger's and is not changed here. Nothing below is inferred from the image; the transcript is the
only evidence, and it carries no sound other than dialogue.

**Dimension 1 — audible link:** `SPOKEN-BRIDGE`, `ECHO`, `NONE`, `EQUIVOCAL`.
**Dimension 2 — agency:** `DELIBERATE`, `INCIDENTAL`, `NONE`, `EQUIVOCAL`.

### Where the two dimensions disagree in an interesting way

- **Words carry the film, nobody hands it off (SPOKEN-BRIDGE + INCIDENTAL): 4→5, 7→8, 16→17,
  19→20.** Four of the ten spoken bridges are produced by overhearing, recognition, or a request
  made of a stranger. The line that moves the film is spoken by someone with no idea it will.
- **28→29 (SPOKEN-BRIDGE + NONE).** The strongest continuity of speech in the film sits on the
  weakest agency: the club-door conversation runs straight through the ledger's boundary and no
  one does anything to produce what follows. The pairing is a symptom of the boundary, not of the
  film.
- **24→25 (NONE + INCIDENTAL).** The pairing the scheme flags as unlikely does occur once: a
  stranger makes contact across the seam while nothing audible connects the two scenes.
- **30→31 (ECHO + DELIBERATE).** The only deliberate handoff whose audible link is a rhyme rather
  than a directive — the camera is handed over inside segment 30, and what crosses the seam is a
  heckle about it from the other side.
- **17→18 (ECHO + NONE).** Two conversations are audible at once and no agent connects them; the
  link is pure sound.
- **21→22 (ECHO + INCIDENTAL).** A speaker crosses the seam and the topic repeats, yet the
  encounter that follows is one neither party wanted.

Read down the two columns together: 12 of 34 transitions carry something audible *and* some
character act (SPOKEN-BRIDGE or ECHO paired with DELIBERATE or INCIDENTAL), 11 are NONE on both,
and the rest are mixed or equivocal. The two dimensions do not track each other.

## Method note

**What counted as the seam.** The seam is the boundary between the last transcript line the ledger
assigns to segment *n* and the first line it assigns to segment *n+1*. Evidence away from the seam
was admitted where a viewer would still hear it, and the distance is recorded in the note every
time (1→2 and 2→3 are the two cases where it matters most).

**The ECHO threshold.** Applied uniformly: ECHO requires either a verbatim or near-verbatim word or
phrase occurring on both sides of the seam, or a topic on the later side that answers one on the
earlier. A shared *situation* with no shared word was not enough — that rule produced NONE at 8→9
(two failed street pitches), 9→10 (two kinds of leaving), 32→33 (a dismissal and a mourning), and
33→34 (two unaddressed broadcasts), and each of those near-misses is recorded in its row so a
second coder can see exactly what was declined.

**The SPOKEN-BRIDGE / ECHO line.** The scheme separates them by whether the material is exchanged
between the parties. Where a line is both repeated across the seam and answered by someone on the
far side, the exchange criterion decides, which is why 19→20 is coded SPOKEN-BRIDGE despite being
the most literal repetition in the film.

**The agency rule.** Two constraints were applied. First, DELIBERATE requires an act aimed at
another person that intends the next encounter — an offer, an invitation, an address, an
instruction, an object handed over with a purpose attached. A character who announces his own next
move and goes there alone (16→17: "I'm gonna go look at some books") is not scored DELIBERATE;
self-direction sets the location, not the encounter. Second, INCIDENTAL requires the contact to
straddle the seam, with one party in the earlier segment and one in the later. That constraint is
what separates 4→5, 19→20, and 24→25 (contact straddles) from 5→6 and 20→21 (the street contact is
contained entirely inside the earlier segment and no one from it reappears), which are coded NONE.

**Overlapping ledger ranges.** Five pairs share lines: 6/7 at 308–318, 17/18 at 809–815, 18/19 at
848–851, and, in effect, 25/26 and 28/29, where the ledger's boundary falls inside a continuous
exchange without a formal overlap. For these the seam is a passage, not a point, and both segments'
claims on the shared lines were honoured — the shared lines were read as belonging to both. This
inflates the audible-link codes at 6→7, 17→18, and 18→19, since a line assigned to both segments is
by definition on both sides of the seam. Each row says so.

**Where the ledger's boundaries made the coding arbitrary.**

- **5→6.** The ledger's table assigns line 281 to segment 6; the ledger's prose for segment 5 quotes
  "What a day, what a day" as segment 5's own final beat. Both cannot hold. Coded as the table
  defines it and marked EQUIVOCAL.
- **25→26.** The boundary at 1195/1196 falls inside one bar scene with one drinks order running
  across it. Whether there is a transition here at all is a decision the ledger made, not one the
  dialogue forces.
- **28→29.** The boundary at 1359/1360 falls mid-sentence-pair at the club door. The resulting
  SPOKEN-BRIDGE + NONE pairing describes the ledger's cut, not a handoff.
- **7→8.** The bridge lines (362–366) sit inside segment 8, so segment 7 ends on "Cool." and the
  conversation that carries the film forward is filed under the segment it arrives at.
- **20→21.** The ledger names 893 ("Hey, you got a light?") the bridge into segment 21. The
  transcript does not support it, and this coding says NONE on both dimensions. This is the sharpest
  disagreement here with the ledger's own reading.
- **11→12** and **34→35.** The ledger flags its own line 550 and line 1518 as possibly misplaced;
  neither changes a code.

**Subtitle caveat.** The transcript compresses and paraphrases, and several codes turn on exact
wording that subtitles may have altered: the verbatim doubling of "Hey, you got a cigarette?" at
866/867 (19→20), the repetition of "I got a stamp" at 1389 and 1404 (29→30), the placement and
exact form of "What a day, what a day" at 281 (5→6), the unnamed object in "Hey, you forgot
something" at 1031 (22→23), and the referent of "They're back" at 593 (13→14). Where a subtitle has
smoothed two different spoken lines into one written form, an ECHO or a SPOKEN-BRIDGE recorded here
may not exist in the audio; where it has varied one repeated line, a link may exist that this
census does not record. Any of these five that a chapter wants to lean on should be checked against
the film or the 1992 screenplay first.

**What this census cannot see.** No shot, no camera movement, no visual continuity, no gesture, no
prop. Most of the ledger's `NEEDS DISC` flags sit on exactly the question this census needs and
cannot answer — whether two adjacent segments share a location or a person — and each of those is a
place where the image could overturn a code here, most obviously at 12→13, 13→14, 20→21, 24→25, and
31→32.
