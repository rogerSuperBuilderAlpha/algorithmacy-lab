# Handoff census — merged

Two independent codings of all 34 transitions between the 35 dialogue segments of *Slacker*
(1991), reconciled into one table. Coder 1's file is `handoff_census_coder1.md`, coder 2's is
`handoff_census_coder2.md`. Both coded against `research/slacker_transcript.md` (subtitle-derived,
dialogue only, no speaker names) and the segment boundaries in `scene_ledger.md`, independently and
without seeing each other's work.

---

## 1. What this is and what it licenses

This is a census of what can be *heard* at each of the 34 cuts between segments, not a description
of what the film shows. Two coders, working independently from a subtitle-derived transcript with
no speaker names, no stage directions, and no access to the picture, classified every transition on
two dimensions — whether an audible link crosses the cut, and whether a character act produces the
next encounter. They agreed on the audible-link dimension at 28 of 34 transitions (82%, Cohen's
κ = 0.75) and on the agency dimension at 31 of 34 (91%, κ = 0.84). A chapter drawing on this census
may assert the band the two codings jointly support — 15–16 of 34 transitions carry an audible
link, 4–5 are equivocal, 6 of 34 carry a deliberate act — and must state the method (subtitle
transcript, two independent coders, disagreements unresolved) whenever it does. It may not assert a
point estimate in place of the band, and it may not assert either coder's number as the finding. The
whole census rests on a subtitle transcript; the Criterion disc, when checked, may revise individual
codes, and several rows say explicitly where a disc check would settle a live ambiguity.

## 2. The coding scheme

Both coders applied the same two four-value dimensions, stated in each coder's file in nearly
identical terms:

**Dimension 1 — audible link:** `SPOKEN-BRIDGE` (a line is exchanged between the two segments — a
question answered, an offer taken up, a request granted), `ECHO` (a word, phrase, or topic recurs on
both sides of the seam but is not exchanged between the parties), `NONE` (no audible link), `EQUIVOCAL`
(the transcript cannot decide, usually because a line's referent or its assignment to one segment or
the other is unclear).

**Dimension 2 — agency:** `DELIBERATE` (a character performs an act aimed at another person that
intends the next encounter — an offer, invitation, address, instruction, or object handed over with a
purpose), `INCIDENTAL` (contact straddles the seam but no one intends the resulting encounter —
overhearing, a stranger's recognition, a request made of someone who happens to be there),
`NONE` (no character act connects the two segments), `EQUIVOCAL` (the transcript cannot decide).

**Rules both coders report adopting, in the same terms:**

- *Echo requires a shared word or an answered topic, not a shared situation.* Coder 1: "A shared
  *situation* with no shared word was not enough — that rule produced NONE at 8→9 (two failed street
  pitches), 9→10 (two kinds of leaving), 32→33 (a dismissal and a mourning), and 33→34 (two
  unaddressed broadcasts)." Coder 2, independently, states the identical constraint: "Applied without
  limit, thematic affinity would swallow most of the film... I required either a shared word or
  phrase used in the same sense, or a named topic that is unmistakably the same referent."
- *Deliberate requires an act aimed at another person; self-direction does not count.* Coder 1:
  "DELIBERATE requires an act aimed at another person that intends the next encounter... A character
  who announces his own next move and goes there alone (16→17: 'I'm gonna go look at some books') is
  not scored DELIBERATE; self-direction sets the location, not the encounter." Coder 2 states the same
  rule and its consequence explicitly: "Self-routing without a handoff... is coded INCIDENTAL, since
  the scheme's DELIBERATE examples are all transfers between characters. This decision alone moves
  7→8, 16→17, and 22→23." Both coders land on INCIDENTAL, not NONE, for self-routing that produces an
  encounter — this part of the rule did not generate disagreement.
- *Incidental requires the contact to straddle the seam.* Coder 1: "INCIDENTAL requires the contact
  to straddle the seam, with one party in the earlier segment and one in the later. That constraint is
  what separates 4→5, 19→20, and 24→25 (contact straddles) from 5→6 and 20→21 (the street contact is
  contained entirely inside the earlier segment...), which are coded NONE." Coder 2: "INCIDENTAL
  covers a character carrying over into the next segment where contact happens without a handoff:
  overhearing, approaching a stranger, walking out of one scene into another." Both state the rule the
  same way; where they disagree is on whether a *particular* contact (24→25's card-offering stranger,
  28→29's continuing door conversation) actually meets it — see §4.

**Where the two coders' subsidiary rules genuinely diverge — the main source of disagreement:**

1. **The SPOKEN-BRIDGE / ECHO line is drawn on two different tests.** Coder 1's test is whether
   material is *exchanged between the two parties* at the seam: "The scheme separates them by whether
   the material is exchanged between the parties... the exchange criterion decides, which is why
   19→20 is coded SPOKEN-BRIDGE despite being the most literal repetition in the film." Coder 2's test
   is whether the line *produces* the next segment, regardless of who is addressed: at 19→20, "Coded
   ECHO rather than SPOKEN-BRIDGE because the request in segment 19 does not produce segment 20; the
   same words simply sound again to a new addressee." These are different tests and they produce
   different codes at 19→20, 25→26, 28→29, and 30→31 (all four are link disagreements — see §4).
2. **Whether a repeated word in a different sense counts toward ECHO.** At 2→3, coder 1 allows a
   word repeated in a different sense ("touch," meaning contact with an injured body vs. refusing
   association) plus a topic-level rhyme to add up to ECHO, while flagging that "a strict coder codes
   NONE." Coder 2 applies the same-sense requirement strictly and codes NONE: "the shared word 'touch'
   carries opposite senses... I do not count it."
3. **Whether unconfirmed straddling contact is enough for INCIDENTAL.** At 24→25, coder 1 counts the
   card-offering stranger's approach to an unidentified passerby (1127) as contact straddling the seam
   even though the transcript cannot confirm the passerby is the same person from segment 24, and codes
   INCIDENTAL. Coder 2 does not treat this as established contact for this transition and codes NONE.
4. **Whether a continuing act (not a fresh one) counts as agency at the seam.** At 28→29, coder 1
   requires a new act to register agency at all and finds none ("the same people simply keep talking,
   and no act produces the next encounter" — NONE). Coder 2 counts the doorman's refusal, which began
   before the cut and continues across it, as the operative act, coding INCIDENTAL because the refusal
   "does put the group back on the street" even though "he does not intend the meeting that follows."

## 4. The nine disagreements

Six on the audible-link dimension, three on the agency dimension (two transitions — 22→23 and
28→29 — disagree on both, so they appear in both lists). None is resolved here.

**Link — 2→3.** c1 sees an ECHO: "touch" recurs (89 → 118–119) and the busker's "disturbed young
man" rhymes at the level of topic with "some guy ran over his mother." c1 flags the call as weak,
noting "a strict coder codes NONE." c2 codes NONE on exactly that ground: the word "touch" carries
opposite senses (handling an injured body vs. refusing association), and c2's rule requires a shared
word used in the *same* sense. The disagreement is the same-sense threshold stated in §2.

**Link — 19→20.** Both coders hear the identical line doubled verbatim across the seam ("Hey, you
got a cigarette?", 866 and 867) and both note it is answered ("Yeah, sure"). c1 codes SPOKEN-BRIDGE
because the request is exchanged and answered between the parties at the seam. c2 codes ECHO because,
in c2's reading, the request in segment 19 does not *produce* segment 20 — the words simply recur to
a new addressee who happens to be standing there. This is the clearest instance of the exchange-vs-
production split named in §2.

**Link — 22→23.** c1 codes EQUIVOCAL: "Hey, you forgot something" (1031) may name the Marquis de Sade
book the burglars examine at 1036, or may name nothing that reappears; the transcript identifies no
object, so c1 declines to commit. c2 codes ECHO, resting instead on the surrounding theft topic
running from 936 through 1031–1036, audible to the viewer though never exchanged between the old man
and the car's occupants; c2 notes the same unnamed-object ambiguity but treats the broader topical
continuity as sufficient for a code rather than as a reason to withhold one.

**Link — 25→26.** c1 codes ECHO on a drinks order that runs across the ledger's stated seam
(1191 → 1197). c2 codes NONE, arguing that the drink-order and card lines at 1187–1195 are audibly
located in segment 26's bar scene but are assigned by the ledger to segment 25 — on c2's reading there
is no seam here to cross, only a misdrawn boundary. Both agree the code is boundary-dependent; see §6.

**Link — 28→29.** Both quote the same lines (1358–1361): a doorman's refusal answered one beat later
by a companion ("Figures. I'm supposed to be."). c1 codes SPOKEN-BRIDGE on the exchange criterion —
something audible plainly crosses. c2 codes ECHO because the reply comes from a companion, not from
the doorman who refused — c2's exchange test requires the *same two parties* who sit astride the seam
to be the ones exchanging the line, and here they are not.

**Link — 30→31.** c1 codes ECHO: the heckle "there ain't no film in that shit" (1436) is a comment
reaching backward at segment 30's camera, not a pointer forward. c2 codes SPOKEN-BRIDGE, citing an
additional line c1's row does not quote — "Nah, think I'm gonna catch that show" (1431) — read as
pointing forward to segment 31, with the heckle at 1436 treated as a separate, backward-facing echo of
the camera handoff. The two coders searched different stretches of dialogue around this seam and
grounded their codes in different lines.

**Agency — 22→23.** c1 codes NONE: "the encounter in the car is a crew regrouping, which nothing
spoken produces; the old man's giving is a deliberate act but it does not produce that encounter." c2
codes INCIDENTAL, on the possibility that "you forgot something" (1031) is the old man physically
handing the book to someone at the seam — a contact c2 grants provisional weight even while noting
only the disc could confirm it.

**Agency — 24→25.** c1 codes INCIDENTAL: a stranger offers a card to a passerby (1127), and c1 reads
this as contact straddling the seam even without confirming the passerby is the same person seen
leaving segment 24. c2 codes NONE, treating "Sure. I'll be here" (1125) as a standing offer that opens
no door in fact, and not counting the card offer as established seam-straddling contact for this
transition.

**Agency — 28→29.** c1 codes NONE: "the same people simply keep talking, and no act produces the next
encounter." c2 codes INCIDENTAL: the doorman's refusal is a real act, begun before the cut and
continuing across it, and it does put the group back on the street — c2 counts a continuing act as
agency-bearing even though "he does not intend the meeting that follows." c1 requires a fresh act at
the seam and finds none.

## 6. The boundary problem

Three pairs of segments carry formally overlapping line ranges in the ledger, and several ledger
boundaries fall inside what the transcript records as one continuous exchange. In every case listed
below, a line assigned to both sides of a seam — or a boundary drawn mid-exchange — makes the audible-
link code (and sometimes the agency code) partly an artifact of where the ledger cuts, not something
the dialogue itself marks as a transition. Both coders flag this in their method notes, independently
and in the same terms.

- **6→7.** Ledger ranges overlap at lines 308–318. The invitation that grounds SPOKEN-BRIDGE sits
  inside the overlap and belongs to both segments by the ledger's own numbering.
- **7→8.** No formal overlap, but the bridging lines (362–366) sit inside segment 8's stated range
  while the conversation that carries them begins in segment 7; c1: "segment 7 ends on 'Cool.' and the
  conversation that carries the film forward is filed under the segment it arrives at."
- **12→13.** The boundary at 564/565 falls inside what reads as one continuous exchange ("Hmm?" may
  answer either side of the cut); coded EQUIVOCAL by both coders precisely because the boundary does
  not mark a clean break.
- **17→18.** Ledger ranges overlap at lines 809–815. The garage conversation is audible under the
  bookstore scene's tail before the ledger's nominal cut, so the ECHO code is partly a product of the
  overlap rather than a genuine cross-seam link.
- **18→19.** Ledger ranges overlap at lines 848–851; the ride request that grounds SPOKEN-BRIDGE and
  DELIBERATE belongs to both segments by the ledger's own numbering.
- **21→22.** c2: "the ledger says as much" — there is no scene break at all in the dialogue here; the
  seam is the ledger's assertion, not anything the transcript marks.
- **25→26.** The boundary at 1195/1196 falls inside one bar scene, with drink-order and card lines
  that both coders read as belonging, audibly, to segment 26's bar despite the ledger's assignment to
  segment 25 (see §5's correction and §4's disagreement on this row).
- **28→29.** The boundary at 1359/1360 falls mid-sentence-pair at the club door; the resulting
  SPOKEN-BRIDGE-or-ECHO-plus-NONE-or-INCIDENTAL disagreement (see §4, twice) describes the ledger's
  cut, not a handoff the dialogue itself stages.

These eight transitions — 6→7, 7→8, 12→13, 17→18, 18→19, 21→22, 25→26, 28→29 — must be flagged in
the chapter wherever their codes are cited, or excluded from any count that claims to measure the
film's own cutting rather than the ledger's segmentation. They are not dropped from the table above;
every row states the artifact in its own note.

Two further rows carry a related but distinct problem — not a shared range but an uncertain single-
line assignment — and are already handled as EQUIVOCAL or flagged rather than folded into this list:
5→6 (line 281's assignment to segment 5 vs. segment 6 conflicts with the ledger's own prose) and
11→12 (line 550's speaker and location are unclear, and the ledger flags this itself). Neither
inflates a code through double-counting the way the eight above do; both are marked as ledger-flagged
uncertainty in their own rows.

## 7. The finding worth the most

No transition in either coding is `NONE` + `DELIBERATE`. Every act in the film's dialogue that both
intends and produces a next encounter is also audible at the seam — there is no silent handoff in the
dialogue record. The reverse is common: characters state plans, offers, and destinations that the film
does not follow.

**Verification of the "nine of fifteen" claim.** Coder 2's file states: "nine of the fifteen NONE-on-
both rows contain a deliberate act (an offer, an invitation, a stated destination) that the film
declines to follow." Coder 2's own 15 NONE-on-both rows (NONE on both dimensions, coder 2's coding)
are: 2→3, 3→4, 8→9, 9→10, 10→11, 11→12, 14→15, 15→16, 20→21, 24→25, 25→26, 26→27, 31→32, 32→33, 33→34.
Checking each of these 15 against both coder files and the transcript directly, **five** contain a
quoted, checkable offer, invitation, or stated destination that the film declines to follow — not
nine:

1. **2→3.** "Look, can I help you out? Do you need a ride anywhere or anything?" (95) and "Tell you
   what. Take my card. Give me a call." (97) — both refused ("No, I don't think so," 96). Reported by
   c2 only; c1's row for this transition does not mention it.
2. **8→9.** "I better cruise. I gotta check this guy Chico on the east side." (453) and "So, yeah,
   Friday night. Guest list?" (459) — neither followed. Reported by c2. c1 independently records a
   different pair of unfollowed offers in the same row (two failed sales pitches, 441 and 477),
   corroborating the row's general finding without corroborating these specific lines.
3. **24→25.** "Sure. I'll be here." (1125) — a standing offer to a returning customer that the film
   never shows honored. Reported by both coders (c1 in its row note, c2 in its evidence quote).
4. **26→27.** "I'll buy this round if you, uh, score me a pack of smokes, man." (1234–1235) — an
   errand that dispatches a character off-screen; segment 27 never answers it. Reported by both
   coders in nearly identical terms.
5. **32→33.** "You might wanna watch Blow Up tonight at 4:00." (1477) — a film recommendation the
   addressee declines two lines later ("I'm really into photography, not movies," 1481). Reported by
   c2 only, in its row commentary rather than its evidence cell; **c1's row for 32→33 does not mention
   this line at all.**

Of these five, three are corroborated by both coders (24→25, 26→27, and 8→9 in substance if not in
exact quotation) and two rest on a single coder's observation (2→3, 32→33).

**The remaining ten of the fifteen do not substantiate the claim on the evidence in either file.**
3→4, 9→10, 10→11, 11→12, 14→15, 15→16, 20→21, 25→26, 31→32, and 33→34 each contain, at most, a
thematic near-miss (a rhymed situation, an unanswered request for a light, an eviction order that
names no destination) that neither coder characterizes as an offer, invitation, or stated destination.
**The exact number substantiated here is five, not nine.**

**Two of the task's four example quotations belong to rows outside this set entirely**, and should
not be cited as instances of the NONE+NONE pattern:

- "I'll meet you there" (706, 708) belongs to **16→17**, coded SPOKEN-BRIDGE + INCIDENTAL by both
  coders — an audible link and a character act are both present at this seam. The line is a real,
  quoted instance of an unfollowed plan (the film never shows the delayed movie showing kept or
  missed), but it illustrates the film's general habit of dropping spoken plans, not the NONE+NONE
  pattern specifically.
- "We eat an early dinner if you'd like to stay" (1017) belongs to **22→23**, coded ECHO + INCIDENTAL
  by c2 (EQUIVOCAL + NONE by c1) — not a NONE+NONE row under either coding.

No row's quoted evidence directly contradicts the other coder's quoted evidence for the same row in
this set of five; where the two coders differ (8→9, 32→33), they differ by one coder citing evidence
the other does not mention, not by quoting the same lines to opposite effect.
