# Deep research: Royce's community of memory — meeting, communication, loyalty (Packet 3)

**Run date:** 2026-08-12. **Angles:** four (Smith and the monographic commentary; the loyalty literature and its reception; the Bellah lineage and its applications to place, media and virtual community; Royce's primary text against the platform vocabulary). **Verification:** three-lens adversarial, every lens required to state the source's own thesis before ruling.

Fifty-nine claims went to verification. Twenty-nine survived; thirty were refuted. Of the refutations, sixteen came at 2-1 and fourteen were unanimous — an unusually high refutation rate, and the pattern in it is worth naming up front: almost none of the thirty failed on the quotation. They failed on the sentence built around the quotation. Locators drifted by a page, ellipses swallowed the clause that cut the other way, and superlatives ("the one author who," "the closest existing precedent," "the standard commentator") went to verification with nothing behind them. Where a lens dissented at 2-1, the dissent was nearly always an over-reach charge rather than a factual dispute, and in most of those cases the dissenting lens supplied wording that keeps the finding and drops the reach. That corrected wording is printed under each finding.

Gap rows covered: A-R1, A-R2, A-R3, A-R4, A-R6, A-R7, A-R8, A-R9, A-R10, A-R11, A-R12, B-R1, B-R2. (B-R1 and B-R2 are carried in the claim set under the labels *three-terms-are-selves* and *camera-as-interpreter*; that mapping is inferred from the labels the claims carry, not stated anywhere in the packet.)

**The run's central question came back negative.** The chapter's compression at A-R3 — two selves plus one shared past fact make a community of memory — does not survive contact with Royce's own numbering, and A-R11, the claim that a platform meets the conditions more securely than a city because it keeps a permanent record, has no support anywhere the four angles reached; every source that pairs stored records with communities of memory argues the reverse. Read the Absences section before writing a word. Two of the rows the chapter leans on hardest are held up by material that is either unreachable (A-R8) or nonexistent (A-R11), and one of them (A-R7) is the chapter's own to own.

## Finding 1 — Royce's own second condition requires communication among distinct selves, so any formula that makes a shared relation to an outside past sufficient has dropped a condition he states in his own numbering (high, 3-0 on C29, 2-1 on C48, 2-1 on C49) [C29, C48, C49]

This is the load-bearing finding of the packet, and it is as solid as anything in the run: three independent scans of the 1913 Macmillan setting, plus the 1968 Chicago reset, plus Royce's own index to volume II ("second condition, 67 f."), plus Smith's footnote citing II, 67 for the same page. The three conditions are stated serially as conditions "upon which the existence of a community depends." A shared relation to an outside past satisfies the first and third and leaves the second untouched.

Two lenses split off at 2-1 on how far the condition reaches. The dissent on C48 is worth carrying rather than smoothing: Royce's wording is "capable of social communication, and, in general, engaged in communication," and the hedge is his, not a paraphrase. The condition is stated of the community, not of each pair of members, and Royce elsewhere allows that two members "may keep aloof" and "may be enemies" (II, 46). So the condition defeats the compression at community level; it does not license a reading on which any two members must be in communicative contact. The dissent on C49 was narrower still and concerned only the epithet "the first monograph devoted to Royce's community theory," which Smith's own preface does not claim — he says the theme has "not been sufficiently treated." Drop the superlative and the sentence is unassailable.

One citation repair: claim C29 cited Internet Archive item `problemofchristi0000josi`, which is lending-restricted and, on one lens's check, may land a reader in volume I. Cite `theproblemofchri02roycuoft` or `problemofchristi02royc`.

## Finding 3 — Acceptance is inside the definition of a community of memory, not a demand added after the memory conditions are met (high, 3-0 on C18, 3-0 on C52) [C18, C52]

Royce defines the community of memory by what each member does with the past, not by the past's availability. The constituting fact is that "each of its members accepts as part of his own individual life and self the same past events that each of his fellow-members accepts." The parallel definition of a community of expectation follows on the next page, and the governing sentence — "The concept of the community depends upon the interpretation which each individual member gives to his own self" — makes the members' self-interpretation the thing the concept depends on. So the chapter's two-stage picture, in which a group first meets the memory conditions and then some of its members take the day as their own, describes a stage Royce's text does not provide.

All three lenses agreed on both claims and independently fixed the pagination (the 1913 setting prints folios at the foot, established from the signature line "VOL. II E 49"; the SEP's own locator "PC 248" corroborates the 1968 page). Two of them flagged the same tactical point, and it should go into the draft rather than be left for a referee: Royce's very next sentence says a community "exists relatively to the past or future facts to which its several members stand in the common relation just defined." That sounds like the compression until you follow "just defined" back to acceptance. Quote it and disarm it.

One lens added a scope note worth keeping. Royce does present a definition at II, 50–51 and then, separately, conditions of existence at II, 60–68, so a two-stage *exposition* is not itself un-Roycean. What the text forbids is treating the memory conditions as satisfiable without the members' acceptance.

REFUTES the split. Bears on A-R2, A-R3, A-R7.

**Sources:** Josiah Royce, *The Problem of Christianity*, 2 vols. (New York: Macmillan, 1913), II, 50–51 (= 1968 Chicago reset, 248) ; no DOI ; https://archive.org/details/problemofchristi02royc

**Evidence:** "A community constituted by the fact that each of its members accepts as part of his own individual life and self the same past events that each of his fellow-members accepts, may be called a community of memory. Such is any group of persons who individually either remember or commemorate the same dead, — each one finding, because of personal affection or of reverence for the dead, that those whom he commemorates form for him a part of his own past existence." (II, 50)

"A community constituted by the fact that each of its members accepts, as part of his own individual life and self, the same expected future events that each of his fellows accepts, may be called a community of expectation, or upon occasion, a community of hope." (II, 51)

The concept of the community depends upon the interpretation which each individual member gives to his own self, — to his own past, — and to his own future." (II, 51)

## Finding 4 — Roycean membership does wait for the member's yes: the ideal extension is his own act, done for a reason he can give himself, and no one can set its limits from outside (high, 3-0) [C19]

Royce's first condition is the power of ideal self-extension, and immediately after stating it he insists the extension cannot be imposed. The member must "personally share in this task of determining how much of the past and the future shall ideally enter into my life," and must "be able to give myself some sort of reason, personal, or social, or moral, or religious, or metaphysical" for taking the burden on or throwing it off. Three lenses confirmed the doctrine without dissent.

Two calibrations came back. The locator: both quoted sentences stand wholly on II, 62 — page 61 ends mid-word at "All of us regard as be-" — so "II, 61–62" is loose, and the 1968 parallel should be given as 253–54 rather than 254 flat. And the idiom: "waits for his yes" is stronger than Royce's words. He requires an interpretive act performed for a reason the member can own, which is not the same as assent to an offer. Every lens declined to rule on the further step — that the chapter's use of "membership" at A-R12 is an equivocation — on the ground that the chapter was not in evidence. That step is the chapter's to make, and this passage is what makes it available.

## Finding 5 — Smith's 1968 introduction states the two-selves formula, but with appropriation already built into it, and then calls the whole apparatus "the bare structure of community" (medium-high, 2-1; lens 3 dissenting) [C5]

The formula is there and it is close to what the chapter compresses: "Two distinct selves who are willing to acknowledge or to interpret the same past fact as belonging to themselves are said by Royce to constitute a community of memory with respect to that past fact." But every lens noticed the same thing, and the dissent turns on it. Smith's sentence already carries the first-person appropriation requirement — *willing to acknowledge*, *as belonging to themselves* — which is precisely the element the chapter's compression drops. So Smith's formula is not the chapter's compression; the condition his sentence omits is communication, not appropriation. Calling it "something close to the chapter's compression" misdescribes which condition is missing.

Lens 3 also broke "immediately." Between the formula and the "bare structure" sentence lie 2,189 characters — about a full page, including the whole application to the Beloved Community and footnote 22. Smith's "Thus far" gathers up a page of exposition, not the sentence just written. And "says the interpretive process is what actually constitutes members as a community" upgrades his language: he writes that interpretation is "essential to community" and announces he will "now direct attention to" the process, which is a topic transition, not a claim that process rather than structure does the constituting. The two concurring lenses reached the same cautions and still voted to keep the finding, because the substance — that Smith labels the triadic apparatus a bare structure and moves to interpretation — is on the page.

One further gain, noted by two lenses and not in the original claim: the intervening page adds a mutual-knowledge requirement, "each member knows that every other member acknowledges as part of his own self the same sacred past and future which he acknowledges." That blocks the compression a second time.

BOTH — it supports the chapter's reading of what Smith says and complicates the chapter's use of it. Bears on A-R3, A-R2, A-R7.

## Finding 6 — Smith's 1968 introduction makes free acknowledgement essential to community, so appropriation is load-bearing rather than decorative (medium-high, 2-1; lens 3 dissenting) [C6]

Smith's argument against mystical merging carries a positive requirement: members must retain their individual identity "because their involvement must be freely acknowledged and it must express itself in practical tasks which the individual alone can perform." Applied to the Beloved Community: "The many members are identified through their willingness to extend or interpret themselves to include this Christian memory and hope."

Lens 3 refused the claim on three grounds and the dissent is instructive rather than fatal. Smith says *involvement* must be freely acknowledged, not *the shared past* — a different object, and the chapter's paraphrase quietly swaps them. The "because" makes free acknowledgement the *reason* Royce insists on retained individual identity, not a fourth condition standing in its own right. And the p. 25 sentence is about "this Christian memory and hope" specifically, not about a common memory generally. The two concurring lenses reached the same first and second points and judged the finding survives at reduced strength: acknowledgement is a requirement in Smith's account, but do not present it as a named fourth condition added to Royce's three.

One thing all three lenses flagged, and the chapter must not lose it: the sentence immediately following in the same paragraph reads "Through loyalty and practical devotion they are all linked with the cause which defines the community." Cite the p. 25 sentence and you have cited loyalty into the same breath — which matters directly for A-R9 and A-R10.

## Finding 7 — Smith's 1968 introduction imposes a temporal floor and a plurality requirement: there is no community at an instant (high, 3-0) [C7]

Unanimous, verbatim, and correctly located, with the page range independently confirmed from the volume's own contents page (Introduction 1, Author's Preface 37 — so pp. 1–36, and note that C16, which was refuted, cites the same piece as 1–38). ## Finding 9 — "The three conditions" names two different lists: Smith's summary folds Royce's first condition into a goal-and-deeds condition (medium-high, 2-1; lens 3 dissenting) [C10]

Royce's enumeration opens with the power of ideal self-extension; Smith's summary opens with a goal pursued through appropriate deeds. Smith's second and third track Royce's closely. All three lenses verified all four texts verbatim and confirmed the locators in both settings (1913 II, 60–61 / 67 / 68 = 1968, 253 / 255 / 256; Smith p. 75).

Two things did not survive contact with lens 3. First, "in the literature" attributes to a literature what one commentator did once; nothing found shows other commentators propagating Smith's list. Second, and more substantively, Smith does not *replace* or *drop* Royce's first condition — his item 1 keeps the ideal-extension idea inside itself ("deeds which belong both to all the members because part of their ideal selves"), and the two pages preceding the list expound ideal self-extension at length. He folds it in and imports the common-deeds material Royce develops later, at II, 79 ff. Lens 1 independently noted the same thing from the other end: Royce imposes a further restriction later in the same lecture ("Men do not form a community… merely in so far as the men cooperate," II, ~86–88, indexed as "still further restriction of, 86 f."), so Smith is compressing the chapter, not inventing a condition. The claim as written survived only because it said "no counterpart in this list."

The practical upshot for the chapter is a citation-hygiene point: a bare reference to "the three conditions" is ambiguous between Royce's enumeration and Smith's summary of it, and the chapter should name which.

## Finding 10 — Smith holds that cooperation without interpretation yields a corporation of interchangeable cogs, and that the community ends when the web of interpretation breaks down (medium-high, 2-1; lens 3 dissenting) [C4]

Every quoted word is on p. 76 and all three lenses confirmed it. The dissent is about attribution and about what the ellipsis hides.

Lens 3 refused "Smith states flatly." Every clause on the page is marked as report — "according to Royce," "For Royce" — so this is Smith expounding Royce, not asserting on his own authority. All three lenses independently objected to the ellipsis, which deletes a sentence material to the chapter's own argument: "The true community is one in which the members not only love it, together with its past and its anticipated future, but also they understand the part which they are to play in the successful completion of such goals as may be projected and striven for." That sentence puts love on the same page as a condition of the true community, which cuts against a clean loyalty/interpretation separation. ## Finding 23 — Viljoen names the mechanism by which a person is drawn into a data grouping and acted on without any act of his own (high, 3-0) [C58]

Unanimous. All three lenses retrieved the PDF of record from yalelawjournal.org and confirmed the pagination from the running heads. Ben is placed in the population by a feature he shares, not by anything he does, and Viljoen's abstract states that individualist data-subject rights cannot even *represent* those population-level effects. That is the closest thing in the source to "does not wait for his yes."

Three limits, each named by a different lens and each worth carrying. The block quotation is silently truncated: the sentence ends "…in common with him (in this case, his tattoo) and has shared data about this feature with a data collector," and the claim cuts at "with him." Restore or mark the cut. Viljoen's vocabulary is "population-based relations," "group classification," "data subjects," "population features" — never "membership"; the nearest she comes is "gang membership" as an ascribed social meaning imposed on Ben. The translation into membership is the chapter's, and should be marked as such rather than borrowed from her. ## Absences (searched for, not found)

These are the load-bearing part of this dossier. Several of them are more consequential than any finding above.

**Nobody supports A-R11.** No source argues that a permanent or digital record satisfies Royce's or Bellah's memory condition *more securely* than living recollection. The angle searched Internet Archive and Open Library full text for "community of memory" paired with "virtual community," "the Internet," "archive," "digital" and "permanent record"; Crossref for Royce plus digital/internet/online; Scholar Gateway for Royce plus platform/archive/permanent record. Every hit that pairs the concept with stored records argues the reverse (Bowers 1988 and 2000, Freie 1998, Aleida Assmann 2008/2011). The single hedged positive — MacDonald, Couldry and Dickens 2015 — claims only that digitization can, under specific conditions, sustain continuity, and its claim is about materialisation and use rather than storage. **A-R11 should be reversed or dropped, not cited.**

**Nobody has run Royce's three conditions at online platforms, virtual communities, or social media.** A targeted semantic search over the Wiley full-text corpus returned twelve passages across seven articles, none of which discuss Royce and online community together — the hits were brand-community bibliometrics, consumer community cognition, trans activist virtual community, internet restorative justice. Crossref queries pairing Royce with internet/online/virtual/digital return only the standard Royce corpus and its reception. On this evidence, anyone offering a Roycean reading of platforms is first. That is an opportunity and a hazard in equal measure: there is no standing literature to cite either way, and no one to blame for a misreading.

**Nobody has published on the Maori/communication seam.** Zero hits for *aloof*, *enemies*, *New Zealand*, *Maori*, *canoe* across the full OCR of Smith 1950 (202 pp.); zero across Smith's 1968 introduction; the case appears in neither encyclopedia; three web passes and four Crossref queries returned nothing. Treat the seam as open in the secondary literature, not settled either way.

**Smith's 1968 introduction never uses the word "communication."** Zero occurrences of *communicat*, *conversation*, *commemorat* across all ~141,000 characters of the introduction. The author who in 1950 listed communication as the second necessary condition drops the word entirely eighteen years later, organising the exposition around time, distinctness, ideal self-extension and interpretation instead. This is a real datum: even the condition's most authoritative expositor does not always restate it. Note what he replaces it with, though — interpretation, which is a stronger requirement, not a weaker one.

**Neither encyclopedia addresses contemporaneity.** Zero hits for *dead*, *deceased*, *ancestor*, *living members* in the SEP entry; zero for *dead*, *ancestor*, *New Zealand* in the IEP entry. Royce's framing of membership as holding among contemporaries, with the dead as what is commemorated, is carried by neither reference work. The only commentator evidence found is Smith's 1950 schematic gloss (Finding 8).

**No commentator reads the conditions as a loose family.** All three enumerations reachable in full text — Smith 1950, Parker's SEP, Kegley's IEP — present them as gating: "necessary," "can only exist where." Nothing was found presenting them as family resemblances or as individually dispensable. But the sample is three, and the monographs most likely to complicate it were unreachable, so this is an absence of counter-evidence rather than proof of consensus.

**No scholar states in so many words that a community of memory can exist without loyalty.** Royce's text and Smith's exposition entail it — both constitute the community of memory out of acceptance and interpretation alone — but neither says it as a thesis, and no secondary source poses the question. This is the sharpest gap in the answer to the loyalty rows: the conclusion is well grounded in the primary text and unsupported by any explicit scholarly statement.

**No scholarship theorizes failed or partial communities of memory in Roycean terms.** Three semantic searches over the Wiley corpus returned nothing on people standing in a common relation to a reported past without appropriating it. The nearest thing inside the Royce literature is Royce's own contrast between cooperation and community at II, 84–88 — and the claim built on that (C21) was refuted, because Royce's diagnosis there is comprehension failure, not appropriation failure, and he adds a tradition-side deficiency at II, 87 and an escape clause at II, 90. The nearest thing outside it is Bellah's "lifestyle enclave." A-R7 has no direct scholarly support.

**No scholar reads Royce's community of memory as already sufficient for belonging with no further appropriation required** — the reading that would refute the chapter's A-R2/A-R10 split. Searched Crossref, Scholar Gateway (32 passages, 19 unique articles) and Internet Archive. Nothing asserts sufficiency. The evidence runs the other way: appropriation is internal to the condition rather than an add-on, so the chapter's split is wrong in the opposite direction from the one it fears.

**No application to film audiences, fandoms, or a film's mythology taken up by later viewers,** under either the Roycean or the Bellah lineage. Searched Open Library full text for "community of memory" with *film*, *spectators*, *audience*, *fans*, *mythology*; all zero or irrelevant. The closest existing work is Baty on a circulated media figure and Leavy on news media, neither of which could be read to a printable standard. A-R8 has no precedent; present it as an extension.

**No application of Royce's community of memory to a city as such,** and none to a single day or an ephemeral public. Aguilar-San Juan on urban ethnic districts, Kemmis on the politics of place, and Walker on the rural South are the nearest, and all three route through Bellah. Royce's own place-based text is "Provincialism" (1908), whose scale is the province, not the city. On the one-day public, Royce's own nearest sentence runs the other way: at II, 52 he says a community of hope "may be, upon occasion, very rapidly constituted, even in the midst of great revolutions." If the chapter wants a fast-forming public, that is the citation — and it names expectation, not memory.

**No mention of Royce anywhere in *Habits of the Heart*.** Tested two ways: a full-text search by the lens that reached the unrestricted scan, and a corpus test pairing "Royce" with the book's own coinage "lifestyle enclave" (33 hits, none of them any of the five digitized *Habits* editions, all of which surface readily for other queries). The practical consequence: a referee who knows the phrase from *Habits* will not know it as Royce's, and the chapter should say so rather than assume the lineage is common ground.

**Unreachable and therefore unread.** Oppenheim's monographs (*Royce's Mature Philosophy of Religion*, *Royce's Mature Ethics*, *Reverence for the Relations of Life*); Kegley's *Genuine Individuals and Genuine Communities* and *Josiah Royce in Focus*; Auxier's *Time, Will, and Purpose*; Tunstall's *Yes, but Not Quite*; Fuss, *The Moral Philosophy of Josiah Royce*, ch. 8 "Loyalty and the Community," 199–258 (DOI 10.4159/harvard.9780674333000.c11); Cotton, *Royce on the Human Self*, ch. 7 "Loyalty and the Community," 238–265 (DOI 10.4159/harvard.9780674284517.c7); Trotter, *The Loyal Physician*; Foust's ch. 5 itself; all four published reviews of Foust; Radin's *Boilerplate*; Kim's *Wrap Contracts*. **The single highest-value item still unread is Kenneth W. ## How this lands in the chapter

The packet's net effect is to move the chapter's Royce section from an argument about *conditions met* to an argument about *conditions dropped*, and that is a better argument.

Start where the evidence is strongest. Royce's second condition is on the page, in his own numbering, corroborated by his own index, by Smith's summary and footnote, and by both reference works in the order they state things. A shared relation to an outside past satisfies the first and third conditions and leaves the second untouched. That single move retires A-R3, and it retires it without needing loyalty, without needing the Maori passage, and without needing anything a referee can contest.

Then close the appropriation question in Royce's own definiens rather than as a further demand. The community of memory is *constituted by* each member's accepting the same past events as part of his own life and self, and the extension that does the accepting cannot be set from outside. This is where the chapter's staging has to change: there is no stage at which the memory conditions are met and appropriation is still pending. A-R2 lands here cleanly; A-R7 has to be built here by the chapter itself, because nobody has written the failed-case analysis it needs.

Drop A-R11 or reverse it. Nothing supports it. Every reachable source that pairs stored records with communities of memory argues the other way, and Royce's own text supplies the counter-argument in three registers — the temporal floor, the corporation of cogs, and the whole of II, 84–90, where the enemy of community is complexity that outruns comprehension rather than any deficiency of the record. The chapter loses a striking line and gains a defensible one: a platform's permanence is not a strength on Royce's test, because his test never asked about preservation.

Handle loyalty carefully and in one place. The three conditions of existence name no loyalty; that much is verified and testable. But do not let the sentence drift into "Royce's account of community requires no loyalty," because II, 90–95 contradicts it in the same lecture, Smith puts love inside the true community on p. 76, Raposa says loyalty grounds the concept, and Shuffelton reports Royce making loyalty good whatever its object. The honest shape is: loyalty is not a condition of a community's *existence*, it is what furnishes and completes a community's *consciousness of itself* once cooperation has outgrown any member's understanding — and it cannot discriminate causes without loyalty to loyalty, which Royce introduces precisely because loyalty alone cannot.

On membership without assent, the chapter is on new ground and should say so. Nobody has run Royce's conditions at a platform. Warner gives the contrast term and hedges it; Viljoen gives the mechanism and calls it a population-based relation, not membership. The chapter's contribution is the third name, and the strongest form of the argument concedes the translation rather than smuggling it: Royce's membership waits for the member's act, Warner's public waits for attention, and platform enrolment waits for neither — which is why neither vocabulary fits and a new one is needed.

Two hygiene notes for the draft. Cite "the three conditions" by source, since Smith's list and Royce's are different lists. And drop every superlative the run flagged — "the standard commentator," "the closest existing precedent," "the one author," "the first monograph," "the nearest existing bridge." Not one of them survived verification, none of them is doing argumentative work, and each is exactly what a referee checks first.
