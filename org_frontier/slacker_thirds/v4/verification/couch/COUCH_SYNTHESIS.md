## The finding that changes the chapter

**Simmel returns to the deferred theme, and his own index says where.** At 1908 p. 321, inside *Der
Streit*, the *Distanz* he gave as his ground for postponing becomes a positive thesis — a certain
distance between the elements to be united and the point that unites them is an especially
favourable constellation for the bond — and both p. 103 examples come back on that page: the league
against a common enemy, and the God who gathers the most heterogeneous peoples. Simmel indexed it
himself: *Distanz des vereinigenden Momentes 321*. The alliance half recurs at p. 310, and cognate
material sits at pp. 142, 148, 172, 197–207.

He marks no connection anywhere, and **what never returns is the third as a third**. So the claim
the chapter needs is not that Simmel never came back to it. It is the narrower and more interesting
one P1 supplies: *the theme returns, in the dyadic register p. 103 itself predicted, and the third is
never restored.* Every sentence in §§1–3 that rests on non-return has to be rewritten before the
argument can stand — and it can stand, on the narrower claim.

Two corollaries. The invisible church does not simply vanish either: the *phrase* occurs once in the
1908 book, but the case returns in substance at p. 321 as *der weltumfassende Gott des Christentums*,
whose infinite remoteness is what lets him gather the heterogeneous. And the chapter's own gloss —
a third that does not know which two parties will meet — has no counterpart, no name, and no
advocate in seven German full texts. `Nichtwissen` and its neighbours were searched for directly.
That gloss is the chapter's, and A-S6 is `own-as-author`.

## Royce: three sentences must move, and the pass names their replacements

Four 3-0 refutations converge on one seam, and P3 agrees with the independent single-pass dossier.

Royce's second constituting condition requires communication among distinct selves, stated as a gate
on the existence of any community at all by both the Stanford and the Internet Encyclopedia entries.
**A-R3's compression contradicts the scholarship rather than simplifying it.** Royce also writes
first-person acceptance into the definition of the community of memory, so **membership in his sense
does wait for the member's yes** — A-R12 inverts him — and there is no stage at which the memory
conditions are met and appropriation is still outstanding, which collapses the chapter's
memory-then-appropriation split.

Loyalty is not one of the three conditions, which the chapter has right at 3-0. But Raposa reads
loyalty as the *ground* of community rather than as a later test, which undercuts the picture of a
platform that passes the memory conditions and then fails a separate loyalty gate.

The replacements are the most useful thing the pass returned. Salomé Viljoen's horizontal,
population-based data relation names inclusion in a grouping without any act of the person's own
(3-0); Michael Warner supplies "ascriptive belonging" (2-1, assigned to social groups rather than
publics). Either lets the chapter say what it means without spending Royce's word twice.

## P4: the extension is the author's, and the cost is now precise

Nobody in Peirce studies, machine semiosis, biosemiotics, film theory or media theory seats a camera
or any manufactured device in the interpreter's place between two parties. The one Peircean who
names cameras in this debate puts them in the sign-*producing* seat; the one film theorist who comes
near counts his three terms as director, film, and public.

What the run supplies instead is better than couch. Peirce depersonalises the interpreter without
vacating it — the *Quasi-mind*, in print in 1906 — so the move is not an innovation in the lineage
Royce credits. And the cost of the extension is sharper than a bare personhood rule: Royce's three
offices rotate among the terms at pleasure (II, 213). **A camera can occupy the interpreter's chair;
it cannot rotate into the other two.** That non-rotation is what the "extension is mine" flag should
own. The flag is not redundant.

## P2: the negative holds, but narrower than the chapter needs

No scholar claims to be taking up a third Simmel postponed. That negative survived four angles,
Crossref reference deposits, the Semantic Scholar citing graph and two full-text semantic sweeps.
The supporting detail is citable: Kwon et al. define broker and brokered alike as network actors, so
no non-actor can occupy the position; Gould and Fernandez never cite Simmel at all, and their five
types are two-step paths over an ordered pair; across seven core brokerage works, 720 deposited
references contain no German third-theory citation.

Against that, **Feld's focus theory occupied the non-node coordinator cell in the *AJS* in 1981**,
and the *Annual Review of Sociology* has already reviewed a literature on how non-node spatial
context forms ties. Vandenberghe put the "Simmel's third is insufficient" objection in print in 2018.
A-S7 is a `reframe`: true about the brokerage tradition, false as a claim that the cell is empty.

## P5: Reich's strongest yield, and three overclaims to retire

Fifty-eight survivors, the best of the six, as predicted from law reviews being open. Three
corrections land:

- **A-C1 `reframe`.** Reich's 783 is a demand inside his prescriptive Part V, not a naming of a
  grantor power. 751 names only a hedged direction of procedural advantage and never touches reasons.
- **A-C2 `own-as-author`.** No source attributes a four-power taxonomy to Reich, and his own lists
  are three-item, containing no pricing and no evaluation.
- **A-C3 `reframe`.** The form Reich names is feudal tenure over conditionally held wealth, offered
  as an analogy and predicated of the state throughout.

A-C4 and A-C5 come back `found` with tight page pins — terms written on any conditions (740), changed
at will (770), the grantor's own forum (770) — which is what the gap list asked for.

## P6: the joins are the chapter's, with four neighbours it must now name

No source narrates the 1908/1913/1964 genealogy or defines a platform as grant plus
membership-without-meeting plus Distanz. Most rows are `own-as-author`. But four near-neighbours have
to be cited rather than discovered by a referee:

- **Michael Warner** constitutes strangers as a collectivity through a common relation to something
  outside them, with no interaction among them — the A-S8 move, in publics theory.
- **Duranton and Puga** split match quality from match probability, a precedent for the reach/selection
  distinction the chapter claims at A-S9.
- **Silvey's "network community"** is real prior art for A-R4; the Roycean join must now say what
  Royce adds to Wittel plus Tönnies plus cosmopolitanism.
- **Julie Cohen's** "permanent and consistent membership" is a published near-occupant of A-C6.

And one refutation at 3-0 aimed straight at the chapter's inference: **Bordwell argues against
reading network film form as an effect of network technology.**

## What is owed to a library

The class-D residue fell where the brief predicted — German monographs and paywalled pragmatism.
The named debts that matter most:

- **P1, A-S5 and B-S3.** Fischer's "Der lachende Dritte" and "Tertiarität / Der Dritte"; Krech's
  monograph and handbook chapter on Simmel and religion, all closed access.
- **P1, B-S2.** Schlette 2021, the one located Royce–Simmel comparative study, unread.
- **P4.** Elena Esposito, *Artificial Communication*, and de Souza's semiotic engineering — the most
  promising non-ANT candidates, untouched. Chatman and Bordwell need reading on paper.
- **P5, B-C1 to B-C4, and P4, A-R6 / B-R3 / B-R4.** These rows were never tagged by the angle
  agents, so no claim in either packet bears on them. They are `open` for want of a scoped pass, not
  for want of sources — a briefing gap on our side, and the cheapest thing here to fix.
