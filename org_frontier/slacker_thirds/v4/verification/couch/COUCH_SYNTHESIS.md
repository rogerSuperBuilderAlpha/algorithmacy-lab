## What the pass changed

### Royce is where the chapter takes real damage

Four separate 3-0 refutations land on the same seam, and they converge with what
`P3_P4_royce_dossier.md` found independently.

**A-R3 does not survive.** Royce's second constituting condition requires communication among
distinct selves, and the pass could not find a reading that makes it droppable. The Stanford
Encyclopedia entry and the Internet Encyclopedia entry both state communication first and memory
second, as a gate on the existence of any community at all. So "shared relation to an outside past
or future is enough for membership" is not a compression the chapter can hedge; it is a claim the
scholarship contradicts, and the sentence has to move.

**A-R12 is refuted, and refuted in the opposite direction from the chapter's use.** Royce writes
first-person acceptance into the definition of the community of memory itself. Membership in the
Roycean sense *does* wait for the member's yes. The chapter's "membership here does not wait for his
yes" therefore cannot be a Roycean claim about the platform, and the equivocation the gap list
flagged is real rather than stylistic.

**The memory-then-appropriation split collapses.** The chapter treats the memory conditions as met
first and appropriation as a further demand (A-R2, A-R10). Royce puts acceptance inside the
definition, so there is no stage at which the conditions are satisfied and appropriation is still
outstanding.

**Loyalty is not one of the three conditions** — that much the chapter has right, at 3-0. But Raposa
reads loyalty as the *ground* of Royce's concept of community rather than as a later test, which
undercuts the chapter's picture of a platform that passes the memory conditions and then fails a
separate loyalty gate.

**The pass also names the repair, which is the most useful thing it did.** The chapter needs a
non-Roycean word for what a platform does to people who never assented. Salomé Viljoen's horizontal,
population-based data relation names inclusion in a grouping without any act of the person's own
(3-0), and Michael Warner supplies "ascriptive belonging" (2-1, with the caveat that he assigns it
to social groups rather than to publics). Either lets the chapter say what it means without spending
Royce's word twice.

One more constraint arrives from P3 rather than P4: William Ernest Hocking, reviewing *The Problem
of Christianity* on publication, treats the restriction of interpretation to minds as structural
(3-0), and John E. Smith reads the three terms as three selves separated by chasms. That tightens
A-R5 rather than loosening it.

### Simmel: the narrow claim holds, the broad one does not

The non-return survives in its narrow form. Nobody located here says Simmel redeems the promise of
*gesellschaftsbildende Vermittlung eines dritten Elementes*, and the textual work is unusually
strong: the phrase "unsichtbare Kirche" occurs exactly once in the 1908 *Soziologie*, in the
deferral itself, and Simmel's own subject index files p. 103 only under the two pair-broker
headwords. Lindemann's verdict is stronger than the chapter's — the third is repeatedly "forgotten"
and was never built into the foundations at all.

The broad novelty claim does not survive. German third-theory has standing names for exactly this
figure: Fuhse's *abstrakter* and *verallgemeinerter Dritter*, Fischer's *transzendenter Dritter*.
Fischer's terminological line is the one the chapter should adopt — *der Dritte* is by stipulation
the personal third, expressly not *das Dritte* (language, system, discourse). And a locator trap
worth carrying forward: Fischer and most German commentary cite the post-war Duncker & Humblot
pagination, which is why "p. 103" is invisible in that literature. socio.ch is a witness to the
wording only and cannot corroborate the 1908 page.

Bedorf is the live dissent: he argues that a third understood as a generalised other institutes no
triadic relation at all, only a dyad transposed. If he is right, the chapter's central figure is not
a triad. That claim reached only class D — the source was not fully retrieved — and it should be
settled before publication rather than after.

### P2 is the packet that most challenges the chapter, and it is only partly checked

**Feld refutes the chapter's premise twice.** Foci are defined as extra-network — not nodes, not in
the tie graph — and Feld explicitly allows a focus to be impersonal and to act on parties without
intent toward any particular pair. That is the chapter's deferred-third structure under another
name, published in 1981, and it is already in the chapter's apparatus for a different purpose.

**Someone does claim the deferred case.** Nagel, Roth and Kleve claim an impersonal, non-node third
— an object, a procedure, a family constitution, a common enemy — and someone has already argued in
print that Simmel's account is insufficient because every connection between two people presupposes
a third element that is a medium.

Against that, the chapter's caution about brokerage is vindicated in citable detail: Kwon et al.
define both broker and brokered as network actors, so a non-actor cannot occupy the position by
definition; Kellogg, Valentine and Christin inherit Simmel entirely through Burt and Obstfeld,
citing Simmel nowhere in 381 references; Stark and Pais contain no occurrence of "Simmel" or
"tertius" at all; and Ajunwa's *tertius bifrons* is a pair-broker whose Simmel genealogy is wrong in
a specific, citable way (3-0). A-S7 survives as a claim about the brokerage tradition. It does not
survive as a claim that nobody has taken the deferred case up.

### Reich: the strongest yield, and one clean correction

P5 returned 58 survivors, the best of the six, as predicted from law reviews being open.

The correction the chapter must make: **Reich's own fourfold scheme at 740 is a taxonomy of four
judicial theories licensing grantor power** — right versus privilege, the gratuity principle, the
whole and the parts, internal management — **not a taxonomy of four powers, and it contains neither
pricing nor evaluation** (3-0). The chapter's four-power taxonomy is already flagged as its own, and
this says exactly how it differs from Reich's, which is better than a flag.

Mashaw's positivist trap is the objection to walk around rather than into: an entitlement defined by
the grantor can be defined away by the grantor.

### The joins: mostly the chapter's, but with three neighbours it must now name

No source narrates the 1908/1913/1964 genealogy, and none defines a platform as grant plus
membership-without-meeting plus Distanz. A-C8 is `own-as-author` on the evidence.

Three neighbours are close enough that a referee would raise them:

- **Michael Warner** already constitutes strangers as a collectivity through a common relation to
  something outside them, with no interaction among them — the A-S8 move, in publics theory.
- **Kalmijn and Flap** already split tie formation into a supply side that delimits the pool and a
  demand side that chooses from it — reach and selection in all but name — and state the by-product
  condition the chapter needs for a setting that coordinates without intending to (3-0).
- **Seth Lazar** has already claimed the platform/city analogy in print, disclaims it as a working
  metaphor, and runs it in the opposite direction.

And one refutation aimed straight at §6's inference: **Bordwell explicitly argues against reading
network film form as an effect of network technology** (3-0). The chapter's platform-shape and
genealogy joins lean on that inference, and Bordwell is the authority they would otherwise cite.

## What is owed to a library

Class-D residue is concentrated where the brief predicted: German monographs and paywalled
pragmatism. Named debts include Bedorf's argument that a generalised third institutes no triad
(the live threat to the chapter's central figure), the two standing surveys of Simmel's reception in
American sociology — the correct place to test a Simmel-to-Royce link, both paywalled — and the
German announcement of "the third" as a paradigm shift. Each per-packet file lists its own class-D
items under *Class D — the source was never reached*.
