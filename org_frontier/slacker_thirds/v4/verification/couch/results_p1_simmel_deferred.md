# Deep research: contemporary couch for Simmel's deferred third (Packet 1)

**Run date:** 2026-08-12. **Angles:** four — (A) the German *Figur des Dritten* / *Tertiarität* literature and its vocabulary for a non-personal, distant third; (B) the reception history of Simmel's triad chapter and its citation practice; (C) the 1908 *Soziologie* itself, read as a full-text corpus for the fate of the deferred case; (D) the religion/belonging strand, from Simmel's *unsichtbare Kirche* out toward Krech, Harrington and a possible Royce comparison. **Verification:** three-lens adversarial, each lens required to retrieve the source itself and to state the source's own thesis before ruling.

**Counts.** Fifty-one claims went to verification. Twenty-nine survived; twenty-two were refuted. Of the refutations, thirteen came at 1-2 (two lenses against) and nine at 0-3 (all three lenses against). Of the survivors, nine carried unanimous 3-0 votes and twenty survived at 2-1 with a named dissent. The twenty-nine survivors merge into twenty-six findings below; every finding prints its vote, and where the merged claims split their votes both are shown.

**Gap rows covered:** A-S1, A-S2, A-S3, A-S4, A-S5, A-S6, A-S10, B-S2, B-S3, B-S4. A caution about that list: no gap-row table reached the four angle agents, so they worked from their own shorthand (Q1–Q4 for the couch questions, A1–A3 and B1–B2 for the primary-text and religion angles, "chapter claim 1–4" for the counter-sweep). The disposition table below is my reconciliation of that shorthand against the ten assigned rows, and each *why* cell states exactly what was and was not obtained, so a mis-seated row can still be read for its content.

**The central question came back negative — read the Absences section before writing a word.** Two negatives, one expected and one not.

The expected one: the German literature has no couch for this third. Nobody cites Simmel 1908 p. 103 for the deferral; nobody names the deferred configuration; nobody frames "ist indes in späterem Zusammenhang zu behandeln" as a promise, kept or broken. The chapter's own gloss — a third that does not know which two parties will meet — has no counterpart, no name and no advocate anywhere in the seven German full texts read. It is the chapter's own, and the chapter should own it as such.

The unexpected one, and the more consequential: **Simmel's own text refutes the chapter's claim that he never returned.** At 1908 p. 321, inside *Der Streit*, the very *Distanz* he gave as his ground for postponing becomes a positive thesis — a certain distance between the elements to be united and the point that unites them is an especially favourable constellation for the bond — and both p. 103 examples come back on that page, the league against a common enemy and the God who gathers the most heterogeneous peoples. Simmel indexed it himself: *Distanz des vereinigenden Momentes 321*. Two further sites, pp. 200/207 and p. 310, carry the same structure. The theme is not abandoned; what is never restored is the *third as a third*. Anything the chapter says about non-return has to be rewritten before the argument can stand.

## Finding 2 — The German literature does carry a standing name for a third that is not concrete and co-present — Fuhse's "abstrakte" / "verallgemeinerte Dritte," against the "konkrete Dritte" of network research — but the axis is abstractness and observability, not position outside a pair (medium, 2-1) [C2]

Two lenses read the paper and confirmed the quotations, the page footers and the substance; the dissenting lens confirmed the same wording but refused the claim on two counts, and both objections stick well enough to be printed.

First, reach. A single unpublished conference discussion paper cannot establish a "standing name," and the cognate terms are contested inside the same literature — Bedorf's §6.1.1 argues at length that a third conceived as a generalised other is no third at all (Finding 9). What Fuhse does supply is real and usable: he credits the distinction to Lindemann (2010) and Göbel (2011), which lifts it above one author's coinage.

Second, and more sharply, definition. Fuhse's contrast is concreteness and observability, and his own worked instance of an abstract third — the viewing public of a televised debate — he places expressly in a *tertius gaudens* position toward an identified pair of politicians. So an *abstrakter Dritter* in Fuhse is not thereby a third that fails to stand toward a pair. The chapter must not let the two axes blur, because Simmel's deferred case turns on *Distanz* and non-betweenness, not on abstractness. Note also that Fuhse writes only plurals; the singular *abstrakter Dritter* does not occur.

A retrieval discrepancy the chapter needs: two lenses fetched the Oldenburg URL and got Fuhse's 8-page paper; the third fetched the same URL and got a 10-page PDF whose internal title is "Fischer Polymorphie des Dritten - soz. Teilsysteme.doc." Cite the Wayback snapshot. COMPLICATES. Bears on A-S10 and A-S6.

## Finding 3 — Fischer draws the terminological line the chapter needs: "der Dritte" is by stipulation the *personal* third, expressly not "das Dritte" (language, system, discourse) nor the "sachlicher Dritter" as a non-personal magnitude (high, 2-1) [C4]

All three lenses verified both quotations verbatim against the SSOAR deposit and confirmed both page locators from the article's own running heads. What the dissenting lens refused was the claim's second sentence — that Simmel's deferred case therefore "falls into the seam Fischer opens rather than under his category." Two pages before the *sachlicher Dritter* passage, Fischer names exactly that case: the *transzendenter Dritter*, God, in whom *das Dritte* and *der Dritte* are interlocked as an encompassing transcendent magnitude. Simmel's one-God-uniting-all-believers example is the paradigm of that figure, so it falls under an explicit category of Fischer's, not into a seam his stipulation opens. The dissent is the finding here, and it is treated in full as Finding 4.

What survives, and it is enough: Fischer's stipulation confirms that in the leading German programme "der Dritte" means the personal third, and that an impersonal unifying magnitude is not a case under that heading. What Fischer never does is connect his transcendent-third category back to the sentence in Simmel where it is raised and deferred. COMPLICATES. Bears on A-S10 and A-S5.

## Finding 4 — Fischer does name a distant, encompassing, non-party third — the "transzendenter Dritter" (God), in whom "das Dritte" and "der Dritte" are interlocked — but he introduces it as theology's way of grounding its relation to its object, which the social-theoretical figure of the third renders immanent (high, 3-0) [C5]

Unanimous, and the one place in the German literature where a magnitude of Simmel's kind gets a name. All three lenses retrieved the deposit and found both quotations verbatim on pp. 3716 and 3720. The lenses converge on two qualifications the chapter should carry.

Fischer's adjectives are *transzendent* and *umgreifend*, not "distant" and "unifying," and the function he ascribes is instituting and lodging all relation-types — an epistemic-ontological grounding of theology's access to its object, not the unification of separated parties. And "supersedes" overshoots his own verb pair: theology is *depotenziert* into anthropology while philosophical anthropology *bewahrt* its cognitive potential. An Aufhebung that preserves, not a replacement.

So the German literature does have a slot for a transcendent unifying magnitude — it simply files it as the pre-history of the figure of the third rather than as an instance of it. That placement, not an absence, is what the chapter is up against. BOTH. Bears on A-S10 and A-S6.

**Sources:** Fischer, "Der Dritte/Tertiarität" (Campus, 2006), 3715–3735, here 3716 and 3720. URN urn:nbn:de:0168-ssoar-142590.

**Evidence:** p. 3716: "Die Theologie begründet ihr Verhältnis zum Gegenstand in dem unhintergehbaren Vermitteltsein durch einen transzendenten Dritten – Gott –, der sich offenbart als Voraussetzung von Erkennbarkeit überhaupt. Die Verhältnisse im Gegenstand bestimmt die Theologie von diesem umgreifenden transzendenten Dritten her (in dem »das Dritte« und »der Dritte« verschränkt sind), von dem aus alle in der Welt überhaupt vorfindlichen Relationstypen gestiftet und in den sie eingelagert sind."

p. 3720: "In der sozialtheoretischen Figur des Dritten wird schließlich der »transzendente Dritte« – Gott –, in dem das Dritte und der Dritte als umgreifende transzendente Größe verschränkt vorgestellt sind, immanent. Die Funktionen und Figuren des Dritten werden konkrete weltimmanente Größen, deren konstitutive Leistung für die sozio-kulturelle Welt beobachtbar werden. In dieser Denkbewegung vom transzendenten zum immanenten Dritten wird Theologie zur Anthropologie depotenziert, die als Philosophische Anthropologie das kognitive Potential dieser Theologie eines »transzendenten Dritten« bewahrt."

English: "Theology grounds its relation to its object in the ineluctable mediatedness through a transcendent third — God — who reveals himself as the precondition of knowability as such. Theology determines the relations within the object from this encompassing transcendent third (in which 'the third thing' and 'the third person' are interlocked), from which all relation-types found anywhere in the world are instituted and in which they are lodged." / "In the social-theoretical figure of the third, the 'transcendent third' — God — in whom the third thing and the third person are conceived as interlocked into an encompassing transcendent magnitude, finally becomes immanent. The functions and figures of the third become concrete world-immanent magnitudes whose constitutive achievement for the socio-cultural world becomes observable. In this movement of thought from the transcendent to the immanent third, theology is reduced in power to anthropology, which as philosophical anthropology preserves the cognitive potential of that theology of a 'transcendent third'."

*Correction applied, from lens 3:* "Fischer does name a non-party third — the 'transzendenter Dritter' (God), in whom 'das Dritte' and 'der Dritte' are interlocked as an encompassing transcendent magnitude — but he introduces it as theology's mode of grounding its relation to its object, and holds that in the social-theoretical figure of the third this transcendent third becomes immanent, its functions turning into concrete world-immanent magnitudes; theology is thereby 'depotenziert' into anthropology, its cognitive potential preserved."

**Provenance class:** A.

## Finding 5 — Fischer characterises the third person as addressed "in ihrer Distanz oder potentiellen Abwesenheit," but he derives it from the pronoun system and nowhere from Simmel's p. 103 deferral (medium, 2-1) [C6]

The quotation is verbatim and the negative half holds firmly: Fischer's only Simmel references in this piece are to the Berlin 1968 edition ("1968: 73–94"; bibliography "S. 32–100"), so the deferral is never in view. Two lenses accepted the finding; the dissenting lens refused two things, and both refusals should be printed.

"The nearest this literature comes to Simmel's *Distanz* argument" is a survey superlative, and it is refuted from inside the same article — Fischer's own *transzendenter Dritter*, an encompassing third outside the parties from which all relation-types are instituted, sits far nearer to the p. 103 configuration than the pronoun point does. And "grounds the third's distinctness on distance" overstates: distance and potential absence are one feature inside one of four coordinate arguments (*sprachformal, sozialisationstheoretisch, institutionentheoretisch, material*), and the article's own grounding move is anthropological, by way of Plessner's exzentrische Positionalität.

The locator also needs correcting: the sentence begins at the foot of p. 3721 and completes on 3722. COMPLICATES. Bears on A-S10, A-S6.

**Sources:** Fischer, "Der Dritte/Tertiarität" (Campus, 2006), 3715–3735, here 3721–3722. URN urn:nbn:de:0168-ssoar-142590.

**Evidence:** "Nun wird aber zusätzlich jemand in der »dritten Person« – »Er« oder »Sie« – vom Sprecher aus ansprechbar und damit eine weitere Subjektposition markiert, die aber anders als das anwesende »Du« angesprochen – nämlich in ihrer Distanz oder potentiellen Abwesenheit –, und zugleich definitiv anders als ein Etwas – ein »Es« – erfasst wird, nämlich personal. *Correction applied, from lens 3:* "In the language-formal argument of his four (§2.1), Fischer characterises the third person as addressed 'in ihrer Distanz oder potentiellen Abwesenheit' — a distance derived from the pronoun system, not from Simmel's p. 103 deferral, which he nowhere discusses. ## Finding 7 — Lindemann's verdict on Simmel: he leaves the third out of the apriori on which he sees sociology built, and because the third is repeatedly "forgotten," merely introducing it can still count as an "innovation" a century on (high, 2-1) [C12]

Both quoted sentences are verbatim on p. 495, and Crossref confirms the record. The dissenting lens objected to the quantifier, correctly. Lindemann's proposition is specific and bounded — Simmel does not take the third into account *when he explicates the apriorische Annahmen* — and four lines earlier on the same page she grants that Simmel does register the third's relevance for the objectivity of social formations (Simmel 1983: 66f.) and credits him with a typology of third-positions. "He never built the third into his foundations at all" inflates that into something the same page contradicts.

The "forgotten" diagnosis is also hers about the discipline — Simmel, Luhmann and Habermas together — with Simmel adduced as one instance, not as a verdict on his corpus. Read at its proper strength, this is the strongest commentator statement the run reached on whether Simmel delivered, and it says something subtler than "he didn't": the third never entered the foundations, so the question of a debt outstanding never arises for her. SUPPORTS. Bears on A-S5.

DOI 10.1515/zfsoz-2010-0604. Bronze OA publisher PDF; De Gruyter answers direct requests with an empty 202, so all three lenses read it through Wayback captures of the publisher PDF.

## Finding 24 — Two passages Simmel kept already bear on unity without contact: the railway carriage where strangers become a party without a word or glance, and the *Exkurs über den Fremden*'s commonality general enough to unite without pointing any particular pair at each other (medium, 2-1) [C47]

Both quotations verbatim, both pages confirmed, and the *Exkurs* extent verified from the printed contents ("Exkurs über den Fremden. S. 685—691"). The dissent, which the second lens partly anticipated, refuses the framing on three counts, and each refusal makes the material more useful rather than less.

"Strangers who never meet" is contradicted by the passage adduced: the railway passengers are co-present in one compartment, and it is that co-presence that constitutes them a party. What they never exchange is a word or a glance — a different thing, and Simmel classes the case as *konkret aber flüchtig*, expressly against the abstract but lasting solidarity of all wage-labourers on the preceding page, which is the case that would actually fit "never meet" and which the claim passed over. Second, neither passage names a third: at p. 320 the third is the *object of aversion* (Simmel's own phrase a few lines earlier is "eine gleichartige Aversion … gegen einen Dritten"), so the travellers are the unified party; and p. 689 concerns a *Gleichheit*, a shared quality, not a party at all. Third, "as well as or better than the deferred case" is a comparative judgement no source states.

Even so, the first passage strengthens the chapter's case rather than weakening it: here is an outside third unifying strangers who exchange neither word nor glance, in a book that supposedly deferred that configuration. REFUTES (the "only Simmelian third" claim). Bears on A-S2, A-S6.

**Sources:** Simmel, *Soziologie* (1908), chap. IV, p. 320, and chap. IX, "Exkurs über den Fremden" (pp. 685–691), at p. 689. Internet Archive `soziologieunters00simmrich`.

**Evidence:** p. 320: "…wenn einander sonst fremde, aber der gleichen höheren Bildungs- und Empfindungssphäre angehörige Persönlichkeiten sich in einem geselligen Kreise, in einem Eisenbahnwagen, od. ähnl., mit Personen von rohem und vulgärem Benehmen zusammenfinden. Ohne dass es zu irgendeinem Eklat kommt, ohne dass ein Wort oder Blick gewechselt wird, fühlen jene sich als Partei, zusammengehalten durch die gemeinsame Aversion gegen die, wenigstens im ideellen Sinne aggressive Pöbelhaftigkeit der andern."

p. 689: "…es funktioniert zwar als einheitliche Basis der Elemente, aber es weist nicht grade diese Elemente aufeinander hin, eben diese Gleichheit könnte ein jedes auch mit allen möglichen andern vergemeinsamen. … Der Fremde ist uns nah, insofern wir Gleichheiten nationaler oder sozialer, berufsmässiger oder allgemein menschlicher Art zwischen ihm und uns fühlen; er ist uns fern, insofern diese Gleichheiten über ihn und uns hinausreichen und uns beide nur verbinden, weil sie überhaupt sehr Viele verbinden."

English: "…when personalities otherwise strangers to one another, but belonging to the same higher sphere of cultivation and sensibility, find themselves together in a sociable circle, in a railway carriage or the like, with persons of coarse and vulgar behaviour. ## Finding 26 — Lindemann argues that the third must be integrated more far-reachingly than Simmel managed — as the precondition of the dyad rather than an addition to it — and states that her third is distinct from his (medium, 2-1) [C51]

Two lenses reached the full text (one through the author's offprint at uol.de, one through Scholar Gateway); the abstract sentence is verbatim against the Crossref deposit, and §4 "The Dyad and the Triad" is verbatim at pp. 88–90. Crossref confirms the record. Her Simmel references are again to the Berlin reprint ("Simmel 1983"), one more instance of the pagination problem behind Finding 6.

The dissent refuses "claims to go past every third Simmel wrote," and it is right to. Lindemann's engagement with Simmel here is bounded and thin: the absence of the third from the first chapter's apriori, its appearance in chapter 2 as a numerical extension, and the mediator and judge. She never surveys Simmel's thirds — no stranger, no poor, no outside power — and her stated comparison is with "the concepts developed by Simmel and Berger and Luckmann," not with everything he wrote. "Rather than as a resumption of the postponed one" also overstates: she is silent about the postponed case, and silence is not an explicit position against it.

The negative that matters survives intact. The one contemporary theorist who claims to go further than Simmel's third positions her own third as *distinct from his*, not as the resumption of anything he set aside. Nobody has picked the deferred case up. BOTH. Bears on A-S5, A-S6.

**Sources:** Gesa Lindemann, "The Analysis of the Borders of the Social World: A Challenge for Sociological Theory," *Journal for the Theory of Social Behaviour* 35, no. 1 (March 2005): 69–98, here 88–90. DOI 10.1111/j.0021-8308.2005.00264.x. Closed access; author's offprint PDF at uol.de; abstract confirmed via Crossref.

**Evidence:** §4, "The Dyad and the Triad": "With respect to the question of the third actor it is remarkable that Simmel does not mention it in the first chapter of his book Soziologie (Simmel 1983), which contains the basic definitions that constitute the research field 'society' (Simmel 1983: 21-30). … For Simmel the dyad is clearly the basic sociological constellation. … Simmel does not introduce the third actor until the second chapter, in which he points out that the number of involved individuals is of general importance for the sociating process. Simmel views the third actor as an extension that allows for a fuller understanding of the field of the social. Nevertheless the third actor has no foundational relevance for the more general problems concerning the constitution of social order."

Also §4: "The third actor is only important in adding new properties to the dyadic social relationship. … The third actor is not conceived as a third party who joins the preexisting dyad; instead, the third actor must be understood as the precondition for the very existence of dyadic social relationships between Ego and Alter. I have called this the constitutive function of the third actor. If one follows this conception, not the dyad, but the triad has to be conceived as the foundational constellation that is constitutive for sociality."

*Correction applied, from lens 3:* "Lindemann argues that the third must be integrated more far-reachingly than Simmel or Berger and Luckmann managed — as the precondition of the dyad rather than an addition to it — and states that her third is 'distinct from the concepts developed by Simmel and Berger and Luckmann'. She engages only Simmel's apriori, his numerical extension in chapter 2, and the mediator/judge; she nowhere mentions the postponed outside-power case, so she offers no resumption of it."

**Locator and pagination errors that would be caught by a referee**

- **C21 [0-3]** — "Simmel's *Sachregister* … contains no headword at all for the outside power." Three failures: the apparatus is the **Materienverzeichnis**, not a Sachregister, and Simmel names it so himself; it is not "unnumbered" (778, 779, 780, 781, 782 are printed); and it does carry headwords for the configuration — "Distanz des vereinigenden Momentes 321," "Bedrohung mit dem Kampf als zusammenschließendes Moment 321," "Unterordnung unter einen Einzelnen als Vereinheitlichungsmoment der Gruppe 141," "Verdichtung realer zu idealen Herrschaftsmächten 200," "Vereinigung zu Kampfzwecken 316." The index's own headnote also disclaims exhaustiveness: "Die Seitenzahlen geben nicht jede Erwähnung des Gegenstandes, sondern nur den Beginn seiner ausführlicheren Erörterung an." Its silence at p. 103 is therefore consistent with the deferral, not evidence of neglect. *The true and narrower finding:* the apparatus files p. 103 only under the two pair-broker headwords, and files the configuration 218 pages away.
- **C34** — "the only index headword covering the p. 103 problem … points into Der Streit." The pointing is real and good; "the only" is false on the same index (at least six other headwords cover unification through a common outside). Also: "Bedrohung" is ~17–25 entries earlier, on p. 777, not "three entries earlier" on 778; and the scan's missing running heads are 776–777, not 779.
- **C37 [0-3]** — "only reaches the invisible-church half … twenty pages later." The pole-swap reading of p. 315 is right and the locator corrections it volunteered are right (p. 265, p. 315). The interval is six pages, not twenty: p. 315 to p. 321.
- **C39 [0-3]** — the 1898/1905 religion essay. The causal-arrow reading is correct and the "purely derived form" quotation is verbatim on p. 369, but: the priesthood sentence is on pp. 370–371, not 371; the heretics sentence is wholly on 371, not 371–372; the transcription reads "And this, **to** turn"; and, decisively, the essay never uses "invisible" and does not treat the invisible-church configuration at all.

**Claims whose paraphrase inverts or overstates the source**

- **C10 [0-3]** — Lindemann's diagnosis stated backwards. She says Tertius, the personal actor, is tacitly identified with the ORDER and "verschwindet gleichsam in der Ordnung" — the collapse of the *first* sense into the *second*. The claim had it the other way round, and its own quoted German contains the refutation. Correct form: the personal third is absorbed by the order it was invoked to generate, after which theory reverts to a dyadic Ego–Alter relation to that order (p. 495).
- **C22 [0-3]** — "the phrase *unsichtbare Kirche* occurs exactly once … which is direct textual confirmation that Simmel never returns to the invisible-church example." The count is right (one occurrence, p. 103, in both scans). The inference is not: a phrase count establishes that he never repeats the phrase. He returns to the substance at p. 321 under a different description. This claim also contradicts Finding 12 inside the same dossier.
- **C33 [0-3]** — "*Der Streit* contains the promised later treatment … and reverses his own verdict." Both textual halves are sound; use Finding 12 instead. Simmel never called the case un-sociological (he granted at p. 103 that the outside power *stiftet* a unification); what he denied was triadic *Wechselwirkung*, and p. 321 is stated in exactly the two-sided terms he predicted. There is no reversal on the record, and no cross-reference in either direction. The citation also gives chapter IV as opening at p. 246; the *Kapitelverzeichnis* says 247.

Worse for the claim, the widened evidence cuts against it: Simmel *does* name the destination chapter when he defers forward (pp. 69, 82, 176, 430, 569), and at p. 103 he names none — "in späterem Zusammenhang" is the only occurrence of that phrase in the volume, and pp. 321–322 carry no back-reference. Use the fuller picture, which is more interesting than the claim was.
- **C8** — Fischer's inventory as containing no matching entry. The quotation is verbatim and the second, unquoted list is equally pair-facing, but the last entry of the first list is "der Dritte als … gemeinsamer Feind" — Simmel's own p. 103 example. The claim's NOTE asserting a direction of production ("an EXCLUDED third produced by a pair") is read into Fischer, who states none. Concede the entry; the finding is stronger with it, since Fischer files as a third precisely the case Simmel excluded.
- **C3**, **C9**, **C11** — the pagination and terminology claims about Fuhse and Lindemann. Fuhse's bibliography says only "Frankfurt a.M.: Suhrkamp," with no "Gesamtausgabe Bd. 11"; his "114ff." is not a re-pagination of 1908 p. 103 but a different passage; and "never surfaces in German scholarship" is refuted by Koschorke. And C11 splices two Lindemann sentences in reversed order behind an ellipsis, drops von Wiese, and attributes to her, in her own voice, a position she is reporting from Luhmann.

## How this lands in the chapter

Start with the correction, because it is not survivable as a footnote. The chapter's claim that Simmel named the outside-power third and then abandoned it cannot stand. Two hundred and eighteen pages later, in *Der Streit*, the very *Distanz* he gave as his ground for postponing becomes his thesis — a certain distance between the elements to be united and the point that unites them is an especially favourable constellation — and both of his p. 103 examples come back on the page, the league held together by a standing threat and the world-encompassing God who gathers the most heterogeneous peoples. He indexed it himself. Chapter III supplies the rest: the distinction between a God grown out of a group and a God-idea that first unites (p. 142), the height that lets one power bind wide circles (pp. 148, 172), the law and then the concrete object as impersonal coordinators (pp. 197–207), all in the same verb, *stiftet*, and one of them in the same phrasing, *einer außerhalb … gelegenen Instanz*.

But the correction hands the chapter a better argument than the one it loses, and the better argument is exact. What returns is the *configuration*; what never returns is the *third*. At p. 103 Simmel did not say the case was unsociological — he said it yields *Zweierkonfigurationen* rather than interactions encompassing all three. Every later site vindicates that prediction. Page 321 states the thesis in exactly two-sided terms, elements on one side, uniting point on the other. Page 142 treats the God-idea under a group's common subordination to a single power. Page 310 sets the plurality-with-a-shared-enemy beside the group-as-a-whole, and then develops the other branch. Page 320 gives an outside third that welds strangers who exchange neither word nor glance — and files it under aversion, as *konkret aber flüchtig*. Simmel kept his own accounting. He treated the material where it belonged in his scheme and never once said "as promised," because on his own analysis nothing triadic had been promised. The unkept promise is not a lapse; it is a scoping decision holding firm across seven hundred pages.

That reframing is what makes the couch question answerable in the negative without embarrassment. The German literature has no name for this third because the literature is built on the axis Simmel chose. Fischer stipulates "der Dritte" as the *personal* third and expressly rules out the impersonal magnitude; where he does name a distant encompassing one, the *transzendenter Dritter*, he assigns it to theology's pre-history and narrates its immanentisation, so it enters his story as what the figure of the third supersedes rather than as an instance of it. Fuhse's *abstrakte Dritte* runs on abstractness and observability, and his own example is a *tertius gaudens* toward two named politicians. Bedorf's distance is a mediator's equidistance and his refusal of the generalised other comes with *exzentrischer Dritter* already carved out as the exception. Every entry in the fullest German catalogue is grammatically constructed as "not only the other as X, but the third as Y." The field studies thirds that stand toward an identified pair, and Simmel told it to.

So the chapter should stop looking for a couch and say plainly that there is none — then say why, which is the interesting part. The gloss the chapter wants, a third that unifies without knowing which two will meet, has no name, no counterpart and no advocate anywhere in the reachable literature. That is the chapter's own contribution, and it should be claimed as such in the author's own voice rather than smuggled in as a reading of Simmel or of Fischer. Two supports are legitimately available for it, and they are Simmel's, not the commentators': the *Exkurs über den Fremden*'s commonality that "does not point precisely these elements at one another," and the wage-labourers' abstract but lasting solidarity against an abstract enemy at p. 319 — a case the chapter has so far walked past in favour of the railway carriage, which is the weaker instance because those passengers are in the same compartment.

Four things must be fixed in the apparatus before any of this goes to a reader. The index entries for "Unparteiische" and "Vermittler" are on p. 781, not 778. Kostrova's summation passage is on p. 190. Göbel's Simmel passage is on pp. 143–144 and his article is not open access. And every English rendering in the chapter is the author's own translation from the 1908 German — Wolff was never reached, and one archive.org item catalogued under his name is a Sanskrit devotional text. Say so in a note.

Finally, name the risk. Five unread items could still overturn the negative, and one of them is cheap: Fischer's "Der lachende Dritte" in *Die Figur des Dritten*, twelve pages, the standard German essay on Simmel's third, sitting behind a Suhrkamp paywall. Beside it, the Tyrell/Rammstedt/Meyer volume's page-by-page index of GSG 11 would settle the question outright — its entry for GSG 11 p. 126 either registers the deferral or it does not. A chapter that publishes the negative while naming those two as unread is honest. One that publishes it without naming them is not.
