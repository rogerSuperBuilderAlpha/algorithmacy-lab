# Deep research: the eleven untagged rows (Packet 7)

Run date 2026-08-12. Four cluster angles — Simmel and the figure of the third; Royce, Peirce and the
community of interpretation; Reich's *The New Property* and its reception; and the genealogy the
chapter's triad would need. Every candidate claim went through three-lens adversarial verification,
each lens retrieving independently and required to state the source's own thesis before ruling.

This run exists to close eleven gap rows that a prior pass left open for a briefing fault. That pass
sent its angle prompts the packet questions but not the row text, so no claim was ever tagged to
these rows and the rows came back empty for a reason that had nothing to do with the literature.
**This run carried the row text.** Each angle agent worked with the row wording in front of it, and
every claim below names the rows it bears on.

The eleven rows: **A-S5, B-S2, B-S3** (Simmel) · **A-R6, B-R3, B-R4** (Royce/Peirce) · **B-C1, B-C2,
B-C3, B-C4** (Reich) · **A-C8** (the genealogy).

Fifty-nine claims went to verification. Twenty-seven survived; thirty-two were refuted — fifteen of
them unanimously, by all three lenses, and seventeen by two of three. Of the survivors, nine carried
a clean 3-0 and eighteen carried a 2-1, with the dissent recorded in each case below.

**One cluster came back wholly negative.** The Simmel angle (A-S5, B-S2, B-S3) produced eighteen
claims and not one survived. Nothing in that cluster is safe to cite, and the reader should go to
**Absences** before reading the findings — the Simmel material's value in this run is the map of what
is not there, plus a set of verifier corrections to refuted claims that reverse the chapter's
expected contrast. The Royce, Reich and genealogy clusters produced findings.

## Finding 1 — Michael Raposa reads the very page the chapter cites for the three terms as an interior conversation in which a person interprets herself to and for herself, so that selves occupy all three offices even in the solitary case (medium-high, 2-1) [C19]

Two lenses reached the full text of Raposa's article through the publisher's corpus and found both
quoted stretches verbatim, in order, in the section named, with Crossref confirming volume, issue,
pages and DOI. Raposa pins the sentence to Royce 2001, p. 287 — the same reset pagination the
chapter's `[^7]` gives for 2:142 — and he holds that communities exist at all only because
individuals perform acts of interpretation. That is the standard reading the chapter needs.

The lenses disagreed about what follows. Lens 3 could not reach the article at all: Unpaywall reports
`is_oa` false with no OA location, the publisher was closed to that session, and it refused the claim
on retrieval grounds while also objecting that the gloss over-reaches at the "i.e." — in Royce's
solitary case the three terms are temporal phases of one person, and reading the offices as occupied
by plural selves imports the very point in dispute. Lenses 1 and 2 met that objection halfway. Both
insisted that Raposa's next sentence pulls against a self-sufficient solitary interpreter: the
interior conversation "is hardly a private conversation as it is shaped by all of the communities to
which one belongs," and his whole argument is that we need diverse others in order to interpret
ourselves. So the chapter can have the offices-filled-by-selves reading, but not a solitary
interpreter who needs nobody. Both lenses also flagged what nobody could test: the equation of
Royce 2001, p. 287 with 2:142 of the 1913 edition is asserted, not shown — Raposa cites the 2001
edition only. **BOTH.** Bears on B-R3 and B-R4.

**Sources:** Michael Raposa, "Troubled Diversities, Multiple Identities and the Relevance of Royce:
What makes a community worth caring about?", *Educational Philosophy and Theory* 44, no. 4 (2012):
432–443 ; DOI 10.1111/j.1469-5812.2010.00735.x ; https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-5812.2010.00735.x

**Evidence:** sec. "The Relevance of Royce": "Human selves are signs. Human consciousness is a
continuous stream of semiosis, an ongoing, interior conversation whereby a person interprets herself
to and for herself (Royce, 2001, p. 287). Human identity involves some consistency in interpretation
over time; it requires that the process of interpretation be to some extent deliberate and
self-controlled. […] Now, for Royce loyalty as love is also in its most perfectly developed form
manifested as a will to interpret. And communities exist at all only because individuals engage in
acts of interpretation, both of themselves and of each other."

*Correction applied, from lens 1:* "Raposa glosses Royce 2001, p. 287 as consciousness being 'an
ongoing, interior conversation whereby a person interprets herself to and for herself,' adding that
this conversation 'is hardly a private conversation as it is shaped by all of the communities to
which one belongs' … The identification of Royce 2001, p. 287 with 2:142 of the 1913 edition needs
its own check."

## Finding 2 — The standard reference account defines the Community of Interpretation as a totality of minds and expressly counts representation to one's own future self as interpretation (high, 2-1) [C25]

All three lenses found the sentence verbatim in §2.1 of the Spring 2026 SEP entry, confirmed the
section heading against the entry's own table of contents, and confirmed authorship and edition
against SEP's citation page. The disagreement is entirely about what the sentence licenses. Lenses 1
and 2 accepted that a community defined as a totality of minds excludes a non-mind from membership
and that the "or to their future selves" clause covers interpretive relations between terms that
never co-exist — while both insisted the chapter present the first as an inference from a definition
rather than as a statement Parker and Pratt make. Lens 3 refused the pair outright: the sentence
names Royce's substitute for the Absolute, it does not police eligibility, and representation to
one's own future self is a relation inside a single continuous mind, which is a poor model for terms
that never meet.

The dissent is worth carrying into the prose. The entry certifies what the standard account *is*; it
does not argue against machines, animals or nature, because it never raises them. **SUPPORTS**
(with the barring clause demoted to inference). Bears on A-R6 and B-R4.

**Sources:** Kelly A. Parker and Scott Pratt, "Josiah Royce", *The Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy* (Spring 2026 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.) ; (no DOI) ;
https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/royce/

**Evidence:** §2.1 "Metaphysics and Epistemology: Idealism and Interpretation": "In The Problem of
Christianity he was finally able to replace the old terminology of the Absolute with a description of
an infinite Community of Interpretation. This community is the totality of all those minds capable of
representing aspects of Being to one another or to their future selves."

*Correction applied, from lens 1:* "The standard reference account thus defines membership in terms
of minds and includes representation to a future self; that a non-mind is thereby barred from the
interpreter's office is an inference from this definition, not a statement in the entry."

## Finding 3 — Royce's "interpreting spirit" need not be embodied in any single individual, and the reference account calls it a super-human personality rather than a mechanism (medium-high, 2-1) [C26]

The quotation is verbatim in §2.2.2 of the same entry, and all three lenses confirmed it, the
section, and Parker's authorship of §§1–2. Two lenses also ran whole-file searches: the strings
"mechanis", "machine", "automat" and "impersonal" return zero across the entry. That is the finding's
strength and its weakness at once. Lens 2 treated the zero counts as making the negative explicit and
sayable; lens 1 accepted the passage but told the chapter to drop "nowhere a mechanism" as an
argument from silence; lens 3 refused the claim on three grounds — silence is not doctrine, "the
closest thing in Royce scholarship" is a superlative an encyclopedia entry cannot establish, and the
sentence reports Royce's usage rather than stating Parker and Pratt's own commitment.

What survives all three readings: the interpreting spirit is depersonalised with respect to any given
individual — it "may on occasion be embodied by a single person … but this is not always the case" —
while remaining, in the entry's own vocabulary, a personality and a spirit. The entry says nothing
either way about mechanism. **BOTH.** Bears on A-R6 and B-R4.

**Sources:** Parker and Pratt, "Josiah Royce", SEP (Spring 2026), §2.2.2 "Theory of Community" ;
(no DOI) ; https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/royce/

**Evidence:** §2.2.2: "Individuals remain individuals, but in forming a community they attain to a
kind of second-order life that extends beyond any of their individual lives. Where a number of
individuals' loyalty to a cause is coordinated in community over time, Royce spoke non-figuratively
of a super-human personality at work: a genuine community is united by a guiding or 'interpreting
spirit.' The interpreting spirit may on occasion be embodied by a single person such as a leader or
other exemplar, but this is not always the case."

*Correction applied, from lens 1:* "…the office is depersonalised in respect of any given individual
without being vacated. (Drop 'nowhere a mechanism': SEP nowhere raises the question, and silence is
not denial.)"

## Finding 4 — Among the commentators reached, the triadic form of Royce's Community of Interpretation is stable while the identity of the three terms is not (medium, 2-1) [C20]

All three lenses verified Fabbrichesi's §12 sentence and her footnote 11 verbatim — one of them
through the Wayback capture, OpenEdition having gone behind a bot-wall — and all three confirmed the
internal inconsistency the claim flags: her note cites "Briody (1969: 27)" while her bibliography
gives the article's pages as 225–42, so the note's page cannot be right (227 is the likely
truncation). Briody's article is not in Crossref and no lens reached it.

Two things broke. Lens 3 refused the claim on its quantifiers, and lens 2 agreed with the substance
of that objection while still passing the claim: "triadicity is uncontested in the secondary
literature" generalises from a sample of two, one of which is reported at second hand inside the
other. Lens 1 added the sharper limit — no lens retrieved *PC* 2:142, so the comparison that carries
the whole point, that Fabbrichesi's relata differ from Royce's three at that locus, is unevidenced
here. The finding therefore holds in a restricted form: two readers, both treating the community as
triadic, assigning the triad different members. Nothing follows about the literature as a whole.
**COMPLICATES.** Bears on B-R3.

**Sources:** Rossella Fabbrichesi, "The Body of the Community: Peirce, Royce, and Nietzsche",
*European Journal of Pragmatism and American Philosophy* II, no. 1 (2010) ; DOI 10.4000/ejpap.931 ;
https://doi.org/10.4000/ejpap.931

**Evidence:** §12: "The Community of Interpretation traces a triadic sign-relation between the
individual, the community, and their common end; and, yet, it is only due to this act of connection,
which is directed to the fulfilment of a determinate purpose, that this relationship can individuate
its respective poles and eventually succeeds in being significant." — n. 11: "Briody (1969: 27)
proposes to see as members of the triad the Interpreter, the Interpretant and the Interpreted, and
more specifically, the Beloved Community, the Individual and Christ."

*Correction applied, from lens 3:* "Two commentators, both reading the community as triadic,
disagreeing about which three terms; nothing follows about the literature as a whole, and Briody's
own text was not reached."

## Finding 5 — Kegley grounds knowledge of external reality in being-with-others, and quotes Royce's own social test of externality from an 1894 essay independent of *The Problem of Christianity* (high, 3-0) [C23]

The cleanest result in the Royce cluster. Three lenses reached the open-access text, found the §29
prose and the Royce block quotation verbatim and in that order, and confirmed the paragraph number
from OpenEdition's own paragraph markers. Kegley's bibliography identifies the quoted work as Royce,
"The External World and Social Consciousness," *The Philosophical Review* III (5) (1894): 513–45, and
her pin cites at §29 (520) and §30 (519) are internally consistent, so the chapter gains a textual
base for the communication condition two decades before *PC* 2:67.

Two calibrations, both from lens 3, neither a refutation. Royce's syntax attaches "agreed upon by a
number of socially communicating beings" to "the definably localizable in space"; the externality
test in the sentence is the shorter clause, "only what all can agree upon can stand the social test
of externality." And Kegley's own sentence is about *knowledge of* external reality, slightly
narrower than externality itself. Lens 1 noted a third thing: the trailing ellipsis cuts a
conclusion about spatial definiteness as relatively a priori, which does not weaken the use.
**SUPPORTS.** Bears on B-R4 and A-R6.

**Sources:** Jacquelyn Ann K. Kegley, "Josiah Royce: A Neglected Figure and a Valuable Resource in
Mining the Pragmatism/Phenomenology Interactions for Current Philosophical Inquiry", *European
Journal of Pragmatism and American Philosophy* XIV, no. 2 (2022) ; DOI 10.4000/ejpap.3123 ;
https://doi.org/10.4000/ejpap.3123

**Evidence:** §29: "Not only does Royce see self-consciousness as fundamentally grounded in
being-with-others, but he sees all knowledge of external reality as so grounded. For Royce, community
experience is that which distinguishes inner from outer, the outer world being the world whose
presence can only be indicated to you by your definable, communicable experience." — then, quoting
Royce (1894: 520): "Therefore, as only the definably localizable in space can be independently
verified and agreed upon by a number of socially communicating beings, and as only what all can agree
upon can stand the social test of externality, the principle that what is for all must, if in space
at all, occupy a definite place of size and boundaries, becomes a relatively a priori principle for
all the things of the verifiable worlds."

*Correction applied, from lens 3:* "Kegley grounds knowledge of external reality in
being-with-others … She then quotes Royce's claim that 'only what all can agree upon can stand the
social test of externality', the agreeing parties being 'a number of socially communicating beings' —
a textual base distinct from PC 2:67."

## Finding 6 — The SEP and IEP entries state the same conjunction in near-identical words and are not two witnesses (high, 3-0) [C29]

All three lenses fetched both encyclopedia entries and set them side by side. The IEP passage is
verbatim as reported, including the printed typo the claim brackets ("a[c] community of hope" — the
page reads "ac community of hope"), and the SEP comparator is verbatim. The overlap runs further than
the claim states: lens 3 showed it covers two consecutive sentences in each entry, since IEP's

both are compressing Royce at *PC* 248 — which is an additional reason not to count them twice, and
lens 1 noted they also share the "logically prior to individuals" framing and the same *PC* 357
quotation.

No lens could establish a direction of borrowing; the IEP page carries no revision date. The claim is
unusual in this batch for being self-limiting: it draws no inference beyond refusing to double-count,
which is exactly right. **BOTH.** Bears on A-R6.

**Sources:** Jacquelyn Ann K. Kegley, "Josiah Royce", *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
(peer-reviewed, ISSN 2161-0002) ; (no DOI) ; https://iep.utm.edu/royce/ — compared with Parker and
Pratt, SEP (Spring 2026), §2.2.2, https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/royce/

**Evidence:** IEP: "And community is more than any association or collection of individuals;
communities can only exist, in Royce's view, where individual members are in communication with one

## Finding 7 — Traynor's Shively passage, with its Reich string cite at 751–55 and its pin to 752 n.97, was re-quoted verbatim by the California Court of Appeal two years later — inside a decision that narrows Shively (medium-high, 2-1) [C31]

All three lenses pulled the Caselaw Access Project text of *Everett v. Gordon* and found the
re-quotation real and verbatim, Reich string cite and 752 n.97 pin included, two years after
*Shively*. All three also insisted on the same two corrections, which is unusual and makes them
reliable. The pin: CAP's page-label for \*675 falls *inside* the citation, between "Reich, The" and
"New Property", so the quoted sentence begins on 674 and the accurate cite is 674–75. The posture:
*Everett* quotes Traynor while holding that "the ordering of prehearing depositions … is not
authorized by Shively and goes beyond its scope," and affirms against the party invoking it.

Lens 3 refused the claim on the word "settled" and on a drift in "concentrated-function grantor" —
Traynor's concern is an agency that is simultaneously accuser, party and adjudicator, a
prosecutorial-adjudicative concentration rather than a grantor's power over largess. Lenses 1 and 2
made the same objection without refusing: what is established is that one passage has been stated
twice in the California reports, not that a doctrine has settled. Take the weaker claim, which is all
B-C1 needs. **SUPPORTS.** Bears on B-C1.

**Sources:** *Everett v. Gordon*, 266 Cal. App. 2d 667, 674–75, 72 Cal. Rptr. 379 (1968) ; (no DOI) ;
https://static.case.law/cal-app-2d/266/html/0667-01.html — quoting *Shively v. Stewart*, 65 Cal. 2d
475, 421 P.2d 65 (1966) (Traynor, C.J.)

**Evidence:** "In the Shively case, Chief Justice Traynor carefully considered the same argument made
by appellants herein, stating: … 'A disciplinary proceeding has a punitive character, for the agency
can prohibit an accused from practicing his profession. (See Reich, The New Property (1964) 73 Yale
L.J. 733, 751-755, 781, 784.) Since the agency is the accuser, a party to the proceeding, and
ultimately makes a decision on the record, its concentration of functions calls for procedural

*Correction applied, from lens 1:* "Everett v. Gordon, 266 Cal. App. 2d 667, 674-75 (1968),
re-quotes Traynor's Shively passage verbatim … though it does so while holding that Shively does not
authorize the discovery sought. The 751-55 reading of Reich has thus been stated twice in the
California reports; it is not thereby a settled doctrine."

lens verified both passages verbatim and independently fixed both pages — lens 1 by reconstructing
the whole 43-head running-head sequence and landing exactly on 493, the article's last page; lens 2
by splitting the PDF on form feeds; lens 3 by PDF page index. Note 28 reads "Reich, supra note 18, at
783-85," which is what makes this bear directly on B-C3: Van Alstyne demotes the demand *by page

finding while trimming the same honorifics — "the leading contemporary critic" and "the canon's
principal critic" are unsupported superlatives, and Henry Monaghan's essay sits in the very same 1977
Cornell issue. Lens 1 also caught a locator error the chapter must fix: the "real weight on
substantive due process" half is at 485, not at 455. **REFUTES.** Bears on B-C3.

**Sources:** William Van Alstyne, "Cracks in 'The New Property': Adjudicative Due Process in the

## How this lands in the chapter

Three of the four clusters change what the chapter has to write, and one of them changes it in the
direction the chapter will least enjoy.

The Reich section needs its pins moved and its posture reversed. Two pages the chapter leans on — 741
and 786 — are cited by nobody this run could find, in courts or in commentary, while the pages the
reception actually uses are 734–39, 738, 751 and 785. That is a straightforward repair. The harder
repair is 783. The chapter treats it as a central Reichian demand; the canon's most sustained critic
demotes it twice by page number, the twenty-fifth-anniversary assessment routes the whole procedural
program through the 1965 sequel, *Goldberg* quotes the sequel and not the article, and the most
prominent modern reader calls the identification with procedural rules "ironic." The chapter should
stop treating 783 as a demand it inherits and start treating it as a demand the reception buried —
which is a better argument, and one Super has already half-made.

The Royce section can be written with more confidence than expected on the personal-office question
and less on membership without meeting. Every specialist reached fills all three offices with selves,
the standard reference account defines the community as a totality of minds, and the closest thing to
a non-individual interpreter is a *personality*, not a mechanism. But SEP states communication as a
necessary condition of a community's existing at all, and the entry that supplies the shared-events
constitution supplies it as an addition to that condition, not a substitute. Membership without
meeting has to be argued, not cited — most plausibly by showing that Roycean communication does not
require co-presence, with Kegley's 1894 externality passage as the lever, since it makes agreement
among socially communicating beings a *test* rather than a scene.

The genealogy row should be owned outright and framed as convergence. Reich's silence is decisive
because his article carries 235 footnotes and none of them names a philosopher of community; Royce's
silence about Simmel is decisive too; Simmel's silence about Royce is worthless as evidence, since he
names nobody. No one else assembles this triad, and the fields that would — informational capitalism,
relational data governance, infrastructure studies — reach the chapter's concepts through Polanyi,
Marx, Bowker and Star. Name the company the chapter declines, and the row becomes an asset.

The Simmel cluster is the consequential one, and its news is bad in an instructive way. The chapter
expected the German figure-of-the-third paradigm to be a foil: a third that arrives to a dyad, against
which a deferred third could be defined. Three lenses, refuting three separate claims, converged on
the opposite. Koschorke's programmatic essay puts the third *before* the pair, quoting Serres — "Es
gibt ein Drittes vor dem Zweiten; es gibt einen Dritten vor dem anderen" — and concludes that a theory
of the third cannot be derived from intersubjectivity at all. Lindemann's title is "*Statt* der
Dyade"; Bröckling's is "Gesellschaft beginnt mit Drei." The paradigm already admits things as thirds,
so "no broker" is covered by the existing name. What is left unnamed is narrower and, being narrower,
more defensible: a third with no identified pair. The chapter should make that its claim, concede
priority-over-the-dyad to the German literature, and stop opposing a foil that does not exist.
