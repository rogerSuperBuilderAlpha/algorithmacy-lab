# Disc worksheet — one afternoon with *Slacker* (1991)

Nobody on this project has watched the film. Five drafts have argued about it from a
subtitle transcript, and the editor's standing complaint is that the chapter never
describes a shot: "In 5,400 words it never says what the camera looks like doing it... A
1990 newspaper critic did the looking, and the 2026 chapter quotes him doing it." This
worksheet is what one sitting with the Criterion disc converts into evidence. Every
question below is answerable by watching, in one pass, without pausing to research. Every
binary has both branches already written as chapter-ready sentences — tick the one that
matches what is on screen and drop it into the draft. Nothing here asks what a shot
*means*. That stays the author's job, after the notepad is full.

Per `ASSUMPTIONS.md` A3: nothing in this worksheet may be answered from the transcript,
guessed, or hedged around. If a question can't be settled from what's on screen, write
"not settled" and move on — do not fill the blank with an inference.

## 4. Shot descriptions — the part that answers the editor

Both coders' agency coding agrees, transition by transition, on exactly six pairs where a
character deliberately produces the next encounter — an offer, an address, an object
handed over with a purpose. Every other agreement or disagreement in the census concerns
whether something merely *sounds* connected; these six are the only places both coders
independently found someone *doing* the handoff. They are the census's most solid ground
and the chapter's central empirical claim (`ASSUMPTIONS.md` A3a) — so they are also where
a shot description carries the most weight. Leave generous space; write in full sentences
or fragments, whichever is faster while the disc is running.

**Write what you saw, not what it means.** Not what the handoff represents, not what
argument it supports — where the camera is, whether it moves, whether the take holds or
cuts, what is heard. The interpretation is the next draft's job, not this afternoon's.
