Dear [editor],

Four questions about the *Slacker* chapter, in descending order of how much they affect the draft.

**1. The word ceiling.** I have the chapter at roughly 5,120 words of body text, against the
3,000–5,000 range in the call. I can bring it to 5,000, but the remaining words now sit in the argument
rather than in literature review or setup. If the ceiling is firm, say so and I will cut. If the range
allows a small overage, I would rather submit the current version.

**2. Title, editor of record, and deadline.** I have none of these on file and would like them for the
manuscript's front matter.

**3. Citation style.** The chapter is currently in Chicago notes-and-bibliography, with 76 notes. If
the collection uses author-date, or MLA, the conversion is mechanical and I will do it — I would just
rather do it once.

**4. A change of terminology from the accepted abstract.** The abstract you accepted distinguishes a
"hosting" third from an "engineering" third. The chapter now calls these a **setting** and a
**selector**, and defines each in a sentence on first use. The argument is unchanged; the vocabulary is
plainer, and it stopped the chapter from leaning on a figure of speech where it needed a definition. I
am flagging it rather than letting you discover it, and I am happy to revert if you would rather the
chapter matched the abstract you circulated.

One further note, in case it matters for the collection's apparatus: the chapter corrects several
small factual claims that circulate widely about the film — the Dobie Theatre run was eleven weeks, not
a year; Linklater's end credit reads "Should have stayed at bus station," with no definite article, as
the print and the AFI Catalog both have it, though the Criterion website inserts one.
