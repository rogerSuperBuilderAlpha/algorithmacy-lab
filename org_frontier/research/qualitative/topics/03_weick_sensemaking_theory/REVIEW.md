# Weick's Sensemaking and Its Contemporary Critique

Search method: academic-database queries (Scholar Gateway, Consensus) plus targeted web searches for DOIs and recent reviews; run 2026-06-23.

Weick recast organizing as an ongoing accomplishment, locating order in the moment-to-moment activity of making experience sensible (Weick et al., 2005). The mature formulation treats sensemaking as retrospective, social, enactive, identity-anchored, cue-driven, and governed by plausibility, with action and meaning bound in reciprocal cycles (Glynn & Watkiss, 2020). The canonical illustration of breakdown is the Mann Gulch fire, where the simultaneous disintegration of role structure and shared interpretation marks the collapse of sensemaking under a cosmological episode (Weick, 1993). Half-century retrospectives credit the program with initiating the process turn and the "gerundizing" of the field, reframing organizations as organizing (Glynn & Watkiss, 2020).

The most cited critical stocktakings map both reach and limits. Maitlis and Christianson (2014) consolidate the construct around triggers, processes, and outcomes while flagging emotion and embodiment as thin. Brown, Colville, and Pye (2015) read the perspective as a contested family of approaches centered on identity, language, and narrative. Sandberg and Tsoukas (2015) name five recurring shortfalls: neglect of prospective sensemaking, an over-focus on disruptive episodes at the expense of mundane activity, the ambiguous status of enactment, conflation of first- and second-order sensemaking, and inattention to embodiment.

Several lines extend or contest the model. A phenomenological reconstruction grounds sensemaking in practice worlds, distinguishing immanent, deliberate, and representational types and foregrounding the sense–action nexus, temporality, embodiment, and language (Sandberg & Tsoukas, 2020). A Heideggerian reading questions the retrospective premise, arguing that sense also arises through mood and exposure to unknown possibility (Holt & Cornelissen, 2014). Temporal critiques replace the backward-looking emphasis with narrative configurations that connect past, present, and future (Hernes & Obstfeld, 2022). A persistent objection concerns the muted treatment of power and context, which institutional and critical sensemaking accounts seek to repair (Weber & Glynn, 2006). Systematic reviews update the original Darwinian model toward co-evolutionary, affect-laden, power-directed dynamics (Cristofaro, 2022), and bibliometric mapping documents sustained growth and thematic diffusion across management research (Eckstein et al., 2024).

Recent synthesis consolidates the critique into four under-theorized axes—the meaning of sense, process and time, power, and the "missing body"—and proposes more fluid, embodied, distributed accounts as lines of flight (Kuiken, 2026). Open questions concern whether sensemaking can integrate prospective and ongoing modes, accommodate material and affective embodiment, and address asymmetries of power without losing the processual core that gave the program its distinctiveness.
