## Summary
This paper is a bibliometric review of sensemaking research across the entire business and management domain, motivated by the authors' claim that it is arguably the first such review and the first conducted after the COVID-19 public health emergency ended (4 May 2023). The authors search Web of Science and assemble a corpus of 2,838 articles spanning the first sensemaking article (Weick 1988) through the end of 2023, then apply performance analysis and science mapping (citation, co-citation, keyword co-occurrence) using the Bibliometrix R package and VOSviewer. They address three research questions: how sensemaking research has evolved, what topics are of current interest, and where the field is heading including whether the prevailing paradigm supports future directions. Findings show consistently rising academic interest, dominance of Academy of Management journals and the Gioia/Weick lineage, four keyword co-occurrence clusters (red: core sensemaking/communication/emotion; blue: CSR/sustainability/paradox; green: knowledge/innovation/technology/strategy; purple: COVID-19/crisis/resilience), and source co-citation clusters showing a persistent organizational-studies/management/strategy core with marketing and international business (IB) emerging more recently. The authors advance four propositions (sensemaking as a lens for marketing and IB in VUCA contexts, realism as a valuable paradigm for these fields, and sensemaking as a strategy lens for small business) and flag a constructionism-versus-realism paradigm tension as an open problem. The stated contribution is making the sprawling sensemaking literature more accessible and identifying gaps and trajectories for future research and practice.

## Key facts it relies on
- The corpus is 2,838 articles. An initial Web of Science search (title, abstract, keyword fields, January 2024) returned 9,587 records; after removing 2 records published in 2024, restricting to Business/Business Finance/Economics/Management WoS categories (leaving 2,851), and removing 13 non-English records, 2,838 remained.
- The field is dated from the first sensemaking article, Weick (1988); in 2023, 327 records were published, and the trendline shows consistently rising academic interest.
- The corpus is split into four time periods: T1 (before 2000, 77 studies), T2 (2000–2008, 349 studies), T3 (2009–2016, 888 studies), and T4 (2017–end of 2023, 1,524 studies).
- The most-cited paper in the corpus is Gioia et al. (2013) with 5,528 citations (citation counts retrieved from WoS on 2 January 2024); Gioia authored the most-cited articles in all time periods.
- Qualitative methodology dominates: a search of corpus titles and abstracts revealed approximately 100 quantitative and 492 qualitative studies; of the top-five most-cited articles per period, only Gioia and Thomas (1996) and Miron-Spektor et al. (2018) used quantitative methods.
- Out of 508 journals in the corpus, 934 articles (30% of the corpus) were published in the top 15 journals; Organization Studies published the most (121 articles, SJR Q1, ABDC A*, h-index 167). The Journal of Management Studies has published on sensemaking for 35 years.
- Methods/tools used: performance analysis and science mapping via Bibliometrix R package (Aria and Cuccurullo 2017) for analysis and VOSviewer for testing/validation; techniques include citation analysis, co-citation, keyword co-occurrence, PageRank, Walktrap clustering, and H-index.
- Keyword co-occurrence over 6,824 author keywords (Walktrap, repulsion 0.3, top 75 keywords displayed; keywords absent in 54 articles) yielded four clusters: red (core sensemaking, communication, emotion, knowledge management, learning, social), blue (CSR, sustainability, paradox), green (knowledge, innovation, technology, strategy), and purple (COVID-19, crisis, resilience).
- Source co-citation clustering (PageRank to select the 50 most influential sources, Walktrap with repulsion 1) across T1–T4 shows organizational studies and management persistently clustered, strategy always clustered with management, psychology prominent until T4, sociology strong in T1 then fading, and international business significant in the most recent period (T4).
- Sensemaking is framed via Weick's seven conditions abbreviated SIR COPE (Social, Identity, Retrospective, Cues, Ongoing, Plausibility, Enactment) and Weick et al.'s (2005) four-step process (noticing, bracketing, labeling, retention); the corpus includes 568 strategy-and-sensemaking studies.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors state several limitations: they used only Web of Science (other databases and grey literature may hold more work), included only English-language articles in four business categories, and may have missed records because they searched derivatives of "sensemaking" but not phrasings like "make sense" or "making sense."
- They acknowledge clustering requires subjective interpretation, and that the choice of algorithms and parameters (e.g., number of keywords displayed) can change the output.
- The paper itself frames an unresolved paradigm tension as central: sensemaking is traditionally constructionist/qualitative, while co-studied fields like marketing and IB favor realism/quantitative methods; the authors note the constructionism-versus-realism debate remains unresolved (citing Cruickshank 2012 that the two are incompatible vs. Williams 2016 that they can be reconciled), and warn paradigm mismatch may affect validity, reliability, and practitioner acceptance.
- The authors describe their discussion of ontology, epistemology, and methodology (paradigm) as deliberately brief, acknowledging the topic is broad with disparate definitions and that other interpretations exist.
- The four propositions and the "emerging" status of marketing/IB are interpretive claims the authors derive from cluster patterns, not tested hypotheses; they are offered explicitly to stimulate future research rather than as confirmed findings.
