## 1. Methodological foundations for interpretive organizational research
The program's rigor warrant rests on two anchors. Lincoln and Guba (1985) replaced positivist validity and reliability with credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability, established through thick description, member checking, triangulation, audit trails, and reflexivity. Gioia, Corley, and Hamilton (2013) translated comparable standards into a procedure for inductive concept development: first-order informant terms, second-order researcher themes, aggregate dimensions, and a visible data structure. Together they justify treating interview and ethnographic material as evidence about how coordination is experienced (Lincoln and Guba, 1985; Gioia et al., 2013).

## 2. Sensemaking as the interpretive engine
Weick (1995) defined sensemaking as retrospective, social, enactive meaning construction under equivocality. Weick (1993) showed sensemaking and role structure collapsing together at Mann Gulch, and Weick, Sutcliffe, and Obstfeld (2005) consolidated the action-to-meaning account. Half-century reviews and critical syntheses map the perspective's constituents and limits (Sandberg and Tsoukas, 2020; Brown, 2025), giving the program a current sense of contested ground in the theory it borrows.

## 3. Foundational studies of opaque algorithmic management
Rosenblat and Stark (2016) documented information and power asymmetries between Uber and its drivers, with surge pricing the clearest instance. Möhlmann and Zalmanson (2017) identified guessing, resisting, switching, and gaming as autonomy-recovery tactics. Kellogg, Valentine, and Christin (2020) systematized employer control into the "6 Rs" (restricting, recommending, recording, rating, replacing, rewarding) and framed work as contested terrain (Rosenblat and Stark, 2016; Möhlmann and Zalmanson, 2017; Kellogg et al., 2020).

## 4. Methodology for studying systems one cannot see
Christin (2020) confronts opacity directly, proposing algorithmic refraction (reconfigurations when software, people, and institutions interact), comparison, and triangulation. This is the closest methodological precedent for studying opaque, interested intermediaries and bears directly on the lab's object (Christin, 2020).

## 5. Worker experience: affect, folk theories, and the social construction of algorithms
Bucher and colleagues frame algorithmic paranoia as gig workers' affective response to abusive, illegible management (Bucher et al., 2024). Folk-theory and social-construction studies show workers building actionable theories of systems they cannot inspect, shaping how those systems function in practice (Vassilakopoulou et al., 2024; Anwar et al., 2025).

## 6. Resistance, gaming, and labour agency
Workers resist through tactical workarounds, out-of-app activity, and structured agency repertoires. Cameron (2024) analyzes gaming as production of antagonistic subjectivity; Sun et al. (2023) document bypassing constraints via out-of-app activities; Wood et al. (2025) offer a typology of gig-driver labour agency (Cameron, 2024; Sun et al., 2023; Wood et al., 2025).

## 7. Duality, attribution, and fairness
Recent agendas frame algorithmic management as simultaneously controlling and empowering (Meijerink and Bondarouk, 2025; Cai et al., 2025). Attribution accounts explain when the same system reads as fair or unfair to platform workers (Zhang et al., 2025), and perceived algorithmic fairness has been tied empirically to transparency and anthropomorphism (Shulner-Tal et al., 2024).

## 8. Governance and contestation
Worker sensemaking connects to governance through rights to contest automated decisions, examined under the GDPR's Article 22 framework (Lyons et al., 2021). This links individual interpretation of opaque systems to institutional remedies.

## 9. Disagreement-as-data and the intercoder reliability debate
The reflexive thematic analysis tradition treats coder divergence as interpretive signal and rejects intercoder reliability as an inappropriate positivist criterion, while codebook and coding-reliability variants retain agreement metrics for team consistency. For a program intending to mine informant and coder disagreement as evidence about differently-positioned readings of opaque systems, this methodological split is load-bearing.

## Gap
The two literatures rarely meet under one design. Few studies apply the full Gioia data structure or explicit Lincoln-Guba trustworthiness criteria to opaque algorithmic management while also using sensemaking as the analytic frame; the worker-experience corpus tends toward critical-theory or thematic framings without the audit-trail apparatus the program wants. Disagreement-as-data is asserted in the reflexive-TA methodological literature but rarely instrumented as a measured construct in algorithmic-management fieldwork. No located qualitative work connects exact integrated information (IIT/Phi) measures of an intermediary to the lived sensemaking of the coordinated parties, so the bridge from the lab's quantitative Phi construct to its qualitative evidence remains unbuilt and is the program's central open question. Coverage also skews to gig and platform transport and delivery; opaque interested intermediaries in finance, healthcare triage, and organizational HR/talent systems are comparatively thin in qualitative depth.
