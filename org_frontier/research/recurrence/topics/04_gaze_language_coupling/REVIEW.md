# Gaze and Linguistic Coupling

Cross-recurrence quantification analysis (CRQA) has become the dominant tool for measuring how the visual and linguistic behavior of two interlocutors co-evolves over time. The founding observation is that a listener's eye movements track a speaker's at a characteristic lag of roughly two seconds, and that tighter coupling predicts better discourse comprehension (Richardson & Dale, 2005). Extending the paradigm from playback to live, spontaneous dialogue, conversants' gaze couples across several seconds, and the coupling strengthens when partners share prior common ground (Richardson, Dale, & Kirkham, 2007). These results reframed joint attention as a continuously coordinated dynamical process indexed directly through co-visitation of gaze states.

The method underlying these claims treats two behavioral streams as a coupled system and quantifies how often, and at what lag, they revisit shared states. CRQA generalizes lag-sequential analysis to nominal streams, allowing categorical fixation sequences and coded behaviors to be analyzed with the same machinery (Warlaumont & Richardson, 2011). Open implementations and tutorials standardized the workflow for categorical and continuous data alike (Coco & Dale, 2014; Wallot & Leonardi, 2018), and recurrence measures were adapted specifically to the temporal structure of fixation sequences (Anderson, Bischof, Laidlaw, Risko, & Kingstone, 2013).

Recent work pushes beyond gaze alone toward genuinely multimodal coupling. Eye gaze synchronizes with speech at turn-relevant points, with gaze aversion marking turn-holding and shared gaze emerging at turn transitions (Brône et al., 2017). Studies of free face-to-face collaboration show gaze, pointing gestures, and verbal reference coupling both within and between partners, with speaker gaze leading listener gaze and multimodal references producing more sustained attentional coupling (Haraped, Huber, Bischof, & Kingstone, 2024; Haraped et al., 2025). Asymmetric leader-follower structure tracks conversational turn-taking regardless of relative expertise (Haraped et al., 2024).

On the linguistic side, categorical CRQA quantifies alignment of utterance streams in dyads, capturing reciprocation that frequency counts miss (Duong, Davis, Bachman, Votruba-Drzal, & Libertus, 2024). Prosodic coupling has been examined with a family of methods including CRQA, though a systematic comparison shows that estimates of acoustic-prosodic entrainment depend heavily on the chosen method (Kruyt, de Jong, D'Ausilio, & Beňuš, 2023), and entrainment dissociates across global and local timescales and across acoustic features (Xia, Hirschberg, & Levitan, 2023). Most recently, CRQA has been applied to inter-brain coupling during naturalistic negotiation, where balanced leading and lagging predicted collaborative success that conventional synchrony measures did not detect (Goldstein, Burns, Peck, Dale, & Lieberman, 2026).
