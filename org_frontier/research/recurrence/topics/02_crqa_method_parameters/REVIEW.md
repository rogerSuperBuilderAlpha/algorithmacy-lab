# CRQA Method and Parameter Selection

Cross-recurrence quantification analysis (CRQA) generalizes recurrence quantification to two time series: each is embedded into a reconstructed phase space via time-delay coordinates, the two trajectories are compared point-by-point under a distance norm, and matches falling within a threshold radius populate a cross-recurrence plot whose line structures yield measures such as recurrence rate, determinism, and maximal diagonal length (Marwan, Romano, Thiel, & Kurths, 2007). The diagonal of the plot, displaced as a lag, gives a diagonal cross-recurrence profile that captures time-lagged coupling and leader-follower asymmetry (Wallot & Leonardi, 2018). Practical implementation in the cognitive and behavioral sciences was consolidated by the crqa R package, which handles both categorical and continuous streams (Coco & Dale, 2014), with multidimensional extensions for multivariate signals (Wallot, 2018).

Reproducibility hinges on four interacting choices. The embedding dimension is conventionally set by false nearest neighbors and the delay by the first minimum of average mutual information (Zbilut & Webber, 1992; Coco & Dale, 2014); a broad survey of delay-selection techniques, including a persistent-homology spectrum of dynamically significant lags, documents how sensitive downstream analysis is to lag choice (Tan, Algar, Corrêa, Small, Stemler, & Walker, 2023). When the two series demand different parameters, taking the larger delay and dimension is generally preferred, since over-embedding harms less than under-embedding (Wallot & Leonardi, 2018).

Threshold selection remains the least settled choice. Fixed-radius, fixed-recurrence-rate, and fixed-amount-of-nearest-neighbors schemes each impose different biases, and signal-detection power offers one principled criterion (Schinkel, Dimigen, & Marwan, 2008). Because the distribution of pairwise state-space distances shifts with embedding dimension, selecting the threshold as a fixed quantile of that distribution stabilizes recurrence measures across dimensions (Kraemer, Donner, Heitzig, & Marwan, 2018). Variable thresholds keyed to local sampling distance further mitigate the tangential-motion artifact in non-stationary data (Delage & Nakata, 2023).

Normalization is a precondition for valid continuous-data CRQA: z-scoring or unit-interval rescaling ensures measures reflect sequential similarity and not magnitude offsets, and for categorical series the radius is set near zero so only identical categories recur (Coco & Dale, 2014). A recurring caution across this literature concerns artifactual structure from inappropriate embedding, thresholds tuned to yield desired outcomes, and unreported preprocessing (Marwan, 2011). Recent work extends CRQA to series of unequal length without resampling, broadening applicability while reinforcing that parameter provenance must be reported for results to be reproducible (Drews et al., 2024).
