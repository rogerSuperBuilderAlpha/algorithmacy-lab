# Causality Method Benchmarks and Software

Data-driven causal inference for time series rests on four method families: Granger causality and its vector-autoregressive variants (Granger, 1969), transfer entropy as an information-theoretic generalization (Schreiber, 2000), state-space cross-mapping descending from convergent cross mapping (Sugihara et al., 2012), and recurrence- or distance-based state-space indices. For Gaussian variables Granger causality and transfer entropy are formally equivalent (Barnett et al., 2009), which anchors comparisons across the linear and nonlinear families.

Comparative benchmarking forms the empirical core of this topic. A six-method study on bivariate series found standard and extended Granger tests succeed only for autoregressive coupling, while cross-mapping, conditional mutual information, and predictability-improvement measures handle chaotic and noisy systems more reliably (Krakovská et al., 2018). A ten-index review across four simulated systems reported strong inter-method agreement but uneven robustness to scaling, missing data, and noise, recommending transfer entropy and nonlinear Granger causality, and releasing open Python code (Edinburgh et al., 2021). Network-scale benchmarks on coupled neuronal models showed convergent cross mapping attaining the highest precision and transfer entropy higher recall, with both advantages growing with network size (Cui et al., 2020). A unifying information-theoretic formulation links transfer entropy and cross mapping, yielding a unified test (UIC) with lower data requirements than transfer entropy and fewer false positives than convergent cross mapping (Osada et al., 2023). Recent work extends benchmarking to high-dimensional regimes through the Information Imbalance of distance ranks, a recurrence-style approach reported to outperform model-free competitors on coupled chaotic systems (Del Tatto et al., 2024). A 2024 climate study contrasted conditional-independence and information-flow methods, illustrating how method choice shifts which drivers are identified (Docquier et al., 2024). A 2026 review synthesizes Granger causality, transfer entropy, and convergent cross mapping for ecological series and clarifies their interrelations and limitations (Suzuki et al., 2026).

Tooling implements these methods across R and Python. JIDT supplies Java (callable from Python, R, MATLAB) estimators of transfer entropy and related measures (Lizier, 2014); IDTxl extends this to multivariate transfer entropy network inference with hierarchical significance control (Wollstadt et al., 2019). The empirical-dynamic-modeling stack rEDM/pyEDM provides simplex projection, S-map, and convergent cross mapping (Park et al., 2024), and rUIC implements the unified test (Osada et al., 2023). NlinTS packages Granger tests, a neural-network extension, and transfer entropy in R (Hmamouche, 2020), while VLTimeCausality relaxes fixed-lag assumptions for variable-lag Granger causality and transfer entropy (Amornbunchornvej et al., 2021).
