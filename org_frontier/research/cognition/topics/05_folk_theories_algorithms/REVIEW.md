# Folk Theories of Algorithms

Folk theories are the intuitive, causal models lay users assemble to explain how opaque feed and recommender systems decide what they see. The foundational account emerged when users encountered a curated Facebook News Feed and articulated ten distinct theories of its operation, several unanticipated by designers (Eslami et al., 2016). Subsequent work formalized how these theories form, drawing on diverse evidentiary sources and proving more multifaceted and malleable than first assumed, with direct consequences for self-presentation (DeVito et al., 2018). Parallel survey-based studies catalogued the recurring structural theories users hold: economic orientation, popularity, personal interaction, and categorization theories among German internet users (Dogruel, 2021), and the "rational assistant," "unwanted observer," "transparent platform," and "corporate black box" metaphors elicited from large samples (French & Hancock, 2017).

A distinct strand foregrounds affect. Open-ended survey responses from a representative Norwegian sample yielded five theories—algorithms as confining, practical, reductive, intangible, and exploitative—and identified digital irritation as a central response with latent political potential, qualifying the digital-resignation thesis (Ytre-Arne & Moe, 2021). Cross-cultural comparison of US and German users found broadly shared intuitions of automatic functioning alongside more cognitive, less agentic associations among US respondents (Dreston et al., 2025).

Identity and marginalization sharpen the picture. TikTok users theorize a For You Page that suppresses marginalized social identities, captured in the Identity Strainer Theory and the notion of algorithmic privilege (Karizat et al., 2021). Marginalized users collaboratively develop and test theories of shadowbanning, a practice platforms typically deny (Delmonaco et al., 2024). Folk theorization also functions as adaptive sense-making: because platforms change, users continually re-theorize, a process reframed as a route to algorithmic literacy (DeVito, 2021). Stories shared among users sustain these theories within broader algorithmic imaginaries (Schellewald, 2022), and cross-cultural study of Douyin shows theory-driven resistance that oscillates and often stays within sanctioned affordances (Lin, 2025).

Recent design-oriented work extends the program. Workshops with users diagnosed with mental illness surface cause-and-effect folk theories reflecting a breakdown termed entanglement, motivating renewed user control (Milton et al., 2026). A pre-registered experiment specifies when even complex, LLM-based ranking algorithms yield accurate predictive mental models—when underlying concepts are cognitively available, compact, and aligned with the user's own execution (Popowski et al., 2026), suggesting folk theories can be accurate under identifiable conditions and not merely defensive heuristics.
