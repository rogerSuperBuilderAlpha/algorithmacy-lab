# Predictive Processing and Free Energy

Predictive processing casts the brain as a hierarchical generative model that predicts incoming signals and propagates precision-weighted prediction errors upward, with perception equated to inverting that model to explain its sensorium (Friston & Kiebel, 2009; Clark, 2013). The free energy principle supplies the normative objective: agents minimize variational free energy, a bound jointly scoring model accuracy and complexity, and select policies that minimize expected free energy over future outcomes (Da Costa et al., 2020; Sprevak & Smith, 2023). Because higher levels act as empirical priors on lower ones, the same scheme that infers physical causes extends to inferring latent, unobservable causes—including other minds.

Applied to hidden third parties, the framework treats another agent as a structured cause to be inferred. Mentalizing becomes the inversion of a deep generative model in which exteroceptive, proprioceptive, and interoceptive predictions about another's behavior are explained by attributed beliefs, goals, and intentions (Ondobaka et al., 2017). Hierarchical depth matters: temporally deep models let an agent represent another's slowly changing dispositions as priors that contextualize fast-changing observed actions, so that an opaque counterpart's policy is recovered from behavior alone. Pöppel et al. (2022) implement this as "belief resonance," letting one agent's inferred mental states bias another's predictive beliefs about its own goals, producing coordination without explicit communication. Pitliya et al. (2025) build theory of mind directly into active inference, maintaining distinct representations of self and other and using recursive, tree-based planning to infer a partner's beliefs from observable behavior—without task-specific shared models or messaging.

Several recent strands push toward genuinely third-party and collective settings. Friston et al. (2024) show that belief-sharing among agents who hold a common world model yields "federated inference," with communication and even language emerging from free energy minimization as agents resolve uncertainty about latent states each observes only partially. Albarracin et al. (2024) formalize shared goals as shared "protentions," anticipatory structures linking individual generative models into group intentionality. Waade et al. (2025) examine when a collective itself constitutes a group-level agent with its own generative model, mapping the non-trivial relation between individual and emergent models. Pezzulo et al. (2024) caution that such generative models are learned to act on and engage the world, so representations of others may be pragmatic and action-oriented. Friston et al. (2025) extend the account to intentional, goal-directed behavior via inductive inference, relevant to modeling agents that pursue interested ends. Open questions concern recursive depth, the precision dynamics governing trust in an opaque informant, and whether shared-model assumptions hold when third parties are strategically interested.
