## Summary
The paper asks how users with mental illness interpret their interactions with algorithmically curated social media and how that design affects their mental well-being. The authors ran seven design workshops with 21 participants who self-identified as having depression, anxiety, or PTSD, using two activities: an "always/sometimes/never" exploration-and-discovery task and a prototyping task in which participants designed an ideal feed to support their well-being. They analyzed notes and artifacts with inductive thematic analysis. They find participants develop cause-and-effect "folk theories" (emotional mismatch, algorithmic leakage, corporate intentions, engagement abuse) about their feeds, and they conceptualize a recurring breakdown as "entanglement": a disconnect between users' actions and platform outcomes on an emotional level. Entanglement extends Norman's action cycle with six error types — three in the gulf of execution (Hiding, Guessing, Flattening) and three in the gulf of evaluation (Dangling, Overloading, Disempowering). Participants' design solutions clustered into three themes — contextualizing engagement, consumption control, and reclaiming control through explicit user input — leading the authors to recommend "choose your own adventure" interventions, separate/modular feeds, and shifting algorithmic optimization from accuracy to contextual/emotional relevance.

## Key facts it relies on
- Method: seven design workshops (four in person, three remote via Zoom) with 21 participants; each workshop lasted about two hours with small groups of 2 to 4 participants; conducted March to July 2024 until theoretical saturation.
- Participants: aged 18 to 55 (M=25.5), primarily white (N=17) and female (N=13); recruited via X/Twitter, Instagram, Reddit and institutional mailing lists; eligibility required being 18+, diagnosed or self-diagnosed with depression, anxiety, or PTSD, and use of algorithmically curated platforms; received a $40 gift card; screened with a modified UBACC consent-capacity assessment (scored above 15). Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok were each mentioned by over half the pool; most used a platform at least daily, only two weekly or less.
- "Entanglement" is defined as participants' inability to make clear connections between the actions they take on a platform and the resulting system responses they observe — a disconnect on an emotional level. It extends Norman's action cycle (from The Design of Everyday Things), which has four components (user, technology/artifact, execution, evaluation) and the gulfs of execution and evaluation.
- Six entanglement error types: gulf of execution — Hiding (no feature to accomplish a goal), Guessing (multiple unclear features for a goal), Flattening (one feature serves multiple distinct goals); gulf of evaluation — Dangling (cannot perceive any change from an interaction), Overloading (one feature creates multiple/conflicting changes), Disempowering (changes occur without any corresponding control to start/stop them).
- Folk theories identified: Theory of Emotional Mismatch (algorithms ignore "emotional weight," causing "mood swings"), Theory of Algorithmic Leakage (feeds as unintentional disclosure agents / "mirrors" that risk passive mental-health disclosure), and Theories of Algorithmic Exploitation comprising the Theory of Corporate Intentions and the Theory of Engagement Abuse.
- Folk theories framing follows DeVito et al. and Eslami et al.; folk theories are defined as "intuitive, informal theories that individuals develop to explain the outcomes, effects, or consequences of technological systems."
- Three participant design themes: (1) contextualizing engagement (separating interest, amplification, and protection — e.g., separate "show more/less" from "like," softer personal "flagging"/auto-blur); (2) consumption control (engagement sliders, "incognito" engagement, intentional/Pinterest-style block layouts, removing infinite scroll/autoplay, personalized break interventions); (3) reclaiming control through explicit input (separate "containers"/"groupings"/multiple feeds, user-curated "tags" akin to Tumblr/AO3).
- Concrete example quotes: feeds going "from scientific and gardening videos to bare-feet anti-vaccine content" (P12); having to "block them like seven times" yet content "still shows me" (P12); "everyone knows that you get recommendations based on what you have already seen" (P04); platforms "can't tell the difference between watching something and being satisfied or enraged by it" (P01).
- Two design implications: (1) restoring intentional control via "choose your own adventure" interventions and separate/modular feeds (analogized to browser "tabs," referencing BlueSky user-curated feeds); (2) reimagining curation from accuracy to emotional/contextual relevance via an ensemble of specialized models (e.g., separate models for "BookTok" vs. "MentalHealthTok") and user-aware recommendations that model mental state.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors state the main limitation is the participant pool: recruitment leaned toward university campuses, so participants skew college-aged with at least some college education, and skew white and female — continuing a known difficulty recruiting men and racial/ethnic minorities into mental-health studies.
- By design (privacy and standpoint-theory reasons), the authors did not record which specific conditions or co-morbid diagnoses participants had and relied on self-identification rather than formal assessment; they note these participants do not represent the entirety of the mental-health community.
- Findings are qualitative (notes and artifacts, paraphrased rather than recorded verbatim audio) from a single set of workshops; entanglement is a proposed interpretive framework developed in a secondary analysis phase, not a tested or quantitatively validated construct.
- The authors acknowledge some Section 4.1 findings echo prior work (e.g., Milton et al.'s "runaway trains"/"dopamine slot machines," Simpson et al.'s feed "domestication," DeVito et al.'s folk theories); the novel contribution is the entanglement synthesis and the extension of Norman's action cycle.
- The authors flag open implementation challenges: separate feeds and mental-state-aware curation require advances in user modeling and contextual inference, and must balance user control against added cognitive burden — requiring joint HCI–psychology research.
