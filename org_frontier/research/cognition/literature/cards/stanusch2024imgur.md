## Summary
This paper asks what imaginaries users hold about algorithmic recommendation systems, and how memes perform and circulate those imaginaries. Working inductively, the author scrapes a meme "collection" from Imgur (the top-ranked meme-hosting site) using the ambient hashtag #algorithm, then applies a qualitative coding scheme adapted from Shifman's content/stance/form distinction to extrapolate "algorithmic imaginaries" (Bucher) from the memes. From a final set of 15 original memes, the analysis localizes four imaginaries: Algorithmic Recommendation Systems vs Nostalgic Antagonism; Algorithms as Dysfunctional and Flawed; Algorithms as Making Magic; and Humanity vs AI. The author finds that memes about algorithms appear primarily around users' encounters with algorithmic outputs that are glitches, failures, or "happy accidents," and that such glitch moments make otherwise invisible algorithmic logics perceptible. Finally, the paper theorizes "memeing" as a "memetic issue space" (drawing on Castells's placeless space and Rogers's online issue space) where algorithmic counter-imaginaries can be ignited as a form of semiotic resistance to "surveillance realism" and datafication.

## Key facts it relies on
- Data were scraped from Imgur in the first week of May 2023, using the platform's "search" option for the #algorithm hashtag on a research browser with no history or cookies to minimize personalization; no account was created.
- The collection was compiled from three sub-samples: one query ordered by Imgur's default "highest scoring / all time" (circa 20 above-the-fold results, no scrolling), plus two queries over a "one year" timeframe ordered by highest upvotes and highest views respectively.
- A total of 55 memes were collected; after cleaning (removing non-memetic or non-algorithm content and cross-collection duplicates), the analysis focused on 15 original memes, with a total of 861,873 views and 14,380 upvotes.
- The codebook (inspired by Shifman 2013/2014) coded each meme on five categories: content, stance, form, platform, and imaginary (the last a "meta" label assigned inductively).
- The four imaginaries extrapolated are: Algorithmic Recommendation Systems vs Nostalgic Antagonism, Algorithms as Dysfunctional and Flawed, Algorithms as Making Magic, and Humanity vs AI.
- Memes referenced multiple platforms beyond Imgur (Facebook, Twitter/X, YouTube, Reddit) and used formats such as the "Lisa Simpson's Presentation" image macro (in circulation since 2018) and Twitter screenshots.
- The theoretical scaffolding includes sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff & Kim, 2013, 2015), algorithmic imaginaries (Bucher, 2017), and the glitch as a moment that reveals otherwise invisible algorithmic logics (Bucher, 2017, 2018; Shane, 2023, on "algorithmic accidents").
- The three research questions concern (RQ1) what imaginaries emerge from the meme collections, (RQ2) how memes perform the role of an "algorithmic imaginary," and (RQ3) what notions of algorithms and dataveillance are normalized through them.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The author explicitly acknowledges interpretive limits: memes are ambiguous, mocking, and polysemic (Bristow, 2019), so any interpretation must "reconcile with the ultimate incompleteness of the performed interpretation."
- The paper notes that data "cleaning" of social-media data is itself situated decision-making (boyd & Crawford, 2012), and that the composition of a meme collection is shaped by methodological design choices (Rogers & Giorgi, 2024) — i.e., results are sensitive to how the collection is built.
- Scope is narrow and qualitative: the final corpus is only 15 memes from a single platform (Imgur) selected via one ambient hashtag and engagement-based ordering, so it captures dominant/most-engaged vernaculars rather than a representative sample; stance can be ambiguous (e.g., Figure 4 may target political rather than algorithmic discourse).
- The author flags an ethical/representational concern within the data: the face-recognition-failure meme (Figure 9) depicts a Black person — among the groups most harmed and biased against by algorithmic systems (Benjamin, 2019) — and may, perhaps unintentionally, reinforce that marginalization even while mocking the algorithm.
- The distinction between imaginaries and adjacent concepts (discourses, narratives, myths, ideology) is noted as outside the paper's scope, with references deferred to Jasanoff & Kim (2015), Mager & Katzenbach (2021), Natale & Ballatore (2020), and Strauss (2006).
