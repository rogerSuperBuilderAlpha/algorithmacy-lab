## Summary
The paper asks how distributed intelligence ("federated inference") can emerge when multiple agents share a common world and a common generative model of it, communicating their posterior beliefs to one another. Working entirely under the free energy principle and active inference over discrete-state (POMDP) generative models, the authors cast inference, learning, and structure selection as the minimisation of variational, expected, and reduced free energy, respectively. Using a running illustrative scenario of three back-to-back "sentinel" agents (like birds/sisters) surveying a scene with a nearby subject (a potential predator/person), they run three numerical studies: (1) belief-sharing resolves uncertainty about a partially observed world by accumulating evidence through "many pairs of eyes"; (2) the requisite language—a likelihood mapping from beliefs to overt expression—can be acquired by a naive agent (a child) via active learning (Dirichlet-count accumulation) and transmitted across generations; and (3) a precise, shared likelihood mapping emerges de novo in an ensemble of naive agents through active structure selection using Bayesian model reduction. The headline claim is that communication/language is an emergent property of free energy minimisation among agents who broadcast beliefs about a shared world, and that this distributed self-evidencing can be read variously as cultural niche construction, federated learning, or the emergence of complexity in self-organising ensembles. Across the simulations, variational free energy over latent states declines while structural complexity of the likelihood parameters increases as knowledge accumulates.

## Key facts it relies on
- The framework rests on three nested free-energy-minimising processes: minimising variational free energy = inference over states/parameters (maximising model evidence / self-evidencing); minimising expected free energy = active inference/planning and active learning; minimising via Bayesian model reduction = active selection / structure learning (Friston et al., 2018).
- Expected free energy decomposes into expected information gain (epistemic value) plus expected cost/value, equivalently risk plus ambiguity; selecting parameter/structure updates that minimise expected free energy is shown to be equivalent to maximising the mutual information of the likelihood mapping, under constraints.
- The generative model is a POMDP specified by tensors: A (likelihood, states→outcome modalities), B (state transitions under paths), C (priors over paths), D (initial states), E (initial path probabilities); likelihood tensors are parameterised by Dirichlet concentration counts (a).
- Scenario uses three agents sitting back-to-back, each with a field of view covering about a third of the horizon; visual input has four modalities (one central foveal/parvocellular friend-vs-foe classifier with four outcome levels, plus central and peripheral magnocellular contrast modalities with three levels: close, near, none), four hidden-state factors (location with 9 locations, proximity near/far, pose friend/foe, gaze), and eight outcome modalities total; only the gaze factor is controllable.
- Belief sharing differs formally from ordinary Bayesian updating: it assimilates other agents' posteriors (inheriting both likelihood and priors), with identity likelihood mappings for the communication channel; agents are individuated by Markov blankets, so they exchange messages through a shared (auditory "word"/"call") channel rather than passing messages directly.
- Study 1 (Fig. 5/6): with communication the third agent infers the subject's location quickly (committing on the first time step from the first agent's broadcast), whereas without communication it forms precise beliefs only by time step five, over an eight-timestep clockwise traverse; communicating agents attain lower free energy and show simulated electrophysiology (firing rates, local field potentials, phasic dopaminergic responses).
- Study 2 (Fig. 7/8): a naive "child" with all prior Dirichlet counts set to one learns a structured (identity) auditory likelihood mapping after 32 episodes (each 16 timesteps, each step ~250 ms = 1/4 s); KL divergence to the parent's mapping falls and mutual information rises; after four generations of replacement, learned mappings are virtually indistinguishable from the originals (cultural transmission via experience-dependent plasticity, no genetic change).
- A hyperprior on the effective number of retained Dirichlet counts caps total counts at η, implementing Bayesian-optimal forgetting in a volatile environment; in the simulations η = 32, so the last 32 experiences predominate and agents are "young and impressionable."
- Study 3 (Fig. 9/10): three language-naive agents (Dirichlet counts set to one plus an unsigned random Gaussian variate) exposed to 512 episodes endogenously converge on a sparse, shared (but arbitrary, distinct from the earlier language) likelihood mapping; precise mappings emerge by ~128 exposures, selected connections reach high Dirichlet counts (~80), mutual information saturates at ~2 nats (about three bits).
- Supervised structure learning (Fig. 11): a visually naive agent that can hear its "supervisors" renders its visual likelihood inference indistinguishable from supervisors by ~64 exposures, whereas without supervision learning is slow and fails to disentangle causes even after 512 exposures.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors stress that these are synthetic/numerical demonstrations: "communication appears to be an emergent property" is supported by simulations, and they note the whole experiment spans only about two minutes of simulated time (they suggest it could be read as learning a new videogame), so scope/scale conditions are limited.
- Communication is deliberately simplified: a single "word"/call is shared in a separate auditory modality and beliefs (sufficient statistics) are exchanged directly rather than linearised into word sequences; a more elaborate, hierarchically structured linguistic model is left to future work, and only some beliefs (e.g., subject location) are shared, not others (e.g., where each agent is looking).
- The approach requires a strong precondition of "common ground": an isomorphism/shared frame of reference and conserved belief structures across agents (here allocentric); the authors and a reviewer flag that mere similarity or isomorphism may be insufficient when the communication channel itself distorts outcomes (e.g., "communicating under water"), so an intact shared likelihood channel is an additional condition for belief sharing.
- The paper acknowledges teleological readings (free energy / expected free energy as objective functions for Bayes-optimal inference) and that the emergence of complexity as free energy is minimised "does not have a clear teleology"; it also notes that multi-agent active inference can produce maladaptive group outcomes (local minima in a joint free energy landscape, amplification of noisy/irrelevant information), citing prior collective-intelligence work.
