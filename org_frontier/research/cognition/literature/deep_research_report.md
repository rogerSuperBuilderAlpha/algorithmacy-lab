## 1. Foundations: where cognition lives
Symbolic computationalism set the original terms, casting intelligence as search over symbol structures (Newell and Simon, 1976). Embodied and enactive cognition challenged the representation-first picture, treating cognition as the enactment of a world through situated activity (Varela, Thompson, and Rosch, 1991). The extended-mind thesis argued that external resources can be constitutive parts of a cognitive system when coupled tightly enough (Clark and Chalmers, 1998). For a lab studying coordination through external algorithmic intermediaries, this lineage is directly load-bearing: an opaque third party is, in extended-mind terms, a candidate cognitive partner whose internal states are inaccessible.

## 2. Predictive processing as a coordination mechanism
Predictive processing frames the brain as a hierarchical generative model minimizing prediction error (Clark, 2013), formalized through the free-energy principle (Friston, 2010), with a philosophical stock-taking of open problems (Hohwy, 2020). This supplies a concrete account of what a person does when modeling an interested third party: build a generative model of the system, predict its outputs, and update on error, exactly the dynamic that folk-theory and algorithm-awareness studies observe empirically.

## 3. Theory of mind and shared intentionality
Mental-state attribution was framed as theory of mind (Premack and Woodruff, 1978), and human coordination was grounded in shared intentionality, the jointly held goals and motivation to share psychological states (Tomasello et al., 2005). These are the baseline against which machine "minds" are now measured.

## 4. The algorithmic imaginary and folk theories
Bucher (2017) introduced the algorithmic imaginary, showing that users' affective pictures of algorithms reshape behavior and the system itself. Early awareness work found most users oblivious to feed curation, reacting strongly on discovery (Eslami et al., 2015). Folk-theory studies catalogue the lay models built: feed-operation theories (DeVito, 2017) and irritation-driven theories of algorithms as confining, reductive, intangible, and exploitative (Ytre-Arne and Moe, 2021). Seaver (2018) reframes algorithms as enacted cultural practices rather than fixed mechanisms. Recent work uses vignettes and scenarios to elicit otherwise inarticulate models (Das et al., 2024), and extends folk theorizing to data labor (Fink and Goldkind, 2025).

## 5. Algorithm awareness, literacy, and the divide
Awareness is unevenly distributed and can entrench a new digital divide (Gran, Booth, and Bucher, 2021). Literacy develops experientially through expectancy violations and personalization cues (Swart, 2021), a finding synthesized across 169 studies into an experiential-learning account (Gagrčin, Naab, and Grub, 2024) and connected to library and information practice (Ridley and Pawlick-Potts, 2022). Transparency interventions can backfire, producing an illusion of understanding from the mere prospect of explanation (Ostinelli, Bonezzi, and Lisjak, 2024).

## 6. Strategic modeling of an interested system
Creators treat the algorithm as a strategic, interested adversary, inferring rules from outcomes and "playing the visibility game" (Cotter, 2019). This is the closest existing analogue to the lab's framing of coordination through an opaque, interested third party, though it is studied qualitatively, without a formal model.

## 7. Machines as communicators (CASA, HMC)
People apply social rules to computers automatically (Nass, Steuer, and Tauber, 1994; Reeves and Nass, 1996). Human-machine communication formalizes the machine as a communicative subject and studies the ontological boundaries people draw between humans and computers (Guzman, 2020; Guzman and Lewis, 2020).

## 8. AI-mediated communication
AI-MC names communication where an agent modifies, augments, or generates messages on a communicator's behalf (Hancock, Naaman, and Levy, 2020). Belief that text is AI-written lowers trust (Jakesch et al., 2019); suggested replies shift language and interpersonal perception (Mieczkowski et al., 2021); AI participation measurably alters relationships and cooperation (Hohenstein et al., 2023).

## 9. Machine theory of mind and the analytic instrument
LLMs now pass several theory-of-mind tasks while failing others such as faux-pas detection (Strachan et al., 2024), sharpening the question of how people model machine minds. Integrated information theory and its Phi measure provide the lab's quantitative criterion for when components form an integrated whole (Tononi et al., 2016).

## Gap
The corpus has two halves that do not yet meet. Cognitive-science theory (predictive processing, extended mind, shared intentionality) explains the mechanism of modeling a hidden partner, while communication and platform-studies work (algorithmic imaginary, folk theories, AI-MC) documents the phenomenon empirically, but no located source uses an exact integrated-information (Phi) measure to characterize coordination through an opaque interested third party. IIT is represented only as a consciousness theory (Tononi et al., 2016), not as a coordination or communication metric; its critical literature (the 2023 pseudoscience controversy) was found in commentary but not captured as a peer-reviewed citation here. Missing: (a) formal treatments connecting Phi or integrated information to multi-agent coordination and communication channels; (b) experimental work modeling the "interestedness" (incentive opacity) of a third party rather than only its mechanism opacity; (c) cross-cultural and longitudinal studies of how imaginaries of interested intermediaries form and stabilize. These define the program's open frontier.
