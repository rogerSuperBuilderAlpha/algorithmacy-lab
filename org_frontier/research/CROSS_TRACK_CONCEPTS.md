# Ten concepts the six research tracks share

The watch runs six programs — computational, recurrence, cognition, field, qualitative, survey — that look
unrelated on their surfaces: exact integrated information, cross-recurrence analysis, theories of mind,
organizational ethnography, qualitative methods, and psychometrics. The per-paper reference cards (217 cards
over the open-access full texts, under `*/literature/cards/`) make the connective tissue visible. Reading
the "Key topics covered" and full text across the cards surfaces ten concepts that recur across the tracks.

The counts below are card matches per track, computed over the open-access subset that has cards (217 of the
694 references). Track breadth, not raw count, is the ordering: a concept in five tracks ranks above a larger
concept concentrated in one.

Two concepts sit just below the ten and are worth naming: algorithmic literacy, awareness, and competence
(survey 12, cognition 3, qualitative 1), and theory of mind and mental models of the system (cognition 6,
qualitative 2, recurrence 1). Predictive processing and active inference span two tracks (cognition 7,
computational 2).

## The shape of it

The ten fall into three clusters that track the lab's thesis, a coordination read two ways and the people
inside it.

**The measure.** Information theory, directed causality, coordination dynamics, and integrated information
are the formal cluster, concentrated in computational and recurrence. These are the tracks that quantify
whether and how a coordination holds together.

**The human side.** Trust, transparency, algorithmic management, and mediated communication concentrate in
cognition, field, and qualitative, with algorithmic literacy and theory of mind close behind. These are the
tracks that study the person coordinating through an opaque, interested system.

**The shared spine.** Measurement validity and reproducibility are the only concepts in all six tracks. Every
program, formal or empirical, answers to the same standards of evidence.

## Scope

The counts are over the 217 open-access cards, 31% of the 694 references, and open-access coverage skews
toward computational and recurrence (62% and 55% acquired) over the social-science tracks (21–31%). The
formal-measure concepts are therefore over-represented relative to the full bibliographies, and the human-side
concepts are undercounted. The matches use keyword families over card text, so they catch presence, not
depth; a concept named once in a card weighs the same as one carried throughout. The ranking is a map of what
the tracks hold in common, read off the cards on disk, and re-derivable from them.
