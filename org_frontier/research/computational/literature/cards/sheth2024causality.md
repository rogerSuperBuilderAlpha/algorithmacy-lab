## Summary
This is a survey of time-series and spatiotemporal causality methods and their applications in Earth science, aimed at both the data science community (data-driven causal study) and the Earth science community (AI-driven causal analysis of dynamic and thermodynamic processes). It separates the field into two tasks: causal discovery (causal structure learning, recovering the causal graph / data-generating process) and causal inference (estimating causal effects). The paper lays out foundational causal assumptions (causal sufficiency, Causal Markov Condition, faithfulness, acyclicity, data assumptions), evaluation metrics (TPR, FPR, SHD, SID, AOC for discovery; RMSE, PEHE for inference), and three classes of discovery approach (constraint-based, score-based, functional/SCM-based). It then reviews specific methods — Granger Causality, the PCMCI family, LiNGAM variants, TiMINo, Tidybench, NAVAR, TCDF, Mapped-PCMCI, STCD, etc. for discovery; and do-calculus, the potential-outcome/Rubin framework, MSMs, CCM, instrumental variables, Causal-ARIMA, DiD, ITS, Causal Impact, RDD, and spatiotemporal methods (STCINet, SITS) for inference — documenting strengths, assumptions, and limitations. It catalogs Earth science applications (teleconnections, ENSO/NAO, Arctic sea ice, wildfire, evapotranspiration, air pollution, climate-change attribution) and a holistic list of synthetic/simulated/observational datasets and open-source toolboxes. The authors emphasize that spatiotemporal causal inference remains a comparatively under-explored area because inferring causation in space and time under latent confounders and spatial interference is hard.

- PCMCI (Runge et al. 2019a) combines the PC-stable algorithm with momentary conditional independence (MCI) in a two-step approach; PCMCI+ extends to autocorrelated nonlinear data with instantaneous + lagged links; LPCMCI handles hidden confounders. Implementation lives in the Tigramite repository (github.com/jakobrunge/tigramite).
- Spatiotemporal discovery methods reviewed include Mapped-PCMCI (Tibau et al. 2022, paired with the SAVAR stochastic climate model for teleconnection benchmarking via a 4-step dimensionality-reduction pipeline), ICSD (Melkas et al. 2021, interactive expert-knowledge inclusion, tested on forestry data with PC/GES/LiNGAM), STCD (Sheth et al. 2022, extends TCDF with a spatial constraint = distance d × spatial coefficient λ, motivated by hydrological river-flow direction), Group Elastic Net (Lozano et al. 2009), and pg-Causality (Zhu et al. 2017, air-pollution causal pathways via frequent-episode-pattern mining + Gaussian Bayesian networks).
- Causal inference rests on do-calculus (do() operator, Eq. 3; ACE = P(Y|do(X=1)) − P(Y|do(X=0)), Eq. 4) and the potential-outcome / Rubin Causal Model framework, with identifiability assumptions: consistency, positivity, conditional exchangeability ("weak ignorability"), and SUTVA. - Time-varying inference methods include Marginal Structural Models (MSM, using IPTW), Convergent Cross Mapping (CCM), Instrumental Variables / Conditional IV, and deep models (R-MSN, Time-series Deconfounder, CRN — binary only; TCINet and G-Net — both binary and continuous treatment, flagged as more suitable for Earth science). ## Critical notes from the literature
- The paper repeatedly stresses a core Earth science limitation: lack of ground-truth causal graphs and the inability to run controlled/randomized experiments on observational climate data, so causal findings often cannot be validated; it lists this among six fundamental challenges (data scarcity, high dimensionality, complex multi-variable interactions, confounding, no controlled experiments, limited climate-model fidelity).
- The authors explicitly note that Granger Causality is a statistical hypothesis test that "does not guarantee a true causal relation," is data-hungry, assumes stationarity/linearity/causal sufficiency, and yields misleading results under unobserved confounders.
- Several reviewed methods carry acknowledged weaknesses: ICSD uses a greedy approach that can get stuck in local optima and is sensitive to initial model state; STCD cannot model spatial and temporal interactions simultaneously; V-Granger is unsuitable for high-noise data; MSMs struggle with complex treatment–confounder interactions, longitudinal missing data, and censoring; DiD relies heavily on the parallel-trends assumption.
- The survey states that spatiotemporal causal inference "still remains a lesser tapped area," with most effort concentrated on spatiotemporal causal structure learning (discovery), because of the difficulty of inferring causation under latent confounders and spatial interference.
- As a survey/primer, the paper is descriptive: it does not run a unified head-to-head empirical benchmark of the methods it reviews; comparative claims rely on the original method papers and qualitative comparison tables (Tables 5, 8).
