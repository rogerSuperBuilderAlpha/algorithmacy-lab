## Summary
The paper addresses a practical limitation of the existing measure of "integrated information" Φ_DM (Φ-discrete/Markovian, from Balduzzi & Tononi), which quantifies how much information a system generates over and above the sum of its parts but is defined only for discrete, Markovian systems and so can rarely be measured from real (continuous, often non-Markovian) biological time-series data. Barrett and Seth introduce two new measures, Φ_E ("empirical Φ") and Φ_AR ("auto-regressive Φ"), that are applicable to stationary stochastic time-series data and easy to compute. The key conceptual change is that Φ_E uses the actual empirical distribution of the past state (information measured as reduction in Shannon entropy from the empirical distribution) instead of an imposed maximum-entropy past distribution, removing the requirement that the system be discrete and Markovian. For Gaussian-distributed systems, Φ_E can be computed directly from empirical covariance matrices; for non-Gaussian systems Φ_AR (based on auto-regressive prediction error / linear regression) provides an easily-measurable alternative that equals Φ_E in the Gaussian case. Through simulations on Markovian Gaussian networks, MVAR(p) processes, and exponentially-distributed (non-Gaussian) dynamics, they demonstrate in-practice applicability, explore the measures' properties (e.g., sensitivity to topology, integration across time lags), and compare to Φ_DM. They argue these process-based empirical measures pose challenges for theories (such as the integrated information theory of consciousness) that ascribe direct physical meaning to integrated information, and instead support a Jamesian view of consciousness as a process rather than a capacity.

## Key facts it relies on
- Φ_DM (the prior measure being improved on, Balduzzi & Tononi, ref [11]) is defined only for discrete, Markovian systems and measures information generated by a system transitioning to a state above and beyond information generated independently by its parts; it measures information as reduction in entropy from a prior maximum-entropy distribution representing the repertoire of possible states.
- Integrated information is defined as the effective information evaluated with respect to the minimum information partition (MIP, for Φ_DM) / minimum information bipartition (MIB, for Φ_E); the MIP/MIB is the partition minimizing the *normalized* effective information, while the reported Φ value is the *non-normalized* effective information for that partition (Eqs. 23-24, 29-30).
- Normalization is required because subsystems almost as large as the whole generate almost as much information as the whole; without it MIBs would be biased toward single-element-vs-remainder bipartitions giving trivially small Φ. The normalization factor K is defined via min over parts of the part entropies (Eqs. 22, 31, 34).
- Φ_E is defined for stationary stochastic systems and is state- and time-independent given a timescale τ; it is the integrated information generated by the current state X_t about the state τ time-steps in the past, equal to mutual information of the whole minus the sum of mutual informations of the parts within the bipartition (Eq. 28).
- For Gaussian systems, entropy reduces to a log-determinant of covariance form (Eq. 8), so Φ_E can be computed analytically or numerically directly from empirical covariance matrices Σ(X) and partial covariances Σ(X_{t-τ}|X_t) via the partial-covariance identity (Eq. 0.1) and formulae (0.33)-(0.34).
- A Gaussian stationary system is always equivalent to an MVAR(p) (multivariate auto-regressive) process X_t = A_1·X_{t-1} + ... + A_p·X_{t-p} + E_t; for MVAR(1), Σ(X) solves the discrete-time Lyapunov equation Σ(X) = AΣ(X)A^T + Σ(E) (Eqs. 35-37, 71).
- Φ_AR ("auto-regressive Φ") is constructed analogously to Φ_E but with information replaced by reduction in generalized covariance of the past state under linear-regression prediction; Φ_AR equals Φ_E exactly for Gaussian systems (because conditional entropy and linear-regression prediction error coincide, Eq. 49) but may differ for non-Gaussian systems, and remains easy to measure from empirical covariance matrices (Eqs. 50-56).
- Simulation results (Table 1, Fig. 1): seven 8-element Markovian Gaussian MVAR(1) networks (connection strengths 0.25; system 1(d) homogeneous with strengths 1/14) were analyzed at τ=1; analytically derived Φ_E values matched numerical estimates from 10 trials of 3000 and 10,000 data points within ≈1 SD for all systems except 1(g). Example values: network 1(a) Φ_E = 0.0323, 1(c) = 0.1283, optimized network 2(a) Φ_E = 0.2502, 2(b) = 0.2965.
- Φ_E detected integrated information at the correct timescale: for MVAR(1) networks Φ_E peaks at τ=1, and for an example MVAR(3) process (Eq. 44) Φ_E peaks at τ=2, matching the generative mechanism's lag (Fig. 4).
- Φ_AR was computed for networks re-animated with exponentially-distributed (non-Gaussian) noise (mean and variance 1, motivated by Poissonian spiking aggregates); in each case Φ_AR for the non-Gaussian dynamics was approximately equal to Φ_E (=Φ_AR) for the Gaussian dynamics, supporting Φ_AR as a useful non-Gaussian alternative (Fig. 5, Table 1).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors explicitly state that the normalization procedure (embedding minimization over the MIB into the definition of Φ) leads to instabilities and "challenges ascription of physical meaning to any measured value of Φ," because the value depends on an arbitrary normalization process; they show Φ_E can be discontinuous under continuous perturbation of dynamics and highly sensitive to finite-data entropy estimation (network 1(g), Fig. 1(h), Fig. 3(e)).
- Φ_E is also noted to be non-invariant under a coordinate change, since this leads to a different set of subsystems over which to minimize effective information — a further challenge to treating Φ as a strict physical quantity.
- The authors restrict to stationary dynamics and to bipartitions (for computational efficiency); they acknowledge entropy estimation for non-Gaussian systems scales poorly with the number of elements and "may typically require the collection of more data than is practical," and that the combinatorial explosion of partitions remains a shared computational challenge.
- They argue their empirical, process-based measures depart from the maximum-entropy "capacity" interpretation and from the IITC's identity claim between consciousness and integrated information, implying a "Jamesian modification" treating consciousness as a process rather than a capacity; this is framed as a challenge to theories ascribing direct physical meaning to Φ.
- The paper itself notes (citing ref [44]) that the original neural-complexity formulation contained an error in calculating the covariance matrix from a generative model, subsequently corrected, which "may still affect extant calculations of Φ_C."
