## Summary
This is a philosophy paper arguing that Integrated Information Theory (IIT), usually read as supporting panpsychism, is better understood as a form of emergentism that is incompatible with panpsychism. Negro's question is whether the two properties on which the panpsychist reading of IIT rests — the intrinsicality and fundamentality of integrated information — are actually expressed by IIT in a way that fits panpsychism. He argues they are not: IIT's pandispositionalist and dispositional-essentialist ontology (existence = having cause-effect powers) clashes with the panpsychist's categoricalist ontology in which causal powers are grounded in phenomenality. Negro then "plugs" anti-panpsychist assumptions into IIT and uses Hoel and colleagues' formal result of causal emergence (Φ^Max can be maximized at a macro spatiotemporal scale rather than the micro level) to build "Emergentist IIT." He analyzes candidate metaphysical relations for dependence (functional realization, causation, supervenience, fusion) and autonomy (non-linearity, multiple realizability, fundamentality, downward causation), concluding that integrated information is best understood as dependent on the fusion of the cause-effect powers of a complex's components and autonomous via downward causation. The upshot is that consciousness is "global-to-local intrinsic constraining" — the constraining power of a system as a whole upon itself — and that Emergentist IIT, by excluding e.g. atoms and molecules from consciousness, is less liberal than IIT proper.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT is presented as a theory of phenomenal consciousness whose starting point is its axioms (essential features of experience from the first-person perspective), which are mapped onto physical postulates; from the postulates IIT derives an information-theoretic measure of consciousness symbolized Φ^Max (citing Oizumi et al. 2014; Tononi et al. 2016).
- IIT's notion of information is intrinsic and causal, differing from Shannon's (1948) correlational information; per IIT, information is equivalent to causation interpreted as difference-making, and consciousness is identified with the intrinsic cause-effect structure of a system in a state (Oizumi et al. 2014, p. 24 and p. 14).
- The panpsychist reading rests on two properties of integrated information — intrinsicality and fundamentality — which Tononi & Koch (2015, p. 11) frame as treating consciousness as "an intrinsic, fundamental property of reality."
- Negro argues IIT endorses a pandispositionalist and dispositional-essentialist ontology (the essence of an entity is given by its cause-effect powers), citing Grasso (2019) and Tononi (2017), which is at odds with the categoricalist ontology common to contemporary panpsychism (Goff 2017).
- IIT's fundamentality is a dependence-based notion (Bennett 2017; Leuenberger 2020): integrated information is a property of a complex of elements, not of elementary particles, so its instantiation depends on relations among the substrate's components (the "complex").
- The paper builds on Hoel et al. (2013, 2016) showing that a system can achieve maximal integrated information at the macro-level (e.g., a six-element micro system ABCDEF coarse-grained into macro elements α=AB, β=CD, γ=EF; Hoel et al. 2016, p. 8), a result IIT proponents call "causal emergence" (Hoel et al. 2016, p. 1).
- IIT's Exclusion postulate entails that only a maximum of integrated information across nested systems contributes to consciousness, guaranteeing that one definite integrated information structure corresponds to consciousness (Oizumi et al. 2014, p. 3); Negro adds an Integration-axiom requirement that consciousness is definite in space and time.
- IIT's causal analysis uses an interventionist/manipulationist conception of causation (Woodward 2003; Pearl 2000; Menzies & Price 1993), with difference-making analyzed via a transition probability matrix (TPM); Negro frames this as having a "Janus-face" extrinsic description of an intrinsic phenomenon.
- Emergentist IIT's two anti-panpsychist desiderata are: (1) take IIT seriously as a scientific theory while keeping its pandispositionalist ontology and the explanatory (not numerical) identity of consciousness with integrated information (Haun & Tononi 2019, p. 5); and (2) be incompatible with panpsychist emergentism by requiring the microphysical to be non-mental (Montero & Papineau 2005).
- The final proposal: dependence is best captured by fusion (the complex self-defines as a whole by fusing its components' cause-effect powers in space and time; analogy to gamete fusion at fertilization, Humphreys 1996, 1997), and autonomy by downward causation (Kim 2000; Flack 2017) as macro-to-micro intrinsic constraining.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The paper is explicitly metaphysical/interpretive philosophy, not new empirical or formal work; Negro states his scope is "a philosophical analysis of how IIT fits in the debate about the nature of consciousness," and he calls Emergentist IIT "embryonal" and in need of further development.
- Negro acknowledges Dewhurst's (2021) objection that causal emergence may be only "weak" (epistemic/observer-dependent) emergence — a matter of description, not being — and that the interventionist tool IIT uses appears observer-dependent; Negro's reply (that the extrinsic analysis tracks an intrinsic phenomenon) depends on accepting IIT's axiomatic claim that an intrinsic perspective exists independently of any observer.
- He concedes a residual worry that if a simple system like an atom generates Φ^Max and Φ^Max indicates consciousness, Emergentist IIT risks collapsing back toward panpsychism; his answer is that the emergentist framework justifies restricting Φ^Max as an indicator to certain systems (e.g., adaptive systems that learn to exploit intrinsic constraining, citing Flack 2017), making it less liberal than IIT proper.
- Negro notes fusion and downward causation are "prima facie" in tension (after fusion the micro-level seems to leave nothing for the macro to act on), addressed only by appeal to more detailed accounts of causal powers (Hiddleston 2005; Mumford 2009; Fallon & Blackmon 2021), and flags open questions about whether the macro-level can be slower than the micro and whether it escapes Markovian dynamics (Großmann et al. 2020; Muñoz et al. 2020).
- A panprotopsychist reading of fusion-based IIT remains available (Mørch 2019a is cited for a fusion-based panprotopsychist view of IIT); Negro's anti-panpsychist conclusion holds only under his stipulation that the dispositional properties of the substrate are non-phenomenal.
