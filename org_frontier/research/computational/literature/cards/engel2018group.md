## Summary
The paper asks whether "integrated information" (phi), a metric originally developed by Tononi and colleagues as a measure of consciousness in brains, can usefully characterize group interactions among people and computers. Phi captures, in a single quantity, two properties the authors argue matter for many kinds of groups: differentiated information and integration. The authors apply two data-estimable versions of phi proposed by Barrett and Seth (an "empirical" phi and an "auto-regressive" phi) to three datasets. In Study 1, phi computed from communication patterns of 4-person work groups is significantly correlated with the groups' collective intelligence (r = 0.370, p = 0.003). In Study 2, groups of Wikipedia editors who produce higher-quality articles have significantly higher phi. In Study 3, the measured phi of a sample of computers and people communicating over an Internet backbone increased over a roughly six-year period. The authors conclude that integrated information can predict certain kinds of group performance, at least sometimes, and that phi can be viewed as a potential observational metric of effective group collaboration.

## Key facts it relies on
- Phi ("integrated information" or "phi") was proposed by Tononi and colleagues as a measure quantifying information generated by a system as a whole that is more than the sum of its parts; it requires both differentiated information and integration (a fully independent system has phi = 0).
- Because complete models of state-transition rules are rarely available for observational data, the authors use two data-estimable versions of phi proposed by Barrett and Seth: Phi_E ("empirical phi," used in Study 1) and Phi_AR ("auto-regressive phi," used in Studies 2 and 3), computed with a MATLAB toolbox provided by Adam Barrett.
- Study 1 used data from a prior study of 68 groups of four people performing diverse online tasks (generating, choosing, remembering, sensing, taking physical actions); collective intelligence was the first factor from a factor analysis, explaining around 40% of variance in performance across tasks.
- For face-to-face groups, average phi plotted against time delay peaked at around 2 seconds (Fig 1), which was the value used; for online (chat) groups the time delay was one timestep.
- Pooling the two communication conditions (face-to-face vs. online), phi was significantly correlated with measured collective intelligence: r = 0.370, p = 0.003 (Fig 2); even with a possible lower-left outlier removed, r = 0.290, p = 0.025.
- In Study 2, the authors analyzed edit histories of 999 Wikipedia Vital Articles (1000 downloaded, with the front-page article discarded as an outlier), examining 30-, 60-, and 90-day periods before each quality change; article quality classes in decreasing order were FA, A, GA, B, C.
- In Study 2, article quality was significantly correlated with phi when controlling for number of editors and edits per editor (likelihood ratio test F = 3.6847, p = 0.0053); pairwise Wilcoxon ranksum tests showed groups editing FA and A articles had higher phi than GA and B, which were higher than C (z = 5.6024, p < 0.00001 between C and B; z = 3.5132, p = 0.0004 between GA and A).
- In Study 3, Internet traffic data came from the CAIDA database (San Jose monitor, ~6-month intervals from 2008 onward); a time step size of 100 ms (delta) and time delay tau = 1 were used, with node subsampling to a standard sample size of 100 nodes (the number of nodes per month ranged from about 200,000 to 1.6 million).
- In Study 3, regression of phi on date was positive and very significant for three of four sampling methods (Table 1: random walk 1.675, forest fire 1.676, breadth first 1.715, all p < 10^-8; random nodes -0.26, not significant); one example regression of phi on date gave beta = 1.779, p < 0.0001 (Fig 5), with the number of sampled machines held constant.
- The authors verified the "atomic" partition approximation (used instead of enumerating all minimum-information-bipartitions) against the full phi for Wikipedia edit histories with 14 or fewer editors, finding the two highly correlated (r = 0.83, p < 0.001).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors note ongoing debate about whether phi actually measures consciousness (citing Koch & Tononi's "Can a photodiode be conscious?"), and they explicitly do not claim phi measures consciousness in groups; their argument is only that phi captures differentiation and integration that matter for many systems.
- They acknowledge that the Barrett-Seth Phi_E and Phi_AR are estimates of, not identical to, the original theoretical definitions of phi, and that later work (Oizumi et al.; Tegmark) pointed out limitations and possible corrections; Phi_AR can produce values below 0 or exceeding the number of nodes, which is theoretically impossible for the original definition.
- Numerical instabilities required dropping low-variance nodes: in Study 2 these problems occurred in 19.5% of cases (repeating the drop procedure 2.27 times on average); in Study 3 they occurred initially in 67.14% of cases (resolved after dropping low-variance nodes 2.12 times on average).
- The authors assume, but do not test for, stationarity of the time series used to compute phi, and acknowledge that correlations between phi and other variables could be caused in part by factors leading to non-stationarity; they flag examining alternative explanatory factors as future work.
- The results are correlational; the authors caution that other, possibly simpler, metrics (e.g., information-theoretic components of phi, or other complexity measures) might have similar predictive power, and that comparing phi against alternatives (member participation, effort/ability, network topology) is needed.

## Key topics covered
- Integrated information theory (IIT) and the phi metric
- Differentiation and integration as system properties (Adam Smith, Lawrence & Lorsch, design structure matrices)
- Barrett-Seth empirical phi (Phi_E) and auto-regressive phi (Phi_AR); minimum information bipartition (MIB); atomic partitions
- Collective intelligence factor in small work groups (Woolley et al.)
- Wikipedia article quality and editor groups
- Internet backbone traffic (CAIDA) and large-graph node sampling (random walk, forest fire, breadth first, random nodes; Leskovec & Faloutsos)
- Observational vs. interventional metrics of group performance
- Time-delay and time-step calibration for phi; robustness checks
