## Summary
This Perspective argues that collective intelligence is a unifying concept for integrating biology across scales (molecules, cells, tissues, organs, bodies, swarms) and substrates (neural and non-neural). The authors propose that biology is a "multiscale competency architecture" in which each level of organization solves problems in its own relevant problem space (metabolic, physiological, transcriptional, anatomical, behavioral), and that adaptive functionality percolates from lower-level competent subunits up to higher functional levels through collective dynamics. They extend William James' definition of intelligence (the ability to reach the same goal by different means) to cellular collectives by adding the notion of an expanded "perceptual field" (the region of space and time an agent can survey to find alternate paths to a goal). The paper reviews a series of biological examples — embryonic blastoderm decisions, melanoma conversion, planarian regeneration, left/right anatomical asymmetry, ectopic eye induction, neural crest migration, the vertebrate segmentation clock, and bacterial biofilms — that they interpret as cases of cellular collectives making goal-directed decisions in morphospace. They claim an important symmetry exists between the behavioral science of animal swarms and the competencies of cells and other biological systems at different scales, and that importing tools from behavioral/cognitive science can advance regenerative medicine and synthetic bioengineering.

## Key facts it relies on
- The paper frames biology as a "multiscale competency architecture" in which each level (molecular networks, cells, tissues, organs, bodies, swarms/societies) solves problems in its own problem space — metabolic, physiological, transcriptional, anatomical, and behavioral landscapes (Fig. 1).
- It adopts William James' definition of intelligence: "a degree of ability to reach the same goal by different means" (problem-solving in changing or novel circumstances); the authors cite James, The Principles of Psychology, 1890.
- It extends James' definition to collectives via the concept of the "perceptual field" — the area in space and time an agent can survey to find alternative paths to a goal; larger perceptual fields improve the ability to find variant paths (Fig. 2).
- In the Xenopus melanocyte/melanoma model, targeting only a handful of "instructor" cells (via the chloride channel activator GlyCl or molecular-genetic mutant, acting through a serotonergic pathway) can convert the whole tadpole to a hyperpigmented metastatic-melanoma-like phenotype; the conversion is all-or-none — about 70% of animals could be converted, but any given animal was either fully converted or fully normal (Fig. 4).
- A computational/machine-learning model of the melanocyte signaling pathway reproduced the all-or-none behavior and fit observed incidence percentages, and was used to predict a novel intervention (two drugs plus a dominant-negative construct) that "breaks the concordance" — experimentally confirmed to produce the first partially-converted animals seen in almost a decade of work (Fig. 4d).
- In planaria, if 50% of the neoblasts of one species (round head) are killed by radiation and replaced with transplanted neoblasts of another species (flat head), the resulting head shape is not predictable from single-cell regulatory pathways — a collective decision over morphospace; bioelectric perturbations (e.g., Ivermectin) produce 1-head and 2-head forms at a set frequency of roughly 1:2 (Fig. 5).
- Embryonic blastoderms are considered "1 embryo" because cells are aligned physically (planar polarity) and functionally (toward one anatomical attractor); scratching the blastoderm produces conjoined twins, triplets, etc., showing embryo number is not genetically fixed at 1 but determined in real time by physiological processes (Fig. 3).
- Ectopic eyes can be induced in frog embryos far from the anterior neurectoderm (e.g., on the gut) by microinjecting potassium channel (Kir6.1) mRNA that sets up a voltage gradient interpreted as an eye-induction signal; voltage-modified cells can recruit neighboring wild-type cells into the ectopic lens/organ (Fig. 6).
- The vertebrate segmentation clock (coordinated Notch-pathway oscillations) shows functional robustness: oscillations re-emerge after dissociation and reaggregation, and cells hetero-grafted from one clock phase synchronize to the host's lateral neighbors, which the authors interpret as collective-level memory in their perceptual-field framework (Fig. 8); grafted wild-type cells can rescue oscillation in mutant non-cycling fish (her1).
- Neural crest cells (NCCs) regulatively adapt migration to compensate for ablation; cells transposed between anterior-posterior axial domains change gene expression to match neighbors when individual, but maintain original expression when transposed as collectives — interpreted as expanded positional memory at the collective level (Fig. 7).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors explicitly state they make no claims that these unconventional collectives have first-person experience, nor that the phenomena are of the same degree as familiar human-level cognition; they aim to investigate minimal, plesiomorphic versions of decision-making (stated on p. 4-5).
- Box 1 anticipates the anthropomorphism objection and defends the cognitive terminology on pragmatic grounds — that the framing's empirical utility (prediction, control, and new research programs) is the justification — while acknowledging this fearless application of cognitive framings is "sometimes uncomfortable" and may have empirical consequences contradicting prior assumptions.
- The paper is a Perspective, not a primary research report; its evidence is a synthesis/reinterpretation of prior published experiments (largely from the Levin lab and collaborators) under the collective-intelligence framing rather than new experimental data.
- The authors acknowledge conceptual gaps: e.g., for the planarian neoblast-replacement thought experiment they note "we still lack the conceptual tools and data to understand how collectives of cells make unified decisions," and that the mechanism of stochastic head/tail collective decisions is "not known but likely involves gap-junctional communication."
- Competing interest disclosed: Tufts University has a sponsored research agreement with Astonishing Labs, a company funding projects relevant to the collective intelligence of cells.
