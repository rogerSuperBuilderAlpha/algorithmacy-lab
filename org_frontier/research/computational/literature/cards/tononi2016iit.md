## Summary
This review by Tononi, Boly, Massimini and Koch presents Integrated Information Theory (IIT), which approaches consciousness by starting from the essential phenomenological properties of experience and inferring the requirements that a physical substrate of consciousness (PSC) must satisfy. The acquired PDF is the paper's Supplementary Information S1 ("Axioms and postulates of IIT"), which lays out the theory's foundational logic: five axioms describing self-evident properties of every conscious experience (intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, exclusion), each mapped to a corresponding postulate stating what a physical system must have to support consciousness. The postulates are illustrated with a worked example of a small physical system of three binary elements ABC (plus a background element D), where mechanisms specify cause-effect repertoires that combine into a cause-effect (conceptual) structure. Integration is quantified by integrated information Φ ("big PHI"), the irreducibility of the system's cause-effect structure to that of its parts under the minimum information partition (MIP). By the exclusion postulate, the actual PSC is the set of elements (a "complex") that specifies a maximally irreducible conceptual structure (Φ = Φmax) over all candidate subsets, supersets, and spatio-temporal grains. The result is a principled bridge from the qualitative character of experience to a measurable physical quantity.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT posits five axioms of consciousness, each treated as self-evident about every experience: (i) intrinsic existence, (ii) composition, (iii) information, (iv) integration, and (v) exclusion.
- Each axiom maps onto a corresponding postulate about the physical substrate: a PSC and its mechanisms must have cause-effect power upon themselves, be composed of subsets that have cause-effect power, specify a specific cause-effect structure, be irreducible (integrated), and be definite/exclusive.
- The worked example uses a system of three binary elements ABC in state 111 (yellow = 1 = ON), with the cause and effect repertoire graphs showing probabilities over the 8 possible past and future states; a background element D is fixed to 0.
- Element C is described as an AND gate / majority (MAJ) gate over inputs A and B (state 1 if at least two of three inputs are ON); if C = 1 its cause repertoire specifies that A and B were in state 11 rather than 00, 01, or 10.
- Integrated information Φ ("big PHI") measures irreducibility as the distance between the cause-effect structure of the intact system and that of the partitioned system, evaluated at the minimum information partition (MIP) — the partition that makes the least difference; strong integration is assessed using unidirectional partitions.
- In the example over ABCD, the set ABC is the maximally irreducible system with Φmax = 0.66, compared with subset AB (Φ = 0.22) and superset ABCD (Φ = 0.07).
- At the mechanism level, "small phi" (φ) measures a mechanism's irreducibility; the maximally irreducible cause-effect repertoire (φmax) is called a core concept (or concept), and the constrained elements form its purview.
- Overlapping concept purviews specify a relation (second- or higher-order), corresponding to the binding among phenomenal distinctions; a relation exists intrinsically only if maximally irreducible.
- A maximally irreducible conceptual structure (Φ = Φmax) over a definite set of elements and a definite spatio-temporal grain (e.g., quarks/atoms/neurons/neuronal groups/columns; microseconds/milliseconds/seconds) defines a complex, which constitutes the PSC; each experience is said to encompass roughly a hundred milliseconds.

## Critical notes from the literature
- This acquired PDF is only Supplementary Information S1 of the article (the axioms-and-postulates exposition), not the full main-text review; numbers and figure details here reflect the illustrative toy example, not empirical neural data.
- The framework is explicitly assumption-laden: the axioms are taken as self-evident phenomenological starting points, and the postulates and the maximum-existence/exclusion principle are theoretical inferences (the paper draws analogies to least-action and conservation principles in physics rather than deriving them empirically).
- Φ and Φmax require evaluating cause-effect structures over all candidate subsets, supersets, and spatio-temporal grains, which the exposition implies but does not solve computationally; the example is deliberately tiny (3-4 binary elements).
- The exclusion postulate is invoked as a stipulated conservation principle ("would amount to multiplying existence for free") to single out one PSC; this is a normative theoretical choice within IIT rather than an independently established physical law.
