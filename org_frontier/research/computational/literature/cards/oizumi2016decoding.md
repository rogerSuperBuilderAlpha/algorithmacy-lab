## Summary
The paper addresses how to compute Integrated Information Theory's (IIT's) integrated information Φ from experimental neural data, given that the original measure relies on a maximum entropy distribution over past states that is impractical for real systems (it cannot be uniquely defined for continuous variables and requires a full transition probability matrix that is nearly impossible to estimate). The authors note that two previously proposed empirical-distribution measures from Barrett and Seth (Φ_I and Φ_H) fail the theoretical requirements that integrated information should be lower-bounded by 0 and upper-bounded by the information generated by the whole system. They propose a new practical measure, Φ* ("phi star"), built on the information-theoretic concept of "mismatched decoding": Φ* is the difference between the actual mutual information I(X^{t-τ};X^t) and a "hypothetical" mutual information I* obtained when the system is decoded as if its parts were independent. Φ* quantifies the information lost when interactions between parts are ignored, and the authors show it satisfies both bounds (since 0 ≤ I* ≤ I). They derive an analytical expression for Φ* under a Gaussian assumption to make it computable in large systems, and apply Φ*, Φ_I, Φ_H, and mutual information to electrocorticogram (ECoG) data recorded from an awake monkey, demonstrating that Φ* stays within the theoretical bounds while Φ_I and Φ_H violate them on real brain data.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT predicts the amount of integrated information Φ in the brain is related to the level of consciousness; integrated information is information generated by a system as a whole above and beyond information generated independently by its parts (Eq 2: Φ = I(^max X^{t-τ};X^t) − Σ_i I(^max M_i^{t-τ};M_i^t)).
- Two theoretical requirements for a measure of integrated information: it should not be negative (lower-bounded by 0), and it should not exceed the information generated by the whole system; the original Φ satisfies 0 ≤ Φ ≤ I(^max X^{t-τ};X^t) (Eq 4) and can be written as a Kullback-Leibler divergence.
- The maximum entropy distribution assumption limits the original Φ because it cannot be uniquely defined for continuous variables, and it requires knowing the full transition probability matrix p(X^t|X^{t-τ}) for all possible past states, which is nearly impossible to estimate experimentally.
- Barrett and Seth [15] proposed two empirical-distribution measures, Φ_I (Eq 5) and Φ_H (Eq 6); Φ_H was also derived independently as "stochastic interaction" from information geometry [25,26].
- The new measure is Φ*(X^{t-τ};X^t) = I(X^{t-τ};X^t) − I*(X^{t-τ};X^t) (Eq 9), where I* is the mutual information for mismatched decoding using the partitioned distribution q(X^t|X^{t-τ}) = Π_i p(M_i^t|M_i^{t-τ}) (Eq 8); Φ* satisfies the theoretical requirements because 0 ≤ I* ≤ I.
- I* (the mismatched-decoding information rate, Eq 20) was first derived in information theory [17, Merhav et al. 1994], first introduced into neuroscience in [18], and first applied to neural data in [19]; in prior neuroscience applications I* was quantified between stimuli and neural states, not between past and present system states as done here.
- In a two-unit linear regression / autoregressive model X^t = AX^{t-1} + E^t (Eq 13) with connectivity matrix A = a[[1,1],[1,1]] and noise covariance Σ(E) = [[1,c],[c,1]]: when connection strength a = 0 there is no information (I_1 = I_2 = 0), yet Φ_H increases monotonically with noise correlation c (violating the upper bound), while Φ* and Φ_I stay 0.
- When parts are perfectly correlated, Φ* = 0 (Eq 17), but Φ_I becomes negative, Φ_I = −I(M^{t-τ};M^t) (Eq 18), violating the lower bound; in the example with a = 0.4, Φ_I is positive for c less than ~0.2 but negative for larger c (Fig 4 shows Φ_I = I − (I_1 + I_2) = −0.15).
- Under a Gaussian assumption, mutual information and Φ* are computed analytically from covariance matrices (Eqs 22–33); the maximization of I* over β reduces to a quadratic equation in β so β can be computed analytically without gradient ascent.
- ECoG analysis: 128 ECoG electrodes (platinum discs, 1 mm diameter, 5 mm inter-electrode distance) were implanted in the subdural space in four adult macaque monkeys, sampled at 1 kHz; the authors used 64 bipolar re-referenced signals, applied the "atomic partition" (all 64 channels treated as independent), estimated covariances with a 2 s window and 2 s time step averaged over 600 s. Φ* peaked around τ = 20 ms and stayed within bounds, while Φ_I went negative at small τ and Φ_H stayed large even as mutual information I approached 0 with increasing τ (Fig 5).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors note their study used IIT 2.0 (chosen for computational feasibility), which considers only information flow from present to past, whereas IIT 3.0 additionally considers present-to-future flow; how to practically compute integrated information accounting for both directions using an empirical distribution is left as an unanswered question.
- An acknowledged unresolved difficulty is how to partition a system: IIT requires using the minimum information partition (MIP), but the number of possible partitions grows exponentially with system size; in the ECoG analysis they used only the atomic partition (an upper-bound partition), not the MIP.
- The authors note theoretical problems with comparing integrated information across different partitions and with normalization: IIT 2.0's normalization factor can be negative for continuous variables (since entropy can be negative), so a different normalization factor is needed for continuous systems.
- The authors attribute Barrett and Seth's [15] failure to detect the problems of Φ_I and Φ_H to their fixing the Gaussian noise covariance E to 0; the present work shows both connectivity strength a and noise covariance c strongly affect integrated information.
- The authors emphasize Φ* aims to measure global integration in a system as a whole, in contrast to bivariate measures like Granger causality or transfer entropy that measure local pairwise interactions; clarifying the mathematical relationship between Φ* and such connectivity measures is left for future work.
