## Summary
The paper asks whether the individualism-holism debate in social ontology can be productively recast in "gradualist" terms, and whether a precise formal language exists to express such gradations. Christensen argues that the formal tools of Integrated Information Theory (IIT) — originally devised by Tononi to characterize consciousness in the human brain but increasingly applied as a general framework for complex systems across physics, biology, and computer science — supply exactly this missing formalism. The core move is to import IIT's difference-making criterion: a system (and analogously a social entity) is causally irreducible to its parts to the degree that differences are made by and to it over and above the differences made by and to its parts. Christensen aligns this with "gradualist causal-explanatory holism," a position drawn from Kincaid and from List and Spiekermann's taxonomy of non-reductive individualism. To demonstrate feasibility, he formalizes two deterministic toy models — a three-person turn-taking cooperation scenario ("T") and a six-node model of a computer supply chain ("SU") derived from Wieland (2018) — and computes their integrated information using the PyPhi software package. Model T yields a system-wide Φ of 0.410218 with major complex ABC (irreducibly social), whereas the supply-chain model SU returns Φ = 0.0 (reducible to its parts). The paper concludes that IIT can serve as a formal language to formalize, analyze, and quantify degrees of social structure, and flags a mereological objection about IIT's exclusion principle as an avenue for future research.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT's core ontological commitment is the difference-making principle ("Eleatic principle" from Plato's Sophist, 247d-e: to exist is to make a difference); IIT predicts a system is causally irreducible to its parts to the degree it constrains its states more than the sum of its parts does (Tononi 2012, 297).
- Integrated information is computed in two steps using a distance metric (the Earth Mover's / "Hamming" distance): lower-case φ for individual functional units ("mechanisms"), upper-case Φ for the whole system; a mechanism is a subset of S that both causally affects and is causally affected by another subset (its "purview") (Oizumi et al. 2014, 4-14).
- The φ of a mechanism equals the distance between the information generated by the intact mechanism and that generated by its "minimum information partition" — the partition suffering the smallest decrease in information (Oizumi et al. 2014, 8).
- In the toy turn-taking model T (three individuals A, B, C; C must translate between A and B), in state ABC^c = 011, four of seven possible mechanisms generate φ: A (0.071429), B (0.125), C (0.25), and AC (0.25); BC is causally reducible in that state.
- Model T returns a system-wide Φ of 0.410218 in state ABC^c = 011, with major complex ABC, meaning the whole system makes differences over and above A, B, C individually and over AB, AC, BC (PyPhi, appendix 8).
- The supply-chain model SU (six nodes: CM, RM, CL, C, CO, SC, formalizing Wieland 2018) returns Φ = 0.0 in state 111111 and in every other state — a causally reducible (fully decomposable) social-science model.
- Calculations use the Python PyPhi package developed by Mayner and colleagues (Mayner et al. 2018); all appendices show PyPhi code with TPMs, connectivity matrices, and outputs (e.g., subsystem.cause_info((B,C),(A,B,C)) = 1.5).
- The paper builds on List and Spiekermann's (2013) taxonomy of four individualism-holism spectra (type-identity, token-identity, supervenience, causal-explanatory) and on Kincaid's (2014, 140, 147-150) claim that the live issues are local empirical questions of "how much" social structure an entity has.
- IIT here adopts "operational physicalism" (Albantakis et al. 2023b, 6) and a notion of causality as "robust regularities between certain variables or properties" (List & Spiekermann 2013, 636), not production-based causality.
- The paper cites Engel and Malone (2018), who calculated integrated information for three social-group datasets (small four-person groups, Wikipedia co-editing, internet activity) and found significant correlations between integrated information and collective intelligence / article quality / activity — noted as nearly the only prior use of IIT in social science.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The author acknowledges the exclusion-argument objection (Kim 2000): under IIT's original "winner-takes-all" exclusion principle, individuals with higher integrated information than a social entity S would ontologically exclude S, leaving only individuals irreducible (Tononi 2012, 325); Christensen replies by withholding the claim that integrated information suffices for consciousness, and by arguing not every feature of an individual's integrated information need be "involved" in a given social activity (the Swan Lake dancer example).
- The paper relies on simplified deterministic toy models for illustration; the author notes IIT is not limited to deterministic models (Markovian models suit nondeterministic systems) and that appendix calculations are simplified, leaving out full mathematical detail.
- Christensen notes the exclusion/intrinsic-extrinsic-properties issues, and Kim's micro-physicalism/point-particle picture of causal closure, are themselves contested by current physics (e.g., Rovelli's relational ontology, quantum field theory), per an anonymous reviewer's prompting.
- The conclusion frames the precise "mereological stance" — whether the parts of wholes must be completely involved — as an unresolved live avenue for future research into IIT's philosophical assumptions, not a settled result.
- Scope is explicitly a feasibility/proof-of-concept demonstration ("principally feasible" via two toy models), not an empirical analysis of real social data.
