## Summary
According to Integrated Information Theory (IIT), consciousness is a fundamental, observer-independent property of physical systems, and the measure Φ of integrated information is identical to the quantity or level of consciousness. The authors argue that for this claim to be plausible, Φ must be well-defined and there should be no alternative formulae consistent with the axioms and postulates of the theory. Setting aside the well-known practical-computability critiques of Φ, the paper addresses the deeper theoretical question of whether Φ can even in principle be a fundamental physical quantity. After rehearsing the construction of Φ (the cause-effect distribution, the maximum-entropy prior, the earth mover's distance, the "cruelest cut" partition, and the maximisation over grainings), the authors present three ways in which Φ, as formulated in IIT 3.0, is not well-defined: (1) there is no canonical metric on the space of states or on probability-distribution space; (2) it requires a discrete (or compact) set of states, which fails for continuous variables such as neuron membrane potentials; and (3) it requires Markovian dynamics, which fails for real brain dynamics that are non-Markovian. They conclude that further development and operationalisation of Φ is required, and discuss possible directions such as a field-based or topological reformulation and the use of Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT (Oizumi et al, 2014; Tononi et al, 2016; Tononi and Koch, 2015) holds that the overall quantity of consciousness generated by a system is identical to its value of Φ, and that consciousness is a fundamental observer-independent property analogous to mass, charge or energy, implying Φ is a fundamental physical quantity.
- The computation time required to compute the effective Φ for a particular system graining grows faster than exponentially with the number of components, and Φ has only ever been computed on toy models with just a handful of components (Mayner et al, 2017 / PyPhi).
- The construction uses the joint cause-effect distribution Pce(X0, X1) = P(X1|X0)Pu(X0), where Pu(X0) is the uniform (maximum entropy) distribution and P(X1|X0) is given by the system's dynamics; from this Pc(X0|X1) is extracted (Eqs. 1-2). The final Φ is the minimum of that computed for causes and for effects.
- Integration is quantified via the earth mover's (Wasserstein) distance between Pc(X0|X1=x) and the product over partition parts ∏k Pc(Mk0|Mk1=mk); Φ is the minimum of this distance over all possible partitions (the "cruelest cut"), and the final Φ of the system is the maximum over all possible grainings (in space, time, and state set).
- Problem 1: there is no canonical metric on the space of states (IIT-3.0 proposes Hamming distance for binary states, but for non-binary states L1, L2, L∞ norms are all valid with no canonical choice) nor on probability-distribution space (earth mover's distance is not unique; Tegmark 2016 lists alternatives). Mediano et al (2018) showed in simulation that different metric choices produce profound differences in behaviour even on small systems.
- Problem 2: the maximum entropy distribution Pu(X0) is not well-defined if the state set is infinite, except when the set is compact (closed and bounded) (Barrett and Seth, 2011). Hence neuron membrane potential cannot be the state variable, and systems with Gaussian or exponentially distributed state variables have no well-defined Φ.
- Problem 3: Φ is not well-defined for non-Markovian dynamics, because P(X1|X0) and Pce(X0, X1) are undefined unless future states depend only on the current state; brain dynamics are non-Markovian at multiple levels, from EEG (von Wegner et al, 2017) to ionic current fluctuations in membrane channels (Fuliński et al, 1998). It only takes one graining of a system to be non-Markovian for Φ to be ill-defined for that system.
- The worked example (Eqs. 4-5) is a non-ergodic system with a random walk St+1 = St + Bt (Bt i.i.d. taking ±1 with equal probability) binarised at threshold θ; P(X1=1|X0=1) is not well-defined because S0 has no compact range and no well-defined probability density.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors explicitly distinguish their three problems from the (separate) practical-computability challenge: maximisation over grainings is intractable for any real system because no compelling short-cuts/approximations exist (Barrett, 2016), but they argue a system could intrinsically instantiate its own maximisation even if no external observer can compute it.
- The paper acknowledges that variant Φ measures (Tegmark 2016; Mediano et al 2018; Tajima and Kanai 2017) sidestep some problems by using the empirical (rather than maximum entropy) distribution and applying to continuous/stationary/non-Markovian systems, but warns that these measures behave very diversely even on small simple networks, so one should be cautious about treating them as generalisations or approximations of any eventual "fundamental" Φ.
- The authors note proposed but unproven fixes: a field-based reformulation (Barrett, 2014, 2016) targeting the electromagnetic field; a reformulation in terms of the geometrical/topological structure of the instantaneous state to address Problems 2 and 3; and possible use of Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy (well-defined for ergodic systems as a supremum over grainings) generalised to non-ergodic systems via Thurner and Hanel (2012). Problem 1 is described as harder to solve.
- The paper frames the variable-graining maximisation premise itself as "a debatable supposition" (citing Bayne, 2018) regarding whether the state of consciousness is determined by whatever spatiotemporal scale and graining happens to maximise Φ.
