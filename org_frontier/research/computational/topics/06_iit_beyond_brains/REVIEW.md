# Integrated Information Beyond Neuroscience

Integrated information theory (IIT) supplies a measure, Φ, that scores the degree to which a system's cause-effect structure is irreducible to its parts. Although the formalism was built to characterize neural substrates, the underlying quantity—simultaneous differentiation and integration—applies to any discrete dynamical system, and a research line now exports it to social, collective, and artificial substrates. The reference implementation PyPhi makes such application tractable for small systems (Mayner et al., 2018), and a system-level reformulation clarifies how Φ identifies maximally irreducible complexes within larger networks (Marshall et al., 2023).

The social and organizational thread is the most direct departure from neuroscience. Engel and Malone (2018) compute Φ over human-computer groups and report that higher integrated information predicts collective-intelligence task performance, Wikipedia article quality, and a rising trend across Internet-mediated interaction. Christensen (2024) develops the philosophical counterpart, proposing IIT as a formal language for social ontology in which social entities are causally irreducible to their members by degree, supplying a gradualist alternative to the individualism-holism dichotomy.

Animal collectives furnish empirical substrates. Niizato et al. (2020) apply IIT 3.0 to real fish schools (Plecoglossus altivelis) and find a discontinuity in Φ at schools of four or more, coinciding with an emergent form of leadership invisible to mutual information or transfer entropy. Subsequent work locates maximal group Φ at criticality, with heterogeneous critical subgroups distributed across the school (Niizato et al., 2024). This collective-substrate framing connects to broader arguments that integration-based competence spans biological scales (McMillen and Levin, 2024).

The artificial-substrate thread divides between measurement and in-principle analysis. A systematic review maps how AI and cognitive-science methods address IIT's computational obstacles (Guerrero et al., 2023). Findlay et al. (2024) use IIT to argue that a digital computer can be functionally equivalent to a conscious system while differing in Φ-structure, separating artificial intelligence from artificial consciousness and contradicting computational functionalism.

Two cross-cutting concerns temper these applications. Candidate Φ measures disagree markedly on non-trivial networks, so substrate-level claims depend on operationalization (Mediano et al., 2019). The Integrated Information Decomposition framework (ΦID) generalizes the machinery into a taxonomy of information dynamics applicable to economies, brains, and coupled systems alike, recasting integration as one mode among several higher-order interactions (Mediano et al., 2021). The open problems concern measure selection, computational scaling beyond small systems, and whether high Φ in a social or artificial substrate carries the interpretive weight assigned to it in neural cases.
