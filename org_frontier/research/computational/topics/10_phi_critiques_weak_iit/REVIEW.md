# Critiques, Well-Definedness, and Weak IIT

Integrated information theory (IIT) identifies consciousness with a maximally irreducible cause-effect structure quantified by Φ, and the program's controversies bear directly on whether such a quantity is usable outside consciousness claims. A first cluster of critiques concerns definability. Φ is not uniquely fixed: multiple formulae are consistent with the stated axioms, and the measure becomes ill-defined or non-computable for general physical systems whose decomposition into discrete units is not given (Barrett & Mediano, 2019). A recent synthesis from within the critical-but-constructive camp concedes that Φ has not been computed on any real physical system and that only proxies, not approximations, have been evaluated, while arguing these limitations are remediable through a field-theoretic reformulation (Barrett et al., 2026). Earlier work showed that trivial theories can match IIT's empirical predictions and questioned the unargued status of the exclusion principle (Cerullo, 2015).

A second cluster targets the axiomatic method and the postulates. Bayne (2018) argues that none of the five axioms functions as genuinely axiomatic or imposes a substantive constraint, recommending a natural-kind methodology instead. The exclusion postulate, which selects a single definite substrate and grain at a Φ maximum, draws particular scrutiny for generating quale-underdetermination and for sitting uneasily with the theory's own partition-based formalism. IIT 4.0 restated the axiom-postulate mapping and introduced an intrinsic-information measure intended to secure uniqueness (Albantakis et al., 2023).

A third cluster concerns testability. The unfolding argument holds that any causal-structure theory either is already falsified or lies outside science, since functionally identical feedforward and recurrent systems would receive different Φ yet identical behavior (Doerig et al., 2019). Hanson and Walker (2021) formalize this across a computational hierarchy, showing IIT can be simultaneously falsified at one level and unfalsifiable at another. Herzog et al. (2022) press the charge toward dissociative epiphenomenalism. Negro (2020) replies that phenomenology-first theories are grounded, and that falsificationism is an inadequate demarcation criterion. The dispute escalated into an open letter labeling IIT pseudoscience (Fleming et al., 2023).

The exclusion postulate connects to causal emergence: effective information can peak at a macro grain, so macro mechanisms can "beat" micro ones (Hoel et al., 2013), a result Negro (2022) reads as favoring an emergentist over a panpsychist interpretation. For non-consciousness applications, the most actionable move is the strong/weak distinction: weak IIT detaches pragmatic information-dynamics hypotheses from the metaphysical identity claim and the Φ-maximum machinery, retaining empirically tractable measures (Mediano et al., 2022). This weak reading lets opaque algorithmic coordinators be characterized by integration-type quantities without inheriting the definability, exclusion, and falsifiability burdens of strong IIT.
