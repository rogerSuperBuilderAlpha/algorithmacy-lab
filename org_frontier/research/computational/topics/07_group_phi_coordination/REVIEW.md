# Integrated Information as a Coordination Metric

The proposal that integrated information (Φ), a quantity built to measure consciousness in brains, can also index the quality of coordination in groups was given its clearest empirical test by Engel and Malone (2018). Encoding who is acting at each moment as a binary state vector, they computed empirical and autoregressive Φ for 68 four-person teams, 999 Wikipedia articles, and Internet backbone traffic. Higher Φ correlated with collective intelligence in work groups (r = 0.37) and with article quality among Wikipedia editors, positioning Φ as a candidate metric of effective collaboration that captures both differentiation and integration of group activity.

That work sits atop two literatures. The collective-intelligence program established that a general factor, c, explains group performance across diverse tasks and is predicted by interaction process more than by individual ability (Woolley et al., 2010), a result extended and quantified across 1,356 groups and validated in online settings (Riedl et al., 2021). The formal machinery descends from the IIT 3.0 specification of Φ (Oizumi et al., 2014), though direct group applications draw more on information-decomposition reformulations that make emergence and synergy computable in multivariate time series (Rosas et al., 2020).

A parallel neural line tests whether between-brain coupling carries the same predictive signal. Reinero et al. (2020) found inter-brain synchrony measured by EEG hyperscanning predicted collective performance over and above self-report and behavioral indices. A pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis of 41 studies and 1,326 teams reported positive associations between inter-brain synchrony and team process and performance (Hu et al., 2024). The signal is not unconditional: a trajectory study found inter-brain synchrony neither changed reliably over time nor predicted performance, with neither composition nor synchrony patterns explaining outcomes (Réveillé et al., 2025), tempering claims that coupling straightforwardly indexes team cognition.

Behavioral analogues converge on the synergy idea without invoking neural data. Multidimensional recurrence analysis of speech, movement, and interface use in 32 triads linked patterns of collective regularity and reorganization to collaborative-problem-solving facets (Amon et al., 2019). The framing now extends to hybrid and artificial collectives: a partial-information-decomposition treatment of time-delayed mutual information distinguished performance-relevant cross-agent synergy from spurious temporal coupling in multi-agent language-model groups, showing prompt design can move systems from aggregates toward integrated collectives (Riedl, 2025). Across these strands, integrated-information and synergy measures show a recurring association with joint performance, alongside open questions about estimator choice, timescale, and the boundary conditions under which the association holds.
