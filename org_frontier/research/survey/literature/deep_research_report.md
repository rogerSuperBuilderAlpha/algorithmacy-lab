## 1. Psychometric scale-development frameworks

The program's instruments should follow an established pipeline. Boateng et al. (2018) define the three-phase primer (item development; scale development; scale evaluation) that dominates applied measurement, and Streiner and Kottner (2014) specify how development and validation studies should be reported. El-Den et al. (2020) and DeVon et al. (2007) detail content-validity quantification (CVR/CVI) and the exploratory-to-confirmatory factor-analytic transition. These supply the methodological spine for any new algorithmic-literacy instrument.

## 2. Reliability beyond coefficient alpha

A literacy scale reporting only Cronbach's alpha is now considered under-specified. Dunn et al. (2014) show alpha's reliance on tau-equivalence and present omega as a practical alternative; Green and Yang (2015) argue factor analysis should precede internal-consistency estimation and recommend omega and omega-hierarchical for multidimensional or bifactor structures. Kelley and Terry (2011) provide sample-size planning so reliability point estimates carry adequately narrow confidence intervals. For a multidimensional literacy construct, omega is the defensible default.

## 3. Measurement invariance for cross-population comparison

Comparing literacy across worker groups or survey waves requires invariance evidence. Vandenberg and Lance (2000) synthesized the early literature; Cheung and Rensvold (2002) established change-in-fit criteria (e.g., ΔCFI); Putnick and Bornstein (2016) provide the current conventions and reporting state of the art across the configural, metric, and scalar sequence. Partial invariance suffices for valid latent-mean comparison, a practical point for instruments that must travel.

## 4. Conceptualizing algorithmic literacy

Dogruel et al. (2021) operationalize algorithm literacy on two cognitive dimensions, awareness and knowledge, validated via item response theory, and their broader conceptualization adds critical evaluation and coping/influence skills. Hargittai et al. (2020) articulate why this is hard: algorithm skills concern a black box users cannot directly observe. DeVito (2021) reframes literacy developmentally as movement from functional to structural folk theories, useful for a panel program tracking growth.

## 5. Awareness measurement instruments

Zarouali et al. (2021) developed and validated the AMCA scale across four dimensions (content filtering, automated decision-making, human-algorithm interplay, ethical considerations) on Facebook, YouTube, and Netflix. The recommender-systems ALRS scale (Technology in Society, 2026) spans five dimensions (awareness, knowledge, assessment, reflection, practical use) with strong internal consistency. Scheibenzuber et al. (2026) separate instrumental from critical-reflective AI skills using a scenario-based self-assessment, distinguishing them from generic digital skills.

## 6. Social distribution of awareness and knowledge

Gran et al. (2021) found 41% of Norwegians report no algorithm awareness and derived a six-group typology, arguing for a new digital divide. Gruber and Hargittai (2024) document an algorithmic knowledge gap within and between countries with misinformation implications. Oeldorf-Hirsch and Neubaum (2023) link awareness to attitudes and behavior across German and U.S. social-media users. These establish that any literacy instrument will encounter strong demographic structure that invariance testing must address.

## 7. Qualitative foundations: folk theories

Rader and Gray (2015) mapped user beliefs about Facebook news-feed curation; DeVito et al. (2017) studied resistance to algorithmic change (#RIPTwitter). These ground the latent constructs that scales attempt to quantify and supply item content for awareness and reflection dimensions.

## 8. Panel and growth methods for skill acquisition

Hamaker et al. (2015) demonstrate that the classic cross-lagged panel model fails to separate within- from between-person variance, motivating the random-intercept CLPM; Lucas (2023) extends the critique and clarifies when each model is appropriate. For a program tracking workers' skill trajectories across waves while the mediating algorithms change, these methods isolate genuine learning from stable individual differences.

## 9. Substantive setting: coordination through algorithmic intermediaries

Zhang and Liu (2024) characterize and assess algorithm literacy among a vulnerable group (older adults) interacting with video recommendations, combining interviews and cognitive mapping. Platform-labor reviews (e.g., the ARIST systematic review, 2024) document algorithmic management, opacity, and the platform's interested position, the exact conditions algorithmacy-lab studies.

## Gap

No located instrument was purpose-built for workers coordinating through opaque, interested algorithmic third parties as a coordination problem; existing scales target consumers, students, or media users, not the coordination-through-an-interested-intermediary case the lab defines. Longitudinal validation is thin: most literacy scales are cross-sectional single-sample validations, with no published measurement-invariance evidence across survey waves and almost no latent-growth or RI-CLPM modeling of literacy acquisition over time. The lab's signature construct, integrated information (IIT/Phi) as a measure of coordination, has no demonstrated linkage to any validated literacy or awareness instrument; building and testing that bridge (whether Phi predicts or correlates with measured algorithmic literacy in coordinating groups) is the open empirical question. Cross-national and worker-population invariance, objective rather than self-report competence measures for the coordination case, and panel designs that accommodate a changing algorithmic target all remain to be established.
