## Summary
This study adapts and validates a Turkish-language version of the Algorithmic Media Content Awareness (AMCA) scale, originally developed in English by Zarouali et al. (2021) as a 13-item, four-factor, 5-point Likert measure of algorithmic awareness/literacy. The question is whether the Turkish version retains good psychometric properties. Using a descriptive cross-sectional design, the authors translated the scale via a back-translation procedure (Merenda's framework), then collected responses from 414 undergraduate students at one Turkish state university via convenience sampling (spring 2022-2023). They tested construct validity with a second-order confirmatory factor analysis (CFA, maximum likelihood) and reliability with Cronbach's alpha. The four-factor structure showed good model fit (χ2/df = 2.902, CFI = .95, GFI = .939, TLI = .933, RMR = .035, SRMR = .0466, RMSEA = .068) with no model modifications. Factor reliabilities ranged from .74 to .81 and overall alpha was .90; convergent and discriminant validity were satisfactory. The authors conclude the Turkish AMCA is a valid and reliable tool for measuring algorithmic awareness among undergraduates.

## Key facts it relies on
- The original AMCA scale (Zarouali et al., 2021) is a 13-item, 5-point Likert (1 = not at all aware to 5 = completely aware), four-factor measure; the four dimensions are content filtering (FIL, 4 items), automated decision-making (ADM, 3 items), human-algorithm interplay (HAI, 3 items), and ethical considerations (ETC, 3 items).
- Sample: 414 undergraduates (after excluding 5 of 419 responses for lack of consent) from one Turkish state university; 306 female (73.9%) and 108 male (26.1%); mean age 21.50 (SD = 5.14), range 18-65; data collected February-September 2023 via Google Forms and convenience sampling.
- Six univariate/multivariate outliers were removed, leaving 408 students for the CFA; the 1:10 sample-size rule of thumb (13 items) was met.
- CFA fit indices (reported in Table 3): χ2/df = 2.902, CFI = .95, GFI = .939, TLI = .933, RMR = .035, SRMR = .0466, RMSEA = .068, with no modifications to the model; a second-order CFA was used (factors operate as distinct scales and as one overarching scale).
- Factor loadings of items ranged from .54 to .83, all exceeding the .40 threshold (Stevens, 2002).
- Per-factor reliability (Cronbach's alpha): FIL = .81, ADM = .74, HAI = .76, ETC = .77; overall alpha = .90. Composite Reliability (CR): FIL = .82, ADM = .74, HAI = .75, ETC = .76.
- Average Variance Extracted (AVE): FIL = .61, ADM = .50, HAI = .51, ETC = .52; square roots of AVE (FIL .787, ADM .683, HAI .704, ETC .718) exceeded inter-construct correlations, supporting discriminant validity.
- Item-total (item-subscale) correlations ranged from .463 to .709, all above the .30 homogeneity threshold (Mason et al., 2021), so no items were dropped.
- Analyses used IBM SPSS 28 and IBM AMOS 20; ethics approval no. 571441 (February 3, 2023).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors explicitly note the study was limited to undergraduate students from only one university, recommending future work span diverse educational and cultural backgrounds and multiple universities.
- Convenience (non-random, non-stratified) sampling was used, which the authors acknowledge (citing Creswell) does not permit comprehensive generalization to a population.
- The sample is skewed toward female students (73.9%), limiting representativeness.
- Recommended cross-cultural validation with bilingual subjects (Sireci, 2005, via Merenda) could not be carried out due to difficulty finding bilingual participants; translation idioms such as "human judgments" (item 6) and "prejudices and stereotypes" (item 12) required expert adjustment.
- The paper reports validity/reliability of the adapted instrument but does not include test-retest reliability, exploratory factor analysis, or external criterion/predictive validity evidence.
