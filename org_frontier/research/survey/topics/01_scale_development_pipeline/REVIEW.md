# Scale Development Pipeline and Reporting Standards

Building a new algorithmic-literacy instrument inherits a mature, three-phase pipeline whose conventions are well codified. The dominant template divides work into item generation and content validation, scale construction, and scale evaluation, spanning roughly nine to eighteen documented steps (Boateng et al., 2018; Stefana et al., 2025). Recent communication-field syntheses condense this into ten reportable decisions and document that scholars often deviate from them, so explicit reporting of each choice is itself a quality signal (Carpenter, 2018). The foundational logic remains Loevinger's construct-validity scheme: begin with a clear conceptualization of the target construct, write an overinclusive item pool with careful attention to wording, and prioritize unidimensionality over raw internal consistency (Clark & Watson, 1995, 2019). For organizational and consumer constructs the same logic is framed as define-operationalize-confirm, with independent samples required to confirm structure (Lambert et al., 2022; Lim & Kumar, 2026).

The item-generation phase carries distinct standards. Item pools should draw on both deductive (literature, theory) and inductive (target-population) sources, and content validity is quantified through the content validity ratio, item- and scale-level content validity indices, and interrater agreement among experts (Gupta et al., 2025). Reporting expectations for the construction and evaluation phases—abstract content, construct rationale, statistical choices, and the principle that reliability and validity are properties of a score-in-context rather than fixed traits of an instrument—are set out in dedicated guidance (Streiner & Kottner, 2014). The COSMIN program supplies the prevailing taxonomy and reporting checklist, defining structural validity, internal consistency, reliability, measurement error, construct validity, cross-cultural validity, and responsiveness as separately appraisable properties (Gagnier et al., 2021).

Application to algorithmic and AI literacy reveals where these standards bind. The first dedicated algorithm-literacy scale used item response theory across two studies to retain awareness and knowledge subscales (Dogruel et al., 2022). A COSMIN-based systematic review of sixteen AI-literacy scales found generally adequate structural validity and internal consistency yet sparse evidence on content validity, test-retest reliability, responsiveness, measurement error, and cross-cultural validity, with almost no raw data shared and a heavy tilt toward self-report over performance-based formats (Lintner, 2024). A recurring critique across the broader literature is that authors underreport methodological limitations—sample composition, social-desirability bias, and item deficiencies—even when present (Morgado et al., 2017). A new instrument in this space should therefore preregister its construct definition, report content-validity indices and measurement invariance, and favor performance-based items where the construct is knowledge-bearing.
