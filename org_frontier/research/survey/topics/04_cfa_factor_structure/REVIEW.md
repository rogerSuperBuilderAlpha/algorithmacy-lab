# Confirmatory Factor Analysis and Dimensionality

Multidimensional literacy and awareness constructs are typically operationalized through a staged psychometric workflow: exploratory factor analysis (EFA) to surface candidate dimensions, followed by confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to test a hypothesized structure on independent data (Flora & Flake, 2017; Schmitt, 2011). This sequence recurs across AI literacy (Laupichler et al., 2023; Koch et al., 2024), digital and data literacy, and awareness-laden feedback and food-literacy scales, where EFA-derived multifactor solutions are subsequently fit and reported with chi-square, RMSEA, SRMR, CFI, and TLI. A persistent tension is that independent-clusters CFA, which constrains each item to load on one factor, often underfits genuinely multidimensional measures whose items carry secondary loadings (Marsh et al., 2014; Goretzko et al., 2023).

The dominant practice of judging fit against fixed Hu and Bentler (1999) benchmarks (RMSEA ≤ .06, SRMR ≤ .08, CFI ≥ .95) is now substantially contested. Simulation evidence shows these thresholds were derived from one narrow model class and do not generalize, since fit indices are confounded with the number of items, loading magnitude, factor correlations, and response format (Groskurth et al., 2023; McNeish, 2023). The proposed remedy is model-tailored "dynamic" fit index cutoffs generated through Monte Carlo simulation of the analyst's own model, implemented in accessible software (McNeish & Wolf, 2023; Wolf & McNeish, 2023). Tailored cutoffs extend to one-factor unidimensionality tests and, relevant to many literacy scales, to Likert and ordinal responses where continuous-data benchmarks lose sensitivity to misspecification (McNeish, 2023).

Dimensionality decisions carry their own hazards. Using fit indices to select the number of factors in EFA tends toward over-extraction because most indices except SRMR overreact to correlated residuals and minor misspecification (Montoya & Edwards, 2021). For constructs theorized as a broad attribute with narrower facets, such as overarching "literacy" comprising specific competencies, bifactor models clarify whether variance is dominantly general or specific; ancillary indices (explained common variance, omega-hierarchical, PUC) are advocated over fit alone for these judgments (Pretorius & Padmanabhanunni, 2025). Exploratory structural equation modeling offers a middle path, permitting confirmatory testing while allowing cross-loadings that pure CFA forbids (Marsh et al., 2014).

For applied literacy and awareness work, the synthesis points toward several practices: split-sample EFA-then-CFA designs, reporting full fit information, supplementing or replacing fixed cutoffs with dynamic ones matched to model and data characteristics, and treating dimensionality as a question answered through bifactor and ESEM comparisons together with substantive theory.
