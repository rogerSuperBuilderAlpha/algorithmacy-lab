# Folk Theories and Qualitative Foundations

Folk theories name the informal, intuitive, often-incomplete causal beliefs users build to explain opaque algorithmic systems they cannot inspect. The foundational qualitative work establishes that users do reason actively about curation even when the mechanism is hidden: open-ended survey responses about Facebook revealed structured beliefs about why some posts appear and others do not (Rader & Gray, 2015), and a feed-manipulation lab study surfaced ten distinct folk theories, showing that "seams" exposing the system accelerate theory formation (Eslami et al., 2016). Bucher's (2017) algorithmic imaginary reframes these beliefs as affectively charged encounters that themselves feed back into how people use—and thereby shape—the platform.

A second strand specifies how theories form and what governs their content. Folk theories draw on diverse evidentiary sources and prove more multifaceted and malleable than early models assumed (DeVito et al., 2018). Surveys map recurring theory types across populations—confining, practical, reductive, intangible, exploitative in Norway (Ytre-Arne & Moe, 2021), and economic, popularity, categorization, and interaction theories in Germany (Dogruel, 2021). Content also tracks identity and governance: TikTok users articulate an "Identity Strainer" theory of suppressed marginalized identities (Karizat et al., 2021), and shadow-banning discourse circulates as informally transmitted algorithmic folklore in tension with official accounts (Savolainen, 2022).

The developmental bridge to literacy is the topic's organizing claim. DeVito (2021) argues that ongoing, adaptive folk theorization on changing platforms constitutes a path toward an extensible algorithmic literacy, with theorization complexity and perceived platform "spirit" as developmental dimensions. This grounds item content for literacy instruments: the Algorithm Literacy Scale operationalizes awareness and knowledge sub-dimensions traceable to lay reasoning (Dogruel et al., 2022), while the field's status quo and an experiential-learning-cycle framework synthesize how literacy accrues and where measurement diverges (Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum, 2025; Gagrčin et al., 2026). Developmental and qualitative foundations extend to children, whose theories grow more accurate and complex with age yet retain misconceptions about data flows (Starks & Reich, 2024), and to critical-literacy production, where #BookTok creators perform "algorithmic imaginings" that anthropomorphize and negotiate the system (Low et al., 2025). Across this literature, folk theories function simultaneously as the qualitative substrate for literacy item content and as the developmental process literacy education seeks to scaffold, with open questions around measurement validity, cross-cultural variation, and the gap between knowing and acting.
