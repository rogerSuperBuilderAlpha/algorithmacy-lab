# Phi computation tooling and limits

PyPhi is the canonical reference implementation of integrated information theory (IIT), unfolding the full cause-effect structure of discrete dynamical systems of binary elements from a state-transition probability matrix (Mayner et al., 2018). A later release generalized the package to discrete multi-valued (ternary, quaternary, mixed) elements and examined how common binarization choices distort the recovered causal structure (Gómez et al., 2020). The toolbox tracks an evolving formalism: the mechanism-and-system measures of IIT 3.0 (Oizumi et al., 2014), the system-integrated-information measure φs and its sensitivity to determinism, degeneracy, and connectivity fault lines (Marshall et al., 2023), and the restated postulates of IIT 4.0 (Albantakis et al., 2023).

The dominant limit is combinatorial. Quantifying Φ requires searching for a Minimum Information Partition (MIP) over bipartitions while also evaluating cause-effect repertoires across candidate substrates, a cost that grows super-exponentially and becomes impractical beyond roughly 15-20 units (Kitazono et al., 2018; Díaz-Arancibia et al., 2026). Several strategies push the ceiling without changing the formalism. Submodularity-exploiting optimization finds the MIP in polynomial time for some measures and works near-perfectly even for non-submodular Φ (Kitazono et al., 2018). A geometric-topological recasting of the MIP search on the hypercube graph reports 165-326x speedups over PyPhi with near-identical partitions and heuristic reach to about 25 variables (Díaz-Arancibia et al., 2026). Graph neural networks trained on exactly solved 5-7 node systems can qualitatively extrapolate integrated-information and major-complex patterns to larger graphs (Hosaka, 2025). Analytic upper bounds on mechanism integrated information exploit symmetry to prune computation (Zaeemzadeh & Tononi, 2024), and mean-field treatments expose how Φ scales toward critical points in the thermodynamic limit (Aguilera, 2019). Heuristic and approximate measures track exact Φ closely in small binary systems yet do so without major reductions in computational demand, and their fidelity to large systems remains untested (Nilsen et al., 2019).

Applying exact Φ to real sociotechnical systems carries deeper cautions than runtime. System individuation is not given: the formalism must select a grain and boundary, and the value attributed to a substrate depends on whether it is embedded in a larger candidate complex (Marshall et al., 2023; Albantakis et al., 2023). The non-uniqueness analysis shows that the partition and substrate yielding maximal Φ need not be unique, so a reported Φ can hinge on undisclosed tie-breaking (Hanson & Walker, 2023). Earlier critique stresses that intuitively non-conscious feedforward systems can register substantial Φ, questioning what a numeric value licenses (Cerullo, 2015). For an opaque algorithmic third party, these results recommend treating any computed Φ as contingent on stipulated units, grain, time step, and boundary, with sensitivity reporting over those choices.
