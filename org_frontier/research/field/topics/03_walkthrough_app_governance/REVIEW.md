# Walkthrough method and app governance

The walkthrough method is the field's canonical procedure for surfacing an app's control affordances before any user is consulted. As formulated by Light, Burgess, and Duguay (2018), it asks the analyst to first establish an "environment of expected use" by describing three registers: the app's vision (purpose, target users, scenarios of use), its operating model (business strategy and revenue sources, indexing political-economic interests), and its modes of governance (how the provider manages and regulates user activity to sustain the operating model and realize the vision). The analyst then steps forensically through registration and entry, everyday use, and discontinuation, slowing mundane interactions until embedded cultural meanings and implied ideal users become legible. Governance, in this framing, is read off the interface as much as off policy documents.

The governance reading has proven the method's most productive vein. Duguay, Burgess, and Suzor (2018) coined "patchwork platform governance" to describe how Tinder, Instagram, and Vine relied on terms of service and flagging while neglecting the architectures and technocultures that actually shaped queer women's participation. Sun and Wang (2022) used the method to unpack the layered, multi-actor structuration of China's COVID-19 health-code system, treating algorithmic standardization as state infrastructure. Zavala Barreda and Milan (2025) applied a critical walkthrough to Google Play and the Apple App Store, showing how marketplace governance and "industry lore" co-construct children as an audience commodity. Tan and Zhang (2026) trace "contingent affective governance" in an AI mental-health app, pairing the walkthrough's account of intended governance logics with user-side resistance.

Application studies repeatedly use the vision/operating-model/governance triad as their analytic spine. Taylor (2023) reads BeReal's vision of "sporadic authenticity" off its affordances; Poliakova (2022) finds Wattpad's governance subordinates reader-users to writer-users; Riggs (2024) shows Lex's affordances resisting compulsory data collection and binary sorting; Basaraba and colleagues (2019) adapt the method to a corpus content analysis of heritage-trail apps.

Methodological reflection has sharpened since. Dieter and colleagues (2019) reposition single-app walkthroughs within "multi-situated app studies" attentive to app stores, packages, and connections. Duguay and Gold-Apel (2023) catalogue the method's stumbling blocks: datafication, algorithmic logic, and automated personalization (using TikTok) largely elude a procedure built for traceable, screen-by-screen flows, prompting calls to combine it with adjacent methods and a feminist ethics of care. The governance register remains robust where control is materialized in interfaces and documents, and weakest where it is delegated to opaque, personalizing algorithms (Duguay & Gold-Apel, 2023).
