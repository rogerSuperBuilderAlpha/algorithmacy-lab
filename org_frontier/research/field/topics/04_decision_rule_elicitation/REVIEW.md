# Eliciting decision rules from experts

Eliciting the if-then determination logic that experts apply under uncertainty is the core problem of cognitive task analysis (CTA), a family of knowledge-elicitation, analysis, and representation methods (Brown et al., 2024). The foundational instrument is the Critical Decision Method (CDM), introduced as a variant of Flanagan's critical incident technique extended with cognitive probes that surface perceptual and conceptual discriminations, typicality judgments, and critical cues (Klein et al., 1989). CDM asks an expert to recall a single non-routine, challenging incident, then re-traverses that incident across multiple passes, each pass deepening the probe set to expose situation assessment, goals, expectancies, and the cues that triggered action (Hoffman et al., 1998). The multiple-pass retrospective structure is what distinguishes CDM from an ordinary semi-structured interview and what allows tacit determination rules to become articulable.

A persistent finding motivates this work: experts omit much of their own logic when describing tasks, because automated knowledge is not readily available to introspection. Studies of surgical and procedural experts document omission of substantial fractions of indications, decision steps, and alternatives, which probe-driven retrospection recovers (Brown et al., 2024). To lower the method's cost, Applied Cognitive Task Analysis (ACTA) streamlined elicitation into a task diagram, knowledge audit, simulation interview, and cognitive demand table, making the approach usable by designers without cognitive-science training (Militello & Hutton, 1998).

Analysis and representation remain the weaker link. The original CDM publications underspecified how to convert transcripts into structured outputs, prompting Emergent Themes Analysis as a systematic, low-a-priori coding alternative that preserves insights otherwise lost to expectation-driven coding (Wong, 2003). Comparative work shows complementary yields: CDM produces richly populated decision requirements tables while concept mapping captures task structure, and combining methods raises coverage (Hoffman et al., 2002).

Clinical research has become the dominant proving ground. A systematic review of 80 studies found CDM and CTA interviews the most frequent techniques, applied to surgical and critical-care decision-making, training design, and the justification of decision-support tools (Swaby et al., 2022). Recent applications extend the toolkit: an adapted CTA framework structures health-services elicitation into planning, environmental analysis, elicitation, and analysis stages (Graham et al., 2022); CDM scaled to a 102-anesthesiologist simulation study (Militello et al., 2023); and CDM interviews exposed misapplied volume-based heuristics and biases in ICU trainees against a Recognition-Primed Decision frame (Mansoori et al., 2025). Firefighting work continues to yield cue inventories and intuition models from retrospective incident accounts (Okoli et al., 2021), and integrative models now position intuitive and deliberative determination as a single elicited continuum (Gyles & Bearman, 2025).
