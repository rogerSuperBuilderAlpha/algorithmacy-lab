## Summary
This is a conceptual review/position paper that aims to clarify integrated information theory (IIT) of consciousness by distinguishing its genuine achievements from its limitations and from common misunderstandings. The authors first summarise IIT's essentials with reference to an arbitrary "patch" of matter (rather than the usual discrete computational units), describing the key algorithmic outputs: system integrated information φs, the cause-effect structure C, and structure integrated information Φ. They then defend IIT against two misconceptions: that high Φ is synonymous with "more consciousness" (they argue for a multi-dimensional characterisation of global states instead, which defuses the "expander grid" objection), and that IIT's implied panpsychism is distinctively problematic. They then press three substantive challenges: (a) the IIT algorithm is not well-defined for real systems because formulae require a unique next-state distribution that fails under non-Markovian grainings (e.g. brains); (b) only "proxies" for Φ (such as PCI) have been computed, never "approximations" in the technical sense, and the two should not be conflated; and (c) IIT's discrete-unit, discrete-time formalism is incompatible with fundamental physics, which is built from continuous fields, so a field-based reformulation would be needed. The paper closes with a box of five recommendations for advancing IIT.

## Key facts it relies on
- IIT's current version, IIT 4.0 (Albantakis et al. 2023), starts from six "axioms" (properties of conscious experience): Existence, Intrinsicality, Information, Integration, Exclusion, and Composition; the authors note only Exclusion is not obvious from introspection.
- The IIT algorithm's three key outputs are system integrated information φs (where consciousness arises), the cause-effect structure C (the quality/contents of consciousness), and structure integrated information Φ (the level/amount); Φ sums the integrated information across sets of parts in C.
- IIT traces back to the dynamic core hypothesis of Tononi and Edelman (1998, late 1990s), with IIT proper beginning in 2004 (Tononi 2004); IIT is described as canonically non-computational and non-algorithmic.
- Algorithm outputs depend on three "grainings" (Marshall et al. 2024): spatial graining into components, temporal time-difference, and state definition; all quantities assume a maximum-entropy (uniform) prior over states, treating all states as a priori equally likely.
- Empirical support cited centers on the perturbational complexity index (PCI; Casali et al. 2013), which quantifies signal diversity in the EEG response to TMS and is reliably lower in deep sleep and anaesthesia than in wakeful rest, with clinical use in disorders of consciousness.
- IIT links neural architectures to qualia: a non-directed grid connectivity to the spatial canvas of visual experience (Haun and Tononi 2019), and a directed chain to the feeling of temporal flow (Comolatti et al. 2024).
- The well-definedness failure: φs, C, and Φ require a unique probability distribution for the immediate future state given only the present state, which is not specified under non-Markovian grainings; brain dynamics are non-Markovian under typical grainings (Fuliński et al. 1998), so the algorithm produces no output for brains.
- The IIT algorithm cannot use continuous state variables (e.g. membrane or local field potentials) because the uniform/maximum-entropy distribution is undefined for continuous variables (Barrett and Mediano 2019).
- Proxy vs approximation distinction: A approximates B only if both are well-defined and a bound on their maximal discrepancy can be determined (analogy: Newtonian vs general-relativistic rocket trajectory, transfer entropy estimation); Φ proxies typically capture at most two of the six axioms (functional segregation and/or integration).
- "Weak" vs "strong" IIT distinction (Mediano et al. 2022): strong IIT identifies experiences with IIT quantities; weak IIT pursues explanatory correlates valid in specific scenarios; PCI results are compatible with both, and arguably with global neuronal workspace theory.

## Critical notes from the literature
- The authors themselves argue Φ is not well-defined for real physical systems and has never been computed on any realistic model; the only systems used are toy models of finite binary units with memoryless (Markovian) discrete-time dynamics, not realistic models of neurons (which subdivide down to particle level).
- They concede PCI and other empirical measures are proxies, not approximations, so empirical evidence does not strongly test IIT's specific claims and is equally compatible with weaker IIT variants and other theories (e.g. global neuronal workspace).
- Even a multi-dimensional reformulation, the authors note, is unlikely to capture dimensions like metacognition, sense of self, or capacity for suffering, challenging IIT's claim to comprehensiveness; they cite Singhal et al. (2022) on IIT missing the multi-scale nested nature of temporal phenomenology.
- The paper does not contest the axioms or axiomatic approach (pointing to Bayne 2018 for that critique), and addresses Mørch's (2019) argument that Exclusion is inconsistent with Intrinsicality, which they rebut.
- The authors are explicitly agnostic about whether IIT can be revised to be both mathematically well-defined and compatible with fundamental physics; they note COGITATE adversarial tests (Cogitate Consortium 2025) tested only some IIT intuitions and used no integrated-information proxy measures.
