## Summary
This is a short Opinion article arguing that Integrated Information Theory 4.0 (IIT) can keep its mathematics and computational methodology while shedding the ontological commitments that imply our own bodies "do not truly exist." The authors target IIT's claim that only systems specifying maximal system integrated information (ϕ*_s) "truly exist" for themselves as subjective experiences, while all non-conscious physical entities (bodies, organs, tables, rocks) merely exist relatively, for an observer — the so-called "Great Divide of Being." They contend this rests on conceptual conflations (a necessity–sufficiency equivocation and an epistemic–ontological equivocation around "existing for itself" vs. "existing in itself") and generates problematic implications documented in their prior work. As a remedy they propose IIT adopt "causal-physical realism" via three conceptual revisions: reject the principle of true existence (PTE) and its Great Divide; replace the principle of maximal existence (PME) with a principle of maximal conscious existence (PMCE), so ϕ*_s marks consciousness rather than exclusive existence; and replace the operational principle of being (PB) with a principle of realistic being (PRB), "to truly exist is to have causal power." They stress that none of these revisions require any change to IIT's formalism or methodology — only its conceptual interpretation.

## Key facts it relies on
- The paper focuses on maximal system integrated information ϕ*_s, which IIT uses to identify, among candidate systems, the one(s) that support consciousness and "exist for itself"; systems not specifying ϕ*_s, per IIT, at best exist only relative to another conscious entity and "do not truly exist" (Albantakis et al., 2023; Koch, 2024; Tononi et al., 2022).
- IIT 4.0 grounds six "postulates of physical existence" (existence, intrinsicality, information, integration, exclusion, composition) in the Principle of Being (PB): "to be is to have cause–effect power" (Albantakis et al., 2023, p. 11).
- System integrated information ϕ_s is computed as the minimum of integrated cause information (ϕ_c) and integrated effect information (ϕ_e): ϕ_s = min{ϕ_c, ϕ_e} (Albantakis et al., 2023, p. 17, Equations 19–21), using the Minimum Information Partition (MIP), the partition minimizing integrated information.
- The complex is the subset of units within a universe U_k with maximal ϕ_s, ϕ*_s = max_{S⊆U_k} ϕ_s (Albantakis et al., 2023, p. 19, Equation 24); per the Principle of Maximal Existence (PME), "what exists is what exists the most" (p. 11), and overlapping substrates with lower ϕ_s are "excluded from existence" (p. 18).
- IIT's "Great Divide of Being" is "the divide between what truly exists in an absolute sense... and what only exist in a relative sense, for something else" (Tononi et al., 2022, p. 8); it entails that "bodies and organs, tables and rocks... do not truly exist" (Tononi et al., 2022, p. 8).
- IIT's principle of true existence (PTE), per Cea et al. (2023), holds that "only phenomenal existence is true existence" (p. 4); its core intuition is that "consciousness truly exists because it exists for itself—it exists absolutely" (Tononi et al., 2022, p. 9).
- The authors' three proposed revisions: reject PTE/Great Divide; adopt principle of maximal conscious existence (PMCE) — "what exists consciously is what holistically... exists the most"; adopt principle of realistic being (PRB) — "to truly exist is to have causal power."
- They argue the monad/"ontological dust" escape (Hendren et al., 2024; Tononi et al., 2022) fails because IIT's integration postulate requires valid partitions into at least two non-overlapping, non-empty parts; for indivisible units ϕ_s is not computable and must be replaced by intrinsic information, which IIT deems insufficient for consciousness (Cea et al., 2024c).

## Critical notes from the literature
- The paper is an Opinion piece (TYPE Opinion, Front. Comput. Neurosci. 18:1510066), not new empirical or formal work; it explicitly directs readers to the authors' prior papers (Cea et al., 2023, 2024a,b,c; Signorelli et al., 2023) for detailed argumentation and "briefly introduce[s]" the problems here.
- The authors concede IIT's formalism is "comparatively opaque and harder to grasp" than familiar physical frameworks, and that computing the Φ-structure is not feasible for realistic systems due to combinatorial explosion, leaving IIT "testable in principle" only — though IIT-inspired tools like the Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) have given some clinical empirical validation (Massimini et al., 2009; Casarotto et al., 2024).
- A footnote flags a tension internal to IIT: its operational framework tracks state transitions via the TPM/cause-effect structure, while its ontological narrative claims only consciousness "truly exists" and conscious states cause one another — a tension critiqued in Signorelli et al. (2023).
- The authors note their proposal is conceptual only: it requires no change to IIT's methodology or mathematics, so it does not adjudicate IIT's empirical adequacy; the byline is Cea and Signorelli (Negro is acknowledged for comments, not listed as a published author).
