## 1. Studying opaque algorithmic systems in the field
Opacity is the defining methodological obstacle. Seaver (2017) reframes algorithms as enacted "multiples" and offers ethnographic tactics that do not require access to source code. Christin (2020) names three meso-level strategies—algorithmic refraction, comparison, and triangulation—that turn opacity into an object of study. For mediated coordination specifically, Geiger and Ribes (2011) introduce trace ethnography, reconstructing distributed coordination from documentary traces (logs, version histories, transaction records). Light, Burgess, and Duguay (2018) provide the walkthrough method to characterize an app's vision, operating model, and modes of governance. Together these define how a field program can observe a determination rule it cannot directly inspect (Seaver, 2017; Christin, 2020; Geiger & Ribes, 2011; Light, Burgess & Duguay, 2018).

## 2. Eliciting decision and determination rules from people
Where rules live in expert heads, structured elicitation applies. The Critical Decision Method uses multiple-pass retrospection guided by probes to surface explicit and tacit decision knowledge (Hoffman, Crandall & Shadbolt, 1998). The recent cognitive task analysis synthesis consolidates elicitation-in-context and distinguishes decision-focused from skill-inventory aims (Brown, Power & Gore, 2024). These methods map onto extracting the if-then logic that an interested third party enforces.

## 3. Inductive coding from interviews, observation, and documents
Determination rules are abstracted from coded qualitative data. Grounded theory supplies the inductive, comparative coding apparatus (Glaser & Strauss, 1967; Charmaz, 2006). Krippendorff's content analysis adds the unitizing, sampling, and reliability scaffolding needed when coding will feed a quantitative estimate (Krippendorff, 2019).

## 4. Platform and gig-work coordination as the empirical substrate
The program needs concrete coordination it can code. Kellogg, Valentine, and Christin (2020) organize algorithmic control into six mechanisms (restricting, recommending, recording, rating, replacing, rewarding). Vallas and Schor (2020) theorize platforms as "permissive potentates" that concentrate power while externalizing responsibility. Jarrahi et al. (2021) treat algorithmic management as a sociotechnical concept and analyze technical and organizational opacity. Empirical field studies populate the rules: Wood et al. (2019) across six countries; Griesbach et al. (2019) on food delivery "algorithmic despotism"; McDaid, Andon, and Free (2023) on control and resistance at Uber; and Cameron and Rahman (2022) on the co-constitution of control and resistance via a comparative ethnography of two platforms. These supply the interested third party and the coordination patterns a Phi model must represent.

## 5. Exact integrated information as the measurement target
The measurement leg is integrated information theory. Tononi (2004, 2008) defines Phi as information a whole generates beyond its parts. Oizumi, Albantakis, and Tononi (2014) formalize IIT 3.0 from axioms to maximally irreducible cause-effect structure; Tononi et al. (2016) review the physical-substrate account; Albantakis et al. (2023) present IIT 4.0. Exact computation over discrete systems is implemented in PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018), the reference tool any exact-Phi program would use.

## 6. Cautions on porting Phi outside neuroscience
Barrett et al. (2026) emphasize that a high Phi is not synonymous with "more consciousness," that Phi is not well-defined for arbitrary real physical systems, and that exact Phi has rarely been computed on real systems. For a program that applies exact Phi to coded coordination, these constraints argue for transparent system definitions and for treating Phi as one quantity among several rather than a scalar of "coordination amount" (Barrett et al., 2026).

## 7. Bit calibration and inter-rater reliability
Turning codes into calibrated bits requires defensible reliability. O'Connor and Joffe (2020) give practical guidelines for intercoder reliability in qualitative research. McDonald, Schoenebeck, and Forte (2019) document that inter-rater reliability appears in only about one-ninth of qualitative CSCW/HCI papers and propose reporting norms. Krippendorff's alpha is the recommended coefficient for nominal-through-ratio data with missing values, and an open, client-side calculator is now available (Marzi, Balzano & Marchiori, 2024; Krippendorff, 2019). Reliability of the coding bounds the precision of any downstream bit estimate.

## Gap
The literature supports each leg of the program in isolation but not the join. No located source specifies how coded coordination rules—elicited via CDM, walkthroughs, or trace ethnography and stabilized by Krippendorff alpha—are converted into the transition probability matrices that exact Phi (PyPhi) requires, nor how coding uncertainty should propagate into the Phi estimate as a calibrated confidence interval on bits. Equally absent is guidance on system individuation when the "interested third party" is a firm's algorithm rather than a neural substrate: which units, time grain, and intervention semantics make Phi meaningful for a coordination episode. Barrett et al. (2026) flag that exact Phi has essentially never been computed on real systems, so external validity for field-derived TPMs is untested. The program's open problems are therefore the rule-to-mechanism translation, reliability-to-bit propagation, and a defensible individuation/grain convention for sociotechnical coordination.
