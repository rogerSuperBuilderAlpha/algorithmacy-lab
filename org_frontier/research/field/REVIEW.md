# Field research — literature review

Search method: Consensus and Semantic Scholar academic search plus targeted WebSearch/WebFetch across organizational ethnography, platform and gig fieldwork, knowledge elicitation, intercoder reliability, and integrated information theory; every cited identifier was confirmed against a retrieved record. Run date 2026-06-23.

The "field" program of algorithmacy-lab studies how people coordinate through opaque, interested algorithmic third parties, and it measures that coordination with exact integrated information (Phi). Its literature sits at the junction of three bodies of work: methods for studying algorithmic systems in situ, empirical accounts of platform and gig coordination, and the formal and reliability machinery needed to turn observed coordination into calibrated bits.

Method scholarship establishes how to study systems that resist direct inspection. Seaver (2017) argues that algorithms are enacted through practices and should be approached "as culture," giving ethnographers tactics that do not depend on opening a black box. Christin (2020) extends this with refraction, comparison, and triangulation, treating opacity as data rather than an obstacle. Geiger and Ribes (2011) supply trace ethnography, reconstructing coordination from documentary traces such as logs and edit histories, which matters when an interested third party mediates every interaction. Light, Burgess, and Duguay (2018) formalize the walkthrough method for stepping through an app's expected use, vision, and governance. These approaches map directly onto eliciting determination rules from documents and observation. For eliciting rules from people, the Critical Decision Method (Hoffman, Crandall, and Shadbolt, 1998) provides structured retrospection on decisions, and the recent cognitive task analysis synthesis by Brown, Power, and Gore (2024) consolidates elicitation in context.

Empirical platform research supplies the coordination substrate. Kellogg, Valentine, and Christin (2020) frame algorithmic control as a contested terrain organized by six mechanisms of direction, evaluation, and discipline. Vallas and Schor (2020) characterize platforms as permissive potentates that concentrate power while externalizing responsibility. Field studies make the determination rules concrete: Jarrahi and colleagues (2021) treat algorithmic management as a sociotechnical concept and unpack technical and organizational opacity; Wood and colleagues (2019) document algorithmic control across six countries; Griesbach and colleagues (2019) and McDaid, Andon, and Free (2023) trace control and resistance among delivery and ride-hail workers; and Cameron and Rahman (2022) use a comparative ethnography of two platforms to show how customer-mediated control and worker resistance co-constitute one another. These accounts identify the interested third party and the rules it enforces, which is the input any Phi calculation requires.

The measurement side rests on integrated information theory. Tononi (2004, 2008) defines Phi as information generated by a whole above its parts; Oizumi, Albantakis, and Tononi (2014) formalize IIT 3.0 from phenomenological axioms to cause-effect structure; Tononi and colleagues (2016) review the substrate account; and Albantakis and colleagues (2023) present IIT 4.0. Exact computation is delivered by PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018). Barrett and colleagues (2026) caution that Phi is not synonymous with "more" of anything and has rarely been computed on real systems, which bears on a program that ports Phi outside neuroscience.

Bit calibration depends on reliable coding. Krippendorff's Content Analysis (2019 edition) provides alpha and the unitizing and sampling apparatus for reliability. O'Connor and Joffe (2020) and McDonald, Schoenebeck, and Forte (2019) give practical guidelines and document how rarely reliability is reported in CSCW and HCI, while Marzi, Balzano, and Marchiori (2024) supply an open alpha calculator. Charmaz (2006) and Glaser and Strauss (1967) ground the inductive coding from which determination rules are abstracted.

The program's distinctive move is to read coordination through an interested third party as a causal mechanism and quantify it with exact Phi. The literature supports each leg separately; what remains underdeveloped is the bridge that turns coded coordination rules into the transition probability matrices Phi requires, with reliability propagated into the bit estimate.
