# Wave 2 — midpoint (week 4, after the first build-and-review cycle)

Shown during the Wave 2 window, after the first project's build and its peer-review-and-vote week. About
13 minutes. Consent carries over from Wave 1; a participant who did not consent at Wave 1 is shown the
consent form first.

*Intro:* You have now built a project and gone through review once. Answer for how things are for you
now.

## Part 1 — How you read tools and feedback (scale A7)
1. `acs_ci_1` I can usually work out what reviewers want from the feedback they leave, even when they do not spell it out.
2. `acs_ci_2` When a tool or system gives me a result, I can reason back to what it was checking for.
3. `acs_ci_3` I form accurate expectations about what will pass review before I submit.
4. `acs_ci_4` I read past the surface of automated feedback to the rule behind it.
5. `acs_sc_1` I am good at expressing what I mean in the narrow format a tool or reviewer will accept.
6. `acs_sc_2` I can reduce a complex piece of work to the few signals that decide whether it is accepted.
7. `acs_sc_3` When a system accepts only certain inputs, I can package what I intend to fit them.
8. `acs_sc_4` I know which parts of my work to make visible so the system or reviewer reads it correctly.
9. `acs_rt_1` I notice when the rules of a tool or process have quietly changed.
10. `acs_rt_2` I keep track of how requirements shift over time, even when no one announces it.
11. `acs_rt_3` I adjust quickly when a system starts behaving differently than before.
12. `acs_rt_4` I can tell when what worked last time will no longer work.

## Part 2 — The review process and the platform (scale A7)
13. `sa_1` The platform and review process here decide outcomes, not just pass information along.
14. `sa_2` When the system produces a result, it stands, and people act on it as settled.
15. `sa_3` The tools we coordinate through only relay what people have already decided.
16. `sa_4` The review-and-vote process commits a decision that no individual could reach alone.
17. `sa_5` The system only carries our messages; the real decisions are made directly between people.
18. `sa_6` What the platform determines binds everyone, whether or not they agree.

## Part 3 — Working with the cohort (scale A7)
19. `ti_1` I have to work closely with other members of the cohort to get my work done.
20. `ti_2` My work depends on the work of others in the cohort, and theirs depends on mine.
21. `ti_3` Members of the cohort rely on one another for information and materials.
22. `ti_4` I cannot complete my work well without input from others in the cohort.
23. `ti_5` How I do my work directly affects others, and how they do theirs affects me.

## Part 4 — Control over your work (scale A7)
24. `au_m_1` I decide how to go about getting my work done.
25. `au_m_2` I choose the procedures I use to carry out my work.
26. `au_m_3` I am free to choose the methods I use in my work.
27. `au_s_1` I control the scheduling of my work.
28. `au_s_2` I have control over the sequencing of my work activities.
29. `au_s_3` I decide when to do particular work activities.
30. `au_c_1` I have some control over what I am supposed to accomplish.
31. `au_c_2` I am able to modify the objectives of my work.
32. `au_c_3` I am able to influence the standards by which my work is judged.

## Part 5 — What you build (scale A7)
33. `po_1` I feel a high degree of personal ownership of the work I build in this cohort.
34. `po_2` I sense that the project I build is mine.
35. `po_3` This is MY platform.
36. `po_4` I feel the work I build here is mine to look after.
37. `po_5` It is hard for me to think of the project as mine.
38. `po_6` I invest a great deal of myself in what I build here.

## Part 6 — How the cohort knows what it knows (scale A5: Strongly disagree → Strongly agree)
39. `tms_s_1` Each cohort member has specialized knowledge of some aspect of our work.
40. `tms_s_2` I have knowledge about an aspect of the work that no other member has.
41. `tms_s_3` Different members are responsible for expertise in different areas.
42. `tms_s_4` The specialized knowledge of several different members is needed to finish our projects.
43. `tms_s_5` I know which members have expertise in specific areas.
44. `tms_c_1` I am comfortable accepting procedural suggestions from other members.
45. `tms_c_2` I trust that other members' knowledge about the work is credible.
46. `tms_c_3` I am confident relying on the information other members bring.
47. `tms_c_4` When other members give information, I want to double-check it.
48. `tms_c_5` I do not have much faith in other members' expertise.
49. `tms_co_1` Our cohort works together in a well-coordinated way.
50. `tms_co_2` Our cohort has few misunderstandings about what to do.
51. `tms_co_3` Our cohort has to backtrack and start over a lot.
52. `tms_co_4` We accomplish tasks smoothly and efficiently.
53. `tms_co_5` There is much confusion about how we will accomplish tasks.

## Part 7 — Your place in the cohort (scale A7)
54. `su_1` If I dropped out, the cohort's work would carry on largely unchanged.
55. `su_2` Someone else could easily do the part I play here.
56. `su_3` My specific contribution would be hard to replace.
57. `su_4` The coordination here does not really depend on me in particular.
58. `be_1` I feel like I belong in this cohort.
59. `be_2` I feel like an outsider in this cohort.
60. `be_3` I fit in well with the people in this cohort.
