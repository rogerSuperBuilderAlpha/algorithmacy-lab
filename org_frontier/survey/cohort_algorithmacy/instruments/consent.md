# Informed consent to participate in research

**Study title.** Algorithmacy: Validating a measure of communication competency in AI-mediated work
**Principal investigator.** Roger Hunt, Executive PhD student, Management, Bentley University · rhunt@bentley.edu
**IRB of record.** Bentley University Institutional Review Board (IRB#1, FWA00007335) · Protocol 260511078
**IRB determination.** Exempt under 45 CFR 46.102(e)(2)(ii), determined May 11, 2026.
**Consent version.** 1.0

You are invited to take part in a research study. Read this page before you decide. Taking part is
voluntary.

## Why this study is being done

The study looks at how people learn to coordinate their work through software systems — review tools,
project platforms, and automated processes — over a sixteen-week program. It measures attitudes and
self-assessed skills at three points in time.

## What you will be asked to do

If you agree, you will complete three short questionnaires: one before the program's first working week,
one around week 4, and one at the end of the session. Each takes about 10 to 15 minutes and asks how you
work, how you read the tools and the review process, and how you experience the cohort. There are no
right or wrong answers.

## Voluntary participation

Taking part is entirely your choice. You may skip any question, stop at any time, and withdraw without
giving a reason. **Your decision has no effect on your standing in the cohort, your assessment, your
project outcomes, or your relationship with the program or with Hult or Bentley.** Program staff who
assess your work do not see who chose to take part.

## Risks and benefits

The risks are minimal and no greater than everyday reflection on your own work. You may find the
questions prompt useful reflection, but the study offers no direct benefit to you. The findings may
help researchers and program designers understand how people develop skill at coordinating through
software systems.

## Confidentiality and data handling

Your responses are confidential. Your three questionnaires are linked by a one-way coded identifier
derived from your account, so the responses can be matched across time without storing your name or
handle beside your answers. Responses are stored separately from any identifying records and are
accessible only to the research team. Results are reported only in aggregate or in a form that does not
identify you. Only de-identified or aggregated data is shared publicly or deposited in the study's open
repository. Free-text answers are reviewed and any identifying detail removed before any sharing. Data is
retained per the IRB-approved protocol and applicable Bentley University policy.

## Use of an existing platform

The questionnaires are delivered through the cohort platform you already use. The platform records your
responses for this study only and does not change your participant tools, your access, or your
assessment.
