# Review 1 — Set-analytic / QCA lens

*Final pre-submission review for Organization Theory, Special Themed Section "Theorizing the
Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena." Reviewer lens: set-analytic scholarship in the
Ragin/Fiss/Misangyi/Furnari line. Materials read: manuscript and online supplement only.*

## 1. Verdict

**MINOR REVISIONS** — the complementarity argument is sound and the QCA treatment is unusually fair,
but five submission-blocking items (an unspecified QCA-to-Φ interface, an encoding discipline that has
not yet met the tradition's own calibration bar, a load-bearing sensitivity figure whose evidence is a
placeholder, a catalog result the supplement admits is true by construction, and one overstated
sentence about sufficiency) must be fixed before this goes out.

## 2. The contribution, in my words

The essay proposes a within-case criterion for when a combination of interacting elements constitutes
one causal whole, borrowed from the formal core of integrated information theory: a combination is a
configuration when every partition of its elements destroys part of the cause-effect structure (Φ > 0
under IIT 4.0, computed exactly on small Boolean models). From the criterion it derives a computed
boundary (the complex), a graded membership law (pivotality as identity-sensitivity, not voting power),
and three dissolution operators (substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting), the last of which yields a
necessary/contingent distinction that gives power a formal seat. The division of labour with my
tradition is stated explicitly and, I think, correctly: QCA answers which combinations of conditions
travel with an outcome across cases; this criterion asks, of one arrangement, whether it is a single
joint determination at all. Those are different questions, and the essay knows it. The author has also
read us properly — Ragin, Fiss, Misangyi et al., Furnari et al., Grandori and Furnari, and crucially
Schneider and Rohlfing on the multimethod turn, which preempts the reviewer objection that QCA never
goes inside cases. The manuscript concedes what must be conceded (models, not measurements; encoding
sensitivity; the catalog's non-blind coding) and the supplement backs the headline Φ values with full
transition tables under two measures. This is a real candidate for the Catalysing & Crystallizing
section. The remaining problems are specific and repairable.

## 3. Submission-blocking issues

**B1. The QCA interface is asserted, never specified.** Section 6 claims partnership — "across-case
regularity from one, within-case constitution from the other" — but never shows the handoff. A
set-analytic reader will ask the operational question immediately: I have a solution term, say
Fiss-style `HIERARCHY*ROUTINES*~SLACK → high performance`. What do I do with your criterion? Do the
conditions of my term become your elements? Do they become rules? Is the within-case test run on the
typical cases of each term (the cases Schneider and Rohlfing would send to process tracing), and what
does a *factors* verdict on a consistent term mean for my solution — spurious conjunction, or genuine
sufficiency without constitution? The manuscript says "an arrangement can be a QCA-configuration and an
aggregate" (section 6), which is exactly the interesting case, and then walks away from it. Without the
protocol, the complementarity claim reads as a diplomatic gesture rather than a usable bridge, and this
section's editors will hear that from every set-analytic referee.
*Paste-ready fix (insert in section 6, after "…within-case constitution from the other"):* "The handoff
is concrete. Take a completed set-analytic study: each solution term nominates a candidate whole (its
conditions and the outcome-producing arrangement they describe), and the term's typical cases — the
same cases the multimethod program sends to process tracing (Schneider & Rohlfing, 2013) — supply the
arrangements to encode. The analyst reads the term's claimed couplings as transition rules, computes Φ
and the complex, and learns something set-analysis cannot say: whether the recipe names one causal
unity or a consistent co-occurrence of separable parts. A term that is consistently sufficient yet
factors within its typical cases is a finding, not a failure — jointly sufficient conditions whose
joint appearance, not joint determination, does the work."

**B2. The encoding discipline has not met the calibration bar the tradition will hold it to.** The
manuscript's verdicts depend on three analyst choices (application-layer model, state-individuation
rule, party partition), and its stated discipline is "state the load-bearing rules and report the
verdict with its sensitivity" (section 7). That is thinner than what QCA demands of itself. Ragin
(2008) made calibration a theory-facing act with external anchors and published justification;
Schneider and Wagemann's standards of good practice and the subsequent robustness protocols
(condition-selection, threshold, and calibration perturbations) made sensitivity reporting a norm, not
a virtue. Two gaps follow. First, the essay needs an explicit encoding protocol — the analogue of
calibration anchors: for each rule, what observational evidence licenses it, what the defensible
alternatives are, and a standard report of verdicts under those alternatives (the 4-of-10 exercise,
made into a required table rather than an anecdote). Second, and more pointed: every model here is
crisp-binary, and my tradition's central methodological lesson was that dichotomization distorts —
that is why fuzzy sets exist. The essay never says whether the criterion tolerates graded or
multi-valued states, and IIT's formalism does. Silence on this will read, to this audience, as not
having learned the one thing we insist on.
*Paste-ready fix (append to section 7, boundary 2):* "The discipline this essay can state now is a
protocol in QCA's own spirit (Ragin, 2008): for each load-bearing rule, name the evidence that
licenses it, name the defensible alternative encodings, and report the verdict under each — the
sensitivity table as a standard exhibit, not a confession. One choice deserves its own flag. The
models here are binary, and set-analytic method learned the hard way that dichotomization can distort
(Ragin, 2008); the formalism itself does not require it — IIT admits multi-valued elements — and
graded-state encodings of the same forms are the first robustness study the agenda owes."

**B3. The 4-of-10 encoding-flip figure rests on a placeholder.** The figure appears three times in the
manuscript (sections 2, 4, and 7) and does real argumentative work — it is the essay's own measure of
how much hangs on encoding. The supplement's Part C, however, contains no cases, no rules, and no
per-case verdicts, only a promise that the demonstration "will be included in the submission package."
A reviewer who checks (I checked) finds the essay's most-cited robustness number unsupported by the
materials submitted with it. Either the per-case rule pairs and both verdicts go into Part C now, in
the format Part A already uses, or the figure comes out of the manuscript and the sensitivity claim is
stated qualitatively.
*Fix:* populate Part C with the ten case names, both encodings' rules, and both verdicts per case;
failing that, replace "four of the ten flipped under a defensible re-encoding" (sections 2 and 7) and
the section 4 parenthetical with "verdicts can flip under defensible re-encodings, a sensitivity the
submission package documents case by case."

**B4. The catalog's agreement is by construction, and the manuscript still lets it read as a result.**
The supplement is admirably direct: "the computed class follows the structural template by
construction — margin is constant within a template — agreement between coding and classification
checks the pipeline's consistency, not a prediction," and the class tally (25/13/7/6) is therefore a
property of the hand coding alone. The manuscript's section 5 keeps a residue of the stronger reading:
"Applied to a hand-assembled catalog of fifty-one intermediary types… it reads about a quarter as
necessary integrators" — *it reads* implies the criterion did the sorting. The non-blind-coding caveat
that follows is good but addresses a different weakness (coder knowledge of outcomes), not the
determinism. My community has spent two decades being beaten with exactly this stick — post-hoc coding
presented as classification — and will swing it here.
*Paste-ready fix (section 5, replace "Applied to a hand-assembled catalog… identifiable constraints:"
with):* "Coded onto a hand-assembled catalog of fifty-one intermediary types, real and stylized, the
distinction sorts about a quarter as necessary integrators (clearinghouses, exchanges, escrow) and
about half as contingent conduits held in the core by identifiable constraints. The sorting is the
coding: each entry's class follows from its structural template by construction, so the catalog
exercises the distinction's vocabulary on real cases rather than testing its predictions." (Keep the
existing sentences on coder knowledge and the blind-coding agenda; they then carry the right weight.)

**B5. One sentence overstates what QCA claims.** Section 1: "Ragin's (1987, 2008) truth tables and
Fiss's (2007, 2011) fuzzy sets say precisely when a combination of conditions is jointly sufficient for
a result across a sample of cases." No working QCA scholar would sign "precisely… jointly sufficient."
Solutions are quasi-sufficient, admitted at stated consistency thresholds, hedged by coverage, and —
under limited diversity — dependent on counterfactual assumptions that generate conservative,
intermediate, and parsimonious solutions. The essay elsewhere gets our self-understanding right; this
sentence, in the opening section, is where a set-analytic referee forms a first impression of whether
the author knows the method or only its slogan.
*Paste-ready fix:* "Ragin's (1987, 2008) truth tables and Fiss's (2007, 2011) fuzzy sets identify which
combinations of conditions are consistently linked to a result across a sample of cases —
quasi-sufficiency at stated consistency and coverage, not a determinism."

## 4. Non-blocking suggestions

1. **Disambiguate the two Boolean machineries head-on.** The section-2 translation note is correct but
   brief. Both traditions run on Boolean tables, and readers of this special section will conflate
   them. One added sentence would immunize: a QCA truth-table row is a *case* (a configuration of
   condition memberships); a transition-table row here is a *state of one system at one time*. Same
   algebra, orthogonal objects.
2. **Say something about equifinality and asymmetry.** These are the neo-configurational tradition's
   signature commitments (Misangyi et al., 2017). Does the criterion have analogues — can two distinct
   irreducible structures realize the same outcome (within-case equifinality), and is irreducibility
   asymmetric between a configuration's presence and its absence? Even a paragraph conceding these as
   open questions would show the author has walked the whole length of the bridge being offered.
3. **Cite the practice literature the encoding protocol will be measured against** — Greckhamer,
   Furnari, Fiss and Aguilera (2018) on best practices and Schneider and Wagemann (2012) on standards —
   so B2's protocol visibly descends from norms this audience already enforces.
4. **Connect the enumerated model class to property-space thinking.** The "order of a tenth of that
   class" result (section 3) is a claim about a logical space of forms, kin to Lazarsfeld's property
   space and to QCA's limited-diversity reasoning. Naming the kinship would make the "read as a
   property of the model class, not a census" caveat feel native rather than defensive.
5. **Soften "the analyst chooses the conditions in the truth table… has no answer inside the theory"
   (section 1)** — condition selection in good QCA is theory-guided and iterative, not arbitrary. The
   point survives (the boundary is an input, not an output); the phrasing currently shades toward
   caricature.
6. **Terminology:** "the complex" will collide with "complexity" in this section's discourse; consider
   glossing it as "the complex (IIT's term for the maximally integrated subset)" at every first use per
   section.

## 5. Bottom line

This is the rare borrowing paper that respects the tradition it approaches: the division of labour is
stated correctly, the multimethod literature is acknowledged, and the criterion genuinely answers a
question set-analysis does not ask — whether the arrangement in front of the analyst is one causal
whole. I would want my students to read section 5. But the manuscript currently asks my community to
accept a complementarity it never operationalizes (B1), on an encoding discipline looser than the
calibration standard we impose on ourselves (B2), with one headline robustness figure documented only
by a promissory note (B3) and one catalog result that a careless reader will take as a test the
supplement says it is not (B4). All five blocking items have paste-ready or mechanical fixes; none
touches the architecture. Fix them and this submission will get a fair, probably warm, hearing from
set-analytic referees.
