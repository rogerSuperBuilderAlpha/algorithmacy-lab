# General-reader report — final pre-submission read

*Reader profile: organization-studies PhD-trained reader, no specialist background in IIT, QCA, or
platform economics. Read the manuscript in full and skimmed the supplement. No other materials
consulted.*

## 1. Verdict

**Accessible with rough patches.** I followed the argument end to end on one careful read; three
passages made me stop and reread, and one paragraph lost me outright.

## 2. The thesis, as I would state it

Borrowing the partition mathematics of integrated information theory while leaving its consciousness
claims behind, the essay gives configurational organization theory the within-case test it has never
had — an arrangement is a genuine configuration only if every way of cutting it into parts destroys
some of what the whole jointly determines — and from that single test derives who belongs to a
configuration (the complex, graded by pivotality) and how configurations dissolve (by substitution,
bypass, or the lifting of a constraint, which separates intermediaries doing integrating work from
intermediaries holding a gate).

That I can write that sentence after one read is the strongest thing I can say for the paper.

## 3. Section by section

**Abstract.** Skimmed on first pass, understood fully only after finishing the paper. The sentence
"The theory's complex names membership, pivotality grades cores and peripheries" uses two technical
terms as if I already owned them. An abstract can get away with this, but "the theory's complex" reads
as a typo ("the theory's complex *what*?") until you know "complex" is a noun of art. One clause of
apposition would fix it.

**Section 1.** Followed throughout. The motivating question — when is a combination a configuration
at all? — is stated early and plainly, and the three consequences of the gap (boundary set by research
design, coupling with no scale, dissolution imagined as formation reversed) gave me handholds I used
for the rest of the paper. One stumble: "a termination-at-will term, a rating hostage, a unilateral
price" — I can guess what a rating hostage is, but I'm guessing, and this is the paragraph's only
concrete anchor. One small structural note: the paragraph beginning "Platform and contract
scholarship reaches the same problem" and the one beginning "The gap lands hardest" make the same
point (platforms are described clause by clause, with no test for the joint apparatus) twice in
adjacent paragraphs.

**Section 2.** Followed, with effort in one place. The five-piece walk through the machinery is the
best expository writing in the paper — each piece gets a definition I could repeat, and the dispatcher
example makes "mechanism" concrete immediately. The three modeling commitments are honest and clear.
Where I got lost is the measure-versioning paragraph ("One specification note..."). This sentence
defeated me on first read: "the three subtle factorings — the interior quorum, the synchronized veto,
the maximal wiring — do not, because IIT 3.0 registers residual structure there as positive Φ." At
this point in the paper I have not met the quorum, the veto, or the maximal wiring; the sentence
forward-references three exhibits from a section I haven't reached and asks me to care that a measure
I've just heard of disagrees with another measure I've just heard of about them. I recovered the
point on a second pass — *binds* verdicts are robust across versions, *factors* verdicts belong to
the 4.0 measure and the paper says so wherever it leans on one — and that point genuinely raised my
trust. The paragraph earns its place; its detail does not, at that altitude.

**Section 3.** The best section, and the worked strict-mediation box is the reason. Three named
elements, three one-clause rules, three partitions each shown to lose something, and then the
sentence that organizes the whole paper for a non-specialist: "Every other verdict in this essay is
this construction run on different rules." After that box I could follow every subsequent exhibit by
asking "what are the rules, what do the cuts lose." The quorum result is the paper's most memorable
finding, and the paragraph disarming the voting theorist's objection (outcome pivotality versus
identity sensitivity) answered my objection two sentences after I formed it. The robustness
disclaimer ("Φ of zero at an interior threshold does not condemn the design; it classifies it")
answered my second one.

Do the five exhibits stay distinct? Four do. Quorum (busy but factors), rotation (idle-looking but
binds), and dispatch (the verdict turns on whether the commit reads the unseen third party) are
vivid and I could reconstruct each from memory. The veto exhibit and the maximal-wiring exhibit blur
into each other: both open by separating the criterion from "the network surrogate," both conclude
that wiring is not constitution, and by the fifth exhibit I was nodding rather than learning. The
mechanisms differ — lockstep redundancy versus a cheap party-respecting cut surviving full density —
but the prose presents them as the same lesson at two volumes.

**Section 4.** Followed until the coalition case, which I had to read twice: "Consider a worker and
two counterparts coordinating through an all-required commit while the counterparts also read each
other: every node bidirectionally coupled, every node pivotal on a per-node reading, yet the exact
complex is the counterpart pair alone." I got there, and the payoff ("configurations have coalitions
inside them") is worth the work, but this is the section's densest freight in its least scaffolded
sentence — the one exhibit in the paper with no worked rules in sight. The Shapley passage is the
part I skimmed. Its honesty is admirable — "This is a translation, not an independent confirmation"
is a sentence most authors would not write — but for a reader who doesn't already care about
cooperative game theory, two hundred words of axiom talk buy one number I valued: the mediator holds
two-thirds of the strict-mediation triad's integration, each party a sixth. Keep the number, compress
the apparatus.

**Section 5.** The strongest section conceptually and the site of my one outright loss. The three
operators are crisp, the necessary/contingent distinction is the idea I will remember from this paper,
and the brokerage payoff (the admired *tertius iungens* orientation is, carried to completion,
self-liquidating) is the kind of result that makes a borrowed formalism feel earned. But the
five-design-moves paragraph under Bypass lost me completely. The losing sentence: "Decoupling a
dashboard from the committing determination captures her in a display loop that is its own
irreducible pair yet holds neither the commit nor the counterpart she must reach." Who is "her"? No
worker has been introduced in this paragraph; a dashboard has not been mentioned before in the paper;
five distinct manipulations of an unstated model arrive in five consecutive sentences with no rules
shown for any of them. This is the only paragraph I could not repair on a second read. Either work
one move the way section 3 works strict mediation, or cut to two moves and gesture at the supplement.

**Section 6.** Followed the table easily — it is the right device, and "the transmit / transform /
commit ladder" row connected the formalism to a literature I actually read. The paragraph grading
the rows by strength (the game-theoretic row is internal coherence, the others structural
correspondence) is careful in a way I appreciated and dense in a way I skimmed. The closing
paragraph's tour through Kimsey, Emirbayer, and Tsoukas repeats the Tsoukas point section 3 already
made.

**Section 7.** Followed throughout. The encoding-as-power paragraph is the section's best moment — "a
platform that gets its coordination encoded as pairwise contracts has factored itself out of
accountability for the whole, before any computation runs" is quotable and true — and the consciousness
disclaimer is exactly as long as it needs to be. The agenda items are concrete enough to assign to a
student.

## 4. The glossary test

Term by term, in words I could repeat to a colleague:

- **Φ** — pass. "What the least costly partition destroys." Defined before use, and reusable.
- **Complex** — pass in the body ("the subset of elements whose integration is maximal, the set that
  genuinely forms the irreducible whole"); tripped me in the abstract, where it appears first as an
  unglossed noun.
- **Minimum-information partition** — pass. Glossed at first appearance as "the cut that does the
  least damage," which is all I needed.
- **Strict mediation** — pass. Coined inside the worked box, where its meaning is visibly the rules.
- **Commit** — marginal. The noun is coined cleanly in section 3 ("a determination that reads both
  parties jointly and that both parties answer to next step"), but the verb does load-bearing work
  in section 2 first ("a new state begins when the mediator commits a determination"), where I read
  it as ordinary English and only retroactively understood it was the term of art.
- **Pivotality** — marginal. Section 3 distinguishes outcome pivotality from identity sensitivity
  before section 4 defines causal pivotality ("the sensitivity of the joint determination to that
  element's state"), and the abstract uses the bare word before either. The pieces are all there;
  the order is scrambled.
- **Identity sensitivity** — pass, and the best definition in the paper: the commit "depends on how
  many parties are active, never on which."

Five clean passes, two marginals, no failures. For a paper this technical, that is a good score.

## 5. If forced to cut 1,000 words

In order, with rough savings:

1. **Merge the veto and maximal-wiring exhibits into one** ("wiring without constitution," two
   variants, one lesson) (~150 words).
2. **Rebuild or amputate the five-design-moves paragraph in section 5** — keep two moves (opening
   the direct channel evicts the platform; extraction evicts both parties), send the rest to the
   supplement (~180 words).
3. **Compress the Shapley passage** to the decomposition claim, the honesty sentence, and the
   two-thirds/one-sixth number (~150 words).
4. **Halve the measure-versioning paragraph**, moving the named three-exhibit detail to the
   supplement where the tables already live (~120 words).
5. **Merge the two platform paragraphs in section 1** (~130 words).
6. **Trim section 6's closing paragraph** of the repeated Tsoukas/Emirbayer material (~120 words).
7. **Tighten the coupling-scale paragraph in section 3** (the "order of a tenth" enumeration claim
   can be one sentence) (~100 words).

Total ~950, and nothing on this list touches the worked box, the quorum result, the membership law,
or the necessary/contingent distinction — the four things the paper is for.

## 6. Bottom line

**Too-good-to-be-true check.** Three claims triggered the reflex. The catalog's perfect
coding-versus-classification agreement triggered it hardest — and the paper defuses it itself, twice:
the text says the coders knew the outcomes and the exercise "disciplines the distinction rather than
testing it," and the supplement states flatly that agreement is expected by construction. That is the
right way to handle a result that looks like a test and isn't. The quorum knife-edge (irreducible at
exactly k = 1 and k = n, nothing in between) triggered it second; the supplement's E1–E3 tables let me
verify the three-party case by eye, which converted suspicion into confidence. The one number I could
not check is the four-of-ten encoding flips, cited twice in the text as the price of the method's
discretion: supplement Part C says the per-case rules "will be included in the submission package,"
which means the paper's most-repeated sensitivity figure is currently a promissory note. The 660-form
membership gradient (four in ten to nine in ten) is likewise asserted without supplement backing.
Before submission, either Part C ships with the cases or the in-text citations should say the
demonstration is forthcoming.

**Would I cite it?** Yes, for three things. First and most, the necessary/contingent irreducibility
distinction — if I were writing about platform intermediaries or brokerage, section 5 gives me a
concept and a counterfactual test I could state in a sentence. Second, the dissolution-is-not-reversed-
formation argument, which I would cite against the assumption every time I reviewed a paper that
narrates decline as assembly run backwards. Third, the constitution/aggregate question itself as a
framing device. I would not cite it for empirical claims about real organizations, and the paper
repeatedly, correctly, tells me not to.

The paper asks a real question, answers it with a machine I could watch running, and polices its own
inflation better than most theory essays I read. Fix the one broken paragraph, unblur the fifth
exhibit, and back the four-of-ten number, and a general reader will get through this with pleasure.
