# Review 2 — Configurational-canon lens

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** Organization Theory, Special Themed Section "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena"
**Reviewer lens:** configurational canon (Miller; Meyer, Tsui & Hinings; Miles & Snow; Mintzberg; Doty & Glick; Siggelkow)

## 1. Verdict

**MINOR REVISION — the theory holds, but six submission-blocking repairs stand between this draft and a submittable manuscript; none requires new research beyond completing the supplement.**

## 2. What the paper contributes

The paper asks the question the canon has always presupposed and never operationalized: given one arrangement, is it a single joint determination or an aggregate? It answers with a partition-irreducibility criterion borrowed from IIT's formal core, and it earns three genuine advances on the tradition. First, the criterion cuts differently from every surrogate we have used — interdependence, fit, complementarity, wiring density — and the five small-model results (quorum, rotation, lockstep veto, dispatch, maximal wiring) demonstrate the differences rather than assert them. The quorum result in particular, with its careful separation of outcome pivotality from identity sensitivity, is the sharpest formal statement I have seen of why "everyone matters to the vote" does not entail "the committee is one causal thing." Second, the Thompson (1967) treatment is exemplary canon work: the paper credits pooled/sequential/reciprocal as the closest classical ancestor, maps pooled onto the interior-threshold quorum, confirms reciprocal binding, and then produces the closed sequential cycle as a case the verbal typology genuinely has no slot for. That is how one honors and extends a canonical scheme. Third, the necessary/contingent irreducibility distinction gives the section's power theme a formal object, and the self-liquidating tertius iungens derivation delivers something Obstfeld's verbal theory could describe but not derive.

The paper also polices its own borrowing honestly: the Shapley translation is flagged as internal coherence rather than independent confirmation, the catalog is flagged as coded by hands that knew the outcomes, and the IIT 3.0/4.0 divergence is disclosed with the family-robust/measure-specific distinction. This candor is a strength; the blocking issues below are mostly places where the candor has not yet reached.

## 3. Submission-blocking issues

**B1. The level-of-analysis slippage between attribute-configurations and party-configurations must be named and bridged.** The canon this paper claims to formalize theorizes configurations of *attributes*: a defender's strategy, structure, and process (Miles & Snow, 1978); Mintzberg's (1979) design parameters; the gestalts of Miller; the holism of Meyer et al. (1993), whose "parts" are dimensions of one organization. Every worked model in the paper is a configuration of *interacting parties* — worker, mediator, counterpart, units in a cycle. These are different objects, and the criterion's demands (state variables, synchronous update rules, reachable states) transfer to parties far more naturally than to attributes: what does it mean for "centralization" to read "formalization's state" at each tick, and on what clock? Section 1 quotes Meyer et al.'s attribute-holism as the commitment being formalized, then formalizes something else. The paper does not need to solve this — but it must state it. *Paste-ready fix:* add a second translation note at the end of section 2, parallel to the QCA note: "A second translation note. The canon's configurations are mostly configurations of attributes — a defender's strategy, structure, and process — while the elements here are interacting parties. The criterion applies wherever elements and update rules can be stated; whether a typology's attribute couplings support such a rendering is precisely what the canon-encoding agenda of section 7 must establish. Until it does, the verdicts in this essay are verdicts about arrangements of parties, and the extension to attribute gestalts is a conjecture, not a result." Then soften the Meyer et al. gloss in section 3 accordingly.

**B2. The canon-encoding protocol is not yet credible against the paper's own sensitivity result.** Section 7 proposes to test the gestalt claim by reading couplings from a type's own statement and encoding them as transition rules, and declares "the interesting outcomes are the failures." But the paper elsewhere reports that four of ten stylized cases flipped verdict under defensible re-encoding. Given that, a canonical type that factors is uninterpretable as stated: it may indict Mintzberg, or it may indict the encoder. The typologies state their couplings in causal-narrative prose, not transition-rule form, and the degrees of freedom in the translation are exactly where the 4/10 sensitivity lives. *Paste-ready fix:* add to the "Encode and test the canon" paragraph: "The encoding discipline follows from section 2's sensitivity result: two encoders working independently from the type's text; transition rules fixed before any Φ is computed; every verdict reported with the defensible re-encodings that flip it. A factoring verdict indicts the encoding first, and the type only when the factoring survives every defensible encoding of its stated couplings." Without this, the protocol invites exactly the arbitrariness objection QCA spent a decade answering about calibration.

**B3. The supplement does not cover the manuscript's computed claims, and the manuscript promises it does.** The header asserts that every cited number "is exactly computed and reproduces from the model's stated encoding," and section 7 says the supplement gives full tables "for the constitution exhibits." But: (a) Part C is a placeholder — the 4/10 encoding-flip figure, cited twice in the body and load-bearing for B2, has no per-case rules, no tables, nothing but a promissory note; (b) none of section 4's quantitative claims is supported anywhere — the 660-form enumeration, the four-in-ten-to-nine-in-ten membership gradient, the coalition case where the complex is the counterpart pair, the Shapley shares (two-thirds/one-sixth/one-sixth); (c) neither are section 5's five design moves nor section 3's "order of a tenth of the class" census. *Fix:* complete Part C with per-case rules and both encodings, and add a Part D covering the section 4–5 computations — or cut every number the supplement cannot back. A reviewer who checks (this one did) finds the reproducibility promise currently false for roughly half the paper's exact figures.

**B4. "Cores and peripheries" collides with Fiss (2011), which the paper cites without noticing the collision.** In the neo-configurational vocabulary this section's readers use daily, *core* and *peripheral* conditions are a technical distinction — conditions in the parsimonious solution versus those added in the intermediate solution — and Fiss (2011) is its source. Section 4 introduces a computed core/periphery of *elements* under the same words, cites Fiss (2011) twice in the paper, and never distinguishes the constructs. The section's likely editors and reviewers will read "core" in Fiss's sense by reflex. *Paste-ready fix:* one sentence in section 4, after the Siggelkow sentence: "The terms overlap with, but do not translate, Fiss's (2011) core and peripheral conditions: his distinction grades conditions by their place in set-theoretic solutions across cases, while the present one grades elements by pivotality to a single arrangement's joint determination; an element can be core here and its corresponding condition peripheral there, or the reverse."

**B5. Historical attributions need repair in three places.** (i) "Miller (1986, 1996) called such wholes gestalts" misattributes the term's home: the gestalt vocabulary enters through Miller (1981, *Journal of Management Studies*, "Toward a new contingency approach: the search for organizational gestalts") and Miller & Friesen (1984, *Organizations: A Quantum View*); Miller 1986 and 1996 argue the configurations claim but are not the gestalt source. Cite Miller (1981) and/or Miller & Friesen (1984) for the term, keep 1986/1996 for the small-number claim. (ii) "The core/periphery reading this grounds is Siggelkow's (2002): core elements versus elaborating elements, here computed rather than narrated" over-identifies the constructs. Siggelkow's core elements are defined developmentally — by accumulated interactions with subsequently added choices and centrality to fit — not by partition cost; the computation offers a *candidate formalization*, not the same distinction computed. Rewrite as: "a candidate formalization of Siggelkow's (2002) core versus elaborating elements — his distinction is developmental, this one partition-based, and whether they pick out the same elements is an open question the fringe agenda can test." (iii) The supplement's Part B carries bracketed citations — Granovetter 1973, Gould-Fernandez 1989, Hagiu 2009, Rubinstein-Wolinsky 1987, Simmel 1908 — that appear in no reference list, and "Simmel 1908" conflicts with the manuscript's Simmel (1950) translation cite. Add a supplement reference list and reconcile the Simmel dating (cite Simmel 1950 [1908] in both).

**B6. Internal artifacts must come out before submission.** The manuscript's italic header links a companion planning document; the supplement's preamble names "the lab's committed sources," internal module paths (`org_frontier.questions.q215...`), and CI check names. These break anonymity for double-blind review and read as lab notes rather than a submission package. *Fix:* delete the manuscript header block; rewrite the supplement preamble as "All values reproduce from the code and model specifications in the anonymized reproduction package accompanying this submission," and strip repository paths.

## 4. Non-blocking suggestions

1. **Miller (1996) hands you Φ-as-degree — take it.** Miller (1996) explicitly proposes treating configuration as a *variable*, the degree to which an organization's elements are orchestrated by a theme. That is the canon's own anticipation of "degree of configuration, the quantity coupling imagery gestures at, is this number" (section 2). Citing it there converts a borrowed idea into a canon-continuous one, which strengthens the paper's own "the tradition's language has been reaching for" framing.
2. **Dissolution by over-configuration is a canonical mechanism your operators do not cover.** Miller (1993, *AMR*, "The architecture of simplicity") and the Icarus argument hold that configurations destroy themselves by becoming *too* coherent — peripheral elements pruned, the whole tightened until it is brittle. Section 5's three operators all loosen. The manuscript already notes that a tightly constituted whole is fragile; one paragraph acknowledging over-tightening as a fourth, canon-native dissolution path (and asking whether it appears in the formalism as core shrinkage plus rising Φ) would preempt the obvious canonical objection that dissolution here is theorized only from the slack side.
3. **Equifinality is a cheap, natural extension.** The canon's other signature claim (Doty & Glick, 1994; Gresov & Drazin, 1997) is that multiple configurations reach the same outcome. The enumerated model class could say whether multiple *irreducible* forms implement the same input–output function — equifinality among wholes rather than among condition-sets. Even flagging this in the agenda would tie the criterion to the second pillar of configurational theory, not just the holism pillar.
4. **The communication-theory row of the section 6 table is the weakest correspondence** — "transmit/transform/commit" is the authors' own ladder read into Hancock et al. and Kellogg et al. more than a test those literatures draw. Consider labeling it as the paper's proposed alignment rather than a native test, to keep the table's honesty uniform.
5. **E6/E7 (dispatch with and without the rider) replicates under both measures** per the supplement — the paper's most platform-relevant result is family-robust, and section 2's robustness discussion could say so explicitly rather than leaving the reader to check the tables.
6. Small: section 3 reports Φ = 2.0 "at the state where all three are on" for strict mediation, matching supplement CTRL+; but CTRL+ shows Φ = 0 under IIT 4.0 at the other reachable states — a sentence noting that verdicts are maxima over reachable states *in the body*, not only in section 2's specification note, would prevent misreading of "binds" as "binds always."

## 5. Bottom line

This is the rare cross-disciplinary import that respects the destination tradition: Thompson is honored and extended, Orton & Weick is scoped rather than flattened, the Shapley and catalog results are labeled at their true strength. The theory itself needs no major surgery. What blocks submission is the seam the canon lens exposes — the paper formalizes party-configurations while claiming the attribute-configuration canon as its warrant (B1), and proposes to test that canon with a protocol its own sensitivity finding undermines as stated (B2) — plus a supplement that does not yet back half the exact figures (B3), a term collision with Fiss that this section's reviewers will trip over in the first read of section 4 (B4), three attribution repairs (B5), and lab artifacts in the submission files (B6). All six are fixable in days, not months. Fix them and this is a strong candidate for the section.
