# Review 6 — Formal / IIT-skeptic lens

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** Organization Theory, Special Themed Section on configurational theorizing (theory essay)
**Review round:** final pre-submission check

## Verdict

**MAJOR — do not submit yet.** The theory is now well specified and the headline numbers check out against the supplement, but the supplement covers only a fraction of the numbers the text asserts, one load-bearing figure rests on a placeholder, and three flagship "factors" verdicts are stated unconditionally when the manuscript's own tables show they are measure-specific. All of it is fixable without touching the argument.

## Contribution summary

The essay extracts IIT's partition calculus — cause-effect structure, Φ over the minimum-information partition, the complex — and offers it as a within-case constitution criterion for configurational theory: a combination is a configuration when no partition of its elements preserves what the whole determines. From the criterion it derives a membership theory (computed cores and peripheries, graded by identity-sensitivity rather than outcome pivotality) and a dissolution theory (substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting), with the necessary/contingent irreducibility distinction giving power a formal seat. Illustrations are small exactly-solved Boolean models; a supplement gives transition tables and per-state Φ under both IIT 4.0 and 3.0 for the constitution exhibits, plus a 51-entry intermediary catalog.

As an IIT skeptic I came ready to find the usual sins and found most of them pre-confessed. The measure non-uniqueness problem is faced head-on: one operationalization is fixed (IIT 4.0, PyPhi, exact computation), the verdict policy is explicit (binds = positive Φ at some reachable state), the 4.0-vs-3.0 comparison was actually run, and the split it found — every *binds* verdict replicates, the three subtle factorings (interior quorum, lockstep veto, maximal wiring) do not — is reported accurately: I verified E2 (Φ₃.₀ = 1.2688 vs Φ₄.₀ = 0), E5 (0.415/1.415 vs 0), and E8 (0.2767/0.6917 vs 0) against Part A. The consciousness disclaimer is clean, the encoding-dependence is priced rather than hidden, and the specific numbers I could check, checked: the worked triad's Φ = 2.0 at the all-on state matches CTRL+ at 111; the quorum extremes bind (E1 and E3 reach Φ = 3.0) while 2-of-3 is zero at every reachable state; the dispatch pair behaves as claimed (E6 binds at 111 with Φ = 2.0, E7 is zero everywhere reachable); the catalog tally (13/51 necessary ≈ a quarter, 25/51 contingent ≈ half) matches the prose. This is far better formal hygiene than the field's median IIT import. The remaining problems are concentrated in the gap between what the text asserts and what the supplement lets a reader rebuild.

## Submission-blocking issues

**B1. Part C is a placeholder, and the 4-of-10 flip figure is load-bearing.** The encoding-sensitivity figure ("four of ten cases changed verdict under a defensible re-encoding") appears three times — section 2, section 4 ("a displacement section 7 prices"), section 7 — and carries the essay's most important methodological concession. Part C of the supplement contains no cases, no rules, no Φ values: only a promise that the material "will be included in the submission package." For a final pre-submission manuscript that is not a supplement entry; it is an IOU, and it makes the manuscript's own reproducibility banner ("Every number cited as an illustration is exactly computed and reproduces from the model's stated encoding") false as things stand. **Fix:** populate Part C with the ten cases, both encodings per case, and per-state Φ under both measures, in the same table format as Part A — or cut the figure from all three locations and let the encoding-dependence point stand qualitatively.

**B2. The quorum claim outruns the supplement by two system sizes.** Section 3: "computed for every threshold at three, four, and five parties." Part A contains the three-party thresholds only (E1–E3). The sharp result — irreducible at exactly k = n and k = 1, zero at every interior threshold, no gradient — is the essay's single most surprising formal claim, and its generality across n is precisely what a skeptical reader will want to check, since a coincidence at n = 3 is cheap. **Fix:** add tables (or a compact Φ-by-threshold summary, if full transition tables are bulky at n = 5) for k = 1…4 at four parties and k = 1…5 at five, under both measures. Alternatively weaken the sentence to "computed for every threshold at three parties," which would visibly damage the result.

**B3. The section 4–5 numbers cannot be rebuilt from the supplement.** The following quantitative claims have no supplement backing at all: the 660-form strict-mediation enumeration and its membership law (no non-bidirectional element ever enters the complex; membership rising "from roughly four in ten … to nine in ten" with pivotality); the "on the order of a tenth" triadic-binding rate for the enumerated three-party class (section 3); the Shapley shares on the worked triad (mediator two-thirds, each party one-sixth — the arithmetic is internally consistent with Φ = 2.0, but the coalition worths needed to verify it appear nowhere, and the worth function itself is underspecified: how is the "induced sub-arrangement" embedded — background elements frozen, noised, or removed? Different choices give different Shapley values); the coalition exhibit whose exact complex is the counterpart pair; the read-only-manager and owner-exclusion exhibits; and the five design moves of section 5. Note also that Part A reports whole-system Φ only — no complex computations anywhere — so even the worked triad's "the complex is all three elements" is asserted, not exhibited, and positive whole-system Φ does not by itself entail that the whole system is the complex. **Fix:** extend the supplement with (i) rules and complex identifications for every named exhibit in sections 4–5, (ii) a summary table for the 660-form enumeration (form counts, membership rates by pivotality band), (iii) the triad's coalition-worth table with the sub-arrangement embedding stated as a formal definition, and (iv) a complex column in Part A. Where a claim is not worth that cost, delete its number and state it qualitatively.

**B4. The three measure-fragile verdicts are stated unconditionally at point of use.** Section 2 promises that "where it says *factors* against appearances, the claim is about the minimum-information partition, and is flagged as such." Section 3 does not keep the promise: "A majority gate among three parties has Φ of zero, and the collapse admits no gradient," the veto arrangement "still factors," maximal wiring "still factors" — all flat declaratives, and all three are exactly the exhibits the manuscript's own robustness check shows flipping sign under IIT 3.0 (E2: 1.2688; E5: up to 1.415; E8: up to 0.6917). "Admits no gradient" is a fact about the 4.0 partition family, not about quorums. The reasoned defense of the 4.0 choice (a constitution criterion needs zeros; the 4.0 family contains the party-respecting cuts that detect substitutability) is good and should stay — but a hostile reader who reaches the supplement before rereading section 2 will conclude the essay buried its own counterevidence. **Fix:** one clause at each of the three exhibits, e.g. for the quorum: "has Φ of zero — a verdict of the 4.0 partition family; IIT 3.0 registers residual structure here (supplement, E2), which is why section 2 scopes every *factors* claim to the stated measure." Parallel clauses for the veto and maximal-wiring paragraphs.

**B5. The catalog's by-construction circularity is disclosed in the supplement but not in the text.** Part B states plainly that "the computed class follows the structural template by construction — margin is constant within a template — [so] agreement between coding and classification checks the pipeline's consistency, not a prediction." The manuscript says only that coders knew the outcomes ("disciplines the distinction rather than testing it"), which understates the problem: it is not merely that the coders were unblinded, it is that the computation *cannot* disagree with the coding, so "the sort is consistent with the historical record" is a claim about the hand-assigned templates, with Φ contributing nothing to it. **Fix:** paste after "disciplines the distinction rather than testing it": "— and since the computed class is constant within a structural template, the computation cannot disagree with the coding; the consistency with the historical record is a property of the template assignments, which is where the catalog's real content lies."

**B6. The exclusion step is imported without a destination-domain justification.** The essay borrows not just Φ but the complex — IIT's rule that among overlapping candidate systems, only the one of maximal integration exists as a whole. In IIT that maximality rule is the exclusion postulate, and it is motivated *phenomenologically* (one experience, definite borders); it is the least "mathematically self-contained" part of the machinery, contrary to the section 7 framing. Organization theory has no obvious analogue of that motivation: why should exactly one whole win when candidate complexes overlap? Nested and overlapping wholes are organizational commonplaces (a team inside a division inside an alliance), and section 4's own coalition exhibit — where the counterpart pair beats the four-node arrangement — trades directly on winner-take-all maximality. The essay never defends it. **Fix:** a short paragraph in section 4 or 7 that (i) names the exclusion step as a substantive borrowed commitment, distinct from the partition calculus, (ii) gives its organizational rationale (e.g., the complex as the *tightest* bound unit, with overlapping positive-Φ subsets reported rather than suppressed), and (iii) states what the analyst should do when two candidate complexes overlap. Without this, a reviewer who knows IIT will observe that the one piece of the theory the essay quietly kept is the piece whose only known justification is the one the essay disclaims.

## Non-blocking suggestions

1. **Concordance of names.** The manuscript's exhibit names never appear in the supplement: "strict mediation" is CTRL+ (with elements relabeled E,M,R for the manuscript's W,M,C), the quorum is E1–E3, the rotation E4, the veto E5, the dispatch pair E6/E7, maximal wiring E8, and CTRL− corresponds to the "factoring by disconnection" mentioned only in passing. Add the manuscript's name and element labels to each Part A header. Verification is the supplement's whole job; make it a lookup, not a puzzle.
2. **The unfolding argument touches the modeling bet.** Section 2's bet is that application-layer observables suffice: "the model runs on what the coordination does, not on what the algorithm is." The unfolding argument cuts here: systems with identical input–output behavior can differ arbitrarily in Φ, so two encodings equally faithful to the application layer can disagree in verdict for reasons no observation adjudicates. This is arguably the deep version of the encoding-sensitivity result the essay already reports; a sentence connecting them would pre-empt the objection.
3. **Intractability and the agenda.** Exact Φ is superexponential in system size; the essay's six-element ceiling is honest, but the agenda item "encode and test the canon" will need typologies with more elements than that, and approximate measures reintroduce exactly the version non-uniqueness section 2 worked to contain. One sentence pricing this.
4. **Ride-hailing appears twice with different classifications.** Section 3's dispatch exhibit and section 7's "strict-mediation ride-hailing holds the dispatcher in the core" sit alongside catalog entry 46 (ride_hail_platform: additive template, *partial*, margin 1.585). The essay's own distinction (strict vs. partial mediation) resolves it, but a cross-reference should say so explicitly before a reader files it as a contradiction.
5. **State-dependence could be reported, not just declared.** Under the stated policy, CTRL+ binds on the strength of one reachable state in four, the rotation at all sixteen, and the any-one quorum binds only at the all-inactive fixed point — a curiosity worth a remark. Reporting the binding fraction of reachable states per exhibit costs one supplement column and makes the existential verdict policy visibly honest.
6. Small things: the additive-template margin 1.585 is log₂3 — say so; give Φ its units once; Simmel is cited as 1950 in the references but 1908 in the catalog rows.

## Bottom line

This is the rare IIT import that states its measure, its states, and its verdict policy, runs the cross-version check, and reports the split candidly at the headline level. The formal claims I could test against the supplement are true as stated. But a final manuscript cannot cite a supplement section that is a promissory note (B1), claim computations two system sizes beyond what it shows (B2), assert a dozen numbers with no path to rebuilding them (B3), or scope its fragile verdicts in section 2 while stating them flatly in section 3 (B4). B5 is one sentence of honesty the supplement already contains, and B6 is the one place the borrowing still leans, silently, on the part of IIT the essay elsewhere disowns. Fix these six and I would sign off on submission; none requires changing a single result.
