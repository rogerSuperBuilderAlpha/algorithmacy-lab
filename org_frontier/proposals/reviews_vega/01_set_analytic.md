## Part 1 — Theory and structure (§1–§7)

### §1 The unformalized foundation
The opening move is correctly set-analytic: the special section's three questions presuppose when a combination is a configuration, and the manuscript names that gap before selling an apparatus. The literature arc — Miller gestalts → Meyer, Tsui & Hinings → typologists → Doty & Glick → Ragin/Fiss → Misangyi et al. / Furnari et al. / Campbell & Fiss — is the right lineage and is used accurately. Co-occurrence as a surrogate for constitution (lines 66–67) is the sharpest diagnosis in the opening: QCA finds which combinations travel with outcomes; it does not certify that those conditions constitute one causal whole inside a case.

Three pressures arrive quickly.

1. **Outcome constitution vs. system constitution.** Neo-configurational work already formalizes constitution relative to an *outcome*: necessity, sufficiency, INUS/SUIN structure, and the Boolean algebra that falsifies which combinations cover the outcome set (Ragin, 2008; Fiss, 2007, 2011). The manuscript's gap is real but narrower than "the claim has no formal statement anywhere." What lacks a formal criterion is *system* constitution — whether the conditions selected for a truth table themselves form one irreducible joint determination — not outcome-relative causal constitution. The essay should credit the set-analytic formalization it inherits and locate the gap adjacent to it: QCA answers "configured for *Y*?"; the proposed criterion answers "one configuration rather than a heap?" Without that phrasing, a Misangyi/Furnari reader hears erasure.

2. **"Coherence" as a fourth surrogate.** The Meyer–Tsui–Hinings / Miller branch treats gestalts as internally coherent wholes. Coherence (consistency among elements; low contradiction among attributes) is a real surrogate the lineage used, and it sits next to interdependence, fit, and co-occurrence. Its absence from the surrogate list in §1 makes the gap claim look selective. One clause restores it and sharpens the novelty: coherence can hold in an arrangement that still factors under partition.

3. **Asymmetry is overloaded later.** §1 correctly cites conjunction, equifinality, and asymmetry as neo-configurational shape (Misangyi et al., 2017). §5 redeploys "asymmetry" for formation/dissolution. Keep both, but name them differently — *set asymmetry* (presence vs. absence) versus *process asymmetry* (organizing vs. disorganizing) — so the section's own vocabulary does not collapse when the essay adds a third sense.

### §2 A borrowed formalism
The borrowing rule (keep syntax, rework semantics; Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl, 2021) is the right organizational warrant, and refusing consciousness claims early is disciplined. Elements/mechanisms, cause-effect structure, partition, Φ, and the complex are explained accessibly for a methods-literate organization theorist. The application-layer / state-individuation / party-partition commitments keep the criterion inspectable — high value for a QCA audience trained to treat calibrations as load-bearing.

The QCA translation note (lines 192–195) — across-case recipes versus within-case wholes — is the manuscript's most important set-analytic sentence. It should not wait until the end of §2, and §6 should expand it into the essay's organizational centerpiece rather than a division-of-labour coda. Elevate that paragraph as the closing bridge into §3.

One technical caution: Boolean state machines and Boolean truth tables share vocabulary but not semantics. A short notice that "Boolean" here means discrete dynamical transition systems, not crisp set membership scores, would prevent a methods referee from reading the microscope as a disguised QCA engine.

### §3 Constitution: the criterion
"Constitution is causal irreducibility" is a clean positive statement, and the five model contrasts do the needed work. The quorum result is the best exhibit — maximal interaction, Φ = 0 at interior thresholds — because a truth-table cell can look "configured" and still fail as a causal unity.

What remains under-delivered is a **dedicated co-occurrence-versus-constitution illustration**. §1 names co-occurrence as the set-analytic surrogate; §3 never stages an exhibit where conditions that would co-occur as a QCA solution factor under partition (or a complex whose conditions never recur often enough for set analysis). A single worked table — conditions A/B/C as a truth-table row, outcome present, Φ = 0 — would earn the §1 claim for a Fiss/Ragin reader.

Equifinality needs an explicit handshake. Equifinality concerns plurality of *paths*; the criterion concerns unity *within* a path. Several complexes can yield the same *Y*; a single QCA solution term can name an aggregate. That sentence recruits Campbell & Fiss's configurational-versus-hybrid agenda instead of floating beside it.

### §4 Membership: cores and peripheries
The complex-as-output claim is where the essay most directly addresses QCA practice. Fiss (2011) already distinguishes core and peripheral conditions through necessity/sufficiency structure across cases — parsimonious versus intermediate solutions. The manuscript's cores and peripheries are different: causal pivotality *within* a modeled arrangement. Both are real; presenting the Φ-complex as what "configurational theory has lacked" (line 314 region) under-credits Fiss. Required move: one crosswalk paragraph stating that solution-theoretic core/periphery grades conjunctions relative to an outcome set, while complex membership grades elements relative to a cause-effect structure, and that the two can diverge (a peripherally sufficient condition can sit inside a complex; a "core" QCA condition can be a contingent conduit under bypass).

The bidirectional-coupling membership law and the Shapley convergence are strong. They do not replace consistency and coverage; they audit a different membership problem — who belongs to *this* whole once the encoding is fixed. The closing paragraph that makes boundary an "output" rather than an input is right if tempered: the condition list remains an encoding choice; the complex adjudicates that encoding. Say "decidable on a stated model," not "decidable," full stop — matching the sensitivity discipline already in §7.

### §5 Dissolution: operators, necessary and contingent
For a set-analytic referee this is the most novel payoff section. Substitution (threshold drift without wire-cutting), bypass, and constraint-lifting supply operators that formation-backwards imagery cannot. Necessary vs. contingent irreducibility gives power a seat without turning QCA into a power theory — correct altitude. Process asymmetry between organizing and disorganizing answers Quattrone & Zilber without claiming set asymmetry is thereby explained.

Two demands. First, keep process asymmetry lexically distinct from set asymmetry (see §1). Second, connect the operators to set-analytic practice: when a configuration dissolves by substitution, a QCA solution may retain the same condition labels while the case ceases to be one complex — a within-case change invisible to a static truth table. That implication makes §5 speak to method, not only to dynamics theory.

### §6 One test in four vocabularies
The consolidation table is useful, but the **QCA partnership belongs at the center of §6, not as a trailing note**. The generative claim — a QCA-configuration can be an aggregate; a causal unity can miss set-analytic recurrence — is the manuscript's real contribution to configurational method. Pull Campbell & Fiss (2026) into that paragraph as the OT warrant for testing within-case constitution before treating a recipe as a gestalt. Soften "proves itself": Shapley has computation; the other rows are structural correspondences — so QCA is invited as a fifth partnership of method, not swallowed into one "test."

### §7 Boundaries and agenda
The four boundaries hold discipline a methods audience will reward: models are models; encoding is a commitment; consciousness is out; interpretive configurations are out. The re-derive-the-canon / fringe / map-the-constraints agenda is coherent. Missing is a fourth agenda item that set-analytic scholars can run: take a published QCA intermediate solution, encode one or two cases as transition systems, and ask whether the sufficient combination is a complex. That pair — across-case regularity plus within-case constitution — is the chemistry the essay already promises and should name as a research program line.

## Part 2 — Style / slop audit

The prose is cleaner than the drafts house style was written against. Impersonal voice holds. Residual issues are antithesis stacks, a few self-narration tags, and mild opener uniformity.

**1. Antithesis machine — high in §1, then mostly earned.**
The surrogate passage (lines 60–69) runs "X stands in for constitution" thrice and closes with "None states the thing itself." Compress to one positive claim after a single contrast: co-occurrence finds recipes that travel with outcomes; causal irreducibility asks whether those conditions form one whole. Elsewhere the quorum/rotation, necessary/contingent, and level-split contrasts are earned; trim only the recycled "not surface / but structure" beat in the abstract and closes.

**2. Self-narrating rigor — sparse.**
"inspectable rather than asserted" (lines 172–173); "the altitude of the claim should respect" (lines 434–435); "theoretical economy a pluralist field can actually use" (lines 462–464). Delete the virtue tags; leave the substance.

**3. Mechanized openers — mild.**
§3's parallel result labels aid the taxonomy — keep. Vary one of the nearby "The criterion…" openers into a longer claim-first sentence.

**4. Verbatim refrain — "configuration or aggregate / heap."**
Full statement once early; later say "the partition verdict" or "whether the whole factors."

**5. Triads — earned.** Substitution/bypass/constraint-lifting and constitution/membership/dissolution each carry distinct content.

Overall: **clean with local §1 compression.** No first-person leakage.

## Part 3 — Line-level revisions

**R1 — §1, narrow the gap claim (after line 59 / around line 60).** Replace the absolute "no formal statement anywhere" beat with:
> Set-analytic work already formalizes constitution relative to an outcome: which combinations are necessary or sufficient for *Y*, and with what consistency and coverage (Ragin, 2008; Fiss, 2011). What remains without a formal criterion is system constitution — whether the conditions assembled for analysis themselves form one irreducible joint determination rather than a partitionable aggregate.

**R2 — §1, add coherence as a fourth surrogate (in the surrogate paragraph).** Add after the fit sentence:
> Coherence stands in as well: elements are configured when they form an internally consistent gestalt (Miller, 1986; Meyer, Tsui & Hinings, 1993). Consistency among attributes can hold while the cause-effect structure still factors.

**R3 — §1 / §5, disambiguate asymmetry.** In §1, rename neo-configurational asymmetry as "set asymmetry (presence versus absence)." In §5, name "process asymmetry" for organizing versus disorganizing. One pointing sentence: process asymmetry answers the dynamics call; it does not replace set asymmetry.

**R4 — §2, elevate the QCA translation note.** Move lines 192–195 to close §2 as the bridge into §3, and add:
> Boolean here means discrete transition rules among joint states, not membership scores in a truth table. The shared word marks two complementary calculi.

**R5 — §3, add co-occurrence-without-constitution exhibit.** After the dispatch or density result, add a short labeled result: conditions that would fill one truth-table row as jointly sufficient for an outcome, yet admit a party-respecting partition that loses nothing — co-occurrence without constitution. Pair with one line on the reverse case if a model is ready.

**R6 — §3, handshake with equifinality.** Add:
> Equifinality concerns plurality of paths to an outcome. Irreducibility concerns unity within a path. Several complexes can yield the same *Y*; a single QCA solution term can name an aggregate. The criterion grades wholes; set analysis grades recipes across wholes.

**R7 — §4, Fiss core/periphery crosswalk (before the graded-law payoff).** Add:
> Fiss (2011) already grades core and peripheral conditions through necessity and sufficiency across cases. The complex grades a different membership: causal pivotality inside one arrangement's cause-effect structure. A condition peripheral in a solution can sit inside a complex; a condition core to a solution can be contingently irreducible under bypass. The two grades answer different questions and can be run together.

**R8 — §4, temper "decidable."** Change the closing membership sentence to:
> The complex does not retire substantive condition choice. It audits a stated encoding. Once the rules are fixed, membership is decidable on the model.

**R9 — §6, make the QCA division the OT centerpiece.** After the consolidation altitude note, lead with the level-split (lines 466–475), then add:
> Campbell and Fiss (2026) ask when scholars should engage configurational rather than hybrid theorizing. The criterion supplies a prior within-case check: if the candidate whole factors under every faithful encoding, configurational language names a heap; if it remains irreducible, set-analytic work can ask which other cases share which of its conditions. Furnari et al.'s (2021) heuristics gain one formal cycle at the foundation: after the condition list is set, ask whether the listed elements constitute one complex.

**R10 — §7, paired empirical agenda item.** After "Map the constraints," add:
> *Pair solutions with constitution checks.* Take a published QCA intermediate solution, encode one or two high-consistency cases as small transition systems, and recompute whether the sufficient combination is an aggregate or a complex. Across-case regularity from one method; within-case constitution from the other.

**R11 — Style.** Apply Part 2 items 1–2 to the §1 surrogate stack and to the inspectable / altitude / theoretical-economy tags.

## Verdict

**Major revisions.**

The essay finds a real foundation gap and fills it with a criterion set-analytic work can use. The within-case / across-case division is the right contribution for this special section. Three revisions stand between that contribution and a QCA-literate acceptance, in priority order: (1) **credit outcome-constitution and Fiss cores/peripheries** so the gap claim names what is missing (system-constitution) without denying what neo-configurational method already formalized; (2) **operationalize the co-occurrence critique** with a dedicated exhibit and an expanded §6 that puts QCA partnership at the center rather than in a closing note; (3) **restore equifinality and set-asymmetry** as neighboring properties the criterion complements, and add the paired QCA–Φ agenda item that the essay's own division of labour implies. None requires abandoning the IIT borrow; each makes the buy-in for a set-analytic reader lower and the claim more precise.

**Biggest strength.** The clear level-split between QCA configurations (across-case set-membership recipes) and irreducibility configurations (within-case causal unities), crystallized in the claim that a combination can be jointly sufficient for an outcome and still factor as an aggregate — or bind as a complex without recurring often enough for set analysis to see. That is the manuscript's genuine service to configurational method.

**What the author must supply.** A worked **co-occurrence-without-constitution** illustration on a stated model (preferably tied to a recognizable QCA-style truth-table reading of the same conditions), plus an explicit crosswalk between Fiss's solution-theoretic core/periphery and the complex/pivotality grade — stating what each computes and what only the two together deliver. Only the author holding the laboratory encodings can write that exhibit without inventing structure; the review can demand it, not fabricate it.

---

**Final verdict: Major revisions.**
