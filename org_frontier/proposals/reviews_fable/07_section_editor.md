# Handling Editor's Report

**Special Themed Section: Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena (Catalysing & Crystallizing)**

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"

**Report prepared from the manuscript alone; no external reviews consulted.**

---

## Verdict

**Send to review.** With the three revisions below in hand, I would champion it to my co-editors as one of the section's anchor essays.

## Fit against the section's three themes

### Theme 1: Dynamics — how configurations form, stabilize, come apart

The manuscript's contribution here is lopsided in an interesting way: it is the strongest submission I expect to see on *coming apart* and among the thinnest on *forming*.

Section 5 answers the disorganizing question our call raises (via Quattrone & Zilber) with a directness I did not expect any submission to manage. The three dissolution operators — substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting — are derived from the criterion rather than listed, and each names a mode of dissolution that formation-run-backwards cannot describe. The substitution result is the standout: a configuration can dissolve while every tie stays in place, simply because a threshold drifts off its extreme and pivotality collapses. "Dissolution by slack" is a genuinely new mechanism for our literature, and the derived asymmetry — formation is constructive and path-dependent, dissolution needs one operator to fire — explains the years-to-assemble, season-to-unravel pattern that field researchers keep reporting without a mechanism. The brokerage payoff (Obstfeld's *tertius iungens* as self-liquidating when carried to completion) is the kind of result that makes a verbal literature sit up: the formalism derives a consequence the verbal theory could only gesture at.

Formation and stabilization get one paragraph and an agenda item. The essay concedes this implicitly — "theorize the fringe" is deferred to future work, and the claim that early elements shape which later elements can be pivotal is asserted, not developed. I can live with this in a Catalysing essay; the dissolution half alone carries the theme. But reviewers will notice the asymmetry, and the essay should own it explicitly rather than let a reviewer discover it.

**Fit: strong on half the theme, and the strong half is the half the field lacks.**

### Theme 2: Agency, intentionality, and causality — including power

The necessary/contingent irreducibility distinction is, for my money, the manuscript's single most important idea for this section. Two elements can occupy the same core position, produce the same verdict, and differ in constitution: one does integrating work no direct tie reproduces (the escrow agent), the other sits in the core because a constraint forbids the parties to meet (the car dealer under franchise law). One counterfactual — lift the constraint, recompute — separates them. This gives power a formal seat in configurational theory: a configuration held together by power is one whose irreducibility is contingent, and the power *is* the upkeep of the constraint. The account says where to look (the forbidden tie), what maintenance costs, and what dissolution takes. The connection to Rahman's opacity work is well judged: the constraint can be informational, not just contractual.

Causality is served throughout — the whole apparatus is a cause-effect calculus. Agency and intentionality are served much more thinly. The architect gets one paragraph: build necessary irreducibility by taking on integrating work, or manufacture contingent irreducibility by erecting constraints. That is a real result compressed to a gesture. And there is a deeper agency question the essay raises and drops: *who encodes?* Section 2 admits the verdict turns on the analyst's modeling commitments and that four of ten demonstration cases flip under defensible re-encoding. In organizational life the "analyst" is often an interested party — the platform will model its dispatch one way, the drivers' association another, the regulator a third. Encoding is not just a methodological burden; it is a site of contestation, which is to say a site of power. The essay treats this as a limitation (Section 7) when it is theme-2 material hiding in the discussion section. Revision 3 below addresses this.

**Fit: strong on power and causality; agency and intentionality underdeveloped relative to what the framework can evidently support.**

### Theme 3: Dialogue with other intellectual traditions

This is the manuscript's home ground and the cleanest fit in the pile. The call explicitly invites traditions that developed configurational ideas without the label, and IIT is a near-perfect instance: a mathematical account, built in consciousness science, of exactly the question our tradition asks in words — when is a set of interacting elements one integrated whole? The essay handles the import with discipline: it takes the partition mathematics and refuses the phenomenology, and it places the move in the field's own lineage of borrowed formal cores (population biology, transaction costs, NK), citing our own journal's account of how such borrowing works (Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl). Section 7's handling of the consciousness controversy is exactly right in posture: nothing here asserts that any organization experiences anything, and the field's precedents behaved the same way.

Section 6 then multiplies the dialogue: the five-vocabulary table (IIT, cooperative game theory, platform economics, bargaining theory, communication theory) claims that one criterion consolidates tests these literatures draw separately, and — importantly — the essay grades its own claim, distinguishing the Shapley row (computation behind it) from the structural correspondences (exhibited, not derived). That altitude discipline is rare and I want to protect it through review.

Two soft spots. First, the dialogue runs one way: organization theory imports; nothing flows back to the source disciplines. For a Catalysing essay this is acceptable, but a sentence on what organizational cases might teach the formalism would round the theme. Second, and more consequentially: the dialogue with *our own* formal tradition — QCA and the neo-configurational program — is asserted as complementary but not shown to be. See Revision 1.

**Fit: excellent; this is the theme the essay was written for.**

## The methods-trap assessment

Our exclusion of purely methodological contributions has a specific bite for this manuscript, because a computable criterion drifts toward methods on its own. My reading: **the essay stays on the theory side of the line, but three passages lean over it, and the drift is fixable.**

What keeps it on the theory side. The essay's claims are conceptual and it says so: constitution as irreducibility, membership as graded pivotality with a holistic ceiling, dissolution as three operators, necessary versus contingent irreducibility. Each is a concept an organization theorist can use in prose; the closing sentence — the concepts are usable "with or without the borrowed formalism" — is the load-bearing defense, and I believe it. The Boolean models function as the essay says they do, as a microscope: the quorum result, the rotation result, and the dispatch result are thought experiments with exact answers, in the Davis–Eisenhardt–Bingham sense of simulation as theory development. Limited illustrations that advance theory are exactly what the call welcomes.

Where it drifts. Three places read as method rather than theory, and a co-editor skimming for the exclusion will land on all three:

1. **The "three modeling commitments" passage (Section 2)** is written as a reporting protocol — state the encoding, the state-individuation rule, the partition scheme "alongside every verdict." That is guidance for users of a technique, not theorizing.
2. **The enumeration statistics (Sections 3–5)** — 660 model forms, membership climbing from four in ten to nine in ten, a tenth of the class binding triadically, fifty-one intermediary types sorted into quarters and halves — read as *results reporting from a simulation study*. This is the closest the essay comes to data-driven theorizing about its own model class. The numbers are doing illustrative work, but the prose presents them with the cadence of findings.
3. **The "encode and test the canon" agenda item (Section 7)** is a four-step recipe: read the couplings, encode, compute, read the verdict. As written it is a methods proposal.

None of this makes the manuscript a methods paper — the conceptual payload dominates and the essay's own framing devices ("microscope slides," "the essay is theory") show the author knows where the line is. But the three passages hand a skeptical reviewer the wrong description of the paper, and they should be reframed (the protocol as a theoretical point about where constitution lives; the statistics as properties of a model class stated once, without the census cadence; the recipe as a claim about what the gestalt hypothesis *means*, with the steps compressed).

**On the form question:** the essay suits Catalysing & Crystallizing well — it crystallizes (coupling, near-decomposability, complementarity, brokerage, and the mediating technology consolidated under one test) and it catalyses (a criterion, three operators, an agenda, and a provocation: most of what we call configurations may be aggregates). The prose is disciplined and the argument compounds section to section. Its one formal weakness as an essay is compression: Section 3 fires five model results in sequence without ever putting a single model on the page. A reader who cannot rebuild the quorum example is asked to take every verdict on trust, which is a strange position for an essay whose epigraph promises that every number reproduces.

## What would move my decision

**Desk reject** — if the essay were the protocol its three drifting passages suggest, or if the IIT import carried phenomenological freight. Neither is true.

**Send to review** — where it is now: squarely in scope on all three themes, genuinely novel on dissolution and power, with identifiable vulnerabilities that reviewers should test.

**Champion** — after the revisions below, because the necessary/contingent distinction and the dissolution operators are the kind of contribution a themed section exists to publish, and no other tradition-import I anticipate will match Theme 3 this cleanly.

## Revisions that most raise its odds

**1. Rebuild the QCA relationship from assertion to demonstration — and defuse the demotion reading.** The essay's most dangerous sentence for this reviewer pool is the claim that an arrangement can be "a QCA-configuration and an aggregate." The neo-configurational scholars who will review this (and who edit this section) can read that as: your criterion declares our configurations not configurations. The complementarity claim is repeated three times but never *shown*. Fix it with one worked case an OT reader can hold: a single arrangement where set-analysis answers its question (these conditions travel with the outcome across cases) and the criterion answers a different one (this arrangement is or is not one causal unity), with both answers mattering and neither correcting the other. Make explicit that the criterion's verdict says nothing against the sufficiency claim — the two formalisms quantify over different things (condition-sets across cases; mechanisms within one). One page here is worth more than any other page in the manuscript.

**2. Put one fully worked model on the page, and disclose the formalism's own degrees of freedom.** Choose the quorum or the dispatch: elements, states, rules, the cheapest partition, the verdict — one box or figure, readable in two minutes. This anchors every subsequent verdict, converts the Section 3 barrage from trust-me to see-for-yourself, and simultaneously pulls the enumeration statistics back toward illustration (state them once, as properties of a model class, without the findings cadence; same treatment for the fifty-one-intermediary catalog, whose hand-assembled provenance should be flagged). In the same move, disclose that the borrowed core is itself versioned: IIT 3.0 and 4.0 differ in their partition schemes and measures, and "the formal core is mathematically self-contained" elides that the analyst chooses among candidate measures. State which version the illustrations use and whether the qualitative verdicts (quorum factors, rotation binds, dispatch binds) survive the choice. An informed reviewer *will* ask; the essay should answer first.

**3. Promote encoding-as-contestation from limitation to theory, and grow the agency thread to match the power thread.** Section 7's admission that four of ten cases flip under defensible re-encoding is currently a caveat. It is better than that: in real arrangements the encoder is an interested party, and *which model of the coordination prevails* is itself an exercise of power — the platform's model of its dispatch versus the drivers'. Half a page connecting encoding sensitivity to the theme-2 apparatus (whoever controls the description of the joint determination controls the verdict about who is bound) turns the manuscript's biggest vulnerability into a contribution, and it gives the underdeveloped intentionality thread real content alongside the already-strong power thread. The architect paragraph in Section 5 should grow with it: the two design strategies (build integration versus erect constraints) deserve more than one sentence each, since they are the call's entrepreneurial case answered.

Secondary items for the review letter, not conditions: own the formation/dissolution asymmetry in Section 5 explicitly; reframe the three-commitments protocol and the encode-the-canon recipe per the methods-trap notes; one sentence on what organizational cases could return to the source formalism.

---

## Bottom line

This essay does what our call asked for and most submissions will not: it locates a question underneath all three of our themes — when is a combination a configuration at all? — and answers it with an imported formal core, handled with the discipline our own journal has prescribed for such imports. Its best ideas (dissolution by slack; necessary versus contingent irreducibility as the formal shape of power; the self-liquidating *tertius iungens*) are contributions to configurational *theory*, not to method, and the essay mostly keeps its computable criterion on the right side of that line. Its risks are real but revisable: a QCA relationship asserted rather than demonstrated, verdicts the reader cannot rebuild, an undisclosed choice among versions of the borrowed formalism, and an agency thread thinner than its power thread. Send to review with reviewers drawn from the neo-configurational core and from platform/algorithm scholarship; with Revisions 1–3 delivered, I will argue for it as an anchor piece of the section.
