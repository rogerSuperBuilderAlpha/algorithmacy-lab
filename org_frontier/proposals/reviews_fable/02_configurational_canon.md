# Review 2 — Configurational-canon scholar

**Manuscript:** "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and the constitution of organizational wholes"
**Venue:** *Organization Theory*, Special Themed Section "Theorizing the Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena" (Catalysing & Crystallizing)

---

## VERDICT

**Major revisions.** The essay poses a real question the tradition has never formalized and answers it with an imported criterion of genuine interest, but it has not yet shown that the criterion's object is the tradition's object: gestalts of attributes are not sets of state-updating parties, and until the paper confronts that translation, its claims on the canon — including the "encode and test the canon" agenda and the Miller (1996) connection — run on verbal rhyme.

*Register note.* The piece is a formal-theoretic generative essay in an impersonal register, dense sentences punctured by short ones, and it holds that register well. I judge it against that bar, not against a generic empirical-paper template. Fit with the section's format is good: this is a theory essay, not a methods paper, and the authors police that line themselves in section 7.

---

## Summary of the contribution

The essay claims that configurational theorizing rests on an unstated within-case commitment — a configuration is one joint causal determination, not an aggregate of smaller ones — and that the tradition has formalized everything around this commitment (sufficiency across cases, complementary returns, fit, interaction density) but never the commitment itself. It imports the partition-irreducibility machinery of integrated information theory, stripped of its consciousness semantics, as the missing test: a combination is a configuration when every partition of its elements destroys constraint (Φ > 0), an aggregate when some partition destroys none. From the criterion the essay derives three further pieces: membership (the complex as computed boundary, with a graded core–periphery law driven by pivotality), dissolution (three operators — substitution, bypass, constraint-lifting — none of which is formation reversed), and a distinction between necessary and contingent irreducibility that gives power a formal seat. Small Boolean models, exactly solved, carry the illustrations; a closing agenda proposes encoding the canonical typologies and testing whether their stated couplings bind.

The question is well chosen, the dissolution section is the strongest thing in the paper, and the essay is unusually disciplined about what its models are (section 7's four boundaries are better than most formal imports manage). My major issues concern whether the criterion reaches the tradition's object, and whether the paper's engagements with the canon are load-bearing or ornamental.

---

## Major issues

**1. Level of analysis: the criterion's elements are not the tradition's elements.**
When Miller called configurations gestalts, when Meyer, Tsui and Hinings (1993) said parts take their meaning from the whole, and when Doty and Glick (1994) defended typologies as theories, the "elements" in question were *attributes of one organization* — strategy postures, structural variables, process characteristics, environmental conditions. A defender is a pattern of values: narrow domain, cost-efficient core technology, mechanistic structure, financial-controls dominance. The manuscript's machinery, by contrast, operates on *interacting parties* — a worker, a dispatcher, a counterpart — each in a state, each updating by a rule that reads the others. Every illustration in sections 3–5 is of the second kind: quorums, relays, dispatch commits, escrow agents. These are coordination micro-structures, and for them the criterion is genuinely illuminating. But the paper's title question, its abstract, and its section 1 all promise a criterion for the tradition's wholes, and "narrow product domain" does not read the state of "mechanistic structure" at a time step and update by a fixed rule. Typological couplings are largely synchronic complementarity and constraint claims, not temporal transition rules; converting a fit claim into a Boolean dynamical system is exactly the kind of verdict-determining encoding commitment the paper itself proves is dangerous (four of ten cases flipped, section 2), and the manuscript never confronts it for the class of objects the tradition actually cares about. As written, the essay demonstrates its criterion on platform-shaped triads and *asserts* its reach over gestalts.

*Fix (choose one, and I would accept either).* (a) Narrow the claim honestly: the criterion formalizes constitution for *coordination arrangements* — configurations whose elements are interacting parties and systems — which is where the paper's own examples, its platform material, and its section 7 agenda item three all live, and offer the extension to attribute-gestalts as an open translation problem. Paste-ready, for the end of section 3: "The criterion as developed here runs over interacting parties and systems. The canonical typologies configure attributes, and an attribute is not a party: whether a fit claim among strategy, structure, and process can be faithfully rendered as a transition system — and at what timescale — is a translation problem this essay opens rather than solves." (b) Keep the broad claim and earn it: add a worked half-page showing one canonical coupling (pick a single Miles–Snow defender claim) rendered as a transition rule, with the state-individuation and timescale choices stated, so the reader can judge whether the translation preserves what the typologist meant. Option (b) is riskier and more valuable.

**2. The "encode and test the canon" protocol is underspecified, and its trichotomy is quietly collapsed to a dichotomy.**
Section 7's protocol — read the claimed couplings from the type's own statement, encode each element as a state variable, each coupling as a transition rule, compute — is four verbs standing in for the entire hard part. Mintzberg (1979) states the machine bureaucracy across hundreds of pages of contingency claims at multiple levels, many of them probabilistic tendencies; Miles and Snow (1978) state the defender as an adaptive-cycle narrative. Which sentences count as "claimed couplings"? What is the state alphabet of "standardization of work processes"? What does one time step mean? The paper knows encoding is a theoretical commitment — it says so twice — but at the exact place where the commitment is heaviest, the protocol waves. Worse, the paper writes: "a canonical type whose stated couplings factor is either mis-stated or not a gestalt, and either finding advances the theory of that type." There is a third and far more probable reading of a factoring verdict: *the analyst mis-encoded the type.* The manuscript's own sensitivity result (four of ten flipped under defensible re-encoding) makes this the default explanation, and omitting it here converts an honest instrument into an unfalsifiable one — every failure becomes a finding. Finally, the framing "checkable by an explicit procedure rather than a promise" overlooks that the tradition already made the typologies checkable: Doty, Glick and Huber (1993, *AMJ*) operationalized and empirically tested the fit predictions of both Miles–Snow and Mintzberg, with famously mixed results. The novelty claim survives only in narrowed form — checkable *as within-case causal irreducibility*, which is new — and the paper should cite the prior tests and say so.

*Fix.* Paste-ready replacement for the offending sentence: "A canonical type whose stated couplings factor admits three readings — the encoding missed what the typologist claimed, the type is mis-stated, or the type is not a gestalt — and the first must be exhausted before the others are available. Doty, Glick and Huber (1993) tested these typologies' fit predictions a generation ago; what the present procedure adds is a test of a different proposition, that the type's couplings constitute one whole." Then add two sentences specifying, for one concrete type, the state alphabet and timescale the encoding would use.

**3. Miller's "central orchestrating themes" are not core members: a category slip.**
Section 4 closes its Shapley discussion with: "When Miller (1996) argued that configurations have central orchestrating themes, the present account says what centrality is: high average marginal contribution to the irreducibility of the whole." This is the kind of connection I was most alert for, and it does not hold. Miller's theme is not an *element* of the configuration; it is the organizing principle — simplicity, a dominant goal, an entrepreneur's vision — by which the elements are orchestrated, and Miller (1996) in fact proposes treating configuration as the *degree* to which a single theme orchestrates the elements. A theme cannot be a node with high pivotality, because it is not a node. The nearest legitimate counterpart of Miller's theme in the paper's formalism is a property of the whole cause-effect structure (perhaps: the pattern of constraint that the minimum-information partition destroys), not the identity of any pivotal member. What the Shapley result actually grounds is a different and still valuable claim — that configurations have graded cores, and that pivotality predicts coreness — which connects to Hannan and Freeman's core/periphery of organizational features and to Siggelkow's (2002) core elements far more naturally than to Miller's themes.

*Fix.* Paste-ready: "A configuration's core members are its pivotal players. This grades a claim the tradition has carried informally — that some elements are core and some peripheral (Hannan & Freeman, 1977; Siggelkow, 2002) — as computed causal structure. Miller's (1996) central orchestrating theme is a different object, a principle rather than a member, and the present formalism locates its nearest counterpart not in any element but in the shape of the constraint the cheapest partition destroys." If the authors believe the theme *can* be formalized as an element (e.g., a slow variable every rule reads), they must argue it, not assert it.

**4. Prior art on within-case constitution: the claim of "no formal test" has at least one serious rival.**
The essay repeatedly claims the within-case constitution question "has no formal test." Padgett and Powell's autocatalysis program (*The Emergence of Organizations and Markets*, 2012) is a formal, within-case criterion for when a set of interacting elements constitutes a self-sustaining whole — autocatalytic closure — developed explicitly for organizational and market genesis, with organizational-theory citations to show for it. Chemical organization theory offers a cousin. Neither is partition-irreducibility, and I think the Φ criterion cuts differently (closure is about self-reproduction, not joint determination), so the contribution survives — but a paper whose section 6 is devoted to showing that four other literatures draw the same line in their own vocabularies cannot omit the one neighbouring formalism that answers the *same question* rather than a related one. The communicative-constitution-of-organizations literature (CCO) also owns the word "constitution" for a within-case question, unformalized; one sentence of positioning would inoculate.

*Fix.* Add a short paragraph to section 6 naming autocatalytic closure as the nearest rival criterion, stating in one sentence how the two verdicts can diverge (a self-reproducing set that factors; an irreducible set that does not reproduce itself), and adjusting the "no formal test" language in the abstract and section 1 to "no formal test of joint determination."

**5. State-dependence and grain are unaddressed, and both are verdict-carrying.**
In IIT as the paper cites it (Oizumi et al., 2014; Albantakis et al., 2023), Φ and the complex are computed *for a system in a state*, and verdicts can differ across states of the same transition system. The essay speaks throughout of an *arrangement* being a configuration or an aggregate, full stop. Which state? All states? The current one? Averaged? A dispatch triad may bind while a job is committing and factor while idle — and that is either a bug in the criterion's organizational semantics or a genuinely interesting feature (configurations that exist episodically), but the paper must say which. Relatedly, IIT's own literature wrestles with grain (macro over micro can raise integration), and "the encoding is a theoretical commitment" does not yet cover the choice of *level* at which parties are individuated — is "the platform" one element or many modules? Finally, exact computation (Mayner et al., 2018) is superexponential and caps out far below the attribute counts of any canonical type; the section 7 agenda should state that canonical encodings must be small or coarse-grained, and that coarse-graining is itself an encoding choice of the flip-prone kind.

*Fix.* Add a fourth modeling commitment to section 2's list: the state policy (which state(s) the verdict is evaluated at, and what an episodic verdict means organizationally), and one sentence on grain and computational reach in section 7's first boundary.

**6. The quorum result is a construct-validity problem the paper treats only as a triumph.**
Section 3's sharpest result — a majority gate among three parties has Φ of zero — is presented as the criterion cutting deeper than interdependence. It may be. But the majority-rule committee is perhaps the modal joint-determination device in organizational life, and the tradition would not hesitate to call a functioning majority-vote TMT configured with its decision apparatus. When the formal verdict and the tradition's considered judgment diverge this starkly, the essay owes the reader an adjudication: is the tradition's intuition wrong (and what observable difference follows — the paper's substitution-dissolution story gestures at one), or has the criterion changed the subject from "configuration" to something narrower, like *non-substitutable* joint determination? I lean toward thinking the result is defensible — substitutability really is invisible to every existing surrogate — but the paper should argue it as a revision of the construct, with a predicted observable (e.g., interior-threshold bodies should dissolve and reform with distinctive ease), not present it as a discovery about what configurations always were.

**7. The membership "law" is induced from strict-mediation forms and generalized to configurations tout court.**
Section 4's two-part law — bidirectional coupling necessary, pivotality grading membership from roughly four in ten to nine in ten — is enumerated over "660 small strict-mediation model forms." That is a class of mediated-triad models, a platform-shaped corner of the space of organizational forms, and the essay states the law as a property of configurations in general ("The graded law gives configurational theory something it has lacked"). The necessity half is fine — the paper anchors it in the general theory (Albantakis et al., 2023). The graded half is a regularity of one model class and should be stated as such, with an explicit conjecture flag for its generality. Similarly, "on the order of a tenth of that class" in section 3 is handled well (the paper says "not a census of real organizations"); apply the same discipline to the membership law.

## Minor issues

1. **Gestalt provenance.** "Miller (1986, 1996) called such wholes gestalts" — the gestalt language enters this line earlier, in Miller (1981, *JMS*) and above all Miller and Friesen (1984, *Organizations: A Quantum View*), with Khandwalla (1973) behind them. Miller (1986) mostly says "configurations." Cite Miller & Friesen (1984) at minimum; a canon audience will notice its absence from a paper about gestalts.
2. **Missing archetypes.** Greenwood and Hinings (1993) on design archetypes held together by interpretive schemes is directly relevant twice over — to the Miller-theme discussion (issue 3) and to the necessary/contingent distinction (an archetype held by an interpretive scheme vs. by a regulatory track). One paragraph would strengthen section 5's claim to speak to the tradition.
3. **Verification flags.** Campbell & Fiss (2026) is cited as in-press *AMR* — fine if it exists, but the reference must be verifiable by publication; likewise the attribution of the "is disorganizing organizing reversed" question to Quattrone and Zilber (2025) should be checked against that editorial's actual text, since the manuscript leans on it twice.
4. **The communication-theory table row is the weakest.** The "transmit / transform / commit ladder" attributed jointly to Hancock et al. (2020) and Kellogg et al. (2020) reads as the authors' own synthesis wearing borrowed citations. Either own the ladder as this essay's construction or quote where the cited works draw it.
5. **Orton and Weick handled well** — the concession that Φ prices responsiveness and not distinctiveness (section 3) is exactly right and more careful than most borrowings; keep it.
6. **Prose.** The register is strong and mostly slop-free, but three tics recur. (a) The antithesis machine runs hot in section 3: "Connection is not constitution," "siblings, not one idea stated twice," "they do not say whether the traffic binds" — several paragraphs carry two contrast constructions where one would land harder. (b) Section-ending epigrams drumbeat: "the busy quorum factors, the idle-looking cycle binds," "Spectators never belong, however well placed," "With them, the wholes of configurational theory can be told from the heaps." Each is good alone; together they read as manufacture. Let one or two sections end flat. (c) "the microscope slides of the argument" / "the slides the microscope can hold" — the microscope figure appears three times (abstract, section 2, section 7); twice is the maximum.
7. **Small overclaim in section 5.** "a consequence the verbal theory could describe but never derive" — Obstfeld (2005) himself discusses the iungens broker's tendency to work itself out of the position; "never derive" is safe only if the authors have checked that discussion. Soften to "could describe but not derive from a criterion" or verify.

---

## Bottom line

The essay asks the right prior question, and its middle sections — the constitution criterion against its five surrogates, and especially the dissolution operators with the necessary/contingent distinction — are the best formal treatment of configurational coming-apart I have read; section 5 alone justifies a home in this section. But the paper currently buys its relevance to the canon at a discount. Its machinery demonstrably handles coordination structures of interacting parties; its rhetoric claims the gestalts of Miller, Miles and Snow, and Mintzberg; and the bridge between them — attributes rendered as transition systems, at a stated grain and state policy, with the encoding protocol specified past the level of four verbs — is precisely what a revision must build or honestly defer. Fix the level-of-analysis claim (issue 1), repair the canon-testing protocol and its collapsed trichotomy (issue 2), replace the Miller-theme rhyme with the real correspondence (issue 3), and position against autocatalysis (issue 4), and this becomes a paper the configurational tradition will have to answer. I want to see that version.

**Recommendation: major revisions.**
