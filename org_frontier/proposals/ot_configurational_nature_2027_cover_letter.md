Dear Professors Campbell, Fiss, Furnari, and Höllerer,

Please consider the enclosed essay, "When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and
the constitution of organizational wholes," for the Catalysing & Crystallizing section "Theorizing the
Configurational Nature of Organizational Phenomena."

The essay answers a question that sits under all three of the call's themes. Configurational theorizing
claims that organizational phenomena are constituted wholes, and set-analytic method states precisely
which combinations of conditions travel with an outcome across cases. No formal test in the tradition
asks the within-case question: given one arrangement, modeled as its interacting parts, is it a single
joint determination or an aggregate of smaller ones? The essay supplies that test by borrowing the
formal core — not the theory of consciousness — of integrated information theory: a combination is a
configuration when its cause-effect structure is irreducible over every partition of its elements. From
the criterion the essay derives an account of membership (the complex as a computed boundary, with cores
and peripheries graded by pivotality) and a theory of dissolution whose three operators — substitution,
bypass, constraint-lifting — make disorganizing something other than organizing run backwards. The
constraint operator yields a distinction we believe the section's second theme needs: necessary
irreducibility, where an element does integrating work the others cannot reproduce, and contingent
irreducibility, where power holds an element in place by forbidding the others to meet.

On the call's terms: theme 3 is the essay's primary home (a formal tradition from outside organization
studies, developed without the configurational label, brought into dialogue with the configurational
canon, QCA, relational sociology, and conjunctive theorizing); theme 1 is answered by the dissolution
operators; theme 2 by contingent irreducibility and by the essay's argument that contests over how an
arrangement is encoded are contests over its official boundary. The essay is theory. Its illustrations
are small Boolean models, exactly solved, in the role the call assigns to limited empirical
illustration; it proposes no procedure for readers to apply to data. An online supplement provides
every exhibit's transition table, its integrated information under two formalizations of the measure,
and the coding rules of the intermediary catalog the essay draws on, so that every number in the text
can be rebuilt from stated rules.

On novelty: to our knowledge this is the first use of integrated information theory's formal apparatus
in configurational organization theory, and the first in organization theory generally beyond passing
mention. A systematic search of the organization and management literatures conducted in preparing the
essay found no prior application; the nearest neighbours are Kimsey, Besharov, Casasnovas and Höllerer's
(2025) call to move systems thinking from ceremonial to meaningful use, which the essay engages
directly, and Glaser, Sloan and Gehman's (2024) algorithmic metaphor, which names the terrain the
essay's mediated-coordination models formalize. The essay's relation to set-analytic work is
complementary throughout: across-case regularity from QCA, within-case constitution from the criterion.

The manuscript runs approximately [8,500] words excluding references, prepared per the journal's
guidelines. It is not under consideration elsewhere. [Competing interests: none. / Funding statement as
applicable. / AI-use disclosure per SAGE policy as applicable.]

We would be glad to suggest reviewers across the essay's constituencies — configurational and
set-analytic scholars, organization design, platform studies, and formal modeling — if useful.

With thanks for the section and the care in its framing,

## Title decision (recorded)

The question form is the title: **"When is a combination a configuration? Integrated information and
the constitution of organizational wholes."** Rationale: the Catalysing & Crystallizing format rewards
the generative essay; the question is literally the essay's test, applied once per section (constitution,
membership, dissolution); the declarative alternative ("Constitution, membership, dissolution: …") reads
as a survey rather than an argument. Decision taken 2026-07-09; revisit only if the editors' desk
feedback asks.
