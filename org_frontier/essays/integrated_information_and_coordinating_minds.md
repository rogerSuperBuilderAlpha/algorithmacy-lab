# What integrated information adds to the theories of coordinating minds

*A coordination through an opaque, interested third party is something the standard theories of mind
cannot represent. Integrated information can, and this essay says what that representation supplies and
what it leaves alone.*

---

A worker reaching another person through a platform is doing something the theories of cognition were not
built to describe. Each of them models two parties and a medium: a sender, a receiver, and a channel that
passes a signal between them. The platform is not a channel. It reads what passes through, grades it,
withholds it, and rewrites the terms the two parties meet on, and it does all of this in service of
objectives neither party set. Hunt's [paper](../cognition/coordinating_through_the_opaque_third.md) makes
the case that five accounts of mind — classical computationalism, ecological perception, embodied
cognition, theory of mind, and the extended mind — fail at the same place when asked to hold this third
party, and names the competence the failures point to algorithmacy. The failure is shared because it is
representational: a two-party theory has nowhere to put a third party with interests of its own.

Integrated information has somewhere to put it. This essay is about what the formal apparatus supplies to
each theory, stated as what it adds, and about the line past which it has nothing
to say.

## The representation

Integrated information theory is used here for one fact about its central quantity, not for its claims
about consciousness. Φ measures whether a system of interacting parts is irreducible: whether the system,
taken as it is, does more than the sum of its parts considered separately, so that cutting it along any
partition loses information. The cut that matters for coordination is the one separating the worker, the
system, and the counterpart. When the third party only carries a signal, the arrangement factors along
that cut and Φ is zero. When the third party reads the parties and commits a determination of its own, the
arrangement does not factor, Φ is positive, and the major complex — the maximal subset that is irreducible
— names the third party as a member of the bound whole.

This is the representation the theories lack. The third party with interests, which a channel model has
nowhere to hold, is held as a member of an irreducible coordination, a node whose state both reads the
parties and is read by them. The structural skeleton of Hunt's phenomenon is exactly this: a system that
is not a medium but a member.

## What the apparatus adds to each theory

To classical computationalism it adds a criterion and a boundary. The information-processing view casts the
algorithm as a channel that carries symbols and leaves the content alone, and the apparatus shows the
channel to be one corner of a larger space. A system whose next state is a function of the worker alone
carries Φ of zero, for every such function, so no refinement of a channel model produces an actor. As the
system reads its own objective, Φ rises continuously from the channel toward the actor, and the line
between carrying and acting is a gradient crossed by degrees. The variance the computationalist reads as
noise — two equal workers with opposite outcomes — is the irreducible contribution of a third party the
model could not represent.

To ecological perception it adds an account of why the affordance is gone. Gibson's animal perceives what
its environment offers directly, the affordance lawfully present in the light. The worker coordinating
through a platform sees outcomes and needs the rule, and the rule is not recoverable from the outcomes she
can see: a behavioral read of the determination from a run reaches only a little above chance. The
affordance has been taken out of the light, and what remains is reconstruction. The apparatus also locates
the ceiling of that reconstruction. A worker who could observe both inputs would recover the rule exactly;
a worker who sees only her own input and the outcome, with the counterpart hidden behind the system, learns
only the part of the rule that turns on what she can see. The inferential core the paper names is real, and
its bound is the signal asymmetry: what the system reads of the other party, the worker never does.

To embodied cognition it adds a reading of intent compression as membership. Meaning that once rode on a
body and a shared room must squeeze into the system's narrow input, and the apparatus says what survives.
The worker's intent is carried into the bound whole in proportion to how faithfully the system reads her,
the binding degrading with the fidelity of the read. What the system never reads is left outside the
whole entirely. A nuance of the worker's intent beyond the fields the system takes is, when the system
reads it, a member of the coordination, and when the system is blind to it, a part decoupled from the
coordination and outside its core. The distance between what she means and what the system will take is not
a tax on a message; it is the difference between a part that is in the whole and a part that is not.

To theory of mind it adds the phantom as a structural fact. The worker addresses the interface as if a
counterpart sat behind it, and the apparatus shows where her social act lands. She binds to the held
position, the system, which sits with her in the core, while the actual counterpart is read by the system
as a referent and stays outside the bound whole. Something receives her act, since the position takes up
her input, but the person it was for never reads her, so the act never reaches its addressee. The
counterpart is causally read and is not a member, the gap between being processed and being a party to the
coordination. The phantom is not a misfire of mindreading; it is a social act correctly aimed at a position
that holds a counterpart's place and answers to a platform's ends.

To the extended mind it adds governance as core membership. If the platform is a part of the worker's
cognition, the apparatus asks whether the worker is still in the whole that cognition runs, and finds that
she loses her place at a low threshold. The core holds the worker only while her input is the larger share
of what the system commits; once the platform's objective supplies even a small part of the determination,
the worker is displaced and the coordination runs on irreducibly without her. The question the extended
view opens, whether the worker can govern coordination that runs through a system not her own, has a
formal answer: governance is a seat in the irreducible core, and it is lost early.

## The line the apparatus stops at

The apparatus formalizes structure, and the paper's account is not only structural. It says what the
phantom is like to address, how the work of reaching a position that will not answer wears a particular
kind of person down, what it is to pour a decade of work into the terms a system might accept. None of that
is in Φ. The apparatus says when a third party is a constitutive member, how much of the worker's intent
its input can carry, whether the worker is bound into the whole or displaced from it, and where the rule
stops being perceivable. It stays silent on what any of it is like to live. The contribution is a
representation and a set of measurements, and the phenomenal content stays with the account that has the
words for it.

The borrowing is honest in the way the rest of the lab's use of Φ is honest. Integrated information is
taken for its formal content, irreducibility, and whether it is a correct theory of consciousness is a
separate question with no bearing on this use. Nothing here claims a platform is conscious. The claim is
narrower and, for the theories of coordinating minds, more useful: that the measure can hold an interested
third party as a member of a coordination, which the standard theories cannot, and that holding it that way
turns the paper's phenomenology into structure a study could test.

## The exchange

The paper hands cognitive science a phenomenon its theories were not built to handle, a part of the
environment that perceives, transforms, and works toward ends of its own, and a mind that has to coordinate
through such a thing. The apparatus hands back a representation that can hold it and five claims made
precise: the channel is the reducible corner of a space the third party occupies in full; the rule is
perceivable only in fraction and inference is capped by what the worker cannot see; intent is carried into
the whole only as far as the system reads it; the worker's social act binds her to the held position while
the counterpart stays a referent outside; and her governance over her own coordination is a seat she loses
at a low threshold of capture. Each is a prediction a study of real workers could be read against, which is
where the account and the apparatus would meet.
