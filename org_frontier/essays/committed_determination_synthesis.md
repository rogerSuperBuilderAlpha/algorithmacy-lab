# Committed determination: one axis across six exact-Φ studies of coordination

A synthesis of the six papers produced through the lab's research protocol — the core-membership law,
the coordination-logic atlas, the discriminant-boundaries study, the platform-position theory, the
field protocol, and the robustness work folded into the first. It ties them to one structural variable,
maps that variable to its image in five separate literatures, and states plainly what the program adds
and what it recovers. This paper introduces no new computation; it draws on the six studies and the
literature each one verified.

## The claim in one sentence

Every study reduces to a single binary about a mediating system: does it *commit* a determination that
binds all parties irreducibly, or does it *convey* — transmit, transform, or relay — while the parties
remain separable? The exact-Φ dyadic/triadic verdict computes that binary, and the same binary appears,
under different names, in integrated information theory, cooperative game theory, platform economics,
and communication theory.

## The common instrument and the common variable

The lab models a coordination arrangement as a small Boolean dynamical system whose nodes are the
parties — a worker, a mediating system, a counterpart, sometimes more — and reads exact IIT-4.0 Φ over
the minimum-information partition. A form is **triadic** when no partition along party lines factors its
cause-effect structure, **dyadic** when one does. The major complex names which parties are inside the
irreducible structure.

The variable this measures is committed, binding determination. A system commits when it produces an
outcome that depends on the parties together and that the parties must each act on — a determination
neither controls alone. When it does, the parties are bound into one irreducible structure (triadic).
When the system merely conveys, or when a party can be substituted, routed around, or left out, the
structure factors (dyadic). The six studies are six views of this one variable.

## The six studies as one variable

**A — who is bound (core-membership law).** A party is inside the irreducible core when it is
bidirectionally coupled to the determination (it both feeds and reads it) and causally pivotal to it
(membership probability rises with the determination's sensitivity to it). These are the two halves of
"bound by a committed determination": coupling is being in the loop, pivotality is mattering to the
outcome. A party that is substitutable — that the determination can ignore — is not pivotal and sheds.

**D — which logics commit (coordination-logic atlas).** Sweeping the determination-logic space, a
k-of-n quorum mediator binds the parties only at the extremes — unanimity (every party can veto) and
any-one (every party can trigger) — and factors at every interior threshold, where each party is
substitutable. Committed determination requires that every party be individually pivotal, and only the
extreme quorums make that true. Parity logics bind more readily than monotone ones; rotations are
irreducible. The atlas is the catalogue of which determination logics commit.

**B — which mediators commit versus convey (discriminant boundaries).** The neighbouring constructs of
mediated communication fall on a transmit / transform / commit ladder that their own literatures draw.
Computer-mediated communication transmits a human message; AI-mediated communication transforms it on
the sender's behalf (a principal-agent relation, the AI a delegated agent); human-machine communication
is a two-party dyad; directive algorithmic management commits a determination workers must heed. The Φ
verdict reads the convey constructs dyadic and the commit construct triadic. Algorithmacy is the
committing pole.

**F — when the system itself is bound (platform position).** A mediating platform is in the irreducible
core exactly when the parties have no outside option — no substitute path to each other. Asymmetric
outside options lock in the party without one (capture); symmetric options bypass the platform; a
substitute direct tie disintermediates it. The platform is bound into the committed determination only
when it is the parties' only route; an alternative makes it conveying, not committing, and it drops out.

**E — taking the variable to the field (field protocol).** A method for modeling one real coordination
arrangement as a Boolean form and computing the verdict, with a mandatory sensitivity step (because many
models fit one account) and an explicit validation gap (the verdict is exact for the model, not a
measurement of the organization). It places the apparatus in the computational-organization-theory
tradition and uses Φ strictly as a structural-irreducibility statistic, with no consciousness claim.

**C — how stable the verdict is (robustness, folded into A).** The verdict's robustness to encoding,
grain, and aggregation was tested alongside the membership law; the conditions hold in the construct's
natural domain, and the population-dependence and edge cases are reported there.

## The convergence: one verdict, five literatures

The deep-research pass on each study found that the committed-determination axis is not the lab's
discovery. It is the same distinction five literatures already draw, and the Φ verdict is a common
computational image of all of them.

| The Φ verdict says | bound / triadic | separable / dyadic | established image |
|--------------------|-----------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **IIT (A, D)** | a unit in the complex (bidirectional, irreducible) | a unit excluded; a reducible partition | the exclusion postulate; φ_s = min(φ_c, φ_e); PyPhi's causally-significant nodes |
| **Cooperative game theory (A, D)** | a pivotal player (positive marginal contribution) | a null / substitutable player | the Shapley value; the Null Player and Symmetry axioms; the O-ring |
| **Platform economics (F)** | a single-route bottleneck; the captured no-option side | a multi-homing, bypassable, substitutable side | Armstrong's competitive bottleneck; Bakos–Halaburda |
| **Bargaining theory (F)** | a party held to its non-binding default (residual claimant against it) | a party with a binding outside option | the Binmore–Shaked–Sutton outside-option principle; network-exchange power |
| **Communication theory (B)** | a system that commits a determination both must heed | a system that transmits or transforms a message | the transmit / transform / commit ladder; algorithmic management's directive mechanisms |

Read down any column and it is the same line: pivotal versus substitutable, sole-route versus
bypassable, commit versus convey, bound versus separable. The exact-Φ verdict is the line drawn
computationally, on an explicit dynamical model, rather than verbally or in a closed-form economic
model.

## What the program adds, and what it recovers

The honest accounting, made possible only by running the literature on each study, is that most of the
individual laws are recoveries.

- **Recovered.** The necessity of bidirectional coupling is IIT's own requirement, enforced by the
  instrument (A). Pivotality and substitutability are the Shapley value's (A, D). The platform-position
  theory is, in its economic content, Armstrong's competitive bottleneck and the outside-option
  principle (F). The commit-versus-convey distinction is the communication literature's own (B). Parity
  binding more readily than monotone logic, and rotation being irreducible, are prior IIT results (D).
- **Genuinely new.** Two pieces survived the literature. The graded membership law — that across a
  population of forms, complex membership rises monotonically with causal pivotality, where IIT's
  per-form membership is strictly binary — bridges IIT's exclusion to the Shapley value, and is stated
  in neither (A). The extremes-only quorum law — that a k-of-n mediator is irreducible only at k = 1
  and k = n — appears as a named result in no surveyed source, though it composes known pieces (D).
- **The bridge itself.** The program's central contribution is neither of those small results. It is
  the convergence: a single exact, reproducible criterion — Φ irreducibility on a Boolean coordination
  model — that turns out to be the common image of pivotality, bottleneck power, the outside-option
  principle, and committed determination, results that live separately in four disciplines. Each
  discipline draws the line in its own terms and its own models; the program shows they are one line,
  and computes it.

## Why this is worth having

A construct earns its place by doing work no existing one does. The committed-determination axis does
not: each discipline already has it. What the program supplies is a single computable test for it that
crosses the disciplines — one that takes a coordination arrangement, in any of these vocabularies, and
returns the same exact verdict, with the parties bound by the determination named explicitly as the
major complex. The discriminant study shows the test sorts communication constructs; the platform study
shows it sorts platform positions; the atlas shows it sorts determination logics; the membership law
shows what it is measuring. The field protocol shows how to point it at a real arrangement, and what it
may and may not then claim.

## Limitations

Everything here is in-silico, on small Boolean models of coordination; the verdicts are exact for the
models and are evidence about the models, not measurements of organizations. Φ is read only as the
binary verdict and the major complex — its magnitude is not a reliable scale — and it is used as a
structural-irreducibility statistic with no consciousness claim, a point the contested status of IIT as
a theory of consciousness does not touch. The cross-disciplinary convergence is shown by coincidence of
verdicts, not derived: the program demonstrates that the Φ verdict and the established results agree on
coordination models, not that one implies another. And the validation gap is unclosed — no real worker,
platform, or message has been measured. The next contribution is a real case, run through the field
protocol, that breaks one of these models and shows where the axis bends.
