# The Boolean corpus is a pre-disclosed prior catalog

Every study in this lab ends by conceding that its forms are Boolean models, not organizations, and that the
bridge to real coordination is unbuilt. Read as an apology, that concession misstates the work. The Boolean
models are not a stand-in for organizations waiting to be replaced by them. They are the baselines — the
priors — against which real data will be read, and building them first, in quantity, before any real
coordination is measured, is the method, not a delay in it.

## What a form is, in this reading

A coordination form is a stated set of assumptions about who reads whom and how they update, carried to an
exact Φ verdict and an exact cooperative-game structure. It is a prior: under these couplings and these
rules, the integration looks like this, the major complex is this set, the credit splits this way, the core
is empty or not. The verdict is a prediction about a class of arrangements, fixed by the model and computed,
never tuned. A library of such forms is a library of priors, each one a pre-disclosed expectation about a
recognizable kind of coordination — a strict bottleneck, a broadcast, a two-sided match, a chain.

The point of having many is coverage. A single prior tests one expectation. A catalog that spans the
couplings — strict mediation and back-channels, bidirectional and forward-only, three parties and four,
structured and random — lets a real arrangement be placed against the nearest pre-disclosed context rather
than against a model built after the data are in hand. The random-Boolean population and the
strict-mediation family are two priors, and the contrast between them is itself a registered expectation:
the [structured-forms thread](../threads/structured_forms/THREAD.md) records that a mediated triad
has a stable core and an arbitrary coupling does not, so a real coordination whose credit will not divide is
evidence of one regime and not the other, decided in advance.

## Why the order matters

The reason to commit the priors before the data is the reason the field protocol pre-registers a verdict
before computing it: a prediction fixed after the number is not a prediction. Done one arrangement at a
time, this is the protocol's fifth step, write the expected verdict and why, then compute. Done across a
program, it is a catalog: the space of expectations is mapped, with each region pinned to an exact and
reproducible number, before a single real coordination is read. When real data arrive, the analysis asks
which pre-disclosed context the arrangement falls in and whether it behaves as that context predicted. The
alternative — meeting the data first, then reaching for whichever model fits — is the garden of forking
paths, where a model chosen to match the data confirms only that a model can be chosen. A vast pre-disclosed
prior set closes that path. It is the discipline that lets exploration of real data be confirmatory rather
than invented at the moment of need.

## What this asks of the program

Read this way, the value of a thread is not only its result but its disclosure: a documented coupling, a
fixed verdict, a reproducible number under the CI gate, a named place in the directory. The reproduce gate
and the catalog are the registration mechanism — a prior that cannot be reproduced is not registered, and a
prior added after the data would not be a prior at all. So the program's task in this phase is breadth and
honesty of disclosure: more contexts, each stated before it is needed, each falsifiable, each carrying what
would count against it. The cooperative-game cluster, the coordination-logic atlas, the discriminant
studies, the architecture threads — these are not a search for a single law. They are the catalog being
filled in.

## What it does not license

A prior stays a prior, and calling the corpus a prior catalog states the honest limit correctly rather than
retiring it. The numbers here are exact facts about the models and expectations
about the world, and a prior is meant to be moved by data. The catalog can be wrong in two ways the data
will expose: a context the catalog did not anticipate, and a registered expectation that real arrangements
violate. Both are the point of registering first, since both are legible only against a baseline fixed in
advance. The strong claim the corpus can carry is that the expectations are disclosed, reproducible, and
broad; the claim it cannot carry yet is that any of them holds of a real organization. That is for the field
protocol to test, one arrangement at a time, against the priors assembled here.

## The shape of the program

In this light the program has two phases and is honest about being in the first. The first builds the
priors: a wide, documented, reproducible catalog of coordination forms and their exact integration and
game-theoretic structure, with every expectation fixed before it is used. The second reads real
coordinations against that catalog through the field protocol, updating the priors where the world disagrees.
The in-silico work earns its place as the phase that has to come first, and the more of it there is — the
more contexts pre-disclosed — the less the eventual encounter with real data can be talked into whatever
story the data happen to suggest.
