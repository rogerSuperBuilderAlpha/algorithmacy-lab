# What survives disintermediation

The internet lowered the cost of a direct tie. Two strangers can find each other, verify each other, and
transact without the party that used to stand between them. For thirty years this has removed intermediaries —
the travel agent, the newspaper classified, the record label, the stockbroker's commission. It has also left
intermediaries standing that look just as removable and are not — the car dealer, the title company, the app
store taking thirty percent. The irreducibility catalog gives the reason. Whether an intermediary survives the
opening of the direct tie is read off how it sits in the coordination's irreducible core, and the
bypass-counterfactual reads that off in one operation.

## The test is the prediction

q213's bypass-counterfactual restores the forbidden direct edge between the two parties a third stands between
and asks whether the third survives. Disintermediation is that same operation run by the world. The internet
restores the direct edge; the question is which third parties leave the core when it does. The four classes are
four fates.

A **reducible** intermediary is already out of the core. Nothing holds the direct tie shut except the cost of
finding and trusting a stranger, and that is exactly the cost the internet lowered. The newspaper classified
section sat between a seller and a buyer who could not find each other cheaply; Craigslist made the finding
free and the section was gone. The record label sat between an artist and a listener who had no other
distribution; streaming and direct upload opened the channel and, for an artist who can work it, the label
dropped out. The retail middleman sat on an access barrier that direct-to-consumer brands walked around. None of
these was held by anything but a friction, and a friction is what the internet removes.

A **contingent** intermediary is in the core, but only because a constraint forbids the direct tie — and the
internet cannot lower a constraint it does not control. The car dealer relays a car and integrates nothing, and
it is still here because franchise law still forbids the manufacturer selling direct. The bail bondsman is here
because cash bail is still the law. The app store takes its cut because the device platform still forbids
reaching its users any other way. These intermediaries are as removable as the classified section in causal
terms — restore the tie and they vanish — and they survive because the tie is held shut by a law, a monopoly, a
standard, a contract, not by a friction. The catalog records which: franchise statute, three-tier law, cash
bail, walled garden, exclusive venue contract, root authority, interbank standard, collective-bargaining
agreement. The survival of an intermediary through the internet era is a measure of how durable its constraint
was.

A **necessary** intermediary integrates a joint condition the direct tie cannot reproduce, so the bypass never
threatened it. The clearinghouse novates both sides of a trade; two counterparties clearing bilaterally do not
get novation. The exchange matches a buy and a sell into one price; the two trading over the counter do not get
the order book. Air traffic control deconflicts the whole airspace; two pilots talking do not. These did not
have to be protected from disintermediation, because the direct tie was never a substitute for what they do.

## The contested middle

The interesting cases are **partial** — real integration beside a bypassable gate — and these are exactly the
intermediaries fought over now. The pharmacy benefit manager negotiates a formulary, which is integrating work,
and also gates rebates, which is a removable toll, and the transparent-PBM movement is an attempt to open the
second while keeping the first. The ride-hail platform matches a rider and a driver in real time, which the pair
cannot reproduce, and also stands between them in a way they could route around, which is why the fight over
direct driver-rider relationships matters. The group purchasing organization, the talent agent: each holds a
foothold by what it integrates and loses ground on what it merely gates. The contingency margin measures the
mix, and the margin is where the pressure falls.

## The reading

Disintermediation is not a force that removes middlemen. It is a force that removes the *friction* a middleman
stood on, and it removes the middleman only when the friction was all that held it up. A middleman held up by a
law, or by a monopoly, or by a standard, stands until the law or the monopoly or the standard falls — which is a
different fight, fought in legislatures and courts, not in markets. A middleman that integrates a joint
condition was never standing on a friction at all. The catalog sorts the survivors from the casualties before
the fact, and the sorting is the same operation the world runs after it: restore the direct tie, and see who is
left in the core.
