# What the six research lines found

## Abstract

Sixty exact-Φ studies ran one research line through each of the lab's six programs: computational
topology (q143–152), behavioral recurrence (q153–162), cognition under interest (q163–172), field
coding (q173–182), qualitative disagreement (q183–192), and a survey construct (q193–202). The six
lines ask one question in six settings: does the exact-Φ structural verdict survive contact with the
material each arm studies? The answer divides by how the mechanism reaches Φ. Given a specified
mechanism, the verdict is robust and, in the survey arm, an incremental and falsifiable predictor.
Recovered from behavior alone, it is not identifiable: the recurrence bridge fails. Encoded from human
accounts, it is real but as determinate as the coding. The value of Φ to the algorithmacy program tracks
how well the coordination's mechanism is specified, and the program's nulls mark that boundary as
sharply as its confirmations. The results are exact and in-silico, on synthetic and simulated data.

## The common question

Each program owns one half of a bridge. The computational arm computes Φ from a Boolean mechanism it
writes down. The recurrence arm wants Φ from observed time series. The field and qualitative arms want Φ
from coded human accounts. The survey arm wants a Φ-derived score to predict a measured competence. The
cognition arm sits between mechanism and meaning, asking what an interested mediator makes unavailable to
the worker. Running all six at once shows where the exact verdict is load-bearing and where the path to
it breaks.

## Computational: topology decides integration, and the triad's laws do not scale

The structural and value results from the mediated triad mostly fail to extend by their own intuitions.
The two-thirds mediator rent does not dilute monotonically as mediation distributes across more hubs
(q149): the per-hub Shapley share is non-monotone in hub count and can rise at the most-distributed end.
A spanning mediator's Φ tracks the count of parties it spans, so the span fraction is not the controlling
variable across system sizes (q145). Rewiring a conjunctive ring toward random shortcuts lowers Φ
monotonically with no small-world peak and flips the verdict to dyadic (q146). Depth is a serial
bottleneck that holds Φ flat at the chain constant 2.0, while breadth adds one unit of integration per
leaf (q144).

What survives is structural rather than parametric. The conjunctive ring keeps a full-node major complex
at every size (q143). A chain of gating hubs breaks at the first hub seam, so depth caps the integrable
group (q148). A redundant hub or a non-hub cycle each keeps the triadic verdict under ablation, and the
surviving Φ does not reduce to cycle count (q151). The whole-system verdict and major-complex membership
genuinely disagree on seven of nineteen studied topologies, and that disagreement is not captured by the
zero-Shapley marker alone (q152). Topology sets integration, and it does so in ways the three-party
numbers do not forecast.

## Recurrence: behavior does not recover the structural verdict

The recurrence line is the program's central negative result. Behavioral coupling does not recover the
exact-Φ reading. CRQA features do not beat a majority baseline at the triadic-versus-dyadic verdict
(q153). Coupling centrality does not locate the structural bottleneck, and it does not beat
transfer-entropy throughput at the same job (q154). A single coupling-centrality threshold recovers
major-complex membership at a pooled AUC of 0.649 against a 0.494 null, which is recovery in name only
(q155). Determinism does not correlate with major-complex Φ (q158). The verdict is not robust under
update noise (q161).

The positives are narrow and useful. Transfer entropy beats peak-lag DCRP on edge orientation by
about twenty points (q157), and it adds nothing on held-out joint accuracy once CRQA is present (q162).
The dissociation between coupling and core widens with system size (q160). The reading is that the
structural Φ-core is not visible in the coupling statistics. That null is worth as much as a
confirmation: it says the exact verdict is not redundant with the cheap behavioral measures a critic
would reach for first.

## Cognition: interest relocates what is hidden

Interest does not add opacity uniformly. It moves the hidden object. An interested mediator raises the
worker's irreducible surprise above the half-bit floor a merely hidden counterpart sets, and probing the
worker's own channel does not remove the excess, so the agenda joins the opacity floor (q168). The agenda
crowds out the channel that carried the worker's meaning and evicts the nuance bit from the core (q165).
The addressee inverts: as interest rises the worker leaves the core for an agenda-governed bind and drops
from addressee to referent (q166).

Several "interest is simply worse" hypotheses refute. An interested platform does not displace the worker
at a lower capture threshold than a faithful one (q167). Interest is not concentrated on the named survey
facets (q172). A mediator that learns on a lag lets the worker's model track it better, and the advantage
does not vanish as drift speeds up (q169). Interest reshapes the cognitive situation by installing a new
un-perceivable object, the agenda, in the place the worker used to occupy.

## Field: the verdict is real and coder-dependent

The coded-rules-to-Φ bridge works as an instrument. Identical accounts give a reproducible verdict and a
degenerate interval, and a coder-weighted confidence interval brackets the consensus Φ at nominal
coverage (q173, q174). The substantive finding is that the verdict is fragile to coding choices. A bit
threshold flips the verdict for a third of accounts (q178). Splitting one unit into two flips it for
nearly half (q180). The update time-grain flips a sixth of triadic accounts, and the grain-sensitive
subset is predictable in advance (q179).

The bridge makes the fragility measurable: interval width tracks coder agreement, and a stable agreement
floor turns the verdict indeterminate rather than wrong (q174). It does not defend against a hostile
coder. An adversary flips the point verdict on more than forty percent of attacked accounts, and the
agreement-weighted interval does not bracket the adversarial estimate (q182). The field verdict is a
real property of an account and is only as determinate as the coding that produced it, which the bridge
records as interval width.

## Qualitative: disagreement is a measurable, localizable spread

Two divergent accounts of one coordination produce a measured spread that is zero on identical accounts
and symmetric under party relabelling (q183). The spread localizes. It concentrates on a single
policy-team node in a moderation case (q187) and puts editor veto-membership in dispute in a peer-review
case (q189). Its three components are not collinear, so the spread carries more than one dimension of
disagreement (q186). An unread spectator leaves no Φ trace at all despite the accounts disagreeing about
its membership, a clean structural fact shared with the field line (q191, q177).

The magnitude does not predict reconciliation effort. Edit distance between accounts does not rise with
the Φ spread (q192). Disagreement is a construct the bridge can measure and place, and how far apart two
accounts sit says nothing about how hard they are to bring together.

## Survey: the Φ score validates as a predictor

On the simulated cohort panel the Φ-derived coordination score behaves like a valid measure of the
algorithmacy construct. It predicts the competence total and the association survives a control that
should kill a spurious one (q193). It carries incremental information over self-report (q195) and is
moderated by whether the platform commits or merely conveys (q196). The measurement holds metric and
scalar invariance across three waves (q197). Wave-one Φ predicts the growth slope (q198), the
within-person change in Φ tracks change in competence (q199), and the score loads on the general factor
rather than one specific facet (q200). A worker-form shuffle destroys every Φ-to-competence relation,
and the real score beats a cheap edge-density proxy on incremental variance (q202). The bridge the survey
arm's gap named runs, and it passes its own falsification test.

## Where the tool earns its keep

One claim connects the six lines. The exact-Φ verdict is a property of a specified mechanism, and its
worth to the program scales with how well that mechanism is pinned down.

Where the mechanism is written or simulated, Φ is robust and does work the cheap proxies miss. The
computational line carries exact structural facts that the triad's parametric laws do not predict. The
survey line shows a Φ score that is incremental, invariant, and falsifiable on a panel. Where the
mechanism must be recovered from behavior, the recurrence line shows the path breaks: coupling statistics
do not identify the core. Where the mechanism is encoded from contested human accounts, the field and
qualitative lines show a verdict that is real, measurable, and coder-dependent, with the bridges
reporting the dependence as interval width and spread. The cognition line explains why a poorly specified
mechanism hides so much: an interested mediator installs an agenda the worker cannot read and demotes the
worker to a referent.

The nulls are the spine of this picture. The recurrence bridge fails, coder choices flip field
verdicts, Φ spread does not predict reconciliation cost, and several "interest is worse" claims refute.
Those results draw the boundary of the tool. Inside it, where a coordination's mechanism is specified, Φ
gives an exact and non-redundant reading of whether and how the coordination is irreducible. The sixty
studies map that boundary, and the map is the contribution.

## Limitations

The results are exact and in-silico. The computational and cognition lines run exact IIT-4.0 Φ on small
Boolean forms. The recurrence line works on synthetic trajectories with known generators. The field and
qualitative lines encode synthetic coded accounts through the rule-to-Φ and disagreement bridges. The
survey line runs on a simulated cohort, so its construct relations are properties of the simulation and
not yet of a measured population. The Φ-to-meaning bridge stays open: "value", "competence", "agenda",
and "disagreement" name quantities defined over Φ on these models. Each line states its own bounds; this
note sets them side by side.
