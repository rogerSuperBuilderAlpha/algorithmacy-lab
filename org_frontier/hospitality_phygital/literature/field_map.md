# Field map — literatures this paper joins

Four clusters. The paper's contribution sits at their intersection: hospitality as relational
practice × phygital experience design × algorithmic mediation × agency/sovereignty under mediation.

**State (2026-08-07, after the dissertation-library pass — see [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md)).**
The library pass closed the platform-hospitality and algorithmic-management gaps and stocked the
folk-theory, contestability, classification, and infrastructure clusters. Remaining gaps are marked
**[gap]** below. Verification runs in two tiers (`verified` / `inherited`); nothing enters the
manuscript on `inherited` alone.

## 1. Hospitality theory (relational / socio-cultural)

**Job in the paper:** define hospitality beyond efficient service — welcome, recognition, care,
presence, negotiated access; supply the journal's native vocabulary.

**Verified anchors:** Lynch et al. (2021), the journal's own ten-year theorizing audit;
Lugosi (2021); Lashley (2000) on the commercial / private / social domains;
Derrida and Dufourmantelle (2000) on conditional vs unconditional hospitality.

**[gap]** Host–guest power and cultural scripts of welcome. Two seed anchors handed over by the CFP
are not enough density for this journal — the section that grounds the paper in hospitality social
science is also the section a domain reviewer reads first.

## 2. Phygital hospitality and service research

**Job in the paper:** locate the argument inside the special issue's frame without reducing the
contribution to experience-design restatement.

**Verified anchors:** Batat (2024) PH-CX framework; Batat (2024) guest editorial on the phygital
*research paradigm*; Batat (2026) phygital service research; Tlili et al. (2023); Addis et al. (2022).

**Naming care.** PH-CX, the phygital research paradigm, and PSR are three distinct things by the
same author. The research paradigm is a 2024 guest editorial in *Qualitative Market Research*; PSR
is the 2026 *Journal of Services Marketing* article advancing FSR and TSR toward human-first
experience design. Earlier drafts of this map used "PSR" for the editorial. Cite them separately —
a special issue Batat guest-edits is the wrong venue in which to blur her frameworks.

**Stance:** the paper accepts hybrid physical–digital realms as the setting, then asks who hosts
when algorithms mediate.

## 3. AI, automation, and digital service in hospitality

**Job in the paper:** show that existing service-tech discussions often treat AI as tool or
acceptance problem; open space for constitutive mediation.

**Verified anchors:** Wirtz et al. (2023) on digital service technologies, robots, and
cost-effective service excellence — the efficiency framing the paper argues past.

**Algorithmic management — stocked.** Möhlmann et al. (2021), Rahman (2021) on control through
opaque measurement rather than command, Christin (2017) on judgement relocated from practitioner
discretion into a vendor's model, and Lipsky (1980), which the dissertation ruled orthogonal and
which applies natively to a hotel's frontline employee.

**[gap] Hospitality-specific algorithmic management.** The evidence above comes from ride-hail,
freelancing, journalism, and short-term rental. Hotel PMS, revenue-management opacity, and
algorithmic scheduling still need their own sources before the employee half claims hospitality
grounding rather than analogy.

**Classification — stocked.** Fourcade and Healy (2013) on classification situations; Cheney-Lippold
(2011) on the inferred, continuously recomputed category that leaves no stable standard to appeal
to; Lyon (2003) on sorting as differential access; Bowker and Star (1999), whose *torque* names the
guest the category does not fit.

**The threshold — stocked 2026-08-07.** Cui et al. (2020, *Management Science*): a single positive
review closes the acceptance gap between guests with white-sounding and African-American-sounding
names, so a reputation artefact administers negotiated access. Edelman et al. (2017) documents the
gap it closes. Boo and Chua (2022, *IJCHM*) and Xu et al. (2020, *JHMM*) put the guest at
facial-recognition check-in running a privacy calculus. Kim et al. (2021, *IJHM*) and Hou et al.
(2021, *Tourism Management*) give human accessibility an empirical floor, with the preference
conditional rather than fixed. Scott and Orlikowski (2012, *AOS*) show ratings reconfiguring
accountability in the travel sector. Tussyadiah (2020, *Annals*) is the survey of the framing this
paper argues past. See [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md) Part 3.

## 4. Platform hospitality — who hosts, already contested

**Job in the paper:** the literature that has already asked this paper's title question. Platform
stays make the algorithm's hosting role unmissable: ratings govern access, the platform assigns the
role of "host," and recognition is mediated by artefacts neither party controls.

**Anchors — the gap is closed.** Eleven sources, several in hospitality venues: Cheng and Foley
(2019, *IJHM*) naming hosting as algorithmically managed; Roelofsen and Minca (2018, *Geoforum*) on
the Superhost badge as a device that grades without seating; Liang et al. (2017, *Tourism
Management*) on why hosts chase it; Leick et al. (2024, *IJHM*) replicating rurally; Bosma (2022) on
professionalization without standing; Ladegaard (2021) on offloaded risk; Zervas et al. (2021) and
Teubner et al. (2019) on non-portable reputation as engineered lock-in; Spier (2024) on the platform
as rule-writer; Ert et al. (2016); Edelman et al. (2017).

The paper's move is to generalize: platform stays are the visible case; branded phygital properties
are the same structure with the mediation less legible. Note the direction of the evidence — it
answers the title question from the *host's* side, which is [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md)
finding 2 and should shape where the argument puts its weight.

## 5. Algorithmic mediation, literacy, contestability, sovereignty

**Job in the paper:** import algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty with hospitality roles
filled in; keep the triad distinct from dyadic HMC / chatbot-as-partner frames.

**Public lab anchors:** literacy vs algorithmacy essay; interested third party; necessary vs
contingent irreducibility; coordinative sovereignty chapter and its literature map.

**External neighbors (verify when used):** algorithmic literacy and folk theorization work already
engaged in the sovereignty chapter (e.g., DeVito; Dogruel; Jarrahi & Sutherland; Pangrazio &
Selwyn; Ananny & Crawford on transparency limits). Re-read for guest and frontline positions.

**Seamful design and infrastructure:** Chalmers and Galani (2004) on seams as resources; Star and
Ruhleder (1996) on infrastructure becoming effective as it sinks out of sight; Star (1999) on master
narratives and invisible work. Section 7's argument is a design-theory position with a lineage, not
a complaint about frictionless interfaces.

**The published nulls.** Ananny and Crawford (2018), Vaccaro et al. (2020), and Edwards and Veale
(2017) each show a naive form of one of the paper's affordances failing to deliver standing. Section
6 has to cite them rather than walk into them — see [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md) finding 3.

**Dissertation:** the genealogy pass in [`../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md`](../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md)
sharpens the Paper 1 construct-gap argument. It no longer gates the construct freeze
([`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §5.A).

## Gap the paper claims

Experience and automation literatures describe tools, journeys, and outcomes. Hospitality theory
describes relational hosting. Platform research shows the algorithm taking the host's role in one
setting. What is still thin: a general account of **algorithmic systems as parties that redistribute
hosting functions**, plus competences and design affordances that keep guest and employee agency
alive inside that triad.

## Round 2 revisions to this map (2026-08-09)

Prompts P1, P2, P6 and P7 run. Full write-up in [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md) Part 4. Four
clusters change and the gap statement above narrows.

**Cluster 1, hospitality theory — now stocked.** The theorizing line is covered from the journal's
founding editorial (Lynch et al. 2011) through its reprise (2021) to recent editorials (Gill et al.
2022; Anastasiadou et al. 2024). Host authority beyond the threshold comes from *H&S* itself
(Bulley 2015). Cultural variation is evidenced at scale (Walters et al. 2021; Munasinghe et al.
2022). The field's own instrument locates hospitality in guest-side acknowledgment (Pijls et al.
2017). Reciprocity as an internal achievement, therefore removable by design, comes from
Kekstaite (2022).

**Cluster 3, AI and automation in hospitality — the employee half splits.** Algorithmic relocation
of judgement is now DOCUMENTED for back-of-house hotel work (Spektor et al. 2023, 2025; Mutari and
Figart 2026) and remains UNDOCUMENTED at the guest interface. Revenue management runs the other way
and must be cited as a complication (Bendoly 2013). Hospitality-native algorithmic management now
exists as a small literature (Jianu, Tussyadiah and Miller 2025; Jianu, Ashton and Lugosi 2025;
Kamalahmadi et al. 2021), with adjacent-sector work supplying the mechanism vocabulary
(Vieira and Junte 2025; Cheon and Erickson 2025; Griesbach et al. 2019).

**Cluster 2, phygital hospitality and service research — two names to add and one paper to answer.**
Batat's corpus is four works, not two: PH-CX (2024a), the research paradigm (2024b), PSR (2026a) and
HFPV (2026b). TPSR states the algorithmic-governance gap this paper claims, and should be cited
rather than having the gap asserted (Shabnam et al. 2026). **The triadic move is already published
in a hospitality journal** (Gursoy 2026), with supporting instances elsewhere (Roederer et al. 2026;
Zheng et al. 2025; Xing and Zhang 2026).

**New cluster — outcome variables.** Trust is well evidenced in hospitality (Tussyadiah et al. 2020;
Della Corte et al. 2023, and its corrigendum). Well-being has TSR's multi-level remit
(Anderson and Ostrom 2015), a hospitality individual/collective treatment (Galeone and
Sebastiani 2021), and a route to population scale (Uysal et al. 2016). Employee discretion is
carried by two *Tourism Management* papers (Pan et al. 2025; Li et al. 2019). Belonging is
established as a service outcome and is technology-sensitive, but not in hospitality and not under
algorithmic mediation (Rosenbaum et al. 2007; Parkinson et al. 2022; Feng et al. 2023).

**The target journal has been here once already.** *Hospitality & Society* has published a virtual
domain extending Lashley and the counter-concept of algorithmic hostility (Riordan 2024). Any map of
this paper's position that omits it is wrong.

### Absences confirmed by searching, and therefore citable

- No study of belonging as a measured outcome in a hotel or restaurant encounter under algorithmic
  mediation.
- No study testing any of the five affordances against any outcome in hospitality. **Contestability
  returns nothing at all in the hospitality corpus.**
- No measurement literature for interpersonal recognition under that name or its near synonyms.
- No hospitality study of collective or community well-being effects of hospitality automation. The
  field has individual-level automation effects and collective-level non-automation well-being, and
  the halves do not meet.
- No empirical work on PMS or CRM task allocation, hotel-specific algorithmic scheduling, employee
  performance scoring built from guest reviews, or automated upsell prompts constraining what a
  front-desk agent may offer.
- No Kantian argument made by a hospitality scholar in a hospitality venue.
- No theorization of the guest's reciprocal obligation in a commercial setting.
- No hospitableness instrument measuring anything flowing from guest to host.
- No published critique or rejoinder to the Lynch et al. theorizing line.
- Genuine nulls on service technology and well-being are essentially unpublished; write the neutral
  case as conditionality and moderation instead.

### The gap statement, revised

The gap above claimed "a general account of algorithmic systems as parties that redistribute hosting
functions." **Half of that is now taken.** Redistribution of agency inside a customer-employee-AI
triad is published (Gursoy 2026), and algorithmic mediation of agency is already named in the
phygital literature (Shabnam et al. 2026).

What remains, and what the paper should claim:

1. **The host role rather than the third node.** Hospitality theory loads the host with obligation in
   a way "employee" does not. The normative asymmetry between an employee-in-a-triad and a
   host-in-a-triad is an argument only a hospitality-theory paper can make.
2. **Seamlessness as the object of critique.** TPSR critiques opacity and rigidity, not smoothness,
   and PH-CX positively endorses fluidity. The friction literature that would trouble it sits
   entirely outside phygital scholarship (Padigar et al. 2025; Phillips et al. 2024). Nobody has
   turned friction on these frameworks. **This is the paper's only uncontested move.**
3. **The affordances as a specified set.** Every affordance-to-outcome arrow is untested in
   hospitality, which is a novelty claim and a discipline on how the abstract may be worded.

## The gap statement, revised again (2026-08-09, after the prior-art map)

The revision above still assumed the triad was half available. It is not available at all. The
customer–employee–technology triad has been explicit since Larivière et al. (2017), carries the
literal name "service triad" (Odekerken-Schröder et al. 2021) and "encounter triad" (Li et al. 2021,
in *IJHM*), and has been re-derived at least five times. See [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md) Part 5
for the dated table.

**What the paper contributes, stated as narrowly as the evidence allows:**

1. **The third party occupies the host role.** Every prior characterization is a tool, an automated
   social presence, an employee substitute, a frontline actor, an object of blame attribution, a
   mediator, a facilitator, a collaborator, a co-creator, or an algorithmic actor. None reaches for
   hospitality's own ethical vocabulary — welcome, threshold, guest-right, host-obligation — to
   characterize the technology. Hospitality loads the host with obligations the employment relation
   does not generate, and that asymmetry is an argument only a hospitality-theory paper can make.
2. **Seamlessness is not self-evidently hospitable.** No peer-reviewed critique of seamlessness,
   frictionlessness or smoothness as a design value was found in this literature. PH-CX positively
   endorses "fluidifying the journeys of customers." Held with slightly less confidence than 1,
   because the search was narrower.

The triad is now a **concession**, not a claim. §3 cites Larivière et al. (2017), Li et al. (2021)
and Odekerken-Schröder et al. (2021), notes Gursoy (2026) as the most recent hospitality statement,
and moves immediately to the host question. Roughly sixty words, and it removes the paper's single
largest exposure.
