# Field map — literatures this paper joins

Four clusters. The paper's contribution sits at their intersection: hospitality as relational
practice × phygital experience design × algorithmic mediation × agency/sovereignty under mediation.

**State lives in [`../library/CARDS_INDEX.md`](../library/CARDS_INDEX.md), not here.** Cluster
membership, counts, read depth and open debts are computed from card frontmatter on every build, so
they cannot drift. This file keeps only what a generated index cannot write: what each cluster is
*for* in the argument, and the naming rulings that took judgement to reach.

That division exists because the previous arrangement failed. This file carried gap and stocked
labels by hand, two research rounds skipped the update step, and it went on advertising gaps that
later acquisitions had already closed.

## 1. Hospitality theory (relational / socio-cultural)

**Job in the paper:** define hospitality beyond efficient service — welcome, recognition, care,
presence, negotiated access; supply the journal's native vocabulary.

**Verified anchors:** Lynch et al. (2021), the journal's own ten-year theorizing audit;
Lugosi (2021); Lashley (2000) on the commercial / private / social domains;
Derrida and Dufourmantelle (2000) on conditional vs unconditional hospitality.

## 2. Phygital hospitality and service research

**Job in the paper:** locate the argument inside the special issue's frame without reducing the
contribution to experience-design restatement.

**Verified anchors:** Batat (2024) PH-CX framework; Batat (2024) guest editorial on the phygital
*research paradigm*; Batat (2026) phygital service research; Tlili et al. (2023); Addis et al. (2022).

**Naming care.** PH-CX, the phygital research paradigm, and PSR are three distinct things by the
same author. The research paradigm is a 2024 guest editorial in *Qualitative Market Research*; PSR
is the 2026 *Journal of Services Marketing* article advancing FSR and TSR toward human-first
experience design. Earlier drafts of this map used "PSR" for the editorial. Cite them separately —
a special issue Batat guest-edits is the wrong venue in which to blur her frameworks.

**Stance:** the paper accepts hybrid physical–digital realms as the setting, then asks who hosts
when algorithms mediate.

## 3. AI, automation, and digital service in hospitality

**Job in the paper:** show that existing service-tech discussions often treat AI as tool or
acceptance problem; open space for constitutive mediation.

**Verified anchors:** Wirtz et al. (2023) on digital service technologies, robots, and
cost-effective service excellence — the efficiency framing the paper argues past.

## 4. Platform hospitality — who hosts, already contested

**Job in the paper:** the literature that has already asked this paper's title question. Platform
stays make the algorithm's hosting role unmissable: ratings govern access, the platform assigns the
role of "host," and recognition is mediated by artefacts neither party controls.

The paper's move is to generalize: platform stays are the visible case; branded phygital properties
are the same structure with the mediation less legible. Note the direction of the evidence — it
answers the title question from the *host's* side, which is [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md)
finding 2 and should shape where the argument puts its weight.

## 5. Algorithmic mediation, literacy, contestability, sovereignty

**Job in the paper:** import algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty with hospitality roles
filled in; keep the triad distinct from dyadic HMC / chatbot-as-partner frames.

**Public lab anchors:** literacy vs algorithmacy essay; interested third party; necessary vs
contingent irreducibility; coordinative sovereignty chapter and its literature map.

**External neighbors (verify when used):** algorithmic literacy and folk theorization work already
engaged in the sovereignty chapter (e.g., DeVito; Dogruel; Jarrahi & Sutherland; Pangrazio &
Selwyn; Ananny & Crawford on transparency limits). Re-read for guest and frontline positions.

**Seamful design and infrastructure:** Chalmers and Galani (2004) on seams as resources; Star and
Ruhleder (1996) on infrastructure becoming effective as it sinks out of sight; Star (1999) on master
narratives and invisible work. Section 7's argument is a design-theory position with a lineage, not
a complaint about frictionless interfaces.

**The published nulls.** Ananny and Crawford (2018), Vaccaro et al. (2020), and Edwards and Veale
(2017) each show a naive form of one of the paper's affordances failing to deliver standing. Section
6 has to cite them rather than walk into them — see [`FOUNDATION.md`](FOUNDATION.md) finding 3.

**Dissertation:** the genealogy pass in [`../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md`](../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md)
sharpens the Paper 1 construct-gap argument. It no longer gates the construct freeze
([`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §5.A).

## Gap the paper claims

Experience and automation literatures describe tools, journeys, and outcomes. Hospitality theory
describes relational hosting. Platform research shows the algorithm taking the host's role in one
setting. What is still thin: a general account of **algorithmic systems as parties that redistribute
hosting functions**, plus competences and design affordances that keep guest and employee agency
alive inside that triad.
