# Field map — literatures this paper joins

Four clusters. The paper's contribution sits at their intersection: hospitality as relational
practice × phygital experience design × algorithmic mediation × agency/sovereignty under mediation.

## 1. Hospitality theory (relational / socio-cultural)

**Job in the paper:** define hospitality beyond efficient service — welcome, recognition, care,
presence, negotiated access; supply the journal's native vocabulary.

**Seed anchors:** Lynch et al. (2021); Lugosi (2021); broader *Hospitality & Society* theorizing
line.

**Verification:** CFP-cited; confirm DOIs before manuscript lock.

**Open reads:** classic and recent work on commercial vs ethical hospitality, host–guest power,
cultural scripts of welcome.

## 2. Phygital hospitality and service research

**Job in the paper:** locate the argument inside the special issue's PH-CX / PSR frame without
reducing the contribution to experience-design restatement.

**Seed anchors:** Batat (2024a) PH-CX; Batat (2024b) phygital research paradigm; Tlili et al.
(2023); Addis et al. (2022).

**Verification:** CFP-cited; confirm DOIs.

**Stance:** the paper accepts hybrid physical–digital realms as the setting, then asks who hosts
when algorithms mediate.

## 3. AI, automation, and digital service in hospitality

**Job in the paper:** show that existing service-tech discussions often treat AI as tool or
acceptance problem; open space for constitutive mediation.

**Seed anchors:** Wirtz et al. (2023) and neighbors on robots / AI / cost-effective service
excellence.

**Open reads:** hotel PMS/CRM personalization, revenue management opacity, reputation systems,
kiosk and app check-in studies, employee deskilling / upskilling under automation.

## 4. Algorithmic mediation, literacy, contestability, sovereignty

**Job in the paper:** import algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty with hospitality roles
filled in; keep triad distinct from dyadic HMC / chatbot-as-partner frames.

**Public lab anchors:** literacy vs algorithmacy essay; interested third party; necessary vs
contingent irreducibility; coordinative sovereignty chapter and its literature map.

**External neighbors (to verify when used):** algorithmic literacy and folk theorization work
already engaged in the sovereignty chapter (e.g., DeVito; Dogruel; Jarrahi & Sutherland; Pangrazio
& Selwyn; Ananny & Crawford on transparency limits). Re-read for guest and frontline positions.

**Dissertation:** complete [`../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md`](../DISSERTATION_REVIEW.md) before treating
Paper 1–2 claims as locked inputs.

## Gap the paper claims

Experience and automation literatures describe tools, journeys, and outcomes. Hospitality theory
describes relational hosting. What is still thin: a named account of **algorithmic systems as
parties that redistribute hosting functions**, plus competences and design affordances that keep
guest and employee agency alive inside that triad.
