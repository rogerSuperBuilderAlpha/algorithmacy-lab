## Summary

Argues that seams — the visible joins and imperfections of a system — are resources for action
rather than defects, and that revealing them lets people work with a system as well as through it.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Argues against treating one medium as primary: theory and analysis 'suggest that we move away from a treatment of one system or medium as primary' [full-text]
- Seamful design is 'carefully revealing differences and limitations of systems, in ways that support social interaction' — revealing, not merely tolerating [full-text]
- Grounds the account in Heidegger's ready-to-hand and present-at-hand distinction, which is why the seam is about engaged use rather than about interface aesthetics [full-text]
- DIS 2004, pp. 243-252 [metadata]
