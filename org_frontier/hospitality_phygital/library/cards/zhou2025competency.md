## Summary

Develops and validates the first scale of algorithmic competency across five samples of Chinese on-demand platform workers, with four dimensions: understanding, embracing, leveraging and remediating algorithmic management. Competency predicts customer-oriented service behaviour and gig-work identification.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Understanding is operationalized as folk theorizing; an interview exemplar reads "I develop theories about task-allocating and score-calculating algorithms based on my own experience" [full-text]
- Embracing is defined as "willingness to trust the efficiency and accuracy of AM" [full-text]
- Remediating is the "capacity to solve/supplement the deficiencies of platform's AM system", with an exemplar about using recorded evidence to appeal to the algorithmic management system [full-text]
- No dimension corresponds to tracking rule change over time [full-text]

## Critical notes & threats

- Three differentiators hold: worker-only and gig-specific; embracing is an attitude rather than a competence, which is why the scale cannot carry a normative argument; and remediating treats appeal capacity as a property of the person.
- The third is the paper's opening. Lodging an appeal is something a person can do and being answered is something an institution grants, and a validated scale conflating them is the best available motivation for separating competence from standing.
- Specification and tracking are absent from this scale and from every definition in the literacy literature, which is where the paper's decomposition earns its keep.
