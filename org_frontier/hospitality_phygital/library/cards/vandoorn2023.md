## Summary

Proposes the consumer-autonomous technology-worker framework for frontline service, arguing that
consumer-worker relations strengthen where autonomous technology augments the worker rather than
replacing him, and illustrating the argument with interviews in hospitality settings. It is the
nearest rival to our triad: three parties on the frontline, with an augment-versus-replace
distinction.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Places consumer, autonomous technology and worker in one framework, with technology as either consumer-facing or worker-facing [abstract]
- Expects consumer-worker relations to strengthen when technology augments rather than replaces the worker [abstract]
- Illustrated with hospitality interviews — workers co-working with robots, and the consumers they serve [abstract]
