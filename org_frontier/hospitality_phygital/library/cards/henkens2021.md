## Summary

Finds the well-being effects of smart service contingent on the person receiving it, so one
touchpoint may sit at different poles for different guests.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- The well-being outcomes are specific: self-efficacy and technology anxiety, not well-being in general [full-text]
- Smartness reaches well-being through engagement with the *smart product*; the total effect of smartness on engagement with the service provider is 'insignificantly different from zero' [full-text]
- 'technological anxiety is an important inhibitor of customer well-being - and thus a provoker of customer ill-being - in a smart service system context' [full-text]
- *International Journal of Research in Marketing* 38:2, pp. 425-447 [metadata]


## Critical notes & threats

- A finding the paper should probably use rather than merely cite: smartness moves well-being through the guest's engagement with the device, and not through engagement with the provider. That is close to the substitutive pole stated in another vocabulary.
- The governor on the well-being claim, and the audit confirmed nothing better exists for mixed or contingent effects.
- Also does work in section 6: guest heterogeneity defeats any single specification of a good touchpoint.
