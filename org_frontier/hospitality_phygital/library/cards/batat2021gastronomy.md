## Summary

Ethnography of Michelin-starred restaurants arguing that the lived luxury experience is co-produced through interaction with service providers, other customers and environmental elements rather than delivered to a passive recipient. Frames luxury consumption as personal experience with symbolic, social, ideological, subjective and emotional dimensions.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Quotes her own earlier work defining luxury consumption as "a response to a search for emotions, pleasure, uniqueness, consideration, and greatest services" [full-text]
- Defines luxury as "a personal experience... incorporates symbolic, social, ideological, subjective, and emotional dimensions" [full-text]
- Explicitly names co-production with "other social actors (e.g., service providers, other customers)" as constitutive of the experience [full-text]
