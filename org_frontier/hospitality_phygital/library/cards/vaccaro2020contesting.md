## Summary

Built an appeal process, offered it under three designs, and tested it against a baseline with no
appeal while holding the outcome constant. It produced no improvement in perceived accountability.
Participants contested the goals and the automation itself rather than the single decision. This
is the paper's central null: a grievance channel internal to the party that writes the rules
absorbs the contest instead of transferring standing.

## Key facts / verified quotes with loci

- Appeal tested against a no-appeal baseline with the outcome held constant; no lift in perceived accountability [abstract]
- Participants' objections reached the goals and the automation, beyond the individual decision [abstract]

## Critical notes & threats

- This null is what converts section 6 from a plausible checklist into an argument that survives a referee who knows the field. It must not be cut under any budget pressure.
- It is also a constraint on our own recommendation: we cannot propose an internal appeal as conferring standing, and the roadmap must respect that.
- The audit found no published study where contestation produced a measured lift in perceived accountability. Martin and Waldman (2022) is the nearest positive result and remains uncited.

## Where it enters the manuscript

Sections 5 and 6. In section 5 it supports the claim that accountability is a relation no
competence reaches; in section 6 it sets the bar for the contestability affordance — an appeal
routing back to the rule's author does not confer standing.
