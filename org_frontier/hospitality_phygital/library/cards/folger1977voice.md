## Summary

The canonical voice-effect experiment: the opportunity to be heard reduced experienced injustice
independently of whether the outcome improved. This is the empirical warrant for the paper's anti-
seamlessness argument — an encounter can produce good outcomes efficiently and still withhold the
surface on which a guest might have said something, and that withholding carries a cost of its own
that better outcomes do not offset.
