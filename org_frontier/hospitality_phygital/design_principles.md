# Design principles — five guest-centric affordances

These affordances are the paper's bridge from theory to organizational design. Each one tilts
phygital mediation toward **augmentative hospitality** and away from **substitutive hospitality**.
None alone guarantees hospitality; together they mark whether guests and employees retain agency.

## 1. Transparency

Consequential algorithmic decisions are noticeable and intelligible at the point they affect the
guest or employee.

- **Augments when:** room assignment logic, pricing changes, queue priority, or personalization
  categories can be explained in ordinary language.
- **Substitutes when:** outcomes arrive without a readable reason, so negotiation has no object.

Transparency without standing produces disclosure no one can act on. Pair with contestability.

## 2. Adjustability

Guests and employees can modify parameters that shape the encounter without exiting the
relationship.

- **Augments when:** preference, accessibility, privacy, and pacing controls are real and honored.
- **Substitutes when:** the only "choice" is accepting a default path authored entirely by the
  system.

## 3. Contestability

Decisions can be challenged, reviewed, and remedied through a known path.

- **Augments when:** an incorrect identity match, denied upgrade logic, or flagged behavior can be
  disputed and overturned by a human host with authority.
- **Substitutes when:** appeals loop into the same opaque classifier, or contestation is punished
  as friction.

## 4. Human accessibility

A human host remains reachable for recognition, care, and exception handling.

- **Augments when:** digital triage routes hard or culturally sensitive cases to people with
  discretion and time.
- **Substitutes when:** chatbots and kiosks exhaust the guest before any employee appears, or
  staffing models treat human contact as failure.

## 5. Bypass-ability

A meaningful alternate path exists — non-digital, less mediated, or differently mediated — without
punishing the guest for taking it.

- **Augments when:** cash / desk / phone / unpersonalized options remain first-class.
- **Substitutes when:** bypass is formally available but slower, costlier, or socially marked as
  non-compliant.

Bypass maps to the lab's contingent-mediation / exit language. Where mediation is effectively
necessary (no real bypass), design weight shifts to voice: transparency, adjustability,
contestability, and human accessibility.

## Against seamlessness-as-hospitality

A frictionless path that conceals decisions and removes negotiation can weaken recognition and
cultural responsiveness. Guests whose needs fall outside algorithmically constructed norms lose
the surface on which to be received as exceptions. Guest-centric phygital design keeps technology
participatory and accountable; it does not optimize for invisibility alone.

## Organizational checklist (draft)

For each major touchpoint (booking, arrival, in-stay personalization, service recovery, departure):

1. What does the algorithm decide?
2. Who can see that decision?
3. Who can change it, contest it, or reach a human?
4. What happens to a guest who refuses the digital default?
5. Does the residual design look augmentative or substitutive?
