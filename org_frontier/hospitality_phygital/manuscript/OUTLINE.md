## 0. Title and abstract

- Title: Who Hosts the Guest? Algorithmic Mediation, Guest Agency, and the Reconfiguration of
  Hospitality in Phygital Spaces
- Abstract: [`../ABSTRACT.md`](../ABSTRACT.md)

## 1. Introduction

- Phygital tools framed as seamless / personalized / guest-centric
- Hospitality as relational practice (welcome, recognition, care, presence, negotiated access)
- Algorithms already perform hosting functions
- Research question: how mediation reconfigures guest–employee–organization relations, and when it
  enhances rather than diminishes relational hospitality
- Contribution preview: triad; augmentative vs substitutive; hospitality algorithmacy;
  coordinative sovereignty; five affordances; seamlessness critique

## 2. Hospitality beyond seamless service

- Anchor in hospitality social science (*Hospitality & Society* line)
- Distinguish service efficiency from hosting as recognition and negotiated access
- Why "guest-centric" cannot mean only preference prediction

## 3. Phygital hospitality as triadic mediation

- PH-CX / PSR as setting (Batat), then move to mediation
- Guest–algorithm–host triad
- Hosting functions algorithms take on
- Redistribution of knowledge, discretion, authority
- Dual potential: responsiveness/inclusion vs depersonalization/surveillance/exclusion/dependence

## 4. Augmentative and substitutive hospitality

- Definitions ([`../CONSTRUCTS.md`](../CONSTRUCTS.md))
- Mechanisms that push an encounter toward each pole
- Ordinary vignettes (check-in, assignment, concierge, recovery) — illustrative, not case claims

## 5. Hospitality algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty

- Literacy insufficient inside a binding triad
- Hospitality algorithmacy as situated competence (guest and employee)
- Coordinative sovereignty as standing / meaningful influence
- Link to bypass vs voice when mediation is contingent vs effectively necessary

## 6. Five affordances of guest-centric phygital design

- Transparency, adjustability, contestability, human accessibility, bypass-ability
- How each conditions agency, discretion, recognition, belonging, trust, well-being
- Organizational implications without vendor checklist tone
- Detail: [`../design_principles.md`](../design_principles.md)

## 7. Against frictionless hospitality

- Seamlessness can conceal decisions and erase negotiation surfaces
- Cultural and exception-handling costs when norms are algorithmically fitted
- Hospitality may require productive friction

## 8. Discussion and contributions

- AI as constitutive mediator of hospitality relationships, meanings, and power
- Theory contribution to hospitality and phygital scholarship
- Design principles for organizations
- Limits: conceptual paper; lab formal results are model priors only
- Future research: qualitative and survey arms parked on [`../AGENDA.md`](../AGENDA.md)

## 9. Conclusion

- Guest-centric phygital hospitality depends on agency over how technology participates in hosting,
  not on making technology invisible
