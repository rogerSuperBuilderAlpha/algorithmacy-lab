# Part II — the mechanism

## §3 Phygital hospitality as triadic mediation — 1,692 words, nine paragraphs

> **Abstract delivered:** "The paper theorizes phygital hospitality as a system of triadic
> coordination among the guest, the algorithmic intermediary, and the human or organizational host.
> Within this arrangement, algorithms perform functions traditionally associated with hosting:
> interpreting guest needs, directing encounters, regulating access, anticipating preferences, and
> responding to requests. Their participation redistributes knowledge, discretion, and authority
> across the hospitality encounter."

**Editor spine.** Casalegno, Civera, Mosca and Freeman 2020 carries the redistribution premise —
roles reallocate, power and responsibilities overlap and converge — so the paper never has to argue
that redistribution happens and can go straight to what fails to travel with it. Mosca et al. 2025
supplies the phygital instance. Batat's HFPV and PHIVE establish that the frameworks have begun
criticizing themselves, which is where §3's question enters. De Vos's ethical-mindedness chapter
gives the discretion paragraph its opening sentence: compliance cannot guarantee ethical behaviour
when professionals meet unforeseen dilemmas. Shabnam et al. name the agency gap the section fills.

**Professionalized by.** The triad literature (concession); machine ethics (Kropf, Santoni de Sio,
Introna); algorithmic management (Rahman, Lipsky, Christin, Möhlmann, Spektor, Krzywdzinski, Keegan);
Scott and Orlikowski on authority.

1. **Definition and concession.** The triad defined — guest; intermediary that classifies,
   recommends, gates, personalizes, prices, queues; human or organizational host accountable for
   welcome. Not our discovery: Larivière 2017; Odekerken-Schröder 2021; Li 2021; van Doorn 2023;
   Gursoy 2026. Our question begins where the structure stops.
2. **The host question.** Not in the literature's vocabulary. *P9 rewrite:* the list of declines is
   longer and more precise. Liu et al. 2026 declines the position by construction, treating welcome
   as a designed output; Lee and Lu 2024 asks whether guests can *attribute* hospitableness, which
   puts a perceptual question exactly where the normative one belongs; Belanche remains the nearest
   miss inside service research. Then the duty asymmetry and the template point — the machine is
   compared with the employee constantly, and whether the employee was the right template goes
   unexamined. Ends: a party performs recognition, routing and admission, and what obliges this one?
3. **Machine ethics engaged.** Kropf et al. 2026 on role responsibility for care robots; Santoni de
   Sio and Mecacci 2021 on responsibility gaps. *P9 addition:* Sharma and Mattila 2025 put robot
   responsibilities on the hospitality agenda as governance — liability, safety, data protection,
   consent — which completes a pattern rather than breaking it. Care is owed in virtue of need,
   fiduciary duty in virtue of trust, governance duties in virtue of risk; welcome is owed in virtue
   of arrival and is the only one of the four that constitutes its role rather than regulating it.
   Introna 2010, with Mestre 2025 confirming the reversal is still unmade where Derrida meets
   information ethics.
4. **The frameworks are not silent** — the editor's physicalization and digitalization ask lands
   here. Batat 2024 and 2026; Shabnam et al. 2026. *P9 addition:* HFPV criticizes technocentric and
   efficiency-driven logics and PHIVE takes on AI's harms directly, so the paper meets a literature
   already turning critical and enters where the self-criticism stops. Phygitalization as dual
   process; the guest crosses repeatedly; fluidity re-described as a relay, and who holds authority
   at each handover?
5. **The claim precisely, and the three redistributions.** Not that a third element appeared; that
   the intermediary performs hosting functions — Pierre's five verbs. *P9 couching:* Casalegno,
   Civera, Mosca and Freeman 2020 states the premise in an editor's words. Knowledge first: Rahman
   2021 and 2024; Calo and Rosenblat 2017.
6. **Discretion.** *P9 couching:* Ala, De Vos et al. 2022 opens it — professional judgment exceeds
   what rules specify — before Lipsky 1980 gives it a name. Then Jianu et al. 2025 on managers
   translating algorithmic decisions; Spektor et al. 2023; Christin 2017; Möhlmann et al. 2021 on
   matching and control as twin dimensions. *P9 additions:* Spektor et al. 2025 brings unionized hotel
   housekeeping, where a carefully configured tool still disrupts coordination; Krzywdzinski et al.
   2024 shows knowledge centralizing with no surveillance intent, which detaches the argument from
   any claim about what managers wanted.
7. **The split verdict.** Documented back of house (Spektor 2023, 2025); the front desk differs
   (Bendoly 2013 on sanctioned latitude; Garcia et al. 2026 on durable, anticipated deviation); the
   guest-facing half thin and largely unstudied. Pedersen and Pors 2022: standardization generates
   new discretionary work, so displacement is the wrong word.
8. **Authority, and the differentiation.** Scott and Orlikowski 2012 on rating systems materializing
   accountability relations; Zervas 2021 with Teubner 2019 on reputation that does not travel and
   exit made expensive by design. The three movements summarized: best placed to know, to decide, to
   revise — least available to answer. *P9 rewrite, mandatory:* Riordan 2024 can no longer be
   introduced as this journal's nearest engagement with algorithmic mediation. Riordan names the
   labour pole; Germann Molz 2026 names the host pole. The differentiation belongs here, once and
   plainly: she asks what becomes of hospitality when the host is erased, and this paper asks whether
   the successor inherits the obligation. No verdict on the technology.
9. **The craft paragraph** (phronesis; Roger's h-team insight). A practised team manufactures the
   guest's sense of control — defers visibly, decides invisibly, heads off self-harming choices — and
   the skill rests on holding the decisions it appears to surrender. Algorithmic direction breaks the
   craft at the hinge; felt control must now be produced by an interface that holds nothing. Fuller
   and Smith 1991: management by customers, an old control form with a new instrument. Ends: the
   industry has never before had to manage a gap between felt and actual control inside its own front
   desk.

## §4 Augmentative and substitutive hospitality — 911 words + Table 1, four paragraphs

> **Abstract delivered:** "To explain these contrasting outcomes, the paper distinguishes between
> **augmentative hospitality**, in which technology supports human recognition, discretion,
> responsiveness, and care, and **substitutive hospitality**, in which algorithmic direction
> displaces relational judgment and restricts the capacity of guests and employees to shape the
> encounter." Also discharges ¶2's close: "Although such mediation can expand responsiveness and
> inclusion, it may also produce depersonalization, surveillance, categorical exclusion, and
> dependence on opaque technological systems."

**Editor spine.** Mosca and Civera 2017 distinguish residual from integrated corporate responsibility
— performing the form of a responsibility against occupying the position of it — which is the
paper's distinction in another domain and carries its intuition before the coordination conditions
make it checkable. Batat's AR restaurant study is the augmentative pole in the lead editor's own
hospitality venue: technology enriching a meal that people still host.

**Professionalized by.** Okhuysen and Bechky; Bovens; Raisch and Krakowski; Parasuraman; and the
rival conditions the section must beat.

1. **Definitions and the coordination spine.** Pierre's definitions, intuitive and unusable as
   stated, usable once tied to coordination's requirements. Okhuysen and Bechky 2009: accountability,
   predictability, common understanding. The translation sentence: their accountability is task
   clarity among colleagues, and the guest's requires Bovens' answerability because the guest is not
   a member of the organization. *P9 additions:* Mosca and Civera 2017 couches the distinction first;
   Keegan et al. 2025 on the gray zone of actors ambiguously inside or outside organizational
   boundaries supports the translation rather than leaving it asserted. *P9 obligation:* cite and
   differentiate Hemmer et al. 2025, the one published checkable condition — complementary team
   performance is an ex-post performance test over internal decision tasks, with no place for
   answerability to an outsider — and note that Yuan, Hao and Liu 2026 stipulate augment-or-replace
   as a manipulation rather than deriving a criterion.
2. **The withholding inversion and the condition.** Mediation can withhold all three at once: common
   understanding in both directions; predictability inverted, since the system forecasts the guest
   while revising its own rules silently; accountability wherever no forum owes an answer.
   Substitutive is all three withheld together; augmentative is not all. Opacity with stable rules and
   an empowered employee can be augmentative; perfect transparency answering to nobody is
   substitutive. *P9 addition:* Hatherley 2025 names the inversion — update opacity, a kind that
   survives every standard explainability remedy. Condition applies to touchpoints → **Table 1, the
   five diagnostic questions**: what decided, who sees, who overrides, what answers a dispute, what
   refusal costs.
3. **Vignettes, not cases.** *P9 upgrades:* room assignment stands on adjacent evidence, since no
   empirical work on hotel room-assignment algorithms exists (verified absence). Pricing gains Garcia,
   Tolvanen and Wagner 2026, where the hotel pricing algorithm biases its own recommendations in
   anticipation of human adjustment, so the operative rule is not the rule the human believes she
   follows. Service recovery replaces Hou 2021 with Huang and Lo 2025: human preference is contingent
   on failure type, process rather than outcome — a coordination-shaped contingency rather than a
   taste. Schmidt et al. 2025 adds the field experiment where guests were indifferent between human
   and robot at an information desk, a touchpoint where all three conditions survive. Batat's Le Petit
   Chef study is the editor-native augmentative case.
4. **Threshold vignette and scope.** Recognition at the threshold — confirming against scoring. The
   pole is settled by the three withholdings and never by sophistication. Touchpoints, not properties:
   mixing inherited from Raisch and Krakowski 2021, and the levels-of-automation concession
   (Parasuraman et al. 2000), whose ladder has no dimension for silent rule movement or answerability
   and places the guest outside the loop entirely.

# Part III — what the parties have, and what they lack

## §5 Hospitality algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty — 1,311 words, six paragraphs

> **Abstract delivered:** "It introduces **hospitality algorithmacy** as the situated capacity of
> guests and employees to recognize, interpret, strategically engage with, contest, and, when
> necessary, circumvent algorithmic mediation. This capacity supports **coordinative sovereignty**:
> the practical ability of participants to retain meaningful influence over how hospitality is
> organized and enacted."

**Editor spine.** De Vos, Qesja and Lipnickas 2023 isolate "service manner and need identification"
as the interactional work that makes mediated service feel hosted — the paper's interpretation and
specification components under another name, in an editor's own study. Mosca 2026 has managerial work
shifting toward "interpretation and validation" under AI infrastructure, which names the surviving
human competence. Civera, Mosca, Casalegno and Maple 2018 is accountability's nearest approach in the
editor corpus: customers judge the firm's account-giving, and what the account lacks is the forum.

**Professionalized by.** The folk-theory literature; Bovens and the accountability line; Hirschman;
Morrison; and the collision the section must turn.

*Open with Roger (AGENDA 43): the specialization wording in ¶1, where the affective facet went, and
reconciliation with the public definition. The render guard blocks the deanonymizing lab citation
either way.*

1. **The competence, named and specialized.** Guests and employees are not passive; folk theories get
   acted on. Hospitality algorithmacy defined in Pierre's wording, including "contest" — his call
   (AGENDA 1b), with the dissertation's derivation offered as a recommendation and not an edit.
   Documented in hospitality: Jhaver et al. 2018 on hosts orienting to two audiences. General form:
   Eslami 2016; Bishop 2019; Cotter 2019; Ytre-Arne and Moe 2021; DeVito et al. 2017; DeVito 2021.
   *P9 obligation, new and load-bearing:* Zhou et al. 2025 published the first validated
   algorithmic-competency scale — understanding, embracing, leveraging, remediating — and it must be
   cited. Three differentiators hold: worker-only and gig-specific; embracing is willingness to trust,
   an attitude rather than a competence; and remediating folds appeal capacity into individual
   competency, which is the exact conflation this section exists to undo. The collision is the best
   available motivation for coordinative sovereignty, because it shows a serious literature treating
   the capacity to appeal as a property of the person. *P9 support:* Gagrčin et al. 2024 review 169
   studies and report the field still lacks defined core competencies, calling for domain-specific
   approaches — which turns the paper's decomposition into a response rather than an assertion.
   Neither Dogruel's four components nor Swart's triad nor Cotter's practical knowledge names
   specification or tracking.
2. **Three components and the floor-level form.** Interpretation, specification, tracking, each
   answering a withholding. The agent who rephrases a wish is lending the guest her own specification
   — the phronesis beat. *P9 couching:* De Vos on need identification and Mosca on interpretation and
   validation open the paragraph. Employee competence and its limit: Christ-Brendemühl and
   Schaarschmidt 2019; Cheng et al. 2025 — the employees who understand the system best may stop
   speaking about it.
3. **The compounding paragraph (P8), verified intact.** The Cheng finding registers an increment on a
   baseline: silence produced by undermining from supervisors, coworkers and guests (Jung and Yoon
   2019; Al-Hawari 2020); silence structurally dominant in UK hotels (Papadopoulos et al. 2021); exit
   preferred to collective voice (Zientara et al. 2023); the exacerbation anticipated (Rydzik and
   Kissoon 2021). Stated as inference from converging evidence, with the untested interaction named
   as the research opening. *P9 verification:* the interaction is still untested, and Lin et al. 2026
   is the proof — 351 matched pairs, algorithmic direction suppressing voice through felt
   responsibility, moderated on work locus of control with no climate construct in the design. Duggan
   et al. 2026 names enforced silence qualitatively. Both enter; the claim's wording does not change.
4. **Why the list stops at three.** The fourth condition differs in kind. Accountability as an
   actor-forum relation (Bovens 2007; Metcalf et al. 2023, cited rather than argued fresh); Citron and
   Pasquale 2014 on the procedural version; Ananny and Crawford 2018 on visibility without an
   audience; Vaccaro et al. 2020 as the null — internal appeal transferred no standing. *P9 couching:*
   Civera, Mosca, Casalegno and Maple 2018 is the editors' proto-version, and naming what it lacks is
   how the paper introduces the forum.
5. **Coordinative sovereignty.** Named for what competence cannot reach. Hirschman's exit and voice
   with Dowding et al. 2000; exit disciplines where available, and where mediation is unavoidable
   voice is what remains — with Morrison 2014 on the difference between channels that dignify a
   decision and input that bears on one. Competence and standing vary independently; the expert guest
   has no more influence. *P9 additions:* Lin 2025 on folk-theoretic competence that does not convert
   into power; Draper et al. 2024 detaching resignation from any knowledge deficit, which forecloses
   the reading that education would fix this; Are 2025 as the direct heir to Vaccaro — appeals exist
   and transfer no standing.
6. **The precision paragraph.** Guests do bargain with algorithms and do defer more, worst at lower
   incomes (Shen and Jin 2024); data-protection law grants expression and contest (Bayamlıoğlu 2022);
   automated recovery compensates. What mediation removes is the counterparty with authority over the
   rule. Boundary: coordinative sovereignty is not consumer sovereignty (Denegri-Knott et al. 2006);
   Shryock 2012 acknowledged — hospitality as a test of sovereignty, and this the coordinative case.

# Part IV — the design argument

## §6 Five affordances of guest-centric phygital design — 877 words, five paragraphs

> **Abstract delivered:** "The proposed framework identifies transparency, adjustability,
> contestability, human accessibility, and bypass-ability as central affordances of guest-centric
> phygital design. These affordances influence whether algorithmic mediation strengthens or weakens
> guest agency, employee discretion, interpersonal recognition, belonging, trust, and well-being."
> *(The proximal/distal restructure of the second sentence remains advisory to Pierre — AGENDA
> 31–32.)*

**Editor spine, and the section's best new move.** The paragraph opens on an editor arguing the
opposite. Mosca and La Rosa 2019 found customers hostile to technology in luxury production and
recommended keeping implementation undisclosed to preserve perceived authenticity. Against it: Mosca
2026 endorses transparency and human oversight for high-risk systems, and De Vos et al. 2026 find
employees resist when the technology's purpose is obscured. The editors disagree with each other, and
the affordance is a way of settling it — concealment answers distrust by hiding rather than by
answering, which manages a withholding of common understanding instead of repairing it. Mosca,
Giacosa and Zagni 2021 on direct against indirect distribution carries bypass-ability; De Vos and
Qesja 2022 carries the guest who wants a person.

**Professionalized by.** The contestability and oversight literatures; Yeung; Lv; Morosan and
DeFranco; Hewagama.

1. **Derivation and transparency.** Affordances follow from the withholdings: four answer, one exits.
   Transparency at the point of effect. Objections split by kind: normative (Ananny and Crawford),
   legal (Edwards and Veale); worth designing only when paired with standing. *P9 rewrite:* the
   Mosca-against-Mosca opening, then Pigac et al. 2026 for the guest-side empirical form — tiered,
   segment- and stage-adaptive disclosure, with low-digital-comfort guests wanting a person to mediate
   it. Cited as convergent evidence and as the measure of what the other four affordances still have
   to do.
2. **Adjustability.** Modify parameters without exiting the relationship; the guest whose need does
   not fit the fields. Choice architecture is not choice (Yeung 2017). Mechanism: perceived
   information autonomy (Lv et al. 2024), with a decade of hotel-app control findings behind it
   (Morosan and DeFranco 2016).
3. **Announced change and contestability.** Predictability answered: silent revision converts
   competence into a moving target, and update opacity (Hatherley 2025) explains why after-the-fact
   disclosure does not repair it. Contestability: Vaccaro sets the bar; Martin and Waldman 2022 on
   appeals as the only legitimacy-conferring mechanism; Yurrita et al. 2023 on contestability moving
   procedural fairness where oversight moves nothing; Lee et al. 2019 on outcome control. *P9
   additions:* Yurrita et al. 2025 interviewed short-term rental hosts and found that decision
   subjects need clarity about who is accountable before they can contest at all — the paper's
   counterparty argument in the subjects' own words, in a hospitality-adjacent setting. Hirsbrunner et
   al. 2025 warn that contestability by design is too system-centred and needs organizational culture
   and structural capability, which is the paper's own division stated from the design side. Lifecycle
   design (Almada 2019; Alfrink et al. 2023); a reviewer with authority to reverse.
4. **Human accessibility and bypass-ability.** The person is not the affordance; the authority is.
   *P9 strengthening:* Fink 2025 on Article 14 is the anchor — a pro forma human who rubber-stamps
   fails the effectiveness standard, meaningful oversight requires genuine override authority, and
   overseers without it become liability sponges absorbing responsibility without control. Sterz et
   al. 2024 make token oversight nameable through causal power, epistemic access, self-control and
   fitting intentions. Li and Sun 2025 show accessibility insufficient without an answerable party.
   *P9 complication, welcomed:* Choi and Chao 2024 find that under unfavourable outcomes people rate
   AI deciders as *fairer* than humans, which is evidence against any warm-body reading and evidence
   for the authority reading. Hewagama et al. 2019: hotels already ration reversal authority by rank.
   Guests attribute failure to the firm (Belanche). Bypass-ability removes the mediation: real
   alternatives restore exit (Mosca, Giacosa and Zagni 2021 on channel structure as governance
   choice); formally available but punished alternatives are none (Xu; Boo and Chua); Kim, Chung and
   Chung 2025 on mandatory self-service as shadow work transferred to the guest. A declining guest is
   not a problem to be managed.
5. **The division — the practical contribution.** Transparency, adjustability and announced change are
   partly self-suppliable, so design and training move them. Contestability, and the authority a
   reachable person carries, cannot be supplied by any individual; they take organizational design — a
   reviewer with authority, a staffing model, a decision about who answers. A property answering a
   contestability problem with guest education has misread its condition. Outcomes proposed, not
   established, and P9 confirms the gap: no empirical test of contestability affordances on fairness
   perceptions exists in any hospitality or commercial-service setting.

## §7 Against frictionless hospitality — 1,101 words, five paragraphs

> **Abstract delivered:** "The paper also challenges the assumption that seamlessness is inherently
> hospitable. By concealing decisions and eliminating opportunities for negotiation, a frictionless
> encounter may become less relational, less culturally responsive, and less capable of accommodating
> guests whose needs fall outside algorithmically constructed norms." *(One movement of the paper, as

## §9 Conclusion — 96 words, one paragraph

> **Abstract delivered:** "Ultimately, guest-centric phygital hospitality depends not on making
> technology invisible, but on ensuring that guests and employees retain the agency to determine how
> technology participates in the performance of hospitality."

Algorithmic systems perform the host's functions; their participation will not reverse; the question
is the terms. Guest-centric phygital hospitality does not depend on invisibility but on retained
agency over how technology participates — and on organizations accepting that part of that agency
cannot be trained into people and has to be designed into the arrangement. *With Germann Molz framing
the paper, the "somebody still owes the guest a welcome" beat now belongs here rather than in the
short draft: her guests without hosts, answered.*
