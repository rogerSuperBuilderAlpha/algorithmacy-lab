# Buildout plan — extending DRAFT.md

DRAFT.md stands at 3,276 words with citations. The plan below adds roughly 2,400, landing near
5,700 — a legal submission whether or not the reference list counts against the ceiling, with room
to grow if the exclusive reading is confirmed. Every extension is written in Pierre's voice:
explanatory, hedged where the evidence hedges, one idea per sentence, signposts kept. Nothing from
the compressed register comes back.

The rule for choosing extensions: each one must either discharge an editor ask, pay a P9 repair, or
add a concrete case that makes an abstract claim checkable. Nothing goes in because it is
interesting. The reserved ALC property names stay out, as always.

## §1 Introduction — add ~150 words

One move only: the Germann Molz engagement, which currently shares a citation with Riordan and
deserves the three sentences the differentiation needs. She asks what becomes of hospitality when
platforms erase the human host; this paper asks whether the party that took over the host's
functions inherited the host's obligations. Her title poses the question; the paper answers it.
Pays the first P9 repair where a referee will look for it first.

## §2 Hospitality is more than service — add ~300 words

Two extensions. First, a short paragraph introducing the five criteria through the editors' own
vocabulary before the hospitality canon deepens them — Batat's culinary-tourism ethnography has the
experience co-produced with service providers and names consideration among what the guest seeks
(Batat 2021), which lets the criteria arrive from inside the special issue's scholarship rather
than as an import. Second, the two narrowed absences stated properly, since Pierre's "paying much
less attention" currently gestures at what should be two precise sentences: the nearest existing
work theorizes what the grateful guest gives back, not what the guest owes (Manfreda and Harkison
2025), and the one validated instrument measuring a guest-to-host flow measures benefits accruing
to destination residents, not conduct toward a commercial host (Shi et al. 2025). Pays two P9
repairs and adds one editor-spine move.

## §3 The three-party arrangement — add ~350 words

Three extensions. First, the host question needs its nearest misses named, because they are the
literature the referees know: current work asks whether guests can experience a machine as
hospitable (Lee and Lu 2024; Liu et al. 2026) and what governance duties robots create (Sharma and
Mattila 2025), and both questions stand exactly where the obligation question should be. Second,
one compact machine-ethics paragraph: care is owed in virtue of need, fiduciary duty in virtue of
trust, governance duty in virtue of risk; welcome is owed in virtue of arrival, and it alone
constitutes the role (Kropf et al. 2026; Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021; Introna 2010). Third,
the split verdict on discretion, which keeps the paper honest: the constraint is documented back of
house, the front desk retains sanctioned latitude, and the guest-facing half is thin (Bendoly 2013;
Garcia et al. 2026). Pays the Lee-and-Lu/Sharma-and-Mattila repair.

## §4 When technology helps and when it replaces — add ~350 words, plus Table 1

Two extensions and the table. First, the vignettes, which the section promises and does not yet

## §5 What people can learn, and what they cannot — add ~300 words

Two extensions. First, the Zhou differentiation, which is one sentence of citation in the draft
and should be three of argument: the first validated competency scale folds the capacity to appeal

undo (Zhou et al. 2025). Second, the employee-silence paragraph completed to the calibrated P8

convergent inference, with the interaction between prior voice climate and algorithmic management
named as untested — and the study that came closest moderated on individual disposition, not

## §6 Five design features — add ~350 words

Two extensions. First, the section's best available opening, currently absent: an editor has
argued the other side. Mosca and La Rosa found luxury customers hostile to production technology
and recommended keeping it undisclosed; the same editor now endorses transparency and human
oversight for high-risk systems, and the third editor's team finds employees resist when a
technology's purpose is obscured (Mosca and La Rosa 2019; Mosca 2026; De Vos et al. 2026). The

preserving authority — the point Pierre's "liability sponge in all but name" sentence already
makes. Pays no repair but discharges the editor-spine commitment where it shows most.
