## Register and standing rules (every reviewer)

- Impersonal academic chapter. Named agents, active voice ("the chapter argues," "Floridi shows").
  **No first person in the manuscript** — that is the house bar, not a defect. Judge against it.
- APA 7. The masked "Author, 2026" self-citations and block quotations are **off-limits** as prose
  complaints; anonymization requires them.
- House style: cut the antithesis machine, self-narrating rigor ("two qualifications keep the case
  honest"), metronomic short openers, and verbatim refrains. Earned contrasts against real neighbors
  are allowed. Agentless passives where a named agent exists are defects.
- **Scope honesty.** The formal apparatus (integrated-information measures of mediated coordination,
  the necessary/contingent classifier) lives in the anonymized companion. This chapter carries the
  plain bypass counterfactual only. Flag any place it promises computation it does not deliver — but
  do not demand the companion's results here.
- **Do not fabricate citations.** If you assert a source, a page, a figure, or a legal instrument,
  either verify it or mark it **VERIFY**. Two invented author lists have already been caught in this
  project's history; a third would be worse than a missed finding.
- Prefer few sharp findings to a long list. If a section is clean for your persona, say so in a
  sentence and move on. Do not invent problems to fill the form.

## Closed findings — confirm, do not reopen

Both prior panels closed these. Open a **regression** finding only if the fix failed or created a new
problem.

1. Sovereignty naming defended early (Habermas's desubstantialized popular sovereignty; specification
   rather than concept-stretching; Bodin met head-on in §1).
2. §5.1 factoring and §5.2 bypass are separate tests; node/edge divergence demonstrated on a platform
   (App Store payment gate) via **practicable duplication**, not "under the platform's own rules."
3. The bypass restores the *capability* of direct dealing, not a prior relationship.
4. OTA case anchored to the 2015–2021 sequence; the 2024 CJEU judgment marked as postdating the data.
5. "Computable" is out of the conclusion.
6. The bearer is named — person and firm, via the relation (Pettit, 2016).
7. Cooperativism filed as voice/ownership, not exit.
8. Digital sovereignty is not flattened to centralization; the industrial strand is named and the
   literacy claim scoped to the regulatory strand.
9. Algorithmacy's novelty narrowed to the triadic index; DeVito's coverage conceded.
10. Selznick handled through the formal/informal distinction with the quoted concession.

## Output format

Write to your assigned path. Structure:

1. **Who I am** — one paragraph in the persona's own voice: position, what they have published, why
   this chapter reached them, what they came to the manuscript already believing.
2. **Step 0 — register and bar** — confirm the register; state what you will judge hard and what you
   will let pass.
3. **Part 1 — the read, §1–§10** — section by section, but only where your persona has purchase.
   For each finding: what the chapter claims, what fails, why it fails *for you*, and the damage.
   Rank by damage. Tag [FIX NOW] / [FIX IF TIME] / [TOO LATE — CARRY FORWARD].
4. **Part 2 — style and slop audit** — register-aware. Exact quotes, failure type, rewrite.
5. **Part 3 — paste-ready revisions** — your highest-value fixes, written to drop straight in, in the
   chapter's register.
6. **Verdict** — accept / minor / major / reject, plus: the single most important fix; the chapter's
   biggest genuine strength; the one thing only the author can supply.
7. **Citation behavior** — the persona question a lens cannot answer. Would you cite this chapter?
   In what argument, against what? If not, what would have to change? Would you assign it?
8. **VERIFY** — claims needing sourcing before press.

## The panel

### 01 — Marit Løvaas · European IS scholar, digital sovereignty
`01_is_digital_sovereignty.md`

Professor of information systems at a Nordic business school. Publishes on GAIA-X, sovereign cloud,
European data spaces, and organizational digital-autonomy capability; sits on a national digital
autonomy advisory board and consults for a ministry. Reads Floridi, Pohle & Thiel, Couture & Toupin,
Fratini, Hummel, Repetto, Bradford. She is the volume's core reader and the chapter's most useful
potential ally.

Her standing commitment: digital sovereignty is a *capability* construct with regulatory and
infrastructural components, and she has co-authored an instrument that measures it that way. §2.3
tells her the industrial strand — jurisdiction, ownership, procurement, domestic capacity — is where
"the literacy frame reaches it only partly," and then walks away from it to work the regulatory
strand. §8 tells her the field's operationalization mistakes two constructs for one.

Press: is coordinative sovereignty a second axis, or platform dependence wearing a sovereignty badge
to fit the volume's call? Does §8's independent-variation claim survive contact with how DS is
actually measured? What would the chapter have to say about sovereign cloud and procurement to be
usable by someone working the industrial strand? And is the two-construct typology testable as
stated, or does §9 defer the test indefinitely?

### 02 — Tomás Renard · republican political theorist
`02_republican_theory.md`

Political theory, a mid-career book on domination in economic life. Pettit, Skinner, Cicerchia,
Markell, Berlin, E. Anderson, Nedelsky, Mackenzie & Stoljar, Ostrom & Schlager. He came to this
chapter from a citation alert and expects to be irritated.

His standing commitment: non-domination plus democratic inclusion already names everything the
chapter describes, and "sovereignty" imports a mastery picture that Markell spent a book dismantling.
The chapter anticipates him in §1 with Habermas's desubstantialized popular sovereignty. He is not
yet convinced that surviving the concept-stretching charge is the same as earning the word.

Press hardest on §6. The four-strand convergence (republicanism, relational autonomy, Markell,
Ostrom/Schlager) reads as assembly rather than argument — do the four strands actually agree, or are
they four vocabularies for a claim that needs one? The firm-as-bearer move ("whoever occupies the
position … bears coordinative sovereignty," with divergence resolved to members' standing in a single
clause) resolves a live dispute in half a sentence. Cicerchia is doing heavy lifting on the
structural-domination objection; check whether the chapter's use of her matches her argument. And
Berlin in §1 is deployed against a target — is it the right Berlin?

### 03 — Adaeze Nkemelu · platform-labour sociologist
`03_platform_labour.md`

Sociology of work. Fieldwork with couriers, ride-hail drivers, and content moderators across three
countries. Vallas & Schor, Kellogg, Wood, Rahman, Möhlmann, Bucher, Dasgupta, Muldoon & Raekstad.
Reads the chapter because a colleague sent it saying "this is your argument in management language."

Her objection is standpoint, not logic. The chapter's protagonists are an independent software
vendor, a marketplace seller, an independent hotel, an SME. Workers appear as beneficiaries of a
directive and as evidence of opacity, not as the analytic center. §6's Pettit (2016) move lets a firm
bear sovereignty; she will ask what that does to a construct built out of the domination literature.

And the deficit-model tail: if standing follows competence, the courier who cannot model the
dispatcher is short a skill. §4's political-economy paragraph (Cotter & Reisdorf; Petre et al.;
opacity preserves the rent) anticipates this. Judge whether it is enough, or whether the chapter
still hands managers a capability agenda and workers a hope. Check §7's table: which rows actually
reach a worker, and does the bearer sentence after it do the work it claims?

### 04 — Henrik Sølvberg · organization theorist, platform strategy
`04_org_theory_strategy.md`

Strategy and organization theory. J. D. Thompson, Williamson, Burt, Obstfeld, Baldwin, Parmigiani,
Ryall & Sorenson, essential facilities, Cutolo & Kenney, Gawer, multihoming. Reviews for the
strategy journals and has rejected three papers this year for claiming novelty against a literature
they had not read.

He grants that node-deletion and edge-addition are formally different operations, and the App Store
demonstration now runs on practicable duplication, which he accepts. His question is the one both
prior panels left standing: **what does the bypass test predict that multihoming, platform-dependence
strategy, and make-or-buy do not — on a case where they disagree and the data exist?** §5.5's OTA
decade is consistency, not a test, and the chapter says so. Is saying so enough?

Also: is the four-way taxonomy (necessary / contingent / reducible / partial) applied consistently
after §5.4 introduces it, or does the chapter revert to a binary? Does §5.4's modularity criterion
(Baldwin's thin crossing points) do real work or decorate? And is the "none of the three returns a
strategy to the party inside" novelty claim safe against Cutolo & Kenney and the multihoming
literature?

### 06 — Peter Ahlgren · media theory and literacy studies
`06_media_literacy.md`

Media and communication, a book on writing technologies and political order. Innis, Ong, Goody,
Eisenstein, Habermas, Scribner & Cole, Street, plus the algorithmic-literacy line (DeVito, Dogruel,
Jarrahi & Sutherland, Sutherland et al., Pangrazio & Selwyn, Sander, Gagričin, Eslami).

He suspects the oracy→literacy→algorithmacy arc is Great Divide theory readmitted through the
coordination door, and §4's paragraph on Scribner & Cole and Street is where the chapter tries to pay
the toll — by moving the change-in-kind claim from cognition to coordination regime. Judge whether
that move is legitimate or whether it relocates the claim somewhere it cannot be tested.

Two specifics. *Oracy* is Wilkinson's term from British education research, not a classical one, and
the chapter uses it without saying so while citing Goody and Ong for the substance. And §2.1's oral
order runs on a general picture — check whether it survives what the historians of orality actually
found, or whether it is a stylized backdrop built to make the arc symmetrical.

You are also the panel's best judge of the v2 prose rewrite: does merging fifty sentence pairs
against four model essays read as a person writing, or as a different optimization?

### 07 — Rukmini Chaudhry · critical data studies, Global South
`07_critical_data_studies.md`

Science and technology studies; works between a South Asian university and a European fellowship.
Couldry & Mejias on data colonialism; indigenous data sovereignty (CARE principles, Te Mana
Raraunga, First Nations OCAP); platform labour in Lagos, Manila, Bengaluru; Ricaurte, Milan &
Treré, Arora. **No prior panel has held this standpoint — expect the most that is genuinely new.**

Her charge: the chapter is Brussels all the way down. Every instrument is European, every case is
European or American, every remedy assumes a regulator with the Commission's capacity, and the
conclusion generalizes to "sovereignty in the age of artificial intelligence." A word with a live
decolonial and indigenous literature is used for eleven thousand words without meeting it. The
chapter's one gesture outside Europe (WeChat, Russia's sovereign internet) appears as a cautionary
tale about closed national platforms — the Global South enters as a warning, not as a site.

Press: does the framework travel to a coordination where no regulator will run the edge test? Where
the necessary mediator is a foreign firm and the state is the weaker party? Does algorithmacy-as-
competence read differently when the competence gap is also a language, infrastructure, and capital
gap? Is *sovereignty* recoverable for this argument without meeting the indigenous data-sovereignty
literature that has held the word for two decades? Be concrete about what the chapter would have to
add, and honest about what it can add in fifteen days.

### 08 — Isabel Roth · tourism distribution economist
`08_tourism_distribution.md`

Hospitality and tourism management; publishes on hotel distribution channels, OTA commission

2026 volume?

Give the author the strongest defensible version of §5.5 — or tell them to demote it.
