## The headline: the hotels did the right thing

Three reviewers, arriving from tourism economics, hostile generalism, and trade-association practice,
independently destroyed the chapter's flagship narrative — and the truth they left behind is better
for the chapter than the story it prints.

§8 reads the hotel decade as a classification error: "the hotels of section 5.5 show what that waste
looks like at sector scale." §10 closes on it: "Europe's hotels spent a decade prying open a gate
their dependence did not run through, and came out of it more dependent than they went in."

- **Reviewer 2** noticed the internal contradiction without leaving the manuscript. What the hotels
  attacked was the parity clause, which §5.5 itself classifies as *contingent* and which §8's own
  decision procedure prescribes attacking. They ran the procedure correctly and won the remedy.
- **Roth** checked the evidence. Over the chapter's own stated 2015–2021 window direct bookings
  *rose*, 52.9% → 55.2%. Seventy percent of the decade's decline falls in the 2013→2015 wave, before
  the treatment. Meanwhile channel-manager adoption went 39% → 62% and manual rate management fell
  56% → 27%. The hotels did not spend the decade trying to leave. They spent it building algorithmacy.
- **Ekwueme** supplied the mechanism from his own sector: classify correctly, win the remedy, and
  watch the gatekeeper reprice the bypass.

The chapter's best empirical case therefore demonstrates something it does not currently claim: the
diagnostic worked, the actors acted on it, and dependence still deepened — because opening a gate is
not the same as escaping through it. That is a stronger result than a morality tale about
misclassification, and it is the one the data support.

## The six spine findings

Ranked by how many reviewers hit them from independent directions.

### S1. The classification is index-dependent, and the chapter says so once

The chapter already knows this. §5.2 makes the OTA verdict actor-relative for the traveler who knows
the hotel against the traveler who does not, and says "the diagnostic sorts functions for a given
dependent party." It then proceeds as though that were the only index.

- **Ekwueme:** "comparable cost" has no reference point. Self-distribution is contingent for a
  200-person firm and necessary for a 40-person one. The classification is a function of firm size.
- **Chaudhry:** contingency is indexed to a jurisdiction. §5.2 says the DMA's anti-steering
  obligations are the edge test run by a regulator, and Apple's alternative terms apply to apps
  distributed in the EU. Same function, same firm, same month: contingent for a Berlin developer,
  unexitable for a Nairobi one.
- **Nkemelu:** indexed to the actor's legal position. A firm that runs a bypass has a commercial
  dispute; a worker who runs one commits the offence she is removed for.

**Fix:** one paragraph in §5.2 naming the index set — dependent party, firm capability, jurisdiction,
legal standing — and one clause in §8 conceding that the manager's verdict is not portable. Chaudhry
notes this yields the framework's best empirical prediction: exit-seeking inside DMA reach,
voice-building outside, classification held constant.

### S2. The tolled bypass has no cell

Reached independently by three reviewers, and it is the panel's largest genuine theory gap.

- **Ekwueme:** an opened bypass whose price the mediator sets is not exit but a repriced gate, and
  that is the normal outcome rather than the exception.
- **Vaudrey:** the record proves it. The regulator ran the edge test on the App Store; Apple's June
  2025 EU terms re-priced the restored tie through an acquisition fee, store-services fee, and the
  Core Technology Commission. The tie opened. The toll moved. §5.2's "the gate's force disappears"
  is now falsified by the case the chapter uses to demonstrate the test.
- **Chaudhry:** Pix and UPI reproduced payment authorization — §5.4's exemplar of *necessary*
  integrating work — outside the card networks. Neither an edge operation nor node substitution but a
  third move, and public infrastructure made it.

**Fix now:** a qualification in §5.2 conceding that an opened tie can be re-tolled, and that the
counterfactual establishes structure rather than outcome. **Carry forward:** the taxonomy needs a
fifth term, and it belongs to the companion paper or the *Organization Theory* submission.

### S3. §5.2's novelty claim is staked against a comparison set chosen so it survives

Three reviewers, three routes.

- **Sølvberg:** the claim that neighbouring tests "cannot ask" the edge question because each "holds
  the network of who may deal with whom fixed" is false of five works the chapter cites elsewhere.
  Obstfeld (2005) is *tertius iungens*, a broker who creates ties, and the chapter cites him as though
  he were *tertius gaudens*. Ryall & Sorenson (2007) rerun their model with endogenous relation
  formation and find brokerage rents mostly vanish. Hahl et al. (2016) condition brokerage returns on
  a credible disintermediation threat. Gu & Zhu (2021) is platform disintermediation and is cited in
  the same subsection.
- **Vaudrey:** essential facilities has moved. *C-233/23 Alphabet (Android Auto)*, Grand Chamber,
  25 February 2025, holds that the *Bronner* indispensability criterion does not apply where a
  platform was built for third-party use. The Court drew nearly the chapter's own line, which makes
  "infrastructure that cannot be duplicated" both dated and a missed opportunity.
- **Chaudhry:** the surviving standpoint claim — the test "returns a strategy to the party inside the
  coordination" — fails on its own terms, because every strategy the chapter returns on a contingent
  verdict (interoperability, portability, an alternative channel) is a regulatory achievement rather
  than a firm action.

**Fix:** Sølvberg's R1 retreats to the defensible claim — asked from inside, about a function,
returning a course of action — and drops "cannot ask." Chaudhry's index qualification then repairs
what remains of "returns a strategy." Sølvberg's calibration is worth recording: as written the
chapter survives the IGI volume, and §5.2 would be desk-rejected at *Organization Theory* in January.

### S4. Voice has no robustness condition, and no account of removal

Two reviewers from opposite ends of the political spectrum, one hole.

- **Renard:** republican freedom is modally demanding, and Pettit says so on p. 3 of the paper the
  chapter already cites — benefits must remain available "even under counterfactual circumstances
  where others turn indifferent or hostile." §6 makes effective voice the currency, §7 concedes
  oversight boards are "bounded by the constituting platform" and counts them anyway, and §9 measures
  felt voice. Voice a platform can withdraw is the benevolent master's open door.
- **Nkemelu:** Hirschman's exit and voice are both the member's acts. Deactivation is the mediator's,
  and it prices voice. §9's own proposed test item asks whether an actor can speak "without needing
  to … avoid provoking a power that could retaliate" — so retaliation sits in the instrument and
  nowhere in the theory. Three of the chapter's own sources (Rahman, Bucher et al., Curchod et al.)
  describe conduct organized around fear of removal, and the chapter reads all three as evidence of
  opacity.

**Fix now:** Renard's three insertions, under 200 words, propagating into the §7 table and the §9
instrument, plus one paragraph from Nkemelu naming removal as the mediator's counter-power. Together
they close the same hole from both sides and cost less than a page.

### S5. The four-way taxonomy is abandoned after §5.4

- **Reviewer 2:** "partial" and "reducible" appear nowhere after §5.4 — not in §5.5's verdict, §7's
  two-column table, §8's two-branch procedure, §9, §10, or the abstract. Half the taxonomy carries no
  institutional or managerial consequence.
- **Ekwueme:** two of his eleven App Store functions had no cell at all — the mediator as competitor
  (self-preferencing) and non-triadic technical dependency.
- **Sølvberg:** the four terms are three levels of analysis printed as a flat list.

The damage compounds: in both of Reviewer 2's other structural findings, the missing category was the
right answer. Marketplace demand-matching is *partial* in §5.4 on the same reasoning that makes OTA
aggregation *necessary* in §5.5, and the §1 ISV comes back contingent or partial on every function.

**Fix now:** carry *partial* into §7's table and §8's procedure, since the chapter already knows what
to do with a partial verdict — §5.4's "contest the bypassable part and accept the integrating one."

### S6. §5.5's evidence run is broken by the chapter's own cited source

Roth confirmed the two headline figures verbatim in Schegg (2024), in the same weighted column. Four
things around them fail.

1. **The complement claim.** OTAs gained 9.9pp; direct lost 6.7pp; the hotel's own booking engine
   *rose*, 7.4% → 12.3%. What collapsed was telephone, fax and walk-in, and tour operators and
   wholesalers. ECN 2017 §4.5 para 37 — cited in the same paragraph — says exactly this.
2. **The clock.** Direct rose over the chapter's stated 2015–2021 window.
3. **The description.** Not a share of European overnight stays but a country-weighted mean of
   hotel-level self-reports, with unstable coverage across waves ("Rest Europe" carries 25.6% of the
   weight; Italy's 2013 direct value is unreported).
4. **"Spent a decade trying to leave"** is unsupported, and the channel-manager evidence cuts against
   it.

**The replacement evidence is better and Roth found it:** the European Commission's 2022 study
compares Austria and Belgium, which banned parity outright, against four non-banning states and finds
no association with OTA use. A genuine control-group comparison on independent hotels, supporting the
chapter's reading. Paste-ready replacements for §5.5 paragraphs 1 and 4–6 and the §8/§10 collateral
sentences are in `08_tourism_distribution.md` Part 3.

## Which draft ships: `chapter.md`

Ahlgren measured the v2 rewrite against its own objective and it failed. Coefficient of variation
*fell*, 0.565 → 0.540; the standard deviation rose only because the mean rose 15%. Sentences of eight
words or fewer went 62 → 41, sentences of 55+ words went 6 → 15, semicolons 25 → 44, and across
roughly seventy changes there were zero splits. The pass made the prose longer and uniform in a
different register.

**It is also not prose-only, which corrects HANDOFF §0e.** v2 swaps the defined term *mediator* for
undefined *third party* in two of §5.3's definitional sentences, and three merges introduced
pronoun-referent ambiguities. Ship `chapter.md`. Every finding in this panel applies to both drafts
and every reviewer quoted spans rather than line numbers, so the fixes port without translation.

## What carries forward

**To the companion paper.** The fifth taxonomic term for a re-tolled bypass. Sølvberg's stock/flow
distinction between transferable and re-performed integrating work, which yields a within-sector
prediction Armstrong's bottleneck cannot make: dependence falls where repeat guests dominate and
rises where discovery does. Chaudhry's public-infrastructure move (Pix, UPI, ONDC), which is neither
node substitution nor edge restoration.

**To the *Organization Theory* submission, 31 January 2027.** Sølvberg's calibration is the actionable
one: §5.2 as written would be desk-rejected there. The comparison-set repair, the three
quasi-experimental parity papers, and a divergence case with real data are the price of that venue.
Nkemelu's removal-as-counter-power and Renard's robustness condition both belong in a journal-length
treatment rather than a chapter.

## What must survive

Named by multiple reviewers as the chapter's real contribution, and none of the majors touches it:

- **The node/edge distinction.** Nine of ten reviewers would cite it. It is the single most-praised
  thing in the manuscript and the reason four of them would assign the chapter.
- **§5.4's platform-as-portfolio.** Sølvberg would cite it against Cutolo & Kenney and the multihoming
  literature; Ekwueme classified six of eleven functions with it and had a belief corrected.
- **§8's independent-variation typology.** Løvaas has a paper showing top-quartile digital-sovereignty
  firms absorb platform shocks no better than bottom-quartile ones, and says §8's separation is the
  explanation she lacks.
- **§6's structural argument against cooperativism** — ownership cannot make a necessary mediator
  leaveable. Nkemelu says it beats her own managerial-decay explanation and Renard would quote it
  against the platform-cooperativism literature.
- **§3.2's adaptivity-beats-opacity argument** and **§2.3's "the literate response is to demand more
  disclosure."** Ekwueme skipped almost all the theory and fought to keep that paragraph.
- **§4's eBay vignette**, again.

## One thing the panel agrees on that the author will not enjoy

Four reviewers would cite the mechanism and decline the name. Renard would cite the bypass test and
rename the construct with a footnote saying why; Reviewer 2 would cite node/edge and not the term,
because §9 concedes there is no instrument; Chaudhry would not cite *sovereignty* without the
indigenous literature, since that would make her responsible for the omission; Løvaas expects the
durable citation to be §8's typology, with §5's test cited from the companion once it has a name.

The diagnostic is travelling further than the vocabulary. That is a fact about the next paper more
than about this chapter, and §9's third study — the two-step design, runnable now, with no new scale
and no companion — is the cheapest thing that would change it.
