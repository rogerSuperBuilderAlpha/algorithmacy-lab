# Reader's review — *Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era*

## Step 0 — Register

This is an impersonal, double-anonymized academic chapter for an edited volume; the third-person voice
("the chapter argues," "the diagnostic decides") is deliberate and right, and I judge it as such — not
by whether it's chatty. The only question that matters here is whether the prose is *alive and
load-bearing* inside that register: whether an idea lands in each paragraph, or whether the chapter is
performing scholarship (announcing its own rigor, running an antithesis on a metronome, filling a
paragraph shape) rather than doing it.

---

## Part 1 — Section by section (§1–§10)

### §1 Introduction — strong
The opening sentence is the best kind of hook: concrete, falsifiable-sounding, and it *is* the thesis.
"A firm can comply with every instrument of Europe's digital regulation and still watch its market
position turn on ranking decisions it cannot see, predict, or contest." The ISV / merchant / creator
triad that follows earns its keep — three different actors, one shape. The argument advances cleanly:
compliance gap → sovereignty has always been paired with a communicative competence → the platform case
is new → name it. Nothing circles.

One tic already visible: the closer "The two vary independently, and telling them apart is what the
argument turns on." That "is what the argument turns on" formula recurs at least three more times
(§8 "is the first return on algorithmacy," §9 "is where the two-construct thesis stands or falls,"
§3.1 "That is the shape the rest of the chapter works with"). Individually fine; as a habit it's a
verbal fingerprint. Note it now, watch it compound.

### §2 Sovereignty and its sensibilities — earns its length, but the front is slack
**Does the history earn its place before the platform argument? Yes — because of §2.3, not §2.1–2.2.**
The oracy and literacy subsections are vivid ("Law was what the elders could recite"; "a scribe is a
different creature from a better speaker") but they're the expected move and could each shed a few
sentences. What justifies the whole detour is the *nesting* argument at the end of §2.3: because each
sensibility is real and hard-won, its holders meet a new medium with the competence they already have,
so "the literate response is to demand more disclosure, more documentation, more rights to read." That
is the diagnostic engine the rest of the chapter runs on. Without it §2 would be throat-clearing; with
it, §2 is load-bearing. Keep the section, trim §2.1–2.2.

Slack spots: "What the chapter needs is the modest claim that survives every qualification of media
determinism" — this narrates the chapter's own caution instead of just making the modest claim. Mild
but it's the first instance of a habit that gets worse in §5–§6.

### §3 The platform turn — the analytic high point of the front half
This is where a skeptical neighbor is won over, because the chapter argues *against itself first*: "The
observation is important, and it is easy to overclaim. Do platforms really introduce a new coordination
logic...? What they run is largely the existing logics in algorithmic form." Conceding the obvious
objection (algorithmic management is just hierarchy in new clothes) before answering it is exactly the
move that makes the "opaque + interested + adaptive mediator" distinction credible. The three-things-
kept-apart structure (logic old / topology old, Simmel / conjunction new) is genuinely clarifying.

§3.2's four-part "why literacy cannot reach it" is a real enumeration, not padding — Burrell's third
opacity, Ananny & Crawford's transparency-without-institution, Cutolo & Kenney's no-leverage, Hildebrandt's
text-vs-code. §3.3 lands a keeper: "A transparent bottleneck is still a bottleneck, and a disclosed
objective the coordinated actor has no standing to contest is still an objective imposed on it." No
boredom in §3.

### §4 Algorithmacy — the most vivid section, and slightly overstuffed
The "one morning her orders halve" vignette is the best writing in the chapter: it dramatizes inference-
from-effects with no jargon, and "She has read nothing. There was nothing to read." earns its bluntness.
The three-part competence (inferential / translational / temporal) is clean and the positioning against
algorithmic literacy / critical data literacy is fair and well-cited.

The one structural weakness: §4 illustrates the *same* point twice at length. The marketplace-seller
paragraph ("A seller on a large marketplace who has learned to succeed...") and the "one morning"
vignette both show inference-from-effects on a marketplace ranking. The second is better; the first is
now largely redundant with it. House rule 4 (say each point once) applies — compress the abstract seller
paragraph into a sentence and let the vignette carry it. The "unevenly held / rent-preserving" paragraph
is a strong idea ("a competence distributed like capital distributes standing like capital") and should
stay.

### §5 The diagnostic — the intellectual core; also where jargon first spikes
The necessary/contingent bypass test is the chapter's original engine, and the four-way sort
(necessary / contingent / partial / reducible) is genuinely useful. The §5.4 hotel/OTA case is the best
worked example in the piece: billboard effect = necessary integrating work, rate-parity clause =
contingent gate, and "a hotel that spends its whole effort trying to leave the agency has mistaken the
necessary function for the contingent one" is the thesis made operational. This is the paragraph to
lead a talk with.

Jargon spike: §5.1 is the densest passage for a neighbor — "does some cut of the modeled system lose
causal information that exists only in the joint behavior of its parts?" The chapter handles it well by
*deferring* the integrated-information formalism to the companion paper and giving the reader a runnable
counterfactual (open the direct tie, recompute) instead. That's the right call; don't add more IIT here.

Watch the self-narration creeping in: "The claim that the platform situation is new in kind can be made
precise, and the precision pays." and "Formalizing his categories is what yields the payoff." Both
announce that a payoff is coming instead of delivering it. Cut them; the payoff (exit maps to
bypassability, voice to necessity) is right there and stands alone.

### §6 The definition — where the theory piles up past a neighbor's comfort
This is the densest section by name-count: republican non-domination (Pettit, Skinner, Muldoon &
Raekstad), relational autonomy (Mackenzie & Stoljar, Nedelsky), Markell's involvement critique, Ostrom's
commons — then, in the same section, concept-stretching (Repetto), relational sovereignty (Bannerman),
functional sovereignty (Pasquale), the platform sovereign (Cohen). Eight theoretical positions in about
two pages. Each is correctly used, but the cumulative density is fatiguing, and a smart neighbor from
management or law will start skimming the roll-call. Consider cutting the neighbor-comparison to the
two nearest (Bannerman and Pasquale/Cohen) and moving concept-stretching to a footnote.

The prose problem here is the **"From X it takes Y" anaphora**: four consecutive paragraphs open "From
republican theory it takes... From relational autonomy it takes... From Markell it takes... And from
Ostrom it takes..." One or two would read as elegant parallelism; four is a metronome. Vary the entries.

The three clarifications are useful and do real defensive work (the protected-but-unstanding worker is a
sharp figure). But the section closer — "That is the object the crowded neighborhood describes around
and leaves unoccupied" — is the worst sentence in the chapter (see Verdict), and it also *restates*
"The nearer neighbors mark the space without occupying it" from four paragraphs up. One of the two goes.

### §7 Realizing coordinative sovereignty — correct, thorough, and the most boring section
This is where I got bored, and I can name why: seven institutions surveyed in seven paragraphs of nearly
identical shape — name the institution, state what it does, state its one limit. Platform cooperativism
(limit: scale/capital), data trusts (limit: leaves mediator in place), bargaining, contestability
(limit: case-level only), interop (exit move), oversight boards (limit: dependence on the platform),
regulation. The content is right and the diagnostic-sorting frame (exit instruments at contingent
functions, voice instruments at necessary ones) holds it together, but the uniform paragraph rhythm
makes it read like a catalogue. Break the shape: fold the two weakest entries into others, and let at
least one paragraph open on a full sentence rather than the institution's name.

### §8 Implications — recovers energy; the concrete pairings work
The independent-variation cases are the payoff of the whole two-construct apparatus and they're
concrete: the marketplace seller who "can see how it ranks without letting it contest the ranking"
(high digital / low coordinative) against the platform worker who "owns none of the infrastructure...
yet under the European platform-work directive now holds information rights" (low digital / high
coordinative). That reversal is the single most persuasive evidence that the two constructs really do
vary independently. The openness/control reframe at the end ("a closed, sovereign national platform in
which users have no voice reproduces the co-optation it was meant to escape, now under a domestic flag")
is a genuinely good idea landing. Strong section.

### §9 Future research — competent, standard, skimmable
Four measurement directions, in dependence order. Nothing wrong; nothing alive. It reads like the
required future-directions section, which is fine for the genre. The one idea worth protecting is the
fourth — the correspondence between *reported* standing (survey) and *afforded* standing (formal model),
which is a construct-validity move only this framework can make. Consider promoting it above the two
scale-building directions, which are more routine.

### §10 Conclusion — earns its close, but recaps the arc one time too many
"constitutive priesthood" / "algorithmate few" is a good closing image and the final line lands. But the
oracy→literacy→algorithmacy arc is narrated a fourth time here (§1, §2, §4, §10). By the conclusion the
reader has it; the recap can be halved. The last two paragraphs also both perform a summary-and-charge
("The chapter has offered a conceptual apparatus, and it is meant to be put to work..."); one would do.

## Part 2 — The machine tells I'd bet on

These are the specific places a sharp reader's ear catches the machine, with fixes in the chapter's own
impersonal-agentive register (named agent + active verb, never first person).

**Tell 1 — self-narrating the payoff ("does work," "pays," "yields the payoff").**
The chapter repeatedly announces that a move is valuable instead of showing the value.
- "The portfolio view does organizational work." (§5.4)
- "The diagnostic thus does real institutional labor." (§5.3 / §7)
- "...can be made precise, and the precision pays." (§5)
- "Formalizing his categories is what yields the payoff." (§5.3)
Why it reads as machine: it's the prose equivalent of a section heading — it labels the paragraph's
function rather than advancing it. House rule 2 forbids exactly this.
Fix pattern (keep the agent, delete the announcement, state the result): replace "The portfolio view
does organizational work" with a sentence that *does* the work — "Disaggregating the platform by
function tells the coordinated actor where to spend effort: exit against the placement toll, voice
against the fulfillment it cannot reproduce."

**Tell 2 — the demonstrative closer "That is the X."**
- "That is the shape the rest of the chapter works with." (§3.1)
- "That is the object the crowded neighborhood describes around and leaves unoccupied." (§6)
- "That is high digital sovereignty beside low coordinative sovereignty." (§8, this one is fine — it
  labels a concrete case)
Why it reads as machine: used to *end* a paragraph on an abstract noun, it's a filler cadence. Fix by
either cutting (the paragraph already made the point) or naming the agent: "The rest of the chapter
works with this triad" rather than "That is the shape the rest of the chapter works with."

**Tell 3 — the "From X it takes Y" anaphora run four deep (§6).**
Four consecutive paragraph openers on the identical frame. Why it reads as machine: uniform paragraph
shape is the single most reliable AI tell, and here it's literal. Fix: vary the verb and the entry
point so each theory enters on its own terms — "Republican theory supplies non-domination... Relational
autonomy corrects a hidden assumption... Markell adds what non-domination omits... Ostrom shows the
positive form..." (see Part 3).

**Tell 4 — the antithesis metronome on the two-sovereignty pair.**
The infrastructure-vs-standing contrast is the thesis, so it's *allowed* to recur, but it's currently
run as a paired-sentence machine at least four times in near-identical shape:
- "Digital sovereignty asks who controls the infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty asks what autonomy
  remains for the actor coordinating through it." (§1)
- "Openness and control are properties of the infrastructure. Coordinative sovereignty is a property of
  the coordinated actor's relation to it." (§8)
- "Digital sovereignty is a literacy construct... Coordinative sovereignty is an algorithmacy construct." (§8)
Why it reads as machine: the reader learns the rhythm and stops hearing the content. Keep the strongest
instance (§8's literacy/algorithmacy pairing is the sharpest), and rewrite one or two of the others so
the contrast is carried by the sentence's logic rather than by twinned clauses.

**Tell 5 — hedging that narrates caution rather than stating the limit.**
- "What the chapter needs is the modest claim that survives every qualification of media determinism..." (§2.2)
Fix: state the modest claim; drop the meta-narration. "Media theory's strong determinism is not needed
here. The chapter needs only this: a form of sovereignty and its communicative sensibility are made
together, and neither can be exercised without the other."

**A note on what is NOT a problem:** the chapter is refreshingly light on agentless "has been shown"
passive — it keeps a named agent doing the verb almost everywhere ("Burrell distinguishes," "Ananny and
Crawford show," "the diagnostic decides"). That is the house voice working. The tells above are the
exceptions, not the texture.

## Part 3 — Paste-ready rewrites of the worst offenders

**(a) §6 closer — the single worst sentence, plus its redundancy.**
Current:
> The nearer neighbors mark the space without occupying it. [...four paragraphs...] That is the object
> the crowded neighborhood describes around and leaves unoccupied.
Cut the second sentence entirely (it restates the first and the "neighborhood describes around an
object" metaphor collapses). If a closer is wanted, end §6's comparison on content:
> Bannerman, Muldoon and Raekstad, Pasquale, and Cohen all read the mediation from the platform's end —
> the authority it holds over the coordinated. Coordinative sovereignty reads it from the other end, and
> arrives with a diagnostic that says when standing inside the mediation is the only autonomy on offer.

**(b) §6 anaphora — break the four-deep "From X it takes Y."**
Current openers: "From republican theory it takes... / From relational autonomy it takes... / From
Markell it takes... / And from Ostrom it takes..."
Rewrite (vary the agent and verb, keep the impersonal register):
> Republican theory supplies the first requirement. Freedom is non-domination, the absence of another's
> arbitrary power, a stronger condition than the absence of interference (Pettit, 1997; Skinner, 1998):
> an actor left alone is still unfree if it lives at the mercy of a power that could interfere at will.
> Muldoon and Raekstad (2022) show that this is the platform-dependent actor's exact condition. So the
> mediator's power must be constrained by rules the actor can know and contest.
>
> Relational autonomy corrects a hidden assumption. Autonomy is constituted through the relationships
> that structure a person (Mackenzie & Stoljar, 2000; Nedelsky, 2011), so the choice between being
> mediated and being autonomous is a false one: a well-shaped mediation enlarges autonomy, a badly-shaped
> one shrinks it, and the object of sovereignty is the shape.
>
> Markell (2008) adds what non-domination leaves out. Protection against arbitrary power still grants no
> share in the determinations that govern one. Coordinative sovereignty asks for the share — a place in
> the determination, not only insulation from it.
>
> Ostrom (1990) shows the positive form. Actors interdependent on a shared resource can govern it
> themselves, through institutions of their own design, without privatizing it and without a central
> authority. Her design principles are the institutional shape of effective voice under interdependence,
> carried here from the shared pasture to the shared platform.

**(c) §5.4 — replace the function-labeling closer with the function itself.**
Current:
> The portfolio view does organizational work. A coordinated actor's dependence is not monolithic...
Rewrite:
> Disaggregating the platform by function tells the coordinated actor where to spend effort. Dependence
> is not monolithic, and different functions admit different responses. Against the marketplace's
> contingent gate — the placement toll — the move is toward exit: interoperability, portability, an
> alternative channel. Against its necessary fulfillment, exit is a mirage and the move is toward voice.
> A manager who reads platform dependence as one risk to be diversified misses this; the diagnostic
> splits the dependence into the parts that can be exited and the parts that can only be governed
> (Author, 2026).

**(d) §5 — cut the two "payoff is coming" announcements.**
Delete "and the precision pays" from "...can be made precise, and the precision pays." and rewrite
"Formalizing his categories is what yields the payoff" as the payoff:
> Hirschman's categories, formalized, become computable: exit maps onto bypassability, voice onto
> necessity, and the necessary/contingent test says which obtains.

## Verdict

**Does it earn its length? Yes.** This is a real contribution with an original engine (the
necessary/contingent bypass test) that turns a loose intuition — some platforms you can leave, some you
can't — into a designable, sortable property, and it wires that engine to a genuine political-theory
construct and to concrete institutional advice. The two-construct thesis (digital vs coordinative
sovereignty varying independently) is demonstrated with the right kind of evidence in §8. A serious
reader — an editor at a policy-intellectual outlet, an academic in management, law, or STS — would
finish it, and would remember the hotel/OTA case and the "one morning her orders halve" vignette a week
later. Those two passages alone would carry the argument in a talk.

**What holds it back from excellent:** three fixable things. (1) §6 over-stuffs the theory roll-call and
should shed three or four names. (2) §7 is a uniform catalogue that dulls the middle — vary the paragraph
shape and merge the weakest entries. (3) The self-narrating "does work / pays / yields the payoff" tic
and the four-deep anaphora are the machine showing through an otherwise disciplined agentive voice; they
are a half-day's editing to remove. §4 also illustrates one point (inference-from-effects) twice at
length — compress the first.

**Single best sentence:**
> The one completed change in kind is the first, from speech to writing: a scribe is a different creature
> from a better speaker. (§2.3)
It does the chapter's hardest conceptual job — the difference between change of *degree* and change of
*kind* — in one concrete image, no jargon.

**Single worst sentence:**
> That is the object the crowded neighborhood describes around and leaves unoccupied. (§6)
The metaphor breaks (a neighborhood cannot "describe around" an object), it restates a sentence four
paragraphs earlier, and it ends a dense section on a strained flourish instead of a claim.

**The one thing only the author can supply:** a single *real, sourced* case worked all the way through
the diagnostic. The chapter's examples are illustrative composites — the marketplace seller, the app
store, and even the hotel/OTA case, which carries specific-sounding figures ("fifteen to thirty percent
commission," direct-booking "roughly a third and then plateau") with no citation. The argument is
plausible everywhere and *demonstrated* nowhere on the world. One named industry (hotels/OTAs is the
strongest candidate) with cited commission ranges, a documented rate-parity ban and its measured margin
effect, and a sourced direct-booking plateau would convert the necessary/contingent split from a
compelling model into an observed fact — and only the author has the field knowledge to know which case
is real enough to bear that weight and whether those numbers survive a fact-check.
