## Verdict

**Accept with nits.**

The chapter fits the volume. It opens on the compliant-but-captured firm, delivers a teachable diagnostic, sorts institutions for policymakers, and gives SMEs a decision procedure the digital-sovereignty literature has not been supplying. Template gates pass. Anonymization holds. Prior editor VERIFY items on the OTA case are closed. Prior agentless-passive tells are closed. "Computable" stays out of the conclusion. The title's discoverability cost remains, and the author has declined revision — treat that as settled packaging, not an open rewrite demand.

**Single most important fix:** source or soften the unsourced empirics in §8 (Apple €500 million / April 2025; P2B repeal proposal) and the "nine-tenths" concentration clause in §5.5. Those are press blockers; the rest is polish.

**Biggest genuine strength:** the disaggregation of organizational digital sovereignty into a literacy capability and an algorithmacy standing, delivered through a function-level bypass diagnostic that tells a manager whether the next euro of platform strategy buys exit or voice. §8's "impeccably compliant and strategically captured" (l. 753) is the sentence the volume introduction should quote.

**The one thing only the author can supply:** the primary sources (or deliberate softening) for the Apple fine, the P2B repeal proposal, and the three-agency concentration figure. The editor cannot invent those citations. Title revision remains available if the author reconsents; it is no longer this review's condition for acceptance.
