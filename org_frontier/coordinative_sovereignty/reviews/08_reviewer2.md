# Reviewer 2 — "Algorithmacy and Sovereignty: Toward a Coordinative Sovereignty for the Platform Era"

Recommendation up front: **major revision.** The chapter is intelligent, well-read, and mostly
well-written. It is also carrying two coined terms and a four-way diagnostic on a load-bearing
apparatus the reader is not allowed to see, and its single most quotable move — the bypass test —
is at real risk of being make-or-buy in a new coat. What follows presses on the weakest joints, in
order, and says for each what would change my mind.

---

## STEP 0 — Register

Impersonal academic chapter, APA 7, double-anonymized, for an edited volume on digital sovereignty.
I judge substance hard and prose against the house invariants only (named agents, active voice,
claim-first, verified citations, rhythm). The impersonal register is correct and not a defect;
"Author, 2026" self-cites and any block quotes are off-limits as prose complaints. The style bar
the drafts usually fail — the "It is not X, it is Y" antithesis machine — is, for once, mostly
absent here (see Part 2, which redirects the slop audit to what is actually wrong: near-verbatim
repetition of the core formula and a metronome of punchy one-line closers). Citations spot-check as
real and correctly attributed. I did not verify the two empirical assertions flagged VERIFY below;
those are the author's to source.

The register is not where this chapter is weak. The substance is.

---

## PART 1 — Section-by-section attack

### §1 Introduction

The opening is the best-argued page in the chapter. The GDPR/AI Act/DMA/DSA/NIS2 "comply and still
be helpless" gap is set up crisply, with the ISV, the merchant, and the creator as concrete anchors,
and Cutolo & Kenney and Rahman carry it. No complaint about the framing.

**Objection 1.1 — the whole argument is pre-loaded onto an unseen paper, and §1 already leans.**
Line 41: "a new one, which the companion literature to this chapter names algorithmacy (Author,
2026)." Line 43–46: "What sets the platform situation apart can be stated exactly. Model a
coordination as a small causal system and ask whether the mediating third party could be replaced by
a direct tie... That single distinction decides whether an actor's remaining power is the power to
leave or the power to be heard." The word "exactly" is doing heavy promissory work. Nothing in this
chapter is exact; the exactness lives in Author (2026). A reader evaluating *this* chapter cannot
check that the "small causal system" formalization exists, is well-defined, or delivers the clean
binary §1 advertises. **What would satisfy me:** either soften "can be stated exactly" to "can be
stated as a single question about the coordination's structure," or carry enough of the formal
definition into §5 that the chapter stands without the companion (see §5 below). As written, the
central promise of the introduction is an IOU.

**Objection 1.2 — "decides" overclaims for a distinction nothing in the chapter applies.** Line
45–46: "That single distinction decides whether an actor's remaining power is the power to leave or
the power to be heard." No coordination is ever run through the test in this chapter. Every
"application" (§5.4) is armchair. "Decides" is the language of a demonstrated result; the chapter
has a conjecture and a worked intuition. Flag it here because the same verb recurs (§5.3 "becomes a
designable fact," §8 "predict different outcomes") and compounds.

### §2 Sovereignty and its sensibilities

The oracy/literacy pairing is handled with genuine learning (Innis, Eisenstein, Habermas, Anderson,
Scott), and §2.2's retreat to "the modest claim that survives every qualification of media
determinism" (line 106–108) is exactly the right scholarly caution. I have no quarrel with §2.1–§2.2
as intellectual history.

**Objection 2.1 — §2.3 sets a trap the architecture then springs on itself.** This is the most
serious structural problem in the chapter and I want to state it fully.

§2.3 draws the schema's one firm line at line 129: "The one completed change in kind is the first,
from speech to writing: a scribe is a different creature from a better speaker." Digital sovereignty
is explicitly *not* a change in kind — it is "the literate sensibility carried into the digital
domain" (line 112), "continuous with the literate order" (line 121), "a compliance officer is still a
reader" (line 128). So the chapter commits, on the page, to: oracy →[kind]→ literacy →[not a kind,
mere extension]→ digital literacy.

Then §4 needs algorithmacy to be a change in kind, and asserts it: "Algorithmacy is to platform
coordination what literacy was to written coordination: a change in kind" (line 341). So the full
claim is: →[not a kind]→ digital →[kind]→ algorithmacy. The reader is owed a principled reason why
the digital step is *mere extension* while the algorithmic step is a *break*, and the chapter never
isolates one. It gestures at two different grounds and lets them blur:

- **(a) reflexivity / the triad** — line 281–284: "Algorithmacy is what algorithmic literacy becomes
  when your own conduct is one of the variables the system is optimizing and the system is reading
  your moves back to you." This is a genuine candidate for a difference in kind.
- **(b) opacity + adaptivity** — line 173–175: the mediator "is opaque... and it is interested and
  adaptive." These are differences of *degree*. A dense tax code is opaque; a case-law system adapts.
  The literate order already contains opaque, shifting, high-dimensional written objects, and §2.3
  itself concedes digital sovereignty "demands new registers of reading" — i.e., more of the same.

If the change in kind rests on (a), say so and drop (b) as the differentiator, because (b) cannot
carry a "kind" boundary without also promoting digital sovereignty to a kind (models are opaque; so
are the systems digital-sovereignty instruments already govern). If it rests on (b), the §2.3
architecture collapses, because opacity is a matter of degree all the way down. **What would satisfy
me:** name reflexive enrollment in a joint determination — the triad — as *the* boundary condition,
state it once in §2.3, and demote opacity/adaptivity to aggravating features rather than the
kind-making ones. Right now the spine's central claim is equivocal at its load-bearing joint.

**Objection 2.2 — nesting and rupture cannot both be true of algorithmacy.** §2.3 says each
sovereignty "layers on the old instead of replacing it" and coordinative sovereignty "will likewise
sit above digital and democratic sovereignty" (line 131–134) — a continuity claim. §4 says
algorithmacy is a change in kind, like writing — a rupture claim. But writing did *not* merely nest
on oracy; §2.3's own rhetoric ("a scribe is a different creature") is anti-nesting. The chapter wants
algorithmacy to be both the continuous top floor of a nested stack and a Goody/Ong-style rupture.
Pick one register for the relation, or explain why a rupture nests where a rupture normally does not.

### §3 The platform turn

§3 is strong and I want to credit it before the objections. The three-way separation at line 166–175
— coordination *logic* is old (price/authority/trust), *topology* is old (Simmel's triad, Burt,
Obstfeld), and only the *conjunction* of opacity and interested adaptivity is new — is the most
intellectually honest paragraph in the chapter. It pre-empts the "algorithmic management is just
hierarchy/digital Taylorism" objection (Kellogg, Rahman) instead of hiding from it. Good.

**Objection 3.1 — §3.2 is the chapter's best case and it undercuts the diagnostic.** §3.2 argues,
in four well-cited parts (Burrell, Ananny & Crawford, Cutolo & Kenney / Rahman, Hildebrandt), that
literacy cannot reach platform coordination because the object is opaque, disclosure without a
channel yields nothing, the dependent actors have no leverage, and the law is text-driven. This is
persuasive. But notice what it establishes: the platform problem is a problem of **opacity plus power
asymmetry plus institutional gap** — none of which is the *irreducibility* the §5 diagnostic is built
to detect. §3.2 makes the case for coordinative sovereignty *without ever needing the
necessary/contingent test*. The reader arrives at §5 already convinced, then meets a formal apparatus
that addresses a different question (does the coordination factor?) than the one §3.2 raised (is it
opaque and unaccountable?). **What would satisfy me:** a sentence at the top of §5 that connects
irreducibility back to the §3.2 harms — i.e., why does whether-the-triad-factors matter for whether
the actor is dominated? As it stands, §3.2 and §5 argue for the same conclusion by two unconnected
routes, and the formal route is the weaker of the two.

**Objection 3.2 — §3.3 "rent independent of any service" needs one guardrail.** Line 243–248: the
bottleneck "is a source of rent independent of any service the mediator provides." Fair as far as it
goes, but a hostile economist will note that the two-sided-markets literature the chapter cites
(Rochet & Tirole, Armstrong & Wright) also shows the mediator often internalizes cross-side
externalities that the parties could not internalize themselves — i.e., it *does* provide a service
that looks like rent. The chapter's own §5 "necessary mediator" category concedes exactly this. One
clause acknowledging that bottleneck rent and integrating value can be the same dollar would stop a
referee from calling §3.3 tendentious.

### §4 Algorithmacy

The three-part decomposition (inferential / translational / temporal) is clean, and the positioning
against algorithmic literacy (Dogruel), critical data literacy (Pangrazio & Selwyn, Sander), folk
theories (DeVito, Eslami), and algorithm sensemaking (Möhlmann) is careful and generous to prior
work. The seller vignette (line 296–316) earns its length. §4 is the chapter's strongest
constructive section.

**Objection 4.1 — the "change in kind" rhetoric outruns the defended claim.** §4 does the honest
thing at line 330–342: it concedes the strong Goody/Ong "writing restructures the mind" thesis "did
not survive the ethnographers" (Scribner & Cole, Street) and retreats to a claim about *coordination
regimes* — "In that infrastructural sense the transitions are changes in kind." Good. But having
banked the modest version, the section keeps spending the vivid one: "a scribe is a different
creature" (§2.3), "the position of a literate merchant beside an illiterate one" (line 305–306),
"stands where a fluent speaker once stood before a written contract in a script she could not read"
(line 339–340). The imagery asserts a cognitive/personal transformation the chapter has just
disclaimed. **What would satisfy me:** keep the infrastructural claim and cut or re-key the two or
three cognitive-rupture images so the rhetoric and the defended thesis are the same size.

**Objection 4.2 — is translational algorithmacy separable from ordinary strategic literacy?** The
inferential part (model the unseen system) is well-differentiated from reading. The temporal part
(hold a strategy through undisclosed change) is real. But the translational part — "render one's own
conduct into the terms the system reads" (line 269–271) — is what every advertiser, SEO consultant,
and résumé-writer has always done against any legible-but-gameable ranking, including pre-digital
ones. The seller "composes her titles, images, prices" (line 299–301) is recognizably keyword
optimization. If translational competence is not new, then two of algorithmacy's three legs
(translational, and arguably temporal) predate platforms, and the genuinely novel core is inferential
modeling under reflexivity. That is a *narrower* and more defensible construct than the three-part
one. **What would satisfy me:** either show translational algorithmacy differs from SEO/impression
management in kind (the reflexivity argument again — the system reads your moves *back to you as
inputs*), or concede that the load is carried by the inferential leg and rebalance the definition.

### §5 The diagnostic — the chapter's central risk

This is where I press hardest, because §5 is what the abstract sells and what the title's "coordinative
sovereignty" is supposed to be paired with.

**Objection 5.1 (the single most damaging point) — the bypass test, as presented in this chapter, is
substitutability / make-or-buy, and the IIT machinery that would make it more is not in the chapter.**
Line 373–374: "Restore the direct tie between them... and recompute whether the mediator still binds."
Line 371–372 cites the source honestly: "Brokerage research reached the difference informally: a
broker's returns depend on whether the brokered parties can credibly threaten to close the gap
themselves (Hahl et al., 2016)." So the chapter's own genealogy for the test is brokerage/structural
holes. "Can the two parties transact directly and does the middleman survive it?" is the
make-versus-buy question, the contestable-bottleneck question, the multihoming question (the chapter
cites Eisenmann on exactly this at line 412–413). Every worked case is decided by ordinary reasoning
about whether the tie does work: the franchised dealer "integrates nothing" (line 382), the
clearinghouse "does something two parties dealing directly could not do" (line 378–379), the OTA
billboard "a single hotel's website does not reproduce" (line 454). No integrated-information
quantity is ever computed or needed to reach any of these verdicts.

The chapter's answer is at line 363–365: the companion paper "develops the integrated-information
measures that make the check exact and that grade the borderline cases... For the argument here, the
runnable counterfactual of the next section is enough." That sentence concedes the objection. If the
counterfactual alone is enough for this chapter, then this chapter's diagnostic *is* the
counterfactual — i.e., substitutability — and Φ is invoked but idle within these pages. The four-way
typology (necessary/contingent/partial/reducible, line 391–392) maps one-to-one onto standard
gradations of substitutability (non-substitutable / substitutable-by-rule-change / partially
substitutable / already-substitutable). A skeptical reader will say: the chapter has relabeled
transaction-cost/brokerage substitutability with IIT vocabulary and outsourced the part that would
justify the vocabulary to a paper I cannot read.

I want to be fair about what this does and does not prove. It does **not** show Φ is worthless; the
companion paper may well demonstrate that IIT grades the borderline many-party cases that plain
reasoning botches, and that would be a real contribution. It **does** show that *this chapter* has not
earned the formal apparatus it leans on, because it never exhibits a case the plain counterfactual
gets wrong and the measure gets right. **What would satisfy me:** one worked borderline case, in this
chapter, where the naive "can they deal directly?" answer diverges from the graded integrated-
information verdict — a coordination that looks bypassable but is not, or vice versa, decided by the
measure. One such case would convert Φ from decoration to necessity in the reader's hands. Without
it, the honest framing is that §5's diagnostic is a substitutability test the companion paper
*refines*, and the chapter should say so in those words rather than borrowing the word "exact."

**Objection 5.2 — §5.3 dresses a definitional identity as a discovery.** Line 401–402: "Exit maps
onto bypassability, voice onto necessity, and the necessary/contingent test tells which obtains." But
"necessary" is *defined* as surviving the bypass (line 376–379) and "exit" is *defined* as
bypassability. So "the necessary mediator cannot be exited" and "whether a coordinated actor can leave
becomes a designable fact" (line 402–403) are true by construction. The Hirschman mapping is elegant,
but it is a renaming, not a result. Its only empirical bite is whether the underlying test is
non-trivial to apply — which returns to Objection 5.1. **What would satisfy me:** state that
exit/voice and contingent/necessary are two vocabularies for one distinction, and locate the actual
content in the (formal, non-trivial) classification step, not in the mapping.

**Objection 5.3 — the four-way typology collapses to a binary everywhere it is used.** §5.2 builds
four kinds. §5.3, §5.4, §7, and §8 then use exactly two: exit (contingent/reducible) versus voice
(necessary/partial). "Partial" and "reducible" never drive a different action than
"contingent" and "necessary" do. §7's institutions sort into exactly two bins (exit-restoring vs
voice-building, line 594–596). If four categories act as two, the four-way sort is presentational,
and the novelty claim for it is inflated. **What would satisfy me:** show one decision that turns on
"partial" versus "necessary," or on "reducible" versus "contingent." If none exists, present the
diagnostic as binary with two refinements, not as a four-cell typology.

**Objection 5.4 — the worked cases are asserted, not tested, and one carries an unsourced empirical
claim.** §5.4's app store, ride-hail, and OTA are presented as the diagnostic *classifying* mediators,
but they are intuitive judgments; no model is built, no counterfactual is computed. The OTA case adds
an empirical assertion offered as if it validated the test: line 458–459, "Direct-booking shares climb
to roughly a third and then plateau." **VERIFY** — this is a specific quantitative claim about hotel
distribution and needs a citation; as written it reads as stylized fact doing evidentiary work. The
"billboard effect" (line 454) is trade jargon and also wants a source. **What would satisfy me:** mark
these explicitly as *illustrations of how the test would apply*, not as results, and source the
one-third figure or drop the number.

### §6 The definition — the concept-stretching charge, aimed back

The chapter is admirably self-aware here: it raises Repetto (2025) on concept stretching (line
508–509) and answers. But the answer defends against the wrong neighbor.

**Objection 6.1 (Repetto's charge, boomeranged) — the construct is distinguished from the far
neighbor and left undistinguished from the four near ones.** §6 distinguishes coordinative sovereignty
from *digital* sovereignty: "Digital sovereignty... is a move to centralize control... Coordinative
sovereignty is actor-centric and mediation-native" (line 510–514). Granted. But the stretching worry
does not come from digital sovereignty; it comes from the four theories the section says the construct
"takes" from at lines 480–505: non-domination (Pettit/Skinner), relational autonomy
(Mackenzie/Stoljar, Nedelsky), Markell's involvement, and Ostrom's commons governance. Each near
neighbor already has the properties §6 uses to distinguish the construct from the far one:

- non-domination is already actor-centric and already about arbitrary power within a relation;
- relational autonomy is already mediation-native ("autonomy is constituted through the relationships
  that structure a person," line 489–490);
- Ostrom is already about governing-in-common a resource one can neither privatize nor exit.

So what is the *residue* that is new after you have non-domination + relational autonomy + involvement
+ commons governance, applied to a mediator you cannot leave? On the evidence of §6, the residue is:
(i) the pairing with the §5 diagnostic, and (ii) the specific object (the opaque interested mediator).
(i) reduces to §5, which Objection 5.1 has already put under pressure; (ii) is a domain of
application, not a new construct. A hostile reviewer will say coordinative sovereignty is
"non-domination-plus-voice-plus-Ostrom, applied to platforms, with a substitutability test bolted on"
— precisely the portmanteau move Repetto warns against, now performed by the chapter that cites
Repetto. **What would satisfy me:** state, in one paragraph, the proposition coordinative sovereignty
asserts that *none* of the four source theories asserts on its own — the genuinely additive claim —
and show it is not entailed by their conjunction. If the additive content is "these four converge
under irreducible mediation and the diagnostic says when that condition holds," then the construct's
novelty is entirely hostage to §5, and that dependency should be admitted.

**Objection 6.2 — mild circularity in the core definition.** Line 471–473: "Coordinative sovereignty
is the autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated: the power to shape the terms on which one is
mediated..." Its currency is "effective voice — terms that hold because the coordinated actors have
the standing to make them hold" (line 473–474). Standing is defined via voice; voice is glossed as
standing. And "irreducibly coordinated" is anchored only in §5's irreducibility, which is anchored
only in the companion paper. The definitional loop (irreducible → requires algorithmacy → enables
coordinative sovereignty → autonomy proper to the irreducible) closes inside the chapter only by
pointing outside it. **What would satisfy me:** anchor one node — either give "effective voice" an
operational gloss independent of "standing," or carry enough of the irreducibility definition into §5
that the loop closes on-page.

**Objection 6.3 — the "falsification" question the construct must answer.** Nowhere does the chapter
say what observation would show a coordination has coordinative sovereignty *absent* or *present* in a
way that could come out either way. §8 concedes this ("can be argued but not tested," line 649–650)
and §9 proposes instruments, which is the right place for it. But §6 asserts "the construct" as though
it were established (definite article throughout: "the construct is built to name," line 531). A
construct with no falsifier and no instrument is a proposal. The register mismatch between §6's
confident "the construct" and §9's honest "no validated instrument" is the quarantine problem I take
up next.

### §7 Realizing coordinative sovereignty

Sensible, well-sourced survey (Scholz, Delacroix & Lawrence, Alfrink, Klonick, Lei). The composition
point at the end (line 612–621, institutions stack and the diagnostic keeps the stack aimed) is a nice
close.

**Objection 7.1 — the section is a catalog, and the diagnostic does less work in it than claimed.**
Line 594: "The diagnostic thus does real institutional labor." What the diagnostic actually does in §7
is sort instruments into exit-restoring versus voice-building — a binary any reader had by §5.3. The
four-way typology contributes nothing here (Objection 5.3 again). Each institution gets one
even-handed paragraph with its promise and its limit, which is competent but undifferentiated; the
section reads as a literature tour with a two-bin sorter laid over it. **What would satisfy me:** show
the diagnostic making a *non-obvious* institutional recommendation — a case where an actor's intuition
says "exit" (build a competitor) but the necessary/partial classification says "the fight is voice,"
with the misallocation cost named. The OTA case (§5.4) nearly does this; pull that logic into §7 so
the diagnostic earns "real institutional labor."

### §8 Implications

The independent-variation thesis (a firm can be high on digital sovereignty and low on coordinative
sovereignty) is the chapter's cleanest empirical hypothesis and the platform-work-directive example
(line 639–644, cited to 2024/2831) is well chosen.

**Objection 8.1 — "predict different outcomes" asserts what §8 later admits is untested.** Line 632:
"the two predict different outcomes." Line 649–650: the central claim "can be argued but not tested."
These are in the same section. The first is the language of an established relationship between two
measured constructs; neither construct is measured (§8's own concession; §9's whole premise). "Predict"
should be "would predict, if the constructs were measured." This is the clearest instance of the
chapter asserting-as-established what it elsewhere concedes is conjectured. **What would satisfy me:**
change "predict" to a conditional throughout §8, matching §9's honesty.

**Objection 8.2 — "the market-conduct remedies of 2025."** Line 636–637: "The market-conduct remedies
of 2025 reached platforms' anti-steering rules and mandated ranking transparency." **VERIFY** — which
remedies, which jurisdiction, what instrument? This reads as a specific regulatory event and is
uncited while the adjacent directive is cited precisely. Either source it or generalize it.

### §9 Future research — does the quarantine hold?

Mostly yes, and this is the chapter's most honest section. Line 646–653 states plainly that
algorithmacy "has no validated instrument," coordinative sovereignty "is introduced here for the first
time," and "Until both can be measured, the typology's central empirical claim... can be argued but not
tested." The four directions are well-ordered (instruments first), the discriminant-validity targets
are named (psychological empowerment, Spreitzer 1995; the digital-sovereignty measures), and line
727–729 correctly identifies the digital/coordinative discrimination as "where the two-construct thesis
stands or falls." That is exactly right and exactly where a conceptual chapter should leave it.

**Objection 9.1 — the quarantine in §9 does not retroactively cure the overclaims in §1, §5, and §8.**
§9 is candid; the problem is that a reader reaches it only after §1 said the distinction can be stated
"exactly" and "decides" the outcome, §5 said the check is made "exact," and §8 said the two constructs
"predict" different outcomes. The honesty is quarantined to §9 while the confident claims are
distributed across the sections a skimming reader takes as the results. **What would satisfy me:** a
single forward-pointer early (end of §1 or top of §5) that says, in the chapter's own voice, that the
apparatus is conceptual and unmeasured and that §8's independent-variation claim is a hypothesis this
chapter proposes rather than tests. One sentence up front would let the confident language in between
read as framing rather than as findings.

**Objection 9.2 — the fourth direction quietly re-imports the unseen paper as a validity criterion.**
Line 739–744: coordinative sovereignty "can be operationalized twice: as a survey... and as a
structural property of a formal model... developed in the companion work (Author, 2026). Whether the
two agree... is a construct-validity test." This proposes to validate the new self-report construct
against the companion paper's formal model. If the formal model is itself unvalidated (it is a theory
paper), then agreement between a new survey and an unvalidated model is not construct validity — it is
two unvalidated instruments agreeing. **What would satisfy me:** name an *external* criterion (an
outcome: margin recovered, deactivation contested successfully, terms actually changed) against which
both the survey and the model are checked, rather than checking them against each other.

### §10 Conclusion

Well-controlled; it restates the spine without introducing new claims, and the "constitutive
priesthood" image (line 776) lands. The distribution-of-competence politics (algorithmacy concentrates
like capital, §4 line 318–328 and §10) is one of the chapter's best and most defensible contributions
and I would foreground it more, because it does not depend on the contested §5 apparatus at all.

**Objection 10.1 — the conclusion inherits §1's "computable."** Line 763: "the line between mediators
one can leave and mediators one cannot is computable." Computable in principle, by machinery in another
paper, never computed here. Consistent with §1 and therefore consistently overclaimed. Same fix as
1.1/5.1.

## PART 2 — Register-aware slop audit (real slop only)

The house antithesis machine is, unusually, **not** the problem here. Combined count of the four
priority tics (`, not` + ` rather than ` + `is not a/the/an` + `It is not`) is **3 across ~10,300
words** — an order of magnitude under the 12x drafts the style guide was written against. Credit where
due; the drafter cleaned this. The slop that remains is different and I redirect the audit to it.

**1. Verbatim / near-verbatim repetition of the core definitional formula (the real offender).** The
phrase "interprets/reads both sides and commits determinations/outcomes neither controls" appears at
least four times, near-identically: abstract (line 8–10), §4 (line 265–267), the seller frame implicitly,
and Key Terms (line 1050). "commits outcomes/determinations neither (side) controls" recurs six times
across §1, §3.1, §4, §6, §10. This is the house-forbidden "restate a conclusion in near-identical words
across sections." The formula is the chapter's signature and should be stated once in full (§3.1 or §4),
then referred to — not re-executed each time the mediator is named.

**2. Metronome of punchy one-line closers.** The chapter ends sections and paragraphs on a short blunt
sentence often enough to become a tic: "That is the shape the rest of the chapter works with." (§3.1,
line 194); "One firm, four kinds of mediator at once." (§5.4, line 433); "Knowing which is which is the
first return on algorithmacy." (§8, line 665); "and none of it will show up on a compliance audit."
(§8, line 672); "Neither reading alone nor control alone secures it." (§10, line 784); "She has read
nothing. There was nothing to read." (§4, line 311). Individually fine; as the recurring shape of the
close they are a rhythm the style guide names ("the mechanized punchy opener," here as closer). Vary
two or three — let some sections end on the long sentence that carries the idea.

**3. Tautological tails.** Line 303–305: "None of it is reading in the literate sense, and none of it
is contained in the platform's disclosures, because the disclosures do not contain what she needs to
know." The subordinate clause restates the main clause. Line 258–259: "A transparent bottleneck is
still a bottleneck, and a disclosed objective the coordinated actor has no standing to contest is still
an objective imposed on it." — two parallel "X is still X" figures in one sentence; one earns its
place. These are decorative, not load-bearing.

**4. "cannot / does not" negation density.** 67 instances of `cannot/does not/do not` etc. Most are
content-bearing (cannot see, cannot leave, cannot contest — these are the argument). But they cluster
in §5.3 and §6 into strings of negation ("cannot leave," "cannot be exited," "does no work," "takes
nothing," "form no part of it") that read as a second antithesis engine even though they escape the
literal tic list. Worth a pass to convert two or three to positive statements.

Agentless passive: rare and mostly historical (§2.1 "disputes were settled," "authority was carried"),
which is defensible for the oral-order description. Not a finding. The prose is otherwise strongly
active with named agents (the chapter, the mediator, the platform, the seller). The register invariants
are met; do not spend revision effort here.

## PART 3 — Line rewrites I would accept

- §1 line 43–44. From: "What sets the platform situation apart can be stated exactly." To: "What sets
  the platform situation apart is one structural question about the coordination." (Drops the
  unearned "exactly"; keeps the claim.)

- §5.2 line 373–374, add the honest genealogy inline rather than only in the Hahl cite. From: "Take a
  mediator that binds two parties. Restore the direct tie between them... and recompute whether the
  mediator still binds." To: "Take a mediator that binds two parties and ask the make-or-buy question
  in structural form: restore the direct tie the mediation forbids, and see whether the mediator still
  binds. Where it does, the position rests on integrating work no direct tie reproduces; where it does
  not, the position rested on the tie's absence." (Names what the test is, so the companion paper's

## Verdict

**Major revision.** The chapter is a real contribution in outline — the digital/coordinative
distinction, the algorithmacy-as-distributed-competence politics, and the §3.2 case that literacy
cannot reach platform coordination are all worth publishing — but three joints are currently
over-torqued: the change-in-kind spine equivocates at §2.3/§4, the §5 diagnostic borrows formal
authority it does not exhibit, and the confident language of §1/§5/§8 outruns the honest §9.

**Single most damaging point.** The bypass test, *as it appears in this chapter*, is
make-or-buy / brokerage substitutability — the chapter's own Hahl (2016) genealogy and its own
"the runnable counterfactual is enough" concession say so — and the integrated-information machinery
that would make it more than that is entirely in a companion paper the reviewer cannot see. Until the
chapter exhibits one borderline case where the naive counterfactual and the graded measure *disagree*,
"coordinative sovereignty paired with a formal diagnostic" reduces to "relational autonomy plus voice,
applied to platforms, with substitutability relabeled" — which is the concept-stretching Repetto warns
against, performed by the chapter that cites Repetto.

**Biggest genuine strength.** The nested-sensibilities frame turned into a *political* claim about the
uneven distribution of algorithmacy (§4 line 318–328, §10): whoever can model the systems that
coordinate economic life holds a sovereignty that "will not show up on a compliance audit," and the
competence concentrates like capital. That argument is original, defensible, and independent of the
contested §5 apparatus. It could carry the chapter on its own.

**The one thing only the author can supply.** A single worked coordination — in these pages, not in the
companion paper — where the plain "can they deal directly?" answer and the integrated-information
verdict come apart, and the measure gets it right. That one example is what converts Φ from borrowed
vocabulary into a necessary tool, answers the most damaging point, and retroactively earns the word
"exact." No reviewer can produce it; only the author, from the companion work, can.
