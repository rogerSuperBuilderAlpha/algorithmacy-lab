# Operationalizing algorithmacy and coordinative sovereignty

The chapter (`../chapter/chapter.md`, §8) argues that organizational digital sovereignty conceals two
constructs that vary independently, and that the second — coordinative sovereignty — and the competence that
enables it — algorithmacy — need validated instruments. This folder specifies those instruments. It is the seed
of the empirical follow-on, and it answers the first item on the project `AGENDA.md`.

Two operationalizations run in parallel and are meant to be checked against each other.

- **Survey instruments** — `algorithmacy_instrument.md` and `coordinative_sovereignty_instrument.md` define each
  construct's dimensions, propose candidate items anchored on the closest validated scales, name the constructs
  each must be discriminated from, and set out a scale-development and validation plan.
- **Formal standing** — `formal_standing.md` operationalizes coordinative sovereignty structurally, as a
  coordinated actor's pivotality (Shapley share) and core membership in the integrated-information game the lab
  already computes. The survey measures reported standing; the game measures afforded standing; the program asks
  whether they agree.

## The nomological net

The instruments are specified against a hypothesized web of relationships, so that construct validity can be
tested by whether the measures behave as the theory predicts.

- **Algorithmacy → coordinative sovereignty.** The sensibility is the precondition of the standing. An actor
  cannot contest, press for terms, or hold a share in governance without modeling the system, translating its
  own conduct, and holding a course. Predicted: algorithmacy is a positive antecedent of an actor's coordinative
  sovereignty, partially mediating the effect of experience and resources.
- **Digital sovereignty ⟂ coordinative sovereignty.** The chapter's H15. Predicted: measured organizational
  digital sovereignty (regulatory and infrastructural capability) is weakly or not correlated with coordinative
  sovereignty, and the two predict different outcomes. This is the discriminant-validity claim the whole
  argument rests on, so it is the primary empirical target.
- **Coordinative sovereignty → outcomes.** Predicted: coordinative sovereignty predicts bargaining outcomes with
  platforms, resilience of market position under an algorithm change, and — the chapter's sharper claim —
  survival when a mediator is disintermediated, but only for actors facing *contingent* mediators.
- **Mediator type moderates.** From the diagnostic: the value of exit-seeking versus voice-building depends on
  whether the mediator is necessary or contingent. Predicted: response-fit (matching strategy to mediator type)
  predicts outcomes above coordinative sovereignty alone.

## The two-step design the diagnostic affords

The necessary/contingent test lets an empirical study do something a monolithic digital-sovereignty measure
cannot: classify the mediation before it measures the response.

1. **Classify the mediation.** For each platform dependency an organization faces, assign a mediator type —
   necessary, contingent, partial — from the diagnostic, using the modeled coordination or expert coding where a
   model is not yet built.
2. **Measure the response and its fit.** Measure the organization's coordinative sovereignty and the strategy it
   pursues, and score whether the strategy fits the mediator type: exit-seeking against contingent gates,
   voice-building against necessary ones.

A firm's coordinative sovereignty is then not only how much standing it holds but whether it holds the right
kind given the mediators it faces, and misallocation — treating a necessary mediator as contingent, or the
reverse — becomes a measurable predictor of poor outcomes.

## Validation strategy (shared)

Both survey instruments follow the same development sequence, standard for construct measurement in management
and information systems:

1. **Construct definition and content domain** — fixed by the chapter and restated at the head of each
   instrument.
2. **Item generation** — candidate items anchored on the styles of the closest validated scales (named per
   instrument), generated to cover each dimension.
3. **Content validity** — an expert panel rates item–dimension fit; items below threshold are cut or revised.
4. **Dimensionality** — exploratory factor analysis on a first sample, then confirmatory factor analysis on an
   independent sample, to confirm the hypothesized dimensions.
5. **Reliability** — internal consistency per dimension and composite reliability.
6. **Convergent and discriminant validity** — correlations with the adjacent constructs named per instrument,
   predicting moderate convergence and clear discrimination; the algorithmacy-vs-algorithmic-literacy and the
   digital-vs-coordinative-sovereignty discriminations are the load-bearing ones.
7. **Nomological validity** — the net above, tested as a structural model.
8. **Common-method-bias controls** — procedural (separated measurement, marker variable) and statistical, since
   the antecedent, the construct, and outcomes may be self-reported.

## Status and scope

These are specifications, not fielded instruments. No data are collected here, and the candidate items are
proposals for the content-validity stage, not a validated scale. The formal standing note adds no new Φ
computation; it reuses the cooperative-game threads. The deliverable is a development plan detailed enough that
a survey study and a formal correspondence test can each begin from it.
