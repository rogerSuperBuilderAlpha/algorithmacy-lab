# Coordinative sovereignty

A framing statement for the project. The claims here are the opening hypothesis, grounded in the literature
(see `literature/deep_research_report.md`) and in the lab's formal work on mediated coordination. They set
out a program to build, ahead of its results.

## The condition

Most coordination now runs through platforms. A worker reaches customers through an app, a seller reaches
buyers through a marketplace, a developer reaches users through a store, a citizen reaches the public sphere
through a feed. These platforms set the terms, and the terms are not negotiated. The platform stands between
the parties, and standing there is where its power sits: it can rank, gate, price, suspend, and rewrite the
rules, and the parties coordinate on whatever terms it leaves them.

The natural response is to want the old autonomy back — to be independent, to deal directly, to not be
mediated. This response is a mistake, and it leaves the one who makes it less sovereign, not more.

## Why preserving the old autonomy fails

The autonomy people want back is the autonomy of independence: sovereignty as the power to act without external
control, freedom as the absence of interference. It is Bodin's indivisible authority and Mill's private sphere,
and it rests on a single assumption — that one can stand apart. In a platform-mediated world that assumption
usually fails. The mediator often does integrating work the parties cannot reproduce by dealing directly, so
removing it does not return the parties to an unmediated state; it dissolves the coordination itself. To demand
independence from a mediator you cannot do without is to demand the end of the thing you were trying to be
autonomous within. The demand looks like a claim to freedom and functions as a withdrawal from the field where
freedom would be exercised.

The lab's own results make this precise. The bypass-counterfactual (q213) separates a mediator held in the
core by a **constraint that could be lifted** — a contingent gate — from one that is there by its own
integrating work — a necessary mediator. For a contingent gate, the old sovereignty still works: open the
bypass, and the parties deal directly. Deregulation, interoperability, and breaking an exclusive are exactly
this move, and they are the right move where the gate is contingent. For a necessary mediator, the bypass takes
nothing, and the demand to remove it is futile. The instrument is a diagnostic for which sovereignty is even
available. Where a mediator is necessary, independence is not on the menu, and continuing to demand it spends
one's effort on the one option that cannot be had.

Hirschman named the two options: exit and voice. The sovereignty of independence is a sovereignty of exit — its
power is the power to leave. Where exit is foreclosed, that sovereignty is empty, and only voice remains. A
politics that keeps demanding exit from arrangements no one can exit trains people to mistake their remaining
power, which is voice, for a consolation prize.

## Digital sovereignty is the same mistake, scaled up

Digital sovereignty answers the platform condition by reclaiming control. The European version seeks command of
infrastructure and data; the cyber-sovereignty version partitions the network into national territory; the data
and technological versions assert a jurisdiction's or a community's authority over a digital domain. Each is a
move to centralize control and restore a boundary, which is the preservationist reflex transposed from the
state to the stack. Digital sovereignty asks who owns the infrastructure. It is a real question, and it is a
different question. It gives the state or the community a form of exit — a wall, a fork, a domestic cloud — and
it does little for the actor who must still coordinate through whatever infrastructure wins. The seller inside
the sovereign marketplace, the driver inside the sovereign platform, is exactly as mediated as before.

## What coordinative sovereignty is

Coordinative sovereignty is the autonomy proper to being irreducibly coordinated. It is standing within the
coordination, neither independence from the mediator nor command of the infrastructure: the power to shape
the terms on which one is mediated, to contest the mediator's arbitrary power, and to hold a share in the
governance of the arrangement one cannot leave. Its currency is voice, made effective — not the chance to
speak, but terms that hold because the coordinated actors have the standing to make them hold.

The construct draws on the relational line the literature already opened. Pettit's non-domination supplies the
first condition: a coordinated actor is sovereign to the degree the mediator's power over it is not arbitrary,
constrained by rules the actor can contest. Nedelsky's relational autonomy supplies the frame: autonomy is
constituted through the relationships that structure a person, so a well-shaped mediation can enlarge autonomy
and a badly-shaped one can shrink it, and the question is the shape. Markell supplies the correction that keeps
this from collapsing into mere protection: what a coordinated actor needs is involvement, a place in the
determination that goes beyond a guarantee against interference. Ostrom supplies the institutional precedent — actors
interdependent on a shared resource can govern it themselves, without either privatizing it or surrendering it
to a central authority. Coordinative sovereignty is the extension of that precedent from a shared resource to a
shared mediation.

It differs from relational autonomy, which is an ethical and largely individual notion, by being political and
structural: it concerns the standing of parties inside coordination systems, and the design of those systems.
It differs from digital sovereignty by being actor-centric and mediation-native: it seeks voice through the
platform, where digital sovereignty seeks command of it.

## The connection to the lab

The lab has spent its program on the mediated triad — coordination through a third party, and the competence,
algorithmacy, that a mediated world demands of the people inside it. Coordinative sovereignty is the political
form of that competence. Algorithmacy is knowing that you are coordinated through a system and being able to
read which party the system binds; coordinative sovereignty is the standing to act on that knowledge — to see
that a mediator is necessary rather than merely mandated, and therefore to spend one's political effort on voice
within it rather than on an exit that is not available. The necessary/contingent instrument is the bridge: it
tells a coordinated actor whether its situation calls for the sovereignty of exit or the sovereignty of voice,
and the contingency catalog and the disintermediation forecast already sort real arrangements into the two.

## The agenda

The construct is a hypothesis, and the project is to build it out. The open questions are in `AGENDA.md`. The
first is to make coordinative sovereignty operational: if the necessary/contingent test says when only voice is
available, what makes voice effective inside an irreducible coordination, and can the lab's apparatus measure
it. The distinction from digital sovereignty, the relation to non-domination and relational autonomy, and the
worked cases — the gig worker, the app developer, the seller, the citizen in a feed — are the material the
program will work through.
