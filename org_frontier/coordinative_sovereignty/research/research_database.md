# Research database — "Algorithmacy and Sovereignty" chapter

Every proposition and concept the chapter proposes, decomposed for deep-research grounding and adversarial
testing. The chapter's argument is only as strong as its weakest load-bearing claim, so each is stated as a
falsifiable proto-hypothesis with the evidence that supports it, the evidence that would threaten it, and a
verdict updated as research lands.

Status legend: **grounded** (well-supported, canonical sources) · **partial** (supported but needs deepening
or has open edges) · **contested** (the literature disagrees; the chapter must engage the disagreement) ·
**novel** (the chapter's own contribution; test for originality and defensibility) · **open** (not yet
researched).

Verdict is filled from the deep-research passes in `findings/` and summarized here.

## Priority for deep research

The novel and contested claims carry the chapter and are researched first, adversarially — the point is to find
what would refute each, not to confirm it.

1. **H5** — co-optation as a fourth coordination mechanism. Verify Stark & Vanden Broeck (2024); locate the
   markets/hierarchies/networks canon (Powell 1990) and Selznick's (1949) original co-optation; find critics who
   would deny that co-optation is distinct rather than a mode of hierarchy or network.
2. **H12 + H13** — the exit/voice ↔ necessary/contingent bridge, and coordinative sovereignty's novelty. Is the
   mapping original and defensible? Is the term or a near-equivalent already occupied (Bannerman relational
   sovereignty; Pasquale functional; Cohen platform sovereign; "networked sovereignty"; "digital
   self-determination")? What would a reviewer say is not new.
3. **H10 + H2** — algorithmacy as a change in kind from digital literacy, and the change-in-kind claim for the
   oracy→literacy→algorithmacy series (orality-literacy studies: Ong, Goody, Havelock; the "literacy thesis"
   and its critics, e.g., Street's ideological model).
4. **H1** — the pairing of sovereignty with a sensibility. Is there precedent (media theory: Innis, McLuhan,
   Eisenstein; Kittler) and does the chapter's version add something.
5. **H6, H11, H15, H17** — the triadic-coordination claim, the computable necessary/contingent distinction, the
   independent-variation claim, and the openness/coordinative-sovereignty orthogonality.
