## PART 1 — REVIEWER THREAT-HUNT

### 1.1 The verdict, stated first

**NARROWS.** Not "survives" as written, and not "dies."

The chapter makes two claims in §4. The weaker one — that algorithmacy has three parts (inferential,
translational, temporal) — the chapter already concedes is not novel, and the concession is correct and
complete. The stronger one — "Algorithmacy adds no new part. It adds their integration, and the setting that
forces the integration" (line 305) — is **half-wrong as stated**, because the integration *by itself* is
already published. What survives is the *setting*: the triadic, mutually-read coordination, plus the political
payoff (competence → standing → sovereignty). The sentence at 305 needs to be rewritten so the load falls on
the setting rather than on the integration, or a hostile referee kills it in one citation.

### 1.2 Nearest rival: DeVito (2021), adaptive folk theorization

This is the single most dangerous citation in the chapter's own reference list, and §4 currently cites it as an
ally supplying "a part."

DeVito's definition of algorithmic literacy, quoted verbatim in Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum's review (p. 684):

> "the capacity and opportunity to be aware of both the presence and impact of algorithmically-driven systems
> on self- or collaboratively-identified goals, and the capacity and opportunity to crystalize this
> understanding into a strategic use of these systems to accomplish said goals." (DeVito, 2021: 3)

Read that against §4's three parts:

| §4 part | DeVito 2021 coverage |
|---|---|
| inferential (model an unseen system) | "aware of both the presence and impact of algorithmically-driven systems" — and the whole folk-theorization apparatus |
| translational (render conduct into the system's terms) | "crystalize this understanding into a *strategic use* of these systems to accomplish said goals" |
| temporal (hold a strategy through undisclosed change) | the word **"adaptive"** in the title; the paper is explicitly about "changing platforms" and "theorization complexity level" revised as platforms evolve |
| **integration** | the definition is a single integrated construct spanning awareness → strategic use, held across platform change |

So: the parts are covered, the integration is covered, and "integration under conditions of undisclosed change"
is *literally the paper's thesis*. The claim "Algorithmacy adds ... their integration" is anticipated by a work
the chapter cites approvingly two paragraphs earlier. A referee from media/information literacy will find this
in thirty seconds.

**VERIFIED.** DeVito, M. A. (2021). Adaptive folk theorization as a path to algorithmic literacy on changing
platforms. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 5*(CSCW2), Article 339, 38 pp.
DOI 10.1145/3476080. Crossref record confirms title, sole author, PACM HCI, vol 5, issue CSCW2, 2021;
Crossref reports pagination as 1–38, and the ACM canonical form is "Article 339 ... 38 pages." The chapter's
reference entry is correct as written.
Source: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3476080 ; article-number corroborated at
https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3476080 (page metadata; ACM DL blocks direct fetch, confirmed via search index).

### 1.3 The rest of the rival field, and what each takes

Ordered by how much of §4's claim each one eats.

**(a) Sutherland, Jarrahi, Dunn & Nelson (2020), "gig literacies."** The closest thing in the literature to a
*triadic* competence construct, and the chapter does not cite it. Upwork freelancers; the study interviewed
**20 freelancers and 19 of their clients** — the counterpart is empirically present. Gig literacies are
defined as the adaptive competencies workers cultivate to "leverage platforms creatively and productively"
including "negotiating or working around its imposed structures and control mechanisms." This covers
inferential + translational + a plural, situated framing. What it does *not* do: theorize the client as also
being read and scored by the same system, and it stays at the level of worker navigation rather than standing.
**VERIFIED.** *Work, Employment and Society, 34*(3), 457–475. DOI 10.1177/0950017019886511.
https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0950017019886511

**VERIFIED.** In *Information in Contemporary Society* (iConference 2019), LNCS, pp. 578–589. Springer, Cham.

### 1.4 Is there already a construct that covers the INTEGRATION claim?

**Yes for integration. No for the triad.** That is the whole finding.

- Integration of awareness → strategic action → adaptation across change: **DeVito 2021**, definitionally.
- Integration in a *work* setting with an employer-like counterparty: **Sutherland et al. 2020** ("gig
  literacies"), **Jarrahi & Sutherland 2019** ("algorithmic competencies").
- Integration named as a research need: Oeldorf-Hirsch & Neubaum's agenda item (3) is "developing the
  affective and behavioral facets of literacy," and they write that the field must "decide whether to
  cultivate a cohesive definition of literacy as an overarching construct, or accept definitions as
  collections of other concepts that make up literacy" (p. 685). A referee can read that as: the field has
  already claimed the integration problem and is working on it; algorithmacy is a competitor entry, not a new
  category.

What nobody has done, on this sweep:

1. **Defined the competence by the structure of the coordination rather than by the user's relation to a
   feed.** Every construct above indexes to *a user and a system*. None indexes to *two parties bound by a
   system that reads both and commits a determination binding both*. Curchod et al. (2020) describe that
   structure — they even call it "triadic interactions" — but describe it as a power structure, not as a
   competence.
2. **Connected the competence to political standing.** Nothing in the algorithmic-literacy literature runs
   competence → contestation → sovereignty. The literature runs competence → visibility/earnings, or
   competence → wellbeing, or competence → critical awareness. The chapter's §6 hinge (line 587–597,
   "the sensibility is the precondition of the sovereignty") is unoccupied ground.
3. **Set the actor at the firm level.** Dogruel et al. measure *internet users*. DeVito studies activists,
   Cotter studies a video community, Klawitter & Hargittai study individual creative entrepreneurs. The
   chapter's protagonists are an ISV, an SME seller, a hotel. Tambe (2026, *Management Science*) is the only
   firm-level "algorithmic literacy" work found on this sweep, and it is about internal workforce reskilling,
   not about facing an external mediator.

### 1.5 What §4 should therefore concede and where the novelty should be re-planted

Recommended rewrite of the load-bearing sentence (lines 304–310). Currently:

> "These accounts already supply the parts: inference about an unseen system, action from within it, revision
> over time. Algorithmacy adds no new part. It adds their integration, and the setting that forces the
> integration."

The problem: "It adds their integration" is false, and the true clause ("and the setting") is subordinated to
the false one. Invert. Concede integration explicitly to DeVito, then claim the setting. Something structurally
like: the parts are supplied, and DeVito already binds them into one competence held across platform change;
what none of this work does is index the competence to a coordination in which the counterpart is *also* read.
Then the difference is checkable, not rhetorical.

Two further honest concessions worth making, because making them first is cheaper than being caught:

- The e-commerce/seller setting is not new to this literature (Klawitter & Hargittai 2018).
- "Algorithmic competencies" is already a coined term (Jarrahi & Sutherland 2019). Say why algorithmacy is not
  a synonym: theirs is a worker-side navigation competence, the chapter's is a competence defined by a
  structural property of the coordination and tied to a claim about standing.

### 1.6 The "change in kind" retreat (lines 356–368)

**Coherent, defensible, and currently under-argued in one specific way.**

The retreat itself is the right move and is standard: nobody defends the strong Goody–Ong cognitive thesis
after Scribner & Cole and Street, and Halverson's is the canonical statement of its collapse.
**VERIFIED.** Halverson, J. (1992). Goody and the implosion of the literacy thesis. *Man, 27*(2), 301–317.
DOI 10.2307/2804055. https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Halverson+Goody+implosion...

Two problems, one easy and one real.

**Easy problem: the retreat has no positive ancestor.** The chapter cites Goody 1977 and Ong 1982 *only* to
disown them, then asserts a coordination-regime claim with no source. The coordination-regime claim has a
perfectly good ancestor, including in Goody's own later work: the institutional argument that writing enables
administrative, legal, and accounting forms an oral order cannot instantiate (Goody, *The Logic of Writing and
the Organization of Society*, Cambridge University Press, 1986 — **UNVERIFIED**, not fetched this session; the
year/publisher should be confirmed before use). The chapter already cites Scott (1998) and Eisenstein (1979) in
§2.2 for exactly this. Pulling one of those forward into §4 costs a clause and turns an assertion into a
descent.

**Real problem: Street's objection is not only about cognition.** The ideological model denies that there is
one thing called "literacy" with uniform consequences; it insists on plural, situated literacy *practices*.
Applied to §4, that objection says: there is no one algorithmacy either — there are gig literacies, algorithmic
gossip, practical knowledge, folk theories, algorithm skills, and the field's own plural vocabulary proves it.
A New Literacy Studies referee will say the chapter reproduces the autonomous model one level up: it swapped
"cognition" for "coordination" and kept the singular. The answer is available and is not in the text: the
chapter's singular is the *regime* (a structural property of the coordination, checkable by the §5
diagnostic), not the competence; the competence indexed to that regime can be — and empirically is —
realized plurally. Write that in. Two or three sentences.

**Third, smaller:** §2.3 line 139 says "The one completed change in kind is the first, from speech to
writing," and §4 line 367 needs algorithmacy to *be* a change in kind. REVIEW.md already flagged this
(its line 126). The word "completed" is doing the reconciling work and it is doing it too quietly.

### 1.7 THE VIGNETTE PROBLEM — three citable triadic cases

The panel is right and the defect is real: lines 322–342 stage seller-vs-ranking with no buyer. Below are
three empirical cases where the triadic structure is documented, in descending order of fit. All three are
cases in which the *counterpart's* conduct is read by the same system that reads the focal actor, and the
determination binds both.

---

#### **CANDIDATE 1 (strongest): Curchod, Patriotta, Cohen & Neysen (2020) — eBay sellers, buyers, and the feedback algorithm**

**VERIFIED.** *Administrative Science Quarterly, 65*(3), 644–676. DOI 10.1177/0001839219867024.
Crossref: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0001839219867024
Open full text (accepted manuscript, Warwick WRAP):
https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/122860/1/WRAP-working-algorithm-power-asymmetries-agency-settings-Patriotta-2019.pdf
Method: 77 high-performing eBay business sellers, France and Belgium.

This paper is a near-perfect fit and it is *not currently cited anywhere in the chapter*. It uses the word
"triad" for the exact structure §3.1 defines. Verbatim from the accepted manuscript (page numbers not visible
in the extracted text; located in the Discussion):

> "in online settings monitoring results from ongoing interactions among a triad of actors: buyers post online
> reviews, which are interpreted by the platform's algorithm and provide visible feedback to both the seller
> and the wider market. **These triadic interactions** reproduce power asymmetries through two complementary
> mechanisms: the disaggregation/aggregation of individual agency and the formation of a coalition of
> interests between a mass of invisible buyers and a distant platform owner."

The mechanism by which the system reads *both* sides, verbatim (Research Context section):

> "eBay uses these ratings to manage sellers. Regular sellers with high volumes of sales are granted
> 'PowerSeller' status if they sustain positive ratings above 98 percent over the prior 12 months. ... eBay
> also uses ratings to rank sellers in search results. The order of sellers is determined by the algorithm,
> and detailed ratings are compiled in it. Additionally, eBay calculates a seller level every month (above or
> below standard) based on a series of requirements, such as a maximum percentage of transactions with defects
> or a maximum percentage of transactions dispatched late. Sellers whose detailed ratings are persistently
> below standard are suspended from selling for a certain period of time."

The seller managing the *counterpart* because of how the system will read the interaction — verbatim seller
quote (respondent O4, a jewelry seller):

> "There are people who blackmail me, who negotiate everything. And I receive negative feedback because I
> refuse, but what can I do? I can't just accept any request. No later than this morning, I received a
> message. I sold an item by auction, 19.90 euros, and the woman wants it for 9.90 euros. Or somebody who is
> not happy with his purchase, so I asked him to return the item to get a refund, but he doesn't want to pay
> the postage. They all threatened to post negative evaluations."

And the translational/relational move, verbatim (Findings, "Sellers' Individual Practices vis-à-vis Buyers"):

> "Our interviews revealed that sellers developed distinctive practices to prevent buyers from posting
> negative reviews. To tackle the problem of customer anonymity and invisibility, some sellers bypassed the
> standard modes of interaction and communicated directly with their buyers. In this way, they tried to create
> a more personal and positive contact than the automatic procedures allowed."

The collective-inference / temporal-revision material is there too: sellers used eBay's own discussion forums
"as a tool for collective" organizing and to "keep themselves informed of others' views," while describing the
forums as monitored ("eBay controls the forums, so it's like the Stasi ... V3") and reporting "censorship on
forums" and fear of retaliation. That last strand is *also* direct support for §4's "opacity is a
rent-preserving strategy" paragraph (see Part 4).

**Why this is the pick.** It keeps the chapter's marketplace-seller scene, which REVIEW.md says is the best
prose in the chapter, and supplies the buyer without inventing anything. The rewrite: the order-halving morning
stays, but the seller's diagnosis has to include what the *buyers* did — a run of late-delivery complaints
from one warehouse, feeding detailed seller ratings, feeding placement — so the ranking is visibly reading
both sides. And the seller's countermove is not only "move the listings to faster fulfillment" but also
something addressed to a buyer (pre-emptive contact, a refund offered before a rating is left) because she
knows the buyer's next click is an input the system will read.

#### **CANDIDATE 2: Jhaver, Karpfen & Antin (2018) — Airbnb hosts, guests, and the search/evaluation algorithm ("double negotiation")**

**VERIFIED.** *Proceedings of the 2018 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '18)*, Paper
421, 1–12. DOI 10.1145/3173574.3173995. Method: interviews with 15 Airbnb hosts.
Author-hosted record (abstract read verbatim): https://shagunjhaver.com/research/articles/jhaver-2018-airbnb/
(ACM DL blocks direct fetch; DOI corroborated at https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3173574.3173995 via index.)

Abstract, verbatim:

> "Algorithms increasingly mediate how work is evaluated in a wide variety of work settings. Drawing on our
> interviews with 15 Airbnb hosts, we explore the impact of algorithmic evaluation on users and their work
> practices in the context of Airbnb. Our analysis reveals that Airbnb hosts engage in a **double negotiation**
> on the platform: They must negotiate efforts not just to attract potential guests but also to appeal to only
> partially transparent evaluative algorithms. We found that a perceived lack of control and uncertainty over
> how algorithmic evaluation works can create anxiety among some Airbnb hosts. We present a framework for
> understanding this double negotiation, as well as a case study of coping strategies that hosts employ to
> deal with their anxiety."

"Double negotiation" is, in the chapter's vocabulary, the triad seen from one vertex: the host is coordinating
with a guest *and* being read by the system, simultaneously, and the two negotiations are not separable.
Airbnb's two-way review system means the guest is also rated, which makes the mutual-reading claim literal.

**Use.** Either as the vignette itself (a host, a guest, a search ranking) or — better — as the citation that
licenses the seller vignette's structure, quoted in the sentence that introduces it. "Double negotiation" is a
gift of a phrase for a chapter arguing that the two negotiations are one competence.

#### **CANDIDATE 3: Bucher, Schou & Waldkirch (2021) — Upwork/Fiverr freelancers, clients, and anticipatory compliance**

**VERIFIED.** *Organization, 28*(1), 44–67. DOI 10.1177/1350508420961531.
https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1350508420961531
Method: 12,294 comments from an online freelancer community.

Already cited in §4 for the translational part, but cited *dyadically* ("gig workers shape their conduct in
advance to pacify an algorithm they cannot see," line 317). The paper is triadic and the chapter is
underusing it: the freelancer's compliance practices are performed *toward clients* — underpricing services,
accepting bad jobs, staying visible — because the Job Success Score reads the client's behavior (hires,
completions, feedback) and converts it into the freelancer's search placement. From the Crossref abstract:
the authors show "how workers co-construct the power of algorithms through their anticipatory compliance
practices." *Note: abstract-level only; the in-text mechanism above should be checked against the full paper
before a specific sentence is written from it.*

**Use.** Cheapest fix of the three, since the citation is already in the chapter. One clause added to line 317
— naming the client as the party whose conduct the score also reads — converts a dyadic gloss into a triadic
one at no cost in length.

### 4.2 "A competence distributed like capital distributes standing like capital" (line 353)

**Verdict: soften, and ground the softened version. Do not defend it as written.**

Three problems with the sentence as it stands. (i) It is a claim about *standing*, and no study measures
standing — §8 says so itself (line 703–708). (ii) The evidence for the antecedent is correlational and about
*individuals*, while the chapter's actors in this paragraph are *firms*. (iii) The chiasmus does real
rhetorical work and hides the fact that the second half is asserted, not shown.

**Support available for a softened version:**

- **Ragnedda, M. (2018). Conceptualizing digital capital. *Telematics and Informatics, 35*(8), 2366–2375.
  DOI 10.1016/j.tele.2018.10.006.** **VERIFIED**, Crossref: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.tele.2018.10.006
  This is the exact theoretical move the sentence needs. Ragnedda theorizes digital capital as a Bourdieusian
  capital that *interacts with and converts into* the "five capitals" (economic, social, cultural, political,
  personal), and argues that this conversion produces the second- and third-level digital divides. A competence
  that behaves like a capital, and converts into other capitals, is a documented claim — that is exactly the
  form of the chapter's sentence, minus the leap to standing.
  Follow-on if wanted: Ragnedda, Ruiu & Addeo (2020), "Measuring digital capital: An empirical investigation,"
  *New Media & Society* — **UNVERIFIED**, bibliographic only, DOI not fetched this session.
- **Cotter & Reisdorf (2020)** supplies the empirical antecedent (competence tracks socioeconomic position).

**Suggested reformulation shape** (not prose — the writer's job): a competence that tracks capital will tend to
distribute the standing built on it along the same lines, and the second half is a prediction the chapter's
research program is designed to test, not a finding. That version is defensible, keeps the point, and costs
nothing but the chiasmus.

abstract is about power asymmetries, risks, and strategies, with no rent-from-opacity claim.

Sources that *do* make a version of this argument, in descending order of directness:

1. **Petre, C., Duffy, B. E., & Hund, E. (2019). "Gaming the system": Platform paternalism and the politics of
   algorithmic visibility. *Social Media + Society, 5*(4). DOI 10.1177/2056305119879995.** **VERIFIED**,
   Crossref: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/2056305119879995. Analysis of 105 news articles plus
   platform guidelines from Google, Facebook, and Instagram. The finding is the chapter's claim, almost
   word for word: the line platforms draw between "illegitimate algorithmic manipulation" and "legitimate
   strategy" is "nebulous and largely reflective of their material interests," and gaming accusations are "an

   distributional claim. **This is the replacement citation.**
2. **Cotter, K. (2023). "Shadowbanning is not a thing": black box gaslighting and the power to independently
   know and credibly critique algorithms. *Information, Communication & Society, 26*(6), 1226–1243.
   DOI 10.1080/1369118X.2021.1994624.** **VERIFIED**, Crossref:
   https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/1369118X.2021.1994624. Sole author, Penn State. Instagram

5. **Burrell (2016)**, first opacity (intentional corporate/state secrecy). Correct and standard, but see the
