# q189 — review

## What holds

The instrument control passes: the faithful triad reads triadic at max_phi = 2.0. The control arm
is a genuine null: a no-editor pair has core_jaccard = 1 and pivotality_agrees = 1, so the
editor-pivotality flag does not fire where there is no editor to contest. Both hypotheses were
fixed before the probe ran. The run is deterministic and byte-identical across three executions.

## What the result does and does not show

The finding is that an agreeing whole-system verdict (phi_gap = 0, both triadic) can hide a
membership dispute that core_jaccard and the pivotality flag recover. This is a property of the
two coded rule sets, not evidence about real editorial practice. The gate and conduit accounts are
synthetic, chosen to instantiate the two narrations cleanly.

## Threats considered

The pivotality flag is defined as major-complex membership. An alternative operationalization —
freezing the editor node and rereading the verdict — collapses the triad under both accounts,
because freezing any node in a small loop breaks integration. That alternative makes every node
trivially pivotal and was rejected. Membership in the integrated core is the operationalization
that distinguishes a party in every integrating coalition from a droppable one, matching the
veto-player reading (Probe 11).

The two accounts were built so the conduit account stays triadic rather than collapsing to dyadic;
a dyadic conduit would have produced verdict_agreement = 0 and made the dispute visible at the
verdict level, which would not have tested whether the spread recovers a dispute the whole-system
verdict hides. The chosen conduit form keeps the whole-system verdict agreeing, which is the
harder and more informative case.

## Scope

In-silico throughout. No worker is measured. The construct is account divergence, validated on the
control; the empirical arms are synthetic.
