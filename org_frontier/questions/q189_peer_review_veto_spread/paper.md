# q189 — The Editor's Core Membership in Dispute: A Gate/Conduit Spread

A manuscript decision passes through reviewers, an editor, and back to the author. Reviewers tend
to narrate the editor as a gate that binds: a party in every coalition that can move the
manuscript forward. Authors tend to narrate the same editor as a conduit that forwards a verdict
the reviewers already reached. The two narrations describe one coordination. They place the
editor differently. This study asks whether the placement difference moves the editor's membership
in the integrated core, and whether an editor-pivotality flag added to the disagreement bridge
disagrees across the two accounts.

The two accounts are rule sets over a three-party editorial triad: reviewer verdict R, editor E,
author-facing outcome A. The reviewers' gate account is the faithful strict-mediation triad, with
the editor reading both R and A and the others reading the editor. The authors' conduit account
keeps a still-integrating triad but the editor forwards the reviewer verdict while the author
reads the reviewers directly, so the integrated core runs reviewer-to-author and the editor drops
out. The disagreement-Φ bridge from q183 scores the spread between the two; its pivotality
extension adds the editor-membership flag.

The whole-system verdict agrees across the two accounts. Both read triadic with max_phi = 2.0, so
phi_gap = 0. A reader checking only the structure label or the Φ magnitude would record no
disagreement. The dispute lives one level down. The major complex is (R, E, A) under the gate
account and (R, A) under the conduit account, giving core_jaccard = 0.667. The editor-pivotality
flag reads True under the gate account and False under the conduit account, so pivotality_agrees =
0. The added flag carries the veto-player claim that the agreeing whole-system verdict misses.

The control is a no-editor two-reviewer pair. With no editor node, editor pivotality is vacuous
and core membership agrees trivially: core_jaccard = 1 and pivotality_agrees = 1. The flag fires
only where there is an editor whose placement the two accounts contest.

H1 is supported: the gate/conduit spread puts the editor's core membership in dispute, with the
editor core under the gate account and droppable under the conduit account. H2 is confirmed: the
editor-pivotality flag disagrees across the two accounts, registering the veto-player claim as
account divergence.

The accounts are coder-supplied rule sets, not measured worker states. The gate and conduit
results stand on synthetic data. What the bridge measures is divergence between two stated
accounts of one coordination, validated here on the control. Whether real authors and reviewers
narrate the editorial triad in the gate and conduit forms modeled here is an empirical question
this study does not settle.
