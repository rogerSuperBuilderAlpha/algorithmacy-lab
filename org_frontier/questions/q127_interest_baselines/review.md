# Q127 — review of prior work

The question is the robustness follow-up to Q126
([`q126_interested_mediator`](../q126_interested_mediator/paper.md)), which introduced the interested
mediator and found that self-interest erodes coordination irreducibility, with a denying agenda the fastest
to collapse it. Q126 ran one faithful baseline (AND) and named the OR baseline as the natural robustness
extension. Q127 runs that extension and two more, and finds the denial asymmetry is baseline-relative.

The re-integration result connects to two standing findings. Q126's own order-averaged sweep already saw a
non-monotone approve ladder — Φ rising at some override sets — without isolating the cause; Q127 locates it
in the baseline, showing a weakly-irreducible faithful mediator (Φ = 0.5) sharpened by self-interest. The
parity scaling results (probes 113–117) established that XOR/parity mediators occupy a structurally distinct
regime from conjunctive ones; Q127 finds the interested-mediator dynamics also split along that line — sparse
conjunctive baselines erode, balanced parity-class baselines re-integrate.

What is new is the separation of the collapse agenda from denial itself, and the minority-output-class
account of which agenda is corrosive on which baseline.
