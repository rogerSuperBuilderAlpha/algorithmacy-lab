# Q127 findings — denial is not special: the collapse agenda flips with the baseline, and balanced mediators re-integrate

The pre-registered universal hypothesis is refuted, and two sharper findings take its place. Q126's "denial
collapses coordination fastest" is not a fact about denial. On the AND baseline the denying agenda is fastest
because it overrides the one state where the parties' joint determination commits; on the OR baseline the
asymmetry flips and the approving agenda is fastest, for the mirror reason. And on a balanced baseline
self-interest does the opposite of corrode — it first sharpens the mediator and raises Φ.

| baseline | minority output | approve Φ ladder (k=0..4) | k* | deny Φ ladder (k=0..4) | k* | faster collapse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AND (iff both) | 1 (one state) | 2.0, 0.5, 0, 0, 0 | 2 | 2.0, 0, 0, 0, 0 | 1 | **deny** |
| OR (iff either) | 0 (one state) | 2.0, 0, 0, 0, 0 | 1 | 2.0, 0.5, 0, 0, 0 | 2 | **approve** |
| AGREE (iff W==C) | balanced | 0.5, 0.5, 0, 0, 0 | 2 | 0.5, 2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 0 | 4 | approve |
| DIFFER (iff W!=C) | balanced | 0.5, 2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 0 | 4 | 0.5, 0.5, 0, 0, 0 | 2 | deny |

| claim | result |
|---|---|
| H1 (universal): the minority-output agenda always collapses fastest, balanced baselines symmetric | refuted |
| refined (1): on sparse baselines (AND, OR) the minority-output agenda collapses fastest | confirmed |
| refined (2): on balanced baselines, self-interest re-integrates (Φ rises above the faithful level) | confirmed |

## The two findings

**The minority-output flip (sparse baselines).** AND commits in exactly one state (both warrant) and conveys
in three; OR conveys in one state (neither warrants) and commits in three. The agenda that overrides the
rare, discriminating output collapses irreducibility at the first step, because that output is what made the
mediator read both parties. For AND that agenda is deny; for OR it is approve. Denial is fastest only when
the faithful mediator's rare output is a commit. The Q126 asymmetry is baseline-relative, not a property of
denial.

**Re-integration (balanced baselines).** The faithful XNOR and XOR mediators are only weakly irreducible
(Φ = 0.5): a mediator that commits whenever the parties agree, or whenever they differ, binds them loosely. A
dose of self-interest can sharpen it. Overriding XOR toward deny, or XNOR toward deny, builds a more
discriminating AND/OR-like rule, and Φ rises from 0.5 to 2.0 and holds there across three interestedness
levels before collapsing at full imposition. Self-interest is not uniformly corrosive; on a balanced
mediator it first integrates, and only the final, total override factors the coordination.

## Reading

The lesson sharpens Q126 rather than overturning it. A fully self-imposing mediator always ends dyadic — at
k = 4, ignoring the parties, the coordination factors on every baseline. What varies is the path there. When
the faithful mediator already reads the parties sharply (AND, OR), self-interest can only erode, and it
erodes fastest where it removes the rare output that did the discriminating. When the faithful mediator reads
them weakly (a balanced rule), self-interest can first concentrate the reading and raise the irreducibility
before destroying it. The corrosiveness of an interested third party depends on how discriminating its
faithful mediation already was.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a three-node Boolean model; evidence about the construct and the instrument, not about a real
platform. Four faithful baselines are tested (the four non-degenerate two-input functions of both parties);
the agenda is a fixed stance. The override order is the rational least-warranted-first path; an order-
averaged view (Q126) blunts but does not remove the asymmetry.
