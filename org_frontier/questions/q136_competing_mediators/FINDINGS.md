# Q136 findings — competition does not return the rent: substitutes destroy the value, complements split it

Both hypotheses hold, and together they refuse the simple market intuition. A second mediator does not lower
the platform's cut and leave the parties more of the same pie. It either dissolves the coordination's
integrated value or shares a larger one evenly, depending on whether the mediators substitute or complement.

## Reading

The two regimes are the two things "a second mediator" can mean. When the mediators are substitutes — the
parties can route through either — neither is necessary, and the irreducible coordination factors. The major
complex collapses to a degenerate two-node fragment; there is no triad, not even a single-mediator one, and
no integrated value for anyone to capture. Competition of this kind does not transfer the rent to the
parties; it abolishes the rent by abolishing the bottleneck, and the coordination's integrated structure with
it.

When the mediators are complements — both required — the value rises (Φ = 4.0, double the single-mediator
triad) and splits evenly: each of the four parties, mediators included, takes a quarter. A required second
mediator does dilute each mediator's rent, from two-thirds to a quarter, and raises each party's share from a
sixth to a quarter. But this is not competition lowering a price; it is a second bottleneck added in series,
raising the total value and distributing it equally. The parties gain a larger share of a larger pie, not a
larger share of the same one.

Neither regime is the market story of competition returning the platform's cut to the users. The structural
options are to destroy the coordination or to stack another toll on it.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a four-node model; the two mediators are identical conjunctive committers, and differentiated
mediators (reading different parties) are untested. Value read at the integrating state; the Φ-to-money
bridge is open (Q122), so "value/share/rent" name Shapley allocations of Φ.
