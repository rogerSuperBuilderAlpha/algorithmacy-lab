# q172 — Where interest concentrates: a facet signature, not a uniform tax

The interested-mediator line ran six studies, one per theory battery. Each watched a mediator turn
from opaque to interested and measured how much harder one facet of the worker's task became. This
study pulls the six difficulty measures into one vector per facet and asks a question the survey arm
cares about: when a mediator starts serving its own agenda, does the difficulty rise uniformly
across the facets the worker must handle, or does it concentrate on a few?

## The facets and the measure

Six facets, one per battery. Three carry the survey scale's names. Counterpart inference is the
direct-perception battery's worker-marginal fit error with the counterpart hidden. Signal
compression is the embodiment battery's Φ shed when the parties read the system at reduced fidelity.
Rule-change tracking is the predictive-processing battery's Φ lost when the commit drifts. The other
three are commitment (the whole-system Φ the actor binds), the phantom addressee (the major-complex
Φ that binds the worker to the held position), and the opacity floor (the residual surprise no
W-only model removes).

For each facet the difficulty is read twice: once for the faithful baseline gate, once for the
interested gate that imposes an agenda on the state the parties least warrant. The gap between them
is the interest tax. The whole vector is rerun on the four Q127 faithful baselines, AND, OR, XNOR,
and XOR, so the tax can be ranked and its ordering checked for stability.

## The result

The tax is selective. It ranges from 0.062 to 1.250 across the six facets, so interest is no flat
surcharge. The selectivity points away from the survey scale. The two facets interest hits hardest
are commitment and signal compression. Commitment, which the scale does not name, carries the
largest tax. Counterpart inference, one of the two facets the pre-registered hypothesis named,
carries the smallest. The ordering is mostly but not fully stable across baselines: eleven of
fifteen facet pairs keep their order on all four, and four re-shuffle.

Both hypotheses fail. H1 predicted the two survey-named facets, counterpart inference and
rule-change tracking, would top the ranking; commitment and signal compression top it instead. H2
predicted the ordering would hold across every baseline; four pairs re-shuffle. The honest reading
is that interest does concentrate, on commitment and compression, and that which facet it hits
hardest shifts somewhat with the faithful baseline underneath.

## What it says for the survey bridge

The survey bridge reads its three facets as the three places the cognitive theories break, and
treats them as the difficulties a competent worker overcomes. This study does not undercut that the
facets are distinct difficulties. It tells against a stronger reading: that interest is the force
that raises all three together and most. The largest structural effect of an interested mediator in
these forms is on the binding it holds and the meaning it compresses, and only secondarily on the
counterpart inference the scale foregrounds.

## Scope

Exact Φ and closed-form information on three- and four-node Boolean models. The numbers are evidence
about the instrument and the construct. "Agenda", "interest", and "facet" label output values and
rule structure, not measured intent. The mapping from facet to survey scale is a formal prediction
the survey arm can test on workers; nothing here is a measurement of a worker.
