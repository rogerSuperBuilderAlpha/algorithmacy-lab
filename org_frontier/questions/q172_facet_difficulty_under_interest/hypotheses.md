# q172 — Hypotheses

The interested-mediator line ran six studies, one per theory battery: computationalism (q163),
direct perception (q164), embodiment (q165), theory of mind (q166), and predictive processing in
two readings, the opacity floor (q168) and the moving target (q171). Each read one battery's
difficulty measure as the mediator went from opaque (a faithful baseline gate) to interested
(Q126's agenda imposed on the states the parties least warrant). This study aggregates the six into
one difficulty vector per facet and asks where interest concentrates.

Three of the six facets carry the survey scale's names: counterpart inference (direct perception),
signal compression (embodiment), and rule-change tracking (the moving target). The other three are
commitment (computationalism), the phantom addressee (theory of mind), and the opacity floor.

## H1 — facet-selective, on the named facets

The interested-minus-opaque difficulty gap is largest for the counterpart-inference and
rule-change-tracking facets and near-zero for at least one other facet, so interest is
facet-selective rather than a uniform tax.

NULL: the gap is statistically indistinguishable across all six facets, so interest raises every
facet's difficulty equally.

## H2 — baseline-invariant facet ordering

The facet ordering of the interested-vs-opaque gap is preserved across all four Q127 faithful
baselines (AND, OR, XOR, XNOR), so which facets interest hits hardest is baseline-invariant even
though the magnitude is not.

NULL: the facet ordering re-shuffles across baselines, so there is no stable facet signature of
interest.

Both hypotheses were fixed before computing. Both were refuted by the run.
