# q172 — Findings

Interest does not tax every facet equally, and it does not tax the facets the survey scale names
most. Both pre-registered hypotheses are refuted.

## Interest tax per facet (mean |gap| over the four baselines, agenda-averaged)

| rank | facet                 | tax   | survey-named |
|------|-----------------------|-------|--------------|
| 1    | commitment            | 1.250 | no           |
| 2    | signal_compression    | 0.981 | yes          |
| 3    | rule_change_tracking  | 0.692 | yes          |
| 4    | phantom_addressee     | 0.250 | no           |
| 5    | opacity_floor         | 0.125 | no           |
| 6    | counterpart_inference | 0.062 | yes          |

The tax spans 0.062 to 1.250, so interest is plainly selective rather than flat. The two facets it
hits hardest are commitment and signal compression. The two facets H1 named in advance,
counterpart inference and rule-change tracking, do not top the ranking: counterpart inference is the
smallest tax of all six, and commitment, a facet the survey scale does not name, is the largest.

## Verdicts

- **H1 (interest is facet-selective, concentrated on the named survey facets): REFUTED.** Interest
  is selective, but the top two facets are commitment and signal_compression, not the pre-named
  counterpart_inference and rule_change_tracking. No non-named facet sits ten times below the
  smaller named facet, because the smaller named facet (counterpart inference, 0.062) is itself the
  floor.
- **H2 (the facet signature of interest is baseline-invariant): NOT SUPPORTED.** Eleven of fifteen
  facet pairs keep their order across all four baselines; four pairs re-shuffle, including
  signal_compression vs rule_change_tracking. The ordering is mostly stable but not invariant.

## Reading

The selectivity is real, the facet target is not where the survey scale points. Commitment, the
binding the actor holds, moves most under interest, which fits the prior result that the binding
collapses fastest when the agenda overrides the discriminating output state. Counterpart inference
barely moves at k=1 because a single override leaves the worker-marginal of most baselines
unchanged. The result tells against reading the survey's three facets as the three places interest
concentrates; the structural difficulty interest imposes lands first on commitment and compression.
This is on synthetic Boolean forms; the survey-facet mapping is a formal prediction, not a finding
on workers.
