# Q141 — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

Q128 found a predatory mediator re-integrates the coordination when its objective adapts to both parties;
Q129 found the immediate adaptive objective displaces a party (core {S, C, O}, the worker pushed out). Q141
asks whether the timescale of adaptation matters: if the objective learns on a lag — through a memory that
holds the parties' joint state for a step — does the displaced worker return?

Immediate (Q128): W' = S, S' = O, C' = S, O' = W ∧ C. Lagged: insert a memory M with M' = W ∧ C and let the
objective read it, O' = M.

- **H1.** The lagging objective re-integrates the worker: the lagged form's major complex includes the worker,
  unlike the immediate form's core {S, C, O}.
- **H2.** The lagged form is more integrated than the immediate one — the memory raises Φ.
- **Null.** The lag makes no difference to the worker's place or the Φ.

Method: build both forms; whole-system verdict and major complex; worker-in-core and Φ comparison.
