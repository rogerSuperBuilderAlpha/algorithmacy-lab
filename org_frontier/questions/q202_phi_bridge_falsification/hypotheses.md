# q202 — hypotheses

The survey-arm bridge maps each simulated worker's coordination row to a W-S-C Boolean form and
reads a per-worker Φ_coord with the exact IIT-4.0 instrument. Studies 1-9 of the line report
Φ_coord-to-ACS relations on the simulated cohorts. This study fixes the cheap-explanation threats
as a pre-registered battery and tests whether the bridge survives them.

**H1 (worker-form-shuffle null).** A randomization test that shuffles the worker-to-form mapping
destroys every Φ_coord-to-ACS relation. The real correlation falls outside the shuffled null at
p < .05 (two-sided), and the shuffled effects center on 0. Null: the real effect lies inside the
shuffled null, so the relation is a labelling artifact that any per-worker scalar paired with ACS
by the same index would reproduce.

**H2 (incremental validity over a cheap proxy).** Φ_coord predicts ACS incrementally over a cheap
node-count/edge-density structural proxy of the same forms (ΔR² > 0, bootstrap CI excludes 0), so
the irreducibility content carries variance the cheap proxy misses. Null: the cheap proxy fully
subsumes Φ_coord, and the incremental ΔR² CI includes 0.

The proxy is the cheap structural reading of a form: edges divided by n² of its connectivity
matrix, computed without any Φ. It is a genuine competitor because it separates the two
whole-system forms exactly as Φ_coord does. The discriminating case is a form space rich enough
that edge density and irreducibility rank the forms differently.

Scope: the cohorts are simulated. No worker is measured. The verdicts are evidence about the
instrument and the proxy on synthetic data.
