# q145 — review

## What the probe shows

A single hub that reads and is read by all parties binds the whole set into one core, with Φ =
n-1. The core follows the hub's span node for node: at span k the core is the hub plus its k
covered parties, and uncovered parties drop out. H1 holds. H2 fails: Φ tracks the span count
k+1, not the span fraction, so curves at fixed f do not collapse across n.

## Strengths

The control passes on a known case (the faithful triad, Φ 2.0). The sweep is small and exact,
and the verdicts are read straight off the printed numbers. The H2 refutation is reported as a
refutation, with the reason given: the count, not the fraction, controls Φ.

## Limits

The hub uses a conjunction (AND) over its spanned parties. A disjunctive (OR) hub or a mixed
gate could give a different Φ profile, and the count-vs-fraction conclusion is not guaranteed to
carry to other gates without checking. The sweep stops at n = 6 for cost; the linear Φ = n-1
pattern is clean over 4-6 but is an extrapolation beyond it. The unspanned-party Φ = 1 at f = 0
is the isolated-singleton artifact, not evidence of integration, and the verdict logic treats it
as such.

## Reach

Synthetic forms only. The law constrains the model, not any organization. It says where to look
if a real coordination form is suspected to run through one spanning coordinator: the size of the
irreducible core should track that coordinator's actual reach. Testing that is future empirical
work, not a claim made here.
