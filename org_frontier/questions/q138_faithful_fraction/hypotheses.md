# Q138 — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

A predatory mediator kills the coordination (Q126, Q131). A regulator can force the mediator to commit the
parties' joint determination on some states. Q138 asks how much forced faithfulness revives the coordination,
and who gets the value. The mediator's default is the predatory constant (always approve); the regulator
forces m of its four states back to faithful AND, highest-warrant first.

- **H1.** A minimum faithful fraction revives the coordination: below it Φ = 0, at or above it Φ > 0.
- **H2.** Minimum-viable regulation revives with an egalitarian split (each party ≈ the mediator), and the
  mediator's two-thirds returns only at full faithfulness — the last increment concentrates the rent.
- **Null.** Faithfulness has no threshold and no effect on the split.

Method: sweep m = 0..4 forced-faithful states; per m the Φ and the Shapley split at the integrating state.
