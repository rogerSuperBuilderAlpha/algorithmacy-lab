# Q212 — Must the merging channel run between the mediators, or does any direct cross-triad link merge two triads? · Stage 1 review

**Question.** q211 merged two triads with a direct channel between their mediators. Does the merger depend
on the channel running mediator-to-mediator, or does a direct cross-triad link at any homologous node pair —
worker-to-worker, counterpart-to-counterpart — merge the two triads just as well?

**Agenda id.** Follow-on from q211 (#365), testing its load-bearing claim about where the link must sit.

## Prior probes that bear on this

| probe | finding | how it relates |
|---|---|---|
| #365 (q211) | a direct mediator-mediator channel merges two triads; AND channel core {S1,W2,S2,C2} at Φ=3.0 spans both triads | the result this question localizes: it linked the mediators specifically |
| #364 (q210) | a shared counterpart does not merge two triads | shows the link's placement and directness both matter |
| single-triad reference | conjunctive triad reads triadic at Φ=2.0 | the per-triad baseline |

## The gap

q211 explained the merger by saying the channel joined "the parts that already carry each triad's
integration, the mediators." That explanation makes a testable prediction: move the channel off the
mediators and the merger should weaken or fail. The mediator is the conjunctive node, the one whose update
reads both other nodes; the worker and counterpart are the peripheral nodes that just pass the mediator's
state. If only a mediator-mediator channel merges, the integrating role of the mediator is what the merger
needs. If a worker-worker or counterpart-counterpart channel merges equally, the merger needs only a direct
cross-triad link anywhere, and the mediator framing of q211 is incidental. The probe sweeps the same AND
channel across the three homologous node pairs and reads which placements produce a core spanning both
triads, and at what Φ.
