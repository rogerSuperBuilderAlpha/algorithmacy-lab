# Q128 findings — adaptation re-integrates a predatory mediator, but only if its objective reads both parties

The pre-registered hypothesis is refined by the result. A predatory mediator — one that commits only its own
objective and never reads the parties directly — does not bind them when its objective is a fixed stance, as
Q126 found. Letting the objective adapt to the parties rescues the coordination, but not just any
adaptation: the objective must encode *both* parties. An objective that tracks one party leaves the form
dyadic; an objective that reads both makes it triadic, with the binding routed through the objective.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## The two findings

**Re-integration is a both-parties condition.** A frozen objective gives a constant mediator that reads no
one; the parties drop out and the form is dyadic. An objective that adapts to a single party (O' = W or
O' = C) still leaves the mediator reading only one party at one remove, and the form stays dyadic. Only when
the objective encodes both parties (W ∧ C, W ∨ C, W ⊕ C) does the predatory mediator become irreducible.
This is the lab's core requirement appearing one level up: irreducible coordination needs both parties
bound, and a self-executing system meets that requirement only through an objective that carries them both.

**The objective is the conduit.** Whenever the form re-integrates, the objective O is in the major complex.
The binding no longer runs party-to-party through a faithful mediator; it routes W, C → O → S → W, C — the
parties shape the objective, the objective drives the commit, the commit returns to the parties. Under the
XOR adaptation all four nodes enter the core, the fullest re-integration: worker, system, counterpart, and
the system's own objective form one irreducible whole.

## Reading

Self-interest and irreducible coordination can coexist. A platform that acts only on its own objective still
binds the parties it coordinates — provided that objective is learned from both of them. The fixed-agenda
erosion of Q126 was not a fact about self-interest as such, but about a self-interest that does not listen.
An adaptive objective that encodes both parties restores the bind and becomes a member of it. What an
interested system cannot do is bind the parties while attending to only one, or to none.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a four-node Boolean model; evidence about the construct and the instrument, not about a real
platform. "Objective", "agenda", "predatory" label the rules, not measured intent. The mediator is the
extreme case S' = O (commits only the objective); intermediate mixtures of objective and direct party-
reading, and objectives with their own memory or a slower adaptation timescale, are the natural extensions.
