# Q128 — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

Q126 modelled a self-interested mediator with a fixed agenda and found it erodes the coordination: a
predatory mediator that commits only its own objective (S' = O), never reading the parties, drives the form
to dyadic. A real platform's objective is not a fixed stance; it tracks the parties. Q128 asks whether
adaptation alone rescues the bind. The model is four nodes — worker W, system S, counterpart C, objective O
— with the mediator predatory (S' = O), the parties reading the system (W' = S, C' = S), and the objective
updating by an adaptation rule O' = g(W, C) swept from no adaptation (frozen) to full.

- **H1.** A predatory mediator is dyadic when its objective is a frozen stance (O' = O) but irreducible when
  its objective adapts to the parties (O' depends on W, C). Adaptation re-integrates a coordination that
  fixed self-interest destroyed.
- **H2.** The adaptive objective O joins the major complex — it becomes a constitutive member of the
  irreducible core — while a frozen objective stays outside.
- **Null.** Adaptation makes no difference; the predatory mediator is dyadic however its objective is set.

H1 and H2 are the pre-registered predictions. As recorded in [`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md), reading a single
party turned out not to be enough; the refined condition the data supports — the objective must read *both*
parties — is stated there.
