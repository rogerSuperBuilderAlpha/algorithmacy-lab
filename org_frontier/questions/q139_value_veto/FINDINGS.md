## Reading

Q112's decoupling — equal power to destroy, unequal share of value — is not a fixed feature of mediated
coordination. It is the integration. When the form is fully integrated (Φ = 2.0) the mediator's bottleneck
commands two-thirds of the value while every party holds an equal veto, so the gap is at its maximum, one
third. When the form is weakly integrated (Φ = 0.5) the value splits evenly, matching the equal veto, and the
gap is zero. The veto is universal throughout; only the value moves.

Interest moves the form along this axis. On the sparse mediator, self-interest destroys integration, dropping
the form from Φ = 2.0 to 0.5, and the value democratizes to match the veto: the gap closes. On the balanced
mediator, self-interest re-integrates, lifting the form from 0.5 to 2.0, and the value re-concentrates: the
gap opens. So an interested platform either gives up its disproportionate share (where its self-interest
loosens its grip) or seizes it (where its self-interest tightens it), but the parties' equal power to destroy
the coordination is untouched in either case. The structural lever the parties hold — the veto — is constant;
what they are paid for holding it is the integration the mediator's faithfulness commands.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on the three-node triad; two baselines and the approve agenda (under deny the re-integrating baseline
is XNOR, by Q133's symmetry). Veto is binary (knockout collapses or not); the integration window is narrow
(only Φ ∈ {2.0, 0.5} occur on these ladders). Value read at the integrating state; the Φ-to-money bridge is
open (Q122).
