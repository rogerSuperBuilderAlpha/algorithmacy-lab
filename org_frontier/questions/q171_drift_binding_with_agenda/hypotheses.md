# q171 — hypotheses

A mediator can be opaque in two ways at once. It can drift: retraining on what it is fed, so its rule
is a moving target (PP4 crosses a faithful gate A = W ∧ C toward a flipped rule B = W ∨ C with
probability d). It can be interested: imposing an agenda a on the k states where the parties least
warrant it (Q126's mediator(agenda, k)). The question is whether the two erosions of the binding add
when both are present, or whether one masks the other.

## H1 — super-additive destruction

Whole-system Φ for a mediator that is both drifting (d) and interested (k) is strictly below the
multiplicative combination of the two separate decays, Φ(d, 0)·Φ(0, k)/Φ(0, 0), at every interior cell.
Drift and agenda destroy the binding together more than the product of their separate effects.

NULL: combined Φ equals the multiplicative combination, so the two erosions are independent.

## H2 — drift re-integrates an interested mediator

There exists a (d, k) region where adding drift to an interested mediator raises Φ above its d = 0 value
at the same k. The drift re-introduces party-dependence on states the agenda had overridden, so
retraining partially re-integrates an interested mediator.

NULL: Φ is monotonically non-increasing in both d and k everywhere, so drift never helps.

## Scope

Exact Φ on a three-node Boolean model. The result is evidence about the instrument and the construct.
No worker, mediator, or platform is measured. "Drift", "agenda", "approve", "deny" label the rule and
its output values. The empirical reading is on synthetic data only.
