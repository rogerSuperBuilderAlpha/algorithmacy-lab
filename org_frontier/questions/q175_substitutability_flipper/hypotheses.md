# q175 — hypotheses

A coded account can read the worker two ways. The specific reading names one irreplaceable party:
the system binds the worker to the counterpart, S = W & R. The substitutable reading names the
worker as one slot in an interchangeable pool: S = (D1 | ... | Dk) & R, where any pool member can
fill the slot. The second reading is a coding choice. These hypotheses fix what that choice does to
the exact-Φ verdict and to the coder-disagreement interval before any number is computed.

## H1 — the substitutability flipper

Re-encoding S = W & R as S = (D1 | ... | Dk) & R drives max_phi to 0 (dyadic) for every pool size
k >= 2 on synthetic accounts.

H1-null: at least one k >= 2 retains max_phi > PHI_EPS, so a substitutable pool does not dissolve
the worker.

## H2 — the interval flags the flip

When the substitutable-vs-specific reading is contested and coders split, the propagated Φ reading
interval spans both zero and the specific-reading Φ, with interval lower = 0 and upper > 0, in more
than 90% of contested forms.

H2-null: the interval excludes 0 or excludes the specific Φ in more than 10% of contested forms, so
it fails to surface the flip.

## Controls

The specific reading must stay triadic at max Φ_MIP = 2.0. A unanimous-pool coder panel, where
every coder reads the account as pooled (Φ = 0), must collapse the bridge CI to [0, 0].
