# q175 — findings

Coding the worker as one slot in an interchangeable pool flips the exact-Φ verdict from triadic to
dyadic, and the coder-disagreement interval marks the flip as coding-driven when coders split. Both
hypotheses hold on synthetic accounts.

The specific worker binds the system to the counterpart and reads triadic at Φ = 2.0. Re-encoding
the worker as a pool of k = 2, 3, 4 substitutable members drops max_phi to 0 at every pool size: any
member can fill the slot, so no single member is irreducibly necessary and the irreducible structure
vanishes. Across 597 contested coder panels the propagated reading interval runs from 0 to the
specific Φ on 97.5% of forms, so the interval surfaces the flip as a coding split rather than a real
structural reading.

## Verdicts

- **H1 (substitutable pool drives max_phi to 0 for all k >= 2): SUPPORTED.** Pooled readings at
  k = 2, 3, 4 all read dyadic at max_phi = 0; the specific reading stays triadic at 2.0.
- **H2 (contested-reading interval spans [0, specific Φ] in > 90% of forms): SUPPORTED.** Span
  fraction 0.9749 over 597 contested panels.

## Scope

The rule sets and coder panels are synthetic, with known ground truth. The flip is a property of the
coding choice, not a measurement of a worker. The interval reports how much the reading is contested,
not whether either reading is correct of any real coordination. Results are on synthetic data.
