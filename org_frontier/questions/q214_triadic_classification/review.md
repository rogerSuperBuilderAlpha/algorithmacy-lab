# Q214 — Triadic classification across the literature · Stage 1 review

**Question.** The scholarship on triadic relationships names many kinds of third party — Simmel's mediator and
*tertius gaudens*, Obstfeld's conduit / gaudens / *iungens*, Gould and Fernandez's five brokerage roles,
Burt's structural-hole broker, the two-sided platform. Each distinction is drawn verbally. q213 built a formal
instrument, the bypass-counterfactual, that sorts a mediator as necessary, contingent, partial, or reducible.
Do the canonical triad types of the literature sort cleanly under that instrument, and does the formal test
recover — and refine — the verbal distinctions?

## The gap

The brokerage literature distinguishes the third party that **joins** two others from the one that **profits by
keeping them apart**. Obstfeld, Borgatti and Davis (2014) name conduit, *tertius gaudens*, and *tertius
iungens* as three orientations; Quintane and Carnabuci (2016) define gaudens as intermediating the flow and
iungens as facilitating direct exchange. Gould and Fernandez (1989) partition brokers into five roles by the
group membership of the three parties. None of this is computed. There is no membership criterion that says
which third parties are in the irreducible core because they integrate and which are there only because the
direct tie is forbidden. q213's bypass-counterfactual is that criterion. Applying it across the literature
tests whether the formal taxonomy recovers the verbal one, and where it refines it — a *tertius iungens* that
fully joins its parties creates the bypass and removes itself from the core, a split the verbal theory does not
draw. The instrument also has a boundary: a Heider-balance sentiment triad is mutual and has no mediator on a
forbidden edge, so the test does not apply, which marks the taxonomy's scope.
