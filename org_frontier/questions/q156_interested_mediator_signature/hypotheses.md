## Construct

An interested mediator's rule serves an objective: it weights one party's warrant over the
other's. A faithful mediator's rule treats the parties as interchangeable and commits a neutral
joint determination of the two warrants. On the triad W' = S, C' = S, S' = f(W, S, C), both kinds
of rule can read all three inputs and both can carry the full {W, S, C} major-complex core, so the
interested and faithful pools are matched on wiring graph and on structural Φ-core. Interestedness
is the symmetry of the rule under swapping the two parties: symmetric is faithful, asymmetric is
interested.

## H1

An interested mediator shows lower outgoing-edge DCRP prominence than a faithful mediator with
identical connectivity, separating the two in over 70% of matched pairs.

Null: outgoing prominence is statistically indistinguishable between interested and faithful
mediators.

## H2

The interested mediator's structural exact-Φ core is unchanged versus the faithful one, so a
behavioral signature would carry interestedness information that Φ-membership alone misses.

Null: major-complex membership already differs between interested and faithful mediators, so a
behavioral reading adds nothing.

## Verdict rule

H1 is SUPPORTED when matched-pair separation exceeds 0.70 and a one-sided Mann-Whitney test on the
per-form means clears alpha = 0.05; otherwise REFUTED. H2 is CONFIRMED when the two pools share an
identical major-complex core, so structural membership does not distinguish them.
