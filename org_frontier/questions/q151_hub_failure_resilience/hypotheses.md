# q151 — Hypotheses

Question. A single hub that gates every party binds them into one triadic complex. Ablate that hub by
freezing its rule to a constant and the mediation it supplied is gone. Does a topology with redundant
mediation — a backup hub, or a ring of non-hub nodes — keep a triadic verdict where the single hub
collapses to dyadic, and does the Φ that survives ablation track the cycle redundancy the topology holds
through its non-hub nodes?

H1 (fixed before computing). A single-hub topology collapses to dyadic when its hub is ablated, while a
two-hub (backup) or ring-backed topology of the same n retains a triadic verdict, so redundant mediation
buys verdict resilience.
Null: hub ablation leaves the single-hub verdict triadic, or collapses the redundant ones equally.

H2 (fixed before computing). The post-ablation Φ retained scales with the number of independent cycles the
topology has through non-hub nodes, which identifies cycle redundancy as the resilience reserve.
Null: retained Φ is independent of non-hub cycle count.

Decision rule. H1 is supported if every unablated form reads triadic, the single hub reads dyadic after
ablation, and at least one redundant form stays triadic after ablation. H2 is confirmed only if retained Φ
rises with non-hub cycle count and the non-hub cycle is necessary for retention, meaning every form with
zero non-hub cycles retains about zero Φ. A zero-cycle form that retains substantial Φ rejects H2: the
reserve would then be something other than the cycle.
