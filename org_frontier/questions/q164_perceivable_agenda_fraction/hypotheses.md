# q164 — hypotheses

Question: a hidden rule and an imposed agenda are two kinds of opacity. The hidden rule turns on a
counterpart the worker cannot see. The imposed agenda overrides the parties on the states where they
least warrant it. Does direct perception fare worse, the same, or better when the rule is interested
rather than merely hidden?

The interested mediator is the Q126 form: agenda a on the k least-warranted (W, C) states, faithful
AND elsewhere. The faithful k=0 gate and the battery's random-gate readings are the controls.

## H1 — the agenda is less perceivable from outcomes

The behavioral AUC for detecting an interested (k>0) mediator from W↔C outcome traces is strictly
below the battery's triadic-detection AUC of 0.67, so an agenda is even less perceivable from
outcomes than a hidden rule.

NULL: the interested-vs-faithful AUC is ≥ 0.67, so interest leaves a perceivable trace.

## H2 — the agenda adds inferential opacity beyond a hidden counterpart

The worker-marginal fit error with the counterpart hidden is larger against an interested mediator
than against a faithful hidden-counterpart gate at matched k, because the agenda decouples the
outcome from W on the overridden states. The test averages the matched-k comparison over the
partial-override regime k ∈ {1, 2, 3}.

NULL: the marginal fit error is equal or smaller, so an agenda adds no opacity beyond the hidden
counterpart.

## Scope

Exact constructions and sampled traces on small Boolean models. The result is evidence about the
instruments and the construct, not a measurement of a real platform. "Agenda", "approve", "deny" are
labels for output values, not measured intent. The D1 and D2 arms run on synthetic outcome traces.
