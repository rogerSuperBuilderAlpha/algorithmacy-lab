# Q131 — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

Q126–Q129 read whether an interested mediator keeps the coordination irreducible. Q131 asks the value
question the synthesis paper left open: as the mediator imposes its own agenda, how is the coordination's
value distributed? The value of a coalition is the integrated information of the subsystem on it, and a
party's Shapley value is its average marginal contribution (Q111). For the faithful mediator the split is
mediator 1.333 — two-thirds of Φ = 2.0 — and each party 0.333.

Two readings are possible. Interested mediation could be **rent extraction**, the mediator grabbing a larger
share as it serves itself, or **value destruction**, the mediator losing its own capture along with everyone
else because a system that stops reading the parties stops being the bottleneck that gave it its share.

- **H1.** Total value (Φ) falls as interestedness rises — self-interest shrinks the pie, consistent with the
  Q126 erosion.
- **H2.** The mediator's share of the shrinking value falls, not rises: serving its own agenda costs the
  mediator the bottleneck position that gave it two-thirds. Interested mediation is value destruction, not
  rent extraction.
- **Null.** The Shapley split is unchanged by interestedness.

Method: the Q126 interested mediator on the AND baseline — at level k the mediator imposes its agenda on the
k input states where the parties least warrant it, committing W ∧ C elsewhere. At each k for each agenda, the
Shapley value of subsystem-Φ for each party (Q111's value function, all-ones background) and the total Φ.
