# Q130 findings — necessity is broader than membership: a party can be pivotal yet outside the core

Both hypotheses hold. In the symmetric four-party forms, fourteen parties are pivotal yet outside the major
complex: removing any one collapses the coordination's irreducibility, but none is a member of the maximal-Φ
core. Every such party is read by the core. And no party is the reverse — no core member is dispensable. So
core membership is a strict subset of pivotality: every member of the core is necessary, and some necessary
parties are not members.

## Two routes, one demonstrated

The forms that produce pivotal-but-excluded parties are the symmetric ones whose major complex is a strict
subset — the majority clique and its relatives. There the exclusion is symmetry degeneracy: the maximal
complex is one representative of several equivalent maximal complexes related by the form's symmetry, and a
party absent from the reported core sits in an equivalent one. It is pivotal because the symmetry makes every
party necessary, and excluded because the major complex names only one of the equivalent subsets.

The curated asymmetric constructions did not produce the second route. A pure input scaffold — a party that
feeds others but reads no one (A' = A) — is dyadic in the forms tried, and under the stops-reading knockout
it is not even pivotal: a party that already interprets nothing is unchanged when made a spectator. So the
asymmetric route, where an excluded party is a one-directional necessary input rather than a symmetry twin,
is not demonstrated here and stays open. The gap shown is the bidirectional, symmetry-degenerate one, where
the core both reads the excluded party and is mirrored by it.

## Reading

A coordination's irreducibility can require a party that its measured core does not contain. The major
complex names where the integrated structure concentrates; pivotality names what the integration cannot lose.
In a symmetric coordination the two diverge because the structure concentrates into one of several
interchangeable cores while necessity spreads across all the parties the symmetry binds. Reading importance
off the major complex alone undercounts who the coordination depends on. This is the structural fact behind
Q129's displacement — an objective that enters the core can push out a party that remains pivotal — and
behind the influence-membership result that a party can move outcomes from outside the core.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on small Boolean models; evidence about the instrument and the construct, not about a real
organization. The pivotality knockout is the stops-reading spectator construct; a different intervention
(cutting a party's outgoing edges) would test the input-scaffold route the stops-reading knockout cannot
reach, and is the natural next step. The symmetry-degeneracy reading is inferred from the forms' symmetry and
the strict-subset cores; enumerating all maximal complexes per form would confirm the equivalent-core
account directly. Four parties; larger systems are untested.
