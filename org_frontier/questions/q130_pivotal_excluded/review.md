# Q130 — review of prior work

The question collects a thread that runs through several results. Q125
([`q125_four_party_higher_order`](../q125_four_party_higher_order/paper.md)) found the majority clique with
core {B, D} and A, C pivotal but outside it — the first explicit pivotal-but-excluded parties. Q129
([`q129_mediator_interpolation`](../q129_mediator_interpolation/paper.md)) found a coupled objective entering
the core and displacing a party that stays pivotal. The influence-membership thread
([`threads/influence_membership`](../../threads/influence_membership/THREAD.md)) established the behavioral
counterpart: a party can move outcomes from outside the major complex. Probe 9's inference-displacement
result is the same separation from another angle — a model in the core, the real party it stands for outside
it.

None of these characterized the gap. Q75's spectator-robustness result (probe 239) fixed the knockout
construct used here — a non-participating party added or removed leaves the core unchanged — and the
multiple-complexes result (Q94, probe 251) showed a coordination can hold several coexisting cores, which is
the mechanism the symmetry-degeneracy reading rests on. Q130 puts these together: it classifies every party
by membership and pivotality at once, and finds membership a strict subset of pivotality in the symmetric
four-party forms.
