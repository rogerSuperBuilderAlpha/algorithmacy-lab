# Q125 findings — no pure higher-order bind at four parties either: redundancy does not free the bind

H1 refuted. Across 267 irreducible four-party forms — the symmetric and explicitly redundant constructions
where a no-pivot bind is most likely — not one survives the knockout of any single party. Every irreducible
four-party form tested has all four parties pivotal, under both knockout definitions. The conjecture that
four-party redundancy frees the bind from any individual lynchpin is not borne out. The Q120 three-party
result extends to four parties on the forms tested, and the pattern is the same: a form is irreducible
exactly when every party is individually necessary to it.

| family | forms | irreducible | pivot count = 4 | pure higher-order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| homogeneous symmetric | 16 | 14 | 14 | 0 |
| curated redundant constructions | 8 | 6 | 6 | 0 |
| named multiparty 4-party | 7 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| canonical heterogeneous (OR/MAJ/AND/parity basis) | 256 | 244 | 244 | 0 |
| **total** | **287** | **267** | **267** | **0** |

The result is identical under both knockout definitions (spectator P' = x[P]; silenced P' = 0).

## Reading

Redundancy was the reason to expect four parties to differ. With four, two parties can in principle stand
in for a third, so a form could have stayed irreducible after losing any one. It does not happen — not even
in the majority cliques, where every party reads the other three and the structure is maximally
interchangeable. The major-complex result for the majority clique sharpens why: its irreducible core is the
pair {B, D}, yet knocking out A or C — parties outside that core — still collapses the whole-system
irreducibility to zero. Whole-system pivotality is broader than core membership; a party can sit outside
the irreducible core and still be necessary to the form's irreducibility.

So the picture from Q120 holds one party wider: irreducible coordination keeps an individual lynchpin, and
in fact every party is one. There is no four-party arrangement (among those tested) whose binding hides from
every single-party intervention.

## Limitations

The search is targeted, not exhaustive. Exact Φ on four nodes is about a second per form, so the full
16^4 = 65536 four-party symmetric family — let alone all non-symmetric wirings — is not swept. The 267
irreducible forms tested are the symmetric and explicitly redundant constructions, chosen because
interchangeability there is greatest and a no-pivot bind most likely. A null over them is strong evidence,
not a proof: a pure higher-order bind in some non-symmetric four-party form is not ruled out, and five or
more parties are untouched. The result is exact but in-silico — evidence about the instrument and the law,
not about a real organization.
