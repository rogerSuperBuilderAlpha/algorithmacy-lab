# Q125 — review of prior work

The question is the direct four-party follow-up to Q120
([`q120_higher_order_binding`](../q120_higher_order_binding/paper.md)), which established that among three
parties no pure higher-order bind exists: every irreducible form has all three parties pivotal. Q120's own
limitation names this question — at four parties, genuine redundancy (two parties jointly standing in for a
third) becomes possible, so the three-party result might not extend.

The lab's multiparty arm ([`multiparty/`](../../multiparty/)) already computes exact Φ on four- and
five-party forms; its named forms (the conjunctive pooled form reproduces the dissertation's Φ = 3.0 for
pooled rideshare) supply real four-party constructions to cross-check. The scaling results there read how
the triadic rate falls as parties are added, but do not ask about single-party pivotality. The
influence-membership thread ([`threads/influence_membership`](../../threads/influence_membership/THREAD.md))
shows influence and core membership come apart; Q125 finds the related fact that a party outside the major
complex can still be pivotal to the whole-system irreducibility, so pivotality is not the same as core
membership at four parties either.

What is open is whether redundancy can carry an irreducible bind that no single party holds. Q125 tests it
where redundancy is greatest — the symmetric and explicitly interchangeable four-party forms.
