# q143 — The Ring's Verdict and Where Its MIP Cut Falls

A conjunctive ring wires n nodes in a cycle and sets each one to the AND of its two neighbours.
The question is whether such a ring reads as an irreducible whole, and where the
minimum-information partition cuts it as the ring grows.

## What the ring is

Each node depends on exactly two others, its left and right neighbours, and the dependency graph
is a single cycle. The form is rotationally symmetric: relabelling the nodes by a rotation leaves
the rules unchanged. Symmetry is the reason to expect a single kind of bottleneck. A cut that
breaks the cycle into two arcs is the same cut wherever it falls, so its information cost should
not depend on n.

## Verdict and geometry

The ring is triadic at every n from 3 to 7. Its Φ over the integrating all-ones state stays
positive throughout. The major complex is the full node set at every size; no node leaves the
core. On the membership read this puts the ring with the pool, whose core is also full, and apart
from the chain, whose core is a sub-segment that sheds its interior.

The MIP geometry splits into two regimes. From n=4 to n=7 the minimal partition is a balanced
two-arc cut and its Φ is 4.0, flat across those four sizes. The balanced-cut, constant-Φ picture
is what rotational symmetry predicts, and it holds once the ring is large enough to have a genuine
two-arc structure. At n=3 the minimal partition is instead the complete three-way split into single
nodes, with Φ=6.0. The smallest ring is the exception: with three nodes, a two-arc cut and a
three-way cut are both on the table, and the three-way split is cheaper to the integrated whole, so
it sets the MIP.

## What the numbers settle

The pre-registered H1 claimed a single balanced two-arc cut of constant Φ at every n=3..7. The
data refute it. The constancy and the two-arc form are real for n>=4 and fail at n=3. H2, that the
core stays full at every size, holds. The ring is a topology that keeps every party bound as it
grows, with one MIP geometry for the minimal ring and another for all larger ones.

## Scope

The forms are synthetic Boolean coordination rules and Φ is the exact IIT-4.0 value over reachable
states. No worker or organization is measured here. The result is a baseline on a clean synthetic
object, the kind of anchor against which coded field cases are later read. The study explores the
instrument's behaviour on the ring; it does not claim Φ is the necessary test for irreducibility.
