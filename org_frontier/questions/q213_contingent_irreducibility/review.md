# Q213 — Contingent irreducibility · Stage 1 review

**Question.** A triad's mediator can be a conduit that relays and does no integrating, the kind the lab's
constitutive law says factors out. Yet some such mediators sit in the irreducible core anyway, because an
external constraint forbids the parties from reaching each other directly. A car dealer relays a car from
maker to buyer and integrates nothing, but franchise law forbids the maker selling direct, so the dealer
cannot be removed. Is this a distinct category of irreducibility — contingent on a constraint rather than on
the mediator's own role — and can a bypass-counterfactual test tell the two apart?

**Agenda id.** New category. Follow-on from the constitutive-mediation law and the design-operations program.

## The gap

The lab can already test whether a party is in the core (`major_complex`), add edges and watch the verdict
move (q51–q62), and write parties in and out by changing the mediator's function (q106). It has a clean law
for when a mediator is constitutive rather than a conduit, with three causal conditions, all about the
mediator's own rule. What it does not have is a category for a party that fails the constitutive test — a
pure conduit — yet sits in the core because a constraint forbids its bypass. The lab has never modeled an
external constraint (a law, a mandate, a required credential) as distinct from a causal update rule, and it
has no instrument that separates a party held in the core by its own integrating work from one held there
only by the absence of an alternative. The bypass-counterfactual is that instrument: restore the forbidden
edge and see whether the party survives. "Contingent on a constraint" is new to the repo; q96's contingency
is on state and resolves the opposite way.
