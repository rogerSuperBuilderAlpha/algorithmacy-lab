# q166 hypotheses (fixed before computing)

The theory-of-mind battery reads its address structure off the phantom-addressee triad
W'=S, S'=W∧C, C'=C. The worker binds the held position S: the major complex is {W, S} with
core Φ 2.0, the counterpart C is a referent the system reads but never joins the bound whole,
and the address is one-way, S depends on W (cm[0,1]=1) while the worker never reads C
(cm[2,0]=0). That is a faithful mediator. These hypotheses concern what happens to that
address when the gate becomes interested: the faithful W∧C is replaced by the Q126 mediator,
which imposes an agenda a on the k input states where the parties least warrant it.

## H1 — the addressee inverts

As k rises the major complex transitions from a worker-binding core (W in the core) to one
where W exits and the agenda's invariant share governs the remaining bind. The "held
position" of theory-of-mind inverts: the worker becomes the outside referent.

NULL: W remains in the core at every k>0 with positive Φ, so the address direction is
preserved under interest.

## H2 — the worker is dropped from the read while a bind survives

The one-way address asymmetry strengthens with k. cm[0,1] (S depends on W) drops to 0 before
the major-complex Φ collapses, so an interested mediator stops reading the worker while a bind
still survives.

NULL: cm[0,1] stays 1 until the major-complex Φ collapses, so the worker is read as long as
the bind survives.

## Scope

Exact Φ on a three-node Boolean model. The result is evidence about the instrument and the
construct, not a measurement of a real platform. "Agenda", "approve", "deny", and "address"
label output values and connectivity, not measured intent. The empirical arm runs on synthetic
data.
