# Q120 findings — no pure higher-order bind: every party in a triadic form is pivotal

H1 confirmed, and the result is sharper than H1 asked. Across 2,312 triadic forms in two families, under
two knockout definitions, no triadic form survives the knockout of any single party. Every triadic form
has not just one pivotal party but all three. Triadic coordination among three parties is maximally
fragile: each member is load-bearing, and removing any one collapses the irreducible bind to a factored,
dyadic one.

The fully-coupled family is the strong test. There the mediator is not a cut vertex — every party reads
the other two, so redundancy across the direct edges could have carried a bind that no single party holds.
It never does. 2,288 of its 4,096 forms are triadic, and all 2,288 lose triadicity under the knockout of
any one party.

| family | forms | triadic | pivot count = 3 | pivot count < 3 | pure higher-order (pivot count = 0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strict mediation | 256 | 24 | 24 | 0 | 0 |
| fully coupled | 4096 | 2288 | 2288 | 0 | 0 |

The pattern is identical under both knockout definitions (spectator: P' = x[P]; silenced: P' = 0), so it
does not rest on how a party is removed.

| H | Result | Verdict |
|---|--------|---------|
| H1 | no triadic form is a pure higher-order bind | confirmed (0 of 2312) |

## Reading

Irreducibility in three-party coordination is an all-or-nothing property of the trio: the form is triadic
exactly when each of the three parties is individually necessary to it. There is no triadic form held
together only as a whole, with each part dispensable. This tightens the law from "substitutability of any
party collapses it" to a biconditional on this domain — a form is triadic if and only if all three parties
are pivotal — and it removes a way the construct could have been soft (a bind no single intervention
reaches). It also bounds the claim: the result is shown for three parties. Whether a pure higher-order
bind appears at four or more parties, where genuine redundancy (two parties jointly substituting for a
third) becomes possible, is the open follow-up.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on small Boolean models; evidence about the instrument and the law, not about a real organization.
The knockout is a structural intervention on the update rules, the model-relative reading of
substitutability. The two families are large but not exhaustive of three-party wirings (they fix which
parties each rule may read). The four-party question is not addressed here.
