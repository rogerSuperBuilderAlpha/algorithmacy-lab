# Q134 findings — the mediator's rent dilutes as the coordination grows

H1 refuted, H2 confirmed. The mediator's two-thirds is not scale-invariant. As outer parties are added the
total integrated value grows (Φ = n − 1, the conjunctive scaling) but the mediator's share of it falls toward
one-half, while the outer parties collectively keep more.

## Reading

The mediator is in every productive coalition at every size, yet its slice shrinks as the pool grows. The
two-thirds is a three-party number, not a law of bottlenecks: in a larger conjunctive coordination the
indispensable mediator still takes the plurality, but a falling one, heading toward one-half, while the
parties together hold the rest. Each individual party holds less as the parties multiply, so the dilution
redistributes from the mediator to the collective of parties, not to any one of them. The concentrated
platform-power reading of Q111 is therefore bounded to small coordinations; the platform that mediates many
parties is structurally less dominant than the one that mediates two.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on the conjunctive star to n = 5 (exact Φ scales steeply); the disjunctive and other topologies are
untested and could dilute differently. Value read at the integrating state; the Φ-to-money bridge is open
(Q122), so "share" and "rent" name Shapley allocations of Φ. The trend toward one-half is read off three
points, not a proven limit.
