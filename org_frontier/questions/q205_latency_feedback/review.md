# Q205 — Does latency in the mediator's feedback path hide integration from the one-step instrument? · Stage 1 review

**Question.** When a mediator's effect on the parties arrives after a delay, does exact one-step Φ still
read the coordination as integrated, and does the answer depend on whether the delay is given its own node?

## The gap

q204 computed exact Φ on a real narrator–listener dyad and reported a specific limitation: Richardson and
Dale's coupling is lagged — the narrator leads the listener — and a one-step transition matrix does not see
a lagged dependency, so a low Φ at the recording grain means "not integrated at one step," not
"uncoordinated." That caveat has never been tested in silico, where the latency can be controlled exactly.

The open question is whether the limitation is a property of the latency itself or of how the latency is
represented. A delay can be modeled two ways: as an explicit buffer node that holds the mediator's last
output, or as unmodeled infrastructure, leaving only the parties and the mediator in the analyzed system.
The first keeps the delay inside the system the instrument reads; the second hides it. No probe has built
an integrated triad, delayed its feedback, and compared the one-step verdict under both representations.
That comparison turns the q204 caveat into a general statement about when exact Φ misses lagged integration
and how to recover it.
