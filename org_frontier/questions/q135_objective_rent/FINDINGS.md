## Reading

The agenda is a rentier equal to the system. In every form where the objective joins the core it takes the
same share the system takes — the two split the dominant value between them. What the human parties keep
follows whether the objective displaces one of them. Under the conjunctive and disjunctive objectives, which
push a party out of the core (Q128 core {S, C, O}), the parties hold negative value: their presence slightly
factors the system-plus-objective core. Under the parity objective, which keeps all four bound (core
{W, S, C, O}), the four split the value equally and the parties hold a fair quarter each. So an interested
system that learns from both parties does not merely re-integrate the coordination — it installs its own goal
as a co-owner, and whether the workers are paid or charged for the arrangement depends on whether that goal
displaces one of them. This is the value side of Q128's re-integration and Q129's displacement.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a four-node model; value read at the integrating state; the negative party shares are small and
sit at weakly-integrated forms where the value function is non-monotone, so they are read as "near or below
zero", not precise debts. The Φ-to-money bridge is open (Q122); "value/share/rent" name Shapley allocations of
Φ. A single objective node and the four canonical adaptations are tested.
