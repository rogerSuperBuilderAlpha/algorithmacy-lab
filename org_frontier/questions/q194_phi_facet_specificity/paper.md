# q194 — Facet specificity: a partition-restricted Φ for counterpart inference

The Algorithmacy Competence Scale separates three skills. Counterpart inference reconstructs a hidden
counterpart's wants from outcomes. Signal compression pours intent into the few signals a system
accepts. Rule-change tracking follows requirements that shift without announcement. The survey_bridge
map gives each facet a formal counterpart, and it singles out counterpart inference as the skill
against a counterpart hidden behind a committing third party. That phrasing names a partition: the
worker-counterpart relation, severed by a cut that the exact-Φ instrument can score on its own.

This study builds that partition-restricted reading and asks two things of it. First, whether the
whole-system Φ measure predicts counterpart inference more than it predicts a generic competence the
construct is meant to be distinct from. Second, whether the worker-counterpart component carries the
counterpart facet beyond what whole-system Φ already explains.

## The instrument

A worker's reported coordination maps to a small W-S-C Boolean form. The whole-system reading is the
maximum exact Φ_MIP over the form's reachable states. The worker-counterpart reading walks the same
states, enumerates every system partition that places W and C in different blocks, takes the least
informative such cut at each state, and maximizes over states. For the faithful mediated triad the
whole-system value is 2.0 and the worker-counterpart value is 1.0: the counterpart relation carries
half the global integration. Both readings come from the same PyPhi apparatus, so the component is a
restriction of the whole measure, not a separate heuristic.

Two reported conditions key the form. A commit gate decides whether the system commits a joint
determination or conveys one party's signal. A counterpart-coupling condition decides whether the
counterpart reads the worker directly or sits hidden behind the gate. The coupled form holds the
worker-counterpart reading at 1.0 while the whole-system reading falls to 1.0, so the component varies
where the whole measure does not. That decoupling is what makes the incremental test mean something.

## Results

On a simulated cohort of 300 workers, whole-system Φ correlates +0.80 with counterpart inference,
+0.83 with signal compression, and +0.83 with rule-change tracking. The worker-counterpart component
reorders them: +0.87 for counterpart inference, +0.78 and +0.77 for the other two. Counterpart
inference is the one facet the partition-restricted reading tracks more tightly than the whole.

H1 holds. The standardized slope of counterpart inference on Φ is +0.80; the slope of general
self-efficacy is −0.17. The difference in the Φ-to-construct correlation is +0.97 with a bootstrap CI
of [+0.86, +1.08], clear of zero. The measure tracks the targeted facet and not the discriminant
covariate.

H2 holds. Adding the worker-counterpart component to a model already holding whole-system Φ raises the
counterpart-inference R² from 0.64 to 0.76, an increment of +0.11 with a bootstrap CI of [+0.08,
+0.15]. The component carries variance the whole-system reading misses.

## Scope and limits

The cohort is simulated. The facet-to-structure loadings are written into the data generator from the
survey_bridge map, so the study demonstrates recovery, not confirmation: when the map holds, the
partition-restricted instrument reads the differential the map predicts. Whether the map holds is a
question for fielded waves. No worker is measured. What this study establishes is narrower and
useful: the W-C partition is computable from the same Φ apparatus, it is not redundant with the
whole-system measure, and it lines up with the facet the survey_bridge map assigns to it. The exact-Φ
lens supports a facet-level reading that a single whole-system number would blur.
