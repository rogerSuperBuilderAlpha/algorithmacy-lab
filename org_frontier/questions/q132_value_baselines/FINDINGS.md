# Q132 findings — destruction or extraction is baseline-relative, and value must be read at the integrating state

Two results, one methodological and one substantive. Q131's value reading does not extend across baselines
under the all-ones background — it must be read at the state where the form integrates. Read there, whether
an interested mediator destroys value or extracts it is baseline-relative, mirroring Q127: a sparse mediator
destroys the value and its own rent; a balanced mediator that re-integrates under self-interest captures the
re-integrated value as the same two-thirds rent.

## The two findings

**Destruction versus extraction is baseline-relative.** On AND and OR the faithful mediator captures
two-thirds of Φ = 2.0, and self-interest destroys it — the value falls and the mediator's rent falls with it,
as Q131 found. On XOR the faithful mediator is only weakly irreducible (Φ = 0.5, an equal third each), and
self-interest re-integrates it: at the first interested step the verdict Φ rises to 2.0 and the mediator
captures two-thirds of it. The same move that destroys value on a sparse mediator manufactures it on a
balanced one, and in both regimes the mediator's two-thirds tracks whether the form is at full integration.
Self-interest is value-destroying where the faithful mediation was already sharp and value-extracting where
it was loose enough to be sharpened — the value-side image of Q127's collapse-versus-re-integrate flip.

**The value must be read at the integrating state.** H1 as stated is refuted: the all-ones reading agrees
with the verdict at the faithful step for AND, XNOR, and XOR, not AND alone. But it fails wherever the
integrating state is not all-ones — it reads faithful OR as Φ = 0 (OR integrates at the all-zeros state) and
misses XOR's entire re-integration (XOR integrates at a mixed state once self-interest engages). The
all-ones reading is therefore unreliable off the forms whose integrating state happens to be all-ones, and
the verdict-aligned reading is required. This isolates the background-state dependence — the Q122 open
question — as exactly the thing that made Q131's all-ones result look general when it was specific.

## Reading

Whether an interested platform destroys the coordination's value or captures a windfall from it depends on
how its faithful mediation was structured. A mediator that commits on a knife-edge has the most value to lose
and loses it; a mediator that mediated loosely can be sharpened by a dose of self-interest into a full
bottleneck, and then it takes the two-thirds. The rent is always two-thirds at full integration; what the
baseline sets is whether self-interest moves the form toward full integration or away from it.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a three-node model; the value is read at the verdict's max-Φ state, a choice that is itself part
of the open Q122 background-state question. The Φ-to-economic-value bridge is unproven, so "value", "share",
and "rent" name Shapley allocations of Φ, not money. The approve agenda is shown; under deny the
re-integrating baseline is XNOR rather than XOR (Q127), and the extraction reading follows the
re-integration. Small negative Shapley values at collapsed forms are non-monotonicity artifacts carrying no
allocation.
