# q176 — Findings

The pass-through flip does not universally collapse the verdict, and the system's
commit-vs-convey bit is the single rule the Phi verdict is most sensitive to.

## H1 — the flip is not universal

Across the basis family, 15 worker-counterpart accounts read triadic under a committing
system. Switching the system to pure relay flipped 4 of them to dyadic and left 11
triadic. The flip rate is 0.267, far from the predicted 1.0. H1 is REFUTED and its NULL
holds: relaying does not guarantee a literacy pipe.

The accounts that stayed triadic share a structure: the worker reads off the counterpart
(or a coupling that includes it) rather than off the system. A worker-counterpart cycle
that bypasses the system keeps the whole structure irreducible even when the system only
relays. Pure relay collapses the verdict only when the system sits on the single path
between worker and counterpart.

## H2 — the commit-vs-convey bit dominates the CI

Holding two parties at consensus and splitting a coder panel across the third, the system
rule's commit / relay / store ambiguity drove the entire induced CI width in all five
accounts. The worker and counterpart ambiguities induced zero width: under a committing
system, no choice of worker or counterpart reading changed the verdict. The median system
share is 1.000, above the 0.5 threshold. H2 is SUPPORTED.

## Reading

The two results pull in one direction. The commit-vs-convey bit is what the
classifier-instrument tracks: it carries the whole sensitivity of the verdict to coder
disagreement (H2). Relay alone does not settle the verdict, because the worker and
counterpart can carry the irreducibility on their own when their rules close a cycle that
skips the system (H1). A coder who wants to certify a literacy pipe must code both the
system as a relay and the worker-counterpart pair as non-cyclic. Coding the system bit
alone is necessary but not sufficient.

## Scope

Synthetic coded rule sets with known structure. The empirical arms report results on
synthetic data. Whether a coded account matches an observed coordination is not tested
here.
