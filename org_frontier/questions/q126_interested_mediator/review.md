# Q126 — review of prior work

The question is the lab's own named gap. The computational literature watch records that no integrated-
information work treats the third party as adversarial or self-interested
([`research/computational`](../../research/computational/README.md)); the cognition watch's strategic-
visibility topic collects the game-theoretic side (algorithms that choose what to reveal to serve their own
objective). Every form in the corpus so far models a faithful mediator that commits the parties' joint
determination. Q126 is the first to give the mediator an agenda of its own.

It builds on two standing results. Probe 9's inference-displacement finding showed that when a worker acts
on its model of the counterpart, the model enters the core and the real counterpart is displaced — a
substitution, not a supplement. Q126 finds the mediator-side analogue: when the system acts on its own
agenda, it displaces the parties from the core. The principal results
([`principal/`](../../principal/)) showed an outside party joins the irreducible core only under
bidirectional coupling; Q126 shows the converse pressure — a mediator that stops reading the parties sheds
them, and the coordination factors.

What is new is the interestedness axis: not whether a system mediates, but how its self-interest changes the
irreducibility it creates. The result that Φ falls, and falls fastest under a denying agenda, has no prior
statement in the lab.
