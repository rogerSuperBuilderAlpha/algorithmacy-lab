# Q126 findings — self-interest disintegrates the coordination, and a gatekeeping agenda corrodes it fastest

Both hypotheses hold, and an asymmetry between agendas is the sharp result. A mediator that imposes its own
agenda over the parties' joint determination reads the parties less, sheds them from the irreducible core,
and drives the coordination to dyadic. Self-interest does not make the system a stronger autonomous player;
it erodes the very irreducibility the faithful mediator created. Φ falls, it does not rise.

## The rational self-interest path (override where the parties least warrant the agenda first)

| k | approve: structure / Φ / core / S reads | deny: structure / Φ / core / S reads |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | triadic / 2.000 / WSC / W,C | triadic / 2.000 / WSC / W,C |
| 1 | triadic / 0.500 / WSC / W,C | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither |
| 2 | dyadic / 0.000 / WS / W,C | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither |
| 3 | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither |
| 4 | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither | dyadic / 0.000 / (none) / neither |

A **denying** mediator collapses the bind at the very first override: overriding toward deny starts at the
parties' point of agreement (both warrant, AND's only commit-state), and removing it leaves the mediator
constant — the coordination factors immediately. An **approving** mediator tolerates one override (Φ drops
2.0 → 0.5, still triadic) before collapsing at k = 2. Gatekeeping self-interest corrodes faster than
permissive self-interest.

## Order-averaged decay (mean Φ over every choice of which k states are overridden)

| k | sets | mean Φ (approve) | mean Φ (deny) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 2.000 | 2.000 |
| 1 | 4 | 0.625 | 1.500 |
| 2 | 6 | 0.417 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 4 | 0.500 | 0.500 |
| 4 | 1 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Averaged over override orders, **deny decays linearly** (2.0, 1.5, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0): every state the
gatekeeper overrides removes a fixed share of the binding. **Approve is non-monotone** — it dips at k = 2
then rises at k = 3 — because some approve-override sets build parity-like mediators that re-integrate the
parties. Permissive self-interest has accidental re-integration pockets; gatekeeping self-interest is
cleanly corrosive.

| H | Result | Verdict |
|---|--------|---------|
| H1 | Φ falls to 0 along the rational path as interestedness rises | confirmed (both agendas) |
| H2 | the mediator sheds the parties as it serves its agenda | confirmed (reads end at neither) |

## Reading

The system's power to bind two parties into an irreducible joint determination is a property of *faithful*
mediation. When the system commits its own agenda instead, it stops reading the parties, they fall out of
the core, and what remained a triadic coordination factors into a dyadic one. The cross-disciplinary
conjecture — that an interested system becomes a stronger, more autonomous player with higher Φ — is not
borne out. Self-interest is disintegrating, and a denying agenda, which overrides the parties exactly where
they agree, is the most disintegrating of all.

## Limitations

Exact Φ on a three-node Boolean model; evidence about the construct and the instrument, not about a real
platform. "Agenda", "approve", "deny" label output values, not measured intent. The faithful baseline is
AND (commit iff both warrant); a different baseline (OR, "commit iff either") would relabel which states the
agendas override and is the natural robustness extension. The agenda here is a fixed stance baked into the
rule; an agenda that adapts to the parties (a learning self-interested system) is a separate model.
