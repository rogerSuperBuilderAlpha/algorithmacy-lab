# q150 — Adding one chord to a conjunctive ring: where the integration goes

A conjunctive ring integrates around one long cycle. Each of six nodes computes the AND of its two
ring neighbors, and the whole-system Φ sits at 4.0 with the major complex spanning all six nodes.
The question is what one long-range tie does to that integration. Add a chord between the opposite
pair A and D, so each endpoint also reads its partner, and the ring gains a shorter competing cycle.

Two effects were posited. The chord might raise Φ by giving the system a cheaper way to stay
integrated, and the minimum-information cut might move off the chord onto the longer arc. Separately,
the chord might pull captured value toward its endpoints, with A and D claiming a larger share of the
Shapley split.

The whole-system Φ does not move. Both the unchorded and chorded rings read 4.0 at the all-ones
state, and both keep the full six-node major complex. The minimum-information cut does move. The
unchorded ring splits into two halves, {ABC,DEF}, which severs A from D. The chorded ring cuts off
the short arc {BC} and keeps A and D together in {ADEF}. The cheapest cut now avoids the chord. That
matches half of the first hypothesis, but the Φ rise it was paired with does not appear, so the
hypothesis fails as stated. Shortening the integration cycle relocates the seam without buying more
integration here.

The Shapley split moves sharply. The unchorded ring divides its Φ evenly: every node holds 0.667, as
symmetry requires. The chord breaks that symmetry. The two endpoints rise to 1.100 each and the four
far-arc nodes fall to 0.450 each, with the total fixed at 4.0. The endpoint pair climbs from 1.334
to 2.200 while the far arc drops from 2.668 to 1.800. A single long-range tie redistributes captured
Φ toward its endpoints. Where the cut and the total are blind to the chord, the value split is not.

The contrast is the result worth keeping. Two summaries of the same six-node system disagree about
what the chord did. The whole-system Φ and the major complex register no change; the cut and the
Shapley split both register the chord, the cut by relocating, the split by concentrating value on the
endpoints. The IIT read separates "how integrated is the whole" from "which parts carry the
integration," and one long-range tie moves the second without moving the first.

## Scope and validation gap

In-silico. Both forms are synthetic Boolean coordination models, not measured organizations. The
study shows how an exact-Φ read of a six-node ring responds to one chord. It does not establish that
any real coordination behaves this way, and no worker is measured. The bridge from coded field rules
to this kind of Φ read is the open step; this study sets a baseline on a clean topology that such a
bridge can be checked against.
