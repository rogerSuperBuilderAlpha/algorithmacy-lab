# Q129 — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

The interested-mediator arc runs from a faithful mediator (S' = W ∧ C, the coordination irreducible) to a
predatory one (S' = O). Q126 found a fixed-agenda predatory mediator erodes the coordination; Q128 found an
adaptive objective makes the four-node system irreducible again. Q129 interpolates between the endpoints —
the mediator serves its objective on m of the four input states and commits faithfully on the rest, m = 0..4
— and asks where the coordination survives, with the objective frozen (O' = O) and adaptive (O' = W ∧ C).

- **H1.** With a frozen objective, the coordination collapses as the mediator turns predatory (m rises),
  reaching its end by full predation.
- **H2.** With an adaptive objective, the coordination stays intact across the whole interpolation,
  including the fully predatory end — adaptation holds the phase boundary open.
- **Null.** The objective's adaptation makes no difference to where the coordination collapses.

These were written assuming a single notion of "the coordination survives." As recorded in
[`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md), that assumption is what broke: two reasonable readings — the parties bound in
the irreducible core, and the whole system irreducible — diverge, and give opposite answers about which
objective preserves the coordination. The finding is the divergence and the party-displacement behind it.
