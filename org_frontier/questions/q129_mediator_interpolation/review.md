# Q129 — review of prior work

Q129 closes the interested-mediator arc by interpolating between its endpoints and reconciling its two
results. Q126 ([`q126_interested_mediator`](../q126_interested_mediator/paper.md)) found a fixed-agenda
predatory mediator erodes the coordination; Q128
([`q128_adaptive_mediator`](../q128_adaptive_mediator/paper.md)) found an adaptive objective makes the
four-node system irreducible again. Read side by side these look like a clean rescue, but they used different
measures — Q126 the parties' bind, Q128 whole-system irreducibility. Q129 runs both on the same
interpolation and finds they diverge.

The displacement it surfaces is the same phenomenon the lab has met before from the other side. Probe 9's
inference-displacement result showed a worker acting on its model of the counterpart displaces the real
counterpart from the core; Q125's majority clique showed a party outside the core can still be pivotal. Q129
adds the mediator-objective case: a coupled objective enters the core and pushes a party out. The
spectator-robustness convention (Q75, probe 239) — read the verdict on the major complex when a form has
idle nodes — is exactly what makes the two readings separable here, since the frozen objective is a
disconnected spectator while the adaptive one is not.

What is new is the explicit divergence: "does interested mediation preserve the coordination" has no single
answer, and the two answers it has point in opposite directions depending on whether the objective is allowed
to join the thing it mediates.
