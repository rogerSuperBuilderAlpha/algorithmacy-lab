# The rotating chair: shared leadership keeps the core and dissolves the veto

## Abstract
Every mediator in the lab's catalog is a fixed seat, and a fixed conjunctive mediator is the coordination's
veto player. We ask what happens when the mediator role rotates on a clock among the parties. Under exact
IIT-4.0 on designed conjunctive Boolean forms, a full rotation preserves the peak party-core Φ of a fixed hub
(Φ = 2 at three parties, Φ = n−1 = 3 at four, read at the highest-Φ state) and removes any permanent veto
player: no party lies in every integrating coalition across states, although at each moment a two-party veto
set exists. Two-seat rotation leaves both seats as veto players. The party core does not persist across
states (it is the maximal complex in 3 of 12 states at three parties; clock-only complexes win elsewhere).
Rotation preserves peak integration and makes the veto rotate rather than vanish.

## Question
Does a rotating chair keep a coordination irreducible, and who holds its bottleneck?

## Method
Parties copy the current chair; the chair's next state is the conjunction of the other parties. A mod-k clock
(one or two bits) selects the chair. We compare a fixed chair with two-seat alternation and full rotation at
three parties, and fixed versus full rotation at four. Measures: whole-system verdict (`classify_rules`),
major complex and its Φ (`major_complex`), integrating coalitions and the veto set
(`threads/veto_player`). Five hypotheses were committed before probe 453 and a sixth before probe 454.

## Results
See `FINDINGS.md`. Peak core membership and peak core Φ (at the highest-Φ state) are invariant to rotation. The
cross-state veto set is {A} for a fixed chair, {A, B} for A/B alternation, and empty for full rotation at three
and four parties; per state, full rotation has a two-party veto set (AB, BC, or AC). All whole-system verdicts
read dyadic (max Φ 0 vs 0). Clock nodes often form the maximal complex at non-peak states.

## Discussion
The lab's political-economy results locate the mediator's rent and veto in the fixed seat (Q111–Q112, the
veto-player thread). Rotation suggests the permanent bottleneck belongs to the seat, not to the structure: the
same peak irreducible core is reached when no party permanently holds the seat, while at any moment a
(rotating) pair still holds the veto. As a prior for real coordination,
rotating chairs, on-call rotations, and term-limited moderators should read as irreducible with no single
indispensable party, while a partial rotation should read as a shared veto among the rotating seats.

## Limits
Readings are aggregated over states or taken at the peak-Φ state; they do not establish continuous core
membership or moment-by-moment veto freedom. Designed deterministic forms, conjunctive chairs only, copy-the-chair followers, a deterministic external
clock, at most four parties. In-silico evidence about the models, not about real organizations. Open next:
non-conjunctive chairs, noisy or endogenous schedules (the chair elected by state), and a clock read by the parties.
