# The rotating chair: shared leadership keeps the core and dissolves the veto

## Abstract
Every mediator in the lab's catalog is a fixed seat, and a fixed conjunctive mediator is the coordination's
veto player. We ask what happens when the mediator role rotates on a clock among the parties. Under exact
IIT-4.0 on designed Boolean forms, a full rotation keeps the same major complex and core Φ as a fixed hub
(Φ = 2 at three parties, Φ = n−1 = 3 at four) and leaves no party in every integrating coalition. Partial
rotation spreads the veto to every seat the chair visits. The clock itself never joins the core. Rotation is
integration-neutral and veto-dissolving.

## Question
Does a rotating chair keep a coordination irreducible, and who holds its bottleneck?

## Method
Parties copy the current chair; the chair's next state is the conjunction of the other parties. A mod-k clock
(one or two bits) selects the chair. We compare a fixed chair with two-seat alternation and full rotation at
three parties, and fixed versus full rotation at four. Measures: whole-system verdict (`classify_rules`),
major complex and its Φ (`major_complex`), integrating coalitions and the veto set
(`threads/veto_player`). Five hypotheses were committed before probe 453 and a sixth before probe 454.

## Results
See `FINDINGS.md`. Core membership and core Φ are invariant to rotation. The veto set is {A} for a fixed
chair, {A, B} for A/B alternation, and empty for full rotation at both three and four parties. All whole-system
verdicts read dyadic because the clock runs outside the core.

## Discussion
The lab's political-economy results locate the mediator's rent and veto in the fixed seat (Q111–Q112, the
veto-player thread). Rotation shows that the bottleneck belongs to the seat, not to the structure: the same
irreducible whole survives when no party permanently holds the seat. As a prior for real coordination,
rotating chairs, on-call rotations, and term-limited moderators should read as irreducible with no single
indispensable party, while a partial rotation should read as a shared veto among the rotating seats.

## Limits
Designed deterministic forms, conjunctive chairs only, copy-the-chair followers, a deterministic external
clock, at most four parties. In-silico evidence about the models, not about real organizations. Open next:
non-conjunctive chairs, noisy or endogenous schedules (the chair elected by state), and a clock read by the parties.
