# Q210 — Do two triads sharing a counterpart merge into one irreducible core, and at what bridge coupling? · Stage 1 review

**Question.** Two conjunctive triads share one counterpart node. Does the major complex span both triads as
a single irreducible core, or stay two separate cores, and how does that depend on how strongly the shared
counterpart bridges the two mediators?

**Agenda id.** Agenda #16, extending the multi-role result (#73) to two full triads.

## Prior probes that bear on this

| probe | finding | how it relates |
|---|---|---|
| #73 (multi-role) | A node holding two roles can pull two cores together or keep them apart | The single-node bridge this extends from a role to a full second triad |
| synthesis | The conjunctive triad W'=S, S'=W∧C, C'=S is triadic, Φ_MIP=2.0 | The single-triad reference the merged form is built from |
| #119 (multiparty pool) | Adding parties raises Φ toward an all-to-all pool | The upper reference for what a fully merged multi-party core can reach |

## The gap

The lab's results are mostly about one triad. Real coordination links many. A platform that mediates a
worker and a counterpart in one market often mediates the same counterpart in a second, so two triads share
a member. #73 showed that a single node carrying two roles can either bind two cores into one or leave them
separate, but it studied a shared role, not two complete triads each with its own mediator. Whether two
full triads sharing one counterpart merge into a single irreducible whole, and what bridge strength that
takes, is unasked. This builds two conjunctive triads on a shared counterpart, sweeps how the counterpart
combines the two mediators (no bridge, an AND bridge that needs both, an OR bridge that needs either), and
reads at each setting whether the major complex is one core or two and what Φ it carries.
