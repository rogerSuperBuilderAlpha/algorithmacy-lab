# q180 — Hypotheses

A coded account names parties and counts them. One system can be coded as a single node or split
into a matcher and a pricer. Two roles can be merged into one. That individuation choice is the
coder's, and it precedes any Phi computation. The question is whether it moves the verdict.

Both hypotheses are fixed before the numbers are computed.

## H1 — individuation moves the verdict

Splitting a single coded party into two sub-nodes changes max_phi enough to flip the
dyadic/triadic verdict for more than 20 percent of synthetic accounts.

H1-null: fewer than 5 percent of accounts flip under any split or merge, so where the coder draws
the party boundary is verdict-neutral.

## H2 — the CI tells benign individuation from load-bearing individuation

Verdicts are stable under splits that preserve the party's joint input-output function
(re-aggregable splits) and flip under splits that change that function (function-changing splits).
The rule_to_phi confidence interval separates the two: the Phi CI crosses 0 (the account could be
dyadic) for function-changing splits in more than 90 percent of cases and stays above 0 for
re-aggregable splits.

H2-null: re-aggregable and function-changing splits give indistinguishable CIs, so the module
cannot tell a benign re-coding from one that changes what the party computes.

## Scope

All accounts are synthetic coded rule sets. No worker is measured. The hypotheses concern the
behavior of the coding operator on the Phi instrument, on synthetic data.
