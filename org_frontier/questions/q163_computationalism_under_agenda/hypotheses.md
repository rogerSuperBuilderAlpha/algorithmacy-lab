# q163 — hypotheses

The computationalism battery draws two objects out of one triad. A channel relays the worker:
S = f(W), and its whole-system Φ is 0. An actor commits the joint determination of the two parties:
S = W ∧ C, and its Φ is 2.0. The mediator in both is faithful. Q126 made the mediator interested:
it imposes an agenda on the k input states where the parties least warrant it and commits the faithful
gate elsewhere. The question here is whether an interested mediator is a third kind of object the
channel/actor dichotomy cannot place.

## H1

On the Q126 ladder the actor surplus Φ(actor) − Φ(channel) is non-monotone in interestedness k: it
rises then collapses to 0. An interested mediator is then a distinct third object, an actor that reads
its agenda but not the parties, which battery_computationalism's channel/actor dichotomy cannot place.

Null: the surplus is monotone in k and collapses straight to 0, so interest only degrades a channel.

## H2

At every k > 0 the parties_read_by_S flip-test shows S's rule sheds at least one party before
whole-system Φ reaches 0. Reading-its-agenda and reading-the-parties then dissociate.

Null: S keeps reading both W and C until Φ hits 0, so agenda-reading never substitutes for
party-reading.

## Scope

Exact Φ on a 3-node Boolean model. The evidence concerns the instrument and the construct. "Agenda",
"approve", "deny", and "interest" name output values and rule structure, not measured intent. The
empirical arm runs on synthetic forms.
