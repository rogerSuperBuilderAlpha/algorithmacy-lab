# Causal emergence: what kind of evidence, and does it converge

A quantitative archival review, in the systematicity + knowledge-weaving style of the reviews arm.

**Question.** Is the causal-emergence / downward-causation literature conceptual, formal, or
empirical, and does it converge on whether macro-scale causal emergence is real?

**Why it matters to the lab.** Causal emergence is the nearest formal neighbor to the lab's move —
reading a coordination form as a macro-scale whole that may do causal work its parts do not, with an
explicit measure (Φ) as the lens. If that neighbor literature is mostly argument and toy models, with
real-system demonstrations rare and its central claim still contested, the lab is entering an open,
unsettled area rather than a closed one.

**Hypotheses** (pre-registered in `hypotheses.md`, committed before coding):
- **H1** — conceptual and formal-model work dominates; empirical demonstrations on real systems are
  rare.
- **H2** — the reality claim is contested; sources split between emergence-is-real and deflationary
  readings, with no convergence.
- **H3** — formal approaches fragment (information-theoretic vs dynamical vs statistical) with limited
  cross-citation.

**Layout.** `hypotheses.md` (pre-registration), `coding_protocol.md` (codebook), `methods.md` (corpus
boundary + procedure), `literature/corpus.jsonl` (the coded corpus), `coding/coder{A,B,C}.jsonl`
(three blind passes), `seeds.json` / `clusters.json` / `edges/` (citation graph), `results/`
(adjudicated dataset + matrices), `FINDINGS.md` (verdicts + κ), `paper.md` (the write-up).
