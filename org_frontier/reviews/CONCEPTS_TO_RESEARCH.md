# Concepts to research — a cross-cutting review of the ten literature experiments

The ten reviews were each pre-registered and run in isolation: one question about one field, tested
against one coded corpus. Read side by side, they repeat. The same structural shapes turn up in five or
six unrelated literatures, and several of those shapes already have names in scholarship the reviews
never cite. This document lifts out the concepts that recur but were never theorized, the adjacent
literatures that bear on a finding but went unengaged, and the places where a review result speaks
directly to the lab's own Φ-on-coordination program. Each entry gives the connection, why it was
missed, a deep-research question worth a full web/literature pass, and seed works. Concepts are ranked
by how much a deep-research pass would strengthen the body of work. Weak candidates are marked and
ranked low rather than dropped, so the ranking is honest about its own tail.

---

## 1. The assertion–measurement gap as a cross-field regularity

**Connects:** algorithmic_management_claims (0 of 22 control-outcome sources quantitative; the control
claim is a stylized fact asserted by 12 and assumed by 28 more), phi_measure_fragmentation (81% of
measures validated internally or not at all; 19% against a ground truth), causal_emergence_evidence (4
of 60 sources measure an emergence quantity on real data; the reality claim is argued and modeled, not
measured), iit_beyond_consciousness (H3: Φ almost never computed on real organizational data),
collective_intelligence_substrates (the only cross-substrate synthesis is conceptual), and the lab's
own stated "validation gap" plus its playbook rule "measure, do not assert."

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** each review found this pattern locally and named it locally
("asserted, never measured," "argued not measured," "internal not ground-truth"). No document names it
as one regularity that holds across five substantively unrelated fields — a field's signature claim
tends to be its least-tested one, carried by conceptual and qualitative work while the quantitative
apparatus points somewhere adjacent and safer. This is the single most repeated finding in the arm, and
it is the arm's strongest bridge to the lab's core: the lab's entire method is the answer to it
(compute the quantity the field only asserts), and the lab's honest limitation (in-silico, validation
gap) is the same gap turned on itself. Lifting it out gives the dissertation a general claim about how
young measurement literatures behave and positions exact-Φ computation as the disciplined response.
There is also a ready adjacent vocabulary the reviews never touch: construct validity's *nomological
network* (a claim earns its status by its place in a web of tested relations), the epistemics of
*stylized facts*, and evidential-hierarchy language.

**Deep-research question:** Across the social and complexity sciences, is there established theory for
why a field's central or "stylized-fact" claim is systematically less tested than its peripheral
claims — and what vocabulary (nomological network, evidential status, stylized facts, the
assertion-to-evidence pipeline) best frames a measurement program that sets out to close such a gap?

**Seeds:** Cronbach & Meehl (1955), "Construct validity in psychological tests"; Daniel Hirschman
(2016), "Stylized facts in the social sciences," *Sociological Science*; Suddaby (2010) on construct
clarity; search "evidentiary status of theoretical claims," "assertion vs. test organization theory,"
"nomological network construct validation."

## 2. Irreducibility as one under-theorized construct measured six ways

**Connects:** phi_measure_fragmentation (six measure families — integrated-information decomposition /
synergy, causal emergence / effective information, practical Φ proxies, total correlation, exact IIT Φ,
geometric/TSE complexity — none holding a majority), causal_emergence_evidence (three formalisms:
information-theoretic, dynamical, statistical), collective_intelligence_substrates (the field's one
question is "does a group compute something no member holds" — synergy by another name),
iit_beyond_consciousness, and the lab's core thesis (algorithmacy = triadic, *irreducible* joint
determination read off exact Φ over the MIP).

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** "irreducibility" appears in four reviews, but no document notices
that integrated information, causal emergence, PID/synergy, collective-intelligence "the whole computes
what no part holds," and organizational non-decomposability are all formalizations of the *same*
underlying idea — a whole whose behavior does not factor into its parts — developed by communities that
the fragmentation findings show do not cite each other. This is the deepest connection between the
reviews arm and the lab thesis. The lab commits to one member of this family (exact Φ). An affirmative
case is far stronger if it can say why irreducibility is the real cross-domain construct, where exact Φ
sits among its rivals, and why the family's failure to converge (finding 1's phi_measure result) is an
opening rather than an embarrassment. This is the argument that makes Paper 2's "Φ is a great tool"
principled rather than asserted.

**Deep-research question:** Is there a unifying formal or philosophical account of irreducibility /
non-decomposability that spans integrated information, causal emergence, partial-information
decomposition (synergy), and collective intelligence — and on what grounds would one prefer exact IIT-Φ
as the operationalization for organizational coordination over effective information or a synergy
measure?

**Seeds:** Williams & Beer (2010), partial information decomposition; Rosas et al. on integrated
information decomposition (ΦID); Hoel, Albantakis & Tononi on causal emergence / effective information;
Herbert Simon (1962), "The architecture of complexity" (near-decomposability); Woolley et al. (2010) on
the collective-intelligence factor; search "synergy information decomposition," "emergence
irreducibility formal," "non-decomposability complex systems."

## 3. Integration by shared method versus shared object

**Connects:** iit_beyond_consciousness (H4 *challenged* — the beyond-consciousness literature is
substrate-diverse yet citation-*connected*, held together by shared Φ machinery and PyPhi tooling; "one
conversation with several application targets"), collective_intelligence_substrates (H1 *supported* —
genuinely fragmented, 6 of ~2,053 external citers span two substrates), new_form_construct_proliferation
(hub-and-spoke: a meta-organization core integrates a few labels, the periphery stays siloed),
phi_measure_fragmentation and causal_emergence_evidence (formal apparatus fragments).

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** two of these reviews predicted isolation and found it; one
predicted isolation and found method-bound *connection* instead. That contrast is the concept: a
scattered literature can be held together by a shared object of study, by a shared formal method, or by
nothing. IIT-beyond-consciousness is held together by method; collective intelligence is held together
by nothing. The reviews report the contrast but never name the axis. It matters to the lab directly:
the H4 result tells the lab exactly what kind of literature its bridge would join — a method-bound
conversation an organizational application enters *through its method*, inheriting that conversation's
live disputes. There is a mature adjacent vocabulary the arm never cites: consilience, trading zones,
boundary objects, and interdisciplinary-integration studies all theorize what makes a multi-substrate
field cohere or splinter.

**Deep-research question:** What determines whether a literature spanning many substrates coheres —
shared method, shared object, a boundary-spanning hub, or a trading zone — and which mechanism does an
imported formal apparatus like IIT-Φ supply when it enters organization theory?

**Seeds:** E. O. Wilson (1998), *Consilience*; Peter Galison (1997), *Image and Logic* (trading zones);
Star & Griesemer (1989), boundary objects; Comolatti & Hoel on a "consilience" of emergence measures
(already cited inside causal_emergence_evidence — a thread to pull); search "interdisciplinary
integration bibliometrics," "method-bound vs object-bound field."

## 4. Construct proliferation, jingle–jangle, and construct clarity — turned on "algorithmacy" itself

**Connects:** new_form_construct_proliferation (one object, ~18 names; the jangle fallacy at subfield
scale — already engaged, but only via the classical psychometric statement),
phi_measure_fragmentation (six competing measure families for one quantity — jangle in the measurement
register), and, reflexively, the lab's own coinage of "algorithmacy" as a *new* construct entering the
new-form space the review just measured.

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** new_form_construct_proliferation names the jangle fallacy (Kelley,
Thorndike) but stops at the classical statement. It does not engage the modern organizational
construct-clarity program (Suddaby's four-part test for a clear construct; concept-goodness criteria;
the identity/proliferation and "bloated construct" literatures), and it never turns the lens on the
lab's own new label. The review's own discussion says the work worth doing is "to state a coordinating
mechanism precise enough to distinguish the form from its incumbents and to cite across the camps," and
that "any measure that claims to grade a form's irreducibility across these camps" earns its place by
the cross-label comparison it enables. That is a pre-registered test the lab must pass for algorithmacy.
Deep research on construct clarity gives the dissertation the defensive apparatus to show algorithmacy
is a distinct, well-formed construct rather than an eighteenth name — and to show what a construct
*measured* by Φ adds that the eighteen unmeasured labels do not.

**Deep-research question:** By the established criteria for construct clarity and concept goodness, what
distinguishes a legitimate new construct from a jangle-fallacy duplicate — and which criteria does
"algorithmacy," defined as Φ-graded irreducible coordination, satisfy that the existing new-form labels
do not?

**Seeds:** Suddaby (2010), "Construct clarity in theories of management and organization," *AMR*; Kelley
(1927) and Thorndike (1904) on jingle–jangle; John Gerring (1999), "What makes a concept good?";
search "construct proliferation organization theory," "jingle jangle fallacy management," "bloated
construct."

## 5. Disciplined theory- and model-borrowing (apply / extend / analogy)

**Connects:** platform_theory_borrowing (the whole study — parent theory, borrowing mode, the finding
that 70% of sources frame their import as an *extension* and only 29% as plain application),
iit_beyond_consciousness (H2 — Φ imported into new substrates with no bridge to coordination theory),
and the lab itself, which imports a formal apparatus (exact IIT-4.0 Φ) from neuroscience into
organization theory — the CLAUDE.md names this lineage (ecology from population biology, TCE from
microeconomics).

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** platform_theory_borrowing discusses "borrowing well" and names the
disciplinary lineage informally, but it engages none of the actual scholarship on theory borrowing,
analogical theorizing, and model transfer — the literature that distinguishes borrowing from blending,
application from extension, and productive analogy from superficial isomorphism. That scholarship is
exactly what the lab needs to defend its own import. The IIT-beyond-consciousness review shows the
import is currently method-only and disconnected from coordination theory; the platform review shows
what mature-versus-immature borrowing looks like. Together they give the lab an evidence base for
framing the Φ import as disciplined rather than decorative — an affirmative move the thesis requires.

**Deep-research question:** What separates a disciplined cross-disciplinary model import (biology →
organizational ecology, microeconomics → transaction-cost theory) from a superficial analogy that
borrows vocabulary without transferring explanatory structure — and by those criteria, what must the
IIT-Φ-into-coordination import demonstrate to count as the former?

**Seeds:** Oswick, Fleming & Hanlon (2011), "From borrowing to blending," *AMR*; Boxenbaum & Rouleau
(2011), "New knowledge products as bricolage," *AMR*; Whetten, Felin & King (2009), "The practice of
theory borrowing in organizational studies," *Journal of Management*; search "analogical theorizing
organization," "model transfer across disciplines."

## 6. Metascience / science-of-science as the arm's own genre

**Connects:** reproducibility_signaling (an open-science-practices audit — a metascience study in all
but name), systematicity_consciousness_reviews (measuring method quality across a field's reviews), and
the arm as a whole ("experiments on the literature," a body of scholarship treated as a dataset).

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** the arm builds its method from two organizational-research-methods
papers (Simsek et al.) and never connects to the much larger metascience / science-of-science / research-on-research
movement that does exactly this at scale — quantifying the reliability, structure, and reform of
scientific fields. reproducibility_signaling in particular measures the credibility revolution's own
markers (open data, code, pre-registration) without citing the credibility-revolution literature that
defined them. Locating the ten reviews inside metascience raises the arm's standing from "a local
homage to Simsek" to "an instance of a recognized research program," and it supplies external
benchmarks (baseline reproducibility-signaling rates, science-of-science network methods) that the
reviews currently lack.

**Deep-research question:** How does the science-of-science / metascience literature quantify a field's
maturity, fragmentation, and evidential health — and what published baselines exist for
reproducibility-practice signaling that the reproducibility_signaling 1.1% abstract rate should be read
against?

**Seeds:** Fortunato et al. (2018), "Science of science," *Science*; Ioannidis (2005), "Why most
published research findings are false"; Munafò et al. (2017), "A manifesto for reproducible science,"
*Nature Human Behaviour*; Nosek et al. on the credibility revolution / open-science practices; search
"metascience meta-research," "reproducibility practice adoption rates management."

## 7. LLM-as-coder construct validity — the arm's load-bearing limitation

**Connects:** all ten reviews (every FINDINGS.md carries the identical limitation: three coders are LLM
agents on a fixed codebook, not trained human raters; high Fleiss' κ measures agreement among agents,
which shared model priors could inflate).

**Why overlooked, why it matters:** the arm states the limitation ten times and never researches it.
Whether agent-coder agreement is a valid substitute for human intercoder reliability is now an active
empirical question in computational social science, with published validation studies. The arm's entire
evidentiary base rests on this substitution, so a deep-research pass that surfaces the known agreement
rates, failure modes, and validation designs would let the arm defend its κ figures with citation
rather than a bare caveat — and would tell it exactly which of its variables (the low-κ, interpretive
ones like claim_type and borrowing_mode) are most exposed. This is methodological hardening, not
thesis-advancing, which is why it ranks here and not higher.

**Deep-research question:** How well does LLM-agent content coding reproduce trained-human intercoder
reliability and validity in systematic review and content analysis, where does it fail, and what
validation design (human-subset gold standard, adjudication protocol) would certify the arm's
agent-coded corpora?

**Seeds:** Gilardi, Alizadeh & Kubli (2023), "ChatGPT outperforms crowd workers for text-annotation
tasks," *PNAS*; Ziems et al. (2024), "Can large language models transform computational social
science?"; search "LLM annotation validity intercoder reliability," "GPT content analysis agreement
human."

## 8. Gap-spotting versus problematization for the entrant — mostly already engaged (low)

**Connects:** gig_work_motivation (90% gap-spotting; problematization is rarer and, fragile evidence
suggests, more cited), new_form_construct_proliferation (coining an 18th name is the gap-spotter's
move; integrating across camps is the problematizer's), and the lab's own rhetorical choice.

**Why low:** gig_work_motivation already engages the primary source (Sandberg & Alvesson 2011) head-on,
so the adjacent literature is not missing. The cross-cutting reading — that the lab, as an entrant,
faces the same gap-spot-or-problematize choice, and that its affirmative thesis is a problematization
(the existing constructs do not reach the mediated triad) — is worth stating once in the dissertation's
framing, but it does not need a deep-research pass. Fold it into the Paper 1 positioning rather than
researching it fresh.

**Deep-research question (if pursued):** Does problematization-based motivation earn higher impact than
gap-spotting once field and age are controlled, across management subfields beyond gig work?

**Seeds:** Sandberg & Alvesson (2011), "Ways of constructing research questions: gap-spotting or
problematization?" *Organization*; Alvesson & Sandberg (2011), "Generating research questions through
problematization," *AMR*.

## Where to start — the top four

1. **The assertion–measurement gap (concept 1).** It is the arm's most-repeated empirical finding and
   its cleanest bridge to the lab's core method and its honest validation gap. Highest leverage per
   hour.
2. **Irreducibility as one construct measured six ways (concept 2).** This is the argument that makes
   Paper 2's affirmative case for Φ principled — why exact Φ, among a fragmented family of
   irreducibility measures, is the right lens on coordination. Directly thesis-advancing.
3. **Construct proliferation and clarity, turned on algorithmacy (concept 4).** The new_form review is a
   loaded gun pointed at the lab's own coinage; deep research here is the defense that lets algorithmacy
   survive its own finding.
4. **Disciplined theory- and model-borrowing (concept 5).** The lab's Φ import needs the borrowing
   scholarship to frame itself as disciplined transfer rather than analogy — the affirmative counter to
   the "decorative apparatus" objection.

Concepts 3 (integration by method vs object) and 6 (metascience framing) are strong second-tier passes
that sharpen positioning; 7 (LLM-coder validity) is worth one focused pass to harden the arm's
foundation; 8 is already covered and needs only a sentence in Paper 1.
