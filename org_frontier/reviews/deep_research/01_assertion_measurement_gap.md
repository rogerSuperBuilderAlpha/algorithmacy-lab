# The assertion–measurement gap as a cross-field regularity — deep-research dossier

*Deep-research pass (fan-out search → adversarial verification → synthesis) on concept 1 of [`../CONCEPTS_TO_RESEARCH.md`](../CONCEPTS_TO_RESEARCH.md). Question: is there established theory for why a field's central / stylized-fact claim is systematically less tested than its peripheral apparatus, and what vocabulary best frames a disciplined measurement response? 100 agents; claims carry a vote and sources.*

## Bottom line

Yes: the social and complexity sciences already carry an established, cross-cutting vocabulary for why a field's central claim stays less tested than its peripheral apparatus, and that vocabulary directly frames a disciplined measurement response. Cronbach & Meehl's nomological network (1955) supplies the criterion: a construct is scientifically admissible only if it sits in a net some of whose laws touch observables, and construct validity is the mode of validation invoked precisely when no operational criterion exists — the situation of a conceptually-carried signature claim. Hirschman's epistemics of stylized facts (2016) supplies the mechanism: a stylized fact is a "simple empirical regularity in need of explanation," a claim of non-robust dependence that is deliberately "stylized" — abstracted away from measurement accuracy and left as a call to others to explain — so the central regularity is insulated from precise testing by design. Suddaby's construct clarity (2010) supplies the diagnostic grid (definitions, scope conditions, semantic relationships, coherence) for grading whether a measure actually operationalizes the construct it names, exemplified by Marzo's finding that the VAIC apparatus captures labour and physical-capital efficiency — adjacent and safer — rather than the intellectual-capital construct it advertises. The recommended framing for the dissertation: cast the exact IIT-4.0 Phi computation as extending the nomological net of a stylized signature claim toward observables, closing an assertion-to-evidence gap that construct-validity theory says is where validation is required, not optional.

## Verified findings

### 1. The nomological network (Cronbach & Meehl 1955) is the master concept for the gap: a construct's scientific meaning is constituted by the interlocking system of laws in which it occurs, and to validate a measure is to locate the construct within that net. A construct is scientifically admissible only if it occurs in a nomological net at least some of whose laws involve observables — so a purely conceptual, conceptually-carried claim that never contacts observation fails the necessary condition for construct validity. Construct validation is required precisely when no criterion or content universe adequately defines the quality to be measured; where criteria are absent, tests are 'left unvalidated, or a finespun network of rationalizations has been offered as if it were validation. Rationalization is not construct validation.'
*confidence **high** · vote merged 6 claims, all 3-0*

Merges verified claims [0][1][2][3][4][5], all unanimous (3-0) against the primary 1955 text confirmed verbatim at two independent full-text mirrors (York psychclassics and Meehl's own umn.edu archive). Verbatim: 'A necessary condition for a construct to be scientifically admissible is that it occur in a nomological net, at least some of whose laws involve observables'; 'to "make clear what something is" means to set forth the laws in which it occurs. We shall refer to the interlocking system of laws which constitute a theory as a nomological network'; 'Construct validity must be investigated whenever no criterion or universe of content is accepted as entirely adequate to define the quality to be measured'; 'Rationalization is not construct validation.' Canonical, uncontested definitional source; later critiques (Messick, Borsboom) target the framework's utility, not this historical characterization. This is the exact concept to adopt as the frame: a Phi computation extends the nomological net of the signature claim toward observables.

### 2. Hirschman's epistemics of stylized facts (2016, Sociological Science) supplies the mechanism and the naming vocabulary. A stylized fact is a 'simple empirical regularity in need of explanation' — an empirical regularity in search of a theoretical, causal explanation — that occupies the middle ground between description and theory and is simultaneously a positive claim (about what is) and a normative one (about what merits scholarly attention). Stylized facts are claims of 'non-robust dependence,' explicitly agnostic as to WHY, functioning as 'calls to other social scientists to hunt for causal explanations,' so the signature claim is asserted and left for others rather than itself rigorously established. The 'stylizing' operation, per Kaldor's originating usage, deliberately abstracts away from measurement accuracy — the theorist may 'concentrate on broad tendencies, ignoring individual detail... without necessarily committing himself on the historical accuracy, or sufficiency, of the facts' — which explains why a field's central claim is systematically less measured than its adjacent apparatus: it is stylized on purpose and thereby insulated from precise testing.
*confidence **high** · vote merged 5 claims, all 3-0*

Merges verified claims [6][7][8][11][12], all unanimous (3-0) against the primary peer-reviewed PDF (Sociological Science v3:604-626, 2016), extracted via pdftotext. Verbatim: 'stylized facts should be understood as simple empirical regularities in need of explanation'; 'both positive claims (about what is in the world) and normative claims (about what merits scholarly attention)'; 'a stylized fact is a claim that X is associated with Y, but the claim is explicitly agnostic as to why... calls to other social scientists to hunt for causal explanations'; Kaldor 1961:178 reproduced verbatim on measurement-insulation. Caveat noted by verifiers: Hirschman frames measurement-insulation as one of several features (alongside attention-allocation and contestability), so it should not be presented as the whole of what stylizing does; and the slide from 'unexplained' to 'not rigorously established' is a defensible framing move for causal signature claims (e.g. 'algorithm controls workers'), not Hirschman's exact wording. This is the vocabulary to name the field's central-but-untested claim.
- https://sociologicalscience.com/download/vol-3/july/SocSci_v3_604to626.pdf

### 3. Suddaby's construct clarity (2010, AMR 35(3):346-357) supplies the four-element diagnostic for grading a measure against its named construct: (1) definitions that capture the essential (necessary-and-sufficient) properties of the phenomenon itself, are parsimonious, and avoid tautology/circularity; (2) scope conditions restricting applicability; (3) semantic relationships locating the construct against neighboring constructs; and (4) coherence (logical consistency). Lack of construct clarity hampers empirical development and validation, not only theory, establishing a definition-before-measurement dependency a measurement program can invoke to justify closing an assertion-measurement gap.
*confidence **high** · vote merged 7 claims, all 3-0*

Merges verified claims [9][10][13][14][15][19][20], all unanimous (3-0). Primary AMR editorial confirmed verbatim: 'Construct clarity is enhanced by the interrelationship of four elements: definitions, scope conditions, semantic relationship, and coherence'; definitions must 'effectively capture the essential properties and characteristics of the concept or phenomenon' and 'avoid tautology or circularity' and be parsimonious. Corroborated by a peer-reviewed 2025 Review of Managerial Science article restating the four elements verbatim and confirming 'lack of construct clarity... significantly hampers both their theoretical and empirical development.' Note: correct DOI is amr.35.3.zok346 (the amr.2010.0481 form used in one source is a citation error, substance intact). Minor: 'semantic relationship' emphasizes historical lineage within a semantic network; rendering it as 'distinguishable relationship to related constructs' is a faithful paraphrase. This is the vocabulary for grading whether the quantitative apparatus operationalizes the signature construct.

### 4. The VAIC case (Marzo 2022, Journal of Management and Governance) is a worked exemplar of the assertion-vs-apparatus gap and a template for the dissertation's move. Marzo evaluates the field-standard intellectual-capital measure (VAIC) against Suddaby's four construct-clarity elements and concludes flatly that 'the VAIC is theoretically inconsistent, and it is very far from being a suitable measure of IC.' Its variables 'complement the definitions of constructs more than operationalising them' — the apparatus captures labour and physical-capital efficiency (adjacent and safer) rather than the intellectual-capital construct it is marketed to measure.
*confidence **high** · vote merged 3 claims; [18] 3-0, [16] 3-0, [17] 2-1*

Merges verified claims [16][17][18]. Concluding sentence verified verbatim in the primary Springer full text: 'the VAIC is theoretically inconsistent, and it is very far from being a suitable measure of IC.' The operationalisation quote is verbatim: 'the three variables measuring the efficiency of capital... complement the definitions of constructs more than operationalising them.' The 'labour and physical-capital efficiency' characterization (claim [17], 2-1) blends Marzo's definitional-incoherence finding with Stahle, Stahle & Aho (2011, Journal of Intellectual Capital), who conclude VAIC 'parameters have nothing to do with intellectual capital' — corroboration rather than a misread. Demonstrates construct clarity as an operational grading vocabulary against a live quantitative apparatus.
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10997-021-09565-x

### 5. The complexity-science analogues confirm the same regularity independently: the reality/emergence claim is argued and modeled, not measured on real data. The most rigorous quantitative definition of causal emergence (Rosas's phiID / integrated-information-decomposition) is 'complex and computationally demanding, making it difficult to apply the method to real-world systems,' so full computation is the exception and cheaper proxies are the rule. Hoel's Causal Emergence 2.0 (2025) demonstrates its framework only on synthetic Markov chains with manually constructed transition matrices, never on real empirical data — real domains are named only as prospective future applications.
*confidence **high** · vote merged 2 claims, both 3-0*

Merges verified claims [21][22], both unanimous (3-0). MDPI Entropy 26/2/108 verbatim: phiID formulations 'can be complex and computationally demanding, making it difficult to apply the method to real-world systems'; corroborated by Rosas et al. 2020 (arXiv:2004.08220) and proxy-estimator follow-ups. arXiv 2503.13395 (Hoel, CE 2.0, March 2025): theory 'demonstrated in coarse-grains of Markov chains'; full-text enumeration shows every worked example is synthetic (8-state and 100-state hand-built TPMs). This grounds the dissertation's parallel diagnosis for IIT/Phi (Phi almost never computed on real data) and motivates exact IIT-4.0 computation as the measurement response — an affirmative closing of the gap, not evidence that measurement is unneeded.

## Caveats

All five findings rest on primary sources with unanimous 3-0 verification, except claim [17] within the VAIC finding, which passed 2-1 and lightly blends Marzo's definitional-incoherence thesis with the adjacent Stahle et al. (2011) 'measures labour/capital efficiency' conclusion — the blend is corroboration, not contradiction, but the precise 'labour and physical-capital efficiency' phrasing is most exactly Stahle et al.'s. Two claims were refuted in verification and excluded: (a) that stylized-fact disputes are driven by folk-concept operationalization ambiguity (0-3), and (b) that the causal-emergence field wholly 'lacks a practical algorithm to identify causal emergence from real data' (1-2) — so avoid the strong 'no real-data algorithm exists at all' framing; the defensible claim is that full rigorous computation is the exception and proxies are the rule. Interpretive bridges from source to dissertation (e.g. 'a stylized fact is exactly the structure of a conceptually-carried signature claim,' 'construct clarity grades a measure against its named construct') are the dossier author's framing moves, reasonable and source-grounded but not literal claims by Cronbach & Meehl, Hirschman, or Suddaby. Time-sensitivity is low for the foundational sources (1955, 2010, 2016 are canonical and stable) but higher for causal emergence (2024-2025, fast-moving), where cheaper estimators are actively being published. One DOI error surfaced (amr.2010.0481 vs the correct amr.35.3.zok346); substance is unaffected. Per the dissertation spine, none of this licenses concluding measurement is unnecessary — the framing is affirmative: construct-validity theory holds that this is exactly the situation where validation is required.

## Refuted / did not survive

- Disputes over a stylized-fact claim are driven by ambiguity in operationalizing folk/everyday concepts into measurable variables, which explains why a central conceptual claim resists clean quantitative testing while adjacent, better-operationalized claims get measured.
- By this survey's own account, the entire body of causal-emergence work has produced quantitative measures, conceptual frameworks, and numeric toy examples on Markovian dynamics, but the field lacks a practical algorithm to identify causal emergence from real data — the reality claim is modeled/argued, not measured.

## What this gives the dissertation

The dissertation now has the exact vocabulary for the gap its ten reviews keep finding, and the vocabulary is affirmative. Cronbach and Meehl's nomological network makes construct validation *required*, not optional, precisely where no operational criterion exists — the situation of a conceptually-carried signature claim; and their line that 'rationalization is not construct validation' is the standard the field's asserted claims fail. Hirschman explains why the central claim stays untested: a stylized fact is stylized on purpose, abstracted away from measurement and left as a call for others to explain. Together they let Paper 1 frame the assertion-measurement gap as a named, general pattern in young measurement literatures, and Paper 2 cast exact IIT-4.0 Φ computation as extending the nomological net of a stylized signature claim toward observables — the disciplined response the theory says is needed. Feeds the framing of Paper 1 (the general claim about how these literatures behave) and Paper 2 (Φ as the operationalization that closes the gap), and connects the `algorithmic_management_claims`, `phi_measure_fragmentation`, `causal_emergence_evidence`, and `iit_beyond_consciousness` findings under one concept.
