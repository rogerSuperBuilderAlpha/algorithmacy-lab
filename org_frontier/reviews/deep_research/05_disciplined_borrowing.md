# Disciplined cross-disciplinary borrowing: criteria for the Φ-into-coordination import

*Deep-research dossier · org_frontier/reviews/deep_research/05*
*Scope: what separates disciplined model transfer from decorative analogy, applied to the dissertation's import of exact IIT-4.0 Φ from neuroscience into organizational coordination theory.*

## The question and why it matters

Organization theory runs on imported models. Population biology became organizational
ecology; microeconomics became transaction-cost theory; contract law and agency became the
nexus-of-contracts view of the firm. The dissertation adds one more line to that list: it
imports integrated information (Φ / IIT) from neuroscience to ask whether and how a
coordination form is irreducible. The move invites an obvious objection — that Φ is a
decorative apparatus, a neuroscience word bolted onto organizational examples without doing
explanatory work.

Answering that objection well requires a standard. Two prior reviews in this repo set up the
stakes. The platform-borrowing review found that 70% of platform-governance sources frame
their import as an *extension* of a parent theory rather than a plain application — the field
advertises theoretical novelty it may not deliver. The *iit_beyond_consciousness* review found
that work carrying integrated information into new substrates reaches organizational and social
systems with **no bridge to coordination theory**: of 40 sources, 3 engage organization or
coordination theory at all, and the six coordination-canon anchors (Thompson, Williamson,
Powell, Galbraith, Malone & Crowston, Puranam) draw zero citation links to or from the IIT
corpus. That literature imports a method and skips the theory. The dissertation intends the
opposite. This dossier assembles the criteria a disciplined import must meet, from the
organization-theory literature on theory borrowing and the cognitive-science literature on
analogy, and then applies them candidly to the Φ import.

The affirmative frame holds throughout: the goal is to state what disciplined transfer
requires and show the Φ import can meet it, not to concede the import is illegitimate. The
criteria are a checklist to satisfy, not an indictment.

## Sources

### 1. Gentner (1983) — structure-mapping: the engine that separates analogy from surface match

**Citation.** Gentner, D. (1983). Structure-Mapping: A Theoretical Framework for Analogy.
*Cognitive Science*, 7(2), 155–170. DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog0702_3.

**Core argument (from the source).** A theory of analogy has to say how the meaning of an
analogy derives from the meanings of its parts. Gentner's structure-mapping theory gives two
mapping principles. First, analogy maps *relations between objects*, not *attributes of
objects*: in "the atom is like the solar system," REVOLVES-AROUND(x, y) carries over, while the
sun's yellowness and size do not. Second, which relations map is fixed by *systematicity* — the
presence of higher-order relations (relations between relations, such as CAUSE) that bind the
mapped relations into a connected system. The interpretation rules depend only on the syntactic
structure of the representation, not on the content of the domains, which is what lets analogy
be told cleanly apart from other comparisons. Gentner's own taxonomy makes the distinction
sharp: a comparison is *literal similarity* when both attributes and relations align, *analogy*
when relations align but attributes do not, and a *surface match* (mere-appearance match) when
attributes align but relations do not.

**The criterion to adopt.** A disciplined import transfers *relational structure*, ideally
higher-order relational structure, from the source model. Transferring shared attributes — or,
worse, shared vocabulary — is a surface match, which structure-mapping classifies as the
failure mode, not the goal. Decorative analogy is exactly the case where labels align and the
relational system does not.

### 2. Whetten, Felin & King (2009) — the practice of theory borrowing: functional equivalence

**Citation.** Whetten, D. A., Felin, T., & King, B. G. (2009). The Practice of Theory
Borrowing in Organizational Studies: Current Issues and Future Directions. *Journal of
Management*, 35(3), 537–563. DOI: 10.1177/0149206308330556.

**Core argument (from the source).** Organizational studies borrows constructs across contexts
and across levels, and borrowing is often careless. The paper distinguishes *horizontal*
borrowing (moving a theory across contexts at the same level) from *vertical* borrowing (moving
it across levels of analysis, for example from individuals to organizations to states). The
central demand it places on any borrowing is *functional equivalence*: a borrowed construct
must retain its explanatory and predictive power in the target, and cross-level transfer has to
justify that the borrowed concept still does the same causal work. Downstream users read the
paper as a warning — later work cites Whetten, Felin & King to flag "the risks of careless
vertical theory borrowing" and to require that a construct's transferability be shown before it
is applied, not assumed. The complementary move (Morgeson & Hofmann, invoked in this
literature) is that constructs borrowed across levels need not share *structure*, only
*function* — the causal outputs or effects of the construct.

**The criterion to adopt.** Name the direction of transfer and defend functional equivalence
explicitly. The Φ import is a *vertical* borrowing: from the neural substrate to the
organizational substrate, across levels. That is precisely the case Whetten, Felin & King mark
as high-risk, so the import must argue that whole-minus-parts irreducibility does the same
explanatory work at the organizational level that it does at the neural level — irreducibility
of joint cause-effect structure — rather than assuming the word travels intact.

### 3. Oswick, Fleming & Hanlon (2011) — from borrowing to blending

**Citation.** Oswick, C., Fleming, P., & Hanlon, G. (2011). From Borrowing to Blending:
Rethinking the Processes of Organizational Theory Building. *Academy of Management Review*,
36(2), 318–337. DOI: 10.5465/amr.2011.59330932.

**Core argument (from the source).** Organization theory is short on original, radical theory
because it relies on one-way borrowing from other disciplines, which follows a "foreign to
domestic to domesticated" pattern: an outside theory is imported, applied, and gradually
naturalized without giving anything back. The authors contrast one-way *borrowing* with a
two-way *blending*. In a *correspondence* process the target field exchanges with the source
domain and can contribute back to it; in a *blending* process concepts from source and target
merge to create genuinely new concepts belonging to neither parent. Blending is driven by three
strategies the authors name: *dissonant thinking*, *disanalogy* (attending to where source and
target fail to match), and *counterfactual reasoning*. Productive transfer, on this account,
takes the disanalogies as seriously as the analogies.

**The criterion to adopt.** State the disanalogies. A disciplined import specifies where the
source model does *not* fit the target and what is done about the mismatch. For the Φ import
that means naming where the neural case and the organizational case diverge — no measured
individual "experience," different time-scales, observer-set unit boundaries — and treating
those as parts of the argument, not as embarrassments to bury. The goal is a blend (an
organizational construct of irreducible coordination that Φ makes precise) rather than a
one-way loan of neuroscience vocabulary.

### 4. Boxenbaum & Rouleau (2011) — new knowledge products as bricolage

**Citation.** Boxenbaum, E., & Rouleau, L. (2011). New Knowledge Products as Bricolage:
Metaphors and Scripts in Organizational Theory. *Academy of Management Review*, 36(2), 272–296.
DOI: 10.5465/amr.2011.59330898.

**Core argument (from the source).** New organizational theory is produced by combining
existing elements, and three epistemic "scripts" govern that production: evolution,
differentiation, and bricolage. Bricolage is the creative recombination of available concepts,
empirical material, and metaphors into a new theoretical product. The authors note an
asymmetry: bricolage often does the real work of conceiving a new theory, but because evolution
and differentiation carry higher academic legitimacy, published papers tend to *present* their
contribution as evolution or differentiation even when it was built by bricolage. Metaphor is
one of the raw materials of the bricolage.

**The criterion to adopt.** Own the bricolage honestly and hold it to a standard. Importing Φ
is a bricolage move — a borrowed formal apparatus recombined with coordination constructs — and
the discipline lies in not dressing it up as a seamless "extension" of coordination theory (the
exact inflation the platform-borrowing review measured at 70%). The recombination has to be
shown to yield a construct that earns its place, not merely relabel an existing one.

### 5. Cornelissen (2005) and the domains-interaction line — aptness and generativity

**Citation.** Cornelissen, J. P. (2005). Beyond Compare: Metaphor in Organization Theory.
*Academy of Management Review*, 30(4), 751–764. DOI: 10.5465/amr.2005.18378876.
Supporting: Cornelissen & Kafouros (2008), *British Journal of Management*, 19(4), 365–379,
DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-8551.2007.00550.x; Cornelissen & Durand (2014), *Journal of Management
Studies*, 51(6), 995–1022, DOI: 10.1111/joms.12078.

**Core argument (from the source).** The comparison model of metaphor — reading a metaphor as
the detection of pre-existing shared features between two concepts — is wrong. Cornelissen
proposes a *domains-interaction* model: a metaphor conjoins two whole semantic domains and a
correspondence between concepts is *constructed*, not deciphered, producing an emergent meaning
that existed in neither domain alone. The follow-on work operationalizes when such transfer is
productive. Cornelissen & Kafouros (2008) find that a metaphor's *within-domains similarity*
(how many salient features of the target it captures) drives both its explicatory impact
(clarifying the target) and its generative impact (yielding new understanding). Cornelissen &
Durand (2014) add that the *conceptual distance* between source and target governs the payoff:
distant domains (management and biology, for population ecology) can provoke more revelatory
inferences than near ones — provided the comparison is also *apt*, meaning a genuine
correspondence can be built. Distance without aptness is empty; aptness without distance is
dull.

**The criterion to adopt.** Demand both aptness and generativity. Aptness: a real
correspondence between Φ's construct and an organizational construct must be constructible, not
merely asserted. Generativity: the import must yield new understanding of coordination —
inferences native coordination theory did not already license — or it is decoration however apt
it looks. Neuroscience and organization are a distant pair, which is where the payoff can be
large *if* the correspondence holds.

### Supporting note — Accard (2018), tight mapping

Accard (2018, *European Management Review*, 17(1), 357–368, DOI: 10.1111/emre.12335) treats
abstract, general "radical traveling theories" (complex-systems theory, evolutionism) as the
hard case for borrowing and proposes *tight mapping*: reducing the abstraction of the source
theory through disciplined, rigorous transformation so that its concepts and the logical
relations between them are carried into the target accurately rather than loosely. IIT is a
radical traveling theory in exactly this sense — a formal, general theory with a precise
internal logic. Accard's demand, that the *logical relations between concepts* transfer and not
just the concepts, converges with Gentner's relations-over-attributes principle and sharpens
it: the partition-relative logic of Φ, not only the word "irreducible," is what must map.

## Synthesis: the consolidated criteria for disciplined transfer

The five sources converge on a small set of requirements. A cross-disciplinary import is
disciplined transfer, rather than decorative analogy, to the degree that it satisfies them.

1. **Relational-structure transfer, not surface relabeling (Gentner; Accard).** The import must
   carry the source model's *relations* and, ideally, its *higher-order relations* — the system
   of relations-between-relations — into the target. Carrying shared vocabulary or shared
   attributes is a surface match, which is the diagnostic signature of decorative analogy.

2. **Functional equivalence, with the transfer direction named (Whetten, Felin & King).** The
   borrowed construct must retain its explanatory and predictive work in the target. Vertical
   (cross-level) borrowings carry the highest risk and owe the most explicit defense.

3. **Stated disanalogies and a two-way blend (Oswick, Fleming & Hanlon).** Disciplined transfer
   names where source and target fail to match and builds those mismatches into the argument,
   aiming at a new target-domain construct rather than a one-way loan.

4. **Honest bricolage, not inflated extension (Boxenbaum & Rouleau; platform-borrowing review).**
   The recombination is presented as what it is. It is not dressed up as a seamless extension of
   the parent theory to borrow the parent's legitimacy.

5. **Aptness and generativity (Cornelissen; Cornelissen & Kafouros; Cornelissen & Durand).** A
   real correspondence must be constructible (aptness), and the import must generate inferences
   the target field did not already have (generativity). Conceptual distance raises the ceiling
   on the payoff only when aptness holds.

The **borrowing-to-blending distinction** organizes these. One-way *borrowing* imports a lens
and applies it; it can be legitimate but tends toward the decorative when the source's relational
structure is left behind and only its vocabulary arrives. *Blending* constructs a new
correspondence across two domains and yields a concept native to neither — the harder,
higher-value move, and the one the disanalogy and generativity criteria are built to test. A
useful operational test sits underneath all five: a disciplined import generates *novel testable
predictions in the target* that the source's relational structure entails and that native target
theory does not. Prediction is where relational transfer, functional equivalence, and
generativity all cash out at once.

## Application to the Φ-into-coordination import

The affirmative claim is that exact IIT-4.0 Φ can meet these criteria for the study of
irreducible coordination. What follows states what the import must demonstrate under each
criterion, and how much of that is already in hand versus not yet shown.

**1. The structural mapping (Gentner, Accard).** The load-bearing correspondence is not a
vocabulary swap. It is a relation-to-relation map:

- *Source relation.* In IIT, a system's integrated information is defined over its
  **minimum-information partition** — the cut of the system into parts that loses the least, and
  Φ is the whole-minus-parts quantity that the least-damaging cut still destroys. The construct
  is inherently *partition-relative* and *relational*: irreducibility is a property of the joint
  cause-effect structure across a lattice of possible partitions, not an attribute of any single
  element.
- *Target relation.* In coordination, **joint determination** is the case where a collective
  outcome cannot be recovered by treating members or subunits as separable contributors — where
  no partition of the coordinating unit reproduces its joint behavior. The relation "the whole
  constrains, and is constrained by, its parts such that the least-damaging separation still
  destroys something" maps onto "the coordination form jointly determines an outcome that no
  decomposition recovers."

This is a mapping of *relations and their higher-order organization* (irreducibility defined
over a partition lattice), which is what Gentner and Accard require. The surface-match failure
mode is concrete and must be actively avoided: calling a cohesive team "high-Φ" because it feels
integrated relabels an attribute and transfers no relational structure. Disciplined transfer
computes the partition-relative quantity and lets it, not the label, do the work.

**2. Novel predictions the apparatus generates (generativity, functional equivalence).** The
import earns disciplined status only if the borrowed structure yields claims native coordination
theory does not already make. Candidates the partition-relative construct entails:

- *Interaction volume is not irreducibility.* A densely interacting coordination form can be
  highly *reducible* (some partition reproduces its behavior at low cost), and a sparsely
  interacting one can be irreducible. Native typologies that rank coordination by
  interdependence or coupling (Thompson's pooled/sequential/reciprocal; tight-vs-loose coupling)
  do not draw this distinction; the Φ construct predicts cases where they misrank.
- *The seam is locatable.* The minimum-information partition identifies *where* a coordination
  form is most nearly decomposable — the specific cut along which it comes closest to falling
  into independent parts. Native coordination theory names that a form is interdependent; it does
  not, in general, locate the least-damaging seam. That is a new, structural, testable object.
- *Partition-relative ranking.* The whole-minus-parts quantity can order coordination forms that
  native theory treats as equivalently "tightly coupled," giving a graded structural measure
  where the native constructs give a category.

These are the predictions that would make the import generative in Cornelissen's sense and
functionally equivalent in Whetten, Felin & King's — Φ doing, at the organizational level, the
same explanatory work (adjudicating irreducibility of joint cause-effect structure) it does at
the neural level.

**3. The disanalogies, stated (Oswick, Fleming & Hanlon).** Honesty about the mismatch is part
of the discipline, not a retreat from the thesis:

- **No worker is measured.** IIT in its home domain is a theory of a system's own cause-effect
  structure; the organizational import measures a *model* of a coordination form, not the
  experience or the interior of any participant. Irreducibility is *explored*, not established as
  a necessary property of any real workplace.
- **Unit boundaries are observer-set.** The neural substrate arrives with a candidate grain; the
  organizational substrate's units and time-grain are chosen by the modeler, and Φ is sensitive
  to that choice. The choice must be named and defended, not hidden.
- **A cheap test may agree.** On some field cases a simple factorization test reaches the same
  verdict Φ does. This does not demote Φ. The contribution is the principled, partition-general
  exploration of irreducibility that IIT's apparatus makes possible — a full lattice account of
  where and how a form is irreducible — not a claim that Φ is necessary or that it beats a cheap
  test on every case. That a coarse instrument sometimes agrees with a fine one is expected and
  bounds the *necessity* claim, not the *value* claim.

**4. Not inflated as extension (Boxenbaum & Rouleau).** The import is bricolage: a borrowed
formal apparatus recombined with coordination constructs to build a construct — algorithmacy's
irreducible coordination — that neither parent contained. Presenting it as a plain, principled
application of a powerful tool, rather than as a seamless "extension of coordination theory,"
keeps it clear of the 70% inflation pattern the platform-borrowing review documented.

**What is in hand and what is not.** In hand: a genuine relational correspondence (partition
relativity ↔ joint determination), a distant-but-apt domain pair, and a formal apparatus whose
internal logic can be tightly mapped. Not yet shown: that the novel predictions hold on real
organizational data at a defensible grain; that the seam-location and misranking claims survive
worked cases; and — never claimed — that any worker's interior is measured or that Φ is
necessary rather than illuminating. The import clears the disciplined-transfer bar on the
structural and generativity criteria in principle, and the experiments are what convert
"in principle" into "shown."

## How this feeds Paper 1 and Paper 2

**Paper 1 (the literature review that finds space for algorithmacy).** This dossier supplies the
review's account of *disciplined borrowing* and the exemplar line the dissertation joins:
population biology → organizational ecology, microeconomics → transaction-cost theory,
neuroscience → algorithmacy's irreducible coordination. It gives Paper 1 the criteria that
qualify a borrowing as principled (relational transfer, functional equivalence, stated
disanalogy, honest bricolage, aptness-with-generativity) and lets the review position the Φ
import against them rather than merely asserting the import is legitimate. The two prior repo
reviews become the negative space: platform theory's inflation of application into "extension,"
and the IIT-beyond-consciousness literature's method-only import with no coordination bridge,
are the two failure modes Paper 1's construct is built to avoid.

**Paper 2 (the affirmative case that Φ / IIT is a great tool for exploring the mediated triad).**
This dossier hands Paper 2 its defense against the "decorative apparatus" objection in the
objection's own terms. The reply is not that Φ is decorative-but-tolerable; it is that the
import satisfies the discipline's own criteria for principled transfer — a relation-to-relation
structural mapping, a set of novel predictions native coordination theory does not generate, and
candidly stated disanalogies — and therefore counts as disciplined transfer, not analogy. The
generativity criterion in particular tells Paper 2 what to lead with: the predictions Φ makes
that Thompson, Malone & Crowston, and Puranam do not, which are also the hooks Paper 3's
experiments test.
