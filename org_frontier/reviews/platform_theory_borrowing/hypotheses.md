# Hypotheses — theory borrowing in platform-governance research

*Committed before any coding. The question: which parent theories does platform-governance research
import, in what mode, and how has the mix shifted over time? This is a descriptive review of a
literature's borrowing behavior. Each hypothesis names the knowledge claim it tests (in the
knowledge-weaving typology), its operationalization, and the outcome that would support versus
challenge it.*

Platform-governance research is a young field that theorizes an object — the multi-sided digital
platform — that no single discipline owns. A young field with a novel object borrows its theory from
established fields before it grows its own. Which fields it borrows from, and whether the mix moves as
the field matures, is a measurable fact about the literature.

## H1 — Early economics, later organization theory
- **Knowledge claim (stylized fact):** transaction-cost economics and two-sided-market theory dominate
  the early platform-governance literature; institutional and ecosystem theory rise in later years.
- **Operationalization:** the `parent_theory` distribution split by publication period (pre-2015 vs
  2015-and-after), where 2015 marks the field's shift from platform *economics* toward platform
  *organization/ecosystem* research.
- **Predicts:** `tce` + `two_sided_market` are the plurality of pre-2015 sources; `institutional` +
  `ecosystem` gain share in 2015+.
- **Challenged if:** the economics theories do not lead early, or institutional/ecosystem do not rise.

## H2 — Borrow-and-apply, not extend-or-critique
- **Knowledge claim (key assumption):** platform research treats an imported theory as a ready tool —
  it applies the parent theory to the platform case rather than extending the theory or turning it
  against the case.
- **Operationalization:** the `borrowing_mode` distribution (`apply` | `extend` | `critique`).
- **Predicts:** `apply` is the majority of sources.
- **Challenged if:** `extend` or `critique` together reach a majority.

## H3 — Single-theory imports dominate
- **Knowledge claim (enduring critique):** platform sources import one parent theory at a time and
  rarely combine several, so the field accumulates parallel single-lens studies rather than
  multi-theoretic integration.
- **Operationalization:** the `multi_theory` rate (share of sources coded `yes` for combining two or
  more identifiable parent theories).
- **Predicts:** `multi_theory=yes` is a minority (well under half).
- **Challenged if:** multi-theory imports are a majority.
