You are invited to take part in a research study. Read this page before you decide. Taking part is

## Something you should know first

**Roger Hunt, who is running this study, also teaches this programme.** You should know that before
you read anything else on this page, because it is the reason for several of the arrangements
described below.

Here is what he does and does not decide about your work. **Whether your project advances is
decided by the gate: your peers file a written review and then cast a private vote.** He does not
cast a gate vote, does not break ties, and does not assign a grade that decides advancement. He
runs the programme, teaches its sessions, and maintains its platforms.

Your choice about this study is handled by **[NAME], who is not part of the programme**. They
record who consents and who does not. Roger receives a list of participants only after the first
gate cycle has finished, and he is never told who declined.

## Why this study is being done

The study looks at how people learn to read a review process they cannot see all of — how you work
out what reviewers want, how you present your work so it is read the way you meant, and how you
notice when the rules have shifted. It also asks what people do when a gate decision seems wrong to
them.

## What you will be asked to do

You can agree to any part of this and decline the rest.

- **Interviews.** One conversation of about 45 to 60 minutes around the middle of the programme,
  and a second of the same length near the end. They are audio recorded if you agree; you can ask
  for the recorder to be off at any point, or for a conversation not to be recorded at all. You
  will never be asked about a project whose gate outcome is still open.
- **Observation.** Notes taken during regular cohort working and review sessions. No recording.
- **Your coordination record.** The pull requests, written reviews, issue threads and timing data
  that the programme's repositories already hold.

## What is never collected

- **Individual gate votes.** They are private and they stay private. Only overall outcomes are used.
- **Your grades, assessments, or personnel records.**
- **A written review is only used if both people agree.** A review has an author and a recipient.
  If you consent and the other person does not, that review is left out.

## Voluntary participation

Taking part is entirely your choice. You may skip any question, stop at any time, and withdraw
without giving a reason. **Your decision has no effect on your standing in the cohort, your
assessment, your project outcomes, or your relationship with the programme or with Hult or
Bentley.**

To withdraw, contact **[NAME]** rather than Roger. Your recording, transcript and record extract
are deleted on request, at any time before the findings are published.

## Risks and benefits

The risks are minimal and social. Two are worth naming. You might feel some pressure to take part
because the person asking teaches you — the arrangements above exist to remove that, and declining
costs you nothing that anyone will know about. And this is a small cohort, so something you say
about a peer's review could be recognisable. Quotations are stripped of project names and
distinguishing detail, roles are reported instead of identities, and nothing is quoted where the
combination of role and incident could identify you to someone else in the cohort.

The study offers you no direct benefit. Some people find the conversation a useful occasion to
think about how they work.

## Confidentiality and data handling

Recordings are transcribed and the audio is deleted once the transcript has been checked.
Transcripts carry a pseudonym, never your name. Your coordination record is pseudonymised with the
same one-way coded identifier the questionnaire study uses, so the two can be discussed together
without either holding your name. Identifiers are stored apart from content. Only de-identified
material is quoted, shared, or deposited in the study's open repository.

## Drafting notes — remove before submission

**The sentence that could not survive the fork.** Protocol 260511078's consent says "Program staff
who assess your work do not see who chose to take part." That was true of an anonymous
questionnaire. It is false here, because the PI conducts the interviews and will know exactly who
sat for one. Replacing it with a softened version of the same promise would be the single worst
thing this document could do. What replaces it is a structural fact the board can check — the gate
decides advancement, the PI does not vote in it — plus a third party who holds the consent records
and to whom withdrawals go.

**[NAME] is load-bearing and does not yet exist.** The recruitment design, the withdrawal route and
the "he is never told who declined" clause all depend on a real person outside the programme.
Identify them before submission; the protocol is not honest without one.

**Separable consent is deliberate.** A participant who will hand over a coordination record but not
sit for an interview is a real case, and forcing an all-or-nothing choice would cost data and read
as pressure.
