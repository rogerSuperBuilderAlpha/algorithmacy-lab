# Submission checklist — qualitative protocol, Hult cohort

**Target submission: ~12 August 2026.** This is the earliest hard deadline in the whole programme,
ahead of the Lima paper due 10 September, because approval has to be in hand before the Fall cohort
starts. Missing the cohort pushes the fieldwork by one cycle, which is four to twelve months with no
recovery path.

## Why 12 August

Working backwards. Bentley's exempt survey protocol for the same site took until 11 May 2026 to
determine. A protocol with recorded interviews, observation and an instructor-PI is a harder read
and realistically needs six to eight weeks including one revision round. Approval by cohort launch
therefore means submitting in the second week of August.

**The clock runs in the background.** Drafting the package costs three to five author-days in the
first fortnight of August; the wait then overlaps the Lima drafting crunch at no cost, and analyst
questions in late August are answered in hours.

**Degradation is partial before it is total.** Interviews belong at weeks 6+ anyway, so approval as
late as mid-October still preserves the core design. What late approval costs is consent at intake,
entry-point observation, and the first gate cycle. Approval after December loses the cohort.

## Blocked on the author — every one of these

**1. The cohort start date.** Every week-numbered window in the protocol is relative. It also fills
the Lima paper's "Wave 1 opens [date]" and anchors the autumn calendar. Nobody has written it down.

**2. Call the Bentley IRB analyst.** Three questions:
- Exempt under 46.104(d)(2), or expedited under 46.110 categories 6 and 7? The protocol requests
  the first and prepares for the second. Recorded interviews plus observation plus an instructor-PI
  is the combination that usually decides it, and guessing in the application costs a revision
  round.
- Realistic turnaround at this time of year.
- Does Hult need its own review or a reliance agreement, or does a permission letter suffice?

**3. Name the study administrator.** A real person outside the programme who posts the notice,
holds consent records, receives withdrawals, and schedules interviews. **The design depends on this
person existing.** Recruitment, the withdrawal route, and the clause that the investigator is never
told who declined all run through them. Without a name the package describes good intentions and
should not be submitted.

**4. Who at Hult signs the site letter** — someone with oversight of the programme and senior to
you. If nobody is, say so to the analyst instead of hunting for a signature.

**5. Who owns the programme's repositories.** If Hult owns them, participant consent may not by
itself cover the coordination record.

## The two decisions already made, recorded so they are not relitigated

**A new protocol, not an amendment to 260511078.** The survey protocol's exemption is tied to the
documents the board reviewed, and it must field Wave 1 before the cohort's first working week.
Amending it to add interviews and observation would put an approved, time-critical determination
back under review on an immovable clock. The two studies share a field site and nothing else.

**The fallback is declared in the application, not held in reserve.** If the board finds
instructor-conducted interviews unacceptable during the programme, observation and the coordination
record run during the cohort and all interviews move to after final assessments close in January
2027. Stating it up front lets the board approve the fallback directly instead of forcing a
revision round, and it costs one paragraph.

## What this package is honest about

The dual role is the review's whole subject and the protocol treats it that way. What it offers the
board is structural: the gate decides advancement and the investigator casts
no vote in it; a third party holds the consent records; no interview touches a live outcome;
withdrawal routes around the investigator. The one sentence the survey study's consent could not
carry forward — "Program staff who assess your work do not see who chose to take part" — is
replaced outright, because the PI conducts the interviews and will know who sat for
one.

The single-coder problem is declared. The methods guide sets α ≥ .80 and a
solo investigator cannot produce it; the protocol substitutes an audit trail and member checks, and
says so in the application instead of leaving a reviewer to discover it.
