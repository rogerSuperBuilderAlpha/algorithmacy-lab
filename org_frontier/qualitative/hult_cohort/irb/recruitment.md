# Recruitment materials

The design constraint governing all of these: **the investigator teaches the cohort, so he does not
do the asking.** Recruitment runs as a written notice on the cohort platform, administered by a
named third party outside the programme. No in-person ask, no session announcement, no one-to-one
message from the investigator.

---

## 1. Platform notice — posted once by the administrator

> **Research study open to this cohort**
>
> A study is running alongside this programme on how people learn to read a peer-review process.
> It involves an interview or two, some observation in sessions, and use of the work record already
> in the repositories. You can agree to some parts and not others.
>
> Roger Hunt, who teaches this programme, is the researcher. He does not vote at the gate and does
> not decide whether your project advances — your peers do, by written review and private vote.
> Your decision about the study is handled by [NAME], who is not part of the programme, and Roger
> is not told who declined.
>
> Taking part or not taking part changes nothing about your standing, your assessment, or your
> project outcomes.
>
> Details and the consent form: [LINK]
> Questions, including any you would rather not raise with your instructor: [NAME], [EMAIL]

**Posted once.** No reminders from the investigator. The administrator may post a single reminder
at the halfway point of the consent window.

---

## 2. Administrator's reply to a question about joining

> Thanks for asking. Taking part is entirely your choice and it has no effect on anything in the
> programme — Roger doesn't see who joined until after the first gate cycle, and he never sees who
> declined.
>
> You can agree to just the parts you want: interviews, observation, or the work record. If you
> join and change your mind, tell me and I'll delete your data — you don't need to give a reason
> and you don't need to tell Roger.
>
> The consent form is here: [LINK]. Happy to answer anything else.

---

## 3. Interview scheduling — sent by the administrator, not the investigator

> Hello,
>
> You consented to be interviewed for the study on reading the review process. Roger has asked me
> to find a time that suits you in the next two weeks. It runs 45 to 60 minutes.
>
> Two things worth repeating: you can stop at any point or ask for the recorder to be off, and you
> will not be asked about any project whose gate outcome is still open.
>
> Reply with a couple of times that work and I'll book one.

---

## 4. What the investigator may say, if asked directly in a session

A participant may raise the study in front of others. The reply is short and does not recruit:

> "There's a study running alongside the programme — [NAME] is handling it and the details are on
> the platform. I'd rather not be the one to talk you into or out of it, so anything about joining
> goes to them."

Then move on. Do not answer follow-up questions about participation in the room.

---

## Notes

**[NAME] must be a real person outside the programme.** Every document in this package leans on
them: recruitment, consent records, withdrawal, the "never told who declined" clause, and interview
scheduling. Without a named administrator the design is a description of good intentions and the
board should not accept it. Identify them before submission.

**No incentive is offered.** Payment or credit in a cohort taught by the researcher would work as
pressure, whatever it was called.

**Consent window.** Opens at cohort launch alongside the questionnaire study's Wave 1 consent, if
approval is in hand by then. If approval arrives later, the window opens on approval, and the
interview schedule shifts with it — the fallback in the protocol covers the case where it arrives
too late for mid-cohort interviews.
