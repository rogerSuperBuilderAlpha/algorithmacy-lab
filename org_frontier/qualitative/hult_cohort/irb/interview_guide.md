# Interview guide

**Committed to the repository before the first interview**, so that what was asked can be checked
against what was planned. Semi-structured: the sequence is a spine, not a script, and follow-ups go
where the participant's own instances lead.

45–60 minutes. Wave 1 at weeks 6–10, after at least one complete build-review-vote cycle. Wave 2 at
weeks 15–17, or after final assessment closes where one is still open.

**Standing rule.** Never ask about a project whose gate outcome is still open. If a participant
raises one, note that they raised it and move on.

---

## 0. Opening (3 min)

Confirm consent is on file with the administrator, that recording is agreed, and that they can stop
or go off record at any point. Then: "There are no right answers here, and nothing you say reaches
the gate or your assessment."

## 1. Critical incident (10 min) — the backbone

> "Walk me through the last submission that didn't go the way you expected."

Anchor to their own work. Let them narrate before probing. Then:

- What did you expect, and when did you form that expectation?
- What was the first sign it was going differently?
- What did you do next?
- Looking back, what would you have needed to know?

*Serves RQ1, RQ3. Method 1 — work backward from concrete instances to the rule that governed the
action, then ask the participant to confirm or correct a stated rule.*

## 2. Reading what the gate wants (10 min) — inbound

- How do you work out what your reviewers are looking for?
- What do you go on — the written review, what passed before, something someone told you?
- Tell me about a time you had it wrong. What did you think it wanted, and what did it actually
  want?
- Has your picture of it changed since week 1? What changed it?

*Serves RQ1, RQ3.*

## 3. Making your work read the way you meant (10 min) — outbound

- When you put a submission together, what do you do so it gets read the way you intend?
- Is there anything you have learned to make visible? Anything you have learned to leave out?
- What gets lost when you package it that way?
- Is this the same skill as the one we just talked about, or a different one?

*Serves RQ3. The last question asks the participant directly whether the two practices are one
skill or two — their answer is data either way, including where it contradicts the theory.*

## 4. When the rules move (8 min)

Open elicitation first, then the ledger.

- Have the rules changed since you started — the platforms, the rubric, how reviews are done?
- How did you find out?
- Who noticed first? How did it travel?

Then, from the dated rule-change ledger the investigator keeps as the cohort runs, probe two or
three specific changes the participant did not mention:

> "On [date] [specific change]. Did you notice that at the time?"

*Serves RQ4. The ledger converts a general question into a checkable one, and it is the instrument
a questionnaire cannot imitate.*

## 5. When the gate got it wrong (10 min) — the sharpest section

> "Tell me about a time a gate decision seemed wrong to you."

If they have none, ask what they imagine they would do.

- What did you do?
- Was there anyone you could ask about it? Who?
- What happened when you did?
- What would being properly answered have looked like?
- Did anything change as a result?

*Serves RQ2, and carries the study's pre-registered falsification condition. The theoretical claim
is that no individual competence supplies answerability, because answerability requires a forum.
**Listen for the participant who reliably gets answered through their own skill, with no forum
involved.** That case refutes the claim and is to be pursued, not smoothed over.*

## 6. Folk theory and how it was learned (7 min)

- If you had to explain to someone joining next cohort what the gate is actually doing and why,
  what would you say?
- How did you learn to read it? Was anyone teaching that, or did it come some other way?
- Who in the cohort reads it best? What do they do that you don't?

*Serves RQ1, RQ4. Method 8 — folk-theory elicitation. The last question surfaces distribution
across the cohort, which no self-report scale reaches.*

## 7. Close (2 min)

- Anything I should have asked and didn't?
- Anything you would want left out of the transcript?

Then: how to reach the administrator, and the member-check offer — they will be shown the account
of their own case and asked whether it is recognisable.

---

## Wave 2 changes

Same spine, with three additions:

- **Change over time.** "When we spoke at week [n] you said [X]. Is that still how it looks?"
- **The arc.** "If you compare yourself in week 1 to now, what can you do that you couldn't?"
- **Member check** on the wave 1 account, where the analysis has progressed far enough.

## What this guide deliberately does not do

It does not ask participants to rate themselves on the three competences. The companion survey
does that, and asking here would import the theory's categories into the participant's own words —
which is exactly what the sensitizing-concepts commitment in the protocol exists to prevent. The
categories are for analysis, not for the room.
