# Preparing a qualitative study for publication

A study in this arm should be able to leave it. The aim is work that a management or organization journal
would review on its merits, the bar Bansal and Corley set in their 2012 *Academy of Management Journal*
editorial on what is different about qualitative research. This guide translates that bar into the steps a
contributor here takes, and it marks the one place the program's method cuts against the grain of that
literature, so a contributor can answer for it before a reviewer raises it.

## What is different about qualitative research

Bansal and Corley start from a fact about the form: qualitative research has no single template. A
quantitative paper can lean on a shared structure a reviewer already trusts; a qualitative paper cannot, so
the author makes a chain of choices about data, analysis, and presentation, and has to justify each. The
diversity is the strength and the difficulty at once. Three consequences follow for a study here.

It answers a different kind of question. Qualitative work earns its place on questions of process, meaning,
and context — how a coordination works and why the parties act as they do — and on building theory rather
than testing it. A study that only confirms a number is the wrong shape. The question should be one that
only fieldwork can answer.

It carries its persuasion in the craft. With no template to lean on, the writing does more of the work. The
reader is convinced by the richness of the evidence and by being able to trace how raw material became a
claim. A study that asserts its findings without showing the path will not convince.

It is judged on its own terms. A reviewer cannot check a qualitative study against a fixed standard, so the
study has to make its own standards visible: what counts as a party, what counts as the system committing
rather than conveying, how a disagreement was resolved. The coding scheme and the prior the study is read
against are where those standards live.

## The tension this program has to answer

Canonical qualitative work in this literature is inductive: theory emerges from the data. This program is
prior-driven. A study reads a real setting against a pre-disclosed prior from the catalog, and a reviewer
trained on Gioia or Eisenhardt will see that as confirmatory illustration, the thing the field warns against.
The answer is to use the prior as the entry point and the comparison, never as the conclusion. The study
holds the prior open to being wrong. Where the field diverges from the prior — the platform the catalog
expects to commit turns out to convey, the worker the model treats as pivotal turns out interchangeable —
the divergence is the finding, and it is where the theory is built. A study that reports only "the prior
held" has not contributed; a study that reports where and why a real arrangement departs from the
pre-disclosed expectation has. State this stance in the paper, so the reviewer sees the prior is being
tested, not assumed.

## What a publishable study shows

**Fit.** The question calls for qualitative inquiry, the methods fit the question, and the contribution
follows from both. State the fit in a sentence: this question is about how the parties experience who
decides, which interviews and observation can answer and a model cannot.

**Authenticity.** The evidence shows the researcher was close to the setting. Vivid, concrete material — what
a dispatcher actually said, what the log actually recorded — does more than a summary. Report the data
sources, the number of interviews and observation hours, the access, and the period.

**Trustworthiness.** The qualitative analog to reliability and validity (Lincoln and Guba 1985) has four
parts, and a study should show each. Credibility: the account matches what the parties recognize, shown by
member checking. Transferability: enough context that a reader can judge where the finding travels.
Dependability: the analysis would repeat, shown by the committed coding scheme and a second coder's
agreement. Confirmability: the claims trace to the evidence, not the researcher's prior, shown by the
chain below.

**A transparent chain from data to theory.** Show how raw material became a claim. The data structure of
Gioia, Corley, and Hamilton (2013) is the standard device: first-order codes in the parties' own terms,
second-order themes in the researcher's, and the aggregate dimensions the theory turns on, with a table of
representative quotes so the reader can check each step. Pratt (2009) on presenting qualitative data is the
reference for the tables. The point is that a reader can trace the move and would reach the same place.

**A theoretical contribution.** The study delivers an idea about coordination, not a verdict and not a
description. Golden-Biddle and Locke (1993) name what makes such work land: it is plausible, connecting to
what the reader knows; and it is critical, pushing the reader to see the arrangement differently. The
contribution here is usually a refinement of a prior — the conditions under which a mediated arrangement
commits or conveys in practice, the lived shape of substitutability, what disintermediation looks like to
the parties — stated as theory the next study can use.

## A checklist before submission

- The question is one only fieldwork answers, and the fit between question, method, and contribution is
  stated.
- The prior is named and held open; the finding includes where the real setting departs from it.
- Data sources, access, sampling, and period are reported; the evidence is vivid and concrete.
- The coding scheme was committed before the fieldwork; a second coder's agreement is reported
  (Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.80); the parties member-checked the encoding.
- A data structure and representative-quote tables show the path from material to claim.
- The contribution is a theoretical idea about coordination, stated so another study can build on it.
- The limits and the validation gap are stated; what observation would overturn the finding is named.
