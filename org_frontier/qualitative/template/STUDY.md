# [Title — the setting and the finding in one line]

[Summary paragraph: the coordination setting, the mode (stand-alone or model-bound), and what the study
found. The directory builder shows this paragraph, so make it stand on its own.]

For work aimed at publication, follow [PUBLISHING.md](../PUBLISHING.md); the sections below carry the
elements a reviewer looks for.

## Fit

One sentence on why this question needs qualitative inquiry, how the methods fit it, and how the contribution
follows. The question should be one only fieldwork answers.

## The prior

State which prior in the catalog this setting is read against, and why it is the nearest one. Link the thread
or corpus form. State the expectation the prior sets before the fieldwork, and hold it open: name what would
count as the real setting departing from it, since the departure is where the contribution is built.

## Setting and boundary

The one recurring act of coordination, and its boundary: who is in, who is out, what repeats. Keep the unit
small, three to five parties.

## Parties

Each party that holds a state and updates it. Mark candidates for spectator (watches but is not read) and for
substitution (one of several interchangeable copies).

## Methods

Which methods from [METHODS.md](../METHODS.md) produced the evidence — interviews, observation, document and
log analysis, folk-theory elicitation. Report the data sources, the access, the period, the sampling, the
number of interviews or observation hours, and the inter-rater reliability where coding was used.

## Data structure

How the raw material became a claim, in the form of Gioia, Corley, and Hamilton (2013): first-order codes in
the parties' terms, second-order themes in yours, and the aggregate dimensions the finding turns on. Include
a table of representative quotes so a reader can trace each step.

## Findings

What the study found about the coordination. For a stand-alone study, this is the thick description: how the
parties experience the arrangement, what they take the system to be doing, where authority over the outcome
is felt to sit, and where the accounts diverge. Ground each claim in vivid, concrete evidence.

For a model-bound study, continue to the model and verdict below.

## Model — the determination rules (model-bound only)

For each party, its next state as a Boolean function of the others, with the evidence behind it and one
alternative the evidence does not rule out. State each node's bit (active versus inactive) and what it
collapses. If the parties disagreed, give both rule sets.

## Verdict (model-bound only)

The verdict pre-registered before computing (committed in `coding_scheme.md` or its own commit), then the
computed verdict and the major-complex membership. The sensitivity result: which rule the verdict most
depends on, the re-encoding the evidence also permits, and whether the verdict held or flipped.

## Disagreement (if any)

Where the parties described the arrangement differently, the two readings, and the verdict or finding under
each. State what observation would settle which one holds.

## Contribution

The theoretical idea about coordination the study delivers, stated so another study can build on it. This is
usually a refinement of the prior — the conditions under which the arrangement commits or conveys in
practice, the lived shape of substitutability, what disintermediation looks like to the parties.

## Trustworthiness

Credibility (member checking), transferability (the context a reader needs to judge where the finding
travels), dependability (the committed coding scheme and the second coder's agreement), and confirmability
(the data structure tracing claims to evidence).

## Limits and what would falsify this

What re-encoding or field observation would overturn the finding. For a model-bound study, the validation gap
between the model and the organization.

## References

Sources, with documents and logs cited by passage.
