# Qualitative research topics — an open agenda

Each topic is a recognizable coordination setting paired with the prior in the catalog it would test and the
qualitative questions it raises. The priors are the threads and corpus forms already on the lab's record; a
study reads the real setting against the nearest one. The recurring questions are four: does the system
commit a determination both parties must heed, or convey a signal they act on alone; who is in the
irreducible core; is the worker pivotal or one of many interchangeable copies; and what would a direct
channel between the parties — disintermediation — look like here.

This list is a starting set. A contributor extends it by adding a setting, naming the prior, and stating the
questions, in the same shape.

## 1. Gig dispatch — rideshare and delivery

A driver and a rider matched by a platform that reads both and commits the assignment. The prior is the false
dyad ([`gig_false_dyad`](../corpus/forms_library.py)), where an arrangement that presents as a driver-app
pair is in fact triadic because dispatch reads the unseen rider. Tests the
[substitutability](../threads/substitutability/THREAD.md) prior — a driver one of many can supply is
non-pivotal — and the [disintermediation](../threads/disintermediation/THREAD.md) prior: a direct
driver-rider channel should weaken the platform's hold only if it runs both ways. Does the driver experience
the dispatch as a commitment or a suggestion? Who would the driver name as deciding the match?

## 2. Algorithmic hiring — the applicant tracking system

A candidate, an applicant tracking system, and a hiring manager. The prior is the strict bottleneck
([`ats_strict_bottleneck`](../corpus/forms_library.py)) against its factoring twin
([`ats_feedback_factors`](../corpus/forms_library.py)): the same topology can commit or merely store. Tests
the commit-versus-convey line directly. Does the system enforce a screening rule that the manager must heed,
or does it store and surface data the manager rules on alone? Where do the candidate, the system, and the
manager each locate the decision?

## 3. Content moderation

A poster, a moderation system, and a platform policy team. The prior is the
[mediator with memory](../threads/memory/THREAD.md), where a system that accumulates a record takes more of
the credit, layered with [oversight](../threads/oversight/THREAD.md): a policy team above the system joins
the top of the core rather than relieving the parties. Does the moderation system commit the takedown or
route it to a human; does the poster experience the policy team as part of the coordination or outside it?

## 4. Clinical handoff

An outgoing clinician, a record system, and an incoming clinician. The prior is
[interdependence](../threads/interdependence/THREAD.md): only reciprocal coupling binds, while a one-way
handoff conveys. Does the record commit a determination both clinicians act on, or pass a note one way? Is
the incoming clinician bound into the coordination, or reading a document the outgoing one left?

## 5. Academic peer review

An author, an editor, and reviewers. The prior is the [veto player](../threads/veto_player/THREAD.md): the
editor sits in every integrating coalition and carries the pivotal weight. Tests whether the editor commits
the determination or relays the reviewers' verdict. Who do authors and reviewers take to be deciding; is the
editor a gate that binds or a conduit that forwards?

## 6. Algorithmic scheduling — shift work

Workers, a scheduling system, and a manager. The prior is [quorum](../threads/quorum/THREAD.md): a system
that fires on a threshold binds at the extremes and factors at a majority. Tests whether the scheduling rule
is all-or-nothing in the sense that binds, and whether worker availability co-determines the schedule or only
feeds it. Do workers experience the schedule as committed by the system or set by the manager?

## 7. Customer-service triage

A customer, a triage system, and an agent. The prior is the [observer](../threads/observer/THREAD.md): a
party that reads but is not read is never in the core. Tests who is a member and who only watches — a
supervisor monitoring the queue may be a spectator on whole-system Φ. Does the triage system commit the
routing, and is the customer a party to the coordination or its object?

## 8. Lending and credit decisions

A borrower, a scoring system, and a lender's policy. The prior is [oversight](../threads/oversight/THREAD.md)
read as principal-agent: a policy above the scoring system joins the top and the parties below are squeezed.
Does the score commit the decision or inform a human who decides; where do the borrower and the loan officer
locate the determination?

## 9. Dating and matching apps

Two users and a matching system that reads both. The prior is the canonical match
([`two_sided_match`](../corpus/forms_library.py)), where the system commits only when both sides are present
and both track the commit. Tests whether the match is a joint determination or a pair of one-sided
suggestions. Do users experience the app as committing a match or proposing options each accepts alone?

## 10. Freelance marketplaces and multi-homing

A freelancer, a counterpart, and one of several competing platforms the freelancer can route through. The
prior is [competing platforms](../threads/competing_platforms/THREAD.md): a genuine substitute loosens a
platform's hold, while differentiated platforms restore it. Tests the outside-option principle in practice.
When a freelancer works across platforms, does any one read as the bottleneck, and what makes a platform
non-substitutable to its users?

## 11. Agents and brokers

A worker who reaches a platform through an agent — a talent agent, a broker, an intermediary. The prior is
[delegation](../threads/delegation/THREAD.md): standing moves from the worker to the agent who stands between
it and the system. Tests whether the worker or the agent is the central party. Who do the platform and the
counterpart deal with as the bound party, and does the worker experience the agent as extending or
replacing its reach?

## 12. Multi-hub coordination — managed platforms

A coordination held by more than one coordinating system — a gig platform with separate dispatch and pricing
systems, a workplace with overlapping management tools. The prior is the
[hierarchy](../threads/hierarchy/THREAD.md) line: a group beyond one coordinator's span needs multiple hubs
that overlap and differ, and the structure breaks rather than bends when a hub is removed. Tests how real
multi-system coordinations divide the work, whether the systems read each other, and what happens when one
goes down.
