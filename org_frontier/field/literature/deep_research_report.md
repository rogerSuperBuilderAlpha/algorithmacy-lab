# Deep-research report — methodological standards for a Boolean-model-of-coordination field protocol

Stage-2 literature pass for the field-protocol methods paper. Generated by the `deep-research` workflow
(search → fetch → adversarial verification → synthesis), re-run after an initial pass was rate-limited,
and reviewed. It situates the protocol in an established tradition, supplies the standards its weakest
step must meet, and surfaces a controversy the paper must address head-on.

## 1. The tradition: computational organization theory

The protocol belongs to a recognized lineage of formal/computational modeling in organization theory.
Rivkin & Siggelkow (2002, 2003) use agent-based NK-landscape simulations to model organizational
design, delegation, interdependence, and coordination, and articulate the field's central
methodological norm: tools imported from the natural sciences (NK landscapes) "must be adapted with
care to the organizational context rather than applied naively." A Boolean-dynamics-plus-Φ apparatus
imported from neuroscience sits squarely in this tradition and inherits that norm.

## 2. The validation-gap language: simulation builds theory, it does not measure

Davis, Eisenhardt & Bingham (2007, *Academy of Management Review*) is the standard reference. It
positions simulation as a theory-development method whose comparative strength is **internal validity**,
between theory-creating and theory-testing methods. Two of its points are load-bearing for the protocol.
First, operationalizing a verbal theory forces researchers to fill unanticipated "specificity gaps" with
defensible but arbitrary assumptions — the modeling judgment the protocol makes explicit. Second,
**equifinality**: many defensible models can operationalize the same theory, so one model "working" does
not validate the theory it stands for. This is exactly the rationale for the protocol's mandatory
sensitivity step, and the accepted language for the protocol's validation gap (internal validity
established; external validity a separate empirical claim) is this paper's, not an idiosyncratic framing.

## 3. The standards the rule-elicitation step must meet

The protocol's named weakness — Stage-4 rule elicitation has no standard method — is exactly where
mature methods exist and should be adopted.
- **Causal cognitive mapping** (Axelrod tradition; Hodgkinson, Maule & Bown 2004) elicits causal
  structure from decision-makers, with a documented trade-off between elaboration (pairwise comparison)
  and ecological validity (freehand). It requires trained analysts and flags inter-coder reliability as
  a requirement.
- **Qualitative Comparative Analysis** (Rihoux & Ragin 2009) is a Boolean, set-theoretic, small-N
  method using truth tables and Boolean minimization — a direct parallel to modeling coordination as a
  Boolean form — and its calibration anchors (full membership 1, non-membership 0, cross-over 0.5)
  supply a standard for turning a state into a bit.
- **Inter-rater reliability** must be reported: Krippendorff's α (≥ 0.80 reliable; 0.667–0.80 tentative)
  or Cohen's κ, with the conventional thresholds (0.61–0.80 substantial, > 0.81 almost perfect).

The methods paper should specify the elicitation step as causal cognitive mapping with QCA-style bit
calibration and a reported reliability coefficient, rather than leaving it open.

## 4. The target construct

Coordination theory (Malone & Crowston 1994) defines coordination as "managing dependencies between
activities" and supplies a dependency taxonomy and a six-step elicitation technique (Crowston & Osborn
2003). Thompson's (1967) interdependence typology (pooled / sequential / reciprocal), already engaged in
the lab's q43, is the organizational-theory baseline; design structure matrices translate it into
dependency networks. These are the constructs a Boolean coordination model represents.

## 5. The controversy the paper must address

The deep research surfaced the September 2023 open letter (124 scholars, PsyArXiv) arguing IIT should be
labelled pseudoscience *as a theory of consciousness*, alongside the high-Φ-XOR-array objection and the
point that Φ is not determinable from input–output behaviour. The methods paper must address this
directly: the lab uses Φ as an exact, well-defined measure of **structural irreducibility** of a small
discrete system — a computable graph/dynamics property — and makes **no consciousness claim**. The
consciousness controversy does not bear on Φ's use as an irreducibility statistic, and the paper says so.

## Net effect on the contribution

The field protocol is a methods contribution within the computational-organization-theory tradition. The
paper (i) positions it there and adopts that tradition's validation-gap language (internal validity;
equifinality; simulation builds theory); (ii) specifies the rule-elicitation step using established
methods (causal cognitive mapping, QCA calibration, reported inter-rater reliability), closing its named
weakness; (iii) names Malone–Crowston and Thompson as the target constructs; and (iv) separates the
structural use of Φ from the contested consciousness claim. The contribution is the protocol so
specified; the mocks are its demonstration.
