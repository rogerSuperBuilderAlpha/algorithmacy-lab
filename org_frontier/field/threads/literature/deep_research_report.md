# Deep-research report — platform position, outside options, and what is already known

Stage-2 literature pass for the platform-position paper, over the two field threads. Generated by the
`deep-research` workflow (search → fetch → adversarial verification → synthesis) and reviewed. Its
verdict is blunt and it reshapes the paper from a claim of discovery to a claim of restatement: the
outside-option theory of platform position is, in its economic content, a rediscovery of established
results. The paper says so.

## What the threads found, and where each part already lives

**Capture = lock-in of the dependent side (asymmetric outside options).** This is Armstrong's
*competitive bottleneck* (Armstrong 2006; Armstrong & Wright 2007). When one side single-homes and the
other multi-homes, the platform holds monopoly power over access to the side that has no alternative
route to its counterparty, and that side is the exploited/captured one; the platform stops competing
for it. The asymmetric-homing-confers-capture-of-the-dependent-side result is the established core of
two-sided-market theory. The threads' "capture" regime is this.

**Symmetric outside options dissolve the platform; asymmetric preserve it.** Bakos & Halaburda (2020)
show two-sided multi-homing eliminates the cross-side strategic power that the competitive-bottleneck
and single-homing benchmarks have. Armstrong notes that if the single-homing side could also multi-home,
"platforms no longer have a monopoly over providing access." The threads' "bypassed" regime (both sides
have a substitute) is this.

**The asymmetric/symmetric split itself.** The *outside-option principle* / "deal-me-out" rule (Binmore,
Shaked & Sutton 1989) states that an outside option changes the division only when it binds — exceeds
the equal-split share. When it binds, the party with the binding option takes exactly that option and
the counterparty becomes the residual claimant; when neither binds, the surplus splits equally. This is
precisely the threads' "asymmetric options → capture, symmetric → equal/bypass" law, derived a quarter
century earlier in non-cooperative bargaining.

**The core = platform + parties with no outside option.** Network-exchange theory (Easley & Kleinberg,
*Networks* ch. 12) states it almost verbatim: structural power rests on dependence and exclusion — a
node is powerful exactly when its partners have no alternative while it has several; when partners gain
alternatives the power collapses; and on the 3-node path only outcomes giving the centre node nearly all
value are stable. The threads' bottleneck (the central, sole-route node) and the outside-option
membership law are the cause-effect-structure image of this.

**Lock-in and dependence.** Farrell & Klemperer on switching costs, and the hold-up / asset-specificity
literature (Williamson; de Meza & Lockwood 1998; Chiu 1998), establish that the party without an
alternative is the dependent, exploitable one. The threads' lock-in is this.

## What, if anything, is not already in the economics

The economic content of the theory is established. The deep research found no part of the
capture/bypass/lock-in/outside-option law that is new to economics. Two narrow things remain candidate
contributions, and the paper states them modestly:

1. **A structural restatement.** These value-theoretic regimes appear as an *exact IIT-4.0 cause-effect
   membership law* — the major complex equals the platform plus the parties with no outside option —
   computed from Φ on a Boolean dynamical model, not assumed. That a bargaining/competitive-bottleneck
   result shows up as an information-theoretic irreducibility membership is a bridge, not a new
   economic result, and its value is only as a bridge.
2. **The enricher regime and its fragility.** The observation that a platform can be in the irreducible
   core yet dispensable (it enables its own fallback), that this regime is rare (~6%) and degenerates
   into capture under perturbation, is a framing the economics literature does not put in these terms.
   It is, at bottom, the knife-edge between the competitive-bottleneck and the both-multi-home regimes,
   so even this is a re-description.

## Net effect on the contribution

The paper does not claim to discover that outside options determine platform power, capture, or
disintermediation. Those are Armstrong's competitive bottleneck, the Binmore–Shaked–Sutton outside-option
principle, and Easley–Kleinberg network-exchange power. The paper's only claim is that the lab's
exact-Φ instrument, applied to Boolean coordination models, reproduces these results as a cause-effect
membership law — a cross-method confirmation, with the enricher knife-edge as a minor re-description.
This is the honest outcome of running the literature, and it is the reason to run it.
