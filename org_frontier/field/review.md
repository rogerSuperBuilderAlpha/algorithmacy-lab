# Field protocol — Stage 1 review (methods paper)

## The contribution

A protocol for taking one real coordination arrangement to an explicit, falsifiable dyadic/triadic
model and an exact-Φ verdict, with a demonstration on ten mock organizations. This is a methods paper:
its claims are about a procedure, not about a finding.

## What is already here

`PROTOCOL.md` (the nine-step field protocol with a mandatory sensitivity step and an explicit
validation gap) and the ten mocks (`mocks.py`, `run.py`, `FINDINGS.md`, CI-gated `field-mocks`),
which demonstrate the four judgment points: system-versus-conduit, substitutability, gate-versus-store,
and reading the complex. The protocol's stated weaknesses are real and named in `PROTOCOL.md`: rule
elicitation has no standard method, no inter-rater reliability, no procedure for conflicting accounts.

## The gap, and why the literature pass is essential here

This is the category where the literature is not a novelty check but a *standards* check. A
Boolean-model-of-coordination protocol does not exist in a vacuum: organization theory has a long
computational-modeling tradition (agent-based models, NK landscapes), an established language for the
internal-versus-external-validity gap in simulation-as-theory-building, and mature methods for
eliciting decision rules and causal maps from field data with inter-rater reliability. The protocol's
weakest step — rule elicitation — is exactly where those methods exist and should be borrowed.

The deep-research report (`literature/`) must answer: (1) what computational-organization-theory
tradition this protocol belongs to and what its validation norms are; (2) the accepted framing of the
validation gap (simulation builds theory, it does not measure), so the protocol's gap language matches
the field's; (3) which established elicitation methods (process tracing, causal cognitive mapping, QCA,
inter-rater reliability coding) the protocol's Stage-4 rule elicitation should adopt to be rigorous.
The paper then positions the protocol within that tradition and specifies the elicitation procedure it
currently lacks, rather than presenting an ad hoc tool. The mocks are the worked demonstration; the
methods contribution is the protocol plus the borrowed elicitation standard.
