# Two against one, or one and a follower: Caplow's coalitions under exact Φ

## Abstract

Caplow's theory of coalitions in the triad predicts, from the distribution of power alone, which pair forms
against the third: eight types, a predicted coalition for each, and the paradox that strength is weakness.
The theory rests on four assumptions — a stronger member controls a weaker one, each member seeks control,
strength is additive, and coalitions form in an existing triadic situation — and sixty years of experiments
have tested its predictions while taking the assumptions as given. This paper renders the assumptions
themselves as Boolean forms: twenty-four coalitions across the eight types, each a four-element system in
which the stronger partner sets the coalition's stance, the outcome is the weighted majority with the
coalition voting as a bloc, and every member reads the outcome. A coalition binds when both partners are in
the major complex. One claim is confirmed: among binding coalitions the strongest member is a party to 2 of
5, the others to 4 each. Three are refined. The predicted coalition is the binding one in Types 1, 2, 4, and
6 and not in Types 3, 5, 7, and 8; when the strongest member outweighs the other two no coalition against
him binds, though the blocking coalition at exact equality pulls him and his rival into one whole; and in
Type 5 the revolutionary coalition alone removes the strongest from the core while none binds both partners.
One is refuted: of twenty winning coalitions, the five with equal partners all bind and the fifteen with
unequal partners never do. Strength adds; wholeness does not. The instrument's addition is a distinction
Caplow's theory has no room for — between two against one and one with a follower — and the finding that his
precoalition condition has no irreducible structure in six of eight types.

## Introduction

Caplow asked the triad's first quantitative question. Simmel had said that three is the number at which
two can combine against one; Caplow (1956) asked which two, and answered that the distribution of power
decides it [caplow1956coalitions]. Three members with strengths A ≥ B ≥ C fall into six types by the
relations among the strengths — two more were added in 1959 — and in each type the theory names the pair
that forms [caplow1959further]. The predictions were tested in the pachisi games of Vinacke and Arkoff
(1957) and their successors, replicated as recently as 2022, and reformulated as a game-theoretic solution
by Shenoy (1978) [vinacke1957experimental; wissink2022strength; shenoy1978coalition]. The most-cited result
is the paradox the experiments named: strength is weakness, because the two weaker members combine against
the strongest whenever they can.

The predictions have been tested. The assumptions that generate them have not, because the assumptions are
about structure — who controls whom inside a coalition, whether strengths add, what a coalition is — and the
field has had no instrument for structure. Caplow's first assumption is that "a stronger member can control a
weaker member and will seek to do so." Inside a coalition of unequal partners, the weaker partner's stance
does not enter the coalition's stance. The lab's criterion asks, of any set of parties, whether the whole's
determination depends on each of them, and it has a standing result that a party whose stance is not read
is not in the core (probe #5). Those two statements meet at every unequal coalition Caplow predicts.

The paper renders Caplow's assumptions as Boolean forms and reads what they produce. For each of the eight
types and each of the three possible pairs, a four-element system carries the members' stances and the
outcome of the issue they decide; the coalition's stance is the weighted majority over its own strength
(assumption 1), the outcome is the weighted majority over the triad with the coalition voting as a bloc
(assumption 3), and every member reads the outcome (assumption 4). A coalition *binds* when both partners are
in the major complex. Five claims are tested against hypotheses fixed before computation, with the lab's
prior stated beside each so the reader sees in advance that the lab expected to disagree in four types. The
sections that follow give Caplow's claims in his words, the translation, the hypotheses, the methods, the
results, and what they say about what a coalition is.

## Caplow's account

The exegesis is in `exegesis.md`. The 1956 and 1959 articles are quoted through Shenoy (1978), who
reproduces them verbatim.

**The assumptions.** "A.1. Members of a triad may differ in strength. A stronger member can control a weaker
member and will seek to do so. A.2. Each member of the triad seeks control over the others. Control over two
others is preferred to control over one other. Control over one other is preferred to control over none.
A.3. Strength is additive. The strength of a coalition is equal to the sum of the strengths of its two
members. A.4. The formation of coalitions takes place in an existing triadic situation, so that there is a
precoalition condition in every triad" (Caplow 1956, in Shenoy 1978: 183).

**C1 — power predicts the coalition.** Eight types and a prediction for each: Type 1 (A = B = C) any pair;
Type 2 (A > B = C, A < B + C) BC; Type 3 (A < B = C) AB or AC; Type 4 (A > B + C, B = C) none; Type 5
(A > B > C, A < B + C) AC or BC; Type 6 (A > B > C, A > B + C) none; Types 7 and 8 (A = B + C) AB or AC. Of
Type 1: "each member strives to enter a coalition within which he is equal to his ally and stronger (by
virtue of the coalition) than the isolate" (1956, in Shenoy 1978: 185). The claim is that the weights fix the
pair.

**C2 — strength is weakness.** In Type 2 the weaker pair combines against the strongest; in Type 5 both
predicted coalitions include the weakest. Vinacke and Arkoff found A in the coalition 28.9% of the time in
Type 2 against 86.5% and 85.4% for B and C [wissink2022strength]. The mechanism is A.4: coercion by the
strong "will provoke the formation of an advantageous coalition to oppose the coercion."

**C3 — no coalition against a dominant A.** Of Type 6: "A is stronger than B and C combined and has no motive
to form a coalition. As in Type 4, true coalition is impossible" (1956, in Shenoy 1978: 188). Of Types 7
and 8: "although a coalition of B and C can block the dominance of A, it is not sufficient to control the
situation, and, therefore, the probable coalitions under the standard assumptions are AB or AC" (1959, in
Shenoy 1978: 189).

**C4 — conservative, revolutionary, improper.** *Two Against One* sorts coalitions by their effect on the
order: conservative when the order stands, revolutionary when the weaker dominate the strongest, improper
when neither [caplow1968two]. In Type 5, AB is conservative, BC revolutionary, AC improper. Under A.2 both
partners in any of them gain control over the isolate.

**C5 — strength is additive.** A.3 in full. The coalition's effectiveness is a function of the sum, and the
predictions are made from sums.

The literature's two lines are in `literature/deep_research_report.md`. The experimental line tested the
predictions and found them roughly right with the paradox intact. The formal line, from Gamson (1961) to
Shenoy (1978), replaced the control motive with payoffs or indices and re-derived the predictions
[gamson1961theory]. Shenoy also found two places where Caplow assumes a control relation his own
definition does not license — B over C before any coalition in Type 6, B over A in Type 3 — and called them
errors. Neither line asked whether control inside a coalition has structural reality, because neither had
a way to compute the structure of a coalition.

## From claims to forms

Four elements: the members A, B, C and the outcome O. A type is a weight vector, taken from Shenoy's
representatives: (1,1,1), (3,2,2), (1,2,2), (3,1,1), (4,3,2), (4,2,1), (3,2,1), (2,1,1). A coalition
{X, Y} against Z is three rules from Caplow's assumptions:

- *bloc* = 1 iff wX·X + wY·Y > (wX + wY)/2 — the stronger partner's stance when the weights differ, X∧Y
  when they are equal (A.1);
- O' = 1 iff (wX + wY)·bloc + wZ·Z > T/2, 0 if less, O if equal, with T the total weight (A.3; a tie is a
  block, not a decision);
- A' = B' = C' = O (A.4: the triad persists and every member takes the outcome as the situation).

The precoalition form of each type has no bloc: O' is the weighted majority of the three individually. A
coalition *wins* when wX + wY > wZ and *binds* when both partners are in the major complex.

| claim | structural restatement | forms | keeps / drops |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | the weights fix the pair | 24 coalition forms, 8 precoalition | keeps A.1, A.3, A.4 as rules; drops A.2, the control motive |
| C2 | the strongest is most often excluded | the binding coalitions across all types | keeps "excluded" as "not a party to a binding coalition"; drops frequency of formation |
| C3 | A ≥ B + C leaves no effective coalition against A | Types 4, 6, 7, 8 | keeps "block" as the tie rule O'=O; drops the extraneous inducements |
| C4 | three kinds of Type 5 coalition | Type 5 forms | keeps the effect on A's standing; drops the payoff split |
| C5 | the sum decides | all winning coalitions | keeps the sum as the winning condition; asks whether the division decides binding |

The control motive, A.2, is deliberately not rendered. It is a statement about what members want; the other
three assumptions are statements about how a coalition works, and the paper asks what those three produce.
That is the main risk in the translation: Caplow's predictions in Types 3, 5, 7, and 8 follow from A.2, so
a form that omits it is expected to disagree with them, and the disagreement is a result about the
assumptions rather than about the predictions. A second rendering of "control" was considered — a graded
within-bloc rule in which the weaker partner's stance shifts the bloc's threshold without deciding it — and
set aside because A.1 says "control," and the weighted majority over the bloc's own strength is the plain
reading.

## Hypotheses

Fixed in `hypotheses.md` before any probe ran.

- **H1 (C1).** In each type the binding coalitions are exactly Caplow's predicted ones. *H0:* they differ in
  some type. *Lab prior:* agreement in Types 1, 2, 4, 6; disagreement in 3, 5, 7, 8, where the predicted
  pair is unequal and the junior partner is unread (probe #5).
- **H2 (C2).** Over all binding coalitions, A is a party to fewer than B and fewer than C. *H0:* A is a party
  to at least as many. *Lab prior:* agrees.
- **H3 (C3).** In Types 4, 6, 7, 8, BC never binds and A is in every core, coalition and precoalition.
  *H0:* BC binds somewhere or A leaves a core. *Lab prior:* agrees for 4 and 6; Types 7 and 8 open.
- **H4 (C4).** In Type 5 all three coalitions bind; A is out of the core under BC and in under AB and AC.
  *H0:* a partner is out, or A is in under BC. *Lab prior:* A-pattern yes, both-partners no.
- **H5 (C5).** Every winning coalition binds regardless of the division of strength. *H0:* binding tracks
  the division. *Lab prior:* binding tracks equality within the pair (finding 3).

## Methods

**Instrument.** Exact IIT 4.0 via PyPhi 4.0. Whole-system verdict Φ_MIP, maximized over reachable states;
major complex by `new_big_phi.maximal_complex`, maximized over reachable states. The control form A'=M,
M'=A∧B, B'=M must read Φ = 2.000000, core {A, M, B}; every probe runs it first and halts on failure. It
passed in all five.

**Forms.** As in the translation table; full rules in Appendix A and `forms.py`. Names `t<k>_<XY>` and
`t<k>_pre`.

**Decision rules.** H1 CONFIRMED iff all eight types match; PARTIAL iff at least four; REFUTED otherwise. H2
CONFIRMED iff count(A) < count(B) and count(A) < count(C) over binding coalitions. H3 CONFIRMED iff BC never
binds in Types 4, 6, 7, 8 and A is in every core there; PARTIAL iff BC never binds but A leaves a core. H4
CONFIRMED iff all three Type 5 coalitions bind and A is out only under BC; PARTIAL iff the A-pattern holds
without all three binding. H5 CONFIRMED iff every winning coalition binds.

**Reproduction.** `python -m org_frontier.thinkers.caplow.probe_caplow_<claim>` from the repository root
on the 4.0 environment. Each form takes under a second; the full set of 32 runs in about twenty seconds.
The five checks are registered in `ci/reproduce.json`.

### H1 — power predicts the coalition: PARTIAL

The binding set equals Caplow's predicted set in Types 1, 2, 4, and 6 and differs in Types 3, 5, 7, and 8.
In Type 3 the equal pair BC binds and the predicted AB and AC are dominance dyads, {B, O} and {C, O}, with
the weakest member A a spectator to the coalition he is predicted to join. In Type 5 no coalition binds: AB
and AC leave {A, O}, BC leaves {B, O}. In Types 7 and 8 the predicted AB and AC leave {A, O}. Four of eight;
PARTIAL by the decision rule, and the four that differ are the four the lab prior named.

### H2 — strength is weakness: CONFIRMED

Five coalitions bind across the eight types — the three of Type 1, BC in Type 2, BC in Type 3 — and A is a
party to two of them, B to four, C to four. The decision rule is met. Counted differently, over all
twenty-four coalition forms, A is in the core 18 times, B 7, C 6: strength is weakness among coalitions that
are wholes and strength is strength among cores.

### H3 — no coalition against a dominant A: PARTIAL

BC never binds in Types 4, 6, 7, or 8. In Types 4 and 6 every form, the precoalition included, has core
{A, O}: A and the outcome, the others spectators. In Types 7 and 8 the winning coalitions AB and AC have
core {A, O}, and the blocking coalition BC does something Caplow did not predict. In Type 7 (3,2,1) the bloc
is B's stance, the outcome moves only when B and A agree, and the core is {A, B, O} at Φ = 2.000: the block
binds A to his rival. In Type 8 (2,1,1) the bloc is B∧C and the core is A with one of the two equal partners
and the outcome, {A, C, O}, at Φ = 0.158. The precoalition forms of Types 7 and 8 have no complex at all, so
A is not in every core; PARTIAL by the decision rule.

### H4 — conservative, revolutionary, improper: PARTIAL

In Type 5 the conservative coalition AB has core {A, O}, the improper AC has {A, O}, and the revolutionary
BC has {B, O}. A is out of the core under BC and in under the other two, as Caplow's kinds imply. No Type 5
coalition binds both partners: B is a spectator in AB, C in AC, C in BC. PARTIAL.

### H5 — strength is additive: REFUTED

Twenty of the twenty-four coalitions win. Five bind, and they are the five whose partners have equal weight;
the fifteen winning coalitions with unequal partners never bind, in any type. Binding coincides with equality
within the pair exactly (5/5 against 0/15). The decision rule required every winning coalition to bind;
REFUTED.

*Observed, not pre-registered.* The precoalition form has no complex in Types 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, and 8, and the
core {A, O} in Types 4 and 6. Where no member outweighs the other two, the triad before coalition has no
irreducible structure; the coalition is what makes a whole, and only an equal one.

## Discussion

**C1, refined.** Caplow's predictions are of two kinds, and the instrument separates them. In Types 1 and 2
the predicted coalition is an equal pair, and the instrument finds it a whole: two partners and the outcome,
each needed. In Types 3, 5, 7, and 8 the predicted coalition is an unequal pair chosen because the weaker
member gains control over the isolate, and the instrument finds one partner and the outcome, the other
partner reading and unread. The theory says "AB forms"; the structure says "A decides and B follows." The
disagreement is not with the prediction — the experiments say those pairs do form — but with what Caplow
calls the thing that forms. This is Shenoy's second error made general: Caplow's control relation inside a
coalition is a relation of one party's stance to the outcome and the other party's to nothing.

**C2, recovered, with a split.** Among the coalitions that are wholes, the strongest member is the one most
often left out, as Caplow and the pachisi games say. Among cores of any kind he is the one most often in,
because a dominance dyad is a core and he heads most of them. Strength is weakness for membership in an
equal whole and strength for membership in any whole. The paradox is a fact about equal coalitions, which is
where the experiments found it: Type 2.

**C3, refined.** Caplow's "true coalition is impossible" holds in Types 4 and 6 in the strong form: A and the
outcome are the whole, everyone else a spectator, coalition or none. At exact equality the instrument finds
what his word "block" describes. In Type 7 the coalition that cannot control the situation binds its leader
to the member it blocks: A and B are each needed for the outcome to move, and the whole is {A, B, O}. That
is q214's *tertius* structure in another dress — the balance of two makes both necessary — and it is not
in Caplow's typology, which has no place for a whole made of opponents.

**C4, refined.** The three kinds of Type 5 coalition differ in one way the instrument can read — whether A is
in the core — and are alike in the way Caplow's assumption 2 says they differ: no partner gains a place. B in
the conservative coalition "controls C," on the theory; on the structure B is a spectator to A's control of
the outcome. The revolutionary coalition replaces A with B and leaves C where he was.

**C5, refuted.** Strength adds; the sum decides who wins; wholeness does not add. Two winning coalitions of
the same margin — BC in Type 2 (4 against 3) and AB in Type 3 (3 against 2) both win by one — are a whole and
a dominance dyad, respectively, and the only thing that separates them is whether the partners are equal. This is finding 3 in
`STRUCTURAL_FINDINGS.md`, that irreducibility needs every party read and returning, and Simmel's H5 (probe
#373) in coalition form: the core is whoever holds the decision plus the decision.

**The through-line.** Caplow's title is *Two Against One*. On the structure there are two things the title
covers. Two against one is a whole of two and the outcome, and it occurs when the two are equal. One with a
follower is a whole of one and the outcome, and it occurs whenever the two are not. Caplow's theory has one
word for both and predicts the second more often than the first. The lab's criterion has a word for the
difference, and it is the same word Peirce's paper (probe #376) used for the difference between giving and
its imitation: the follower is "externally appended."

**What the instrument added.** The distinction between coalition and dominance dyad. The finding that the
precoalition condition has no irreducible structure unless one member dominates. The whole made of opponents
at exact balance. And a reading of strength-is-weakness that says where it lives: in equal coalitions, not
in coalitions.

## Limitations

Translation risk first. "Control" is rendered as the stronger partner's stance being the bloc's stance;
under that rule the weaker partner is unread by construction, and the result that unequal coalitions never
bind follows from probe #5 rather than from anything new. A graded rendering, in which the weaker partner
shifts the bloc's threshold without deciding it, would let binding vary with the margin and might grade
Caplow's types rather than split them; it was not run. The control motive A.2 was not rendered, so where
Caplow's predictions depend on it the paper reports what the structural assumptions produce without it, and
that is a result about the assumptions and not a test of the predictions. One weight vector represents each
type.

The scope is in-silico: four binary elements, one issue, one step of determination. Nothing here is about
three people at a pachisi board. These coalitions do not bargain, split payoffs, or misperceive the game, which is where Kelley and Arrowood (1960) located the effect [kelley1960coalitions].

The rendering does not carry time. Caplow's continuous, episodic, and terminal situations are about how
often the coalition question recurs, and a fixed form cannot say whether a dominance dyad that persists
becomes something else.

## Conclusion

Rendered by his own assumptions, Caplow's coalitions are wholes when their partners are equal and one-plus-
a-follower when they are not. Power predicts the coalition in the four types where the prediction names an
equal pair or no pair; strength is weakness among wholes and strength among cores; a dominant member is the
whole with the outcome, and a blocked one is bound to his blocker; the revolutionary coalition alone removes
the strongest; and the sum of strengths decides who wins and nothing about whether what wins is two. The
triad before coalition has no irreducible structure in six of eight types. The next thinker on the roster
will be read against the same line between two-against-one and one-with-a-follower.

## Appendix A — Forms

Node order (A, B, C, O); `s[i]` is node i's current state; T = wA + wB + wC.

- **control** (A, M, B): A'=M; M'=A∧B; B'=M.
- **coalition(w, XY)** with isolate Z: bloc = [wX·X + wY·Y > (wX+wY)/2]; O' = 1 if (wX+wY)·bloc + wZ·Z > T/2,
  0 if < T/2, O if = T/2; A'=B'=C'=O.
- **precoalition(w)**: O' = 1 if wA·A + wB·B + wC·C > T/2, 0 if <, O if =; A'=B'=C'=O.
- Weights by type: 1 (1,1,1); 2 (3,2,2); 3 (1,2,2); 4 (3,1,1); 5 (4,3,2); 6 (4,2,1); 7 (3,2,1); 8 (2,1,1).
