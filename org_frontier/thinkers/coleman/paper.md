# The price of closure: Coleman's social capital under exact Φ

## Abstract

Coleman (1988) argued on a triangle that closure — a tie between the two parties a third can harm — is what
lets them combine to sanction, makes obligations trustworthy, and makes an association a unit; and that
social capital so created is a public good its creator captures little of. Burt (1992, 2001) made the
opposite case for the open triangle, and the lab's Burt paper found closure removes the broker's value. This
paper renders Coleman's claims as Boolean forms — a party yields only to a joint sanction, a sanctioner acts
when the party defects and, under closure, when the other sanctions — and asks exact IIT-4.0 Φ what closure
does to the structure. Closure makes the norm hold: compliance with the sanction standing is a fixed point
of the closed form and of no state of the open one, and the sanctioners are one complex (H1 confirmed). On
Burt's own forms closure holds or raises the core's Φ and drives the broker's advantage to zero (H2
confirmed). The credit ring is one complex of all its members with equal value added (H4 confirmed). But the
effective norm is a looser whole than the ineffective one — the open structure is a whole of three at
Φ = 2.000, the closed one reducible with a core of the two sanctioners at 0.369 and the sanctioned party
outside — and the closer captures all of the loss (H5 refuted). Intergenerational closure makes the parents
the core and drops the children (H3 refuted). Closure buys rest, coalition, and equality at the price of
irreducibility; Coleman's public good is not Φ. Results are about Boolean models.

## Introduction

Coleman draws a triangle to explain why norms sometimes fail to arise. A has relations with B and C, and B
and C have none with each other. A "can carry out actions that impose negative externalities on B or C or
both. Since they have no relations with one another ... they cannot combine forces to sanction A in order to
constrain the actions. Unless either B or C alone is sufficiently harmed and sufficiently powerful vis-à-vis
A to sanction alone, A's actions can continue unabated" (1988, p. S105). Add the B–C tie — closure — and "B
and C can combine to provide a collective sanction, or either can reward the other for sanctioning A"
(p. S106). The rest of the paper's structural argument follows from this figure: closure makes obligations
trustworthy because a defaulter answers to all rather than one; it makes the rotating-credit association
possible; it lets parents whose children are friends reinforce each other; and, being a property of the
structure rather than of any party, it is a public good that "the actor or actors who generate ... ordinarily
capture only a small part of" (p. S119).

The lab has already computed the other side of this argument. The Burt paper found that a broker spanning
an open triangle adds Φ = 2.000 to the whole and loses everything to a single tie between its contacts; that
paper's H1 asked whether closure removes the broker and found it does. Those are Burt's questions. Coleman's
are about the collective: whether closure makes the parties one, whether the norm holds, whether the whole
gains what the broker loses. The same forms answer both, and the lab's criterion — a set is an irreducible
whole when Φ over its minimum-information partition is positive, and the major complex is the set exclusion
returns — can say what closure does to a structure as a structure.

Five hypotheses were fixed before computing. Whether closure makes the norm effective and the sanctioners
one (H1); whether, on Burt's forms, closure raises the whole and lowers the broker (H2); whether
intergenerational closure makes a community of four one (H3); whether the credit ring is one unit (H4); and
whether the party who closes captures less than the whole gains (H5). Three confirmed, two refuted, and the
refutations set the price.

## Coleman's account

Social capital "is defined by its function" (p. S98): aspects of social structure that facilitate action.
Three forms are named. Obligations and expectations: "If A does something for B and trusts B to reciprocate
in the future, this establishes an expectation in A and an obligation on the part of B. This obligation can
be conceived as a credit slip held by A for performance by B" (p. S102). Information channels. And norms
with sanctions.

The first and third depend on closure. The norm argument is the triangle above. The obligation argument is
the same structure read for trust: "Defection from an obligation is a form of imposing a negative
externality on another. Yet, in a structure without closure, it can be effectively sanctioned, if at all,
only by the person to whom the obligation is owed. Reputation cannot arise in an open structure, and
collective sanctions that would ensure trustworthiness cannot be applied" (pp. S107–S108). The
rotating-credit association is the case where trust carries the whole: n members each contribute n times
and receive once, and "a person who receives a payout early in the sequence of meetings could abscond and
leave the others with a loss" (p. S103). Intergenerational closure is the four-node version — two parents,
two children who are friends: with a tie between the parents, "parent A is reinforced by parent D in
sanctioning his child's actions; beyond that, parent D constitutes a monitor not only for his own child, C,
but also for the other child, B" (p. S107).

The public-good property closes the argument: "the actor or actors who generate social capital ordinarily
capture only a small part of its benefits, a fact that leads to underinvestment in social capital"
(p. S119).

Burt (2001) framed closure and holes as rivals and proposed closure within, brokerage beyond; Portes (1998)
reviews the concept; Granovetter (1985) is the embeddedness on which Coleman's trust rests.

## From claims to forms

A **party** is a Boolean node; a **tie** is reading. In the norm forms A's state is 1 when A complies and B's
or C's is 1 when it sanctions. A **yields only to a joint sanction**: A' = B ∧ C. A sanctioner **sanctions
when A defects**: B' = ¬A; under closure it also sanctions **when the other does**: B' = ¬A ∨ C — Coleman's
"either can reward the other for sanctioning A." The norm is **effective** when compliance with the
sanction standing, (1, 1, 1), is a fixed point. In the credit forms a member pays when the member it depends
on has paid. The **structure** is the major complex; a party's **value added** V is the core Φ of the whole
minus the core Φ with the party deleted (a deleted variable reads 0), and **positional advantage** is V minus
the largest V among the others — both the Burt paper's quantities.

Eight forms. **norm_open**: A' = B ∧ C; B' = ¬A; C' = ¬A. **norm_closed**: A' = B ∧ C; B' = ¬A ∨ C;
C' = ¬A ∨ B. Burt's **info** and **control** brokers, open and closed, imported unchanged from the Burt
paper. **parents_open**: K1' = P1 ∧ K2; K2' = P2 ∧ K1; P1' = K1; P2' = K2 — a child holds the norm when its
parent enforces and its friend holds it; a parent reacts to its own child. **parents_closed**: the parents
also read each other, P1' = K1 ∨ P2; P2' = K2 ∨ P1. **ring4**: A' = D, B' = A, C' = B, D' = C.
**two_dyads**: A ⇄ B, C ⇄ D.

The rendering's risk is the negation. B' = ¬A makes the open structure a permanent sanction cycle — A defects,
B and C sanction, A complies, they stop, A defects — which is Coleman's "continue unabated" as an
oscillation rather than a standing defection. It is the simplest reading of a sanction that responds to
defection.

## Hypotheses

Fixed in `hypotheses.md` before any form was run.

- **H1 (Coleman) — closure makes the norm effective.** (1, 1, 1) is fixed in norm_closed and not in
  norm_open; B and C are one complex under closure. *Prior:* with on the fixed point; open on Φ.
- **H2 (Coleman) — closure is the collective's gain.** On both Burt brokers, coreΦ_closed ≥ coreΦ_open and
  advantage_closed < advantage_open. *Prior:* with on advantage; open on info's core Φ.
- **H3 (Coleman) — intergenerational closure.** Closed core = all four; open core smaller; basin of 1111
  larger under closure. *Prior:* open.
- **H4 (Coleman) — the credit ring is one unit.** ring4 core = all four, V equal and positive; two_dyads no
  complex above two. *Prior:* with.
- **H5 (Coleman) — the public good.** gain = coreΦ_closed − coreΦ_open > 0 and the closer B captures a
  fraction < 1 of it. *Prior:* open.

## Methods

**Instrument.** Exact IIT-4.0 Φ (Albantakis et al., 2023) via PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018); forms through
`org_frontier.probes.lib`; the Burt forms through `org_frontier.thinkers.burt.forms.evaluate`, unchanged.
Fixed points and basins from the deterministic TPM.

**Control.** Every probe first runs the conjunctive triad (Φ = 2.000000, core {A, M, B}). All passed.

**Procedure.** Five probes, one per hypothesis; each prints the control, one line per form with value added
by party and fixed points, and a verdict. Results in `results/`, registered in `ci/reproduce.json`
(#429–#433).

**Scope.** Results are about Boolean models. No community, norm, or credit association is measured.

*Table 1. All forms. The Burt rows are the Burt paper's numbers.*

### H1 — closure makes the norm effective: CONFIRMED

The open norm has no fixed point: A defects, both sanction, A complies, both stop, A defects. Compliance
never stands. Under closure (1, 1, 1) is a fixed point — A complies and B and C hold the sanction, each
because the other does — and the major complex is {B, C}: the sanctioners are one, as Coleman says, and A
is outside. Both clauses held. The numbers around them are the paper's finding: the open structure that
cannot rest is a whole of three at Φ = 2.000 with every party's value added 2.000; the closed structure
that rests is reducible (Φ_MIP = 0) with a core of two at 0.369.

### H2 — closure is the collective's gain: CONFIRMED

On Burt's information broker, closure leaves the core's Φ at 2.000 and moves it from {E, X, Y} to {X, Y};
the broker's positional advantage goes from 2.000 to 0. On the control broker, closure raises the core's Φ
from 2.000 to 6.000 and leaves all three in it with value added 4.000 each; the broker's advantage goes from
2.000 to 0. Both clauses held for both brokers. The Burt paper reported these numbers as a partial
confirmation that closure removes the broker. Read for the collective they are Coleman's claim in full: the
whole holds or gains, and no one holds an edge over anyone.

### H3 — intergenerational closure: REFUTED

Without the parents' tie the core is one parent and one child, {P2, K2} at 2.000. With it the core is the
two parents, {P1, P2} at 2.000, and the children's value added falls to 0: the parents' coalition is the
structure and the children are its dependents. The community of four is one in neither form. The all-norm
state has a basin of one in both — only itself, because a child holds the norm only when its parent
enforces and its friend already holds it — so the basin clause could not discriminate and did not. One
clause of three held.

### H4 — the credit ring is one unit: CONFIRMED

The ring of four is one complex of all four at Φ = 2.000, every member's value added 2.000 — delete any one
and nothing remains, which is Coleman's absconder. Two bilateral credits are a pair (exclusion returns
{C, D}) with no complex above two.

### H5 — the public good: REFUTED

Closing the norm form does not raise the core's Φ. It lowers it, from 2.000 to 0.369, a gain of −1.631; B's
value added falls by the same 1.631, so B "captures" the whole change — all of a loss. The hypothesis was
stated for a gain, and there is none.

## Discussion

Closure does what Coleman says. Given the tie, the sanctioners combine — they are one complex — and the
norm holds — compliance is a fixed point. The credit ring is one unit that any absconder destroys. On Burt's
forms the broker's edge vanishes and the whole holds or grows. Three of five hypotheses confirm, and the two
that fail do not fail on the mechanism. They fail on what Coleman thought the mechanism produced.

The open norm structure is a tighter whole than the closed one. That is the finding, and it follows the
Heider paper's: tension binds, and rest dissolves. The open triangle never settles — A's defection and the
sanction chase each other round a four-state cycle — and in that permanent unrest each party's state is
irreducibly a function of the others', Φ = 2.000, all three in the core, every value added 2.000. Add the
B–C tie and the system finds a fixed point: A complies, the sanction stands. At that fixed point the
sanctioners are one, weakly (0.369), and A, who now does what it is told, is no longer a member. An
effective norm is a structure in which the sanctioned party has become a background condition. Coleman's
"they cannot combine forces" is right about the open form and the lab adds that the form they cannot
combine in is the one in which all three are bound; his "collective sanction" is right about the closed form
and the lab adds that the collective it produces is the two sanctioners, the third party outside it.

*Cross-reference.* The Ostrom paper in this series (`../ostrom/paper.md`, probes #434 and #437) runs the same
joint sanction with every party on both sides of it. The sanctioned parties then become the complex —
{c1, c2, c3} at 2.862 — and compliance rests with the sanction retired rather than standing. What ejects A
here is not the sanction but its one-way direction.

This is why H5 falls. Coleman's public good is a benefit to the whole that its creator captures little of.
In the norm form there is no benefit to the whole in Φ; there is a loss, and B who closes bears all of it in
value added — as do A and C, equally. What closure distributes is not a gain but a leveling: every party's
value added goes from 2.000 to 0.369, and no one holds an edge. That leveling is real and Burt's forms show
it too — under closure the control broker's advantage is 0 with everyone at 4.000 — and it may be what
Coleman's public good is, once Φ is the measure: not a larger whole but an equal one. Where the whole does
grow under closure is the control-broker form, in which every party reads every other disjunctively and
closure completes an OR-clique at Φ = 6. Whether closure raises Φ depends on whether the closed ties are
returns among peers or reinforcements among sanctioners; the first integrates, the second settles.

The parents' result is the same pattern one level up. Coleman's intergenerational closure is a tie between
parents that lets each monitor the other's child. Under Φ it makes the parents the complex and the children
its dependents — value added 0, outside the core, exactly as A is outside the closed norm. The community
does not become one; a coalition forms above the parties it governs. Serres's cascade in this series had
the same shape from the other side: the party that is outside the complex and indispensable to it. Here the
children are outside and dispensable, and the parents are the whole.

## Limitations

One rendering of each claim. The sanction responds to defection by negation and lifts at once on compliance,
which is what makes the open form cycle; a sanction with memory or a lag would be another form and might
rest in the open structure too. Value added reads a deleted variable as 0, which under closure makes a
sanctioner whose target is deleted a constant. H2 is a re-reading of numbers the Burt paper computed, not a
new computation. The parents form's children require both parent and friend, which gives the all-norm
state a basin of one in both forms and left H3's third clause without discriminating power. Forms are three
and four nodes; Burt's (2001) closure-within-brokerage-beyond was tested in the Burt paper, not here.

Results are about Boolean models. No community is measured.

## Conclusion

Coleman's closure lets the harmed combine, makes the norm hold, makes the association one unit, and takes
the broker's edge; the instrument confirms each. It also finds that the closed structure is a looser whole
than the open one, that the sanctioned party leaves the complex when the norm becomes effective, and that
what closure spreads among the parties is not a gain but an equal share of a smaller thing. The public good
Coleman describes is real in these forms as leveling. It is not integration.
