# Davis — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in his own words

Source: James A. Davis, "Clustering and structural balance in graphs," *Human Relations* 20(2): 181–187
(1967), cited by page. Where the paper's wording is available only through a secondary quotation, the
quoting source is named.

## C1 — Clustering generalizes balance to more than two plus-sets

Cartwright and Harary (1956) had shown that a balanced signed graph partitions into at most two "plus-sets"
— every positive line inside a set, every negative line between the two. Davis's paper opens from the
observation that groups often have more than two cliques, and defines a signed graph as *clusterable* when
its points can be partitioned into any number of subsets such that each positive line joins two points of
the same subset and each negative line joins two points of different subsets (181). Balance is the special
case of two subsets.

Structural restatement. A structure is clusterable when persons can be assigned to camps — any number of
camps — with friends in the same camp and enemies in different ones.

## C2 — The theorem: no cycle with exactly one negative line

> "A signed graph is clusterable if and only if it contains no cycle with exactly one negative line."
> (Davis 1967: 181; quoted in Doreian and Mrvar 1996: 156 as "A signed digraph is k-balanced if and only if
> it contains no semicycles with exactly one negative arc.")

Cartwright and Harary's condition for balance was no cycle with an *odd* number of negative lines. Davis
replaces odd with exactly one. The all-negative triangle — three negative lines — is forbidden by balance
and permitted by clustering. Davis notes (182, n. 2) that the statement and proof "were kindly provided by
Professors Dorwin Cartwright and Frank Harary as a substitute for the author's more lengthy and cumbersome
one."

Structural restatement. Of the four signed triangles, one — two positive lines and one negative — is
forbidden under clustering. The other three are permitted. The all-negative triangle is permitted with
three camps.

## C3 — On complete graphs, triangles decide

For a complete signed graph, Davis's condition reduces to the triangles: a complete signed graph is
clusterable iff it has no triangle with exactly one negative line (183). And the clustering of a complete
clusterable graph is unique — the plus-sets are the components of the positive subgraph, and every pair of
components is joined by negative lines.

Structural restatement. In a complete group, the camps are fixed by the structure; incomplete groups can
have more than one consistent assignment.

## C4 — The sociological reading: coalitions and crosspressure

Davis ties the formal result to his earlier sociology (Davis 1963) and to the "crosspressure hypothesis"
(184, n. 3): a person with a positive tie into each of two mutually hostile camps is the one case the
structure cannot place. Groups with several mutually hostile cliques are, for Davis, the normal case that
balance theory had ruled out.

Structural restatement. The one unplaceable position is the friend of two enemies. Enemies of enemies need
not be friends.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

The directed-graph (digraph, semicycle) version of the theorem; the extension in Davis and Leinhardt (1972)
to ranked clusters and the triad census. These add order to the camps, which the forms here do not carry.
