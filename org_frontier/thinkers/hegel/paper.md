## Abstract

Hegel's doctrine of the syllogism is a doctrine of the middle term. Two extremes hold together through a
third; a middle that only relays connects them without uniting them; a genuine middle is one through which
each extreme "joins itself together with itself"; and the complete whole is not one syllogism but three, in
which each term "runs through the determination of the middle term and the extreme." This paper renders
term as node, mediation as reading, and the syllogism as the conjunctive triad the lab uses as its control,
and reads five of Hegel's claims with exact IIT-4.0 Φ. The syllogism is a whole of three at Φ = 2.000 — the
same as the judgment, two terms and no middle (H1 refuted as pre-registered). A relaying middle leaves
Φ = 0 with every term reachable (H2 confirmed). An extreme that does not return through the middle is
outside the core (H3 confirmed). In the single syllogism the middle bears the whole and the extremes
nothing; in the system where every term is middle to the other two, each bears 4.000 of a Φ of 6.000, three
times the syllogism's (H4 partial). The rotating middle — each term middle in turn by a clock — is a whole
of three at exactly one syllogism's Φ, not the system's (H5 partial). Hegel's totality is three syllogisms
*at once*; taken in turn they are one at a time. Results are about Boolean models.

## Introduction

"Everything rational is a syllogism" (Hegel, 2010b, p. 588). Read as a claim about proofs the sentence is
false; read as Sans (2018) reads it, as a claim that "conceptual relations as such are real" (p. 192), it is a
claim about the shape of wholes, and the shape has a middle. A syllogism has three terms, and the one that
matters is the one between the others: the extremes hold together through it, and without it they have
nothing that joins them. Hegel's *Logic* then spends its length on what makes a middle genuine. The formal
syllogism's middle is "only the abstract particularity" (Hegel, 2010a, §182, p. 254), and the syllogism is
"rational but non-conceptual"; the rational syllogism's middle is one through which "the subject joins itself
together with itself" (p. 255). The objectivity section repeats the distinction in things: communication,
where a determinateness passes from object to object "without transition into the opposite" (2010b, p. 635),
against free mechanism, where the objects hold together through "the real middle term ... their objective
universality" (p. 641) and the structure is three syllogisms, "in which each of the three different objects
runs through the determination of the middle term and the extreme" (pp. 642–643).

The lab has a criterion for a middle. A set of parties is an irreducible whole when Φ over its
minimum-information partition is positive; the major complex is the set exclusion returns; a party is *in*
when it is a member. On that criterion the lab has already found what it calls the conduit-versus-integrator
line: a middle that relays leaves the parties it connects unintegrated, and a middle that conjoins them and
is read back by them makes the three one. The lab's Hegel series (`submissions/hegel_coordination/`, post 3)
mapped that line onto Hegel's arc from communication to the real middle and stopped at what its receipts
could not reach: a fixed star, one mediator with unconnected extremes, is "closer, in wiring, to Hegel's
defective middle than to his rotating one." The rotating middle — every term middle to the other two, or each
in turn — is the form this paper builds.

Five hypotheses were fixed before computing: the middle against the judgment (H1), the relaying middle (H2),
the extreme that does not return (H3), the system of three syllogisms (H4), and the alternation of middles in
time (H5). The syllogism form is the lab's control under Hegel's labels, and the paper says so where it
matters.

## Hegel's account

The *Encyclopaedia* gives the thesis flat: the syllogism is "the essential ground of everything true," and
"everything is a syllogism" (§181R, p. 254). The judgment — subject, copula, predicate — is the syllogism with
its middle hidden; the syllogism drags the copula's work into the open by naming the term through which the
link holds (Sans, 2018, p. 194).

Not every middle does that work. The formal syllogism connects its extremes through "only the abstract
particularity," an external link between terms that stay what they were, and Hegel's verdict is that it is
"rational but non-conceptual [*begrifflos*]" (§182, pp. 254–255). The objective register says the same of
mechanism's first stage. Communication "consists simply in giving to the determinateness which is generated
the form of universality — and this is communication, which occurs without transition into the opposite"
(2010b, p. 635); "laws, morals, rational conceptions in general" are "communicable entities of this kind;
they pervade individuals unconsciously imposing themselves on them" (p. 636), spreading "like a scent freely
spreading in the unresisting atmosphere" (pp. 635–636). Connection, and each term unchanged.

The genuine middle is defined by return. In the rational syllogism "the subject joins itself together with
itself by means of this mediation" (§182, p. 255); in free mechanism "the prior or the essential
determinateness constitutes the real middle term between the many interacting objects; it unites them in and
for themselves and is their objective universality" (2010b, p. 641). What goes out from a term through the
middle comes back to it, and that is what makes the middle real rather than external.

The complete whole has no fixed middle. "This totality, whose moments are themselves the completed relations
of the concept, the syllogisms in which each of the three different objects runs through the determination
of the middle term and the extreme, constitutes free mechanism" (pp. 642–643), its objects having for their
fundamental determination "the pervasive gravity that persists self-identical in the particularization" (p.
643). The *Encyclopaedia* compresses it: like the solar system, "the state is a system of three syllogisms"
(§198R, p. 273) — government, citizens, and needs each in turn the middle of the other two (2010b, p. 642).
Moss (2013) reads "runs through" temporally, as "a process of alternating middle terms" (p. 78); the text
bears that reading and the simultaneous one, and the two are different forms.

## From claims to forms

A **term** is a Boolean node. **Mediation** is reading: X is mediated by M when X's next state depends on M's.
A **syllogism** with middle M and extremes A, B: the middle reads both extremes and conjoins them, M' = A ∧ B;
each extreme reads the middle, A' = M, B' = M. This is the lab's conjunctive triad and instrument control.
**Being one** is membership in the major complex; the whole's integration is Φ. A term's **share** is what
its removal costs: Φ of the whole's core minus Φ of the core with the term and its edges deleted, an
orphaned term holding its own state.

Seven forms. **Judgment**: A ⇄ B, no middle. **Syllogism**: as above. **Communication**: A holds, M copies A,
B copies M — the relay, the source unchanged. **Scent**: A holds, M and B both copy A — the broadcast.
**Half-return**: M' = A ∧ B, A' = M, B holds — B is read by the middle and does not read back. **System**:
every term reads the other two, A' = B ∧ C, B' = A ∧ C, C' = A ∧ B — the three syllogisms at once, each term
middle to the other two. **Rotating**: A, B, C with a two-node clock cycling 00 → 01 → 10; in phase i term i
is the middle (conjoins the other two, which copy it) — the three syllogisms in turn.

The rendering's risk is the conjunction. Hegel's middle "unites" the extremes; AND is the simplest rule
under which a middle's state depends on both extremes at once, and the lab has found disjunction to behave
alike and majority to factor. The deletion rule for shares carries a second risk that materialized: a term
left with no inputs holds its state, and the instrument counts a self-holding single node as a one-node
complex at Φ = 1. The Results say where this bit.

## Hypotheses

Fixed in `hypotheses.md` before any form was run.

- **H1 (Hegel) — the middle founds the connection.** (a) M is in the core and necessary: delete M and Φ = 0
  with no complex. (b) Φ(syllogism) > Φ(judgment). *Prior:* with (a); against (b) — both forms are known to
  be 2.
- **H2 (Hegel) — the external middle connects without uniting.** Communication and scent: Φ = 0, no complex
  containing M, B reachable from A. *Prior:* with.
- **H3 (Hegel) — the real middle is one the extremes return through.** Half-return core {A, M}; syllogism
  core {A, M, B}. *Prior:* with.
- **H4 (Hegel) — the system has no distinguished binder.** Syllogism shares (A 0, M 2, B 0); system shares
  equal and positive. *Prior:* with. *Descriptive:* whether Φ(system) = 3 × Φ(syllogism).
- **H5 (Hegel, Moss's reading) — the alternation is the totality.** Rotating core {A, B, C}, clock outside;
  Φ(rotating) = Φ(system). *Prior:* with on the core, against on Φ.

## Methods

**Instrument.** Exact IIT-4.0 Φ (Albantakis et al., 2023) via PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018); whole-system Φ
as the maximum over reachable states; major complex as PyPhi's maximal complex, maximized over reachable
states; `org_frontier.probes.lib` throughout.

**Control.** Every probe first runs the conjunctive triad (Φ = 2.000000, core {A, M, B}). All five passed.

**Procedure.** Five probes, one per hypothesis; each prints the control, one line per form, share lines
where computed, and a verdict. Results in `results/`, registered in `ci/reproduce.json` (#409–#413). One
post-hoc computation — shares with orphaned terms as constants rather than holding — is reported as
descriptive.

**Scope.** Results are about Boolean models. No argument, state, or solar system is measured.

### H1 — the middle founds the connection: REFUTED

M is in the core, and removing it leaves the whole at Φ = 0. The pre-registered clause "no complex" failed:
with M deleted each extreme holds its own state, and the instrument returns a one-node complex, {B}, at
Φ = 1.000. Nothing joins the extremes; each merely is. With orphaned terms rendered as constants instead
(post hoc), the minus-M form has no complex at all. On (b) the prior held: the syllogism's Φ is 2.000 and so
is the judgment's. The middle adds a member and no integration. Hegel's claim that the judgment is a
syllogism with its middle hidden reads, on this criterion, as a claim the instrument confirms in the wrong
direction — the two are equal because the judgment already has all the integration the syllogism has.

### H2 — the external middle connects without uniting: CONFIRMED

Communication and scent have Φ_MIP = 0. In each, M and B are reachable from A, so the terms are connected;
the only complex is the source A holding itself at 1.000, and M is in no complex. The relay is Hegel's
*begrifflos* syllogism: connection, and no unity. This is the lab's conduit result under Hegel's names.

### H3 — the real middle is one the extremes return through: CONFIRMED

The syllogism's core is {A, M, B}. In the half-return form, where M reads B but B does not read M, the core is
{A, M} at 2.000 and B is out. The extreme that returns to itself through the middle is united by it; the one
that is only read is not. "The subject joins itself together with itself by means of this mediation" is, in
these forms, the membership condition.

### H4 — the system has no distinguished binder: PARTIAL

In the syllogism an extreme's removal costs nothing — delete A and {M, B} remain a dyad at 2.000 — and the
middle's costs 1.000 on the pre-registered rule, 2.000 with orphans as constants. The pattern (extremes 0,
middle everything) holds; the pre-registered number did not, for the reason given under H1. In the system
every term's removal costs 4.000 — the clique at 6.000 falls to a dyad at 2.000 whichever term is deleted —
and the shares are equal and positive as predicted. The descriptive: Φ(system) = 6.000 = 3 × 2.000. The
system of three syllogisms carries exactly three syllogisms' worth of integration, and each term carries
two-thirds of it.

### H5 — the alternation is the totality: PARTIAL

The rotating form is a whole of three: its major complex is {A, B, C} at Φ = 2.000, and the clock nodes are
outside. Its Φ is the single syllogism's, not the system's 6.000. Each term is middle in turn, and at each
moment the form is one syllogism with one middle; Φ, a property of the system in a state, sees that
syllogism and no other. Alternation in time distributes the middle's role and does not sum the syllogisms.
Simultaneity does.

## Discussion

The three confirmed or near-confirmed results are the lab's conduit line in Hegel's vocabulary, and the
vocabulary fits. The relay is *begrifflos*: connected, not one. The extreme that does not return is not
united. The middle that conjoins and is read back makes the three a whole. Hegel drew the distinction
between the external and the real middle in the logic of thought and again in the logic of the object, and
the instrument draws it at the same place both times, on wiring alone. That much the Hegel series had
already argued; the numbers here are the receipts under his labels.

What the paper adds is the two results about the system. First: the system of three syllogisms is not more
of the same thing but a different thing. A single syllogism has a binder — remove the middle and nothing
holds; remove an extreme and the other two hold as before — and the system has none: every term costs the
same to remove, 4.000 of 6.000, and each is middle to the other two at once. Hegel's "each of the three
different objects runs through the determination of the middle term and the extreme" is, read
simultaneously, the clique, and the clique's shares are the numerical form of "no term is the distinguished
binder." The additivity is the surprise: 6.000 is three times 2.000, and the totality carries exactly its
three syllogisms' integration, no more. What Hegel calls "the pervasive gravity that persists self-identical
in the particularization" has, here, a number, and it is the sum of the parts' — which is not what a Hegelian
would expect a totality to be.

Second: the reading of "runs through" matters, and the instrument decides between Moss's and the
simultaneous one. Taken as process — each term middle in turn — the rotating form is a whole of three at
one syllogism's Φ. The rotation does what Hegel says of the positions: every term occupies the middle, and
none is the fixed binder over time. It does not do what Hegel says of the totality: the whole is not
more integrated for it, and at every moment it is a syllogism with a middle and two extremes. Φ is a
property of a state, and the sum over time of what a system is in each state is not what the system is.
Alternation spreads the role; simultaneity multiplies the integration. If free mechanism is the process of
alternating middles, it is, on this criterion, one syllogism at a time; if it is the three at once, it is
the clique, and the clique is where "no distinguished binder" and "three times the integration" are both
true.

The H1 result cuts against Hegel and toward Simmel. The syllogism does not out-integrate the judgment; it
matches it, at 2.000, with one more member. What the middle buys is a third party's membership at no gain
in Φ, and what it costs is a single point of failure — remove it and the whole is gone — where the judgment
had two members each necessary and each sufficient with the other. Hegel's syllogism is the judgment's
truth in his sense; in the instrument's it is the judgment's integration spread over three, with a binder
introduced. The lab's Simmel paper found the third to be "superindividual" in the same sense: a member,
not an increase.

## Limitations

One rendering: conjunctive middle, extremes copying it, rotation by an exogenous clock. The deletion rule
let orphaned terms hold their state, and the instrument counts a self-holding single node as a one-node
complex; this turned H1(a) and the pre-registered syllogism share, and the post-hoc with constants is
reported alongside. The syllogism form is the instrument control, so H1(a), H3's second clause, and H4's
first form test a form already known to bind; the judgment, half-return, system, rotating, and both relays
are the informative forms. Rotation with a three-phase clock is one way to alternate; a state-driven
rotation was not built. The gravitating many-body system, Hegel's own case, remains unbuilt.

Results are about Boolean models. No argument, state, or solar system is measured.

## Conclusion

Hegel's external middle connects without uniting and his real middle unites what returns through it; the
instrument says both on wiring. His syllogism is a whole of three at the judgment's Φ, with a binder the
judgment lacked. His system of three syllogisms is a whole with no binder and three times the integration,
exactly — a sum of its syllogisms. And his alternation of middles, taken as process, is one syllogism at a
time; taken as three at once it is the totality. Everything rational is a syllogism; the whole is three
syllogisms at once.

## Appendix A — Forms

All in `forms.py`, little-endian. `∧` is AND; a term with no inputs holds.

- **judgment** A' = B; B' = A.
- **syllogism** A' = M; M' = A ∧ B; B' = M.
- **communication** A' = A; M' = A; B' = M.
- **scent** A' = A; M' = A; B' = A.
- **half_return** A' = M; M' = A ∧ B; B' = B.
- **system** A' = B ∧ C; B' = A ∧ C; C' = A ∧ B.
- **rotating** clock (k₁, k₂): 00 → 01 → 10 → 00 (11 → 00). Phase i: term i' = AND of the other two; the
  other two copy term i.
