# Hegel — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in his own words

Sources: *The Science of Logic* (1816), di Giovanni translation (Cambridge, 2010b), cited by page;
*Encyclopaedia Logic* (1830), Brinkmann and Dahlstrom translation (Cambridge, 2010a), cited by section and
page. Every quotation below was verified in the lab's Hegel series
([`submissions/hegel_coordination/posts/post3_syllogism.md`](../../../submissions/hegel_coordination/posts/post3_syllogism.md)),
which records the page checks; this file takes them from there.

## C1 — The middle founds the connection

> "Everything rational is a syllogism." (SL, 588)
>
> The syllogism is "the essential ground of everything true," and "everything is a syllogism." (EL §181R,
> 254)

Structural restatement. Two terms hold together through a third. The middle is what makes the extremes one;
without it they have nothing that joins them. The syllogism is the complete form of a whole, and the
judgment — two terms and a copula — is that form with its middle hidden.

## C2 — The external middle: connection without unity

> The formal syllogism connects its extremes through a middle that is "only the abstract particularity"; it
> is "rational but non-conceptual [*begrifflos*]." (EL §182, 254–255)
>
> Communication "consists simply in giving to the determinateness which is generated the form of universality
> — and this is communication, which occurs without transition into the opposite." (SL, 635) "Laws, morals,
> rational conceptions in general, are in the region of the spirit communicable entities of this kind; they
> pervade individuals unconsciously imposing themselves on them" (636), spreading "like a scent freely
> spreading in the unresisting atmosphere." (635–636)

Structural restatement. A middle that relays a determination from one term to another, each term staying
what it was, connects without uniting. The extremes remain external to the middle and to each other.

## C3 — The real middle: the extremes return to themselves through it

> "The subject joins itself together with itself by means of this mediation." (EL §182, 255)
>
> "The prior or the essential determinateness constitutes the real middle term between the many interacting
> objects; it unites them in and for themselves and is their objective universality." (SL, 641)

Structural restatement. A genuine middle is one through which each extreme closes with itself: what goes
out from a term through the middle comes back to it. A term that is only acted on through the middle, and
does not return, is not united by it.

## C4 — The rotating middle: each term middle to the other two

> "This totality, whose moments are themselves the completed relations of the concept, the syllogisms in
> which each of the three different objects runs through the determination of the middle term and the
> extreme, constitutes free mechanism." (SL, 642–643)
>
> Its objects have for their fundamental determination "the pervasive gravity that persists self-identical in
> the particularization." (SL, 643)
>
> Like the solar system, "the state is a system of three syllogisms." (EL §198R, 273) — the government, the
> individual citizens, and their needs each in turn the middle of the other two (SL, 642).

Structural restatement. The complete whole is not one syllogism with a fixed middle but three, in which each
term is the middle of the other two. No term is the distinguished binder; the integration is the totality's
and belongs to each equally.

## C5 — The alternation is a process

> Moss (2013) reads free mechanism as grasped via "a triad of syllogisms, which altogether constitute a
> process of alternating middle terms" (78) — Hegel's "runs through" (SL, 642) taken temporally.

Structural restatement. The three syllogisms may be simultaneous — every term middle at once — or
successive — each term middle in turn. Hegel's text supports both readings; the two are different forms.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

The figures of the qualitative syllogism and their transitions (SL, 588–620); the syllogisms of reflection
and necessity; chemism and teleology; the *Philosophy of Right*'s civil society (the lab's Hegel series
treats these). The ontological reading of the syllogism (Sans 2018) is taken as licence for the rendering,
not as a claim to test.
