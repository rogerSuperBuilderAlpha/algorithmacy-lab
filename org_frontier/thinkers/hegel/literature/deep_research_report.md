# Hegel — Stage 2 literature

## Primary text

The syllogism chapter of the *Science of Logic* (1816) ends on the sentence "everything rational is a
syllogism" (di Giovanni, 588), and the *Encyclopaedia Logic* (1830) restates it as "everything is a
syllogism," the syllogism being "the essential ground of everything true" (§181R, 254). The middle term is
what the doctrine is about. The formal syllogism has a defective one — "only the abstract particularity,"
a syllogism "rational but non-conceptual" (§182, 254–255) — and the rational syllogism a genuine one, through
which "the subject joins itself together with itself" (§182, 255). The objectivity section carries the same
distinction into things. Mechanism's first stage is communication, a determinateness passed from object to
object "without transition into the opposite," spreading "like a scent" (SL, 635–636). Its summit, free or
absolute mechanism, is a system whose objects hold together through "the real middle term between the many
interacting objects; it unites them in and for themselves and is their objective universality" (641), and
whose structure is "the syllogisms in which each of the three different objects runs through the
determination of the middle term and the extreme" (642–643). The *Encyclopaedia* compresses this into "the
state is a system of three syllogisms" (§198R, 273), each of government, citizens, and needs the middle of
the other two.

## What the literature did with it

**The ontological reading.** Sans (2018) argues the chapter is an ontological argument for "Hegel's
conviction that conceptual relations as such are real" (192): the syllogism is a shape of wholes, not of
proofs. Moss (2013) reads free mechanism as "a triad of syllogisms, which altogether constitute a process of
alternating middle terms" (78), a real activity of objectivity. Both license taking the syllogism as a
structural claim about how three terms hold together, and neither says what "hold together" would mean under
a criterion.

**The lab's Hegel series.** Post 3 of `submissions/hegel_coordination/` maps the conduit-to-integrator
ascent onto Hegel's communication-to-real-middle arc and stops at two limits it names: the gravitating
many-body system (unbuilt) and the rotating middle. Its receipts hold a fixed star — one mediator, outer
parties unconnected — which is "closer, in wiring, to Hegel's defective middle than to his rotating one." The
rotating triad it defers to the symmetric three-cycle of q101/q102, which has even shares and no
distinguished binder.

**Sociology of the triad.** Simmel (1902) is Hegel's nearest sociological descendant on the triad, and the
lab's Simmel paper computed his forms; the Hegelian claim that the whole is *three syllogisms*, not one, has
no counterpart there.

## The open gap

Hegel makes four structural claims and one that depends on how a phrase is read. That two terms hold
together only through a third; that a middle which relays connects without uniting; that a genuine middle is
one each extreme returns through; that the complete whole is a system in which every term is middle to the
other two and none is the binder. The fifth is whether "runs through" names a simultaneous structure or a
process in time. The lab has computed the first three in its own vocabulary and never under Hegel's terms and
labels; it has never built the system of three syllogisms as such, and never the rotating middle, which the
series flagged as the shape its fixed-star receipts do not reach. The gap is to render term as node,
mediation as reading, and syllogism as the conjunctive triad, and ask what Φ says about the judgment, the
relay, the half-return, the system, and the alternation.
