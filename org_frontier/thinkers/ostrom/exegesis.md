# Ostrom — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in her own words

Source: Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action*
(Cambridge University Press, 1990), cited by page. Wording of the design principles and the commitment
passage checked against Ostrom's own restatement in *Neither Market nor State* (1999), which reprints it.

## O1 — The monitors are the appropriators (design principle 4)

> "Monitors, who actively audit CPR conditions and appropriator behavior, are accountable to the
> appropriators or are the appropriators." (Table 3.1, p. 90)
>
> "Monitoring is a by-product of their own strong motivations to use their water rotation turn to the
> fullest extent. The fishing site rotation system used in Alanya, Turkey, is similar in that cheaters are
> observed at low cost by those who most want to deter another cheater at that particular time and
> location." (p. 95)

Structural restatement. There is no separate sanctioner. Each appropriator both complies and watches the
others; whoever is sanctioned is also a monitor of the ones who sanction.

## O2 — Contingent self-commitment

> "Individuals who think a set of rules will be effective in producing higher joint benefits and that
> monitoring will protect them against being a sucker are willing to make a contingent self-commitment of
> the following type: 'I commit myself to follow the set of rules we have devised in all instances except
> dire emergencies if the rest of those affected make a similar commitment and act accordingly.'" (p. 100)

Structural restatement. Compliance is conditional on the others' compliance, not on a standing sanction.

## O3 — Compliance rests with sanctions rarely used

> "In long-enduring institutions, monitoring and sanctioning are undertaken primarily by the participants
> themselves. The initial sanctions used in these systems are surprisingly low." (p. 94)
>
> Levi's "quasi-voluntary compliance": individuals "choose to comply in many situations where they are not
> being directly coerced." (p. 94)

Structural restatement. The steady state is compliance with the sanction retired; sanctions fire on a
lapse and then withdraw.

## O4 — Three solutions: Leviathan, privatization, self-governance

Chapter 1 sets out the "only way" arguments — a central authority that monitors and sanctions ("Leviathan,"
pp. 8–12) and the division of the commons into private holdings (pp. 12–13) — and the book's cases are the
third way: appropriators who devise, monitor, and enforce their own rules (pp. 14–21). The Alanya fishers'
rotation (pp. 18–21) is the first case.

Structural restatement. Central enforcement is a star with the enforcer at the center; privatization severs
the ties; self-governance is a clique of contingent commitments.

## O5 — Nested enterprises (design principle 8)

> "Appropriation, provision, monitoring, enforcement, conflict resolution, and governance activities are
> organized in multiple layers of nested enterprises." (Table 3.1, p. 90; discussion pp. 101–102)

Structural restatement. Local groups keep their own rules and are also bound into a larger unit; the layers
are semi-autonomous, neither dissolved into the whole nor severed from it.

## O6 — Clearly defined boundaries (design principle 1)

> "Individuals or households who have rights to withdraw resource units from the CPR must be clearly
> defined, as must the boundaries of the CPR itself." (Table 3.1, p. 90)

Structural restatement. An outsider may watch or take from the commons, but the enterprise's rules do not
depend on him; a breached boundary is one in which a member's compliance turns on an outsider's conduct.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

Graduated sanctions (principle 5) need a sanction with memory and a source of lapses; the natural rendering
is a stochastic counter and is deferred. Congruence with local conditions (principle 2), collective-choice
arrangements (3), conflict resolution (6), and recognition of rights to organize (7) are institutional, not
structural. The game-theoretic models of chapter 1 and the framework of chapter 6 are not modeled.
