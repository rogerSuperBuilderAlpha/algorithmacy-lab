# The excluded producer: Serres's parasite under exact Φ

## Abstract

Serres's *The Parasite* makes five structural claims about the third party: the one-way arrow from host to
parasite is the atom of relation; a chain of parasites has a last position that wins; noise gives rise to a
new system where the chain had none; the noise on a channel is excluded from the relation and yet included
in it; and a circulating quasi-object makes a collective of those who pass it. This paper renders each as
a Boolean form — taking as reading, producers as constants, noise as a node the receiver XORs with the
sender — and asks exact IIT-4.0 Φ which parties are the structure. The arrow binds nothing (H1 refuted, as
the lab predicted). The theorem holds: the chain has no complex and the interrupting noise creates one, at
the last position, with the producer and the tax farmer outside (H2 confirmed). The token ring binds all
three players and the stopped token none (H5 confirmed). Two refutations carry the finding. Noise from
outside does not lower the pair's Φ but destroys it at every noise rate including zero, because the
instrument's cause side infers a background input's past from the present and a coin's past leaves no trace;
the system's own noise is outside the complex (H3 refuted). And in a cascade where each level ejects the one
above, the complex is the whole cascade less the producer, and the shares invert Serres: the producer,
outside the complex, costs everything to delete; the noise, inside it at the last position, costs nothing
(H4 refuted). Membership and indispensability come apart, and the one who is excluded and indispensable is
the one who produces. Results are about Boolean models.

## Introduction

Serres's book begins at a table. The tax farmer eats what the farmer grew; the city rat eats the tax
farmer's leftovers; the country rat eats at the city rat's invitation; a noise at the door scatters them.
"The noise, the ultimate parasite, through its interruption, wins the game. In the parasitic chain, the last
to come tries to supplant his predecessor" (1982, p. 4). From the fable he draws a figure — the parasite,
who takes without giving, in three senses at once: "an abusive guest, an unavoidable animal, a break in a
message" (p. 8) — and a set of claims about how the figure structures relations. The relation is one-way,
and it is elementary: "just one relation appears, the simple, irreversible arrow" (p. 8), "the atomic form
of our relations" (p. 8). Chains of it have a last position. Noise, the parasite on a line, makes systems:
"noise gives rise to a new system, an order that is more complex than the simple chain" (p. 14). The third
on a channel is excluded from the communication and included in it. And an object passed among players
"makes the collective, if it stops, it makes the individual" (p. 225).

Each of these is a claim about which parties are one and which are outside, and the lab has a criterion for
that. A set of parties is an irreducible whole when Φ over its minimum-information partition is positive,
and the major complex is the set exclusion returns. The lab's standing result that a reader not read back
does not bind puts it against Serres on the arrow. On the rest the lab has no prior, and the roster
question for Serres was exactly the one his theorem raises: a third that only perturbs the channel — does
noise on the edge bind or factor?

Five hypotheses were fixed before computing. The arrow (H1); the theorem (H2); the excluded third (H3); the
last position (H4); the quasi-object (H5). Two confirmed, three fell, and the two that fell in ways not
anticipated are the paper.

## Serres's account

The parasite takes; the host gives; the direction never changes. "There is no exchange, nor will there be
one. Abuse appears before use. ... this relation is of the simplest sort; there is none simpler or easier:
it always goes in the same direction. The same one is the host; the same one takes and eats; there is no
change of direction. This is true of all beings. Of lice and men" (p. 7). Exchange — the two-way tie of
economics and of Simmel's dyad — is for Serres a later construction on this one-way base.

Parasites chain. The farmer produces; "the tax farmer produced neither oil nor ham nor cheese; in fact, he
produced nothing. But using power or the law, he can profit from these products. Likewise for the city rat
who takes the farmer's leftovers. And the last to profit is the country rat" (p. 3). Each level acts back on
the level above: "a given parasite seeks to eject the parasite on the level immediately superior to his
own" (p. 4). The noise is last and wins; and the cascade "collapses when P1 = P4" (p. 4) — when the chain
closes on itself.

Noise makes systems. "The noise temporarily stops the system, makes it oscillate indefinitely. To eliminate
the noise, a nonstop signal would be necessary; then the signal would no longer be a signal and everything
would start again, more briskly than usual. Theorem: noise gives rise to a new system, an order that is more
complex than the simple chain. This parasite interrupts at first glance, consolidates when you look again"
(p. 14). The chapter titled "The Excluded Third, Included" carries the thesis over from *Hermès*: two who
communicate do so by excluding the noise between them, and the noise is what the channel is made of. "The
bit of noise, the small random element, transforms one system or one order into another" (p. 21).

The quasi-object makes the collective. "This quasi-object is not an object, but it is one nevertheless,
since it is not a subject, since it is in the world; it is also a quasi-subject, since it marks or
designates a subject who, without it, would not be a subject. ... The quasi-object, when being passed, makes
the collective, if it stops, it makes the individual. ... The moving furet weaves the 'we,' the collective;
if it stops, it marks the 'I'" (p. 225). It is "an astonishing constructor of intersubjectivity" (p. 227).

Brown (2002, 2013) reads the parasite as Serres's operator of translation and carries it into organization
theory as the relation exchange theory cannot see. Shannon (1948) is the picture Serres inverts: noise on
a line, valued the other way.

## From claims to forms

A **party** is a Boolean node. **Taking** is reading: the parasite's state is a function of the host's. A
**producer** at the origin of a chain is a constant 1 — it always produces and reads nothing. The Hegel and
Girard papers found that a *held* origin is a one-node complex at Φ = 1, an artifact that would confound
every chain form here; the constant avoids it. **Noise** on a channel is a node the receiver XORs with the
sender: B' = A ⊕ N. It is **exogenous** when N is a fair coin each step and **endogenous** when the system's
own activity makes it, N' = A ∧ B — the noise of the meal. The **structure** is the major complex; a term's
**share** is its deletion cost on the core's Φ, orphaned terms rendered as constants 0.

Nine forms. **Arrow**: F = 1; P' = F. **Exchange**: A ⇄ B. **Chain**: F = 1; T' = F; R' = T. **Chain +
noise**: the chain with N' = R (the feast wakes the house) and R' = T ∧ ¬N (the noise stops the feast).
**Cascade**: every level ejects the one above — T' = F ∧ ¬R; R' = T ∧ ¬N; N' = R. **Dyad**, **dyad +
exogenous noise**, **dyad + endogenous noise**. **Passing**: the token ring A' = C, B' = A, C' = B.
**Stopped**: the token frozen at A — constants 1, 0, 0.

## Hypotheses

Fixed in `hypotheses.md` before any form was run.

- **H1 (Serres) — the arrow is the atom of relation.** The arrow is a complex containing host and parasite
  with Φ > 0. *Prior:* against.
- **H2 (Serres) — noise gives rise to a new system.** The chain has no complex; chain + noise has one that
  contains N and excludes F and T. *Prior:* with.
- **H3 (Serres) — the excluded third, included.** Exogenous noise: core {A, B} with Φ below the clean
  dyad's 2. Endogenous noise: N in the core. *Prior:* with on exogenous; open on endogenous.
- **H4 (Serres) — the last position.** Cascade core ⊇ {R, N}, excluding F and T; share(N) ≥ share(R);
  share(T) = 0. *Prior:* open.
- **H5 (Serres) — the quasi-object.** Passing: core {A, B, C}, Φ > 0. Stopped: no complex. *Prior:* with.

## Methods

**Instrument.** Exact IIT-4.0 Φ (Albantakis et al., 2023) via PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018); deterministic
forms through `org_frontier.probes.lib`; the coin-noise form on its stochastic TPM through the Granovetter
paper's machinery, maximized over reachable states. Shares by deletion.

**Control.** Every probe first runs the conjunctive triad (Φ = 2.000000, core {A, M, B}); the probe using
the stochastic path also runs it through that path (2.000000). All passed.

**Procedure.** Five probes, one per hypothesis. Two post-hoc checks after the verdicts: the exogenous noise
swept over ε ∈ {0, .05, .1, .25, .4, .5} (N' = 1 with probability ε), and shares in the chain + noise form.
Results in `results/`, registered in `ci/reproduce.json` (#419–#423).

**Scope.** Results are about Boolean models. No fable, table, or channel is measured.

### H1 — the arrow is the atom of relation: REFUTED

The arrow binds nothing. A parasite reading a producer who does not read back has Φ = 0 and no complex; the
exchange A ⇄ B has Φ = 2.000. This is the lab's standing result and it was the prior. Serres's "atomic form
of our relations" is, on this criterion, not a relation — it is the absence of one, and the exchange he
calls derivative is where relation begins.

*Cross-reference.* The Blau paper in this series (`../blau/paper.md`, probe #439) puts the same arrow under
Blau's condition that the supplier's service be contingent on the receiver's compliance. The constant
supplier binds nothing, as here; the contingent one is a complex of two at 2.000. What the arrow lacks is a
second direction of reading, and Blau's power has it where Serres's parasite does not.

### H2 — noise gives rise to a new system: CONFIRMED

The chain farmer → tax farmer → rat has no complex: three parties, two arrows, nothing returns. Add the
noise — the rats' feast wakes the house (N' = R) and the noise stops the feast (R' = T ∧ ¬N) — and a
complex appears: {R, N} at 2.000, the rat and the noise. The producer and the tax farmer are outside it.
Serres's theorem is exact here: the interruption is the chain's first and only return, and the new system
sits at the last position, made of the last parasite and the one it interrupts.

### H3 — the excluded third, included: REFUTED

Both clauses fell, differently. Noise from a coin does not lower the pair's Φ; it removes the pair. The
dyad with an outside N that the receiver XORs has no complex at all — and, in the post-hoc sweep, none at
any ε from 0.5 down to 0. The mechanism is the instrument's cause side. PyPhi's backward TPM infers a
background unit's past state from the present state of the whole network; a coin's past leaves no trace in
the present, so on the cause side B's input N is maximally uncertain, B' = A ⊕ N carries no information about
A, and the pair has nothing to bind on. At ε = 0 the coin never comes up heads, and the verdict is the
same: a channel that an outside unit *could* flip carries no cause information about its sender. The
system's own noise, N' = A ∧ B, is a different case: the whole is integrated at Φ = 2.000, and exclusion
returns {A, B} at 2.000 with N outside. The third the system makes for itself is excluded, and the pair's
integration is what it was without it.

### H4 — the last position: REFUTED

In the cascade where every level ejects the one above, the complex is not at the last position. It is
{T, R, N} at 2.000 — the tax farmer, the rat, and the noise, the whole cascade except the producer. And the
shares invert the claim. Deleting F, the constant producer outside the complex, leaves T' = 0 ∧ ¬R = 0, and
the cascade collapses: share 2.000. Deleting T or R likewise: 2.000 each. Deleting N, the noise at the last
position, inside the complex, leaves T ⇄ R as a negation dyad at 2.000: share 0. The last to come is the
one who costs nothing. In the single-level form (chain + noise) every term's share is 2.000, the noise
included; it is the reaction one level up — T ejecting R — that makes the noise redundant, because the
return it supplied is now supplied from above.

### H5 — the quasi-object weaves the "we": CONFIRMED

The token passing round the ring makes the three players one complex at Φ = 2.000. The token stopped at A
makes none. "The moving furet weaves the 'we'; if it stops, it marks the 'I'" is, in the forms, exact: the
collective is the circulation and nothing else.

## Discussion

Three of Serres's claims survive contact with the criterion — the theorem, the quasi-object, and the
distinction he draws between the arrow and exchange, though he values it the other way. The chain is
nothing and the interruption makes it something; the circulating token makes a whole and the stopped token
none; and the arrow, which he calls the atom, is on this criterion no relation at all, while exchange is
Φ = 2. On the arrow the instrument sides with Simmel against Serres. A parasite that takes and is not read
back leaves the host exactly as it was, and a relation that leaves one party untouched is, for a
criterion of irreducibility, not one.

The two refutations are where the instrument says something Serres did not. The first is about noise from
outside. The pre-registered picture was Shannon's: a noise source on the line lowers what gets through,
and Φ should fall with ε. It does not fall; it is zero throughout, including at ε = 0. What the instrument's
cause side asks of a channel is whether the receiver's present state tells us about the sender's past, and
it answers that question by inferring the past of everything the receiver reads — including units outside
the candidate whole — from the present. An outside unit whose past leaves no trace in the present is, for
that inference, a coin regardless of its actual statistics, and a receiver who XORs it with the sender has
lost the sender. This is a strong reading of "the excluded third": a channel that is *open* to a third is
already parasitized, whether or not the third ever acts. Serres's line that "to eliminate the noise, a
nonstop signal would be necessary" is close to what the instrument does — a channel is clean only if nothing
outside it could have been on it. The system's own noise is a different matter, and the difference is
what the endogenous form shows: N' = A ∧ B has a past the present constrains, and the pair keeps its Φ = 2
with N as an excluded background. The third the system makes for itself is excluded and harmless; the third
from outside is excluded and fatal.

The second refutation is about the cascade, and it produces the paper's title. Serres puts the noise last
and says the last position wins. The instrument puts the noise inside the complex and finds that it costs
nothing to remove, and puts the producer outside the complex and finds that removing it costs everything.
Two properties that the lab has mostly seen move together — membership in the complex and indispensability
to it — come apart here, and they come apart along Serres's own axis. He wrote that "the first member of
the chain must be excluded" (p. 5) — the peasant, who leaves for the countryside and does not return to the
feast. The forms agree that the producer is excluded: a constant has no mechanism, and F is in no complex.
They add that the excluded producer is the one whose absence dissolves the whole, and that the noise Serres
crowns is, once the levels above it react on their own, the one member the whole can spare. The "excluded
third, included" is real in the cascade, and it is not the noise. It is the host at the bottom of the
chain, who gives and is not read, and without whom there is nothing to take.

Across the series this closes a pattern. Girard's mediator was the one term in every complex; Bowen's
third displaced an insider; Simmel's *tertius* profited from a pair it did not join. Serres's parasite is
the first third in the series that the instrument finds *inside* the whole and *dispensable* to it — and
the first form in which the party outside every complex is the one the complex cannot do without.

## Limitations

One rendering of each claim. Producers are constants; a held producer would be a one-node complex, and a
producer that read its parasites would be a different form. Noise is XOR; multiplicative or gating noise
would be other forms. The exogenous result is a property of the instrument's cause side and is reported as
found; a different treatment of background units — conditioning on their current state on the cause side as
on the effect side — would give the Shannon picture, and the lab does not adjudicate between them here.
Serres's "the cascade collapses when P1 = P4" was not tested; the passing ring of H5 is a closed chain of
takers, and it binds — whether that is what he meant by collapse is not decidable from the forms.
Escalation, oscillation "indefinitely," and the thermal-exciter chapters have no Boolean rendering.

Results are about Boolean models. No fable, feast, or channel is measured.

## Conclusion

Serres's arrow is not a relation the instrument can see, and his theorem, his quasi-object, and his
excluded-included third are — with the last one turned around. Noise from outside does not weaken a channel
but, on the instrument's cause side, voids it at any rate including zero; noise the system makes for itself
is excluded and harmless. In the cascade the whole is every parasite and not the producer, and the producer,
outside the whole, is the one term it cannot lose, while the noise at the last position, inside it, is the
one it can. The excluded third that the whole includes by depending on it is the host.
