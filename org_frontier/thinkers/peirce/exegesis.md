# Peirce — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in his own words

Peirce's account of the triad is a thesis in the logic of relations, stated across forty years and never in
one place. The citations below use the standard *Collected Papers* (CP) volume.paragraph convention and the
*Essential Peirce* (EP) volume:page where the passage is there; the 1897 *Monist* article is cited by page.
Each claim is quoted, then restated in structural terms, and labelled C1–C5 for `hypotheses.md`.

## C1 — Triadic irreducibility: a genuine triad is not a complexus of dyads

> "The triadic relation is genuine, that is its three members are bound together by it in a way that does
> not consist in any complexus of dyadic relations." (CP 2.274; EP2: 272–273; 1903)

> "…when we attempt to imitate this proceeding with dual relatives, and combine two of them by means of an
> X, we find we only have two blank places in the combination." (CP 1.363; c. 1887–88)

> "…it is sufficient to say that no branching of a line can result from putting one line on the end of
> another." (CP 8.331; 1904)

Structural restatement. A relation among three terms is genuine when no assembly of two-term relations
reproduces it. Composing dyads end to end yields chains and rings, never a fact that binds three terms at
once. In a dynamical rendering: a system in which every element is determined by exactly one other element
contains only two-term causal facts, however it is wired.

## C2 — Degeneracy has grades: monadic, dyadic, genuine

> "Every triad is either monadically degenerate, dyadically degenerate, or genuine. A monadically degenerate
> triad is one which results from the essence of three monads, its subjects. A dyadically degenerate triad
> is one which results from dyads. A genuine triad is one which cannot be resolved in any such way." (CP
> 1.473; c. 1896)

> "That A is father of B and B father of C is a genuinely dyadic degenerate triad. … A is mother of B and B
> is wife of C. Here the two component dyads are more independent of one another. This is a purer case of
> the dyadic degenerate triad." (CP 1.473)

Structural restatement. Three terms with independent properties are a monadically degenerate triad; a chain
of two dyads sharing a middle term is a dyadically degenerate one; only a fact irreducibly about all three
is genuine. The three grades are ordered by the largest number of terms any single irreducible fact in the
arrangement binds: one, two, three.

## C3 — Giving: the type case of a genuine triad

> "Take, for example, the relation of giving. A gives B to C. This does not consist in A's throwing B away
> and its accidentally hitting C … If that were all, it would not be a genuine triadic relation, but merely
> one dyadic relation followed by another. There need be no motion of the thing given. Giving is a transfer
> of the right of property." (CP 1.345; 1903)

> "It does not consist [in] A's putting B away from him and C's subsequently taking B up. … It consists in
> A's making C the possessor according to Law. … But now suppose that giving did consist merely in A's
> laying down the B which C subsequently picks up. That would be a degenerate form of Thirdness in which the
> thirdness is externally appended." (CP 8.331; 1904)

Structural restatement. In genuine giving the receiver's possession is determined jointly by the giver's
act and the thing given, under one rule — the law. In the degenerate imitation the thing's state is
determined by the giver and the receiver's by the thing, two dyads in sequence. The sequence may run and
still contain no three-term fact.

## C4 — The sign relation is a genuine triad

> "A Sign, or Representamen, is a First which stands in such a genuine triadic relation to a Second, called
> its Object, as to be capable of determining a Third, called its Interpretant, to assume the same triadic
> relation to its Object in which it stands itself to the same Object." (CP 2.274; EP2: 272; 1903)

> "…by 'semiosis' I mean, on the contrary, an action, or influence, which is, or involves, a coöperation of
> three subjects, such as a sign, its object, and its interpretant, this tri-relative influence not being in
> any way resolvable into actions between pairs." (CP 5.484; 1907)

Structural restatement. The object determines the sign; the sign determines the interpretant, and does so
with respect to the object, so that the interpretant's determination is a three-term fact. Peirce's
dynamical object determines and is not determined: it is the source of the chain. The claim is that the
three are nonetheless "bound together" and the influence is not "resolvable into actions between pairs."

## C5 — Polyadic reducibility: tetrads and higher are compounds of triads

> "…the tetrad 'A sells C to B for the price D' is analyzed into 'A makes with C a certain transaction E'
> and 'E is a sale of B for the price D'." (CP 1.363, paraphrased in Koshkin; the analysis is Peirce's)

> The reduction thesis in its two clauses: "some triadic relations cannot be reduced to combinations of
> monadic and dyadic ones" and "all tetradic and higher adicity relations can be reduced to combinations of
> triadic ones." (Koshkin's statement of Peirce's thesis; the primary sources are CP 1.363, CP 3.144, CP
> 3.423–424, and the 1897 *Monist* article.)

Structural restatement. Three-term facts compose into four-term facts; two-term facts do not compose into
three-term facts. The triad is the smallest building block from which relations of every higher adicity
can be built, and the dyad is not a building block for the triad. In a dynamical rendering: elements each
determined by two others can produce an irreducible four-term fact; elements each determined by one other
cannot produce a three-term fact.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

Peirce's phenomenological categories — Firstness, Secondness, Thirdness as modes of being — and his claim
that "every genuine triadic relation involves thought or meaning" (CP 1.345) are about what triads *are*,
not about which terms they bind; a Boolean form carries the second and not the first. The doctrine of
hypostatic abstraction, by which the "transaction E" is minted as a new term, is the mechanism of C5 and is
rendered only as the presence of a mediating element.
