# Simmel — Stage 2 literature

The primary text is the 1902 two-part essay in the *American Journal of Sociology*, "The Number of Members as
Determining the Sociological Form of the Group" [simmel1902number1; simmel1902number2], in Albion Small's
translation, which Simmel revised into chapter 2 of *Soziologie* [simmel1908soziologie]; Wolff's 1950
translation of that chapter is the version most English readers cite [simmel1950]. Both 1902 parts are
public domain and were read in full for the exegesis. Page references in `exegesis.md` are to the 1902
*AJS* pagination where the text carries a page marker, and otherwise to the part.

## What the secondary literature did with the triad

Three lines took up Simmel's triad, and none of them computed irreducibility.

**Coalition theory.** Caplow read Simmel's dyad-plus-one as a coalition problem and derived which pair forms
under each distribution of power [caplow1956coalitions; caplow1968two]. Mills ran the first small-group
experiments on three-person power relations and then drew a set of testable hypotheses directly from Simmel
[mills1953power; mills1958hypotheses]. The question in this line is which pair forms; the question here is
whether the three are one irreducible whole at all.

**Network sociology.** Krackhardt built the Simmelian tie — a reciprocated strong tie embedded in a clique —
as the structural counterpart to Simmel's claim that the triad constrains the individual more than the dyad
does, and set it against Burt's structural hole [krackhardt1999ties; burt1992structural;
tortoriello2010activating]. Krackhardt and Handcock contrasted Simmelian and Heiderian triadic dynamics in
network models [krackhardt2008heider; heider1946attitudes]. Gould and Fernandez and Obstfeld formalized the
third's positions and orientations [gould1989structures; obstfeld2005tertius]; q214 (probe #368) ran those
through the bypass-counterfactual and found the *gaudens* family contingent at margin 2.0 and the mediator
necessary.

**Small-group experiment.** Mills's 1953 groups and the coalition experiments that followed Caplow measured
behavior; the structural claim — that three are a whole two are not — was the premise, never the dependent
variable.

## The open gap

No study renders Simmel's own claims about the dyad–triad difference, the majority, the mediator–arbitrator
distinction, and the *tertius gaudens* as explicit dynamical models and tests whether an irreducibility
criterion returns his verdicts. The gap is open. The five hypotheses in `hypotheses.md` fill it at n = 3–5 with
exact IIT-4.0 Φ [albantakis2023iit4; mayner2018pyphi].
