# Simmel — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in his own words

The source is the 1902 *American Journal of Sociology* essay in two parts [simmel1902number1;
simmel1902number2], read in full. Each claim below is quoted, then restated in structural terms — what the
claim says about which parties are bound to which — and given a label C1–C5 that `hypotheses.md` picks up.
Page numbers are given where the 1902 text carries them; otherwise the part.

## C1 — The dyad has no superindividual energy; the triad has, through the "broken line"

> "…the decisive factor in this case is that each must actually perform something, and that, when he
> refuses to do this, only the other remains, without any superindividual energy such as, even in the case
> of a combination of only three, is in some measure present." (I: 45)

> "Where three elements, A, B, C, constitute a community, there is added to the immediate relationship
> which exists, for example, between A and B, the immediate relationship which they gain by their common
> relation to C. … apart from the bond by the straight and shortest line; each pair of elements are now
> joined by a broken line." (I: 45)

Structural restatement. In a dyad the whole is exhausted by the two; remove the tie and nothing collective
remains. In a triad each pair is bound twice — directly and through the third — so the whole is a
superindividual unit and the pair survives the loss of its direct tie.

## C2 — The first numerical step is the decisive one

> "…the type which presented the duality of the elements in general as so decisive that further numerical
> increase did not change it in a marked degree … A third and fourth member of the alliance would produce no
> further essential variation after the principal change had once occurred." (II)

> "…in a combination of two there is no majority which can override the individual, and that occasion for
> such a majority is given so soon as a single unit is added." (II)

Structural restatement. The passage from two to three changes the form of the group in kind; later additions
change it in degree. Read on a graded quantity, the increment at the 2→3 step should dominate.

## C3 — The majority overrides the individual

> "Relationships, however, in which the domination of an individual by a majority is possible, not merely
> depress the individuality, but, so far as they are voluntary, they will not be sought by the qualitative
> individuality…" (II)

Structural restatement. Once three are present a majority can determine the individual's position against
his will; the individual is bound into a whole he does not control. Rendered as a rule: each member's next
state is the majority of the three.

## C4 — The nonpartisan: mediator or arbitrator

> "In that case the mediator will either secure a consensus of the other two colliding elements, in which
> instance the mediator seeks to eliminate himself, and only to bring to pass that the two disunited or
> ununited parties may unite directly; or he acts as arbitrator and attempts to reconcile to each other the
> conflicting claims…" (II)

> "…the mediator's office is, then, … to constitute a sort of central station which, in whatever form the
> controverted material may come in from one side, may give it out to the other side only in objective
> form…" (II)

> "In the case of the ideal mediator, the ending of the conflict rests finally in the hands of the parties
> themselves. By choice of the arbitrator they have put this ultimate decision out of their own hands. …
> It has become a person in the arbitrator…" (II)

> "…very many intermediate grades are produced." (II)

Structural restatement. Three positions on one scale. The arbitrator determines the outcome and the parties
adopt it: the decision is his, so he is bound into the whole. The mediator filters what passes between the
parties but "holds himself this side of actual decision": the parties keep their own will and read him. The
successful mediator has brought the parties to "unite directly" and is no longer read by anyone. Simmel's
"intermediate grades" say the mediator binds, but less than the arbitrator.

## C5 — The *tertius gaudens* and the balance of forces

> "In both cases the advantage of nonpartisanship, with which the tertius originally stood in antithesis
> with the other two, consists in the fact that he can set his own conditions for the decision." (II: 177)

> "…the necessary amount of the energy which he must have for the purpose is determined exclusively by the
> relationship which the energies of the parties exhibit toward each other. Evidently all that is necessary
> is that the addition of his reserve force to one of these shall give to the same a preponderance. When,
> therefore, the quantities of force are practically equal, a minimum of addition often suffices in order to
> give a final decision to one of the sides." (II)

> "It is merely necessary that the energies of two antagonistic elements paralyze each other, in order that
> the never so weak position of the unattached third party may attain to unlimited strength." (II)

Structural restatement. The third's leverage is a function of the contestants' relative weights, not of his
own. When they are balanced his vote decides; when one preponderates his vote is idle. Rendered as a weighted
outcome rule with the third's weight fixed and the contestants' weights varied.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

*Divide et impera* ("the third instigates the difference intentionally, in order to gain a controlling
situation," II: 187) and the *gaudens*'s dependence on the parties' failure to combine were classified in q214
(probe #368) as contingent irreducibility at margin 2.0. The paper cites that result. Simmel's remarks on the
dyad's intimacy, jealousy, and the triad's "three parties" of feeling (I: 46) are about affect and are outside
what a Boolean form can carry.
