# Latour — Stage 2 literature

## Primary texts

*Reassembling the Social* (2005) is the systematic statement of actor-network theory, organized as five
"sources of uncertainty" about groups, action, objects, facts, and the writing of accounts. Two passages
carry the theory's structural content. Page 39 draws the line between intermediary and mediator — the one
"transports meaning or force without transformation," a "black box counting for one"; the other transforms,
and "might count for one, for nothing, for several, or for infinity." Page 71 gives the criterion of
participation: whatever "does modify a state of affairs by making a difference is an actor," and the test is
a trial that detects the difference. The book's closing move (pp. 216–217) inverts the word "actor" itself:
an actor-network is "what is made to act by a large star-shaped web of mediators flowing in and out of it";
"attachments are first, actors are second."

"On Technical Mediation" (1994) is the earlier and more vivid statement. Its first meaning of mediation is
*translation*, illustrated by the citizen and the gun: the Neutral Tool and the Autonomous Destiny are
"symmetrical" myths, each making one party an intermediary of the other; the third possibility is "a new goal
that corresponds to neither agent's program of action," and the actor is "someone else (a citizen-gun, a
gun-citizen)."

## What the literature did with it

**The ANT line.** Law (1992) gave the theory its most compact statement — the social is "nothing other than
patterned networks of heterogeneous materials," and an actor is "a patterned network of heterogeneous
relations" that has been "punctualised" into a single point. Punctualization is Latour's black-boxing: a
network that behaves predictably is counted as one. Callon's scallops and Latour's own Pasteur are the
canonical cases. The line's method is to open black boxes and follow the mediators; it has no way to say,
of a given assemblage, whether it is one thing or several except by following controversies about it.

**The critics.** Sayes (2014) asks what it means to say nonhumans have agency and finds four senses in the
ANT corpus, of which the weakest — a nonhuman is an actant when it makes a difference — is the one Latour
defends on page 71; the stronger senses (nonhumans as stabilizers, as mediators of moral force) depend on
it. Elder-Vass (2015) argues that ANT's refusal of structure leaves it unable to say why some networks hold
and others do not, and that "assemblage" smuggles in a notion of whole that the theory officially denies.
Harman's *Prince of Networks* (2009) reads Latour as a metaphysician of relations for whom an actant is
exhausted by its alliances.

**Organization studies.** ANT entered the field through Callon and Law and became a method for tracing how
technologies and standards enroll users. The intermediary/mediator line is used constantly and measured
never; "does this device mediate or merely transmit" is answered by ethnography case by case.

## The open gap

Latour's two structural claims — that an intermediary counts for one however many parts it has, and that a
mediator counts for one, none, or several — are claims about how many participants a configuration has.
The lab's criterion answers that question directly: the major complex is the set of parties the whole cannot
do without, and its size is the count. The lab also has two standing results that bear on Latour's line.
The first, probe #19, says a conveyor factors and only a joint determination is triadic — which is Latour's
line drawn with the lab's pen. The second, from the Peirce paper (probe #374), says a cycle of one-input
copies is an irreducible whole with every node in the core — which says an intermediary in a loop counts for
one more, not for one. Whether Latour's intermediary is the lab's conveyor or the lab's copy-in-a-ring
depends on whether the intermediary sits in a loop, and Latour's "star-shaped web of mediators flowing in
and out" is all loops. Page 71's trial — does it make a difference — is the lab's membership counterfactual
with one clause missing: the lab requires that a party be read *and* return, and the trial tests only the
first. Elder-Vass's complaint that ANT cannot say what holds is a request for exactly this computation. The
gap is to run it: chains of intermediaries, mediators of two kinds, a source that makes a difference and is
not returned to, the citizen-gun in its three readings, and the star.
