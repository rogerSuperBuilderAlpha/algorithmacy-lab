# Latour — Stage 1 exegesis: the claims in his own words

Sources: Bruno Latour, *Reassembling the Social: An Introduction to Actor-Network-Theory* (Oxford University
Press, 2005), cited by page; and "On Technical Mediation," *Common Knowledge* 3(2), 1994, 29–64, cited by
page. The 2005 passages were checked against three independent quotations of the printed text (the book is
in copyright); the 1994 passages against the reprint quoted at length in the secondary literature.

## C1 — Intermediary: transports without transformation; counts for one

> "An intermediary, in my vocabulary, is what transports meaning or force without transformation: defining
> its inputs is enough to define its outputs. For all practical purposes, an intermediary can be taken not
> only as a black box, but also as a black box counting for one, even if it is internally made of many
> parts." (2005: 39)

Structural restatement. An intermediary is a party whose next state is a function of one input alone. It
adds nothing to the structure it sits in: a chain of intermediaries between two parties is, for the whole,
the same as a direct tie, and however many parts it has, it counts as one — or as none.

## C2 — Mediator: transforms; input does not predict output; counts for one, nothing, several

> "Mediators, on the other hand, cannot be counted as just one; they might count for one, for nothing, for
> several, or for infinity. Their input is never a good predictor of their output; their specificity has to
> be taken into account every time. Mediators transform, translate, distort, and modify the meaning or the
> elements they are supposed to carry." (2005: 39)
>
> "No matter how apparently simple a mediator may look, it may become complex; it may lead in multiple
> directions which will modify all the contradictory accounts attributed to its role." (2005: 39)

Structural restatement. A mediator's next state is not a function of its one input: something else enters
— another party, or the mediator's own state ("its specificity"). A mediator is a member of the structure
it sits in; its presence changes what the whole is.

## C3 — The actor is whatever makes a difference

> "Any thing that does modify a state of affairs by making a difference is an actor — or, if it has no
> figuration yet, an actant. Thus, the questions to ask about any agent are simply the following: Does it
> make a difference in the course of some other agent's action or not? Is there some trial that allows
> someone to detect this difference?" (2005: 71)
>
> "For all the other members of society, it does make a difference under trials and so these implements,
> according to our definition, are actors, or more precisely, participants in the course of action waiting
> to be given a figuration." (2005: 71)

Structural restatement. The criterion of participation is counterfactual: a party is a participant when
some other party's next state would differ were it otherwise. Whatever passes that trial is in.

## C4 — Translation: the citizen-gun

> "Agent 1 falls back on Agent 2, here a gun. Agent 1 enlists the gun or is enlisted by it — it does not
> matter which — and a third agent emerges from a fusion of the other two. ... If it returns, after its
> detour, to Goal 1, then the ... gun is a tool, merely an intermediary. If agent 3 drifts from Goal 1 to
> Goal 2, then the ... gun's intent, the gun's will, the gun's script have superseded those of Agent 1; it
> is human action that is no more than an intermediary. ... The myth of the Neutral Tool under complete
> human control and the myth of the Autonomous Destiny that no human can master are symmetrical. But a
> third possibility is more commonly realized: the creation of a new goal that corresponds to neither
> agent's program of action. ... I call this uncertainty about goals translation." (1994: 31–32)
>
> "Who, then, is the actor in my vignette? Someone else (a citizen-gun, a gun-citizen). ... You are another
> subject because you hold the gun; the gun is another object because it has entered into a relationship
> with you. ... Neither subject nor object (nor their goals) is fixed. ... It is neither people nor guns that
> kill. Responsibility for action must be shared among the various actants." (1994: 32–34)

Structural restatement. Three readings of one situation. Neutral tool: the act follows the citizen, the gun
carries it. Autonomous destiny: the act follows the gun, the citizen carries it. Translation: the act is a
joint product of both and neither's program alone, and each is changed by the association. The first two
are structurally alike with the roles swapped; the third is a different structure in which both are
participants.

## C5 — The actor-network: attachments first, actors second

> "What's wrong with the word [actor] is not that it is often limited to humans — this limit we have learned
> to encroach upon — but that it always designates a source of initiative or starting point, the extremity
> of a vector toward some other end." (2005: 216)
>
> "An actor-network is what is made to act by a large star-shaped web of mediators flowing in and out of
> it. It is made to exist by its many ties: attachments are first, actors are second." (2005: 217)
>
> An actor "is not the source of an action but the moving target of a vast array of entities swarming toward
> it." (2005: 46)
>
> Translation is "a relation that does not transport causality but induces two mediators into coexisting."
> (2005: 108)

Structural restatement. The unit of action is the star — the center together with the mediators that flow
into and out of it — not the center. A center attached to mediators is a whole with them; a center attached
to intermediaries is a center alone, since intermediaries count for one.

## Claims not carried to hypotheses

The four meanings of mediation beyond translation — composition, reversible black-boxing, delegation (1994:
32–39); the five sources of uncertainty as a method (2005: 21–25); the symmetry principle as a
methodological refusal of a priori asymmetry (2005: 76); and the "flat" topology of sites (2005: 165–172).
These are prescriptions for how to describe, not claims about what a structure is.
