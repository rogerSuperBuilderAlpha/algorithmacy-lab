## Abstract

Latour draws one structural line through actor-network theory: an intermediary "transports meaning or force
without transformation" and counts "for one, even if it is internally made of many parts," while a mediator
transforms and "might count for one, for nothing, for several, or for infinity." Whatever "makes a
difference" is an actor; an actor-network is "a large star-shaped web of mediators flowing in and out." This
paper renders those claims as small Boolean forms — copy-relays for intermediaries, joint or self-referring
rules for mediators, Boolean influence for making a difference — and reads each with exact IIT-4.0 Φ and the
major complex. Five hypotheses were fixed before computing, Latour's prediction beside the lab's prior.
Latour's intermediary counts for one in Φ and for one more in membership: a chain of k copy-relays inserted
into a mutual dyad leaves Φ at 2.000 for k = 0..3 and puts every relay in the core (H1 refuted). Both kinds
of mediator are in the core, and so is the intermediary; the joint mediator evicts one of the parties it
mediates (H2 partial). Making a difference is necessary for membership and not sufficient: a source that
determines the whole and is determined by nothing but itself is outside it (H3 refuted). The citizen-gun's
translation form binds citizen, gun, and act; the autonomous-destiny form leaves the gun alone; the
neutral-tool form binds the gun it was meant to demote and is structurally identical to translation (H4
partial). The star of mediators is a whole of four at Φ = 5.000 and out-integrates the star of
intermediaries at 3.000, which is also a whole of four (H5 confirmed). Latour's line sorts Φ, not
membership: a mediator adds integration, an intermediary adds a member and no integration. Results are
about Boolean models, not about any network.

## Introduction

Actor-network theory has one distinction it cannot do without. Everything else in Latour's method — follow
the actors, open the black boxes, refuse to explain the social by the social — depends on being able to say,
of a given party in a given chain, whether it transports or transforms. The party that transports is an
intermediary. It can be treated as a black box, and "a black box counting for one, even if it is internally
made of many parts" (Latour, 2005: 39). The party that transforms is a mediator, and mediators "cannot be
counted as just one; they might count for one, for nothing, for several, or for infinity." Sociology of the
social, Latour says, treats the world as intermediaries and a few social forces; sociology of associations
treats every party as a potential mediator and counts afresh each time.

Counting is the structural content of the distinction. To say an intermediary counts for one is to say that a
chain of them between two parties is, for the whole, the same as a direct tie. To say a mediator may count for
several or for nothing is to say that a mediator's presence changes how many participants a configuration
has, in either direction. Latour never computes these counts — the method forbids deciding in advance and
prescribes following controversies instead — but the claims are about how many parties a structure has and
which ones. Elder-Vass (2015) makes the complaint that ANT, having refused structure, cannot say why some
networks hold and others dissolve. Sayes (2014) finds that the theory's strong claims about nonhuman agency
rest on its weakest: a nonhuman is an actant when it makes a difference.

The lab has a criterion that answers counting questions directly. A configuration of parties with update
rules is an irreducible whole when its integrated information Φ is positive, and its major complex — the
subset the whole cannot be cut from without loss — is the set of parties that count. The lab also has two
standing results that speak to Latour's line from opposite sides. Probe #19 found that a conveying medium
factors and only a joint determination is triadic: a relay is a wire. The Peirce paper (probe #374) found
that a cycle of one-input copies is an irreducible whole with every node in the core: a relay in a loop is a
member. Which of these is Latour's intermediary depends on whether it sits in a loop, and Latour's networks
are all loops — "mediators flowing in and out."

This paper puts five of Latour's claims through the criterion. The intermediary chain (H1), the mediator
against the intermediary between the same two parties (H2), the criterion of making a difference (H3), the
citizen-gun in its three readings (H4), and the star of mediators against the star of intermediaries (H5).
Latour's account and its rendering come first, then the hypotheses, the methods, the results, and what they
say about where his line falls.

## Latour's account

Latour's *Reassembling the Social* (2005) is organized as five sources of uncertainty about the social, and
its structural claims sit in the second and third. The intermediary/mediator distinction (p. 39) is
introduced to explain what is wrong with the "sociology of the social": it treats most parties as
intermediaries, whose outputs are given by their inputs, and reserves transformation for a few social forces.
The sociology of associations reverses the default. An intermediary, "for all practical purposes," is a
black box counting for one. A mediator's "input is never a good predictor of their output; their specificity
has to be taken into account every time," and "no matter how apparently simple a mediator may look, it may
become complex; it may lead in multiple directions."

The criterion of participation comes with the third uncertainty, that objects too have agency (p. 71). Latour
lists kettles, knives, baskets, hammers, and asks whether the verbs — boil, cut, hold, hit — designate
actions. His answer is a trial: "any thing that does modify a state of affairs by making a difference is an
actor." The two questions to ask about any agent are whether it "make[s] a difference in the course of some
other agent's action" and whether "there [is] some trial that allows someone to detect this difference."
What passes is a participant, "waiting to be given a figuration."

The actor-network itself is defined at the end (pp. 216–217), against the word "actor," which "always
designates a source of initiative or starting point." An actor is instead "the moving target of a vast array
of entities swarming toward it" (p. 46), and "an actor-network is what is made to act by a large star-shaped
web of mediators flowing in and out of it. It is made to exist by its many ties: attachments are first, actors
are second." Translation, the relation among mediators, "does not transport causality but induces two
mediators into coexisting" (p. 108).

The earlier essay "On Technical Mediation" (1994) gives translation its canonical case. A citizen with a goal
is interrupted and falls back on a gun; "a third agent emerges from a fusion of the other two." If the fused
agent pursues the citizen's goal, the gun is "merely an intermediary"; if it pursues the gun's, "it is human
action that is no more than an intermediary." These are the Neutral Tool and the Autonomous Destiny, and
Latour calls them "symmetrical" myths. The third possibility, translation, is "a new goal that corresponds to
neither agent's program of action," and the actor is "someone else (a citizen-gun, a gun-citizen)." Each is
changed by the association: "you are another subject because you hold the gun; the gun is another object
because it has entered into a relationship with you." Responsibility "must be shared among the various
actants."

## From claims to forms

The rendering takes Latour's words for the two kinds of party literally. An **intermediary** is a node whose
rule reads one other node and copies it: I' = X. Its input defines its output. A **mediator** is a node whose
rule reads more than its one input. Two renderings of "more": another party enters (M' = A ∧ B, the joint
mediator), or the mediator's own state enters (M' = A ⊕ M, the specific mediator — "their specificity has to
be taken into account every time"). Both make the output underdetermined by the one input.

**Making a difference** is Boolean influence: node j makes a difference to node i when there is a
configuration at which flipping j's state flips i's next state. This is Latour's trial with the trial made
exhaustive. **Counting** is membership in the major complex: a node counts for one when it is in the core, for
nothing when it is not.

Five families of forms follow. `chain(k)` (H1) inserts k intermediaries into the tie of a mutual dyad:
A' = B, I₁' = A, I_j' = I_{j−1}, B' = I_k, so that k = 0 is the dyad A ⇄ B and k = 3 is a five-node ring. The
**third** forms (H2) put one node M between A and B in a cycle — A' = B, B' = M — and vary only M's rule:
copy, joint, or self-referring. The **difference** forms (H3) contrast a source S (S' = S; M' = S ∧ A;
A' = M), which determines M at every step and is determined by nothing but itself, with a spectator (S' = M;
M' = A; A' = M), which reads and is not read, and with the control triad in which every node does both. The
**citizen-gun** forms (H4) put citizen C, gun G, and act X in three wirings: neutral tool (G' = C, X' = G,
C' = X — the gun carries intent to the act), autonomous destiny (G' = G, X' = G, C' = X — the gun's script
runs and the citizen watches), and translation (X' = C ∧ G, C' = X, G' = X — the act needs both and reshapes
both). The **star** forms (H5) attach a center A to three nodes that A reads jointly, A' = M₁ ∧ M₂ ∧ M₃, and
vary the three: mediators, M_i' = A ∧ M_{i+1}, each transforming the center with a neighbor — translation as
"inducing two mediators into coexisting" — or intermediaries, M_i' = A.

Each family holds everything fixed except the thing Latour's claim turns on.

## Hypotheses

Fixed in `hypotheses.md` before any form was run; the lab's prior beside each.

- **H1 (Latour) — the intermediary counts for one.** Φ(chain k) = Φ(chain 0) for k = 1..3 and no relay is in
  any core. *Prior:* against, on the Peirce copy-ring result.
- **H2 (Latour) — the mediator counts, the intermediary does not.** M in both mediator cores, M out of the
  intermediary core. *Prior:* half with Latour — the joint mediator is the lab's committing third — and the
  intermediary expected in too.
- **H3 (Latour) — the actor is whatever makes a difference.** Membership equals the set of nodes with
  influence on some other node. *Prior:* against, on sufficiency: the source makes a difference and is
  expected outside.
- **H4 (Latour) — the citizen-gun.** Translation binds {C, G, X}; the neutral tool drops G; autonomous destiny
  drops C. *Prior:* with Latour on translation and on the autonomous form; against on the neutral tool, whose
  gun is a relay in a loop.
- **H5 (Latour) — attachments first.** The mediator star binds all four and out-integrates the intermediary
  star. *Prior:* with Latour on the mediator star; open on the intermediary star's core.

Decision rules are in `methods.md`.

## Methods

**Instrument.** Exact IIT-4.0 Φ (Albantakis et al., 2023) via PyPhi (Mayner et al., 2018), through the lab's
`org_frontier.probes.lib`: `verdict` for whole-system Φ_MIP and `major_complex` for the core and its Φ,
maximized over reachable states. Boolean influence over all 2ⁿ inputs for difference-making.

**Control.** Every probe first runs the conjunctive triad (A' = M, M' = A ∧ B, B' = M) and requires
Φ = 2.000000 with core {A, M, B}. All five passed.

**Forms.** As in the previous section and Appendix A; two to five nodes; synchronous deterministic update.

**Procedure.** Five probes, one per hypothesis. Each prints the control line, one line per form (form,
Φ_MIP, core, core Φ, the set of nodes that make a difference), and a verdict against the decision rule. Results
are saved to `results/` and registered in `ci/reproduce.json` (probes #389–#393).

**Scope.** Results are about the Boolean models. No network, laboratory, or gun is measured.

### H1 — the intermediary counts for one: REFUTED

The dyad A ⇄ B has Φ = 2.000 and core {A, B}. Inserting one, two, or three copy-relays into its tie leaves Φ
at 2.000 exactly. That is the half of Latour's claim the instrument confirms: however many parts the tie is
made of, the whole is no more integrated. The other half fails at every k. Each relay is in the core — the
three-node ring binds {A, I₁, B}, the five-node ring {A, I₁, I₂, I₃, B} — and cutting any of them costs the
whole its Φ. An intermediary in a loop does not count for one. It counts for one more.

### H2 — the mediator counts, the intermediary does not: PARTIAL

Between the same A and B, the specific mediator (M' = A ⊕ M) is in the core with both parties, {A, M, B}, at
Φ = 2.000. The joint mediator (M' = A ∧ B) is in the core too, but the core it is in is {M, B}: A has been
evicted. A' = B makes A a delayed copy of B, and once M reads B directly as well as through A, the whole can be
cut from A at no cost. The mediator's arrival changed the count from three to two — Latour's "counts for one,
for nothing, for several" in one form, with the party that counts for nothing being not the mediator but the
one it displaced. The intermediary is in the core with both, {A, M, B}, at the same Φ = 2.000 as the
mediators. Mediators count; so does the intermediary; the sort fails on its side, as the prior said.

### H3 — the actor is whatever makes a difference: REFUTED

The source S has influence on M at every step — M' = S ∧ A cannot be computed without it — and the whole
{S, M, A} has Φ_MIP = 0.000. Its major complex is {M, A}, Φ = 2.000. S makes a difference to the whole and
is not in it. The spectator, which reads M and is read by nothing, makes no difference and is likewise outside
{M, A}. In the control every node makes a difference and every node is in. Across the three forms every core
member makes a difference — the trial is necessary — and one node makes a difference without being a member.
Latour's trial detects one direction. Membership requires the other as well: to be read, and to return.

### H4 — the citizen-gun: PARTIAL

Translation (X' = C ∧ G, C' = X, G' = X) binds {C, G, X} at Φ = 2.000: the act is a joint product and both
parties are reshaped by it, and the whole cannot be cut from either. Autonomous destiny (G' = G, X' = G,
C' = X) leaves the gun alone. Its whole has Φ_MIP = 0.000, and its major complex is {G}, a single self-reading
node at Φ = 1.000: the citizen who only watches is outside, and so is the act, which follows the gun and
returns nothing to it. The neutral tool (G' = C, X' = G, C' = X) is the surprise, and it is the H1 surprise
again: the gun that "merely" carries intent is a relay in a three-node loop, and the loop binds {C, G, X} at
Φ = 2.000 — the same Φ, the same core, as translation. The two myths are not symmetrical on this criterion.
Making the gun a tool leaves it a participant; making the citizen a spectator removes him.

### H5 — attachments first: CONFIRMED

The star of mediators binds all four, {A, M₁, M₂, M₃}, at Φ = 5.000, and the star of intermediaries binds all
four at Φ = 3.000. The inequality Latour needs holds by a margin of 2.000, and the mediator star is a whole of
the center with its web. The intermediary star is also a whole of four. Three copies of A that A reads back
conjunctively are not a black box counting for one; they are three members, and the whole's Φ is 3.000
against the 2.000 of the dyad A relays to itself through one copy.

## Discussion

The results sort into one pattern. Latour's line between intermediary and mediator is real on the instrument,
and it runs through Φ rather than through membership. Every intermediary form that closes a loop puts its
intermediaries in the core (H1, H2, H4 neutral tool, H5). Every mediator form does too. What separates them
is the integration of the whole: the intermediary chain adds members at constant Φ = 2.000 from k = 0 to k = 3,
while the mediator star lifts Φ from the intermediary star's 3.000 to 5.000. A mediator adds integration. An
intermediary adds a member and no integration. Latour's "counting for one" is exactly right about Φ and
exactly wrong about membership, and his own phrase for the mediator — it may count "for one, for nothing, for
several" — turns out to describe what the joint mediator does to the party beside it (H2), where M's entry
evicted A.

The lab's two standing results are reconciled by this. Probe #19's conveyor factors because it sits in an
open chain: a relay that is read and does not return, or returns nothing that is read, is a wire. The Peirce
copy ring binds because the relay is in a loop, read on one side and returning on the other. Latour's
intermediary is an open-chain concept — a channel, a black box between input and output — applied in a theory
whose networks are all loops. His method says to follow the mediators "flowing in and out," and once a relay
is in the flow it is a member.

H3 states the missing clause. Latour's trial asks whether a party makes a difference to some other party's
action. The source passes and is outside; the spectator fails and is outside; the control's nodes pass and
are inside. Membership requires both that a party make a difference and that a difference be made to it —
that it be read and return. This is the lab's standing finding 3, and it is what Sayes (2014) was reaching for
when he found ANT's strong claims about nonhuman agency resting on the weak criterion of difference-making:
the weak criterion admits every exogenous cause. A speed bump makes a difference to every car and is
determined by none of them; on Latour's trial it is a participant, on this criterion it is a source outside
the whole it shapes. Which is the right answer depends on what one wants the word "participant" to do, but
the two criteria come apart on a definite class of cases, and the class is the one Latour's examples are
drawn from.

The citizen-gun (H4) is where Latour's symmetry claim meets the asymmetry of reading and returning. The two
myths are symmetrical in his diagram — swap the agents and the picture is the same. On the instrument they
are not, because the neutral tool keeps the gun in the loop (the act follows the gun, the citizen follows the
act, the gun follows the citizen) and the autonomous destiny takes the citizen out of it (the gun follows
only itself). What makes a party an intermediary in Latour's sense — carrying another's program — does not
remove it from the whole. What removes it is having nothing returned to it. The neutral tool and translation
are structurally identical here, both Φ = 2.000 with core {C, G, X}, so the rendering does not distinguish
"the gun carries the citizen's goal" from "a new goal belongs to neither." That is a limit of a
three-node deterministic form: translation's mark, "a new goal that corresponds to neither agent's program,"
is about which attractor the whole reaches, not about whether it is a whole, and the two forms reach
different attractors while being wholes of the same shape.

H5 is the confirmation, and it is confirmed for a reason Latour would accept and by a margin he would not have
predicted. The center attached to mediators is a whole with them, and more integrated than the center
attached to intermediaries. But the center attached to intermediaries is also a whole with them. "Attachments
are first, actors are second" holds in both stars: the center A alone is never a complex, and the four
together always are. What the mediator adds is the Φ, and Φ is the quantity Latour's vocabulary has no word
for. His counts are of members. The instrument's count of members agrees with him wherever a loop closes and
disagrees with his "counting for one" everywhere, and it supplies, beside the count, a graded quantity that
does what he wanted the count to do.

Elder-Vass's (2015) complaint — that ANT cannot say why some assemblages hold — has an answer in this
vocabulary. An assemblage holds when it is a complex, and how strongly is its Φ. Latour's refusal to decide in
advance which parties are mediators is compatible with this: the instrument decides after the fact, from the
rules, and its verdicts are not what a prior sorting of parties into two kinds would give.

## Limitations

The forms are small — two to five nodes — deterministic, and synchronously updated. An intermediary was
rendered as a one-input copy; a relay with delay, noise, or a threshold would be a different form and might
factor. Two renderings of "mediator" were run; Latour's "for infinity" has no rendering here. The citizen-gun
was rendered once per reading, and the neutral tool's identity with translation may be an artifact of the
three-node cycle rather than a fact about the readings. Boolean influence over all inputs is a stronger trial
than Latour's, which is empirical and situated; a party with influence only at unreachable configurations
would pass this test and might fail his. Every intermediary tested sits in a loop, because Latour's networks
do; the open-chain conveyor that factors is the lab's probe #19 and was not rerun here.

Results are about Boolean models. No actor-network, laboratory, device, or weapon is measured, and nothing
here says that any of them is or is not an irreducible whole.

## Conclusion

Latour's intermediary counts for one in Φ and for one more in membership: a chain of relays leaves the
whole's integration unchanged and enlarges its core by one per relay. His mediator counts as he said it
might — for one, or for nothing on the part of the party it displaces — and it adds what the intermediary
does not, integration. His trial for participation is necessary and not sufficient: a source that makes a
difference to everything and is determined by nothing but itself stands outside the whole it drives. His two
myths are not symmetrical, because carrying another's program keeps a party in the loop and watching does
not. His star of mediators is a whole of four and more integrated than its intermediary twin, which is also a
whole of four. The line he drew is real. It falls between two amounts of integration, not between two kinds
of member.

## Appendix A — Forms

All in `forms.py`.

- **chain(k)**, nodes (A, I₁..I_k, B): A' = B; I₁' = A; I_j' = I_{j−1}; B' = I_k.
- **third**, nodes (A, M, B): A' = B; B' = M; M' = A (intermediary), A ∧ B (mediator_joint), A ⊕ M
  (mediator_specific).
- **difference**, nodes (S, M, A): source S' = S, M' = S ∧ A, A' = M; spectator S' = M, M' = A, A' = M;
  control S' = M, M' = S ∧ A, A' = M.
- **citizen-gun**, nodes (C, G, X): neutral_tool C' = X, G' = C, X' = G; autonomous C' = X, G' = G, X' = G;
  translation C' = X, G' = X, X' = C ∧ G.
- **star**, nodes (A, M₁, M₂, M₃): A' = M₁ ∧ M₂ ∧ M₃; star_mediators M_i' = A ∧ M_{i+1} (mod 3);
  star_intermediaries M_i' = A.
