# Thinkers on the triad — one paper per thinker

A series that reads the history of thought on the third party and puts each thinker's account through the
lab's instrument. For each: the claims in the thinker's own words from the primary texts, a rendering of each
claim as a small Boolean form, hypotheses fixed before computing — the thinker's prediction beside the lab's
prior — and exact IIT-4.0 Φ to say which claims the irreducibility criterion recovers, refines, or refutes.
The form of the paper is fixed in [`PAPER_STANDARD.md`](PAPER_STANDARD.md); the process is the six-stage
protocol in [`../protocol/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`](../protocol/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md).

The series complements q214 (probe #368), which classified the brokerage literature's nineteen triad *types*.
Here the unit is the thinker, and the question is whether what that thinker said about which parties are
bound to which holds on a computable criterion.

## Roster — candidates and the claim each would contest

A thinker qualifies when the primary text makes at least three distinct structural claims about a
three-party arrangement, and at least one disagrees with a standing lab result or with another thinker in the
series. The first roster (Simmel through Serres) is complete. Candidates for a second, roughly in order of
fit:

| thinker | primary text | the structural claims | what it would contest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harrison White (1963 / 2008) | *An Anatomy of Kinship*; *Identity and Control* | roles as compound relations; the triad as the smallest role structure | whether role composition (A's B's C) shows up as a complex |
| Karen Cook et al. (1983) | "The distribution of power in exchange networks" | power follows alternatives, not centrality; negatively vs positively connected networks | the Emerson H1 finding that dependents are interchangeable — whether a central actor in a 5-line network has an edge under Φ |
| Georg Simmel, second pass (1908) | *Soziologie*, ch. 3 on superordination and subordination | subordination under a person, a plurality, and a principle; the leveling of the ruled | the Blau H4 finding that the legitimate superior is dispensable to the structure that obeys him |
