# Emerson — Stage 3 hypotheses (fixed before computing)

## The rendering that all five share

An **actor** is a Boolean node; **dependence** of X on Y is X reading Y. **Alternatives** are a disjunction
of sources: an actor with two sources is served by either. The **C–A–B network** (E3) is A served by B or C,
each of whom is served by A alone: A' = B ∨ C; B' = A; C' = A. This is the unbalanced relation of E2 — B and
C have no alternatives, A has two. The four operations are one change of reading each: **withdrawal**, B
stops reading A (B' = B); **extension**, B and C read each other (B' = A ∨ C, C' = A ∨ B — Emerson's "closing
the C-A-B network"); **status-giving**, A comes to read B alone (A' = B); **coalition**, B and C act only
together and A is denied alternatives (A' = B ∧ C, B' = A ∧ C, C' = A ∧ B). **Value added** V and
**positional advantage** are the Coleman paper's quantities (a deleted variable reads 0). A relation is
**balanced** in the instrument's terms when the stronger actor's positional advantage is 0.

## H1 — The unbalanced network (E1)

- **H1 (Emerson).** In the C–A–B network A's positional advantage is positive, A's value added equals the
  core's Φ, and B's and C's value added is 0: A is the whole's indispensable member and the dependents add
  nothing.
- **H0.** A's advantage ≤ 0, or a dependent adds.
- **Lab prior.** With (the Blau paper's unorganized form).

## H2 — Withdrawal balances (E2, operation 1)

- **H2 (Emerson).** When B stops reading A, B leaves the complex and A's positional advantage falls to 0.
- **H0.** B remains a member, or A keeps an advantage.
- **Lab prior.** Open.

## H3 — Extension balances and keeps three actors (E2 operation 2, E3)

- **H3 (Emerson).** With the B–C tie, A's positional advantage is 0, A remains in the core, and all three are
  co-members with equal value added.
- **H0.** A keeps an advantage, or leaves the core, or the members' V differ.
- **Lab prior.** With (the Burt paper's closed control broker: Φ 6, every V 4).

## H4 — Status-giving balances the A–B relation only (E2, operation 3)

- **H4 (Emerson).** When A comes to read B, V(A) = V(B) and both are in the core, while C's value added is 0.
- **H0.** V(A) ≠ V(B), or C adds.
- **Lab prior.** With.

## H5 — Extension and coalition balance differently (E3)

- **H5 (Emerson).** Both extension and coalition bring A's advantage to 0; extension lowers A's value added
  below its C–A–B value (it "reduces the power of the stronger actor"); coalition raises B's and C's value
  added above their C–A–B value (it "increases the power of weaker actors").
- **H0.** Either operation leaves A an advantage, or the two operations move V in the same direction.
- **Lab prior.** Open on the directions; the Burt closed form suggests every V rises under extension.

## Not tested here

Cost reduction; cohesion; legitimation as a case of operation 4 (tested on Blau's terms in the Blau paper);
role and status structure.
