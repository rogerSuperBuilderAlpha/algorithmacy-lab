# Bowen — Stage 2 literature

## Primary text

Bowen's triangle enters print in 1966 as a clinical concept — "when emotional tension in a two-person system
exceeds a certain level, it 'triangles' a third person, permitting the tension to shift within the triangle"
(1978: 174) — and is promoted in the 1976 theory paper to the "molecule or the basic building block of any
emotional system" and "the smallest stable relationship system" (373). The 1976 statement supplies the
positional account: in calm, "a comfortably close twosome and a less comfortable outsider," the outsider
seeking togetherness with one insider; in tension, the uncomfortable insider moving first, the outside
position becoming the desired one, and, when the three cannot hold it, a fourth triangled by the same move
so that "the emotional forces duplicate the exact patterns in the new triangle" (373–374). The clinical
corollary is detriangling: a third who stays in contact with both without reacting lets the twosome's tension
resolve (174–175). Kerr and Bowen (1988) systematize the account and add the phrase the secondary literature
now repeats — a triangle "can contain much more tension" than a dyad because tension "can shift around three
relationships."

## What the literature did with it

**Family systems.** Titelman's (2008) collection is the fullest treatment and reads the triangle as process
rather than structure: constant motion of distancing and pursuing, with the inside–outside pattern as the
structural residue. Minuchin (1974) and Haley (1967) offer the neighboring concepts — cross-generational
coalitions, the perverse triangle — that treat the third's involvement as pathology rather than as the
system's ordinary stabilizer. None of this literature states what "stable" means in a form that could be
computed; stability is what a system does when it does not break down under anxiety.

**Sociology of the triad.** Caplow (1956) and Simmel before him supply the coalition and *tertius* readings
of the same configuration; the lab's Caplow and Simmel papers computed those. Bowen differs from both in
making anxiety, not power or advantage, the variable that moves the third in and out.

**Organization studies.** Bowen's triangle appears in organizational consulting (leadership, family
business) as a diagnostic — who is triangled by whom — and not as a structural claim about the unit of a
system. The claim that the triangle is the *unit* of any emotional system, "in the family or any other
group," has not been tested against any criterion of unithood.

## The open gap

Bowen makes three claims that are structural and computable once "stability" and "togetherness" are given a
form. That the triangle is the smallest stable system is a claim about which configurations keep their
integrity under disturbance. That calm has a twosome and an outsider, and tension involves the third, is a
claim about membership and its shift. That a system of four is a set of interlocking triangles is a claim
about what exclusion returns. The lab has the pieces — flip noise as disturbance (q71, Q6–Q8), a major
complex as the unit, state-contingent membership (q96) — and has never run them on Bowen's forms. The gap is
to render togetherness as reading and anxiety as noise, and ask what Φ says about the dyad, the triangle,
the positions, the interlock, and the neutral third.
