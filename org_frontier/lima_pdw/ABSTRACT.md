## 1. Motivation Letter

Dear Co-Editors of *Organization Studies* and *Organization Theory*:

I am applying for the Lima PDW because my dissertation has reached a stage where editorial
positioning matters more than additional empirical collection. My research develops *algorithmacy*
as a new communication competency construct for the triadic form of coordination that generative AI
introduces to knowledge work. The empirical site where I am measuring this competency is GauntleTT,
an intensive AI engineering cohort I co-designed and manage with the Trinidad government and several
NGOs on the island.

My theoretical contribution rests on three claims.. First, the triadic form of
worker-algorithm-counterpart coordination differs structurally from the dyadic models upon which
traditional communication theory was built; constructs inheriting this dyadic assumption predictably
fail when applied to generative systems. Second, while existing constructs (e.g., algorithm
sensemaking, AI literacy, AI-mediated communication) capture parts of this interaction, none specify
the communication competency the form requires of the worker. Finally, *algorithmacy* names this
missing competency, demonstrating that it develops through participation in intensive AI-mediated
coordination rather than through standard tool instruction.

I would benefit most from feedback on theoretical positioning and journal fit. The construct's
natural home is *Organization Studies* or *Organization Theory*, given the algorithmic management
literature both journals have built and the methodological pluralism both invite. My advisors at
Bentley include Pierre Berthon and Mareike Möhlmann, both of whom have shaped the construct's initial
architecture. I also look forward to engaging the Latin American and Caribbean scholarly community;
the empirical work depends on that community, and the contribution the dissertation makes should be
formed by it.

## 2. Extended Abstract

**Algorithmacy: A communication competency construct for triadic mediated coordination, with
empirical evidence from a Caribbean AI engineering cohort**

**Research question and purpose.** Why do equivalently positioned participants achieve divergent
coordination outcomes when working through identical algorithmic systems? Recent research documents a
clear engagement binary: workers using the same AI tools at the same intensity experience radically
different outcomes (Bedard et al., 2026; Brynjolfsson, Li, & Raymond, 2025; Dell'Acqua et al., 2026;
Microsoft Corporation, 2025). A structural variable other than the volume of technology used
determines whether an algorithmic tool exhausts a worker or sustains them. This dissertation builds
and validates a communication competency construct that explains this variance.

**Theoretical framing.** Triadic mediated coordination places an active third party between two human
parties, operating on objectives neither human endorsed. An Uber algorithm scores inputs against the
platform's optimization function and updates the matching rule as it processes the next interaction
(Möhlmann et al., 2021). Generative AI introduces this exact architecture to knowledge work. This
*co-optation* by an algorithmic apparatus pursuing its own objectives stands as a fourth coordination
mechanism alongside hierarchies, markets, and networks (Stark & Vanden Broeck, 2024).

Existing human-AI interaction constructs carry the dyadic structural assumptions of pre-algorithmic
communication theory. Computer-mediated communication treats the algorithm as a channel (Spitzberg,
2006). Human-machine communication treats the algorithm as a partner (Guzman & Lewis, 2020).
AI-mediated communication treats the algorithm as an agent operating in service of the worker
(Hancock et al., 2020). Algorithm sensemaking captures cognitive interpretation without addressing
coordination with a counterpart (Möhlmann et al., 2023). AI literacy and algorithmic competency
measure technical knowledge rather than the competency to coordinate through the system (Long &
Magerko, 2020; Zhou et al., 2025).

*Algorithmacy* is the competency required to coordinate effectively with a human counterpart through
an opaque, adaptive intermediary. The construct is theoretically operationalized across five specific
components: *asymmetric interpretation, intent specification, machine orchestration, implicit
acquisition,* and *stratified fluency*.

**Methodological approach.** The dissertation follows the standard scale development sequence across
three papers (Hinkin, 1998; Lambert & Newman, 2023; MacKenzie et al., 2011). Paper 1 establishes
algorithmacy as a theoretically distinct construct through an integrative review, mapping the dyadic
inheritance problem and specifying the construct's domain, dimensionality, and nomological network.
Paper 2 generates and content-validates an item pool through subject-matter expert review and pilot
administration.

Paper 3 validates the scale through a three-wave panel study across multiple cohorts of GauntleTT, an
eight-week intensive AI engineering program in Trinidad and Tobago. Surveys at intake (Week 1),
midpoint (Week 4), and exit (Week 8) test factor structure, measurement invariance across waves and
cohorts, and discriminant validity against algorithm sensemaking and AI literacy. Predictive validity
is established against coordination outcomes captured through program operations (weekly deliverable
scores, peer evaluations, final placement results). The eight-week interval and rolling cohort
enrollment generate the within-person and between-cohort variation a longitudinal panel design
requires.  We obtained IRB approval last month.

**Intended contribution.** The dissertation delivers a validated measure of algorithmacy
distinguishable from existing constructs. It provides empirical evidence that the competency develops
through intensive AI-mediated coordination and predicts outcomes that comparison constructs miss. The
Trinidad cohort is constitutive of the contribution, not merely convenient for it. The construct has
been developed through intensive engagement with the AI-mediated coordination literature, and the
GauntleTT cohort is one of the few populations where that formation is observable in compressed time
on an identical curriculum.  Beyond my research, GauntleTT provides a critical support network for
island residents, and the support of the government's AI Ministry and local MP in La Brea validate
not only the ideas but also the work.
