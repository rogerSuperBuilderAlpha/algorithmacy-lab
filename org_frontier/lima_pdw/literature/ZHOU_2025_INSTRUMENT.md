# Zhou et al. 2025 — the algorithmic competency scale, item by item

The nearest rival instrument, pulled 2026-08-13 so §4's discrimination can be argued against the
actual items rather than against a summary. See [`REFERENCES.md`](REFERENCES.md) for why this is the
Lima abstract's most exposed point.

**Source.** Lian Zhou, Xue Lei, Mingwei Liu, Xinran Huang, and Rui Hou, "Algorithmic Competency of
On-demand Labor Platform Workers: Scale Development, Antecedents, and Consequences." Journal version
in *Asia Pacific Journal of Human Resources* (2025), doi:10.1111/1744-7941.70004. **Items below are
transcribed from the open working-paper version** — Rutgers Center for Global Work and Employment
Working Paper 2025.02, February 2025 — Tables 1, 2, and 4. First three authors contributed equally.
Affiliations: Guangdong University of Technology, East China University of Science and Technology,
and Rutgers SMLR. **Check the items against the journal version before citing them as final**; the
working paper predates publication.

## The construct

Algorithmic competency (AC), in four dimensions, developed across five Chinese samples of on-demand
platform workers — ride-hailing drivers and food-delivery workers.

| Dimension | Their definition |
|---|---|
| **Understanding AM** | Having a sophisticated understanding of how algorithmic management works and being able to make sense of its dynamic nature |
| **Embracing AM** | Willingness to trust the efficiency and accuracy of AM in matching workers and customers, and in other respects |
| **Leveraging AM** | Capacity to utilize AM strategically or advantageously for the worker's benefit |
| **Remediating AM** | Capacity to solve or supplement the deficiencies of the platform's AM system |

Sequence: 99 semi-structured interviews (Sample 1) → 14 items → expert content validation (agreement
76.9–100%) → EFA, N = 275 (Sample 2), two items dropped → CFA, N = 213 (Sample 3), second-order
four-factor model preferred → validity, N = 230 (Sample 4) → antecedents and outcomes, N = 225
three-wave (Sample 5).

## The twelve items

Loadings are EFA (Sample 2) with CFA (Sample 3) in parentheses, primary loading only. "AM" is
algorithmic management throughout — their wording, kept verbatim.

**Understanding AM** (α = .83)

1. I am willing to take time to develop a good understanding of AM. — .74 (.59)
2. I am familiar with the platform's AM. — .83 (.94)
3. I understand the dynamic changes in algorithms and big data patterns. — .74 (.78)

**Remediating AM** (α = .77)

4. I can address deficiencies in AM by integrating personal experience. — .76 (.92)
5. I can supplement AM's shortcomings (i.e., imprecise navigation) through the help of WeChat groups
   or other tools. — .84 (.83)
6. I can use platform APP functions (i.e., reporting exceptions and appealing) to resolve
   vulnerabilities in AM. — .62 (.54)

**Leveraging AM** (α = .81)

7. I proactively explore AM rules to obtain more high-quality orders. — .84 (.90)
8. I proactively explore AM rules to minimize negative customer feedback. — .77 (.67)
9. I utilize AM rules to minimize labor input and increase earnings. — .76 (.63)

**Embracing AM** (α = .78)

10. I think the platform AM is accurate in aspects like recording online durations. — .81 (.78)
11. I think the platform AM is highly efficient, such as in customers-workers matching. — .79 (.81)
12. I perceive the AM are undergoing refinement, such as in achieving more accurate location
    tracking. — .72 (.84)

Overall AC α = .85. Dimension intercorrelations run .22–.51; Embracing is the weakest-linked
dimension (.22–.25 with the others).

## What they already discriminated against

| Comparison construct | Their result |
|---|---|
| Digital competence (Wang et al. 2021, 6 items) | r = .37 with AC — **and r = .04, ns, with Embracing AM**, which they explain as digital-competence measures omitting attitude |
| Competence need satisfaction (Chen et al. 2015, 9 items) | r = .54 with AC — convergent, and their highest |
| Perceived AM (Parent-Rocheleau et al. 2024) | r = .17 — discriminant, as predicted |

Incremental validity: AC explained significant additional variance in customer-oriented service
behaviour and identification with gig work, beyond the related constructs.

## Reading this against algorithmacy

The abstract's one-line dismissal — that algorithmic competency measures "technical knowledge rather
than the competency to coordinate through the system" — does not survive the items. Only dimension
one is knowledge-like. **Leveraging** is strategic behaviour toward the system, **Remediating** is
repair of the system's failures, and **Embracing** is an attitude. Where the dismissal *can* be
rebuilt is on a different axis, and the items make the argument available:

- **Nothing in the twelve items concerns the human counterpart.** Every item is worker-to-system.
  The closest approach, item 8, treats customer feedback as an *output to be minimized*, not as a
  party being coordinated with. Algorithmacy is defined on the worker–counterpart-through-system
  axis, so the triad is exactly what the rival scale leaves out. **This is the discrimination worth
  making**, and it is the abstract's own theoretical claim, not a new one.
- **Their site fixes their content.** Ride-hailing and food-delivery under a scoring and dispatch
  regime — hence appeals, WeChat groups, hot spots, order quality. A generative-AI knowledge-work
  setting has no dispatch queue and no ratings appeal, so the items do not transfer even if the
  construct does. Say so; it justifies a new instrument without disparaging theirs.
- **Two component pairs will correlate and should be predicted to.** Their *Leveraging* against
  algorithmacy's *machine orchestration* and *intent specification*; their *Understanding* against
  *asymmetric interpretation*. Predicting moderate correlations in advance is stronger than being
  shown them at the roundtable.
- **Their Embracing has no counterpart in algorithmacy**, and algorithmacy's *implicit acquisition*
  and *stratified fluency* have none in theirs. Both gaps are arguments for distinctness, and the
  second is the abstract's central developmental claim.
