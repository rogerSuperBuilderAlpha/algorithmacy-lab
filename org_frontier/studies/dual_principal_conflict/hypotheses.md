# Dual principal conflict — hypotheses (fixed before computing)

**Question (RESEARCH_AGENDA_50_V2 #29).** With two principals issuing
conflicting commits, whose core wins, and is there a stable shared
core (extends the single-principal study and #37)?

**Already known (cited, not reopened).**
- `principal/`: P joins iff bidirectional (gates + responds); extreme
  coupling can contract to {S,P}.
- Probe #74: {S,P} is a rare high-coupling regime, not generic.
- Probe #78: extractive S←P ejects parties toward {S,P}.
- #37 PROTOCOL_IS_COMMIT — pointer only (encoding cut).
- COMMIT_READ / encoding ladders — conceptual bridges only.
- Construct/omit, estimation, stoch-temporal, formal, AI lanes closed.

**Universe.** Exact binary IIT-4.0 Φ. Nodes `(W, S, C, A, B)` = worker,
system, counterpart, principal₁, principal₂. Designed dual-commit
panel; candid N. In-silico Boolean abstractions.

**Definitions (fixed).**
- **A dominates:** major complex contains A and not B (or symmetrically
  B dominates).
- **Shared core:** both A and B in the same major complex.
- **Collapse:** empty / null complex, or neither principal in core while
  the form fails to host a stable principal-bearing complex
  (party-only or empty).

## H1 — one principal’s core dominates

At least one conflicting-extractive or asymmetric form has exactly one
of {A,B} in the major complex (dominance). Null: no dominance form.

## H2 — stable shared / joint core

At least one joint-commit form has both A and B in the same major
complex with Φ > 0. Null: no shared-core form.

## H3 — split / unstable cores or dyadic collapse

At least one conflict encoding yields empty/null complex or ejects
both principals (collapse). Null: every dual form keeps a principal
in core.
