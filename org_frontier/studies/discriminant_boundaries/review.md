# Discriminant boundaries — Stage 1 review

## The question

Does the dyadic/triadic verdict distinguish algorithmacy (a system that commits a determination
neither party controls) from its neighbour constructs — computer-mediated communication (CMC),
AI-mediated communication (AI-MC), human-machine communication (HMC), algorithmic management, and
sensemaking?

## The gap and the evidence state

This is the pipeline's only category whose evidence did not yet exist: the early probe loop touched
the neighbours (probes 19–23, 31) but never as a discriminant battery, and there was no paper. It
therefore needs a fresh, pre-registered experiment, not just a literature pass and a write-up.

## What the literature settles first

The Stage-2 pass (`literature/deep_research_report.md`) found the communication and management
literatures already separate these constructs on a transmit / transform / commit axis: CMC transmits
(conduit), AI-MC transforms a human's message on the sender's behalf (Hancock, Naaman & Levy 2020),
HMC is a human-machine dyad (Guzman), and directive algorithmic management commits binding
determinations (Kellogg, Valentine & Christin 2020). The discriminant claim is therefore not novel;
what the paper can contribute is an exact-Φ *operationalization* — a computable structural criterion
that sorts faithful Boolean models of each construct by the same commit-versus-convey axis.

## The design

Model each construct as a Boolean coordination form faithful to its definition; pre-register, for each,
whether it reads dyadic (convey) or triadic (commit); classify with exact Φ; and run a sensitivity
that re-models a convey channel as a committing one to show the flip. The contribution is the criterion
and its alignment with the literature's axis, not a new boundary between the constructs.
