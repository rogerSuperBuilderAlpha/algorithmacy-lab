# Discriminant boundaries — hypotheses (pre-registered)

Fixed before the battery is run, framed by the Stage-2 literature pass. The constructs and their
Boolean models are in `discriminant.py` (also committed before the run).

## H1 — Convey constructs read dyadic
A construct in which the mediating technology conveys (transmits or transforms a human's message on
that human's behalf) reads dyadic. Predictions: CMC (transparent channel), AI-MC (transform on the
sender's behalf), HMC (human-machine dyad), advisory algorithmic management (recommending), and
sensemaking all read **dyadic**. Decision rule: H1 holds if all five convey constructs read dyadic.

## H2 — The commit construct reads triadic
A construct in which the mediating system commits a determination both parties must heed reads
triadic. Prediction: directive algorithmic management (the system assigns/prices/evaluates, both
parties read it) reads **triadic** with all three parties in the core. Decision rule: H2 holds if it
reads triadic.

## H3 — The axis is commit-versus-convey, not the technology
Re-modeling the same channel so that the system commits a determination both parties must heed flips
the verdict from dyadic to triadic. Decision rule: H3 holds if the sensitivity construct (a CMC that
starts committing) reads triadic.

## Relation to prior work and what would refute the account
The transmit/transform/commit axis is established in the communication and management literatures
(Hancock et al. 2020; Kellogg et al. 2020); the hypotheses operationalize it, they do not discover it.
Refutation would be a convey construct reading triadic or the commit construct reading dyadic — which
would mean the Φ verdict does not track the commit-versus-convey distinction and the operationalization
fails. A faithful model is a modeling judgment; the paper reports the models in full so the verdict can
be checked against a reader's own encoding.
