# Deep-research report — algorithmacy and its neighbour constructs

Stage-2 literature pass for the discriminant-boundaries paper. Generated by the `deep-research`
workflow (search → fetch → adversarial verification → synthesis) and reviewed. It establishes the
neighbour constructs precisely and, importantly, finds that the communication literature *itself*
draws the distinction the lab's verdict operationalizes.

## The constructs, and the axis the literature already draws

The verified finding is that the mediated-communication literatures distinguish their constructs on a
**transmit / transform / commit** ladder:

- **Computer-mediated communication (CMC)** — technology *transmits* a human message; agency stays
  with the human communicator. The conduit/channel pole.
- **AI-mediated communication (AI-MC)** — Hancock, Naaman & Levy (2020): a computational agent
  *modifies, augments, or generates* a message *on behalf of the communicator, toward the
  communicator's own goals*. The literature frames AI-MC explicitly as an extension of and distinct
  from CMC on a transmit-versus-transform axis, and explicitly excludes systems that do not represent
  a human communicator. The distinguishing variable is content authorship/transformation, not the
  system committing a determination both parties must heed.
- **Human-machine communication (HMC)** — Guzman: the machine is itself the communicator (Siri,
  Alexa), not representing another person. A human-machine dyad with no third human party.
- **Algorithmic management** — Kellogg, Valentine & Christin (2020): algorithms direct, evaluate, and
  discipline workers through six mechanisms (restricting, recommending, recording, rating, replacing,
  rewarding) across directing/evaluating/disciplining. The *directive* mechanisms have the system
  *commit* a determination workers must heed; the *advisory* ones (recommending) merely convey. Ride-
  hail apps assign work, set surge pay, aggregate ratings, and suspend drivers — the system commits
  binding determinations the worker does not control. This is the *commit* pole.
- **Sensemaking** (Weick) — actors construct shared interpretation; the interpretive process is not a
  committing mediator.

So the literature already separates a conduit (CMC), a transformer-on-behalf-of-the-sender (AI-MC), a
machine-interlocutor (HMC), and a committing controller (directive algorithmic management). The lab's
construct — algorithmacy, a system that commits a determination neither party controls — aligns with
the directive pole of algorithmic management and is, by these definitions, distinct from CMC, AI-MC,
HMC, and sensemaking.

## What this licenses, and what is novel

The discriminant *claim* is therefore not new: the communication and management literatures already
hold these constructs apart on the commit-versus-convey axis. What the paper contributes is the
*operationalization*: the exact dyadic/triadic Φ verdict gives a computable structural criterion that
sorts a faithful Boolean model of each construct, reading the convey constructs (CMC, AI-MC, HMC,
advisory algorithmic management, sensemaking) as dyadic and the commit construct (directive
algorithmic management) as triadic, and showing — via the sensitivity step — that the same channel
flips when it starts committing. The contribution is a formal discriminant criterion for an axis the
literature draws qualitatively, not the discovery of the axis.

## Net effect on the contribution

The paper claims: (i) the neighbour constructs are distinguished in their own literatures on a
transmit/transform/commit axis (established); (ii) the exact-Φ dyadic/triadic verdict operationalizes
that axis structurally, separating the convey constructs from the commit construct on faithful Boolean
models; (iii) the distinguishing variable is commit-versus-convey, shown by the sensitivity flip. The
operationalization is the contribution; the axis is prior.
