# Discriminant boundaries — findings

A pre-registered discriminant battery: faithful Boolean models of algorithmacy's neighbour constructs,
classified by exact Φ. The hypotheses (`hypotheses.md`) and construct models (`discriminant.py`) were
committed before the run. Five of six predictions held; the sixth is an informative surprise.
Reproduce with `python -m org_frontier.studies.discriminant_boundaries.discriminant`.

## The convey constructs read dyadic; the commit construct reads triadic

The four three-party convey constructs read dyadic, as predicted: CMC (a transparent channel, core
{W,C}), AI-MC (a transform on the sender's behalf, core {W,C}), advisory algorithmic management
(recommending, core {W,S}), and sensemaking (shared interpretation, core {W,C}). In each, the parties
coordinate and the mediating technology is a conduit or a side process. Directive algorithmic
management — the system commits a determination both the worker and the counterpart must heed — reads
triadic at Φ = 2.0 with all three parties in the core. The verdict separates commit from convey.

## The sensitivity flip pins the axis

The same CMC channel, re-modeled so the system commits a determination both parties must heed, flips
from dyadic to triadic (Φ = 2.0, core {W,S,C}). The operative variable is commit-versus-convey, not the
technology: a channel becomes algorithmacy exactly when it starts committing.

## The surprise: human-machine communication is a dyad, and the verdict presupposes a triad

HMC, modeled as a two-node human-machine mutual relationship, was predicted dyadic but read triadic
(Φ = 2.0). The reason is structural and worth stating: any bidirectionally coupled pair is irreducible
in IIT, so a two-node mutual system has Φ > 0. The dyadic/triadic verdict was built for three-party
coordination forms — it asks whether a mediated triad factors along party lines — and it does not
sensibly apply to a two-party form, where there is no triad to factor and no third party to mediate.

HMC is therefore distinct from algorithmacy not because it reads dyadic, but because it is a two-party
relationship with no third party at all. Algorithmacy is defined on the worker–system–counterpart
triad; HMC has only a human and a machine. The surprise is the pre-registration catching that the
instrument's binary mislabels a two-party form, and it sharpens the boundary: HMC is excluded from
algorithmacy by party count, prior to any Φ verdict.

## The discriminant

For the constructs that are three-party coordination forms, the exact-Φ verdict operationalizes the
commit-versus-convey axis the communication and management literatures already draw: CMC and AI-MC
(transmit/transform; Hancock, Naaman & Levy 2020), advisory algorithmic management, and sensemaking are
convey constructs and read dyadic; directive algorithmic management (Kellogg, Valentine & Christin
2020) is the commit construct and reads triadic. Algorithmacy is the committing pole. HMC sits outside
the comparison as a two-party form.

## Limits

In-silico, on small Boolean models; each construct is one stipulated encoding of its definition, and a
different defensible model could read differently — the models are given in full so a reader can check.
The discriminant axis (commit vs convey) is established in the prior literature; the contribution is the
formal criterion that tracks it, not the axis. The HMC case shows the verdict should be applied only to
three-party coordination forms. The validation gap stands.
