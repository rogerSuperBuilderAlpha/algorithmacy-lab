# Deep-research report — core membership in IIT 4.0, and prior accounts of pivotality

Stage-2 literature pass for the core-membership-law paper. Generated by the lab's `deep-research`
workflow (parallel search → fetch → adversarial verification → synthesis) and reviewed. Its purpose
is to decide whether the lab's two-condition account — a party is in the IIT-4.0 major complex iff it
is bidirectionally coupled and causally pivotal — is novel, implied by existing theory, or a
rediscovery. The answer is split, and it reshapes the paper's contribution.

## 1. Bidirectional coupling is an established IIT 4.0 requirement, not a discovery

IIT 4.0 states the bidirectionality condition for substrate membership explicitly. In *System
Integrated Information* (Marshall, Grasso, Mayner, Albantakis, Tononi; arXiv:2212.14537, Entropy
2023), the directional-system-partitions section states that "a subset of units that cannot affect
the rest of the system, or cannot be affected by it, cannot truly be a part of the system… to be a
part of a system, a subset of units must be able to interact with the rest of the system in both
directions (cause and effect)." It is enforced formally: system integrated information is
φ_s = min{φ_c, φ_e} — the minimum of cause-side and effect-side irreducibility — and directed
partitions cut inputs, outputs, or both, precisely to catch units that only give or only take
influence.

The canonical IIT 4.0 paper (Albantakis, Tononi et al., PLOS Comput Biol 2023) frames integration as
requiring "every mechanistic element [to] have the capacity to act as a cause on the rest of the
system and to be affected by the rest of the system," and the exclusion postulate selects the
maximal substrate (complex) by winner-take-all maximization of φ_s.

**Consequence for the paper.** The lab's *necessity* condition — emit-only sources and read-only
sinks never enter the core — is a confirmation of IIT 4.0's system-integration requirement on
coordination forms, not a new finding. The paper must cite this and present the necessity half as a
behavioural confirmation of the theory, with the quantification (non-bidirectional nodes 0/435 in
the core across the unconstrained 3-node family) as the empirical content.

## 2. Graded inclusion by pivotality is novel relative to IIT

Multiple primary and secondary sources agree that IIT membership is **binary**. The exclusion
postulate yields "a definite set of units, neither less nor more"; overlapping substrates with lower
φ_s are excluded entirely. IIT 4.0 has no notion of a graded inclusion probability that rises with an
element's causal strength. The lab's *pivotality* result — over a population of forms, the
probability a coupled node is in the major complex rises monotonically with the determination's
Boolean sensitivity to it (rank-AUC 0.89 over 256 determinations) — is therefore not stated in IIT.
It is a population-level regularity bridging IIT's per-form binary exclusion to a graded,
across-forms membership law. That bridge is the paper's novel contribution.

## 3. Pivotality and substitutability are formalized in cooperative game theory and economics

The pivotality notion is not new to science, only to IIT. Cooperative game theory's **Shapley value**
formalizes a player's pivotality as the average marginal contribution v(S∪{i}) − v(S) across
coalitions; the **Null Player axiom** assigns zero value to a player who never changes any coalition's
worth (the formal analogue of a non-pivotal, fully substitutable member); the **Symmetry axiom**
treats interchangeable players identically; and the **Shapley–Shubik power index** counts how often a
player is pivotal in turning a losing coalition into a winning one. In economics, **Kremer's O-ring
theory** (1993) models production where quality loss in any essential, non-substitutable task
multiplicatively degrades the whole — the organizational analogue of "substitutability collapses
irreducibility."

**Consequence for the paper.** The pivotality half, and the standing result that substitutability of
any party collapses the triad, are best framed as the cause-effect-structure analogue of Shapley
pivotality and the Null Player axiom — a mapping between an information-theoretic membership criterion
and a value-theoretic one, not an independent discovery of pivotality.

## Net effect on the contribution

The paper does not claim to discover that bidirectional coupling or pivotality matter. It claims: (i)
IIT 4.0's bidirectionality requirement is confirmed on a population of coordination forms and
quantified; (ii) a graded, across-form membership law — inclusion probability monotone in causal
influence — holds empirically although IIT membership is per-form binary; (iii) this graded law and
the substitutability-collapse result are the cause-effect-structure image of Shapley pivotality and
the Null Player axiom. The novelty is the bridge and the quantification, not the conditions.
