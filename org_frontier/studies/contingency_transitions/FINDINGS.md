# contingency_transitions — findings

The four cells of the taxonomy form a state machine, and six named operations are its edges. Each operation is
modeled as a before/after form and both are classified; all six transitions land as expected, and opening the
bypass evicts a contingent party but never a necessary one. n=3 per form, exact Φ.

| operation | transition | the move |
|---|---|---|
| open_the_bypass | contingent → reducible | deregulation, mandated interoperability, an antitrust remedy, breaking an exclusive |
| erect_a_constraint | reducible → contingent | a law, license, exclusive contract, or walled garden goes up |
| integrate | contingent → necessary | the intermediary builds a service that computes a joint condition |
| commoditize | necessary → reducible | the integration becomes reproducible, so the direct tie can do it |
| raise_the_gate | partial → contingent | a partial intermediary closes its direct channel |
| lower_the_gate | partial → reducible | the direct channel takes over until the bypass is default |

## An intermediary's class is a state, not a fact

The catalog and the durability forecast treat an intermediary's class as given. This study shows it is a
state that operations move. A car dealer is contingent today and reducible the day franchise law is repealed;
the app store's payment cut is contingent now and becomes reducible as the Digital Markets Act forces
sideloading. The same operation run backward — erect a constraint — moves a reducible conduit into the
contingent cell: a free reseller that wins an exclusive, a trade that gets itself licensed. The bypass-
counterfactual classifies the form, and the operations change the form, so the class is the current reading of
a moving position.

## The operations are the policy levers

The edges are not abstract. Opening the bypass is exactly what deregulation, mandated interoperability, and
antitrust remedies do: they remove the constraint that held the parties apart, and a contingent intermediary
falls to reducible. Erecting a constraint is what a new licensing regime, an exclusive contract, or a walled
garden does: a reducible conduit becomes contingent, defended by the constraint rather than by its work. The
two operations are inverses, and they are the two things regulators argue about — whether to take a constraint
down or whether one should have been allowed up. The taxonomy gives the argument a target: the operation moves
a named intermediary between two named cells, and the contingency margin says how much is at stake in the move.

## Necessary is the only durable cell

The invariant is the strategic reading. Opening the bypass — the operation a regulator or a competitor can
force — evicts a contingent party to reducible and takes nothing from a necessary one. A firm that builds its
position by erecting a constraint holds a contingent cell, and a contingent cell is a rent that a single policy
move erases. A firm that builds its position by integrating — by computing a joint condition the direct tie
cannot reproduce — holds the necessary cell, and the necessary cell is the one no opening of the bypass can
evict. The durable strategy is to integrate, not to gate. The same reading runs the other way for policy: a
remedy that opens the bypass removes a contingent rent without touching a necessary service, which is the
decomposition the dual-function study made for whole platforms, here as a move rather than a snapshot.

## Caveats

Six operations modeled as before/after pairs of the four templates at n=3, classified by exact Φ; worked
illustrations of the moves, not fitted models of any deregulation or merger. The operations are the obvious
edges between the cells, not an exhaustive set; the partial cell in particular has more edges than the two
shown. The real examples are illustrative attachments to the structural moves, not measured cases. In-silico;
a state machine over the taxonomy, not a measurement of any market.
