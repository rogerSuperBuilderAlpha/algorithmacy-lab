# Human-subjects considerations — gig dispatch

This study interviews gig drivers about a platform that evaluates them and can deactivate them. The
population is economically precarious and already surveilled, and the topic is the system that governs
their livelihood. The ethics are not a formality here; they shape the design.

## Approval and consent

The study requires institutional review board approval before any recruitment. Consent is informed,
voluntary, and revocable: each participant is told the purpose, that participation is unconnected to any
platform and cannot affect their standing, that they may skip questions or stop at any time, and that their
account will be de-identified. Consent is documented without collecting more identifying information than
the consent itself requires.

## Recruitment away from the platform

Drivers are recruited through channels independent of the platforms — driver forums, community
organizations, snowball referral — never through a platform's own systems. Routing recruitment through a
platform would expose participants to it and would bias the sample toward the compliant. The independence
of recruitment is part of protecting participants from retaliation.

## Anonymity against a party that can identify them

The platform can often re-identify a driver from a few details of a described ride. De-identification is
therefore strict: no platform handles, no specific dates or locations in quoted material, no ride details
fine enough to single out a trip. Quotes are generalized where a verbatim would identify the speaker, and
the loss of vividness is accepted for the protection it buys. Data is stored separately from any
identifier, access-controlled, and retained only as long as the analysis needs.

## Member-checking without exposure

The coding scheme calls for member-checking the encoded rules with the parties. This is done so that no
participant is shown another's account in a way that could identify them, and so that checking the rule
attributed to the system does not require a driver to make claims that could be traced. Where member-
checking would risk exposure, it is done in aggregate or forgone, and the limitation is reported.

## The power asymmetry, stated

The researcher, the worker, and the platform hold very different power, and the study says so. The account it produces is the worker's and the documented process, read against a formal model;
never the platform's, which is unavailable and interested. The verdict is reported as evidence about a
model built from what the parties and the documents disclose, with the platform's withheld rule named as a
limit. The study avoids advising platforms on tuning the arrangement; its audience is the worker, the
researcher, and the public account of how this coordination is structured.
