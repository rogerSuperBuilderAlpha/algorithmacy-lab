# Interview guide — gig dispatch

The questions are built to elicit each party's determination rule (step 4) and the three forces the
verdict turns on: whether the system commits or conveys, whether the driver is substitutable, and whether
any direct driver-rider channel sets the terms. Three protocols follow, for drivers, platform-side staff,
and riders. Drivers are the core population; the others triangulate the system's rule. Semi-structured:
ask the listed questions, follow what the participant raises, and probe every claim about the system for
the evidence behind it. Record verbatim where possible, since folk theories of the rule are the data.

## A. Drivers (the core population, target 20–30, to saturation)

Opening, the bounded act. Walk me through the last ride you took, from before the request came in to when
it ended. What did you do to be in a position to get it? What did the app show you, and when?

The driver's rule (what determines D). Before a request comes, what are you deciding, and on what? How do
you choose where to wait? When a ride is offered, what makes you accept or decline? Are you reacting to the
rider, to the app, or to something else? Walk me through a decline.

The system's rule (the driver's folk theory of S). When you get a ride, what do you think the app used to
send you that one? Does it look at what you have been doing, where you are, who the rider is? Has anyone
told you how it decides, or have you worked it out? How sure are you? Tell me about a time the app did
something you could not explain.

Commit versus convey. When a ride is assigned, do you feel it is a decision the app made that you and the
rider then carry out, or a suggestion you are free to shape? Can you change the fare or the route once it
is set? Could you and the rider have arranged this ride without the app in the middle?

Substitutability. If you had not been there, would that rider have gotten a driver anyway? How fast?
Does it feel like the app needs you in particular, or just a driver? Have you seen the app pass a ride you
declined straight to someone else?

The shared, swapped folk theory. Do you talk with other drivers about how the app works? Do you agree
about it? Where do you disagree? Has your sense of how it decides changed over time, and why?

Closing. Is there something the app does that a person across a table never would? What would you want it
to understand about you that it has no way to take in?

## B. Platform-side staff (dispatchers, ops, support; as access allows)

The documented process. Walk me through what happens, in the system, from a rider's request to a match.
What does the matching step read? What does it decide versus surface for a person? Is there a human in the
loop, and where? What is written down, and where does practice depart from it?

Commit versus convey. When the system matches, is the assignment binding on the driver and the rider, or a
proposal either can change? Who sets the fare and the route, and can either party move it after the match?

Substitutability and the pool. From the system's side, is a specific driver ever required for a match, or
is any eligible driver in range interchangeable? What makes a driver eligible? How is the pool managed?

The platform's objective. Does the matching optimize anything beyond pairing this driver with this rider —
utilization, surge, retention? Is that objective separable from the match, or is it the match?

## C. Riders (triangulation, target 10–15)

The rider's rule. Walk me through requesting your last ride. What did you decide, and on what? Did you
choose the driver, or did the app pair you? Could you have reached the driver directly?

Commit versus convey, from the other side. When you were matched, was that a decision the app made, or
options you picked from? Could you change the driver, the fare, or the route after the match?
