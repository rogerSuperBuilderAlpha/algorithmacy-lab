# Gig dispatch: one match

A stipulated description, written to demonstrate the pipeline. It is not field data. Paragraph numbers
are the locators the encodings cite.

**¶1.** The recurring act is a single match. A driver waits near a pickup zone, a rider opens the app and
requests a ride, and the dispatch system decides whether to pair this driver with this rider.

**¶2.** The platform's operations manual describes dispatch this way: "The system assigns a trip only
when an eligible driver is available and a rider has confirmed the request; the assignment is binding
on both and sets the fare."

**¶3.** Drivers describe it differently. One driver said: "The app just pings me with whatever is nearby.
I decide. It's a suggestion, it follows what I do."

**¶4.** Drivers act on what the system shows them: "When the trip comes up I go. If nothing comes up I
wait." Riders do the same: "Once it says a driver is assigned, I wait at the curb."

**¶5.** In dense hours several drivers are idle in the same zone. The manual says: "Any eligible driver
in range may be assigned."

**¶6.** Outside the app, contact varies. Some drivers and riders exchange numbers and arrange repeat
trips directly. One rider said: "If the app is slow I just text my regular driver." Other cities ban
off-app arrangements and drivers there have no way to reach riders except through the platform.
