# Reading the model — register first, then constructions

Two things to read the model for, and the first one is the one that gets skipped.

**Register** is the kind of words it uses: its verbs, its metaphors or lack of them, whether it
repeats a term or varies it, whether it reaches. **Construction** is the shapes its sentences take: a
matched-pair dash, a colon delivering a list, a pro-form standing where a noun should be.

A manuscript can match every construction and still read nothing like the model, because the words are
someone else's. That is what happened, and it is why register comes first here.

**This checklist informs; it never gates.** A trait becomes a rule when it is written into
`MODEL_BRIEF.md` by a person who read it in the model and in the venue.

---

# Part one — reading for register

## The method

**1 · List thirty of the model's verbs.** Write them out. This is the fastest read on a paper's
register and it takes five minutes. Capezio's abstract: *are reshaping, provide, focuses, lags,
overlooks, merge, develop, integrates, reconceptualizes, clarifies, foregrounds, concludes.* Plain,
literal, every one. A paper whose verbs are all literal will not tolerate a metaphorical one.

**2 · Count the metaphors.** Read for them; do not search. Many scholarly papers have none at all,
and a manuscript with three per page beside a model with none is not in the same register no matter
how its punctuation scores.

**3 · Watch what it does with its key term.** Does the model repeat it, or vary it? Capezio writes
*board effectiveness* seven times in eight sentences and never once reaches for a synonym. A paper
that varies is telling you variation is house style; a paper that repeats is telling you elegant
variation will read as strain.

**4 · Read its abstraction level.** Where does it use a plain verb and where a nominalisation? *"It
focuses narrowly"* rather than *"it exhibits a narrowness of focus"*.

**5 · Look for what it never does.** Aphorism. Epigram. The riddle. The knowing aside. *Actually*,
*in fact*, *of course* — the nudges that tell a reader how to feel. Their absence is as much a rule as
any presence.

**6 · Then read three siblings** and record the range, because a house style is not a genre rule.

## The test, applied to every sentence written

> **Take each notable word and figure in the sentence and ask: does the model do this?**

Notable means: any verb that is not literal, any noun doing metaphorical work, any construction that
reaches — a coined phrase, an inversion, a rhetorical flourish. Ordinary words need no check.

**A technical term is not register.** The paper's own constructs stay: its coinages, the things it
names, the vocabulary its argument runs on. What may not travel is the *manner*.

## Three kinds of number, and only one of them is a finding

The skill had no way to tell these apart, and five independent runs collapsed them — every one kept a
result figure on the reasoning that "the venue states counts," which is true of a different kind of
number entirely.

| kind | what it is | the venue's practice | ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **corpus** | how much was read — *523 peer-reviewed articles*, *936 articles*, *697 source appearances* | **five of seven state one** | state it, in the pivot sentence where they put theirs |
| **result** | what was found — *four of twelve*, *nineteen demonstrations*, a rate, a share | **none of the seven states one** | state the finding **in words**; the figure belongs in the body |
| **structural** | *three forms*, *two properties* — a determiner, not a count | ubiquitous | ordinary prose, no ruling needed |

Five of seven abstracts do state a finding — *"we uncover a core paradox"*, *"our work shows that CA
is less about … and more about …"* — so the finding is not the problem. **Its figure is.**

## When a genre rule would discharge a credit

The classification table sends *model-doesn't + venue-doesn't* to **avoid it**. That is right until the
construction is carrying a credit, and then two rules of this skill point opposite ways: the genre
says drop it, the truth axis says the credit may not move.

**Split the construction.** Take from the genre what it forbids in *form*; keep what the credit needs
in *substance*.

Worked: no abstract in the venue carries a citation parenthesis, and this paper extends a proposal
someone else made. Five runs split three ways — two dropped the names entirely, one kept full
citations, and three **kept the names and dropped the years**, so no parenthesis ever appears. That
third answer honours both rules and is the one to take.

An abstract that names nobody discharges a credit only by implication, which is not discharging it.

The evidence that a register axis is working is what it catches that is **not** on this page. In those

## The worked example — the run that failed this

Every mannered phrase in the first abstract this skill produced came from the draft, imported
wholesale while only the punctuation was checked:

| the model's register | what the draft supplied, unchecked | what it should have been |
|---|---|---|
| "focuses narrowly on select dimensions" | "leaves the grantor's power to withdraw it **unpriced**" | "rarely examines who can take it away" |
| "overlooks relevant scholarly research" | "reads it without the older tradition's **apparatus**" | "overlooks the older literature that named the operation" |
| "merge insights from both literatures" | "two operations have **shared a word**" | "whether they describe one operation or two" |
| "for board research and practice" | "the instruments courts and regulators **actually** read" | "for coordination research and for practice" |
| "Few efforts have been made to…" | "**A proposal is not yet a form**" | *(an aphorism; the model writes none)* |

Eight sentences carried a **Style ✅** and not one of those verdicts had looked at a word. The verdicts
were true — the punctuation did match — and useless, because they answered a question nobody had asked
about a defect nobody had checked for.

# Part two — reading for constructions

## The method

**1 · Read the model whole and mark what recurs.** Constructions announce themselves by repetition;
the ones that matter appear more than three or four times.

**2 · Read the same construction in at least three siblings.** Record the model's practice *and* the
range. Two different questions:

> *Does the model do this?* → a fact about the model.
> *Does the venue do this?* → a fact about the genre.

**3 · Write the rule as a construction with the alternative named, never as a rate.** "Reduce
em-dashes by 40%" invites metric-chasing. "The matched pair interrupting a subject has no counterpart
in him; where it carries a list he writes `such as` inside the subject" can be acted on.

**4 · Classify before targeting.**

| the model | the venue | what it is | what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| does it | does it | **a genre rule** | learn it |
| does it | doesn't | **the model's signature** | learn it knowingly; not required |
| doesn't | does it | **the model's abstention** | record it; do *not* drive to zero |
| doesn't | doesn't | **outside the genre** | avoid it |

The third row does the damage. An abstention is not a prohibition, and treating it as one puts the
manuscript outside the venue in the other direction.

## The worked example — four rules, three of them wrong

**Colon form.** The claim was *"both his methodology colons deliver a full clause"* — two sentences,
one section, generalised to a whole manuscript. Read all twenty-two and the dominant form is
**announce a count, then deliver a list**: *"Practitioners highlight three responsibilities: duty of
care…; conformance…"*, *"Three components consistently emerge: future stewardship…"*. Six deliver a
clause. Both are his; the pass repaired away the two of ours nearest his commoner form.

**Em-dash.** Four in 21,000 words, every one a **single trailing dash introducing a gloss**, three
literally `—that is,`. No matched pairs. But the venue runs **1–26 per paper**. Learn the trailing
gloss; do not inherit the abstention.

**Pro-form subjects.** `It` / `This` with no noun attached: he does it 0 times in 499 sentences, the
venue **2–9 per paper**. Not a rule. **Read the referent chain instead** — how far back the antecedent
sits, whether it is named before it is used, whether the pronoun has exactly one candidate.

**Attaching instances.** `such as` and `including`: every exemplar uses both; the manuscript used
**neither, once, in 17,000 words**, and started a new sentence per instance instead. The largest
divergence in the set, and unexamined for two rounds, because nobody read the model for what it *does*
— only for what it avoids. **Read for deficits as hard as for excesses.**

## The wider battery

Each is a question, not a threshold.

**Subject and opener.** Bare pro-form or demonstrative-plus-noun? Definite noun phrase? Existential
*There is* / cleft *It is*? Gerund phrase? Fronted adverbial? A nominalisation where an agent could
stand?

**Punctuation as construction.** Matched-pair dash, trailing gloss, or none? Colon delivering a
clause, a list, or a run-in head? Semicolon joining clauses or separating items? Parenthesis glossing,
cross-referencing, or narrowing scope?

**Glossing a term.** `(i.e., …)`, `that is`, `defined as`, a comma appositive, or a following
sentence? *Six of the model's nine glosses are `(i.e., …)`* — a form the manuscript never used.

**Attaching instances.** `such as`, `including`, `namely`, bare comma list, enumerated `(a)(b)(c)`,
semicolon list, or a new sentence each.

**Citation.** Subject position, mid-sentence, or sentence-final? Narrative or parenthetical? How many
pooled in one parenthesis? Do runs of sentences all close on citations?

**Person and stance.** First person singular, plural, or none — and does it mark authorial labour or
carry a claim? Hedges attached to a named doubt or floating?

**Contrast.** `not X but Y`, `, not X`, `rather than` — and is the wrong alternative one a reader
would actually reach for?

**Paragraph position.** What shape opens a paragraph, and what closes one? One-sentence paragraphs,
and where?

**Shape.** Not the mean, which hides everything. Where do the short sentences fall — paragraph-final
verdicts, paragraph-opening hooks, or mid-paragraph facts?

## The trap: a construction another check scores favourably

The bare pro-form survived because the cohesion measure in use scored it **good**. It counts a
sentence as tail-head linked when the opening span holds a pro-form and shares a content word with its
predecessor. *"It is not yet a form…"* opens with `It` and shares `form`. It scored as a link.

**Where a trait is scored well by any check the project also runs, write that down beside the rule.**
Otherwise a later pass optimises the number and reinstalls the defect, believing it is improving.

> A check that rewards a *proxy* for a quality will reward anything wearing the proxy. A pro-form is a
> proxy for cohesion; a shared word is a proxy for a thread. Both can be present while the thread is
> broken.

When a sentence passes a check and still reads wrong, the check is the thing to distrust.
