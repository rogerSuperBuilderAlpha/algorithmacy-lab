## Summary

### Takeaway
Hult publishes a course with this exact title as a Year 1 core course in its Boston **Bachelor's in Computer
Science for Business (CSB)**. Hult's 2026–27 Academic Regulations and Undergraduate Handbook say how such a
course must be graded, how attendance counts, and how AI use is acknowledged. The draft conflicts with them in
three places: the grading rule, the claim that extra credit erases an absence, and the AI disclosure rule. The
device ban needs an accommodation exception to satisfy Section 504, ADA Title III and the Equality Act 2010. The
coauthorship track, as worded, creates the undue-influence and authorship-criteria problems that IRB guidance
and authorship standards warn about.

### Cited Findings (headline only; detail and quotes in the sections below)
- **The course exists and has a published description.** Hult's Feb 2026 CSB course guide lists "Algorithms &
  Data Structures" in Year 1 and gives "Languages used: Java, Python, Cursor, Claude Code", which differs from
  the draft's "Python only" (Syl. L59, L444) — [CSB Course Guide, Feb 2026](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/f1778bd896/ugcsb_course_description_guides_2026.pdf).
- **Credit definition.** For undergraduates, one US credit is "one hour of in-class student work plus 2 to 2½
  hours of out-of-class learning activity for a term of approximately 15 weeks" (Regs §3.2.4), so a 15-week,
  30-class course fits — [Academic Regulations 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf).
- **Grading.** Hult requires assessment that "Measures the achievement of Course Learning Outcomes", percentage
  scores "weighted and combined to produce a final percentage", approved rubrics, and second-marking (Regs §4.1.2,
  §4.2.1, §4.1.10). A grade from attendance plus effort "as judged by me" (Syl. L359–365) meets none of these as
  written — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf).
- **Attendance.** "Hult does not distinguish between excused and unexcused absences, and all absences are counted
  towards attendance and non-engagement thresholds" (UG Handbook §8.1.8). Extra credit therefore cannot make "an
  absence ... not count against you" (Syl. L370–371) outside the course grade — [UG Handbook 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/9e3db48768/hult-ug-student-handbook-ay-2026-27.pdf).
- **Devices.** Hult lets faculty "require use of devices, and may permit them for note taking" (Regs §9.4.4).
  Section 504 regulations bar rules "such as the prohibition of tape recorders in classrooms ... that have the
  effect of limiting the participation of handicapped students" — [34 CFR 104.44](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-34?part=104&section=104.44).
- **Accessibility office.** Hult's office is the **Accessibility Team** (hultaccessibility@hult.edu), reached
  through the Accommodation Request Form — [Hult Accessibility and Accommodations Policy, Jan 2026](https://a.storyblok.com/f/45434/x/328d073fdc/hult-accessibility-and-accommodations-policy-2026.pdf).
- **AI.** The Regulations say "All use of generative AI tools must be referenced" (Regs §9.6.3); the draft asks
  only that students "Be ready to show which tools you used and how, if I ask" (Syl. L409–410).
- **Coauthorship.** ICMJE says "All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for authorship"
  ([ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)).
  Columbia's IRB says "The most coercive situation is either required participation as a course requirement or
  direct solicitation by a faculty member who is teaching a course in which the student is registered"
  ([Columbia HRPO, 2024](https://research.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/HRPO/Policies%20%26%20Guidance/Students%20as%20Research%20Subjects%20GuidanceFINAL20240523.pdf)).
  "Expected to contribute" plus a promised coauthorship (Syl. L145–147, L421–423) runs into both.
- **Approval.** Changing a course description, its learning outcomes, or "the methods and/or weighting of
  assessments within a course" is a minor modification that goes "to the Curriculum Committee for approval" (Regs
  §5.5.6–5.5.7), and Hult keeps "A master course syllabus for every core course" (Regs §5.4.1).

### Inferences
- **Judgment:** The professor should get the CSB master course syllabus for Algorithms & Data Structures from the
  CSB program team (the Program Management Team or Academic Director) before finishing the draft. Hult's
  regulations make that document, not the draft, the approved baseline, and they route material changes through
  the Curriculum Committee.

### Gaps
- I could not find a public Hult syllabus template, a public "Artificial Intelligence Policy", a public research
  ethics policy, an academic calendar with dates, the class session length, the course code, or the credit
  value of this course. Details are in each section.

### Inferences
- **Judgment (program placement):** The course belongs to the CSB, taken by first-years in the second Year 1
  term. The two-column grid puts it beside the "Decision Lab" challenge and after "Programming & OOP Thinking With
  AI". That makes "Programming & OOP Thinking With AI" the likely prerequisite, but Hult does not print one.
- **Judgment (master's):** I found no evidence that Hult offers this course in a master's program. The PG
  catalog's 2-credit, roughly four-week summer electives do not resemble a 30-class course.
- **Judgment (fit of the 15-week, 30-class format):** It matches Hult's UG credit definition ("approximately 15
  weeks") and the Fall/Spring term structure. Whether 30 sessions fit depends on the actual term calendar (the
  first week is Add/Drop/Switch Week, plus any holidays), which I could not find. The draft already flags "[adjust
  if the term differs]" (Syl. L246).
- **Judgment (credit-hour arithmetic, computed from Regs §3.2.4):** If the course carries 3 US credits, it needs
  about 3 in-class hours and 6–7.5 out-of-class hours per week over ~15 weeks. Two 90-minute sessions supply the
  in-class hours, but the take-home "at least 2 hours" (Syl. L106) gives only 4 hours a week, so each take-home
  would need 3–3¾ hours. If the course carries 2 credits, two 60-minute sessions and 2–2½ hours per take-home
  match exactly. 3 US credits = 15 UK credits = 150 notional hours = 7.5 ECTS (Regs §3.2.4–3.2.5).
- **Judgment (language mismatch):** The current published description lists "Java, Python, Cursor, Claude Code"
  and says students "will build implementations". The draft says "The course uses Python only" (Syl. L59) and "No
  phones or computers" in class (Syl. L450–451). Students who read the course guide may expect Java and AI coding
  tools. This is the professor's call; the published text is Hult's.

## Risk area 1: The grading rule ("Attend every class ... and you earn an A")

### Takeaway
Hult requires course grades built from weighted, rubric-graded assessments that measure the Course Learning
Outcomes, scored as percentages, second-marked and sample-reviewed. The draft's rule (Syl. L359–365) grades
attendance plus instructor-judged effort and promises an A. As written, it does not meet Hult's Regulations. It
also leaves the grade for anyone short of an A undefined (Syl. L384–387 "TBD"). Hult's attendance policy counts
every absence regardless of extra credit.

## Risk area 2: The device ban

### Takeaway
Hult lets faculty set device rules. US disability law (Section 504, ADA Title III) and the UK Equality Act 2010
still require exceptions for students whose approved accommodation is a laptop, tablet or other device, and an
alternative to chalk-at-the-board work where it is a barrier. Hult's January 2026 policy routes these decisions
to the Accessibility Team through a signed agreement the student shares with faculty. The syllabus should say
that and point to the office by name.

### Cited Findings — Hult
- "Use of devices for non-class activity (surfing, messaging, work for other classes). Faculty will sometimes
  require use of devices, and may permit them for note taking, but use for non-class activity is rude and
  disruptive." (Regs §9.4.4) — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- "In compliance with the Disability Section of the Rehabilitation Act and Section III of the Americans with
  Disabilities Act, and the UK Disability Discrimination Act (DDA) of 2005 and the Equality Act of 2010, Hult
  will provide reasonable accommodations to students with disabilities or learning differences. A reasonable
  accommodation is a modification or adjustment to a course, program, or activity that enables a qualified
  student with a disability or learning difference to obtain equal access." (Regs §7.4.1; same in UG HB §8.4.1) — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- "Approved accommodations do not normally alter academic standards, learning outcomes, or essential program
  requirements. Attendance expectations remain in place unless explicitly modified as part of an approved
  accommodation." (UG HB §8.4.3) — [UG Handbook 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/9e3db48768/hult-ug-student-handbook-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- "An accommodation that causes an undue hardship to Hult or that fundamentally alters the nature of a program is
  not reasonable, and Hult will not provide such an accommodation." "The student is responsible for providing a
  copy of this signed agreement to any relevant professor/faculty member". "Accommodations are not retroactive".
  Reviews "may make take up to 5 business days" [sic]. Hult "prohibits retaliation against any person who

- Exams: "Students required to complete an examination or quiz must do so at the scheduled date and time and must
  bring a laptop capable to complete the assessment. Where examinations or quizzes are used, students will be
  required to install the school's anti-cheating software" (UG HB §7.2.1) — [UG Handbook 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/9e3db48768/hult-ug-student-handbook-ay-2026-27.pdf)

### Cited Findings — US law
- Section 504: "Modifications may include changes in the length of time permitted for the completion of degree
  requirements, substitution of specific courses required for the completion of degree requirements, and
  adaptation of the manner in which specific courses are conducted." (§104.44(a)) "A recipient ... may not impose
  upon handicapped students other rules, such as the prohibition of tape recorders in classrooms or of dog guides

  course, rather than reflecting the student's impaired sensory, manual, or speaking skills" (§104.44(c)).
  §104.44(a) also says requirements the recipient "can demonstrate are essential to the instruction being pursued

- ADA Title III: "A public accommodation shall make reasonable modifications in policies, practices, or

  The list of public accommodations includes "A nursery, elementary, secondary, undergraduate, or postgraduate

### Cited Findings — UK law (Equality Act 2010)
- legislation.gov.uk refused automated fetches (empty responses for s.20 and s.91), so I relied on the Equality

  It says: "The duty to make reasonable adjustments comprises three requirements ... Where a provision, criterion
  or practice puts disabled students at a substantial disadvantage compared with those who are not disabled, to
  take reasonable steps to avoid that disadvantage ... Where not providing an auxiliary aid or service puts
  disabled students at a substantial disadvantage compared with students who are not disabled, to take reasonable
  steps to provide that auxili[ary aid]" (paras 7.5–7.7, margin refs "s20(3) and Sch13 ... s20(5) and Sch13") — [EHRC Technical Guidance FE & HE](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/24048/1/EqualityAct2010-TechnicalGuidance-FEandHE-2015.pdf)

### Cited Findings — how universities word device bans with exceptions
- UC Berkeley's Disabled Students' Program: "If a disabled student has an accommodation for the use of a laptop or
  tablet in class for disability-related reasons, the instructor should allow the student to use their
  technology". "In your syllabus, please state that students who need a laptop or other technology should contact
  the instructor if they believe they require an exception to the course's no-technology policy. In this way, you
  can avoid specifically singling out students with disabilities." Students using a device as an accommodation
  "should not be required to sit in a particular section of the class". — [Berkeley DSP FAQ](https://dsp.berkeley.edu/faq/faculty/technology-accommodations-student%E2%80%99s-letter-accommodation-states-they-need-use-laptop)
- Search results (pages not fetched; snippets only, so unverified) show similar guidance from Sonoma State,
  Villanova, Carleton and Washington University in St. Louis: name the accommodation office in the syllabus and

### Inferences
- **Judgment:** The ban is lawful as a default. Hult's Regulations give faculty that discretion, and the EHRC's
  no-needles example shows how a blanket rule with a built-in exception survives scrutiny. Without an exception
  clause, it reads as the kind of "other rule" §104.44(b) names.
- **Judgment:** Chalk-and-paper board work (Syl. L125–126) is a second barrier, separate from devices. A student
  with a manual, visual or motor impairment may need an alternative, such as a typed answer on an approved device
  or a scribe. §104.44(c) requires evaluation methods that measure "the student's achievement in the course"
  rather than the impairment. Whether handwriting in class is "essential to the instruction" (§104.44(a)) is a
  question for Hult's Accessibility Team, not the instructor alone.
- **Judgment:** Hult requires students to "register their attendance during each class session" (UG HB §8.1.6) but
  does not publish how. If registration uses a phone or laptop, locking devices away "for the whole session"
  (Syl. L132–133) would stop students registering and get them marked absent. The professor should ask the
  program team how attendance is taken in Boston.
- **Judgment (jurisdiction):** The CSB is a Boston program (US degree under Regs §1.4.1), so Section 504 and the
  ADA are the primary frame. Hult's own policy also commits it to the Equality Act everywhere, so the same
  exception wording works at every campus.

  There is no coding on a computer in class." **[Drafted by Claude — added text in brackets]** "Devices are locked

## Risk area 3: The Research & Coauthorship Track

### Takeaway
The draft says students who excel "are expected to contribute to, and receive coauthor credit on, a paper I
submit" (Syl. L145–147, L421–423), selected on course performance by the grading instructor (Syl. L425–427), with
work continuing after the term (Syl. L439–440). Authorship standards say authorship follows contribution, not the
reverse. IRB guidance treats instructor solicitation of current students as the most coercive form of
recruitment. If course work or student behavior becomes research data, consent, ethics review and FERPA/UK GDPR
apply. The draft's promise of a written authorship agreement (Syl. L435–437) matches recommended practice and
should stay.

### Cited Findings — authorship standards
- ICMJE: authorship requires "Substantial contributions to the conception or design of the work; or the
  acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data for the work; AND Drafting the work or reviewing it critically
  for important intellectual content; AND Final approval of the version to be published; AND Agreement to be
  accountable for all aspects of the work". "All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for
  authorship, and all who meet the four criteria should be identified as authors." "The criteria are not intended
  for use as a means to disqualify colleagues from authorship who otherwise meet authorship criteria by denying
  them the opportunity to meet criterion #s 2 or 3." Contributors "who meet fewer than all 4 of the above criteria
  for authorship should not be listed as authors, but they should be acknowledged." Groups "ideally should decide
  who will be an author before the work is started". "If agreement cannot be reached about who qualifies for
  authorship, the institution(s) where the work was performed, not the journal editor, should be asked to
  investigate." — [ICMJE, Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)
- CRediT: "a community-owned taxonomy of 14 contributor roles"; "Approved in 2022 as an ANSI/NISO standard";
  roles include Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Funding acquisition, Investigation,
  Methodology, Project administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing –
  original draft, Writing – review & editing. — [CRediT](https://credit.niso.org/)
- APA Ethics Code (2002, amended 2010 and 2016, effective 1 Jan 2017; PDF fetched):
  8.12(a) "Psychologists take responsibility and credit, including authorship credit, only for work they have
  actually performed or to which they have substantially contributed." 8.12(b) "Principal authorship and other
  publication credits accurately reflect the relative scientific or professional contributions of the individuals
  involved, regardless of their relative status. ... Minor contributions to the research or to the writing for
  publications are acknowledged appropriately". 8.12(c) "Faculty advisors discuss publication credit with students
  as early as feasible and throughout the research and publication process as appropriate."
  3.05(a): a psychologist "refrains from entering into a multiple relationship if the multiple relationship could
  reasonably be expected to impair the psychologist's objectivity ... or otherwise risks exploitation or harm".
  3.08: "Psychologists do not exploit persons over whom they have supervisory, evaluative or other authority such
  as clients/patients, students, supervisees, research participants, and employees."
  8.04(a) "When psychologists conduct research with clients/patients, students, or subordinates as participants,
  psychologists take steps to protect the prospective participants from adverse consequences of declining or
  withdrawing from participation." 8.04(b) "When research participation is a course requirement or an opportunity
  for extra credit, the prospective participant is given the choice of equitable alternative activities." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- University practice: "When a student begins work on collecting, analyzing and writing up faculty data, a written
  agreement should be developed relating to any presentations or publications that result from that work. This
  written agreement should be dated and signed by the student and the faculty member." — [University at Buffalo, Guidelines for Student-Faculty Research Collaborations](https://ed.buffalo.edu/content/dam/ed/main/docs/current-students/teaching/Student-Faculty-Research-Collaboration.pdf)

### Cited Findings — undue influence, consent, ethics review
- Common Rule: "An investigator shall seek informed consent only under circumstances that provide the prospective
  subject ... sufficient opportunity to discuss and consider whether or not to participate and that minimize the
  possibility of coercion or undue influence." (45 CFR 46.116(a)(2)) — [45 CFR 46.116](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-45?part=46&section=46.116)
- Exemption category (d)(1): "Research, conducted in established or commonly accepted educational settings, that
  specifically involves normal educational practices that are not likely to adversely impact students'
  opportunity to learn required educational content or the assessment of educators who provide instruction." — [45 CFR 46.104](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-45?part=46&section=46.104)
- Columbia University IRB guidance (effective 15 Feb 2024, revised 21 May 2024): students "can be seen as
  'captive participants' due to the inherent power differential between students and teachers". "Undue influence
  may occur when a researcher presents an excessive or inappropriate reward to a prospective subject, such as
  indicating that extra credit may only be obtained if they participate in the research." "The most coercive
  situation is either required participation as a course requirement or direct solicitation by a faculty member
  who is teaching a course in which the student is registered." "Recruitment by an independent third party so that
  the instructor does not know who participated in the research is another approach." "Penalties or credit
  deductions may not be applied if a research subject chooses not to participate in, or to withdraw from, a study."
  "Identifiable data collected during the study can then not be made available to the faculty member until after
  grades in the course have been submitted". "Including a student's work product in a research project is subject
  to the requirements set forth in Section E" (FERPA). — [Columbia HRPO guidance](https://research.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/HRPO/Policies%20%26%20Guidance/Students%20as%20Research%20Subjects%20GuidanceFINAL20240523.pdf)

### Cited Findings — student records and data protection
- FERPA defines education records as records "(1) Directly related to a student; and (2) Maintained by an
  educational agency or institution or by a party acting for the agency or institution." (34 CFR 99.3) — [34 CFR 99.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-34?part=99&section=99.3)
- FERPA allows release without consent "after the removal of all personally identifiable information provided
  that the educational agency or institution or other party has made a reasonable determination that a student's

### Cited Findings — Hult rules that touch the track
- Intellectual property: "a student holds the intellectual property rights inherent in their assignment work; and

- Relationships: "faculty and staff are required to make an immediate and confidential written declaration to the
  Campus Dean of any such relationship with an applicant or Enrolled student" where "personal, business or other

  "excluded from certain roles in ... (ii) assessment" (Regs §8.2.1–8.2.2) — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- Ethics body: "Research Ethics Committee (reports to Research Committee)" (Regs §2.2.3). NECHE's 2026 Standard

  including its research activities if relevant" — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf); [NECHE Standards 2026](https://www.neche.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Standards-for-Accreditation-2026-formatted-for-PDF-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Judgment (authorship):** "receive coauthor credit for that contribution" (Syl. L422–423) promises authorship
  in advance. ICMJE and APA 8.12 tie authorship to substantial contribution plus drafting/review, final approval
  and accountability. A student who only runs benchmarks belongs in the acknowledgments under ICMJE. Conversely,
  ICMJE bars denying a substantive contributor the chance to draft and approve. The written agreement the draft
  promises (Syl. L435–437) is the right tool; CRediT gives it a vocabulary for recording roles.
- **Judgment (applicability):** ICMJE comes from medical journal editors, and the APA Code binds psychologists. A
  computer science or education journal may use its own authorship policy or COPE's. The target journal is TBD
  (Syl. L433), so the agreement should name the journal's policy once it is chosen.
- **Judgment (undue influence):** "expected to contribute" (Syl. L421) attached to "Students who excel" in a course
  where the same person grades them invites a student to read participation as part of excelling. Inviting
  students "directly" during the term (Syl. L427) is the solicitation Columbia calls most coercive. The track
  makes students collaborators, not subjects, so the Common Rule's consent text does not govern recruitment
  directly. The power dynamic is the same, and APA 3.05/3.08 and Hult Regs §8.2 address it as a dual-role problem.
- **Judgment (when ethics review applies):** If the paper studies the course itself, with students' answer sheets,
  failed attempts, memory estimates, AI-use patterns or in-class performance as data, those students become human
  subjects and their work becomes education records. That calls for Hult Research Ethics Committee review before
  data collection, informed consent obtained by someone other than the instructor or held until grades are in,
  FERPA consent or de-identification, and a UK GDPR lawful basis where Hult's UK entity processes the data.
  Exemption (d)(1) may cover normal classroom practice, but under Hult's structure the Committee, not the
  instructor, would decide that. Whether the Common Rule binds Hult (for example through a Federalwide Assurance)
  I could not verify.
- **Judgment (IP):** Students hold the IP in their assignment work (Regs §4.13.1). Using a student's solutions or
  experiments in the paper needs that student's written permission, which the authorship agreement can cover.
- **Judgment (post-term work):** Work continuing "after the semester ends" (Syl. L439–440) is outside the course.
  Saying so explicitly separates it from the grade and from attendance.

### Suggested syllabus wording (keep the professor's wording; changes only)

  coauthor credit on, a paper I submit to a journal. See Research & Coauthorship Track." **[Drafted by Claude —

- Add to the "Authorship" bullet (Syl. L435–437) **[Drafted by Claude]**: "The agreement will be dated and signed,

## Risk area 4: AI use policy and academic integrity alignment

### Takeaway
Hult allows course-specific AI rules but requires that AI use be acknowledged. The Regulations say "All use of
generative AI tools must be referenced", in APA format. The draft's transparency bullet (Syl. L409–410) asks only
for disclosure on request. Hult's separate "Artificial Intelligence Policy" is referenced but not public.

### Cited Findings
- Academic integrity is incompatible with "Improper use of generative artificial intelligence (further information
  is available in the Artificial Intelligence Policy)" and with "Unauthorized cooperation between students in
  individual work situations" (Regs §9.6.1) — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- "All use of generative AI tools must be referenced. This includes, but is not limited to, their use for content
  creation, proofreading, paraphrasing, idea generation and formatting." (Regs §9.6.3) — [Regs 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/1b37b40b53/hult-academic-regulations-ay-2026-27.pdf)
- "Students must comply with course-specific guidance regarding the use of generative AI tools and must
  appropriately acknowledge such use where required." (UG HB §6.4.4) — [UG Handbook 2026–27](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/9e3db48768/hult-ug-student-handbook-ay-2026-27.pdf).
  This is softer than Regs §9.6.3 ("where required" versus "All use"); the two Hult documents differ.
- A Hult DBA admissions document states the Hult-wide rules: "AI Policy I: AI use is highly encouraged within all
  Hult activities", "AI Policy II: AI use must be tracked and acknowledged", "AI Policy III: Any submissions not
  explicitly identified as AI-generated will be presumed as original"; "The format used and adopted Hult-wide is
  the APA 7TH Ed." — [Hult AI Usage (DBA), Feb 2026](https://a.storyblok.com/f/45434/x/ee5505107b/ai_usage_policy_dba_27022026.pdf)
- The published course description lists "Cursor, Claude Code" among the course's languages/tools — [CSB Course Guide, Feb 2026](https://a.storyblok.com/f/290844/x/f1778bd896/ugcsb_course_description_guides_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Judgment:** The draft's AI stance ("allowed and expected ... outside class", Syl. L392) fits AI Policy I. The
  transparency bullet falls short of AI Policy II and Regs §9.6.3. Requiring acknowledgment on anything submitted
  (the written take-home plan, Syl. L111–115) closes the gap. In-class work is device-free, so the rule bites only
  on out-of-class submissions.
- **Judgment:** The draft's integrity paragraph (Syl. L498–501) is consistent with Hult's rules; naming the Honor
  Code and the Academic Integrity Committee would tie it to the actual process.

### Suggested syllabus wording (keep the professor's wording; changes only)
- Syl. L409–410, currently: "Transparency. Be ready to show which tools you used and how, if I ask."
  **[Drafted by Claude]** "Transparency. Acknowledge any AI use in work you hand in, such as your take-home plan,
  in APA format as Hult requires, and be ready to show which tools you used and how, if I ask."

### Gaps
- Hult's "Artificial Intelligence Policy" (cited by Regs §9.6.1 and UG HB §6.4.2) was not found publicly; it is
  probably on myHult.
